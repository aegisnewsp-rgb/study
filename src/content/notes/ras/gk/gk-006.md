# Indian Economy and Current Affairs

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> One-screen snapshot for the morning of the RAS Prelims — read this twice and you have the spine of the topic.

India's economy is measured through five interlocking national-income aggregates — GDP, NDP, GNP, NNP, and per-capita variants — released by the Ministry of Statistics with **2011-12 as the base year**. The headline aggregate is **GDP at market price**, computed via three independent approaches (production, income, expenditure) that must reconcile.

| Concept | Must-know identity | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| GDP (market price) | C + I + G + (X − M) | ₹ crore |
| Real GDP | (Nominal GDP ÷ Deflator) × 100 | ₹ crore |
| Fiscal Deficit | Total Expenditure − Total Receipts (ex-borrowings) | ₹ crore |
| CRR vs SLR | Cash with RBI vs gold/G-secs held by banks | % of NDTL |

> 💡 **Memory Hook — "G-F-M-C-R-S":** **G**DP, **F**iscal Deficit, **M**onetary Policy rates (**R**epo), **C**RR/SLR, **R**ajasthan schemes, **S**ectoral shares. Walk that ladder the night before the exam.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> The working layer — what examiners actually test once definitions stop being enough.

#### National Income Aggregates — How They Differ

The trap in most MCQs is the *market price vs factor cost* split. Aggregate at market price includes net indirect taxes (indirect taxes minus subsidies); aggregate at factor cost excludes them. Students who swap the sign lose the mark.

| Aggregate | Formula | Includes NFIA? | At Market Price? |
|---|---|---|---|
| GDP | C + I + G + (X − M) | No | Yes |
| NDP | GDP − Depreciation | No | Yes |
| GNP | GDP + NFIA | Yes | Yes |
| NNP | GNP − Depreciation | Yes | Yes |

> 📌 **Formula Check:** NFIA = Factor income earned by Indians abroad − Factor income earned by foreigners in India. Unit: ₹ crore. The "Net" sign decides whether India is a creditor or debtor economy in factor services.

#### Fiscal Architecture and the FRBM Frame

Fiscal Deficit = Total Expenditure − Total Receipts (excluding borrowings). Primary Deficit strips out interest payments, showing the current year's borrowing need. The FRBM Act, originally enacted in 2003, sets the ceiling the Centre targets; states follow parallel fiscal responsibility legislation.

> 💡 **High-Yield Revision Hook:** "**F**iscal includes **F**inance cost, **P**rimary is **P**ost-interest" — i.e. Fiscal Deficit = Primary Deficit + Interest Payments.

#### Monetary Tools the RBI Steers

Six levers are tested repeatedly: Repo Rate (policy rate at which RBI lends short-term to banks), Reverse Repo Rate, Bank Rate, MSF Rate, CRR, and SLR. CRR is *cash* parked with RBI; SLR is *securities and gold* held by banks themselves. Both are expressed as a percentage of Net Demand and Time Liabilities (NDTL). Open Market Operations (buying/selling G-secs) round out the toolkit.

#### GST — What It Covers and What It Doesn't

GST subsumes most central and state indirect taxes into four streams: CGST, SGST, IGST, and UTGST. The GST Council, chaired by the Union Finance Minister, recommends rates under **Article 279A**. Two large categories stay outside GST — petroleum products and alcoholic liquor — and excise/VAT continue on them. Students routinely misclassify these.

#### A Worked RAS-Style Question

Consider a question that reads: *"If Nominal GDP is ₹280 lakh crore and the GDP Deflator is 140, what is Real GDP? Which expenditure component falls under 'G' in C + I + G + (X − M)?"*

Resolution: Real GDP = (Nominal GDP ÷ Deflator) × 100 = (280 ÷ 140) × 100 = **₹200 lakh crore**. The "G" component is Government Consumption and Investment Expenditure — salary bills, defence purchases, capital outlays — *not* total government spending (transfers are excluded because they do not buy goods or services).

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Many candidates divide 280 by 140 and stop at 2, forgetting to multiply by 100, ending up with ₹2 lakh crore — an answer that looks absurd only after you check the units.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> For candidates targeting RAS Mains Paper II/III answers and interview-stage confidence on the economy.

#### Edge Cases and Policy Boundaries

1. **Base-year drift.** The 2011-12 series coexists with a new 2022-23 base in some releases; cross-reference the *National Accounts Statistics* year before quoting growth rates.
2. **CPI vs WPI divergence.** CPI-Combined is the headline for monetary policy under the 2016 agreement between Government and RBI; WPI tracks wholesale prices and excludes services comprehensively. A question asking "which index is used for inflation targeting" expects CPI.
3. **Current vs Capital Account.** Remittances from the Gulf are *Current Account* (invisible exports), not Capital Account. CAD = Current Account Balance (a deficit when outflows exceed inflows) expressed as % of GDP is the textbook stress indicator.
4. **Rajasthan's economic profile.** State GDP share sits in the mid-table nationally; mining (lead, zinc, marble), tourism (Jaipur-Udaipur-Jaisalmer circuit), agriculture (mustard, guar), and handloom anchor output. Schemes to know: Mukhyamantri Chiranjeevi Swasthya Bima Yojana (cashless health cover), Indira Gandhi Smartphone Yojana, Bhamashah, and the MSME Promotion Policy.
5. **PLI sectors.** Fourteen sectors were notified initially (electronics, pharma, semiconductors, drones, textiles, food processing, etc.); sector lists have been expanded — verify against the latest Ministry of Commerce press release before citing a count.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. *Compare and contrast CRR and SLR along four axes — purpose, asset held, regulatory body, and impact on credit creation.* Aim for a crisp 150-word answer linking back to RBI's liquidity framework.
2. *"India's fiscal deficit target was set at X% of GDP under FRBM; recent years have seen deviations."* Without naming a specific figure, walk through (a) the legal escape route (Escape Clause), (b) the macroeconomic rationale, and (c) the consequence for state borrowings — citing only the act and the body that grants the deviation.

#### High-Weight Reading List (Last 12 Months)

- Union Budget — Finance Bill and Expenditure Profile volumes (indiabudget.gov.in).
- Economic Survey — overview chapter and the state-wise appendix.
- RBI Monetary Policy Report — quarterly; track changes to the policy repo rate.
- Rajasthan Economic Review — Directorate of Economics and Statistics, Jaipur.
- NITI Aayog publications on SDG localisation and state performance.

> 📌 **Formula Check (Real GDP):** Real GDP = (Nominal GDP / Deflator) × 100. Deflator is dimensionless; Real GDP inherits ₹ crore as its unit. A deflator above 100 means the current year is *more inflationary* than the base year — useful for cross-year comparison without price noise.
