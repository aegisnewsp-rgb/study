# Theory of Production and Cost

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Theory of Production** studies how inputs (land, labour, capital, enterprise) convert into output, while **Theory of Cost** studies the minimum expenditure to produce each output level. The two are inseparable: cost curves are the rupee mirror of production curves.

- **Short run** = at least one factor fixed (usually capital); behaviour is governed by the **Law of Variable Proportions** in three stages.
- **Long run** = all factors variable; behaviour is governed by **Returns to Scale**.
- **Cost split**: TC = TFC + TVC; per-unit: AC = AFC + AVC; incremental: MC = ΔTC/ΔQ.
- **Must-remember rules**: MC cuts AC and AVC at their **minimum** points; AFC keeps falling as Q rises, so AC and AVC never touch.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Production in the Short Run

Only labour (L) varies; capital (K) is fixed. Output behaviour is captured through **Total Product (TP)**, **Average Product (AP = TP/L)** and **Marginal Product (MP = ΔTP/ΔL)**. As more workers are added to a fixed factory, TP first rises at an increasing rate, then at a decreasing rate, and finally falls — this is the **Law of Variable Proportions**.

| Stage | MP behaviour | AP behaviour | Producer's choice |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | Rising, below AP | Rising | Inefficient — more workers help |
| II | Positive but falling | Maximum, then falling | **Rational zone — equilibrium lies here** |
| III | Negative (TP falls) | Falling | Irrational — workers crowd out capital |

Producer's equilibrium in the short run lies in **Stage II**, where **MP is positive but declining** and AP is at its peak.

#### Cost Curves in the Short Run

| Curve | Formula | Behaviour as Q rises |
|---|---|---|
| TFC | Constant (rent, salary of permanent staff) | Horizontal line |
| TVC | Rises from origin | First increasing rate, then decreasing, then increasing |
| TC = TFC + TVC | Sum of above | Parallel to TVC, shifted up by TFC |
| AFC = TFC/Q | Hyperbola | Always falls, approaches zero |
| AVC = TVC/Q | U-shaped | Falls, hits minimum, rises |
| AC = TC/Q = AFC + AVC | U-shaped | Falls slower than AVC; never equals AVC |
| MC = ΔTC/ΔQ | U-shaped | Cuts AVC and AC at their minimum points |

The vertical gap between AC and AVC equals AFC, which is why AC sits above AVC and the two never meet.

#### Producer's Equilibrium

A firm maximises profit where **MC = MR**, and MC must cut MR **from below** (i.e., MC < MR before the intersection, MC > MR after it). Equivalently, profit is maximised where **TR − TC is maximum**.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Long-Run Production and Returns to Scale

When **all factors** are scaled up proportionally, output may rise more than, exactly, or less than the input change.

- **IRS** (TP rises more than input) — specialisation, indivisibilities.
- **CRS** (TP rises in same proportion) — balanced expansion.
- **DRS** (TP rises less than input) — managerial limits, coordination failure.

> **Trap to avoid:** Returns to Scale ≠ Law of Variable Proportions. The first is a **long-run** phenomenon with all factors variable; the second is a **short-run** phenomenon with one factor fixed. Many MCQ papers test exactly this confusion.

#### Long-Run Cost Curves and Economies of Scale

The **LAC** is the *envelope* of all possible short-run AC curves — each SATC is tangent to LAC but does not lie below it. The **LMC** is derived from LTC (= LTVC, since there is no fixed cost in the long run).

| Source of economies of scale | Trigger |
|---|---|
| Technical | Bulk processing, specialisation of machines |
| Managerial | Division of managerial functions |
| Financial | Cheaper credit at larger borrowings |
| Marketing | Spreading advertising over more units |
| Risk-bearing | Diversified product portfolio |
| External (industry-level) | Skilled labour pool, ancillary firms |

Diseconomies of scale emerge when coordination costs, communication overheads, and principal-agent problems outweigh the gains.

#### Worked Micro-Example

Suppose a firm hires workers at ₹100 each and TFC = ₹200. From the schedule Q=0,1,2,3,4 and TVC = 0, 100, 180, 280, 420 — derive MC and AVC.

| Q | TFC | TVC | TC | MC = ΔTC | AVC = TVC/Q |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 200 | 0 | 200 | — | — |
| 1 | 200 | 100 | 300 | 100 | 100.00 |
| 2 | 200 | 180 | 380 | 80 | 90.00 |
| 3 | 200 | 280 | 480 | 100 | 93.33 |
| 4 | 200 | 420 | 620 | 140 | 105.00 |

MC is minimum (₹80) at Q=2, where AVC also bottoms at ₹90 — confirming the rule that **MC cuts AVC at its minimum**.

#### Practice Prompts

1. **Numerical:** Given TFC = ₹500 and a TP schedule (Q = 5, 12, 20, 26, 30 as L goes 1→5), compute MP, AP, MC and AC. Identify Stage II boundaries.
2. **Short answer:** "Returns to Scale is merely an extension of the Law of Variable Proportions." Critically examine this statement in 5 marks, citing at least three points of distinction.

#### Common Mistakes in CS Executive Papers

- Equating AC with AVC at high output — AFC approaches but never equals zero.
- Saying MC is always below AC — MC is below when AC falls, equal at the minimum, above when AC rises.
- Treating TFC as avoidable in the short run — it is a sunk commitment paid even at zero output.
- Confusing AP per worker with MP per **additional** worker when substituting into TP = AP × L.
