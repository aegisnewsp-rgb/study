# Separation Techniques

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Separation techniques** are **physical methods** used to isolate the components of a mixture without altering their chemical identities. The choice of method depends on differences in **particle size, solubility, boiling point, density, magnetic property**, or **adsorption affinity**.

- **Filtration** — separates an **insoluble solid** (residue) from a **liquid** (filtrate) using filter paper.
- **Crystallisation** — recovers a **soluble solid** by partial evaporation of solvent followed by cooling; impurities stay in solution (mother liquor).
- **Evaporation to dryness** — boils off the solvent completely to leave the dissolved solid.
- **Simple distillation** — separates a **solvent from a dissolved solute** using boiling-point difference; the **thermometer** reads the boiling point of the distillate.
- **Fractional distillation** — separates **miscible liquids** with close boiling points through a **fractionating column**; used in refining **crude oil** into petrol, kerosene, diesel.
- **Separating funnel** — separates **immiscible liquids** of different densities (e.g. oil and water).
- **Sublimation** — purifies solids that pass directly from solid to gas (e.g. **NH₄Cl, I₂, naphthalene**).
- **Chromatography** — separates dissolved solutes via differential travel; the **R_f = distance moved by solute ÷ distance moved by solvent front**.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Principle of Selection
Every separation technique exploits a single physical contrast between mixture components. Before choosing a method, identify whether the mixture is **solid–solid, solid–liquid, liquid–liquid, or gas–gas**, and which property (size, solubility, volatility, density, magnetism, polarity) differs.

#### Solid–Liquid Mixtures
- **Filtration** uses **porous filter paper** in a funnel. The **residue** stays on the paper; the **filtrate** passes through. Used to separate sand from water, or CaCO₃ precipitate from its reaction mixture.
- **Decantation** gently pours the **supernatant liquid** off a settled solid — fast but less precise than filtration.
- **Centrifugation** spins samples at high speed; denser components move outward (form a **pellet**) while lighter components remain on top (the **supernatant**). Used in clinical labs to separate **blood plasma** from red cells.
- **Evaporation** boils the solvent to dryness, leaving the dissolved solute. **Crystallisation** is preferred when purity matters: the solution is evaporated to a **saturated point**, then cooled so pure crystals form while impurities remain in the **mother liquor**.

#### Liquid–Liquid Mixtures
- **Separating funnel** handles **immiscible** liquids; the **denser** layer drains out first through the stopcock.
- **Simple distillation** purifies a **solvent from a non-volatile solute** (e.g. obtaining pure water from seawater). Vapour rises, contacts a **Liebig condenser** with cold water flowing counter-current, and condenses.
- **Fractional distillation** adds a **fractionating column** packed with glass beads. Vapours condense and re-evaporate repeatedly, so liquids with boiling points differing by less than 25 °C can be separated. Industrial **crude oil refining** depends on this.

#### Solid–Solid and Specialised Cases
- **Sublimation** cleans **NH₄Cl, I₂, naphthalene, camphor** from non-sublimable residues; vapour is re-solidified on a cool surface.
- **Magnetic separation** lifts **iron filings** from mixtures like sand and iron.
- **Chromatography** (paper, thin-layer, column) separates pigments and dyes. The **R_f value** is constant for a given solute–solvent pair and is used for **identification**.

#### Quick-Reference Table

| Technique | Mixture type | Property exploited |
|---|---|---|
| Filtration | Insoluble solid + liquid | Particle size |
| Separating funnel | Immiscible liquids | Density |
| Simple distillation | Solvent + dissolved solute | Boiling point |
| Fractional distillation | Miscible liquids | Close boiling points |
| Sublimation | Sublimable + non-sublimable solids | Sublimation ability |
| Chromatography | Dissolved solutes | Solubility / adsorption |

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Purity Calculations After Separation
Two recurring quantitative ideas:

- **% yield = (mass of pure product ÷ theoretical mass) × 100** — used after crystallisation or distillation to measure how completely a reaction or extraction proceeded.
- **% purity = (mass of pure substance ÷ mass of sample) × 100** — used when an extracted solid still contains inert impurities.

Worked example: A student crystallises 14.0 g of copper(II) sulfate pentahydrate from 16.0 g of impure ore. **% yield = (14.0 ÷ theoretical) × 100**; if the ore was 80 % pure the theoretical yield is 0.80 × 16.0 = 12.8 g, but a calculated ratio requires the actual stoichiometric maximum. Always state the **theoretical** figure explicitly before dividing.

#### Chromatography in Depth
The **mobile phase** (solvent) carries solutes up **paper** or across a **TLC plate** (stationary phase). Polar solutes bind strongly to the polar stationary phase and travel a shorter distance; non-polar solutes dissolve in the non-polar mobile phase and move further. R_f values are quoted to **two decimal places** and compared against standards for **identification** — never assume identity from colour alone.

#### Edge Cases and Traps
- **Filtration vs evaporation**: filtration keeps the *liquid*; evaporation keeps the *solid*. Examiners regularly swap the ask.
- **Simple vs fractional distillation**: choose **fractional** when liquids are **miscible** AND their boiling points are within ~25 °C. Water–ethanol needs fractional; salt–water needs simple.
- **Decantation vs filtration**: decantation is faster but acceptable only when the solid has **fully settled** and a small amount of solid carry-over is tolerable.
- **Anhydrous drying agents** (anhydrous CaCl₂, Na₂SO₄, silica gel) remove **traces of water** from organic liquids after extraction. They are *not* used to separate water from oil in bulk — that is the **separating funnel**'s job.

#### Connections to Other Topics
Distillation links to the **kinetic theory of matter** (vaporisation and condensation), to **intermolecular forces** (hydrogen bonding explains water's high boiling point), and to **industrial chemistry** (fractional distillation of air into O₂, N₂, noble gases; refining of crude oil). Chromatography underpins **forensic analysis** and **food chemistry**.

#### Common Mistakes
1. Reporting **R_f values greater than 1.0** — impossible, because the solute cannot move further than the solvent front.
2. Drying agents being called "**dehydrating agents**" — they dry liquids but do not remove *elements* of water from compounds.
3. Confusing **sublimation** with **evaporation** — sublimation is solid → gas (no liquid phase); evaporation is liquid → gas.
4. Forgetting to grease the **stopcock** of a separating funnel in descriptive answers.

2. A mixture contains **NH₄Cl, sand, and iodine crystals**. Outline a stepwise procedure that uses **sublimation, dissolution, and filtration** to recover each component separately.
