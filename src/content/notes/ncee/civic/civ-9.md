# Traffic Rules and Road Safety

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Traffic rules are the legal regulations, signs, and signals that control how vehicles and pedestrians move on public roads, designed to prevent accidents and save lives. The **Federal Road Safety Corps (FRSC)** is the Nigerian paramilitary agency that enforces these rules, educates the public, rescues accident victims, and maintains traffic order. Road signs fall into three categories: **regulatory** (Stop, No Entry, Speed Limit), **warning** (sharp bend, school ahead), and **informational** (hospital, parking).

- **Red light = stop**, **Yellow/Amber = slow down and prepare to stop**, **Green = go if the way is clear**.
- **Right of way** belongs to pedestrians on zebra crossings, emergency vehicles (ambulance, fire service, police), and traffic already on the main road.
- Common accident causes: **overspeeding, drunk driving, phone use while driving, ignoring signs, bad roads, and fatigue**.
- Civic duty: wear **seat belts**, motorcyclists and cyclists must wear **helmets**, and never drive under the influence.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Road Signs and Their Meanings

Road signs are visual instructions placed along roadsides to guide, warn, or regulate road users. Each sign carries a unique **shape and colour** that communicates its meaning even to readers who cannot read the text. In NCEE Civic Education, students are tested on sign identification by colour, shape, and category.

| Sign Category | Shape / Colour | Example | Function |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Regulatory | Red octagon, red circle | Stop, No Entry | Compels or prohibits an action |
| Warning | Red triangle, yellow diamond | Sharp bend ahead, School ahead | Alerts drivers to danger |
| Informational | Blue square, green rectangle | Hospital, Parking | Gives directions or facilities |

#### Traffic Light Signals

Traffic lights use three universal colours to control movement at junctions. The amber/yellow light is the most misunderstood: it warns the driver to **slow down and prepare to stop**, not to accelerate.

| Light Colour | Meaning | Driver Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Red | Stop completely | Wait behind the stop line |
| Yellow/Amber | Caution, transition | Reduce speed, stop if safe |
| Green | Proceed | Move only when the way is clear |

#### Right of Way and Civic Responsibility

Right of way decides who moves first at junctions or crossings. Pedestrians on zebra crossings, emergency vehicles with sirens and flashing lights, and vehicles already on the main road hold priority. Every road user has duties: drivers obey speed limits, passengers buckle seat belts, cyclists and motorcyclists wear helmets, and pedestrians use designated crossings.

- Causes of road accidents include overspeeding, drunk driving, mobile phone use, fatigue, poor vehicle maintenance, bad weather, and ignoring traffic signals.
- Pedestrian safety rules: use zebra crossings, look left-right-left before stepping out, walk facing oncoming traffic when there is no sidewalk.
- The FRSC educates drivers, removes accident victims to hospitals, prosecutes traffic offenders, and clears obstructions.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Common Examiner Traps and Misconceptions

NCEE examiners deliberately exploit weak understanding of road signs and signals. The yellow light is the most frequently misanswered item; students who treat it as "go faster" lose easy marks. Another trap is assuming that only car drivers must obey road laws, when in fact cyclists, motorcyclists, and pedestrians are equally bound by FRSC regulations.

| Trap | Wrong Answer | Correct Answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Yellow light meaning | Go faster | Slow down, prepare to stop |
| Right of way at zebra | Cars have priority | Pedestrians have priority |
| FRSC identity | A military unit | A paramilitary road safety agency |
| Speed limits | Same everywhere | Differ by zone (built-up, highway, school) |

#### Functions of the FRSC and Speed Zones

The Federal Road Safety Corps was established to reduce road crashes across Nigeria. Its officers patrol highways, arrest traffic offenders, remove wreckage, and run public awareness campaigns in schools and motor parks. Speed limits vary by location: built-up urban areas typically enforce lower limits, while highways permit higher speeds, and school zones carry the strictest caps to protect children.

#### Worked Practice Prompts

1. A driver approaches a junction and the light turns yellow. State two correct actions the driver should take and explain why accelerating is wrong.
2. List three causes of road accidents, then describe two civic duties of a pedestrian that would reduce crashes in your community.
