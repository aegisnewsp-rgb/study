# Geometry and Trigonometry — IB Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches (AA) Notes

Geometry and Trigonometry is the third topic of the IB Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches syllabus. At SL the topic covers coordinate geometry in 2D, trigonometry of right and non-right triangles, vectors in 2D, and the sine and cosine rules. At HL it extends to vectors in 3D, vector equations of lines and planes, the cross product, and the use of vectors in geometric problems. Together with Calculus, Geometry and Trigonometry is the heaviest section at HL by teaching hours.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### The seven facts every IB student needs

1. **Coordinate geometry** in 2D: the equation of a line, distance and midpoint formulae, the equation of a circle.
2. **Trigonometric ratios**: sin θ = opposite / hypotenuse, cos θ = adjacent / hypotenuse, tan θ = opposite / adjacent.
3. **Sine rule**: a / sin A = b / sin B = c / sin C.
4. **Cosine rule**: c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C.
5. **Area of a triangle**: (1/2) ab sin C.
6. **Radian measure**: π radians = 180°. The arc length s = rθ; the sector area = (1/2) r²θ.
7. **Vectors in 2D**: position vectors, vector arithmetic, scalar product, geometric interpretation.

#### Key notations

- (x, y) — a point in the plane
- |PQ| — the distance from P to Q
- sin⁻¹ x — the inverse sine function (arcsin)
- cos⁻¹ x — the inverse cosine function (arccos)
- tan⁻¹ x — the inverse tangent function (arctan)
- a · b — the dot (scalar) product of vectors a and b

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Coordinate geometry in 2D

**Distance formula**: distance between (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂) is √[(x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²].

**Midpoint formula**: midpoint of (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂) is ((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2).

**Equation of a straight line** y = mx + c, where m is the gradient and c is the y-intercept. The point-slope form is y − y₁ = m(x − x₁). The general form is ax + by + d = 0.

**Gradient** between two points: m = (y₂ − y₁) / (x₂ − x₁).

**Parallel lines**: m₁ = m₂.

**Perpendicular lines**: m₁ × m₂ = −1.

**Equation of a circle**: (x − a)² + (y − b)² = r², where (a, b) is the centre and r is the radius. The general form is x² + y² + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0, with centre (−g, −f) and radius √(g² + f² − c).

#### Trigonometry of right triangles

For a right triangle with hypotenuse h, the side opposite angle θ is h sin θ, the side adjacent to angle θ is h cos θ, and the tangent of θ is sin θ / cos θ = opposite / adjacent.

#### Trigonometry of non-right triangles (sine and cosine rules)

**Sine rule**: a / sin A = b / sin B = c / sin C, where a is the side opposite angle A, etc. Use the sine rule when you know (a) two angles and one side, or (b) two sides and an angle opposite one of them.

**Cosine rule**: c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C. Use the cosine rule when you know (a) three sides, or (b) two sides and the included angle.

**Area of a triangle**: (1/2) ab sin C, where a and b are two sides and C is the included angle.

#### Radian measure and arc / sector

π radians = 180°. Convert radians to degrees by multiplying by 180/π; convert degrees to radians by multiplying by π/180.

**Arc length** s = rθ, where r is the radius and θ is the angle in radians.

**Sector area** A = (1/2) r²θ.

#### Vectors in 2D

A vector a = (a₁, a₂) has magnitude |a| = √(a₁² + a₂²).

Vector operations:

- a + b = (a₁ + b₁, a₂ + b₂)
- a − b = (a₁ − b₁, a₂ − b₂)
- λa = (λa₁, λa₂)

**Position vector**: the vector from the origin O to a point P, written OP or r_P.

**Vector between two points**: PQ = OQ − OP.

**Dot (scalar) product**: a · b = a₁b₁ + a₂b₂ = |a||b| cos θ, where θ is the angle between a and b. Perpendicular vectors satisfy a · b = 0.

#### Vectors in 3D (HL)

A vector a = (a₁, a₂, a₃) has magnitude |a| = √(a₁² + a₂² + a₃²).

**Vector equation of a line**: r = a + t d, where a is a point on the line and d is the direction vector.

**Vector equation of a plane**: r · n = d, where n is the normal vector to the plane and d is a constant.

**Angle between a line and a plane**: sin θ = |d · n| / (|d| |n|).

**Cross product**: a × b = (a₂b₃ − a₃b₂, a₃b₁ − a₁b₃, a₁b₂ − a₂b₁). The magnitude is |a × b| = |a||b| sin θ.

**Volume of a parallelepiped** spanned by a, b, c: V = |a · (b × c)|.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where Geometry and Trigonometry appears across the IB Mathematics: AA papers

- **IB Math AA SL Paper 1**: short to medium structured items on coordinate geometry, the sine and cosine rules, and 2D vectors. The 3D content (HL) is not assessed at SL.
- **IB Math AA SL Paper 2**: geometry items appear alongside other topics, often as part of a multi-step problem.
- **IB Math AA HL Paper 1**: 3D vectors and HL trigonometry items appear.
- **IB Math AA HL Paper 3**: geometry and trigonometry items appear alongside other HL topics in modelling and investigation problems.
- **Across the papers**: drawing a clear diagram with all relevant lengths and angles labelled is the highest-leverage exam technique for trigonometry items.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Sine and cosine rules can be used interchangeably." Use the sine rule when you have two angles and one side, or two sides and an angle opposite one of them. Use the cosine rule when you have three sides or two sides and the included angle.
- "The angle of a triangle is measured in degrees." In IB Math AA, the angle in trigonometric contexts is usually in radians.
- "Vectors in 3D and 2D behave the same way." 3D vectors add a third component; many 2D vector results extend naturally to 3D, but some 2D intuitions (e.g. about skew lines) break down.
- "The cross product is commutative." a × b = −(b × a); the cross product is anti-commutative.
- "The normal to a plane is unique." Any scalar multiple of a normal vector is also a normal. The plane has infinitely many normals.

#### Exam technique

- For coordinate-geometry questions, write the formula before substituting. The formula line is the method mark.
- For sine / cosine rule questions, identify which rule applies (given the information) before computing. The rule choice is the method mark.
- For vector questions, write out the components of every vector. The components are the method mark.
- For dot-product questions, state the formula (a · b = |a||b| cos θ) before substituting. The formula is the method mark.
