# Living World and Cell Biology

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Revision (1h–1d)

**Nine characteristics of living organisms.** The mnemonic to carry is **M R G E N** — Movement, Respiration, Growth, Excretion, Nutrition, Responsiveness — with cellular organisation, reproduction and adaptation completing the list.

**Cell theory, three postulates:** all living organisms are composed of cells; the cell is the basic unit of life; all cells arise from pre-existing cells.

**The five names that carry the most marks:**

| Name | Contribution |
|---|---|
| Robert Hooke (1665) | First to observe cells — cork slices; coined "cells" (Latin for small rooms) |
| Leeuwenhoek (1676) | First to observe *living* cells — bacteria, protozoa, "animalcules" |
| Schleiden (1838) | All plants are made of cells |
| Schwann (1839) | All animals are made of cells |
| Virchow (1855) | *Omnis cellula e cellula* — all cells arise from pre-existing cells |

Hooke saw dead cork compartments; Leeuwenhoek saw living organisms. That distinction is the most commonly confused pair in this chapter.

**Prokaryote vs eukaryote:**

| Feature | Prokaryotic | Eukaryotic |
|---|---|---|
| Nuclear membrane | Absent — DNA as nucleoid in cytoplasm | Present |
| Membrane-bound organelles | Absent | Present |
| DNA shape | Circular | Linear |
| Ribosomes | 70S | 80S |
| Cell wall | Present (peptidoglycan) | Present in plants, absent in animals |
| Examples | Bacteria, blue-green algae | Plants, animals, fungi, protists |

**Organelle one-liners:** mitochondria = powerhouse, ATP from aerobic respiration, cristae, own DNA. Chloroplast = photosynthesis, chlorophyll, grana, thylakoids — plant cells only. Golgi = post office of the cell. Lysosome = suicide bag of hydrolases. Ribosome = protein synthesis. Plasma membrane = selectively permeable, Fluid Mosaic Model (Singer and Nicolson, 1972).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

The discovery sequence matters because each name answers a different question. Hooke supplied the *name* and the *compartment* — he saw the walls left behind in cork, which is dead tissue, so he was naming the containers rather than observing living units. Leeuwenhoek supplied the *living* observation, using single-lens instruments good enough to see bacteria and protozoa in pond water. Schleiden and Schwann together supplied the *generalisation* in 1838 and 1839 — one for plants, one for animals — which is what turned cell observation into cell theory. Virchow closed the loop in 1855 with *Omnis cellula e cellula*, which is the third postulate and the reason spontaneous generation had to be abandoned.

The prokaryote/eukaryote split is best understood as a packaging difference. A prokaryote keeps its DNA loose in the cytoplasm as a nucleoid and carries no membrane-bound organelles, so everything happens in one undivided space with 70S ribosomes. A eukaryote encloses linear DNA in a nucleus and partitions work into membrane-bound compartments using 80S ribosomes. That compartmentalisation is why a plant cell can run photosynthesis in chloroplasts while an animal cell cannot — not a different kind of cell, a different set of compartments.

Organelles by role: the plasma membrane is a phospholipid bilayer with proteins, selectively permeable, containing cholesterol in animal cells but not plant cells, and handling diffusion, osmosis, facilitated diffusion and active transport. The nucleus holds DNA on histone proteins forming nucleosomes, is bounded by a double membrane with pores, contains the nucleolus for ribosome assembly, and governs cell activity. Mitochondria have a smooth outer membrane and a cristae-folded inner membrane and are semi-autonomous with their own DNA. Chloroplasts stack thylakoids into grana and run both light and dark reactions. Rough ER carries ribosomes and makes protein; smooth ER has none and handles lipids and detoxification.

Organelles sorted by the job they do:

| Organelle | Primary role | Signature detail |
|---|---|---|
| Plasma membrane | Selective entry and exit | Phospholipid bilayer, Fluid Mosaic Model |
| Nucleus | Stores and governs DNA | Nuclear pores, nucleolus, nucleosomes |
| Mitochondria | Aerobic respiration, ATP | Cristae, own DNA — semi-autonomous |
| Chloroplast | Photosynthesis | Chlorophyll, thylakoids, grana — plants only |
| Rough ER | Protein synthesis and transport | Ribosomes on its surface |
| Smooth ER | Lipid synthesis, detoxification | No ribosomes |
| Golgi apparatus | Modifies, packages, ships | Stack of cisternae — post office |
| Ribosome | Protein synthesis | 70S prokaryote, 80S eukaryote |
| Lysosome | Intracellular digestion | Hydrolases — suicide bag |
| Centrosome | Spindle fibre formation | Basal bodies of cilia and flagella |

The plant-versus-animal comparison is the highest-yield table in the note: cell wall, chloroplast, large central vacuole and mostly-absent centrioles on one side; flexible shape, small numerous vacuoles, lysosomes, centrioles and heterotrophic nutrition on the other.


#### Characteristics of Living Organisms

All living organisms share certain key characteristics that distinguish them from non-living matter:

1. **Cellular organization**: All living things are made up of cells
2. **Nutrition**: Taking in food and using it for energy and growth
3. **Respiration**: Breaking down food to release energy (ATP)
4. **Excretion**: Removing waste products of metabolism
5. **Growth**: Increase in size and mass
6. **Reproduction**: Producing offspring of the same kind
7. **Responsiveness/Irritability**: Reacting to stimuli in the environment
8. **Movement**: Locomotion (external) or internal movement (blood, food)
9. **Adaptation**: Adjusting to environmental changes over time

> **MR. GREN** (Movement, Respiration, Growth, Excretion, Nutrition, Responsiveness) — the mnemonic for characteristics of life.

#### Cell: The Basic Unit of Life

#### Discovery

- **Robert Hooke (1665)**: First to observe cells — examined a thin slice of cork under a microscope and saw small compartments, calling them "cells" (Latin for small rooms)
- **Anton van Leeuwenhoek (1676)**: First to observe living cells (bacteria, protozoa) — called them "animalcules"
- **Matthias Schleiden (1838)**: German botanist — all plants are made of cells
- **Theodor Schwann (1839)**: German zoologist — all animals are made of cells
- **Rudolf Virchow (1855)**: "Omnis cellula e cellula" — all cells arise from pre-existing cells

#### Cell Theory

The cell theory has three main postulates:
1. All living organisms are composed of cells
2. The cell is the basic unit of life
3. All cells arise from pre-existing cells (not spontaneous generation)

#### Types of Cells

##### Prokaryotic Cells (Pro = before, karyon = nucleus)

- **No nuclear membrane** — genetic material (DNA) is scattered in the cytoplasm as a nucleoid
- **No membrane-bound organelles** (no mitochondria, Golgi bodies, etc.)
- Smaller, simpler cells
- Example: Bacteria, Blue-green algae (Cyanobacteria)

**Key features**:
- DNA is circular, present in the cytoplasm
- 70S ribosomes (smaller)
- Cell wall present (made of peptidoglycan)
- Reproduction is asexual (binary fission)

##### Eukaryotic Cells (Eu = true, karyon = nucleus)

- **Has a definite nucleus** with a nuclear membrane
- **Membrane-bound organelles** (mitochondria, chloroplast, Golgi apparatus, etc.)
- Larger, more complex
- Example: Plants, Animals, Fungi, Protists

**Key features**:
- DNA is linear, present inside nucleus
- 80S ribosomes
- More complex internal compartmentalization

#### Comparison: Plant Cell vs Animal Cell

| Feature | Plant Cell | Animal Cell |
|---|---|---|
| Cell Wall | Present (cellulose) | Absent |
| Chloroplast | Present (photosynthesis) | Absent |
| Vacuoles | Large central vacuole | Small, many |
| Centrioles | Mostly absent (except in lower plants) | Present |
| Shape | Rectangular, fixed shape | Irregular, flexible |
| Lysosomes | Rare | Common |
| Mitochondria | Present but fewer | Many |
| Nutrition | Autotrophic (mostly) | Heterotrophic |
| Size | Larger (10–100 µm) | Smaller (10–20 µm) |

#### Cell Organelles and Their Functions

**Plasma Membrane**:
- Made of phospholipid bilayer + proteins (Fluid Mosaic Model — Singer and Nicolson, 1972)
- Selectively permeable — controls what enters and exits
- Contains cholesterol (in animal cells — absent in plant cells)
- Transport: Diffusion, osmosis, active transport, facilitated diffusion

**Nucleus**:
- Contains genetic material (DNA) organized as chromosomes
- Surrounded by double nuclear membrane with pores
- Contains nucleolus (for ribosome assembly)
- Controls all cell activities (the "brain" of the cell)
- DNA carries genetic code — arranged in histone proteins forming nucleosomes

**Mitochondria (Powerhouse of the cell)**:
- Double membrane — outer smooth, inner folded (cristae)
- Site of aerobic respiration — produces ATP (adenosine triphosphate)
- Has its own DNA (semi-autonomous)
- Called the "powerhouse" because most ATP is produced here

**Chloroplast (in plant cells only)**:
- Double membrane organelle
- Contains chlorophyll (green pigment) — absorbs light energy for photosynthesis
- Has thylakoid membranes stacked into grana
- Semi-autonomous (own DNA and ribosomes)
- Site of photosynthesis (light reaction + dark reaction/Calvin cycle)

**Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER)**:
- Rough ER: Has ribosomes on surface — protein synthesis and transport
- Smooth ER: No ribosomes — lipid synthesis and detoxification

**Golgi Apparatus (Golgi body/Bodies)**:
- Stack of flattened membrane sacs (cisternae)
- Modifies, packages, and ships proteins and lipids
- "Post office of the cell"

**Ribosome**:
- Site of protein synthesis
- Made of rRNA and proteins
- 70S in prokaryotes, 80S in eukaryotes

**Lysosome**:
- Contains digestive enzymes (hydrolases)
- Digests worn-out organelles (autophagy), foreign particles
- Called the "suicide bag" — if it bursts, it digests the cell itself

**Centrosome/Centrioles**:
- Present in animal cells
- Involved in cell division (formation of spindle fibers)
- Forms the basal bodies of cilia and flagella

### 🔴 Deep — Mastery (3mo+)

The traps in this chapter are traps of attribution, not of recall. Pairings to distrust:

| Distractor | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| Hooke discovered the cell | He saw dead cork walls and named them; Leeuwenhoek saw living cells |
| Schleiden and Schwann proposed cell theory jointly | They worked separately — plants in 1838, animals in 1839 |
| Virchow discovered cells | He stated the third postulate in 1855 |
| Mitochondria contain chlorophyll | Chlorophyll and grana belong to the chloroplast |
| Lysosomes synthesise protein | Ribosomes do; lysosomes digest |
| Animal cells have a cell wall | Only plants and fungi among the eukaryotes here |

> **Examiner Trap:** "first to observe the cell" and "first to observe a living cell" are separate items with separate names. A candidate who remembers that someone looked down a microscope rarely remembers *what* they were looking at.

Three structural claims follow from the packaging argument and are worth being able to derive rather than recite. If organelles need a membrane to function, a prokaryote cannot have chloroplasts or mitochondria — so *all* energy production in a prokaryote occurs at the plasma membrane and in the cytoplasm. If ribosome sedimentation differs (70S against 80S), then protein synthesis differs systematically, which is why antibiotics targeting bacterial ribosomes work without harming the eukaryotic cell. If the cell wall is peptidoglycan in prokaryotes, then Gram staining is a prokaryote-targeting technique and does not separate plants from animals.

The Fluid Mosaic Model (Singer and Nicolson, 1972) is the one named model CTET asks directly. "Mosaic" refers to proteins embedded irregularly in the lipid bilayer rather than sitting in tidy layers, and "fluid" to the lateral mobility of both lipids and proteins. Selectively permeable is the functional consequence and the reason transport mechanisms must exist at all — diffusion and osmosis move material down a gradient, while active transport moves it against one at cost.

> **Examiner Trap:** cholesterol in the plasma membrane is an animal-cell fact, and options often offer it as a general membrane component. The bilayer itself, and the transport across it, are shared; the cholesterol is not.

For exam strategy the density is in the tables, not the prose. Learn the plant-versus-animal table and the prokaryote-versus-eukaryote table as two separate grids — they overlap on cell wall but disagree on almost everything else, and conflating them is the single largest source of lost marks in this topic.
