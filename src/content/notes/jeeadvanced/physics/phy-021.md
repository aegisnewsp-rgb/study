# Alternating Current (AC) Circuits & Resonance

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

> Ultra-concentrated formulas and high-yield invariants for rapid revision before JEE Advanced.

Alternating current varies sinusoidally with time:
$$i(t) = I_0 \sin(\omega t + \phi)$$

where $I_0$ is the peak current amplitude, $\omega = 2\pi f = 2\pi / T$ is the angular frequency in $\text{rad/s}$, and $\phi$ is the initial phase.

**Key Mathematical Invariants:**
1. **Mean (Average) Value over Half-Cycle:**
   $$I_{avg} = \frac{1}{T/2}\int_0^{T/2} I_0 \sin(\omega t)\,dt = \frac{2I_0}{\pi} \approx 0.637 I_0$$
   *(Over a full symmetric cycle, $\langle i \rangle = 0$. DC ammeters and voltmeters measure average current, registering $0$ in AC circuits).*

2. **Root Mean Square (RMS) or Virtual Value:**
   $$I_{rms} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{T}\int_0^T i^2(t)\,dt} = \frac{I_0}{\sqrt{2}} \approx 0.707 I_0$$
   $$V_{rms} = \frac{V_0}{\sqrt{2}} \approx 0.707 V_0$$
   *(Hot-wire ammeters and standard AC household meters read RMS values based on Joule heating dissipation: $\langle P \rangle = I_{rms}^2 R$).*

3. **Reactance and Impedance:**
   - Inductive Reactance: $X_L = \omega L$, with voltage leading current by $\pi/2$.
   - Capacitive Reactance: $X_C = \frac{1}{\omega C}$, with voltage lagging current by $\pi/2$.
   - Series Impedance: $Z = \sqrt{R^2 + (X_L - X_C)^2}$.
   - Phase Angle: $\tan \phi = \frac{X_L - X_C}{R} = \frac{\omega L - \frac{1}{\omega C}}{R}$.

4. **Series Resonance Essentials:**
   - Resonant frequency: $\omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}$, $f_0 = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{LC}}$.
   - Impedance minimum: $Z_{min} = R$ (purely resistive; current $I_{max} = V_{rms}/R$).
   - Quality factor ($Q$):
     $$Q = \frac{\omega_0 L}{R} = \frac{1}{\omega_0 C R} = \frac{1}{R}\sqrt{\frac{L}{C}} = \frac{\omega_0}{\Delta \omega}$$
   - Reactive voltage magnification at resonance: $V_L = V_C = Q \cdot V_{source}$.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

> Rigorous circuit analysis, phasor mechanics, power factor dynamics, and comparison frameworks.

In AC circuits driven by harmonic sources $v(t) = V_0 \sin(\omega t)$, current responses depend on the reactive nature of circuit components. Rather than solving trigonometric identities at every junction, JEE Advanced problems are most efficiently tackled through rotating vector **phasors** or complex impedance methods ($Z = R + j(\omega L - 1/\omega C)$).

#### Phasor Mechanics & Impedance Triangles
A phasor is a two-dimensional vector rotating counter-clockwise at angular speed $\omega$ in the complex plane. The instantaneous physical quantity corresponds to the projection onto the vertical axis.

- In a pure resistor $R$, current and voltage are collinear ($\phi = 0$).
- In an inductor $L$, induced back-EMF opposing current buildup dictates that voltage leads current by $\pi/2$ radians ($+90^\circ$).
- In a capacitor $C$, charge accumulation must precede electrostatic potential difference, meaning voltage lags current by $\pi/2$ radians ($-90^\circ$).

| **Pure Inductor ($L$)** | $\omega L$ | $+\pi/2$ ($+90^\circ$) | Voltage leads current by $90^\circ$ | $\frac{1}{2}V_0 I_0 \sin(2\omega t)$ | $0$ (Wattless current) |
| **Pure Capacitor ($C$)** | $\frac{1}{\omega C}$ | $-\pi/2$ ($-90^\circ$) | Voltage lags current by $90^\circ$ | $-\frac{1}{2}V_0 I_0 \sin(2\omega t)$ | $0$ (Wattless current) |
| **Series $R-L$** | $\sqrt{R^2 + \omega^2 L^2}$ | $\arctan\left(\frac{\omega L}{R}\right)$ | Voltage leads current by $\phi \in (0, \pi/2)$ | Fluctuating with DC offset | $V_{rms} I_{rms} \cos\phi$ |
| **Series $R-C$** | $\sqrt{R^2 + \frac{1}{\omega^2 C^2}}$ | $-\arctan\left(\frac{1}{\omega C R}\right)$ | Voltage lags current by $|\phi| \in (0, \pi/2)$ | Fluctuating with DC offset | $V_{rms} I_{rms} \cos\phi$ |

#### Power Decomposition in AC Circuits
For arbitrary impedance $Z \angle \phi$, when $v(t) = V_0 \sin(\omega t)$ and $i(t) = I_0 \sin(\omega t - \phi)$:
$$p(t) = v(t)i(t) = V_0 I_0 \sin(\omega t) \sin(\omega t - \phi) = V_{rms} I_{rms} \cos\phi [1 - \cos(2\omega t)] - V_{rms} I_{rms} \sin\phi \sin(2\omega t)$$

Integrating over a complete period $T = 2\pi/\omega$ yields three distinct power expressions:
1. **Real / Active Power ($P$):**
   $$P = V_{rms} I_{rms} \cos\phi \quad [\text{Watts, W}]$$
   This represents irreversible energy dissipated as heat in resistive components.
2. **Reactive Power ($Q_r$):**
   $$Q_r = V_{rms} I_{rms} \sin\phi \quad [\text{Volt-Amperes Reactive, VAR}]$$
   This represents oscillating energy shuttled back and forth between source fields and magnetic/electrostatic storage without net thermodynamic loss.
3. **Apparent Power ($S$):**
   $$S = V_{rms} I_{rms} = \sqrt{P^2 + Q_r^2} \quad [\text{Volt-Amperes, VA}]$$
4. **Power Factor ($\cos\phi$):**
   $$\text{PF} = \cos\phi = \frac{R}{Z} = \frac{P}{S}$$
   Industrial transmission grids penalize inductive lagging power factors because low $\cos\phi$ requires elevated line currents ($I_{rms} = P / (V_{rms} \cos\phi)$), increasing $I^2 R_{line}$ ohmic losses. Power factor correction is achieved by shunting parallel capacitor banks.

> Advanced differential formulations, resonance selectivity, quality factor derivations, parallel anti-resonance, and coupled circuits.

#### Differential Equation of Driven Series LCR
Applying Kirchhoff's Voltage Law around a closed loop driven by $v(t) = V_0 \sin(\omega t)$:
$$L \frac{di}{dt} + R i + \frac{q}{C} = V_0 \sin(\omega t)$$

Differentiating with respect to $t$ (where $i = dq/dt$):
$$\frac{d^2 i}{dt^2} + \frac{R}{L}\frac{di}{dt} + \frac{1}{LC} i = \frac{V_0 \omega}{L} \cos(\omega t)$$

This is mathematically isomorphic to a damped mechanical oscillator driven by an external harmonic force ($m \ddot{x} + b \dot{x} + kx = F_0 \cos(\omega t)$).
- Mass $m \longleftrightarrow$ Inductance $L$
- Damping coefficient $b \longleftrightarrow$ Resistance $R$
- Spring constant $k \longleftrightarrow$ Inverse capacitance $1/C$
- Position $x \longleftrightarrow$ Charge $q$
- Velocity $\dot{x} \longleftrightarrow$ Current $i$

The complete solution contains a transient complementary function $i_c(t)$ decaying exponentially with time constant $\tau = 2L/R$, and a steady-state particular integral $i_p(t) = I_0 \sin(\omega t - \phi)$, which dominates in all continuous AC regimes.

#### Resonance Sharpness & Bandwidth
The power delivered to the circuit as a function of driving frequency $\omega$ is:
$$P(\omega) = I_{rms}^2 R = \frac{V_{rms}^2 R}{R^2 + \left(\omega L - \frac{1}{\omega C}\right)^2}$$

At natural resonance $\omega_0 = 1/\sqrt{LC}$, $P_{max} = V_{rms}^2 / R$.
The **half-power frequencies** $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$ occur where $P(\omega) = \frac{1}{2} P_{max}$, which requires:
$$\left(\omega L - \frac{1}{\omega C}\right)^2 = R^2 \implies \omega L - \frac{1}{\omega C} = \pm R$$

Solving the quadratic equations for positive frequencies gives:
$$\omega_1 = -\frac{R}{2L} + \sqrt{\frac{R^2}{4L^2} + \frac{1}{LC}}, \quad \omega_2 = +\frac{R}{2L} + \sqrt{\frac{R^2}{4L^2} + \frac{1}{LC}}$$

Subtracting gives the **bandwidth ($\Delta \omega$)**:
$$\Delta \omega = \omega_2 - \omega_1 = \frac{R}{L}$$
$$\text{Resonance Quality Factor: } Q = \frac{\omega_0}{\Delta \omega} = \frac{\omega_0 L}{R} = \frac{1}{\omega_0 C R} = \frac{1}{R}\sqrt{\frac{L}{C}}$$

Higher $Q$ produces a narrower, sharper response curve, providing superior frequency discrimination in radio receiver tuning stages.

### Table 2: Series vs. Parallel Resonant Circuits

| Parameter | Series Resonant LCR Circuit | Parallel Resonant Circuit ($R-L$ branch $\parallel C$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Circuit Topology** | $R$, $L$, and $C$ connected end-to-end | Inductor with resistance ($R, L$) in parallel with $C$ |
| **Resonant Frequency ($\omega_r$)** | $\omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}$ | $\omega_r = \sqrt{\frac{1}{LC} - \frac{R^2}{L^2}} = \omega_0 \sqrt{1 - \frac{1}{Q^2}}$ |
| **Impedance at Resonance** | Minimum: $Z_{res} = R$ | Maximum (Dynamic Resistance): $Z_{dyn} = \frac{L}{CR}$ |
| **Current at Resonance** | Maximum: $I_{max} = \frac{V}{R}$ | Minimum: $I_{min} = \frac{V}{Z_{dyn}} = \frac{V C R}{L}$ |
| **Phase Relation at Resonance** | $\phi = 0$; Current in phase with source voltage | $\phi = 0$; Source current in phase with voltage |
| **Functional Role / Name** | Acceptor Circuit (passes selected frequency) | Rejector / Tank Circuit (blocks selected frequency) |
| **Branch Currents / Voltages** | Voltage magnification: $V_L = V_C = Q V$ | Current magnification: $I_C \approx I_L \approx Q I_{source}$ |

## Worked JEE Advanced Exam Problems

### Problem 1: Series LCR Quality Factor & Voltage Magnification

**Statement:** A series LCR circuit consists of an inductor $L = 20\,\text{mH}$, a capacitor $C = 0.5\,\mu\text{F}$, and a resistor $R = 10\,\Omega$. It is driven by an alternating voltage source $v(t) = 50\sqrt{2}\sin(\omega t)\,\text{V}$.
1. Find the natural resonant angular frequency $\omega_0$ and the Quality Factor $Q$.
2. Calculate the RMS voltages across the resistor, inductor, and capacitor at resonance.
3. Determine the frequency difference between the upper and lower half-power frequencies.

**Step-by-Step Solution:**

1. **Calculate Resonant Angular Frequency ($\omega_0$):**
   $$\omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{20 \times 10^{-3} \times 0.5 \times 10^{-6}}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{10^{-8}}} = 10^4\,\text{rad/s}$$
   The corresponding cyclical frequency is $f_0 = \frac{\omega_0}{2\pi} = \frac{10000}{2\pi} \approx 1591.5\,\text{Hz}$.

2. **Calculate Quality Factor ($Q$):**
   $$Q = \frac{\omega_0 L}{R} = \frac{10^4 \times 20 \times 10^{-3}}{10} = \frac{200}{10} = 20$$
   *(Alternatively: $Q = \frac{1}{R}\sqrt{\frac{L}{C}} = \frac{1}{10}\sqrt{\frac{20 \times 10^{-3}}{0.5 \times 10^{-6}}} = \frac{1}{10}\sqrt{40000} = \frac{200}{10} = 20$).*

3. **Voltages at Resonance:**
   - At $\omega = \omega_0$, total impedance is purely resistive: $Z = R = 10\,\Omega$.
   - The source RMS voltage is $V_{rms} = \frac{V_0}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{50\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2}} = 50\,\text{V}$.
   - Circuit current:
     $$I_{rms} = \frac{V_{rms}}{R} = \frac{50}{10} = 5\,\text{A}$$
   - Resistor voltage:
     $$V_R = I_{rms} R = 5 \times 10 = 50\,\text{V}$$
   - Inductor voltage:
     $$V_L = I_{rms} X_L = I_{rms} (\omega_0 L) = 5 \times (10^4 \times 0.02) = 5 \times 200 = 1000\,\text{V} = Q \cdot V_{rms}$$
   - Capacitor voltage:
     $$V_C = I_{rms} X_C = 5 \times \frac{1}{10^4 \times 0.5 \times 10^{-6}} = 5 \times 200 = 1000\,\text{V} = Q \cdot V_{rms}$$
   *Notice the striking result:* While the source delivers only $50\,\text{V}$, reactive voltages across $L$ and $C$ each reach $1000\,\text{V}$! Because $\vec{V}_L$ and $\vec{V}_C$ are exactly $180^\circ$ out of phase, their phasor sum is identically zero ($\vec{V}_L + \vec{V}_C = 0$).

4. **Bandwidth Calculation:**
   $$\Delta \omega = \frac{R}{L} = \frac{10}{20 \times 10^{-3}} = 500\,\text{rad/s}$$
   $$\Delta f = \frac{\Delta \omega}{2\pi} = \frac{500}{2\pi} \approx 79.58\,\text{Hz}$$

### Problem 2: Parallel AC Branching with Complex Phasors

**Statement:** A coil with resistance $R_1 = 30\,\Omega$ and inductive reactance $X_{L1} = 40\,\Omega$ is connected in parallel with a capacitive branch having negligible resistance and capacitive reactance $X_C = 50\,\Omega$. The combination is connected across an AC mains supply $v(t) = 200\sin(100\pi t)\,\text{V}$.
Determine:
1. The admittance of each parallel branch.
2. The total current amplitude drawn from the mains and the overall circuit power factor.
3. The value of an auxiliary capacitor needed in parallel to bring the net power factor to unity.

**Step-by-Step Solution:**

1. **Branch Impedances and Admittances:**
   - Branch 1 (Inductive branch):
     $$Z_1 = R_1 + j X_{L1} = 30 + j40\,\Omega, \quad |Z_1| = \sqrt{30^2 + 40^2} = 50\,\Omega$$
     Admittance $Y_1 = \frac{1}{Z_1} = \frac{30 - j40}{30^2 + 40^2} = \frac{30 - j40}{2500} = 0.012 - j0.016\,\Omega^{-1}$ (Siemens)
   - Branch 2 (Capacitive branch):
     $$Z_2 = -j X_C = -j50\,\Omega$$
     Admittance $Y_2 = \frac{1}{-j50} = +j\frac{1}{50} = +j0.020\,\Omega^{-1}$

2. **Total Admittance ($Y_{total}$):**
   $$Y_{total} = Y_1 + Y_2 = 0.012 - j0.016 + j0.020 = 0.012 + j0.004\,\Omega^{-1}$$
   - Conductance $G = 0.012\,\text{S}$
   - Susceptance $B = +0.004\,\text{S}$ (Net susceptance is positive, meaning the overall circuit is capacitive!)
   - Magnitude of total admittance:
     $$|Y_{total}| = \sqrt{(0.012)^2 + (0.004)^2} = \sqrt{1.44 \times 10^{-4} + 0.16 \times 10^{-4}} = \sqrt{1.60 \times 10^{-4}} = 0.01265\,\text{S}$$

3. **Current and Power Factor:**
   - Supply RMS voltage: $V_{rms} = \frac{200}{\sqrt{2}} \approx 141.42\,\text{V}$.
   - Supply peak voltage: $V_0 = 200\,\text{V}$.
   - Peak total current drawn:
     $$I_0 = V_0 |Y_{total}| = 200 \times 0.01265 \approx 2.53\,\text{A}$$
   - Circuit phase angle $\phi$:
     $$\tan \phi = \frac{B}{G} = \frac{0.004}{0.012} = \frac{1}{3} \implies \phi = \arctan(1/3) \approx 18.43^\circ$$
   - Power Factor:
     $$\cos \phi = \frac{G}{|Y_{total}|} = \frac{0.012}{0.01265} \approx 0.9487 \quad (\text{leading})$$

4. **Condition for Unity Power Factor:**
   - Net susceptance must vanish ($B_{net} = 0$).
   - Since $Y_1$ has inductive susceptance $-0.016\,\text{S}$, the capacitive branch must provide susceptance $+0.016\,\text{S}$.
   - Currently, Branch 2 provides $+0.020\,\text{S}$, which overcompensates.
   - To achieve unity power factor, total capacitive susceptance must be reduced to $0.016\,\text{S}$:
     $$B_C = \omega C_{opt} = 0.016 \implies X_{C,opt} = \frac{1}{0.016} = 62.5\,\Omega$$
     At $\omega = 100\pi\,\text{rad/s}$:
     $$C_{opt} = \frac{0.016}{100\pi} = \frac{0.00016}{\pi} \approx 50.93\,\mu\text{F}$$

## Critical JEE Advanced Traps & Heuristic Checkpoints

- **Trap 1: Simple Algebraic Sum of Voltages**: Never write $V_{source} = V_R + V_L + V_C$. Voltages are vectors! The scalar relationship is always $V_{source} = \sqrt{V_R^2 + (V_L - V_C)^2}$.
- **Trap 2: Resonance Definition in Parallel Circuits**: Unlike series LCR where resonance always occurs at $\omega = 1/\sqrt{LC}$ regardless of resistance, parallel resonance frequency shifts with coil resistance $R$: $\omega_r = \sqrt{\frac{1}{LC} - \frac{R^2}{L^2}}$. If $R^2 > L/C$, parallel resonance cannot occur at all!
- **Trap 3: Peak vs. RMS Energy Calculations**: Magnetic energy stored in an inductor reaches peak value $E_{max} = \frac{1}{2}L I_0^2 = L I_{rms}^2$. The time-averaged magnetic energy is $\langle E_L \rangle = \frac{1}{4} L I_0^2 = \frac{1}{2} L I_{rms}^2$.
- **Trap 4: Wattless Current Decomposition**: Current can be split into two orthogonal phasors: an active component $I_{active} = I_{rms}\cos\phi$ in phase with voltage, and a wattless reactive component $I_{wattless} = I_{rms}\sin\phi$ at $90^\circ$ to voltage. Average power dissipated by the wattless component is identically zero.
