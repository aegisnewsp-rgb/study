# Spelling and Vocabulary

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Spelling and Vocabulary in NAT-I Verbal Reasoning tests two micro-skills in roughly 10 MCQs (≈3% of the 90-mark NTS paper): **orthographic accuracy** (picking the correctly spelled option) and **lexical precision** (synonyms, antonyms, one-word substitutions, idioms). The fastest recall lever is memorising the **silent-e rule**, the **i-before-e rule**, the **CVC doubling rule**, and the **British vs American** conventions (e.g., *colour/color*, *theatre/theater*). High-frequency NTS traps: **accommodate** (two c's, two m's), **occurrence** (two c's, two r's), **mischievous** (no 'i' before the 'ous'), **liaison** (single 'i' after 'l'), and homophones **affect/effect**, **principal/principle**, **stationary/stationery**. For vocabulary, learn Greek/Latin roots (*bene-*, *mal-*, *chrono-*, *bio-*) so you can decode unfamiliar words under time pressure.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Spelling Rules Tested

| Rule | Pattern | Example |
|------|---------|---------|
| i before e | except after c, or when sounded like "ay" | *receive* (c+ei), *believe* (ei = "ay") |
| Silent e dropped | before a vowel suffix | *make + ing = making* |
| Silent e kept | before a consonant suffix, or to preserve soft c/g | *manage + able = manageable*, *change + able = changeable* |
| CVC doubling | CVC + vowel suffix = double the final consonant | *run + er = runner* |
| y → i | consonant + y + suffix → change y to i | *happy + ness = happiness* |

NAT-I consistently uses words where one of these rules is violated in the distractor options. Practice by reading each option aloud and matching sound to standard spelling.

#### Synonyms and Antonyms
A **synonym** carries identical or near-identical meaning; an **antonym** is opposite. NTS questions typically pair a moderately rare word with a familiar one. Build recall with pairs such as *ephemeral–transitory*, *ubiquitous–omnipresent*, *sporadic–continuous*, *ameliorate–aggravate*, *laconic–concise*. Watch the **connotation trap**: *famous* and *notorious* are NOT synonyms — one is neutral-positive, the other negative.

#### One-Word Substitution
Replace a long definition with a single word: polyglot (many languages), acrophobia (fear of heights), bibliophile (lover of books), philanthropist (lover of humanity), somnambulist (sleepwalker). Memorising 40–50 such terms covers most NAT-I items.

#### Homophones and Confusables
Distinguish by meaning, not sound: **affect** (verb, to influence) vs **effect** (noun, result); **principal** (head, main) vs **principle** (rule); **stationary** (still) vs **stationery** (paper goods); **complement** (completes) vs **compliment** (praise); **desert** (arid land / to abandon) vs **dessert** (sweet course).

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Etymology as a Decoding Tool
Roughly 60% of NAT-I vocabulary items derive from Greek or Latin roots. Learning a small root set unlocks dozens of words: *bene-* (good: *benevolent, benefactor*), *mal-* (bad: *malicious, malevolent*), *chrono-* (time: *chronology, synchronize*), *bio-* (life: *biology, autobiography*), *auto-* (self: *autonomous, autocrat*), *mono-/bi-/tri-* (number: *monochrome, bilateral, trilateral*), *-phobia* (fear: *claustrophobia, xenophobia*), *-phile* (lover: *Anglophile, bibliophile*). If you can decompose the word, you can often eliminate three distractors without seeing the definition.

#### Contextual Usage
Some NAT-I items give a sentence with a blank and four words. The right choice fits **collocation, register, and connotation** — not just dictionary meaning. Example: "The minister offered a ____ apology." The correct answer is *contrite* (sorrowful, formal) not *creative* or *casual*. Always reread the full sentence; isolated synonym matching fails here.

#### Practice Prompts
1. **Spelling:** From the four options, identify the correctly spelled word: (a) *accomodate* (b) *accommodate* (c) *acommodate* (d) *accommmodate*. Apply the i-before-e rule and the CVC doubling rule to the syllable *mod*.  
   **Answer: (b) accommodate.**

2. **One-word substitution:** "A person who is afraid of heights." Apply the root *acro-* (high) + *-phobia* (fear).  
   **Answer: acrophobic / acrophobia.**

3. **Synonym trap:** Choose the true synonym of *laconic*: (a) verbose (b) concise (c) loud (d) cheerful. Eliminate (a) and (c) as antonyms; (d) is unrelated.  
   **Answer: (b) concise.**

#### Exam Strategy for the 3% Weight
Allocate ≤12 minutes for the entire Spelling & Vocabulary block. Read the question stem first, then scan options for the rule-based or root-based giveaway. If two options remain, substitute each into the stem and read aloud — collocation usually breaks the tie.
