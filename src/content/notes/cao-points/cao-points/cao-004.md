# Foundation Level Maths and Points

Foundation Level Mathematics is the only Irish school-leaving subject that is recorded on your certificate and contributes **nothing** to your CAO aggregate. That single fact drives almost every decision attached to this topic: whether you take it at all, whether you sit it alongside Ordinary Level Mathematics, and how a specific course's entry requirement treats it. Every other subject on the Common Points Scale converts a grade into points. Foundation Level Maths converts a grade into a tick.

## What the grade actually is

Foundation Level Mathematics is examined at one level only and is graded **F1, F2 or F3** — a pass, a merit, and a distinction. The grade appears on your Leaving Certificate results statement and in your CAO application. It does not appear on the Common Points Scale. There is no F1 = n points line in the grid, because the grid has no F row at all.

| What you sat | How it is graded | Points toward the CAO aggregate |
|---|---|---|
| Higher Level Mathematics | H1 to H8 | Full scale, H1 = 100 down to H8 = 0 |
| Ordinary Level Mathematics | O1 to O8 | Full scale, O1 = 56 down to O8 = 0 |
| Foundation Level Mathematics | F1 to F3 | **0** |

Two consequences follow. First, an F1 is worth exactly the same as an F3 in the points arithmetic — the grade still matters, but for entry requirements and for the certificate, not for the aggregate. Second, if you sit Foundation Level Mathematics *instead of* Ordinary Level, you have not saved marks by taking the easier paper; you have removed a subject from the six that count.

## Why courses still ask for it

The CAO operates two separate filters, and conflating them is the most common and most expensive mistake in this area.

**The matriculation minimum** is the general threshold every applicant must clear for any offer to be considered: a pass in English, a pass in Mathematics, and passes in two other subjects. Where a course offers Foundation Level Mathematics as the named mathematics subject, an F6-equivalent pass at Foundation Level satisfies the mathematics limb of that minimum. This is what makes Foundation Maths useful — it unlocks the arithmetic of an application rather than adding to it.

**Course-specific entry requirements** sit on top of the minimum, published by each institution in the CAO Handbook, and they are stated per course. Some courses list an ordinary-level or a specified grade in mathematics; some accept Foundation Level in place of Ordinary Level; some accept neither and require Higher Level. A pass at Foundation Level that unlocks the matriculation minimum does **not** automatically satisfy a course that has set its own mathematics requirement.

So the practical rule: the minimum gets you considered. The course requirement gets you the place. Check both, on the same course page, in the CAO Handbook.

## The one exception worth knowing

A small number of courses in the Handbook state that they accept Foundation Level Mathematics where they would otherwise require Ordinary Level. These are flagged explicitly in the course entry requirements, and the flag is the only thing that makes an F grade count. Do not generalise from one such course to the next — the arrangement is course-specific and changes between admission cycles. Read the current Handbook entry for the specific course code you are applying to.

## The stacking question

Foundation Level Mathematics and Ordinary Level Mathematics cannot both appear on the same application. The subject sits once. That is not a CAO rule so much as a school-level one — a candidate presents one result per subject — but it means the choice is genuinely either/or at the point of leaving school, not a later addition.

What *can* be stacked is a language. A third language studied at Foundation Level is a separate subject in the CAO system and is handled on its own terms under the language bonus and exemption arrangements. That is a different subject with a different rule set; do not transfer the mathematics logic across to it.

## Why a student takes it anyway

Foundation Maths is not a mistake in itself. It is the right choice when Ordinary Level Mathematics is genuinely out of reach in the time available, because a pass in mathematics is a condition of matriculation for a large share of Level 7 and Level 8 courses. A candidate with F1 in Foundation Maths and five strong subjects can be competitive; a candidate who fails to reach a pass in mathematics cannot be considered for many courses at all, regardless of what else they have.

The failure mode is not taking Foundation Maths. It is taking Foundation Maths and then assuming the aggregate still contains a mathematics entry. It does not.

> Confirm the current points grid, the bonus-points rule and the per-course entry requirements on cao.ie before committing to a plan. The Handbook is the authority on what a specific course will accept, and it is revised between admission cycles.

## Self-check before you close the page

1. You have an F1 in Foundation Level Mathematics, five O-distinction results and one H4. What is your CAO aggregate? *(Answer: the arithmetic runs on the five counted results plus the H4; the F1 contributes nothing. Identify which five subjects are actually your six-best selection before you compute anything.)*
2. A course page says "O6/H7 in Mathematics". Does an F1 satisfy it? *(No. F1 satisfies the general matriculation minimum only, where the course offers Foundation Maths as its named mathematics subject. The course-specific requirement is separate and is not automatically met.)*
3. A different course page says "F6 or O6/H7 accepted in Mathematics". What changes? *(The mathematics limb of the course requirement is now satisfied by the pass you already have — the application becomes viable rather than merely matriculation-eligible.)*
4. Can a candidate sit both Foundation Level and Ordinary Level Mathematics and have both counted? *(No — one subject, one result, one slot at most in the six-best count.)*
5. Does an F3 score more CAO points than an F1? *(No. Neither scores any. The grade is recorded and is checked against entry requirements; it does not move the aggregate.)*
