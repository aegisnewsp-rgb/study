# Income from Property & Business

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Under the Pakistan Income Tax Ordinance 2001 (ITO 2001), **Income from Property** (Section 15) and **Income from Business** (Section 18) are two of the five heads of chargeable income. Rental receipts from letting a building — including deemed rental on attached fixtures, plant or machinery at **15% of FMV per annum** under Rule 4 of the Income Tax Rules 2002 — fall under the property head. Profits from trading, manufacture, or any commercial activity fall under the business head, and only expenses that are **wholly and exclusively** incurred (Section 20) are deductible.

- **Property income** = Gross rent received (or deemed) − allowable deductions (tax paid, ground rent, mortgage interest, insurance, repairs).
- **Business income** = Accounting profit − non-deductible items + admissible depreciation and initial allowance under Sections 22–23.
- Cross-head loss set-off is restricted: business loss cannot be set off against salary under Section 16.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Classification by source, not by asset
The ITO 2001 taxes income under five heads, and the **source** of the receipt decides the head. A landlord charging rent on a flat declares Section 15 income; a property dealer buying and reselling plots declares Section 18 income on the trading profit. Mis-classification is the single most-tested error in the paper.

#### Computing chargeable property income
For a property let to a tenant, chargeable rental income begins with gross rent actually received, plus any **deemed rental**. Where the tenant also uses furniture, fittings, machinery or plant installed in the building, Rule 4 deems the rent for those items to be **15% of fair market value per year**, split between owner and tenant where appropriate. Allowable deductions under Section 15 are then claimed against this gross figure.

#### Computing chargeable business income
Business income starts from the accounting profit per the financial statements. The tax computation layer adds back book depreciation, provisions, and capital expenditure, then deducts **initial allowance** (Section 23 — generally 25% on plant and machinery, 15% on certain buildings) and **normal depreciation** (Section 22) at the rates in the Third Schedule. Non-deductible expenses listed in Section 21 (e.g., personal expenses, capital expenditure, income tax itself) are removed regardless of how they sit in the books.

| Stage | Property (Section 15) | Business (Section 18) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gross measure | Rent received + deemed rental (Rule 4) | Revenue receipts + stock withdrawn for personal use at FMV |
| Key deductions | Tax paid, ground rent, mortgage interest, insurance, repairs | Wholly & exclusively incurred expenses (Section 20); initial allowance + depreciation (Sections 22–23) |
| Common add-back | Capital improvements treated as repairs | Book depreciation, provisions, disallowed expenses |
| Loss set-off | Section 16 limits against other heads | Carry forward up to 6 years under Section 57 |

#### Withholding tax interaction
Where annual rent exceeds Rs. 200,000, the tenant must withhold **15%** of the rent under Section 155 for individuals and AOPs, and similar withholding applies to many business contract payments. The tax withheld is **adjustable** against the final assessed liability under Section 156 — not an extra cost.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge cases and the deemed-rental trap
Owners who let out a fully furnished apartment often miss that Rule 4 imposes a statutory minimum deemed rent on the attached fixtures and machinery — even where the lease states "inclusive of furniture". The FBR practice is to allocate value between building (Section 15 income) and fittings (Rule 4 deemed income) using the FMV at the start of the tenancy. Owner-occupied property generates **no** rental income and is not deemed-rent taxed.

#### Speculation business and stock-in-trade
Stock-in-trade withdrawn by the proprietor for personal use is deemed a disposal at **FMV** under Section 18(1)(b), creating business income for the entity. **Speculation business** (Section 19) covers dealing in securities and commodities on recognised markets; speculation loss can only be set off against speculation profit, with carry-forward limited to six years.

#### Cross-border and mixed-income nuances
Non-resident owners receiving Pakistan-source rent are taxed on the gross amount with limited deductions, while residents can claim Section 15 deductions in full. Where a person carries on both a property-letting activity and a trading activity, the two heads must be computed **separately** before being aggregated into total income.

#### Common mistakes examiners test
1. Treating capital improvements as deductible repairs.
2. Setting off business loss against salary (blocked under Section 16).
3. Forgetting initial allowance on plant & machinery installed in the rental property itself.
4. Double-counting withholding tax as an expense instead of adjusting it under Section 156.

#### Worked micro-example
A landlord earns rent of Rs. 3,600,000 in the year. Furniture and machinery of FMV Rs. 5,000,000 are installed; deemed rental = 15% × 5,000,000 = Rs. 750,000. Allowable deductions: tax paid Rs. 80,000, ground rent Rs. 60,000, mortgage interest Rs. 400,000, insurance Rs. 50,000, repairs Rs. 110,000 (no capital additions). Chargeable property income = 3,600,000 + 750,000 − 700,000 = **Rs. 3,650,000**.

#### Practice prompts
1. Classify each receipt — rent received, gain on sale of a business asset, FMV of stock withdrawn for personal use, lease rental of plant & machinery — into the correct head under Sections 15, 18, and 19.
2. Reconcile a business's accounting profit to chargeable business income by removing depreciation per accounts, adding back provisions, and claiming initial allowance plus Third Schedule depreciation on Rs. 10,000,000 of plant & machinery purchased in the year.
