# Indian Economy and GDP

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

GDP (Gross Domestic Product) is the total money value of all final goods and services produced within India's geographic boundary in a financial year (April–March). For IBPS Clerk, three identities show up every year: GDP at Market Price, GDP at Factor Cost, and GNP. The base year used by MoSPI is **2011-12**; older 2004-05 series questions are now outdated.

The single must-remember formula is the expenditure identity: GDP at MP = C + I + G + (X − M). C is private consumption, I is investment, G is government spending, X − M is net exports. Two conversions matter: GDP at FC = GDP at MP − Net Indirect Taxes, and GNP = GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad.

- **Base year:** 2011-12 (MoSPI/CSO series).
- **GDP → GNP link:** add NFIA; subtract depreciation to reach NNP.
- **Inflation pair:** WPI (wholesale) and CPI (retail, CPI-Combined is headline).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### National Income Aggregates

The Indian economy is tracked through a stack of overlapping measures. GDP measures production inside the territory; GNP also counts what Indian residents earn abroad. Moving down the stack you subtract depreciation (Consumption of Fixed Capital) to get NDP and NNP. Two price bases coexist: Market Price (what buyers pay, includes net indirect taxes) and Factor Cost (what producers receive, taxes stripped out, subsidies added back).

| Aggregate | Formula | What it captures |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GDP at MP | C + I + G + (X − M) | Output valued at purchaser prices |
| GDP at FC | GDP at MP − Net Indirect Taxes | Output valued at producer cost |
| GNP at MP | GDP at MP + NFIA | Output + net income from abroad |
| NNP at FC (National Income) | GNP at MP − Depreciation − NIT | True domestic factor earnings |
| Per Capita Income | NNP at FC ÷ Mid-year Population | Average income per person (₹) |

#### Three Approaches to GDP

MoSPI computes GDP using all three methods; theory says they yield the same number. The production (value-added) method sums GVA across sectors. The income method adds wages, rent, interest, and profits. The expenditure method is the C + I + G + (X − M) identity used for policy framing.

- **Primary sector:** agriculture, forestry, fishing — employment-heavy, share falling.
- **Secondary sector:** manufacturing, construction, utilities — capital-intensive.
- **Tertiary sector:** services, IT, finance — now the largest GDP contributor (~50%+).

#### Common Exam Traps

- Confusing GDP at MP with GDP at FC — always subtract Net Indirect Taxes.
- Using total population instead of mid-year population for Per Capita Income.
- Treating the Planning Commission as still active — replaced by NITI Aayog on 1 January 2015.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Fiscal vs Monetary Levers

The Union Budget drives fiscal policy: Revenue Receipts (tax + non-tax) minus Revenue Expenditure gives Revenue Deficit; Total Expenditure minus Total Receipts (excluding borrowings) gives Fiscal Deficit. RBI drives monetary policy through the Repo Rate (short-term MSF benchmark), Bank Rate (long-term penal rate, last revised May 2020), CRR (cash reserve ratio, currently 4%), and SLR (statutory liquidity ratio, currently 18% as of late 2025). Bank Rate and Repo Rate are not the same instrument.

#### Inflation and Deflators

WPI tracks wholesale/producer prices; CPI-Combined tracks retail prices paid by households and is the RBI's headline inflation target (4% with ±2% tolerance). The GDP Deflator = (Nominal GDP ÷ Real GDP) × 100 captures economy-wide price change and is broader than WPI or CPI because it covers all goods and services in GDP.

| Indicator | Measures | Who publishes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| WPI | Producer/wholesale price change | Office of the Economic Adviser, DPIIT |
| CPI-Combined | Retail consumer inflation | NSO, MoSPI |
| GDP Deflator | Implicit price across all GDP components | MoSPI/CSO |
| IIP | Volume index of industrial output | NSO, MoSPI |

#### Practice Prompts

1. If GDP at MP is ₹280 lakh crore, Indirect Taxes are ₹30 lakh crore, and Subsidies are ₹8 lakh crore, calculate GDP at FC. (Answer: ₹280 − (30 − 8) = ₹258 lakh crore.)
2. Why does Per Capita Income fall when mid-year population rises even if NNP at FC is constant? (Answer: it is a ratio; denominator growth reduces the quotient, holding welfare lower per person.)
