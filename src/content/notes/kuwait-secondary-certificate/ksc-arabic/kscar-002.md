# Grammar (Nahw) — Kuwait Secondary Certificate Arabic Notes

Arabic grammar (Nahw / النحو) is the second heaviest domain by marks on the Kuwait Secondary Certificate terminal Arabic exam, alongside Reading Comprehension and Literary Texts. The Kuwait MOE Arabic curriculum at terminal level covers sentence structure (jumla ismiyya and jumla fi'liyya), case endings (i'rab), subject/predicate (mubtada' / khabar), objects (maf'ul bihi), adverbials, and conditional sentences.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Jumla ismiyya (nominal sentence):** begins with a noun (or pronoun); structure: mubtada' (subject) + khabar (predicate). Example: "العلمُ نور" (Knowledge is light).
- **Jumla fi'liyya (verbal sentence):** begins with a verb; structure: fi'l (verb) + fa'il' (doer) + maf'ul bihi (object). Example: "كتبَ الطالبُ الدرس" (The student wrote the lesson).
- **Case endings (i'rab):** raf' (nominative, -u), nasb (accusative, -a), jarr (genitive, -i).
- **Mubtada' (subject):** always marfu' (nominative).
- **Khabar (predicate):** matches the mubtada' in number and gender; marfu'.
- **Maf'ul bihi (object):** mansub (accusative).
- **Kana wa akhawataha (كان وأخواتها):** introduce a wasf, the khabar becomes "ism kana" (mansub).
- **Inna wa akhawataha (إنّ وأخواتها):** the mubtada' becomes "ism inna" (mansub); the khabar stays "khabar inna" (marfu').

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing i'rab changes for kana vs inna — kana makes the wasf mansub, inna makes the mubtada' mansub.
- Forgetting that khabar (predicate) of inna stays marfu' even though the mubtada' becomes mansub.
- Forgetting that the dual takes "-ani" (nominative) and "-ayni" (accusative/genitive).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Jumla ismiyya structure

The nominal sentence is the basic Arabic sentence form. It has two parts:

- **Mubtada' (subject):** the topic, what is being talked about. Always marfu'.
- **Khabar (predicate):** what is said about the subject. Marfu' if a single word; may be a phrase (jumla or shibh jumla).

Examples:

- "الكتابُ جديد" (The book is new) — single-word khabar.
- "الكتابُ في الحقيبة" (The book is in the bag) — shibh jumla (prepositional phrase) as khabar.
- "الكتابُ قرأتُه" — khabar as jumla, where the jumla must have a rabeet (link) referring back to the mubtada'.

#### Jumla fi'liyya structure

The verbal sentence begins with a verb and has up to four parts:

- **Fi'l (verb):** past (madi), present (mudari'), or command (amr).
- **Fa'il' (subject/doer):** marfu'.
- **Maf'ul bihi (direct object):** mansub.
- **Maf'ul mutlaq (cognate object), maf'ul li-ajlih (purpose), maf'ul fih (time/place), maf'ul ma'ahu (accompaniment):** all mansub.

Examples:

- "قرأ الطالبُ الدرسَ" (The student read the lesson) — fa'il + maf'ul bihi.
- "ذهبَ محمدٌ إلى المدرسة" (Muhammad went to school) — fa'il + half-sentence (jar-majroor) as circumstantial.

#### Kana wa akhawataha

The sisters of kana (كان وأخواتها) are: kana, asbaHa, aTlaHa, baqa, laisa, maa zala, maa fakka, maa bariHa, maa infaqa. They are verbs that act on a mubtada' (ism kana, marfu') and khabar (khabar kana, mansub):

- "كان العلمُ نوراً" (Knowledge was light) — ism kana (العلمُ) marfu', khabar kana (نوراً) mansub.

#### Inna wa akhawataha

The sisters of inna (إنّ وأخواتها) are: inna, anna, ka-anna, lakinna, la'alla, laita. They are particles (or verbs) that act on a mubtada' (ism inna, mansub) and khabar (khabar inna, marfu'):

- "إنّ العلمَ نورٌ" (Indeed, knowledge is light) — ism inna (العلمَ) mansub, khabar inna (نورٌ) marfu'.

The case-endings are the inverse of kana — the wasf is mansub, and the subject becomes mansub.

#### Conditional sentences

Arabic conditional sentences are introduced by idhan (إذا), in (إن), man (مَن), ma (مَا), ayy (أَيّ), idha (إذا), or other particles. The structure is:

- **Shart (condition):** the "if" clause, usually with a verb.
- **Jawab al-shart (response):** the "then" clause, which may have fa (ف) at the start.

Examples:

- "إذا جاءَ الشتاءُ، لبسنا الملابسَ الثقيلة" (If winter comes, we wear heavy clothes).
- "مَن يدرسْ ينجحْ" (Whoever studies succeeds).

#### Direct object (maf'ul bihi)

The maf'ul bihi is the noun (or pronoun) that receives the action of the verb. It is always mansub.

- "قرأ الطالبُ الدرسَ" — الدرسَ is maf'ul bihi, mansub with fatha.

For pronouns, the dependent pronoun suffixes (-hu, -ha, -hum, etc.) take the place of the explicit noun. The pronoun is mansub in function even though its form does not change visibly.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where Grammar (Nahw) appears across the terminal Arabic exam

- **I'rab items:** identify and label the case-ending of each noun in a passage.
- **Sentence-structure items:** identify the parts of a jumla ismiyya or fi'liyya, or transform one form into the other.
- **Wasf items:** kana/akawataha or inna/akawataha transformations.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "The khabar of inna is mansub." No — only the ism inna is mansub; the khabar inna stays marfu'.
- "Adjectives always follow the noun they modify." In Arabic grammar, the na't (adjective) follows the man'ut (the noun it describes) and matches it in case, gender, number and definiteness.
- "Verbs always have a fa'il." Passive verbs do not have an explicit fa'il; the na'ib al-fa'il ( نائب الفاعل, "substitute for the subject") takes the place of the fa'il.

#### Exam technique

- For i'rab items, work systematically: identify each noun, then its grammatical role, then its case ending.
- For sentence-structure items, identify the predicate first (khabar or khabar al-jumla), then the subject (mubtada' or fa'il').
- For wasf items, recall the direction of the case shift — kana shifts the khabar; inna shifts the mubtada'.
