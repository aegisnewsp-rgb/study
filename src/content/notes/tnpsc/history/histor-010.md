# Origins of the Dravidian Movement

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Topic 10** in TNPSC Group 1 History covers the **History of Tamil Nadu** — Dravidian politics, the Self-Respect Movement, social reform, and the Tamil cultural renaissance. The **Justice Party** was founded in 1916 as the South Indian Liberal Federation to represent non-Brahmin interests. **E.V. Ramasamy (Periyar)** launched the **Self-Respect Movement** in 1925, advocating rationalism, women's rights, and rejection of caste hierarchy. The political lineage runs **Justice Party → Dravidar Kazhagam (1944) → DMK (1949)**. Anti-Hindi agitations of **1937 and 1965** became defining milestones in Tamil political identity. **Subramania Bharati** and **Bharathidasan** drove the literary renaissance.

- **High-yield pointers:** 1916 = Justice Party; 1925 = Self-Respect Movement; 1944 = Dravidar Kazhagam; 1949 = DMK.
- Anti-Hindi agitation years: **1937** (Justice Party ministry) and **1965** (under Congress regime).
- **C.N. Annadurai** founded the DMK and led it to power in **1967**.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Origins of the Dravidian Movement
The Dravidian Movement emerged from non-Brahmin mobilisation against perceived Brahmin dominance in education, government service, and religion. The **Justice Party**, established in 1916 under **T.M. Nair, P. Thyagaraya Chetty**, and others, contested legislative council elections in 1920 and captured power in the Madras Presidency. Its ministries (1921–26 and 1930s) introduced the **Community G.O.** reserving jobs for backward classes — a landmark social justice measure.

#### Periyar and the Self-Respect Movement
**E.V. Ramasamy Periyar** founded the **Self-Respect Movement** in 1925 with **Kanchipuram Nattu Desika Mudaliar** and **S. Ramanathan**, fusing it with the Justice Party in 1938. The movement promoted **rationalism, women's education, widow remarriage, inter-caste marriage, and temple-entry rights**. Periyar's newspaper *Kudi Arasu* and later *Viduthalai* became ideological vehicles. In 1944, Periyar renamed the Justice Party as **Dravidar Kazhagam (DK)** to emphasise Dravidian identity over electoral politics.

#### Anti-Hindi Agitations
The 1937 agitation targeted the Madras Presidency's plan to introduce Hindi in schools, leading to the **first ministry's fall**. The 1965 agitation, against the Official Languages Act, saw student-led protests, **self-immolations**, and concessions from the Centre. Both episodes entrenched the DMK's electoral narrative that Tamil cultural autonomy was non-negotiable.

#### Tamil Renaissance and Literature
**Subramania Bharati** (1882–1921), called *Mahakavi*, modernised Tamil prose and poetry through works like *Panchali Sabadham* and *Kannan Pattu*. **Bharathidasan** extended this rationalist, anti-caste literary tradition. **V.O. Chidambaram Pillai** and the **Swadeshi Steam Navigation Company** symbolised Tamil commercial self-respect.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Evolution of Dravidian Political Organisations
The Justice Party functioned primarily as an **elitist forum of non-Brahmin landed gentry and professionals**, contrasting with the mass-based, agitational character Periyar introduced through the DK. The 1949 split occurred because Periyar declared the DK would dissolve upon his death, prompting **C.N. Annadurai** to float the **Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam (DMK)** as a parliamentary successor. In 1972, **M.G. Ramachandran** broke away to form the **AIADMK**, citing personal and policy differences — a schism that structures Tamil politics to this day. The DK persisted as a fringe rationalist platform, contesting but rarely winning.

#### Social Reform Beyond Caste
The reform impulse addressed **temple entry (later codified by the Temple Entry Authorization and Indemnity Act, 1939), widow remarriage, abolition of *devadasi* practice, and women's suffrage advocacy**. **Ayothidasa Pandithar** championed anti-caste discourse through *Paraiyar*P*uthiya Thalattu* and Dravidian folklore, while **Thanthai Periyar** linked script reform and rationalist education. These reforms overlapped with the national social reform lineage of Periyar's contemporary, **Mahatma Gandhi**, but diverged sharply on religion.

#### Architectural and Cultural Heritage
Tamil Nadu's monumental record under the **Pallavas (Mahendravarman, Narasimhavarman)**, **Pandyas**, and **Cholas (Rajaraja I, Rajendra I)** contributed the rock-cut Mahendravalli style, Dravidian gopuram architecture, and the Brihadisvara temple at Thanjavur. The *Sangam* corpus, *Thirukkural*, and *Silappatikaram* form the literary bedrock invoked in Dravidian political discourse.

#### Common Mistakes in the Exam Hall
- Confusing **DK (1944)** with **DMK (1949)** as the same party.
- Attributing the **1967 electoral victory** to Periyar (who had distanced himself from electoral politics) rather than Annadurai.
- Citing **1965** as the *first* Anti-Hindi agitation.
- Treating the Dravidian Movement as post-1947, ignoring its pre-independence Justice Party roots.
- Reducing Periyar to atheism alone, ignoring his **self-respect** framework encompassing gender, labour, and economic justice.

> **Trap tip:** TNPSC mains often pairs a question on Periyar's ideology with one on DMK governance — revise both, not just founding dates.

2. Evaluate how the Anti-Hindi agitations of 1937 and 1965 shaped Tamil Nadu's post-independence political culture.
