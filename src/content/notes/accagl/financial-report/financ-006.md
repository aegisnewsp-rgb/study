# Revenue Recognition & Contracts with Customers

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

IFRS 15 "Revenue from Contracts with Customers" replaces IAS 18 and IAS 11 with a single five-step model. Revenue is recognised when (or as) the entity transfers **control** of a promised good or service to a customer, not when risk and rewards pass. The model applies to nearly all revenue contracts with customers, with limited exceptions (financial instruments, leases, insurance).

| Step | Action | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Identify the **contract** with the customer | Approved, enforceable rights, payment terms, approved in writing/oral/by custom |
| 2 | Identify **distinct performance obligations** (POBs) | Each separately identifiable good or service |
| 3 | Determine the **transaction price** | Fixed + variable (constrained) − financing component + non-cash + payable to customer |
| 4 | **Allocate** the transaction price | Pro rata to standalone selling prices (SSPs) of each POB |
| 5 | Recognise revenue when each POB is **satisfied** | Over time if criteria in IFRS 15.35 met; otherwise at a point in time |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"C-P-T-A-S"** = **C**ontract → **P**erformance obligations → **T**ransaction price → **A**llocate → **S**atisfy. Two POB tests are **C**apable of being distinct + **S**eparately identifiable ("**C**an the customer benefit, and is it **S**eparately identifiable in the contract?").

#### Core Principles and the Control Test

IFRS 15 introduces a **control-based** recognition model. Control of an asset exists when the customer can direct its use and obtain substantially all remaining benefits. Indicators include the customer's present right to payment, legal title, physical possession, transfer of significant risks and rewards, and acceptance. For most goods this triggers **point-in-time** recognition at delivery; for services and qualifying construction-type contracts, **over-time** recognition is required when one of three IFRS 15.35 criteria is met (customer simultaneously receives benefits as the entity performs; entity's performance creates or enhances a customer-controlled asset; the asset created has no alternative use and the entity has an enforceable right to payment for performance completed to date).

#### Concept Comparison Matrix — Commonly Confused Items

| Concept | Treatment under IFRS 15 | Common Error |
|---|---|---|
| Assurance-type warranty (e.g., "fix defects") | Accounted under IAS 37 as a provision; **not** a separate POB | Allocating part of transaction price to warranty |
| Service-type warranty (e.g., extended support beyond fixing defects) | **Separate performance obligation**; allocate portion of transaction price | Treating it as a normal product guarantee |
| Principal (controls goods before transfer) | Revenue = **gross** consideration | Recording revenue net of cost |
| Agent (arranges for another party to provide the goods/services) | Revenue = **net** fee/commission only | Showing gross turnover including pass-through amounts |
| Right of return | Revenue recognised only for goods expected **not** to be returned; refund liability + right-to-recover asset recognised for expected returns | Recognising full sale with no refund provision |
| Bill-and-hold sale | Recognise revenue only if entity has substantive reason, goods separately identified, ready for transfer, and cannot direct the goods | Recognising revenue on shipment despite goods held back |
| Significant financing component (>12 months deferral or prepayment) | Separate financing element; revenue at PV of consideration using **incremental borrowing rate** | Recognising nominal cash price as revenue |
| Licensing (right to use IP) | Revenue at a **point in time** when customer obtains control of the right | Spreading over the licence term like a rental |
| Licensing (right to access IP) | Revenue **over time** as entity provides access | Recognising entire fee upfront |
| Variable consideration | Estimated using **expected value** or **most likely amount**, whichever better predicts the entitlement, then **constrained** | Using full unconstrained estimate or ignoring the constraint test |

#### Key Steps for Multi-Element Contracts

1. Confirm the contract exists and meets the five IFRS 15.9 criteria (approval, identification of rights, payment terms, commercial substance, collectibility probable).
2. List every promised good or service, then test each for distinctness (capable of being distinct **and** separately identifiable).
3. Build the transaction price: fixed + variable (constrained) − significant financing component + fair value of non-cash consideration − payable to customer.
4. Determine each POB's **standalone selling price** (observable; if not, adjusted market assessment, expected cost plus margin, or residual approach).
5. Allocate transaction price pro rata: `AP = TP × (SSPn / ΣSSPn)`.
6. Recognise revenue over time (input/output method such as cost-to-cost) or at a point in time depending on the satisfaction pattern.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

#### Question:
Hitech Co enters into a contract on 1 January 2025 to sell a machine for CU 1,000,000 plus one year of on-site maintenance. The machine's standalone selling price is CU 900,000 and the maintenance standalone selling price is CU 200,000. Hitech expects to receive the CU 1,200,000 cash on 31 December 2025. Its incremental borrowing rate is 10% per year. The customer cannot benefit from the maintenance on its own or with readily available resources, and Hitech provides the maintenance separately. How much revenue is recognised at 31 December 2025 for each POB, and what is the finance income?

#### Solution:
**Step 1 — Identify POBs:** Machine = distinct (capable of being distinct, separately identifiable). Maintenance = distinct (Hitech sells it separately). Total POBs = 2.

#### Step 2 — Transaction price:
- Nominal: CU 1,200,000
- Significant financing component present (deferred >12 months, customer pays at end).
- PV adjustment: PV = 1,200,000 / (1 + 0.10)¹ = **CU 1,090,909**
- Transaction price at inception = **CU 1,090,909**

#### Step 3 — Allocate pro rata to SSPs:
- Total SSP = 900,000 + 200,000 = 1,100,000
- Machine allocation = 1,090,909 × (900,000/1,100,000) = **CU 892,479**
- Maintenance allocation = 1,090,909 × (200,000/1,100,000) = **CU 198,430**

#### Step 4 — Recognise revenue:
- Machine: **CU 892,479** at point of delivery (control transfers on installation/acceptance).
- Maintenance: **CU 198,430** recognised over time (ratably) during 2025 → **CU 99,215** by 30 June 2025, **CU 198,430** by 31 December 2025.

**Step 5 — Finance income** (unwinding of discount on the consideration receivable):
- Closing receivable 31 Dec 2025 = 1,090,909 − 892,479 − 99,215 (mid-year maintenance already earned) handled via amortised cost; full-year finance income = 1,200,000 − 1,090,909 = **CU 109,091** recognised in P/L.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Many candidates allocate first, then apply the discount to each allocated POB separately. The correct sequence under IFRS 15 is to determine the **transaction price first** (PV after the financing adjustment), then allocate the discounted amount across POBs using relative SSPs. Reversing this order inflates both revenue and finance income.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

#### Advanced Traps and Exceptions

| Topic | Edge Case / Trap | Correct Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Variable consideration method | Choosing expected value when the outcome is binary (e.g., "either CU 0 or CU 100,000") | Use **most likely amount** when there are few outcomes; expected value is appropriate across many outcomes |
| Constraint test | Recognising full bonus when only "highly probable" of no significant reversal is met | Apply the **constraint** — only include amount for which a reversal is highly unlikely to occur |
| Contract modifications | Adding distinct goods at SSP | Accounted as a **separate contract** |
| Contract modifications | Adding goods not at SSP, remaining goods distinct | Treated as **termination of old contract + new contract** (reallocate remaining TP) |
| Contract modifications | Adding goods not distinct from existing ones | Adjust TP of existing contract with cumulative catch-up in current period |
| Right of return | Estimating returns at portfolio level vs contract level | Portfolio estimation allowed if reasonably expected to be similar; do not over-generalise |
| Incremental costs of obtaining a contract | Commissions paid, legal fees triggered by contract | Capitalise and amortise over the contract life, **unless** amortisation period is ≤12 months |
| Onerous contract test | When expected costs exceed transaction price | Apply **IAS 37** onerous contract test and recognise provision; IFRS 15 does **not** override IAS 37 |
| Construction-type contract | Using POC % blindly from old IAS 11 | Use input/output method **tied to satisfaction of the POB**, not a generic cost ratio |
| Disclosures | Failing to disclose remaining POBs and transaction price allocated | Required: opening/closing contract assets, contract liabilities, receivables; revenue recognised from prior-period POBs; aggregate transaction price allocated to remaining POBs with expected timing |
| Transition | Modified retrospective vs full retrospective | Modified retrospective: cumulative effect to opening retained earnings of initial period; **do not restate** comparatives (common exam shortcut in CA Pakistan) |
| Principal vs agent | When entity holds inventory temporarily | Test who controls the goods before transfer — control, not physical custody, decides |

#### Integration with Adjacent Topics

IFRS 15 questions in ACCA FR and CA Pakistan Financial Reporting are rarely standalone. They are bundled with **IFRS 16 Leases** (e.g., the lease-vs-sale question — does the customer obtain control or merely a right to use?), **IAS 37 Provisions** (onerous performance obligations), **IAS 12 Income Taxes** (timing of revenue vs tax recognition), and **IFRS 9 Financial Instruments** (significant financing component and impairment of contract assets). The financial-statement presentation also requires separation on the **statement of financial position** of **contract assets** (right to consideration for work done, conditional on something other than passage of time) from trade receivables (unconditional right), and **contract liabilities** (obligation to transfer goods/services for consideration already received) from deferred income under old standards.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. A software vendor sells a perpetual licence for CU 500,000 plus five years of updates for CU 100,000. Standalone selling prices are CU 600,000 and CU 80,000 respectively. Determine the allocation and the timing of recognition for each POB. *(Hint: assess whether updates are a right to **use** IP at a point in time, or a right to **access** IP over time.)*
2. A retailer sells CU 1,000 of goods and expects 10% returns based on historical data. The cost of returned goods is CU 600. Prepare the journal entries on the date of sale, showing revenue, refund liability, and right-to-recover returned goods. Then prepare the adjusting entry when actual returns are 8%. *(Hint: the right-to-recover asset is measured at the carrying amount of the inventory expected to be returned, less any expected costs to recover.)*
