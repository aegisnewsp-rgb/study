# Writing — Communicating, Organising and Register — Qatar Secondary Certificate English Notes

The MoEHE English Curriculum Standards at Grade 12 publish four Writing sub-strands (W1-W4): communicating information, organising/spelling/punctuating written work, developing writing strategies, and using appropriate register. The Grade 12 CEFR exit level is B2 Low-Mid requires clear, detailed text on a wide range of subjects, essays that develop an argument, and letters/essays that highlight the personal significance of events.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Text types:** narrative, descriptive, expository, argumentative, discursive.
- **Paragraph structure:** topic sentence, supporting sentences, concluding sentence.
- **Cohesive devices:** "however", "therefore", "in contrast", "on the other hand", "furthermore", "in addition", "as a result".
- **Punctuation:** comma, period, semicolon, colon, dash, apostrophe, question mark, exclamation mark.
- **Spelling:** commonly confused words (their/there/they're, its/it's, your/you're, affect/effect, lose/loose).

#### Examiner traps

- One paragraph for the whole essay — B2 requires clear paragraphing.
- No planning before writing — the essay drifts off-topic.
- Same word repeated — synonyms and pronouns matter.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### W1: Communicating information

At B2 the writer presents information clearly and develops arguments with reasons, examples, and supporting evidence. A 5-paragraph essay is the standard form: introduction with thesis, three body paragraphs with reasons/evidence, conclusion.

For an argumentative essay on "Should school uniforms be required?": thesis "School uniforms should be required because they reduce peer pressure and improve focus on learning." Body 1: evidence on peer pressure reduction. Body 2: evidence on academic focus. Body 3: counter-argument + rebuttal. Conclusion: restate thesis and project forward.

#### W2: Organising, spelling and punctuating

Paragraphing: one paragraph per main idea. Use transitions between paragraphs. Within paragraphs, use sub-transitions ("firstly", "in addition", "moreover").

Spelling: build a personal list of commonly misspelled words; check before submitting. Use the spell-checker but do not rely on it for homophones ("their" vs "there").

Punctuation: comma before "and" in a list of three or more (Oxford comma optional); comma after introductory clauses; semicolon to link related independent clauses; colon before a list or explanation; apostrophe for possession (the dog's tail) and contractions (it's = it is).

#### W3: Developing writing strategies

Planning: spend 5-10 minutes outlining before writing. Drafting: write quickly to get ideas down. Revising: re-read for content and organisation. Editing: check grammar, spelling, punctuation. Proofreading: final read.

Peer review: have a partner read and identify unclear sentences, missing evidence, or off-topic paragraphs.

#### W4: Appropriate register

Formal: avoid contractions, use precise vocabulary, address the reader indirectly ("one might argue", "it could be said"). Informal: contractions, colloquialisms, address the reader directly ("you"). Match the audience and purpose.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### Where writing appears at the terminal

Writing is the productive skill assessed most heavily at the Grade 12 terminal English paper. The B2 exit descriptor expects clear, well-structured text on a wide range of subjects with appropriate register, range of vocabulary, and grammatical accuracy.

#### Exam technique

- Plan the essay structure before writing — outline the thesis, three body paragraphs, and conclusion.
- Read the question twice: what is it asking? A "discuss both views" essay needs both sides, not a one-sided argument.
- Leave 5 minutes at the end to proofread.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Longer essays score better." No — relevance and quality matter more than length. A focused 300-word essay outscores a rambling 500-word one.
- "Avoid all repetition." No — some repetition is necessary for cohesion (using pronouns, synonyms, summary phrases). The problem is uncontrolled repetition, especially of the same word.
