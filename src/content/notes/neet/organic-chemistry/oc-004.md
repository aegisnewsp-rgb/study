# Aldehydes Ketones

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your NEET UG exam.

Aldehydes and ketones are **carbonyl compounds** built around the polar C=O group, where the carbon is sp² hybridised and the oxygen carries a partial negative charge (μ ≈ 2.3–2.7 D). In an **aldehyde (R–CHO)** the carbonyl carbon bonds to at least one H; in a **ketone (R–CO–R′)** it bonds to two carbon groups, giving the general formula CₙH₂ₙO.

- **Saturated aldehyde:** CₙH₂ₙO, n ≥ 1 (HCHO, CH₃CHO)
- **Saturated ketone:** CₙH₂ₙO, n ≥ 3 (CH₃COCH₃, C₂H₅COC₂H₅)
- **IR fingerprint:** strong C=O stretch near **1700 cm⁻¹** (aldehydes ~1720–1740, ketones ~1705–1720, lowered by α,β-conjugation)
- **NEET hot list:** aldol condensation, Cannizzaro, iodoform, Clemmensen vs Wolff–Kishner, Tollen's/Fehling's/2,4-DNP tests

| Distinction test | Aldehyde | Ketone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2,4-DNP (Brady's) | Orange-yellow ppt | Orange-yellow ppt |
| Tollen's [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ | Silver mirror | No reaction |
| Fehling's (Cu²⁺) | Red Cu₂O (aliphatic) | No reaction |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students working through NEET prep over weeks.

#### Nucleophilic Addition Mechanism
The C=O carbon is electrophilic because oxygen (χ ≈ 3.44) pulls electron density away from carbon (χ ≈ 2.55). A nucleophile (Nu⁻) attacks the planar carbonyl carbon, forming a **tetrahedral alkoxide intermediate** that is protonated to give the addition product. Aldehydes react faster than ketones because two alkyl groups donate electrons inductively and create steric crowding around the carbonyl.

#### Aldol Condensation
Only carbonyls with an **α-hydrogen** undergo this base-catalysed reaction. OH⁻ abstracts an α-H to form an **enolate**, which attacks a second carbonyl molecule. The product is a **β-hydroxy carbonyl (aldol)** that dehydrates on heating to an **α,β-unsaturated carbonyl**. For acetaldehyde: 2 CH₃CHO → CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CHO → CH₃CH=CHCHO + H₂O.

#### Cannizzaro Disproportionation
Carbonyls **without α-H** (HCHO, benzaldehyde, trimethylacetaldehyde) react with concentrated NaOH. One molecule is reduced to a primary alcohol while another is oxidised to a carboxylate; benzaldehyde gives benzyl alcohol and sodium benzoate.

#### Reduction and α-Halogenation
**Clemmensen** (Zn–Hg / conc. HCl) and **Wolff–Kishner** (NH₂NH₂ / KOH / Δ) both reduce C=O to –CH₂–, the former in acid, the latter in base. NaBH₄ and LiAlH₄ stop at the alcohol stage. The **Hell–Volhard–Zelinsky (HVZ)** reaction uses X₂/Red P to introduce a halogen at the α-carbon, a gateway to α-amino and α-hydroxy acids.

- **Acetal/hemiacetal** formation: ROH adds across C=O; acid-catalysed excess ROH gives the acetal R₂C(OR′)₂
- **Gem-diol (hydrate):** H₂O adds reversibly; formaldehyde is ~99% hydrated in water, acetone barely 0.001%
- **Iodoform test:** CH₃CO–R + 3 I₂ + 4 OH⁻ → CHI₃ (yellow ppt, mp 119 °C) + RCOO⁻ + 3 I⁻ + 3 H₂O

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for mastery-level preparation across months.

#### Mechanism Deep Dive — Enolate Geometry and Selectivity
The enolate formed under basic conditions is an ambident nucleophile: carbon attack (kinetic, soft) gives the aldol product, while oxygen attack (thermodynamic) would give an enol ether. Under thermodynamic control with bulky bases like LDA at –78 °C, the less-substituted (kinetic) enolate dominates; with KH or NaH at room temperature, the more-substituted enolate forms. NEET usually tests only the carbon-attack aldol pathway, but recognising enolate ambiguity prevents trap options.

#### Edge Cases and Cross-Topic Links
- **Formaldehyde (HCHO)** is unique: no α-H, undergoes Cannizzaro, gives positive Tollen's and Fehling's, and exists almost entirely as methanediol (HOCH₂OH) in water.
- **Acetone** gives a positive **iodoform test** despite being a ketone because it carries the CH₃CO– group; secondary alcohols like isopropanol also test positive because I₂/OH⁻ oxidises them to acetone first.
- **Aromatic aldehydes** (benzaldehyde) give a slow Tollen's response but a negative Fehling's; this difference is a frequent NEET assertion-reason question.
- **α,β-Unsaturated carbonyls** undergo **1,4-conjugate (Michael) addition** with soft nucleophiles such as CN⁻ or enolates, distinct from the 1,2-addition seen with hydrides.

#### Common Mistakes to Avoid
- Treating the initial aldol product as the final answer; dehydration requires heating.
- Assigning Cannizzaro to acetaldehyde; it has α-H and does aldol instead.
- Forgetting that **conjugation** lowers ν(C=O) by ~20–30 cm⁻¹, mis-identifying cinnamaldehyde IR spectra.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Aldol | Must have α-H | dil. NaOH, Δ | α,β-unsaturated carbonyl |
| Cannizzaro | No α-H | conc. NaOH | Alcohol + carboxylate |

| Wolff–Kishner | Base-stable substrate | NH₂NH₂ / KOH / Δ | –CH₂– |
