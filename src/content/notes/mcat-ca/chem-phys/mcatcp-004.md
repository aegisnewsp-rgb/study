# Periodic Trends and Bonding — MCAT Chem/Phys

Periodic trends and chemical bonding form a large part of AAMC Foundational Concept 5 of the Chem/Phys section. The MCAT tests periodic-table trends (atomic radius, ionisation energy, electronegativity), ionic and covalent bonding, Lewis structures, VSEPR geometry, molecular polarity, and intermolecular forces. These topics are foundational for biochemistry and for organic chemistry reasoning.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### Core concepts

- **Periodic trends across a period (left to right):** atomic radius decreases; ionisation energy increases; electronegativity increases.
- **Periodic trends down a group:** atomic radius increases; ionisation energy decreases; electronegativity decreases.
- **Ionic bonds:** metal + non-metal; electron transfer; strong in the solid state; dissociate in water.
- **Covalent bonds:** non-metal + non-metal; electron sharing; polar or nonpolar.
- **Lewis structures:** dots for valence electrons; lines for shared pairs; brackets and charge for ions.
- **VSEPR:** electron pairs repel; geometry follows from the number of electron domains.
- **Polarity:** a molecule is polar if it has polar bonds AND an asymmetric geometry.

#### One-pass drill

1. Identify the topic: periodic trend, bonding type, geometry, or polarity.
2. Recall the rule for that topic.
3. Apply the rule to the specific case in the passage.

#### Examiner traps

- Treating atomic radius and ionic radius as the same. Cations are smaller than the parent atom; anions are larger.
- Forgetting that double and triple bonds count as one electron domain in VSEPR.
- Calling a molecule with polar bonds "polar" without checking the geometry.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Periodic trends in detail

- **Atomic radius:** depends on the principal quantum number (size of the orbital) and the effective nuclear charge. Larger orbitals → larger radius; higher effective nuclear charge → smaller radius.
- **Ionisation energy:** the energy needed to remove an electron. Trends follow atomic radius (smaller atom → harder to remove an electron).
- **Electronegativity:** the ability to attract electrons in a bond. Follows ionisation energy in general trend.
- **Electron affinity:** the energy released when an atom gains an electron. Less consistent trend but generally increases across a period.

The MCAT often asks about the periodic trend in the context of a biological ion (Na⁺, K⁺, Cl⁻, Ca²⁺).

#### Chemical bonding

**Ionic bonding:** electron transfer. Strong in the solid state (lattice energy). Dissociate into ions in aqueous solution. The MCAT tests ionic bonding in the context of salts (NaCl, KCl) and ionic gradients across membranes.

**Covalent bonding:** electron sharing. Can be polar (between atoms of different electronegativity) or nonpolar (between atoms of the same element). The MCAT tests covalent bonding in the context of biomolecules (C-C, C-H, C-O, C-N bonds).

**Metallic bonding:** a "sea" of delocalised electrons. Less commonly tested on the MCAT.

#### Lewis structures and VSEPR

Drawing Lewis structures: count valence electrons, place the central atom, distribute electrons, and check the octet. For expanded octets (Period 3 and below), more than eight electrons are allowed.

VSEPR: count electron domains around the central atom. Each lone pair, single bond, double bond, and triple bond counts as one domain.

- 2 domains: linear.
- 3 domains: trigonal planar.
- 4 domains: tetrahedral.
- 5 domains: trigonal bipyramidal.
- 6 domains: octahedral.

#### Molecular polarity

A molecule is polar if it has polar bonds AND an asymmetric geometry. The MCAT tests polarity in the context of solubility ("like dissolves like"), membrane permeability, and intermolecular forces.

#### Practice routine

- Take 10 periodic-trend items. Predict the trend for any two elements.
- Take 5 Lewis-structure items. Draw the structure, then identify the geometry and polarity.
- Take 5 polarity items. Identify the polar bonds and the geometry, then decide.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How this topic connects to the other Chem/Phys topics

- **Biomolecules** (in the Bio/Biochem section) depend on the bonding and polarity of amino acids, lipids, and nucleotides.
- **Acid-base chemistry** depends on bond polarity and electronegativity to identify acidic hydrogens.
- **Intermolecular forces** govern protein folding, membrane structure, and DNA base pairing.

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"Cations are always larger than anions."** No — anions are larger than the parent atom; cations are smaller. Compare cation to parent, not to anion.
- **"All molecules with polar bonds are polar."** Polarity requires both polar bonds AND asymmetric geometry. CO₂ has polar bonds but is linear and nonpolar.
- **"Double bonds make a molecule nonpolar."** No — double bonds are still polar if the two atoms have different electronegativity. C=O is polar.

#### Exam technique

- For periodic-trend items, locate the two elements on the periodic table and predict the trend before reading the choices.
- For Lewis-structure items, count valence electrons first. Distribute them, then check the octet.
- For VSEPR items, count electron domains. The geometry follows from the count.
- For polarity items, check both the bonds AND the geometry.
