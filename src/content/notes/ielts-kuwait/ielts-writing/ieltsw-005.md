# Task 2 — Opinion, Discussion and Problem/Solution Essays — IELTS Writing Notes

Three Task 2 prompt types dominate IELTS Academic Writing. Each requires a slightly different paragraph plan. Recognising the prompt type in the first 30 seconds is the single highest-leverage habit for Task 2.

> Verify the live Writing format on https://ielts.org/ before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Opinion essay:** "To what extent do you agree or disagree?" The candidate takes a position.
- **Discussion essay:** "Discuss both views and give your opinion." The candidate discusses both sides.
- **Problem/solution essay:** "What are the causes of this? What solutions can you suggest?" The candidate identifies causes and solutions.
- **Two-part question:** a less common format with two related questions.
- **Time:** about 40 minutes.
- **Word limit:** 250+ words.

#### Examiner traps

- Misidentifying the prompt type — opinion vs discussion requires different structure.
- Forgetting to give an opinion on a discussion essay.
- Skipping the conclusion.

---

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Opinion essay

Prompt: "To what extent do you agree or disagree?"

Structure:

1. **Introduction:** paraphrase + thesis (your position).
2. **Body 1:** first reason supporting your position.
3. **Body 2:** second reason supporting your position.
4. **Body 3:** counter-argument + rebuttal (optional but recommended for band 7+).
5. **Conclusion:** restate your position in different words.

#### Discussion essay

Prompt: "Discuss both views and give your opinion."

Structure:

1. **Introduction:** paraphrase + state that both views will be discussed.
2. **Body 1:** first view + reasons.
3. **Body 2:** second view + reasons.
4. **Body 3:** your opinion + reasons.
5. **Conclusion:** restate your opinion.

#### Problem/solution essay

Prompt: "What are the causes of this? What solutions can you suggest?" (or similar)

Structure:

1. **Introduction:** paraphrase + state that causes and solutions will be discussed.
2. **Body 1:** describe the problem (and its causes).
3. **Body 2:** first solution + rationale.
4. **Body 3:** second solution + rationale.
5. **Conclusion:** summarise the solutions.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where prompt types appear across IELTS Writing

Each of the three prompt types appears regularly in IELTS Academic Writing Task 2. The candidate must identify the type in the first 30 seconds to plan the right structure.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "All Task 2 essays are the same." No — opinion, discussion, and problem/solution require different structures.
- "Discussion essays don't require an opinion." Yes — they require a clear opinion in the third body paragraph.
- "Problem/solution essays don't require causes." Often they do — the prompt typically asks for both.

#### Exam technique

- Read the prompt carefully and identify the type in the first 30 seconds.
- Plan the structure (introduction, body paragraphs, conclusion) before drafting.
- Take a clear position in opinion essays.
- Discuss both views in discussion essays.
