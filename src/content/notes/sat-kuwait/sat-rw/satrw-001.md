# Information and Ideas — Reading Comprehension — SAT R&W Notes

The Information and Ideas content domain is the largest single domain of the SAT Reading and Writing section. It tests the candidate's ability to locate and interpret explicit textual information, identify central ideas and themes, summarise text, and draw logical inferences. This domain accounts for a substantial share of items on the digital SAT.

> Verify the live digital SAT specifications and content domain weighting on https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/ before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Central ideas and themes:** the main claim or unifying idea of a passage or paragraph.
- **Summarising:** paraphrasing a passage's content in fewer words without losing meaning.
- **Drawing inferences:** concluding what the passage implies without stating it explicitly.
- **Locating textual evidence:** citing specific lines that support a claim.
- **Passage types:** literature, history/social science, the humanities, science — single passages and paired short passages.
- **Format:** each item has one passage (or paired short passages) and one question.

#### Examiner traps

- Choosing an option that is mentioned in the passage but does not answer the question.
- Confusing inference (what is implied) with explicit information (what is stated).
- Missing the central idea by focusing on a detail.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Central idea questions

The central idea is the main claim or unifying idea of a passage. It is usually stated in the introduction or the conclusion. To find it:

1. Read the first sentence of each paragraph.
2. Identify the topic the passage repeats or expands on.
3. State the central idea in your own words.

#### Inference questions

Inference questions ask what the passage implies. The answer is the option that is consistent with the passage but goes beyond the explicit text.

Example:

- Passage: "The 19th century saw rapid industrialisation in Europe. Cities grew, factories multiplied, and the workforce shifted from agriculture to manufacturing."
- Question: "What can be inferred about rural populations during this period?"
- Answer: "Many people likely moved from rural to urban areas." (implied, not stated)

#### Summarising questions

Summarising questions ask for a concise paraphrase of the passage. The correct option captures all the key ideas in fewer words.

#### Locating textual evidence

Some questions ask you to cite specific lines that support a claim. The correct answer is the lines that directly relate to the claim, not just any lines from the passage.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where reading comprehension appears across the SAT R&W section

Information and Ideas is the largest domain on the SAT Reading and Writing section. Strong performance here is the single highest-leverage preparation for the SAT R&W section.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "The first sentence is always the central idea." Not necessarily — the central idea may be implied throughout the passage.
- "Inference means guessing." No — inference means concluding from evidence in the passage.
- "The longest option is correct." No — the correct option is the most accurate, not the longest.

#### Exam technique

- For central-idea questions, identify the topic the passage repeats.
- For inference questions, eliminate options that are too literal or that contradict the passage.
- For summarising questions, choose the option that captures all the key ideas.
- For textual-evidence questions, choose the lines that directly support the claim.
