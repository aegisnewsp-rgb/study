# Synonyms and Antonyms

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

A **synonym** is a word sharing the same or near-identical meaning as another (e.g., *happy* ↔ *joyful*). An **antonym** carries the opposite meaning (e.g., *happy* ↔ *sad*). The LAT English section typically frames these as 4-option MCQs where you select the word "nearest in meaning" or "opposite in meaning" to a bolded stem word.

- **Stem words** are usually mid-to-advanced vocabulary drawn from legal, academic, and abstract registers.
- **Options** are deliberately close — distractors differ by **connotation** (positive/negative) or **register** (formal/informal).
- The fastest scoring tactic: identify the **denotation** first, then eliminate options whose **register** clashes with the stem.

| Term | Definition | LAT Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Synonym | Same or similar meaning | *arduous* → strenuous |
| Antonym | Opposite meaning | *arduous* → effortless |
| Near-synonym | Overlapping but not identical | *big* ≠ *colossal* (intensity differs) |
| Gradable antonym | Opposite ends of a scale | *hot* / *cold* |
| Absolute antonym | Mutually exclusive pair | *dead* / *alive* |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### How LAT Frames the Question

Each item presents a stem word in **bold or capital letters** followed by four options lettered A–D. The instruction reads "Choose the word most **nearly opposite/similar** in meaning." The stem word is rarely a basic-level term; expect words like *ephemeral*, *vacillate*, *magnanimous*, or *equivocate*. Time budget per item: 40–50 seconds, since the English section carries 3% of the LAT total but contains roughly 8–10 such vocabulary questions.

#### Denotation vs. Connotation

**Denotation** is the dictionary meaning; **connotation** is the emotional or cultural shade. Two synonyms can share denotation but diverge in connotation — *cheap* and *economical* both mean "low cost," yet *cheap* carries negative tone. LAT options exploit this gap. Always ask: does the option match the stem's **register**?

#### Common Trap Categories

- **Intensity mismatches:** *angry* vs *furious* (same family, different strength).
- **Part-of-speech shifts:** stem is an adjective but option is a noun form.
- **False cognates:** words that look related but mean something different.
- **Antonyms of related but wrong sense:** a polysemous stem has multiple antonyms depending on which sense the stem invokes in context.

| Stem | Best Synonym | Common Wrong Option | Why It's Wrong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *Lucid* | Clear | Luminous | Refers to light, not clarity |
| *Spurious* | False | Spacious | Different root entirely |
| *Mitigate* | Lessen | Instigate | Opposite direction of action |
| *Erudite* | Learned | Eloquent | Eloquent = persuasive speech, not scholarly depth |

#### Memory Strategy That Works

Learn **word families** via their **root word** rather than rote lists. *Ben-/bon-* roots yield *benevolent*, *beneficial*, *bounty* — all sharing a "good" semantic core. One root unlocks 5–7 LAT-style items.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases Examiners Exploit

**Gradable antonyms** sit on a spectrum (*hot/cold*, *rich/poor*) and accept comparatives (*hotter/colder*). **Absolute antonyms** do not (*pregnant/not pregnant* — you cannot be "more pregnant"). When a stem is a gradable adjective, the correct antonym must also be gradable; pairing *hot* with *freezing* is wrong because *freezing* is non-gradable.

**Relational antonyms** reverse a relationship rather than a quality: *buy/sell*, *teacher/pupil*, *borrow/lend*. These appear in roughly 1 in 12 LAT vocabulary items and trap students who scan only for polarity.

#### Collocation and Context

Some synonyms are interchangeable in isolation but not in fixed phrases. *Strong* and *powerful* both modify *argument*, but only *strong* collocates with *accent*. LAT stems sometimes embed the word in a mini-sentence to test this awareness.

#### Practice Prompts

1. Choose the antonym of **ephemeral** (A) eternal (B) swift (C) fragile (D) vivid — answer: A, because *ephemeral* = short-lived; *fragile* is a near-synonym trap.
2. Choose the synonym of **vacillate** (A) dominate (B) waver (C) decide (D) vanish — answer: B; *vacillate* = hesitate between options.

#### Exam-Specific Strategy

In the LAT, vocabulary MCQs are the highest accuracy-per-minute items. Target ≥80% accuracy by building a personal **error log** of mis-matched options sorted by trap type (intensity, register, false cognate). Review the log weekly; patterns surface within three cycles.
