# Time and Attempt Strategy for Paper II

NUMS gives Paper II a hard physical shape: 50 one-best-option multiple-choice questions in 15 minutes. That is 18 seconds per question, and the cap is on time, not on marking. Paper II is also worth mentioning precisely — NUMS\'s structure table gives the Psychological Test component a 5% weightage — which means it is roughly a twentieth of the paper and cannot be allowed to cost you a fifth of the sitting.

Two things make this paper different from Paper I. NUMS states there is no negative marking anywhere in the examination, so on this paper a guess is free. And unlike Paper I, Paper II is almost entirely untrainable by reading — you improve it only by volume, which is why it belongs at the start of your revision rather than the end.

## The 18-second rule

Divide the 15 minutes before you begin and write the budget at the top of the page: 50 questions, 15 minutes. Then work in blocks rather than per question. Twelve questions is 3 minutes and 36 seconds, and 3 blocks of roughly 4 minutes 48 seconds each is a plan you can hold to.

The reason to think in blocks rather than questions is that the correct budget per item is not 18 seconds — it is less. The questions you answer quickly create the time for the ones you do not. Most candidates allocate their time evenly and lose the paper to the last ten items.

The 18-second figure is the cap, not the target. If an item has not resolved by the time you have spent half of it, mark it and move.

## Pass 1, then pass 2

Spend the first 8 or 9 minutes on every question you can answer on first contact. Speed here comes from reading the stem once and the options once, not from recognising the item type. No circling of questions, no flags — mark it and go.

Spend the remainder going back. Second-pass items are usually slow because the stem was misread, not because the content is hard, so re-read the stem rather than the options. Options hide the misreading: they are all plausible precisely because you misread the question they answer.

Close with a sweep for anything left blank. With no negative marking, a blank is a guaranteed zero and a guess is a free lottery ticket. Never leave a blank on this paper.

## What the items look like

You will meet four families and they need different reflexes. Classification items give you a set and ask which one is different; the answer is always a rule you can state, and if you cannot state it you have not found it yet. Series and matrix items require you to find the pattern across two or more steps before you look at the options. Numerical items are mental arithmetic where the only real skill is deciding how much precision the question wants. Verbal analogy and syllogism items transfer a relation from the first pair to the second, and the commonest error is stopping at a relation that is defensible but not the tightest one.

## Common mistakes

Revising syllabus content to prepare for Paper II. Nothing you memorise about biology helps a classification item, and it is the single most common way candidates waste the final fortnight.

Spending more than the cap on one question. The cost is not the question you are stuck on — it is the five that follow.

Circling questions for a second pass instead of marking them. Circling costs two seconds and produces a list you then have to re-scan, which is time you were trying to save.

Assuming a wrong answer costs you and leaving items blank. NUMS states there shall be no negative marking. A blank is the only guaranteed zero available to you on this paper.

Practising untimed. The entire difficulty of Paper II lives in the clock, and an untimed practice session trains a skill you will not use.

Using the time saved on Paper I to slow down on Paper II. NUMS states Paper I and Paper II are separate components of the same sitting; the pacing that works across 150 questions in 2 hours 45 minutes is not the pacing that works across 50 in 15.
