# Pakistan: Political Developments

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> High-yield revision summary of the 1973 Constitution, parliamentary governance, the 18th Amendment, and electoral dynamics for HEC LAT aspirants.

**Constitutional & Political Architecture — Core Essentials**

1. **The 1973 Constitution:** Enacted on 14 August 1973 under Prime Minister Zulfikar Ali Bhutto; established a federal parliamentary republic with a bicameral legislature:
   - **National Assembly (Lower House):** Directly elected on adult franchise; 336 total seats (including 60 reserved for women and 10 for non-Muslims). 5-year constitutional term.
   - **Senate (Upper House):** Permanent house of federation ensuring equal provincial representation; 96 members (reduced from 104 post-25th Amendment merging FATA with Khyber Pakhtunkhwa). Senators serve 6-year terms, with half retiring every 3 years.
2. **Landmark Constitutional Amendments:**
   - **18th Amendment (2010):** Abolished the presidential power to dissolve the National Assembly under Article 58(2)(b); devolved 17 federal ministries to the provinces; abolished the Concurrent Legislative List; restructured the National Finance Commission (NFC) Award under Article 160(3A), guaranteeing that provincial shares cannot fall below the previous award.
   - **19th & 20th Amendments:** Refined judicial appointments under Article 175A and established the caretaker government framework for transparent general elections.
   - **25th Amendment (2018):** Merged the Federally Administered Tribal Areas (FATA) into Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KP), extending constitutional courts (Peshawar High Court and Supreme Court) to former tribal agencies.
3. **Key Constitutional Institutions:**
   - **Supreme Court of Pakistan:** Highest appellate court; exercises original jurisdiction under Article 184(3) on matters of public importance relating to Fundamental Rights.
   - **Election Commission of Pakistan (ECP):** Constitutional body under Article 218 responsible for organizing and conducting honest, just, and fair elections.
   - **Council of Common Interests (CCI):** Formulates and regulates policy on federal legislative list Part II subjects (water, railways, electricity, mineral oil).

⚡ **LAT Exam Trap:** LAT examiners often test Article 58(2)(b). Remember that the 18th Amendment **revoked** the President's unilateral power to dismiss elected governments, restoring supreme executive authority to the Prime Minister and Cabinet under Article 90.

## 1. Separation of Powers and Constitutional Balances

The Islamic Republic of Pakistan operates on a tripartite division of state functions governed by written constitutional supremacy:

| Branch of State | Constitutional Anchor | Composition & Mandate | Key Checks and Balances |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Executive** | Articles 41–49 (President), Articles 90–100 (Prime Minister & Cabinet) | Prime Minister is Chief Executive and Head of Government; President is constitutional Head of State | Executive is collectively responsible to the National Assembly; subject to Vote of No-Confidence (Article 95) |
| **Legislature (Majlis-e-Shoora)** | Articles 50–89 | Bicameral Parliament: National Assembly (proportional to population) + Senate (equal provincial parity) | Money bills originate exclusively in the National Assembly; ordinary legislation requires passage by both houses |
| **Judiciary** | Articles 175–212 | Supreme Court, 5 High Courts (Lahore, Sindh, Peshawar, Balochistan, Islamabad), Federal Shariat Court | Judicial review of legislative and executive actions; custodian of Part II Chapter 1 Fundamental Rights |
| **Federal Federation Guard** | Articles 153–159 | Council of Common Interests (CCI) and National Economic Council (NEC) | Equal provincial representation alongside Prime Minister to resolve inter-provincial resource disputes |

## 2. Fiscal Federalism and the 7th NFC Award

Federal-provincial fiscal harmony is anchored by the **National Finance Commission (NFC)** established under Article 160:
- **Vertical Distribution:** The 7th NFC Award (ratified in 2010) redistributed the Federal Divisible Pool (FDP) by allocating **57.5% to the provinces** and retaining **42.5% for the federal government**.
- **Article 160(3A) Constitutional Lock:** The 18th Amendment inserted clause (3A), legally stipulating that the share of the provinces in each subsequent NFC Award cannot be less than the share given under the previous award.
- **Horizontal Distribution Criteria:** Moved away from a pure population-based formula to a multiple-indicator formula:
  - **Population:** 82.0%
  - **Poverty / Backwardness:** 10.3%
  - **Revenue Generation / Collection:** 5.0%
  - **Inverse Population Density (Urbanization / Area spread):** 2.7%

## 3. High-Yield Political & Legal Casework

#### Landmark Supreme Court Decisions in Pakistan's Constitutional History
1. **Maulvi Tamizuddin Khan Case (1955):** Federal Court invoked the Doctrine of State Necessity to justify the dissolution of the First Constituent Assembly by Governor-General Ghulam Muhammad.
2. **Asma Jilani Case (1972):** Supreme Court overturned the Dosso verdict, declaring General Yahya Khan's martial law illegal and labelling him a usurper.
3. **Nawaz Sharif vs President of Pakistan (1993):** The Supreme Court struck down President Ghulam Ishaq Khan's dissolution order under Article 58(2)(b) and reinstated the National Assembly and Prime Minister.
4. **No-Confidence Resolution Ruling (2022):** The Supreme Court ruled the Deputy Speaker's dismissal of the Vote of No-Confidence under Article 5 unconstitutional, ordering the National Assembly session to reconvene for the vote.

## 4. HEC LAT Practice Examination Questions

**Question 1 (LAT Current Affairs):** Which constitutional amendment abolished the Concurrent Legislative List and devolved 17 federal ministries to the provinces?  
*(A) 17th Amendment (B) 18th Amendment (C) 19th Amendment (D) 21st Amendment*  
**Answer:** (B) 18th Amendment (Passed unanimously in April 2010, significantly enhancing provincial autonomy).

**Question 2 (LAT Current Affairs):** What is the total current membership of the Senate of Pakistan following the 25th Constitutional Amendment?  
*(A) 104 members (B) 100 members (C) 96 members (D) 108 members*  
**Answer:** (C) 96 members (Explanation: Prior to the 25th Amendment, each of the four provinces had 23 seats, ICT had 4, and FATA had 8, totalling 104. With FATA's merger into KP, the 8 FATA seats were phased out upon senator retirements, leaving 96 permanent seats).

**Question 3 (LAT Current Affairs):** Under the 7th NFC Award, what percentage of the horizontal distribution formula is allocated to poverty and backwardness?  
*(A) 82.0% (B) 10.3% (C) 5.0% (D) 2.7%*  
**Answer:** (B) 10.3% (Explanation: Population receives 82.0%, poverty/backwardness receives 10.3%, revenue collection receives 5.0%, and inverse population density receives 2.7%).
