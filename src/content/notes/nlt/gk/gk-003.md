# Pakistan Constitution and Political System

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The **Constitution of the Islamic Republic of Pakistan, 1973** is the supreme law, enforced since **14 August 1973**. Pakistan is a **federal parliamentary republic** with an **Islamic ideological framework** (Objectives Resolution, 1949).

- **Head of State** = **President** (ceremonial, elected by an electoral college of Parliament + provincial assemblies).
- **Head of Government** = **Prime Minister** (leader of the majority party in the **National Assembly**).
- **Parliament (Majlis-e-Shoora)** = **National Assembly (342 seats)** + **Senate (96 seats)**.
- **Federating units** = **four provinces** (Punjab, Sindh, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Balochistan) plus AJK, Gilgit-Baltistan, ICT.
- **Amendment threshold** = **two-thirds majority of both houses**.

High-yield recall: 1973 Constitution, NA 342 / Senate 96, 18th Amendment (2010, devolution), parliamentary (not presidential) system.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Constitutional Foundations

The 1973 Constitution is the **third** foundational document of Pakistan, succeeding the 1956 and 1962 constitutions. It blends the **Government of India Act 1935**, the **Objectives Resolution (1949)**, and lessons from earlier constitutions. **Article 1** declares Pakistan a **federal republic** comprising provinces, AJK, and such territories as may be admitted. **Articles 227–231** define the Islamic provisions; the Objectives Resolution is reproduced as a substantive part of the Constitution.

#### Federation and Parliament

Pakistan follows a **federal parliamentary system**. The federation consists of **four provinces**, AJK, Gilgit-Baltistan, and ICT. Parliament has two chambers:

| House | Composition | Total Seats | Election Mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| National Assembly | General seats + reserved (women, minorities) | **342** | Direct vote (adult franchise) |
| Senate | Equal provincial representation | **96** | Indirect vote by provincial assemblies |

The PM and cabinet are responsible to the National Assembly; the President acts on advice of the PM under **Article 48**.

#### Judiciary and Rights

The **Supreme Court of Pakistan** heads an independent judiciary; **High Courts** operate in each province, with subordinate courts below. Judges are appointed through the **Judicial Commission of Pakistan (JCP)**. **Part II, Chapter 1 (Articles 8–28)** enshrines fundamental rights — equality, free speech, religious freedom, and the right to move freely.

#### Common Exam Traps
> Candidates lose marks by calling Pakistan "presidential" or citing the 1956 Constitution as current.

| Trap | Correct Answer |
|---|---|
| Pakistan is a presidential system | Parliamentary federal republic |
| Constitution adopted in 1962 | Current Constitution is 1973 |
| National Assembly has 96 seats | NA = 342, Senate = 96 |

#### Amendment Mechanics and Key Reforms

Article 239 allows constitutional amendment by a **two-thirds majority of both houses**; since 1973, **twenty-five amendments** have been ratified. The **18th Amendment (2010)** repealed the concurrent list and devolved 18 ministries (including health and education) to provinces, reshaping Centre–Province fiscal relations under the **7th NFC Award (2009)**. The **19th Amendment (2010–11)** reformed judicial appointments through the JCP, replacing earlier executive control.

#### Edge Cases and Office Holders

The **caretaker setup** under Article 224 governs the interim period between assembly dissolution and elections, headed by a caretaker PM and provincial chief ministers. The **Attorney General for Pakistan** advises federal government on legal matters (Article 166). Citizenship is governed by the **Pakistan Citizenship Act 1951**; the **2000 amendment** permits **dual citizenship** with listed countries (Canada, UK, USA among others).
