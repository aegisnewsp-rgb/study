# Alkenes and Alkynes: Properties and Reactions

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

- **Alkenes** contain a C=C double bond (sp² carbons, general formula CₙH₂ₙ for non-cyclic mono-enes); **alkynes** contain a C≡C triple bond (sp carbons, formula CₙH₂ₙ₋₂).
- The π bond comes from sideways overlap of unhybridised p-orbitals, making both classes **nucleophile-rich** and highly reactive toward **electrophilic addition**.
- **Markovnikov's rule**: H of HX adds to the carbon already bearing more H's, because the reaction proceeds through the more stable carbocation.
- **Anti-Markovnikov** addition of HBr occurs only with **peroxides** (Kharasch radical effect); it does not work cleanly with HCl or HI.
- **Degree of unsaturation** = (2C + 2 + N − H − X) / 2; oxygens are ignored. Each double bond or ring counts as 1; a triple bond counts as 2.
- **Terminal alkyne** C–H is unusually acidic (pKa ≈ 25) compared with alkene (≈44) and alkane (≈50) C–H; NaNH₂ deprotonates it to an **acetylide** anion.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Hybridisation and Geometry

Each doubly-bonded carbon in an alkene is sp² hybridised with **trigonal-planar** geometry (~120° bond angles); each triply-bonded carbon in an alkyne is sp hybridised with **linear** geometry (180°). The remaining unhybridised p-orbitals overlap sideways to form the reactive π bond that sits above and below the σ framework.

#### Electrophilic Addition: Markovnikov and Anti-Markovnikov

Electrophilic addition is the defining reaction class. With HX, H₂O/H⁺, or HOX, Markovnikov's rule predicts that the electrophile (H⁺) attaches to the carbon with more hydrogens, generating the **more substituted carbocation** intermediate.

| Reagent | Conditions | Regiochemistry | Stereochemistry |
|---|---|---|---|
| HBr | No peroxide | Markovnikov | — |
| HBr | ROOR (peroxide) | Anti-Markovnikov | — |
| H₂O | H₂SO₄ catalyst | Markovnikov | — |
| BH₃ then H₂O₂/NaOH | Hydroboration–oxidation | Anti-Markovnikov | Syn |
| Hg(OAc)₂/H₂O then NaBH₄ | Oxymercuration–demercuration | Markovnikov | Anti |
| Br₂ | CCl₄ | Adds across π bond | Anti |
| H₂ | Pt, Pd, or Ni | Saturated product | Syn |

#### Alkyne Acidity and Acetylides

sp hybridisation concentrates electron density closer to the nucleus, stabilising the conjugate base. **NaNH₂** (conjugate acid NH₃, pKa ≈ 38) deprotonates a terminal alkyne (pKa ≈ 25) but not an alkene (pKa ≈ 44). The resulting **acetylide** is a strong carbon nucleophile used in alkylation (Sₙ2 with primary alkyl halides) and in the **silver/ammonia test** (Ag(NH₃)₂⁺) that distinguishes terminal alkynes from internal ones.

#### HAAD Question Patterns

HAAD Chemistry tests typically ask you to (1) compute **degrees of unsaturation** from a molecular formula, (2) identify the functional group that fits CₙH₂ₙ or CₙH₂ₙ₋₂, (3) predict the major product given a specific reagent set, and (4) recognise stereochemical descriptors (**syn** vs **anti**) embedded in a mechanism.

> Exam trap: HBr with peroxides is anti-Markovnikov; HBr alone is Markovnikov. The same reagent pair flips outcome — always read conditions.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Ozonolysis and Oxidative Cleavage

Ozonolysis cleaves every C=C into two carbonyl groups. The **workup** decides the oxidation level: **reductive** (Zn/H₂O or Me₂S) gives aldehydes or ketones; **oxidative** (H₂O₂) oxidises any aldehyde fragment to a carboxylic acid. Alkynes under ozonolysis give two carboxylic acids directly, because the intermediate diketone cannot be isolated. Trace the carbon skeleton forward and backward — product carbonyls can be reassembled to deduce the original alkene.

#### Heat of Hydrogenation and Relative Stability

Catalytic hydrogenation is **exothermic**; the released heat (ΔH) measures π-bond energy. More substituted alkenes release less heat per C=C because alkyl groups stabilise the alkene through hyperconjugation — **tetrasubstituted < trisubstituted < disubstituted < monosubstituted < ethene** in ΔH. Cis alkenes are less stable than their trans isomers (van der Waals strain), so cis ΔH is slightly larger.

#### Stereochemical Edge Cases

- **Syn additions** (H₂/Pt, hydroboration–oxidation, OsO₄) place both new bonds on the same face.
- **Anti additions** (Br₂, oxymercuration) place the new bonds on opposite faces; the product stereochemistry depends on alkene geometry (E/Z).
- Carbocation intermediates in Markovnikov hydration can **rearrange** (hydride or methyl shift) toward a more stable cation, whereas hydroboration–oxidation does not rearrange because no cation forms.

#### Worked Micro-Example

Predict the product of 1-methylcyclohexene treated with (a) HBr in CCl₄, then (b) HBr with benzoyl peroxide.

- (a) Markovnikov → Br attaches to C-1 (tertiary carbon, more stable carbocation), giving **1-bromo-1-methylcyclohexane**.
- (b) Anti-Markovnikov → Br attaches to C-2, giving **1-bromo-2-methylcyclohexane**.

> Common mistake: applying the peroxide effect to HCl — it fails because the H–Cl bond is too strong to break homolytically under typical radical-chain conditions.
