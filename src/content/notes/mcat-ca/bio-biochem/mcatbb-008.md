# Musculoskeletal, Immune and Reproductive Systems — MCAT Bio/Biochem

The musculoskeletal, immune, and reproductive systems form part of AAMC Foundational Concept 4 of the Bio/Biochem section. The MCAT tests skeletal muscle contraction, the sarcomere, calcium regulation, innate and adaptive immunity, antibody structure and function, hormonal control of reproduction, and the menstrual cycle. These topics connect cellular biology to whole-organism physiology.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### Core concepts

- **Sarcomere:** the contractile unit of skeletal muscle. Contains thin filaments (actin) and thick filaments (myosin). The sliding-filament model explains contraction.
- **Calcium regulation:** the sarcoplasmic reticulum releases Ca²⁺ in response to an action potential. Ca²⁺ binds troponin, exposing the myosin-binding sites on actin.
- **Innate immunity:** non-specific defence. Barriers (skin, mucosa), phagocytes (neutrophils, macrophages), inflammation, complement proteins, natural killer cells.
- **Adaptive immunity:** specific defence. T cells (cell-mediated); B cells (humoral, produce antibodies). Generates immunological memory.
- **Antibodies (immunoglobulins):** Y-shaped proteins. Five classes: IgG, IgM, IgA, IgD, IgE.
- **Reproductive hormones:** males (testosterone, FSH, LH); females (oestrogen, progesterone, FSH, LH, hCG).
- **Menstrual cycle:** follicular phase (oestrogen rises, LH surge triggers ovulation); luteal phase (progesterone rises, prepares the endometrium).

#### One-pass drill

1. Identify the system: musculoskeletal, immune, or reproductive.
2. Recall the key structures and regulatory molecules.
3. Apply to the specific scenario.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing actin and myosin. Actin is thin; myosin is thick.
- Treating all T cells the same. Helper T cells (CD4⁺) coordinate the immune response; cytotoxic T cells (CD8⁺) kill infected cells.
- Forgetting that hCG is the hormone detected in pregnancy tests.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Skeletal muscle contraction

The MCAT tests the sliding-filament model:

- **Resting state:** tropomyosin covers the myosin-binding sites on actin. The muscle is relaxed.
- **Excitation:** an action potential travels along the sarcolemma and down the T-tubules. The sarcoplasmic reticulum releases Ca²⁺.
- **Contraction:** Ca²⁺ binds troponin, which shifts tropomyosin, exposing the myosin-binding sites. Myosin heads bind actin and pull (power stroke), shortening the sarcomere.
- **Relaxation:** Ca²⁺ is pumped back into the sarcoplasmic reticulum. Tropomyosin covers the binding sites again.

ATP is required for both contraction (myosin ATPase) and relaxation (Ca²⁺ pump).

#### Immune system

**Innate immunity:** non-specific. Barriers (skin, mucosa, lysozyme), phagocytes (neutrophils arrive first; macrophages arrive later), inflammation (redness, swelling, heat, pain), complement proteins (lyse bacteria, opsonise, recruit immune cells), natural killer cells (kill virus-infected cells and tumour cells).

**Adaptive immunity:** specific. T cells mature in the thymus; B cells mature in the bone marrow.

- **Helper T cells (CD4⁺):** coordinate the immune response by releasing cytokines. Target of HIV.
- **Cytotoxic T cells (CD8⁺):** kill infected cells by recognising antigen-MHC complexes.
- **B cells:** produce antibodies. On activation by helper T cells, B cells proliferate and differentiate into plasma cells (antibody factories) and memory B cells.

**Antibodies:** Y-shaped proteins with two heavy chains and two light chains. The variable region binds antigen; the constant region determines the antibody class.

- IgG: most abundant; crosses the placenta; opsonisation, neutralisation, complement activation.
- IgM: first antibody produced; pentamer; strong complement activator.
- IgA: found in secretions (saliva, tears, breast milk); mucosal immunity.
- IgE: involved in allergic reactions; binds mast cells and basophils.
- IgD: function unclear; involved in B cell activation.

#### Reproductive system

**Male:** testes produce sperm and testosterone. FSH stimulates spermatogenesis; LH stimulates testosterone production. Testosterone promotes secondary sexual characteristics.

**Female:** ovaries produce ova and hormones (oestrogen, progesterone). The menstrual cycle:

- **Follicular phase (days 1-14):** FSH stimulates follicle development; oestrogen rises, suppressing FSH (negative feedback). At mid-cycle, the LH surge triggers ovulation.
- **Luteal phase (days 15-28):** the corpus luteum produces progesterone, which prepares the endometrium for implantation. If pregnancy does not occur, the corpus luteum degenerates, progesterone falls, and menstruation begins.

**Pregnancy:** hCG (human chorionic gonadotropin) is produced by the embryo and maintains the corpus luteum. hCG is the hormone detected by pregnancy tests.

#### Practice routine

- Take 5 muscle items. Identify the molecules (actin, myosin, troponin, tropomyosin, Ca²⁺) and the steps.
- Take 5 immune items. Identify the cell type (B, T helper, T cytotoxic, NK, macrophage) and the function.
- Take 5 antibody items. Identify the class (IgG, IgM, IgA, IgE, IgD) and the function.
- Take 5 reproductive items. Identify the hormone (FSH, LH, oestrogen, progesterone, hCG, testosterone) and the effect.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How this topic connects to the other Bio/Biochem topics

- **Cell signalling** underlies hormone action and immune cell communication.
- **Protein structure** is critical for antibody function.
- **Genetics** underlies immune diversity (VDJ recombination, MHC polymorphism).

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"Myosin and actin are the same."** Myosin is the thick filament; actin is the thin filament. They slide past each other during contraction.
- **"Antibodies kill bacteria directly."** Antibodies bind to bacteria and mark them for destruction (opsonisation, complement activation). Phagocytes do the killing.
- **"LH and FSH do the same thing."** LH triggers ovulation and stimulates testosterone production; FSH stimulates follicle development and spermatogenesis.

#### Exam technique

- For muscle items, trace the steps from action potential to contraction.
- For immune items, identify the cell type by its marker (CD4⁺, CD8⁺, etc.) and function.
- For antibody items, identify the class by its location and function.
- For reproductive items, identify the hormone and the phase of the cycle.
