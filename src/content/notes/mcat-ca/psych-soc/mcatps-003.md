# Learning, Motivation and Emotion — MCAT Psych/Soc

Learning, motivation, and emotion form part of AAMC Foundational Concept 6 of the Psych/Soc section. The MCAT tests classical and operant conditioning, observational learning, cognitive learning, reinforcement schedules, motivation theories, emotion theories, and stress responses. These topics underpin many clinical and educational passages on behaviour change.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### Core concepts

- **Classical conditioning:** learning through association of two stimuli. Unconditioned stimulus (UCS) → unconditioned response (UCR); conditioned stimulus (CS) → conditioned response (CR).
- **Operant conditioning:** learning through consequences (reinforcement or punishment). Reinforcement increases behaviour; punishment decreases it.
- **Reinforcement schedules:** fixed ratio, variable ratio, fixed interval, variable interval.
- **Observational learning:** learning by watching others (Bandura's Bobo doll experiment).
- **Cognitive learning:** learning through mental processes (insight, latent learning).
- **Motivation:** drive reduction, arousal, incentive, Maslow's hierarchy.
- **Emotion:** James-Lange (physiological arousal comes first), Cannon-Bard (arousal and emotion occur simultaneously), Schachter-Singer (cognition labels arousal).
- **Stress:** GAS (general adaptation syndrome) — alarm, resistance, exhaustion. HPA axis.

#### One-pass drill

1. Identify the topic: learning, motivation, or emotion.
2. Recall the relevant principle.
3. Apply to the specific scenario.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing reinforcement with punishment. Reinforcement increases behaviour; punishment decreases it. Both can be positive or negative.
- Treating all reinforcement schedules as equivalent. Variable schedules produce more resistant behaviour than fixed schedules.
- Confusing the James-Lange and Cannon-Bard theories.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Classical conditioning

- **Acquisition:** the CS is repeatedly paired with the UCS until the CS elicits the CR.
- **Extinction:** the CS is repeatedly presented without the UCS; the CR weakens.
- **Spontaneous recovery:** after extinction, the CR reappears after a rest period.
- **Stimulus generalisation:** stimuli similar to the CS elicit the CR.
- **Stimulus discrimination:** the organism learns to distinguish the CS from similar stimuli.
- **Higher-order conditioning:** a new CS is paired with the original CS to elicit the CR.

**Applications:** phobias, taste aversion, advertising, drug tolerance.

#### Operant conditioning

- **Positive reinforcement:** adding a pleasant stimulus to increase behaviour.
- **Negative reinforcement:** removing an unpleasant stimulus to increase behaviour.
- **Positive punishment:** adding an unpleasant stimulus to decrease behaviour.
- **Negative punishment:** removing a pleasant stimulus to decrease behaviour.

**Reinforcement schedules:**
- Fixed ratio: reward after a fixed number of responses (piecework pay).
- Variable ratio: reward after a variable number of responses (gambling).
- Fixed interval: reward after a fixed time (paycheque).
- Variable interval: reward after a variable time (fishing).

Variable schedules produce the most resistant behaviour (most resistant to extinction).

#### Observational and cognitive learning

**Observational learning (Bandura):** learning by watching a model. Four steps: attention, retention, reproduction, motivation.

**Cognitive learning:** learning through mental processes. Tolman's latent learning experiment showed that rats learn a maze even without reinforcement, demonstrating that learning can occur without immediate reward.

#### Motivation theories

- **Drive reduction:** motivation arises from biological needs (hunger, thirst) that create drives; behaviour reduces the drive.
- **Arousal theory:** motivation arises from the need to maintain an optimal level of arousal. Yerkes-Dodson law: performance peaks at moderate arousal.
- **Incentive theory:** motivation arises from external incentives (rewards).
- **Maslow's hierarchy:** physiological, safety, love/belonging, esteem, self-actualisation. Lower needs must be met before higher needs motivate.

#### Emotion theories

- **James-Lange:** stimulus → physiological arousal → emotion. We feel afraid because we tremble.
- **Cannon-Bard:** stimulus → simultaneous arousal and emotion. We tremble and feel afraid at the same time.
- **Schachter-Singer (two-factor):** stimulus → arousal → cognitive label → emotion. The label depends on context.

#### Stress

**GAS (Selye):** alarm (fight-or-flight), resistance (body adapts), exhaustion (resources depleted).

**HPA axis:** hypothalamus releases CRH → pituitary releases ACTH → adrenal cortex releases cortisol. Cortisol increases blood glucose and suppresses the immune system.

#### Practice routine

- Take 5 classical conditioning items. Identify the UCS, UCR, CS, CR.
- Take 5 operant conditioning items. Distinguish reinforcement from punishment; identify the schedule.
- Take 5 motivation items. Identify the theory (drive reduction, arousal, incentive, Maslow).
- Take 5 emotion items. Identify the theory (James-Lange, Cannon-Bard, Schachter-Singer).
- Take 5 stress items. Identify the GAS stage and the HPA axis hormones.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How this topic connects to the other Psych/Soc topics

- **Cognition** underlies cognitive learning (insight, latent learning).
- **Biology of behaviour** provides the neural basis of motivation, emotion, and stress (HPA axis, amygdala, prefrontal cortex).
- **Psychopathology** includes anxiety disorders (classical conditioning), addiction (operant conditioning), and stress-related disorders.

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"Negative reinforcement is punishment."** Negative reinforcement removes an unpleasant stimulus and increases behaviour; punishment decreases behaviour.
- **"Fixed schedules produce the most resistant behaviour."** Variable schedules produce more resistant behaviour than fixed schedules.
- **"Cortisol is calming."** Cortisol is a stress hormone that mobilises energy but suppresses the immune system over time.

#### Exam technique

- For learning items, identify the type of conditioning and the schedule.
- For motivation items, identify the theory that best fits the scenario.
- For emotion items, identify the theory that matches the sequence of events.
- For stress items, identify the GAS stage and the HPA axis hormones.
