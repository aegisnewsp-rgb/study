# Share Capital & Debentures

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Share capital** is the money a company raises by issuing shares — every share represents a slice of ownership in the company. **Debentures**, defined in Section 2(30) of the Companies Act, 2013, are debt instruments that acknowledge a loan repayable with interest as per the debenture trust deed. The two sit at opposite ends of the capital structure: shareholders are owners with voting rights, debenture-holders are creditors with a fixed coupon.

- **Authorised capital** (Section 2(8)) is the ceiling fixed in the Memorandum; **issued, subscribed, called-up and paid-up** are progressively narrower sub-sets.
- Equity and preference shares are classified under Section 43; preference shares carry four optional attributes (cumulative, participating, convertible, redeemable).
- Calls in arrears attract **10% p.a. interest** under Section 51; shares can be **forfeited** under Section 53 for non-payment.
- Debentures are redeemed by creating a **Debenture Redemption Reserve (DRR)** under Section 71(4) read with Rule 18 — currently 25% for NBFCs, 10% for specified infrastructure companies, and **NIL** for other listed companies after the 2014 amendment.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Classification under Section 43

Section 43(1)(a) defines **equity share capital** as shares with uniform voting and dividend rights. Section 43(1)(b) defines **preference share capital** as shares carrying a preferential right to dividend and to repayment of capital on winding-up. Preference shares can stack up to four attributes: cumulative/non-cumulative, participating/non-participating, convertible/non-convertible, and redeemable/irredeemable.

| Type of Capital | Defined under | Key feature |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Authorised | Section 2(8) | Maximum ceiling in the Memorandum |
| Issued | Section 2(60) | Offered to the public or private placement |
| Subscribed | Section 2(86) | Actually accepted by applicants |
| Called-up | Section 2(15) | Portion of face value demanded by the company |
| Paid-up | Section 2(64) | Amount actually received on called-up portion |

#### Calls in Arrears and Forfeiture

When a shareholder fails to pay a call, the unpaid amount becomes **Calls in Arrears** under Section 51. The company charges interest at 10% per annum unless the Articles prescribe otherwise. The formula is:

#### Interest = Calls in Arrears × 10% × (Days delayed ÷ 365)

If the default continues, the Board can **forfeit** the shares under Section 53 by passing a resolution. On forfeiture, the company must refund the amount already received (after adjusting any arrears) and the shares revert to the pool of authorised but unissued capital. Re-issue of forfeited shares is permitted at a discount, but the discount cannot exceed the amount actually received on those shares.

#### Share Premium and Discount

Section 52 allows shares to be issued at a **premium**, calculated as:

#### Share Premium = (Issue Price − Face Value) × Number of Shares Issued

The premium collected forms part of **Securities Premium Account** and can be utilised only for the limited purposes listed in Section 52(2) — write-off of issue expenses, buy-back under Section 68, bonus issue under Section 63, etc. Issuing shares at a **discount is prohibited** under Section 53, save for the specific case of forfeited shares re-issued at a discount capped by the amount received.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Debentures: Mechanics and DRR

A debenture is governed by a **Debenture Trust Deed** under Section 71(1)–(3) and a **Debenture Trustee** must be appointed before allotment. The trustee protects the collective interest of debenture-holders and ensures the company creates adequate security.

Redemption requires creation of a **DRR** out of profits available for dividend, under Section 71(4) read with Rule 18 of the Companies (Share Capital and Debentures) Rules, 2014. The current DRR rates are:

| Company category | DRR rate (of nominal value of debentures) |
| --- | --- |
| NBFCs | 25% |
| Infrastructure companies per Rule 18(1)(b) | 10% |
| Other listed companies | NIL (post-2014 amendment) |

A common trap is applying a uniform 25% across the board. Unlisted companies other than NBFCs must also follow the rate applicable to their category, not a blanket figure.

#### Distinction on Winding-Up Priority

Section 327 fixes the payment order on winding-up: secured creditors (including debenture-holders with a charge) first, then preferential creditors, then unsecured creditors, then preference shareholders, and finally equity shareholders. This is why **secured debentures rank above preference shares** despite preference shares being capital.

#### Sweaty Equity, ESOP and Bonus Shares

1. **Sweat equity** under Section 54 is issued to directors or employees for non-cash consideration at a price fixed by a registered valuer — discount is barred.
2. **ESOP** under Section 62(1)(b) is offered only to permanent employees, and the issue price can be at a discount but not below the SEBI-disclosed formula.
3. **Bonus shares** under Section 63 are issued by capitalising free reserves or securities premium; the issuing shares must be fully paid-up, and capital redemption reserve cannot be used.

#### Practice Prompts

1. ABC Ltd issued 10,000 equity shares of ₹100 each at a premium of ₹20, payable as: application ₹30, allotment ₹50 (including premium), first call ₹40. An applicant for 200 shares failed to pay the allotment money. Compute calls in arrears and the 10% interest for 6 months.
2. PQR Ltd, an NBFC, issued ₹5 crore secured redeemable debentures. Calculate the DRR to be created and explain the investment restrictions under Rule 18(2) regarding deposits and bank guarantees.
