# Statement and Assumptions

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Statement and Assumptions** is a Logical Reasoning sub-type in the LAT Analytical Reasoning section (4% of the paper). A *statement* is a declared fact or opinion, and an *assumption* is an unstated premise that **must** be true for the statement to hold. The task is to pick the assumption the speaker is *necessarily* taking for granted.

- An assumption is **presupposed**, not concluded or inferred.
- Test it by **negating** the option: if NOT-P kills the statement, P is the right assumption.
- Watch trigger words in the statement: **"should", "must", "ought to", "let us"** signal a hidden value assumption.
- Distinguish assumption from **course of action** (prescriptive) and **conclusion** (derived).

| Signal in option | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| "It is desirable that…" | Likely course of action — reject |
| "Therefore, X happens." | Conclusion, not assumption — reject |
| "X is taken to be true / valid." | Necessary assumption — keep |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Definition and Logical Position

An **assumption** sits *between* the statement and any conclusion drawn from it. The logical chain is:

#### Statement → Assumption(s) → Inference / Conclusion

The assumption is never voiced; the speaker simply acts as though it is true. If you remove the assumption, the statement collapses into something unjustified or meaningless. This is why LAT answer keys favour options that are *unavoidable*, not merely *plausible*.

#### Necessary vs Sufficient Assumption

- A **necessary assumption** is one without which the statement cannot stand. Negate it and the argument breaks.
- A **sufficient assumption** would, by itself, prove the statement — these rarely appear as correct options because the stem only asks what is *taken for granted*.

#### Typical LAT Question Format

Three to five options follow a single statement. Candidates must select the one assumption that is *implicitly relied upon*. Usually one option restates the statement (trap), one is a course of action (trap), one is weakly related, and one is the necessary hidden premise.

#### Distinctions You Must Lock In

| Concept | What it is | Marker words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Assumption | Unstated belief presupposed | taken for granted, presumed |
| Conclusion | Drawn from the assumption | therefore, hence, so |
| Course of action | What *should* be done | recommend, ought to do |
| Inference | Deduced consequence | implies, suggests |

- A restatement of the statement in different words is **not** an assumption.
- A *possible* or *likely* belief is **not yet necessary** — push for the must-be-true version.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### The Negation Test (Mechanism)

Apply it in two passes:

1. Identify candidate assumption P in the option.
2. Form "NOT-P" and ask: does the original statement still make sense?
3. If NOT-P leaves the statement intact or even stronger, P is **not** an assumption.
4. If NOT-P destroys the reasoning link, P is the **necessary** assumption.

This single mechanism settles most LAT disputes because it forces a binary answer instead of a vibes-based guess.

#### Common Traps

- **Paraphrase trap** — option rewords the statement verbatim in assertive form. Eliminate.
- **Action trap** — option recommends a step (e.g., "the government should…"). Assumptions describe beliefs, not prescriptions.
- **Over-generalisation trap** — option broadens the statement's scope beyond what is actually presupposed.
- **Trigger-word trap** — "should", "must", "ought to" in the *statement* often hide a value assumption about what is desirable; do not confuse them with action options.

#### Edge Cases

- Statements framed as **commands** ("Let us plant trees") hide an assumption that the activity is worthwhile.
- **Comparative** statements ("X is better than Y") assume a shared criterion of comparison exists.
- **Conditional** statements ("If you work hard, you will pass") assume hard work has some causal link to passing.

#### Worked Micro-Example

Statement: *"The college should install CCTV cameras in every classroom."*
Which is the necessary assumption?
- (A) CCTV installation will reduce indiscipline.  ← necessary
- (B) CCTV cameras are affordable for the college.  ← desirable, not assumed
- (C) Students object to being monitored.  ← irrelevant
- (D) The principal should approve the budget.  ← course of action

**Why A**: Negate it — "CCTV will *not* reduce indiscipline." The recommendation becomes pointless, so A must be true for the statement to make sense.

#### LAT-Specific Strategy

The Analytical Reasoning section carries 4% weight, and Statement–Assumption items cluster with related logical-reasoning questions (conclusions, arguments, course of action). Allocate roughly **45 seconds per item**, eliminate paraphrase and action options first, then run the negation test on the remaining one or two candidates.

#### Practice Prompts

1. Statement: *"We should switch to online classes during heavy smog."* Find the necessary assumption and reject two distractors using the distinctions table.
2. Statement: *"Reading newspapers makes citizens more responsible."* Apply the negation test to each option before selecting.
