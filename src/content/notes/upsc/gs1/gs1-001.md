# World History

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

World History in UPSC GS1 covers global developments from the **mid-18th century** onward — industrialisation, revolutions, world wars, the Cold War, decolonisation, and post-1991 global order.

- **Industrial Revolution (Britain, 1760s)** introduced the factory system, steam power, and capitalism; its social fallout shaped 19th-century politics.
- **French Revolution (1789)** dismantled the *Ancien Régime*, exported nationalism through the Napoleonic Wars, and inspired the Congress of Vienna (1815).
- **World War I (1914–18)** and the **Treaty of Versailles (1919)** created the League of Nations and sowed the seeds of WWII.
- **Cold War (1947–1991)** was an ideological US–USSR rivalry fought through alliances (NATO 1949, Warsaw Pact 1955), Truman Doctrine, Marshall Plan, and proxy wars (Korea, Vietnam, Cuba).
- **Decolonisation (1947–1980s)** birthed the Non-Aligned Movement (Belgrade, 1961) and reshaped Asia and Africa.
- **Post-Cold War world** is defined by unipolarity, US invasion of Iraq, 9/11 and the War on Terror, the rise of China, globalisation, and the Arab Spring (2011).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

World History as a UPSC subject is a **causal chain**, not a list of dates. Every major event is linked to the previous one: industrialisation funded imperialism, imperialism caused WWI friction, WWI's peace bred WWII, WWII produced the Cold War, and the Cold War's end reshaped the present.

#### Industrial Revolution and Its World
The transition from agrarian to factory production began in **Britain around 1760**, driven by coal, steam (James Watt, 1769), and colonial raw materials. It produced **capitalism**, an urban working class, and the demand for new markets — the launchpad for 19th-century **imperialism**.

#### Revolutions that redrew the map
- **American Revolution (1776)**: 13 colonies → federal republic under a written Constitution (1787).
- **French Revolution (1789)**: Enlightenment ideas (Rousseau's *Social Contract*, Montesquieu's separation of powers) ended monarchy; **Napoleon** exported these via wars; **Congress of Vienna (1815)** restored balance-of-power politics.
- **Russian Revolution (1917)**: Bolshevik takeover under Lenin created the USSR — the ideological challenger to liberal capitalism for the next 74 years.

#### World War I — causes and consequences
The **MAIN** causes — **M**ilitarism, **A**lliances (Triple Entente vs Triple Alliance), **I**mperialism, **N**ationalism — converged on the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand (Sarajevo, 28 June 1914). The **Treaty of Versailles (1919)** imposed war guilt and reparations on Germany, dismantled its empire, and birthed the **League of Nations**. Its structural weaknesses (no US membership, no enforcement army) guaranteed its failure.

#### World War II — course and consequences
Triggered by Axis aggression: Japan's Manchuria (1931), Italy's Ethiopia (1935), Germany's **Anschluss** (1938), **Munich Pact** (1938), and **invasion of Poland (1 September 1939)**. Theaters spanned Europe, North Africa, and the Pacific. The **Yalta** and **Potsdam (1945)** conferences partitioned Germany and created the UN. Outcomes: decolonisation, Cold War bipolarity, and the **Bretton Woods system** (IMF, World Bank, 1944).

#### Cold War architecture
| Bloc | USA-led West | USSR-led East |
|---|---|---|
| Military | NATO (1949) | Warsaw Pact (1955) |
| Economic | Marshall Plan (1948), IMF, World Bank | COMECON (1949) |
| Doctrine | Truman Doctrine (1947) | Brezhnev Doctrine (1968) |
| Crises | Cuba (1962), Korea (1950–53), Vietnam | Same + Afghanistan (1979) |

The **Non-Aligned Movement (1961, Belgrade)** under Nehru, Nasser, and Tito offered a Third Path — a recurring UPSC angle.

> **Trap:** Truman Doctrine ≠ Marshall Plan. The first is political-military **containment**; the second is **economic** reconstruction of Western Europe.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Decolonisation as a structural shift
After 1945, the Atlantic Charter's promise of self-determination accelerated decolonisation: **India (1947)**, Indonesia (1949), Vietnam (1954), Ghana (1957), and the **1960s African Year** (17 nations in 1960 alone). The **redrawal of national boundaries** often followed colonial-era artificial lines (e.g., the Durand Line, the partition of Africa at the 1884–85 **Berlin Conference**), generating post-independence conflicts that persist today — Kashmir, Palestine, Rwanda, Sudan.

#### Post-Cold War world order
The **fall of the Berlin Wall (1989)** and **dissolution of the USSR (26 Dec 1991)** ended bipolarity. The 1990s appeared unipolar, but three trends reshaped the 21st century:
- **Rise of China**: WTO accession (2001) and post-2008 economic statecraft.
- **Resurgent Russia**: NATO expansion provoked the **Russia–Ukraine war (2022–present)**.
- **Asymmetric conflicts**: 9/11 (2001), the Global War on Terror, the **Arab Spring (2011)**, and the Israel–Gaza crisis.

#### Common analytical mistakes
1. Treating WWI and WWII as having identical causes — they overlap on nationalism and imperialism, but WWII is inseparable from the **failure of appeasement** and **Versailles' punitive terms**.
2. Reading the Cold War as a direct war — it was a **systemic rivalry** fought through proxies, alliances, and ideology.
3. Ignoring India's NAM role — UPSC rewards linking India's foreign policy (e.g., 1971 treaty, nuclear policy) to Cold War geometry.

#### Worked linkage example
**Q:** Trace how the Industrial Revolution shaped 20th-century geopolitics.
**Chain:** Factory surplus → search for markets & raw materials → **Scramble for Africa (1884–85 Berlin Conference)** → colonial rivalries between Britain, France, Germany → friction within the alliance system → **WWI (1914)** → Versailles bitterness → economic depression in Germany → Nazi rise → **WWII (1939)** → Cold War → decolonisation.

#### Practice prompts
1. "The Cold War never ended; it merely mutated." Critically examine with examples from 1991–2026.
2. Compare the unification of Italy and Germany: actors, methods, and consequences.
