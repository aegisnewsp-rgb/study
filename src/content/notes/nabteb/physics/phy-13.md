# Photoelectric Effect and Atomic Physics

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The **photoelectric effect** is the ejection of electrons from a metal surface when light of frequency above a critical **threshold frequency f₀** strikes it. Einstein's equation links photon energy to the kinetic energy of the emitted electron:

**KE_max = hf − φ**, where h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s, f is incident frequency in Hz, and φ is the **work function** in joules.

- **Stopping potential**: eVₛ = KE_max; Vₛ increases linearly with f and is independent of light intensity.
- **Bohr energy levels** of hydrogen: Eₙ = −13.6 eV / n², with n = 1 (ground), n ≥ 2 (excited).
- **Rydberg formula**: 1/λ = R_H (1/n₁² − 1/n₂²), R_H = 1.097 × 10⁷ m⁻¹, used for series identification.

NABTEB tests this 4% topic as short theory plus one-step numericals on KE_max or Vₛ, and series identification from given n₁, n₂.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Einstein's Photoelectric Equation

A single photon transfers its entire energy **hf** to one bound electron. If hf exceeds the **work function φ** (the minimum binding energy), the electron escapes with maximum kinetic energy given by:

**KE_max = hf − φ = h(f − f₀)**, where the **threshold frequency f₀ = φ / h**.

Two consequences fall directly out: below f₀ no photoelectrons emerge no matter how bright the source, and the stopping potential Vₛ, which just halts the fastest electrons (so eVₛ = KE_max), is a linear function of f with slope h/e and vertical intercept −φ/e. Plotting Vₛ against f and reading the gradient is a standard NABTEB graphical item.

#### Threshold, Work Function and Units

Because 1 eV = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J, always convert φ between joules and electron-volts before substituting. Typical values span roughly 2–6 eV (e.g., caesium ≈ 2.1 eV, zinc ≈ 4.3 eV).

#### Hydrogen Atom and Spectral Series

Bohr's postulates restrict the electron to orbits with angular momentum **mvᵣ = nħ**, producing discrete energies Eₙ = −13.6/n² eV. A photon is emitted (or absorbed) on transition between levels, with wavelength given by the **Rydberg formula**:

1/λ = R_H (1/n₁² − 1/n₂²), n₂ > n₁.

#### Series Identification Table

| Series | Final level n₁ | Region | Typical line |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lyman | 1 | Ultraviolet | 121.6 nm |
| Balmer | 2 | Visible | 656.3 nm (Hα) |
| Paschen | 3 | Infrared | 1875 nm |
| Brackett | 4 | Infrared | 4051 nm |
| Pfund | 5 | Infrared | 7458 nm |

> **Trap:** Increasing intensity only increases the *number* of photoelectrons per second; their KE_max depends solely on f.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### de Broglie Matter Waves and Quantization

Extending wave–particle duality to matter, **λ = h / (mv)**, and imposing that an integer number of wavelengths fit into one orbital circumference 2πr gives 2πr = nλ, which recovers Bohr's quantization mvᵣ = nħ. This shows that the angular momentum rule — not arbitrary — emerges from standing-wave conditions on the electron orbit.

#### Compton Effect and Photon Momentum

For higher-energy photons (X-rays), collisions with free electrons shift the scattered photon's wavelength by:

**Δλ = (h / m_ec) (1 − cos θ)**, where the **Compton wavelength** h/m_ec ≈ 2.43 × 10⁻¹² m. NABTEB occasionally asks why the photoelectric model assumes an electron is bound while the Compton model treats it as free — the answer rests on photon energy relative to the binding energy.

#### Worked Numerical

Light of wavelength 400 nm falls on sodium (φ = 2.28 eV). Find (a) KE_max in eV, (b) Vₛ.

- hf = (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3 × 10⁸) / (400 × 10⁻⁹) = 4.97 × 10⁻¹⁹ J = 3.11 eV.
- (a) KE_max = 3.11 − 2.28 = **0.83 eV** (≈ 1.33 × 10⁻¹⁹ J).
- (b) Vₛ = 0.83 V.

#### Common Mistakes

- Substituting **n₂ < n₁** in the Rydberg formula, producing a negative 1/λ.
- Confusing **Balmer (visible)** with **Lyman (UV)** when only the transition numbers are given.
- Treating hf as KE directly, omitting φ for sub-threshold or threshold-edge cases.

#### Practice Prompts

1. A metal has φ = 2.0 eV. Find the threshold wavelength and the stopping potential for 500 nm light.
2. Identify the series and compute the wavelength for a transition from n = 5 to n = 2 in hydrogen.
