# Upper Limb — Brachial Plexus, Axillary Artery, Shoulder Joint & Muscles of Arm and Forearm

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your FMGE exam.

The upper limb is innervated by the **brachial plexus** (ventral rami C5–T1), perfused by the **axillary artery** (continuation of subclavian at the lateral border of the 1st rib), and mobilised by the **glenohumeral (shoulder) joint** — a synovial ball-and-socket articulation reinforced by the **SITS rotator cuff**.

- **Roots (5):** C5, C6, C7, C8, T1 → unite into **3 trunks** (upper, middle, lower).
- **Divisions (6):** each trunk splits into anterior and posterior divisions → form **3 cords** (lateral, posterior, medial — named by relation to axillary artery).
- **Terminal branches (5):** Musculocutaneous, Axillary, Median, Radial, Ulnar.
- **Rotator cuff (SITS):** Supraspinatus, Infraspinatus, Teres minor, Subscapularis.
- **Arm compartments:** Anterior (flexors — biceps, brachialis, coracobrachialis) and Posterior (extensor — triceps), supplied by musculocutaneous and radial nerves respectively.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** "**R2-D2-C3-B5**" — **R**oots (5), **D**ivisions (6), **C**ords (3), **B**ranches (5). Trunks = 3. For cord–branch relation remember "**L**ateral = **M**usculo**M**edian, **M**edial = **M**edian + **U**lnar, **P**osterior = **A**xillary + **R**adial" (LAMM, MMU, PAR).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months of FMGE prep time.

#### Brachial Plexus — Organisation and Key Lesions

The plexus is organised into five sequential levels. **Roots** lie between scalene muscles; **trunks** cross the posterior triangle of the neck; **divisions** lie posterior to the clavicle; **cords** surround the axillary artery; **terminal branches** supply the free upper limb.

Erb–Duchenne palsy ("waiter's tip") results from excessive shoulder abduction — traction injures **C5–C6** roots, paralyzing deltoid, supraspinatus, infraspinatus, biceps and brachioradialis. Klumpke's palsy follows upward traction on a suspended infant or thoracic outlet compression, injuring **C8–T1** and producing a **claw hand** with intrinsic muscle wasting.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix — Cords vs Branches vs Lesions

| Structure | Formed by / Origin | Key terminal branch | Motor signature injury |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lateral cord | Ant. div. of upper + middle trunks (C5–C7) | Musculocutaneous + lateral root of median | Loss of forearm flexion (C5–C6) |
| Medial cord | Ant. div. of lower trunk (C8–T1) | Ulnar + medial root of median | Claw hand (C8–T1) |
| Posterior cord | Post. div. of all three trunks (C5–T1) | Axillary + radial | Wrist drop (C7–C8) |
| Erb's point (C5–C6 junction) | Union of C5–C6 roots | — | Erb's palsy (waiter's tip) |
| Long thoracic n. | Direct from roots C5, C6, C7 | — | Winged scapula (serratus anterior) |

#### Axillary Artery — Three Parts and Six Branches

The axillary artery begins at the **lateral border of the 1st rib** and ends at the **lower border of teres major**, where it becomes the brachial artery. Pectoralis minor divides it into three parts.

| Part | Branches | Mnemonic anchor |
|---|---|---|
| 1st (proximal) | Superior thoracic (1) | **1** branch |
| 2nd (behind pec. minor) | Thoracoacromial, lateral thoracic (2) | **2** branches |
| 3rd (distal) | Subscapular, anterior + posterior circumflex humeral (3) | **3** branches |

> 📌 **Formula Check:** Clinical correlation — surgical neck fractures of the humerus risk the **posterior circumflex humeral artery** and **axillary nerve**, producing deltoid paralysis and loss of sensation over the "regimental badge" area of the lateral shoulder.

#### Shoulder (Glenohumeral) Joint and Arm Muscles

The shoulder joint sacrifices bony stability for maximal range. The **glenoid labrum** deepens the socket by ~50%, and the **SITS rotator cuff** provides dynamic stability during mid-range abduction.

- **Anterior compartment of arm** (musculocutaneous nerve, C5–C6): biceps brachii, brachialis, coracobrachialis.
- **Posterior compartment** (radial nerve, C7–C8): triceps brachii (long, lateral, medial heads).
- **Bicipital aponeurosis:** protects the underlying brachial artery and median nerve during cubital fossa venepuncture.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** A 24-year-old motorcyclist presents 4 hours after a fall with the right arm held adducted, internally rotated, forearm pronated, wrist flexed ("waiter's tip" deformity). He cannot abduct the shoulder or flex the elbow. Which is the **most likely anatomical lesion**?

**Options:** (a) Lower trunk of brachial plexus (b) Upper trunk of brachial plexus (c) Posterior cord (d) Lateral cord (e) Medial cord

#### Solution:

1. The deformity is the classic Erb–Duchenne "waiter's tip" position, produced by unopposed action of muscles supplied **below** the lesion (latissimus dorsi, pectoralis major, wrist flexors).
2. Erb's palsy follows traction on **C5–C6 roots** or the **upper trunk**.
3. Paralysed muscles: deltoid, supraspinatus, infraspinatus, biceps, brachialis, brachioradialis, supinator.
4. Therefore the answer is **(b) Upper trunk of brachial plexus** (C5–C6).

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Candidates often confuse Erb's palsy with Klumpke's palsy (C8–T1, claw hand) or wrist drop (radial nerve, posterior cord). Remember: arm **adducted** + **internally rotated** + forearm **pronated** + wrist **flexed** = Erb's; **claw hand** = Klumpke's; **wrist drop** = radial/posterior cord. Do not pick the posterior cord simply because "wrist is flexed" — flexion here is unopposed wrist flexor tone, not a radial lesion.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for FMGE aspirants on a longer study timeline.

#### Forearm Compartments — Flexor-Pronator vs Extensor-Supinator

The forearm is divided by the **interosseous membrane** and the **medial** and **lateral intermuscular septa** into anterior (flexor-pronator) and posterior (extensor-supinator) compartments, each with superficial and deep layers.

| Compartment | Superficial layer | Deep layer | Nerve supply |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anterior (flexor-pronator) | Pronator teres, FCR, Palmaris longus, FCU | FDS, FDP (lat. half), FPL, Pronator quadratus | Median (FCU = ulnar) |
| Posterior (extensor-supinator) | ECRL, ECRB, ED, EDM, ECU | Supinator, APL, EPB, EPL, EI | Radial / Posterior interosseous |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** "**2 F's are Unar**" — **F**lexor **C**arpi **U**lnaris and the medial half of **F**lexor **D**igitorum **P**rofundus are the two forearm muscles supplied by the **U**lnar nerve; everything else in the anterior compartment is median.

#### Cubital Fossa Boundaries and Content

The cubital fossa is clinically important for venepuncture and brachial pulse palpation. Its boundaries are frequently misidentified in image-based MCQs.

| Boundary | Structure | Common wrong answer |
|---|---|---|
| Roof | Skin, fascia, bicipital aponeurosis | (none commonly mistaken) |
| Floor | Brachialis (medially) + Supinator (laterally) | Biceps tendon alone |
| Lateral | Brachioradialis | Pronator teres |
| Medial | Pronator teres | FCU |
| Content (lateral → medial) | Radial n., Biceps tendon, Brachial a., Median n. | (MNemonic: **R**ead **B**eautiful **A**natomy — Radial, Biceps, Artery) |

#### Advanced Traps and Edge Cases

1. **Ulnar nerve fibres** travel via the **lateral cord** (C7) → median → medial cord before reaching the ulnar nerve. Saying "ulnar nerve = medial cord only" loses the C7 contribution that explains the sparing pattern in some ulnar lesions.
2. **Axillary nerve** is **both** motor (deltoid + teres minor) **and** sensory (superior lateral cutaneous nerve of arm). It is not purely motor.
3. **Biceps brachii** is the most powerful **supinator** of the forearm, not the prime elbow flexor; **brachialis** is the true "workhorse" elbow flexor in all positions (it's also the only pure flexor).
4. **Supraspinatus** initiates the first **15°** of abduction; deltoid takes over from **15°–90°**; trapezius + serratus anterior rotate the scapula for abduction beyond 90°.
5. **Anatomical snuffbox** is bounded by **APL + EPB** (radial side) and **EPL** (ulnar side); the **radial artery** passes through its floor — a classic MCQ image.
6. **Posterior interosseous nerve** (continuation of deep branch of radial nerve after the supinator) supplies all extensor compartment muscles distal to the elbow; injury produces **finger drop without wrist drop** because ECRL is spared.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. A patient with a midshaft humeral fracture cannot extend the wrist or fingers but still abducts the shoulder. Trace the lesion from the nerve to the cord, and explain why shoulder abduction is preserved.
2. A posterior shoulder dislocation compresses which artery against the surgical neck of the humerus, and what is the resulting sensory and motor deficit?
