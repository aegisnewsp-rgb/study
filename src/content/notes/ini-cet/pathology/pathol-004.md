# Hemodynamic Disorders

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Hemodynamic disorders** are disturbances of blood/fluid flow that compromise tissue perfusion and capillary exchange. Four cardinal processes define the topic: **hyperaemia/congestion** (increased intravascular volume), **ischaemia/infarction** (reduced arterial supply), **oedema** (increased interstitial fluid), and **shock** (circulatory collapse). **Starling forces** govern capillary fluid exchange: net flux (Jv) equals hydraulic conductivity × surface area × the net difference between capillary hydrostatic pressure (Pc) and plasma colloid oncotic pressure (πc). **Transudate** is protein-poor fluid (<30 g/L, specific gravity <1.015); **exudate** is protein-rich (>30 g/L) with inflammatory cells.

- **Pale (anaemic) infarcts** occur in solid end-arterial organs (kidney, spleen, heart); **red (haemorrhagic) infarcts** occur in loose tissues with dual blood supply (lung, intestine, testis, ovary).
- **Virchow's triad** — endothelial injury, abnormal flow, hypercoagulability — underlies thrombosis.
- **Fat embolism** triad appears 24–72 h after long-bone fracture: respiratory distress, neurological signs, petechial rash.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Oedema vs Congestion
Congestion is passive pooling of blood within dilated vessels (e.g. hepatic chronic passive congestion producing the **"nutmeg liver"** pattern). Oedema is excess fluid in the interstitium or serous cavities. **Localized oedema** follows a focal insult (DVT, inflammation, lymphatic obstruction). **Generalized oedema (anasarca)** reflects systemic disease — cardiac (raised venous pressure), renal (sodium retention, hypoalbuminaemia), hepatic (reduced albumin synthesis + portal hypertension), or nutritional (kwashiorkor, beriberi).

#### Starling Forces and Transudate/Exudate
Net transcapillary flux Jv = Lp·S·[(Pc − Pif) − σ(πc − πif)]. Raised Pc (heart failure), reduced πc (nephrotic syndrome, cirrhosis), or increased capillary permeability (inflammation) drives fluid out. **Light's criteria** distinguish pleural exudate from transudate.

| Criterion | Transudate | Exudate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pleural protein / serum protein | < 0.5 | > 0.5 |
| Pleural LDH / serum LDH | < 0.6 | > 0.6 |
| Pleural LDH (absolute) | < 2/3 ULN serum LDH | > 2/3 ULN serum LDH |
| Protein content | < 30 g/L | > 30 g/L |
| Specific gravity | < 1.015 | > 1.015 |

#### Ischaemia, Infarction, Embolism
Ischaemia is reversible hypoperfusion; infarction is coagulative necrosis (most solid organs) or liquefactive necrosis (CNS) after sustained arterial occlusion. **Embolism sources** — thromboembolism (~95% from deep leg veins), fat (long-bone fracture), air (decompression sickness, vascular lines), amniotic fluid (post-partum DIC), tumour (renal cell carcinoma invading IVC).

#### Shock Classification
Hypovolaemic (haemorrhage, burns), cardiogenic (MI, tamponade), distributive (septic — warm/early high-output state; neurogenic; anaphylactic), and obstructive (PE, tension pneumothorax). Stages progress non-progressive → progressive → irreversible, with **DIC** and multi-organ failure marking the terminal phase.

#### INI CET Traps
- Warm septic shock ≠ classic cold/clammy hypovolaemic shock (high CO, low SVR).
- Cerebral oedema has two forms: **vasogenic** (BBB breakdown) and **cytotoxic** (Na/K-ATPase failure in stroke).
- Shock kidney = **acute tubular necrosis** with hyaline/granular casts.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Reperfusion Injury and DIC
Restoration of flow paradoxically damages ischaemic tissue through a **ROS burst** (superoxide, hydroxyl radicals), intracellular **calcium paradox**, and **mitochondrial dysfunction**, producing the **no-reflow phenomenon** from endothelial swelling and leukocyte plugging. Clinically this drives post-MI arrhythmias and post-thrombolysis stunning. **DIC** is a consumptive coagulopathy triggered by sepsis, obstetric catastrophes, or malignancy; fibrin-rich microthrombi cause ischaemic injury while consumption of platelets/clotting factors produces bleeding. **Schistocytes** (fragmented RBCs) on smear reflect microangiopathic haemolytic anaemia; elevated **D-dimer** and fibrin degradation products are biochemical markers.

#### ATLS Haemorrhagic Shock Classes
ATLS quantifies blood loss and guides resuscitation.

| Class | Blood loss | Pulse | BP | Mental state |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | < 15% | < 100 | Normal | Slight anxiety |
| II | 15–30% | 100–120 | Normal/systolic ↓ | Mildly anxious |
| III | 30–40% | 120–140 | Systolic < 90 | Confused |
| IV | > 40% | > 140 / bradycardia | Marked hypotension | Lethargic / coma |

#### Edge Cases and Adjacent Links
**Shock lung** = diffuse alveolar damage with hyaline membranes, distinct from cardiogenic pulmonary oedema (Heart failure cells — haemosiderin-laden macrophages — in alveoli). **Chronic passive congestion of liver** shows centrilobular haemorrhage and fatty change peripherally. **Decompression sickness** produces intravascular nitrogen bubbles (caisson disease). **Anaphylactoid syndrome of pregnancy (amniotic fluid embolism)** combines DIC, bronchospasm, and seizures — a classic post-partum catastrophe tested on INI CET.

#### Common Mistakes and Exam Strategy
1. Confusing hyperaemia (active arteriolar dilation, e.g. blushing) with congestion (passive venous obstruction).
2. Treating all infarcts as pale — lung and small bowel infarcts are typically red.
3. Forgetting that **cardiogenic pulmonary oedema** produces transudate; **ARDS** produces exudate.
4. Overlooking the **24–72 h** window for fat embolism syndrome after femoral shaft fractures.

#### Practice Prompts
1. A 28-year-old develops tachypnoea, confusion, and axillary petechiae 36 hours after intramedullary nailing of a femoral fracture. Identify the embolic syndrome and its diagnostic triad.
2. A cirrhotic presents with bilateral pitting oedema and ascites. Trace the Starling-force derangement from reduced plasma oncotic pressure to interstitial fluid accumulation.
