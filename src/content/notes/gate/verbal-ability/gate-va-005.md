# Antonyms

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

An antonym is a word whose meaning is semantically opposite to another word in a given sense, not in every sense. GATE Verbal Ability tests this through single-answer MCQs that ask you to pick the word *most nearly opposite* to a bolded target word in context.

- **Gradable antonyms** sit on a scale (hot ↔ cold, fast ↔ slow, big ↔ small).
- **Complementary antonyms** are mutually exclusive pairs (alive ↔ dead, present ↔ absent, true ↔ false).
- **Relational antonyms** reverse roles (buy ↔ sell, teacher ↔ student, lend ↔ borrow).
- Match **part of speech, register, and connotation**; pick an antonym valid for the sense actually used in the stem.
- Watch for **polysemy traps**: "hard" (difficult) ↔ easy, but "hard" (solid) ↔ soft.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Definition and Scope

Antonymy is a semantic relation between two lexemes whose meanings are contrary in a specific sense. The same word can have different antonyms under different readings — "high" contrasts with "low" for elevation but with "cheap" for price. In GATE, the stem sentence fixes the relevant sense, so context-aware selection beats raw word-pair memorisation.

#### Types of Antonymy

| Type | Property | GATE Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gradable | Opposite ends of a scale; admit intermediates | hot–cold, fast–slow, big–small |
| Complementary | Binary, mutually exclusive | alive–dead, present–absent, true–false |
| Relational | Role reversal; one presupposes the other | buy–sell, teacher–student, lend–borrow |

#### Selection Rules

1. Match the target's part of speech — an adjective target needs an adjective answer ("deep" ↔ shallow, not depth).
2. Match register and connotation — formal stems demand formal antonyms ("courageous" ↔ cowardly, not foolish).
3. Disambiguate polysemy using the surrounding clause — "light" (pale) ↔ dark, but "light" (not heavy) ↔ heavy.
4. Prefer derivation only when the prefixed/suffixed form is a standard lexical item — "uncover" means "reveal," not "hide."

#### Frequent Error Patterns

- Picking a non-opposite distractor (different, unrelated) over the true opposite.
- Confusing **synonyms** with antonyms when options share a meaning field.
- Ignoring collocation: "raise" ↔ lower (height sense) vs. "raise" ↔ lower (price sense), but not "eliminate."

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Sense Disambiguation

The hardest GATE antonym items exploit polysemy and connotation shift. A word like "sound" has at least three senses — healthy (↔ unsound/ill), noise (↔ silence), and valid (↔ invalid/flawed) — and only one antonym applies in a given stem. Similarly, "fine" splits into penalty (↔ refund) and acceptable (↔ poor/coarse); choosing an antonym valid for the wrong sense is the single most common trap.

Connotation also decides ties: "aggressive" has two antonym candidates, "passive" (neutral register) and "peaceful" (positive register). When both appear as options, the one whose connotation matches the stem's tone wins.

#### Word-Formation Awareness

Many distractors are formed by routine prefixes that look antonym-like but are not. Memorise productive patterns and their exceptions:

| Prefix/Suffix | Looks like antonym | Real status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| un- | uncover ≠ "hide" | uncover = "reveal" (synonym of disclose) |
| in- | invaluable ≠ "valueless" | invaluable = "priceless" (not opposite) |
| dis- | dislike ≠ "hate" reversed | dislike = mild negative, not antonym of "love" |
| -less | careless ≠ antonym of "careful" | careless = "heedless," not opposite of careful in all senses |

#### Common Mistakes

- Confusing relational antonyms with gradable ones: "buy" has no scale — its true opposite is "sell," not "give."
- Treating intensifiers ("very," "extremely") as targets; only content words carry antonyms.
- Selecting an antonym that breaks collocation ("deep sleep" ↔ light sleep, not "shallow").

#### Practice Prompts

1. Stem: "His arguments were *cogent*." Options: feeble, verbose, lucid, terse, lucid. Pick the antonym of "cogent" (convincing). Answer: feeble.
2. Stem: "The medicine had an *alleviating* effect." Options: aggravating, diminishing, soothing, mild, curing. Pick the antonym. Answer: aggravating.
