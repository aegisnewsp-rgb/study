# Defining the State

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**State** is a politically organised community occupying a definite territory, bound by a sovereign government. The four **elements of the State** are *population, territory, government and sovereignty* — remove any one and the State ceases to exist.

- **State ≠ Government:** the State is permanent; governments change after elections.
- **State ≠ Nation:** a Nation is a group bound by common culture/language; a State adds the territorial-sovereign element.
- **Sovereignty** has two faces — internal (supreme law-making authority) and external (freedom from external control).
- **Social Contract Theory** has three versions: **Hobbes** (absolute sovereign), **Locke** (limited government with right to revolt), **Rousseau** (general will, popular sovereignty).

| Theory | Thinker | Core claim |
|---|---|---|
| Divine Origin | Medieval theologians | State created by God |
| Social Contract | Hobbes, Locke, Rousseau | State born from consent |
| Evolutionary | Historical school | State grew out of family/tribe |
| Marxist | Marx, Engels | State is an instrument of class rule |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Defining the State

Political scientist **R.M. MacIver** defined the State as "an association which, acting through law as promulgated by a government established to that end, claims to secure for its members the benefits of a common life." **Garner** describes it as a community of people occupying a definite territory under an organised government possessing sovereignty.

The four elements must coexist: **population** (without inhabitants there is no State), **territory** (a State is a territorial unit, not a wandering tribe), **government** (the agency through which the will of the State is formulated and expressed) and **sovereignty** (the power to enforce final authority within and resist control from outside).

#### Distinguishing State from Related Concepts

| Concept | Nature | Relationship with State |
|---|---|---|
| Government | Agent of the State | Temporary, changes with elections |
| Society | Wider social web | Includes families, clubs, churches |
| Nation | Cultural/psychological group | Need not be a State (Kurdish nation) |
| Polity | Organised political life | Broader term than State |

> Mnemonic: **PTGS** — Population, Territory, Government, Sovereignty.

#### Theories of Origin

- **Divine Origin Theory** (St. Augustine, Filmer) holds that the State was created by God and the ruler rules by divine sanction.
- **Social Contract Theory** views the State as a product of an agreement among individuals: **Hobbes** demanded an absolute sovereign to escape the "war of all against all"; **Locke** argued for limited government with natural rights to life, liberty and property; **Rousseau** located ultimate sovereignty in the *general will* of the people.
- **Evolutionary/Historical Theory** (Bagehot, Tyler) traces the State from the family, clan and tribe through gradual growth.
- **Marxist Theory** treats the State as an instrument of the dominant economic class, destined to "wither away" under communism.

#### Exam Pattern in TNPSC Group 1

Expect match-the-following on thinkers-theories, a "distinguish between" item on State and Government, and short notes on sovereignty (Austin's Monistic vs Laski's Pluralist view).

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Sovereignty in Depth

**John Austin's Monistic Theory** places sovereignty in one determinate human superior (the monarch or the electorate), treating law as the command of the sovereign backed by sanction. **Laski's Pluralist Theory** argues sovereignty is distributed among several associations — trade unions, churches, political parties — so no single authority can be truly supreme in a modern welfare society. **Dicey** reinforces legal sovereignty in Parliament while acknowledging political sovereignty of the electorate. Marxists treat sovereignty as a class mask; in their view real power flows from ownership of the means of production, not from constitutional law.

#### Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Writing that sovereignty is *only* supreme power within territory — the **external** aspect (independence in the international order) is equally examined in TNPSC.
- Treating **Social Contract** as a single doctrine; the three versions diverge sharply on whether the sovereign is absolute (Hobbes), conditional (Locke) or the people themselves (Rousseau).
- Confusing **Nation** with **State** — India is a State but also a nation-in-the-making integrating many linguistic communities; the reverse case is the Jewish nation before 1948.
- Equating **Government** with **State** — Government is the *machinery*, State is the *abstract legal entity*.
- Calling the State an **Association** — an association is voluntary and limited in purpose; membership in a State is compulsory and all-pervasive.
