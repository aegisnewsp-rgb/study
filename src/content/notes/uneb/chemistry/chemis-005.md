# Non-aqueous Titration and Phase Equilibria Form Topic of

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Non-aqueous titration and phase equilibria form Topic 5 (3% of UNEB UACE Chemistry). **Non-aqueous titration** lets analysts titrate weak acids or bases that give no sharp endpoint in water, using solvents like **glacial acetic acid** (protogenic), **liquid ammonia** (protophilic) or **ethanol** (amphiprotic). The **Levinstein process** titrates weak bases in acetic acid with HClO4 and **crystal violet** indicator.

**Phase equilibria** rests on Gibbs's rule **F = C − P + 2**, where F = degrees of freedom, C = components, P = phases, and the 2 represents T and P. The **Nernst distribution (partition) law** gives **K_d = c₁/c₂**, the fixed ratio of a solute's concentration between two immiscible solvents at constant temperature.

- **Raoult's law:** (x_A)liq · p°_A = p_A for ideal solutions.
- **Clausius–Clapeyron:** p_T = p_T0 · exp[(ΔH_vap/R)(1/T₀ − 1/T)] tracks vapour pressure with T.
- **Steam distillation** works because p_total = p°_water + p°_organic boils below 100 °C.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Solvent choice and acid-base theory

Brønsted-Lowry behaviour extends to non-aqueous solvents. A **protogenic solvent** (acetic acid) enhances bases by donating protons; a **protophilic solvent** (liquid ammonia) enhances acids by accepting protons. The approximation **pK = pK(H₂O) − 14** estimates how strongly a weak acid dissociates in a basic non-aqueous medium.

#### Titration procedure

A standard **non-aqueous titration** uses n = cV to relate moles of titrant to moles of analyte. For weak bases, dissolve the analyte in glacial acetic acid, titrate against standardised HClO₄ in acetic acid, and detect the endpoint with **crystal violet** (purple → green). For weak acids in liquid ammonia, **methyl red** is preferred.

#### Partition (distribution) law

A solute shaken between two immiscible solvents reaches equilibrium with constant ratio **K_d = c₁/c₂** at fixed T. For solvent extraction, the fraction remaining after n extractions is **(V₁/(V₁ + K_d·V₂))ⁿ**.

#### Phase rule applications

Gibbs's phase rule **F = C − P + 2** is applied directly to one-component systems (water: triple point 0.01 °C, 611 Pa) and two-component systems such as **Pb–Ag** (eutectic at 2.6% Ag, 303 °C) and **Zn–Mg**. The water–phenol system shows an upper consolute temperature above which the two liquids become fully miscible (one phase).

#### Common student errors

- Treating azeotropes as pure compounds; they are constant-boiling mixtures.
- Using total volume, not concentration, in the partition ratio.
- Forgetting the +2 term when the system is non-reactive.

| System | Key point | Indicator / feature |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Glacial acetic acid | Protogenic; enhances bases | Crystal violet |
| Liquid ammonia | Protophilic; enhances acids | Methyl red |
| Ethanol | Amphiprotic; neutral effect | Thymol blue |
| Water (one component) | Triple point 273.16 K, 611 Pa | Three phases coexist |

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Clausius–Clapeyron and steam distillation

The integrated form **p_T = p_T0 · exp[(ΔH_vap/R)(1/T₀ − 1/T)]** lets you calculate ΔH_vap from two vapour-pressure readings, or back-calculate the boiling point at reduced pressure. Steam distillation uses this: p_total = p°_water + p°_organic, so the mixture boils when the sum reaches atmospheric pressure, often below 100 °C. UNEB Paper 3 frequently asks candidates to compute the boiling point of an immiscible organic liquid given its p° at 80 °C and ΔH_vap ≈ 40 kJ mol⁻¹.

#### Raoult's law and deviations

Ideal solutions obey **p_A = x_A · p°_A**. Positive deviations (ethanol–water) arise when A–B forces are weaker than A–A and B–B; negative deviations (chloroform–acetone) signal stronger A–B attraction. **Azeotropes** are the constant-boiling points where the liquid and vapour curves touch; they cannot be separated by simple distillation.

#### Phase diagrams in depth

For the **Pb–Ag** system, the eutectic composition (~2.6% Ag by mass) freezes at 303 °C, the lowest melting point of any mixture. For **Zn–Mg**, intermetallic compounds appear as maxima on the liquidus curve. Always count components as the minimum number of independent species: in CaCO₃(s) ⇌ CaO(s) + CO₂(g), C = 2, not 3.

#### Practice prompts

1. A weak base (0.250 g, M = 82 g mol⁻¹) is titrated with 0.100 mol dm⁻³ HClO₄ in acetic acid. Calculate the volume at the endpoint using n = cV.
2. The vapour pressure of water at 90 °C is 70.1 kPa and at 100 °C is 101.3 kPa. Use the Clausius–Clapeyron equation to find ΔH_vap and compare with the literature value 40.7 kJ mol⁻¹.

| Calculation | Formula | Variables |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Moles from titration | n = cV | c in mol dm⁻³, V in cm³ |
| Partition ratio | K_d = c₁/c₂ | c₁, c₂ in mol dm⁻³ |
| Degrees of freedom | F = C − P + 2 | C components, P phases |
| Vapour pressure vs T | ln(p/p₀) = (ΔH_vap/R)(1/T₀ − 1/T) | ΔH_vap in J mol⁻¹ |
