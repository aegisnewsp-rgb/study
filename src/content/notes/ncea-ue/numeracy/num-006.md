# Numeracy-rich achievement standards from Science, Geography and other subjects — NCEA UE Numeracy Notes

Numeracy-rich achievement standards from Science, Geography, and other subjects (outside of Mathematics) also contribute to UE numeracy. The list is published by NZQA on the UE numeracy page. A student who has not achieved sufficient numeracy credits through Mathematics can meet UE numeracy through standards in other subjects.

> Verify the live UE numeracy standards spreadsheet on https://www2.nzqa.govt.nz/about-us/publications/newsletters-circulars/assessment-matters/a2023-17/ before planning revision around the figures below.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Various credits** depending on the specific standard.
- Selected Level 1 and above achievement standards in Science, Geography, and other subjects are on the "numeracy rich" list published by NZQA.
- Together with US32406 (or 91946/91947), these standards can satisfy the 10-credit UE numeracy requirement.
- The standards involve mathematical and statistical reasoning in applied contexts.

#### Examiner traps

- Assuming all Science standards are on the list — only specific standards with substantial numeracy content.
- Forgetting that standards can be combined across subjects to meet the 10-credit requirement.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Why numeracy across subjects?

Numeracy is not just mathematics — it is the application of mathematical and statistical reasoning across all subjects. Science uses measurement, unit conversion, and data analysis. Geography uses statistics, spatial reasoning, and data interpretation. Other subjects (e.g., Economics, Accounting, Technology) also involve substantial numeracy.

Recognising numeracy across subjects allows students to meet UE numeracy through their broader subject choices, not just through Mathematics.

#### Common numeracy-rich standards outside Mathematics

The list changes from year to year. Common entries include:

- **Science:** AS 90940 (Demonstrate understanding of aspects of mechanics, Level 1) — uses measurement, calculation, graph interpretation.
- **Science:** AS 90944 (Demonstrate understanding of aspects of acids and bases, Level 1) — uses measurement, calculation.
- **Science:** AS 90948 (Demonstrate understanding of biological ideas relating to a mammal as a consumer, Level 1) — uses data interpretation.
- **Geography:** AS 91007 (Demonstrate geographic understanding of environments that have been shaped by extreme natural event(s), Level 1) — uses data interpretation.
- **Geography:** AS 91010 (Apply concepts and basic geographic skills to demonstrate understanding of a given environment, Level 1) — uses measurement, calculation.

The exact list is on the NZQA UE numeracy spreadsheet.

#### Combined pathways

A typical UE numeracy pathway using non-Mathematics standards:

- US32406 (5 credits, dedicated co-requisite).
- AS 90940 (4 credits, Science).
- AS 91010 (4 credits, Geography).
- Total: 13 credits, satisfying UE numeracy with surplus.

This pathway is appropriate for a student who enjoys Science and Geography and is not planning to take Level 2 or Level 3 Mathematics.

#### Preparation strategy

For each standard on the list:

1. **Read the standard** — know what is being assessed.
2. **Check the credit value** — multiply by the number of standards to meet 10 credits.
3. **Practice the numeracy aspects** — measurement, calculation, graph interpretation.
4. **Use exemplars** — NZQA publishes student work at each grade level.

#### Why choose non-Mathematics pathways?

A student might choose a non-Mathematics pathway for UE numeracy if:

- They are not taking Mathematics beyond Level 1.
- They have stronger numeracy performance in Science or Geography than in Mathematics.
- They prefer applied numeracy in a specific context to abstract numeracy.

The flexibility is deliberate — UE numeracy should be achievable for all students, regardless of subject choice.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### The Excellence move

Excellence in numeracy-rich standards requires the same skills as Excellence in Mathematics standards: sophisticated method selection, justification, reflection, and connection to context.

For example, in a Science standard (AS 90940 mechanics):

- Merit: Calculate velocity, acceleration, or force using the appropriate formula.
- Excellence: Calculate the quantity using the appropriate formula, AND explain why the formula applies (e.g., what assumptions about the system justify using F = ma), AND discuss limitations (e.g., air resistance ignored, point mass assumption).

The Excellence response engages with the methodology, not just the calculation.

#### Cross-subject numeracy

The numeracy-rich standards reflect the reality that numeracy is a cross-subject skill. A student who develops strong numeracy in Science or Geography will benefit from those skills in university study, regardless of the specific degree.

Cross-subject numeracy also supports the transition to university, where quantitative reasoning is required across many disciplines (e.g., interpreting research in psychology, analysing data in sociology, evaluating economic claims in political science).

#### Pathway comparison

Compare three UE numeracy pathways for a student who enjoys Science but is not strong in Mathematics:

- **Mathematics-heavy:** US32406 + 91946/91947. 10 credits.
- **Mixed:** US32406 + a numeracy-rich Science standard + a numeracy-rich Geography standard. 13+ credits.
- **Non-Mathematics:** US32406 + multiple numeracy-rich standards across subjects. 10+ credits.

The mixed pathway often gives the best balance of breadth and depth.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "All Science standards count for UE numeracy." No — only those on the published numeracy-rich list.
- "UE numeracy is only about Mathematics." No — numeracy is broader.
- "I can claim credits from non-Mathematics standards without achieving them." No — credits must be earned by meeting the standard.
- "Non-Mathematics standards are easier." Not necessarily — the standards on the list have substantial numeracy content.
