# Ecology: Ecosystems and Food Chains

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

- **Ecosystem** = a community of **biotic** (living) organisms interacting with **abiotic** (non-living: light, temperature, water, soil, air) factors as one functional unit.
- **Food chain** = linear sequence: **Producer → Primary consumer → Secondary consumer → Tertiary consumer**, with **decomposers** (saprophytes) acting on every level.
- **Trophic level** = position an organism occupies in a food chain. **Autotrophs** make their own food; **heterotrophs** cannot.
- **10% rule** of energy transfer: only ~10% of energy passes to the next trophic level; the rest is lost as heat, in respiration, or in undigested matter.
- **Food web** = network of interlinked food chains, providing stability when one species is lost.
- High-yield for WAEC: draw a 4–5 level chain, label each role, and apply the 10% rule in a calculation (e.g., 10,000 J → 1,000 J → 100 J → 10 J).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Definitions

**Population** = group of individuals of one species in an area. **Community** = all populations of different species in the area. **Habitat** = where an organism lives; **niche** = its role and activities within that habitat. **Autecology** studies one species; **synecology** studies communities.

#### Components of an Ecosystem

Abiotic factors include light, temperature, rainfall, humidity, pH, soil type, wind, and topography. Biotic factors include competition, predation, parasitism, and symbiotic relationships (mutualism, commensalism, parasitism). These factors determine which organisms can survive and reproduce in a given habitat.

#### Trophic Levels and Energy Flow

Energy enters as sunlight, is captured by **producers** (green plants) during photosynthesis, and flows in one direction through consumers. **Decomposers** (fungi, bacteria) break down dead organic matter from all levels, releasing nutrients back to the soil and air. Matter is recycled; energy is not.

#### The 10% Rule (Sample Calculation)

| Trophic Level | Energy Available |
|---|---|
| Producers | 10,000 J |
| Primary consumers | 1,000 J |
| Secondary consumers | 100 J |
| Tertiary consumers | 10 J |

> Examiner tip: always show the 10% step explicitly in WAEC Paper 2 essays to score full marks.

#### Ecological Pyramids

Pyramid of **numbers** can be upright or inverted (one tree supports thousands of insects). Pyramid of **biomass** is usually upright but inverted in aquatic ecosystems (phytoplankton vs. fish). Pyramid of **energy** is **always upright** because of thermodynamic losses.

#### Exam Question Patterns

WAEC frequently asks students to draw a named food chain (e.g., Grass → Grasshopper → Lizard → Snake → Hawk), state the role of each organism, and explain why food webs are more stable than single chains.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases in Ecological Pyramids

The pyramid of numbers inverts when a single large producer (e.g., one oak tree) supports many primary consumers (insects, birds). The pyramid of biomass inverts in open water because rapidly reproducing phytoplankton have low standing biomass at any instant, yet support a larger fish biomass over time. The pyramid of energy, however, is always upright — a strict consequence of the second law of thermodynamics, since usable energy is lost as heat at every transfer. WAEC candidates should name the ecosystem type when describing an inverted pyramid to earn the second marking point.

#### Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Placing **decomposers** as a separate trophic level — they act on **all** levels simultaneously.
- Claiming energy is recycled like nutrients — energy flow is **unidirectional**; only matter cycles.
- Putting humans at a fixed trophic level — humans are omnivores and shift trophic position depending on the food chain considered.
- Confusing **habitat** with **niche**: a habitat is the address; a niche is the occupation.
- Drawing a food web without at least three interconnecting chains — a single chain drawn with cross-links does not yet qualify as a web.

#### Worked Numeric Example (10% Rule)

If 50,000 J of energy is fixed by grass in a savanna: grasshoppers receive 5,000 J; lizards 500 J; snakes 50 J; hawks 5 J. To reverse-engineer producers from a given top-consumer value, multiply by 10 at each step backwards.

#### Practice Prompts

1. Draw a food chain of five organisms from a named freshwater pond, label each trophic level, and calculate the energy available to the tertiary consumer if producers store 20,000 J.
2. With diagrams, distinguish the pyramid of numbers from the pyramid of biomass, and give one example where each becomes inverted.
