# Part 2 — Social Monologue

> Verify the live Listening format, computer-delivered vs paper-based delivery, and the 10-minute transfer time policy at your chosen test centre in Qatar on https://ielts.org/ and https://www.britishcouncil.qa/ before planning revision.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Format:** 10 questions. Question types: plan/map/diagram labelling, summary completion, sentence completion, multiple choice.
- **Transcript style:** one speaker delivering a monologue set in an everyday social context. Common Qatar-relevant contexts: a guided tour of a community facility, a speech about local services, event arrangements, opening hours and schedules.
- **Speed:** standard native-speaker pace with natural pausing. The monologue is slightly faster than Part 1 because there is no second-speaker turn-taking.
- **Order of answers:** questions follow the recording in order.
- **Predictable content:** locations on a plan or map, facility names, schedules, opening hours, prices, descriptions of places and events.
- **Common distractor:** the speaker may mention two options then settle on one ("we have either a sauna or a steam room — actually, it's a steam room"). The answer is the second, settled option.

#### Examiner traps

- Writing the first option mentioned before the speaker self-corrects. Always wait for the conclusion of the comparison.
- Confusing two facilities or locations on the plan. The plan/map question asks for a SPECIFIC label — read the question first to know which label is being asked for.
- Spelling the location name correctly — the recording spells or repeats facility names. Use the 10-minute transfer time (paper) to copy from the question paper carefully.
- Date and time formatting — "8:30 AM" and "8.30 am" are both accepted but inconsistent within one paper will look unprofessional.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Question types in Part 2

The most common question types in Part 2:

- **Plan/map/diagram labelling:** a plan, map or diagram with labels (typically A-H) and a list of options to place. Answers are short (1-3 words). Common Qatar contexts: community centre floor plan, hotel layout, university campus map.
- **Summary completion:** a summary passage with gaps. Answers are short (1-3 words from the recording). Common contexts: a speech about museum opening hours, an event schedule, a programme overview.
- **Sentence completion:** incomplete sentences with a single gap each. Answers follow the order of the recording.
- **Multiple choice:** choose one of three options. Distractors are usually mentioned in the recording but the question asks for the correct one.

#### Map and plan labelling strategy

Map and plan labelling is the highest-leverage Part 2 sub-skill — the questions are visual and require spatial reasoning in addition to listening.

1. **Read the question first.** Know which LABEL you are looking for (e.g. "the cafe", "the gift shop", "the main hall"). Do not try to match everything you hear.
2. **Locate the candidates on the plan.** Before the recording starts, identify the likely target locations (entrance, reception, main hall, cafe, toilets). The answer is almost always one of the obvious spots.
3. **Listen for the speaker's directional language.** "To your left", "next to", "behind", "on the corner of", "across from" are the trigger phrases.
4. **Match the function, not the description.** The recording may describe the cafe as "where you can get coffee and snacks" — match the function (cafe), not the description.

#### Qatar-specific practice contexts

When practising Part 2, simulate scenarios a Doha-based candidate is most likely to encounter:

- Guided tour of a hotel (West Bay, Pearl-Qatar, Lusail, Msheireb Downtown).
- Welcome speech at a residential compound (Al Sadd, Al Waab, Al Rayyan).
- Opening speech at a conference or trade fair (DECC, Qatar National Convention Centre).
- A speech about a museum or cultural centre (Museum of Islamic Art, National Museum of Qatar, Mathaf).
- A tour of a university campus (Qatar University, Education City branch campuses).
- A description of a new shopping mall or leisure facility (Place Vendome, Doha Festival City, Mall of Qatar).

These mirror the social-context vocabulary the real test uses; practising with them cuts cognitive load on test day.

### 🔴 Deep — Mastery (2mo+)

#### The cognitive architecture of Part 2

Part 2 is the second-easiest section of the IELTS Listening test. Like Part 1, it is NOT designed to separate high-band from low-band candidates — its purpose is to test the candidate's ability to extract factual information from a single-speaker monologue on a familiar topic.

The differentiation between Parts 1 and 2 is structural: Part 1 has turn-taking (two speakers), which provides natural pacing cues; Part 2 has only one speaker, so the cognitive load on sustained attention is higher. Candidates who lose focus in Part 2 typically have an attention-span issue that no targeted Part 2 practice will resolve. The deeper intervention is to build monologue-listening endurance (e.g. extended TED talks, BBC Radio 4 programmes, audiobook chapters).

A candidate scoring below 6 on Part 2 typically has a vocabulary, attention-span, or plan/map-reading issue.

#### Practice strategy for Part 2

1. **Use the British Council and IDP official IELTS practice tests.** The question styles and difficulty are calibrated against the live test. Third-party materials may diverge.
2. **Practise with Qatari-context scenarios.** Doha-based hotel, museum, conference and university campus scenarios build the local vocabulary you will hear on test day.
3. **Practise plan/map labelling with a stopwatch.** Most plan/map questions can be answered in under 30 seconds if you have practised the skill. Without practice, candidates spend 60-90 seconds per plan question and run out of time for the rest of Part 2.
5. **Single-pass summary completion:** summary completion in Part 2 is read-once (the recording does not replay). Train with single-pass listening drills to build the habit of capturing the answer on first hearing.
6. **Tabular drill:** build a 20-row table of common Part 2 distractors (first vs settled option; facility vs activity; time vs date; general vs specific). Practise choosing the right field for each row.

#### When Part 2 marks reflect a deeper issue

- **Persistent map/plan errors:** the issue is spatial reasoning, not listening. Practise with 5-minute daily map-reading drills (transit maps, building floor plans).
- **Persistent summary-completion errors:** the issue is single-pass listening endurance. Train with progressively longer single-pass drills — 2 minutes, 4 minutes, 6 minutes — until you can capture the answer to a 6-minute single-pass recording.
- **Persistent distractor losses (first option vs settled option):** the issue is self-correction recognition. The same Part 1 self-correction pattern applies — listen for the marker ("actually", "wait", "no, sorry") and write the corrected answer.
- **Persistent word-limit failures:** the issue is question reading, not listening. Train with a "read first, listen second" drill where you read the question and decide the word limit BEFORE the recording starts.

#### Edge cases and known traps

1. **Plan/map labels are NOT letter labels.** The candidate writes the FACILITY NAME (e.g. "cafe", "gym"), not the letter label (A, B, C). Reading the question stem carefully is the difference.
2. **Map directions assume a starting position.** The speaker says "as you enter the building, turn right" — the map answer assumes the starting position shown on the map. If you orient the map incorrectly, every answer is wrong.
3. **Self-correction without a verbal marker.** Some speakers correct themselves without saying "actually" or "wait". The correction is signalled by hesitation, a pause, or a reformulation. Practise with raw monologues (e.g. tourist-guide audio) to build the habit.
4. **"Write NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS".** Strict word-limit enforcement. "the 5th floor" counts as 3 words ("the/fifth/floor"). Plan accordingly.
5. **Plurals you cannot hear.** "Two ticket" is wrong; "two tickets" is right. The recording often does NOT pluralise — you must pluralise in writing. The spelling penalty is on.
6. **Capitalisation.** IELTS accepts lower-case names unless the recording explicitly capitalises (proper nouns, country names). Do not waste time capitalising "london" — both are accepted.
7. **Hyphenated words.** "twenty-five" written as "twenty five" counts as TWO words. If the limit is one, "twenty-five" is the only correct form.
9. **Numbers in Part 2.** Part 2 often uses prices ("QR 50 per ticket", "QR 25 for students"), times ("from 9 AM to 6 PM"), and dates ("the 15th of March"). Train with a daily 5-minute dictation of prices and times.

### High-Yield — IELTS Listening Part 2

The seven most-testable scenarios and the calibrated response framework:

1. **Guided tour of a hotel.** The speaker describes the layout of a hotel (entrance, reception, restaurant, gym, pool, business centre). Map labelling captures the locations.
2. **Welcome speech at a residential compound.** The speaker describes the facilities of a compound (clubhouse, supermarket, mosque, school, clinic). Summary completion captures the key facts.
3. **Opening speech at a conference.** The speaker describes the conference programme, venue, registration desk, exhibition area. Sentence completion captures the schedule.
4. **Speech about a museum.** The speaker describes a museum's layout, opening hours, ticket prices, audio-guide availability. Plan/map labelling captures the locations.
5. **Tour of a university campus.** The speaker describes the campus layout (library, lecture halls, cafeteria, student centre). Map labelling captures the buildings.
6. **Description of a shopping mall.** The speaker describes a mall's anchor stores, dining, entertainment, parking. Summary completion captures the key facts.
7. **Tour of a sports facility.** The speaker describes a sports club (reception, courts, pool, gym, cafe). Sentence completion captures the schedule.

### Previous Year Patterns — IELTS Listening Part 2

Part 2 has been structurally stable across every IELTS cycle since the Academic / General Training split in 1989. The four-section format (Part 1 everyday social context, Part 2 social monologue, Part 3 academic discussion, Part 4 academic lecture) is published verbatim on ielts.org and is not expected to change.

Common year-on-year patterns:
- The 10-question count for Part 2 has been stable since the early 2000s.
- The four question families (plan/map/diagram labelling; summary completion; sentence completion; multiple choice) recur across cycles; weighting shifts slightly but no family is ever absent.
- Self-correction is present in roughly 25% of Part 2 items per cycle.
- A previous-year Part 2 recording is NOT reused verbatim in the same year; recycled recordings appear with at most small contextual changes.
- Computer-delivered Listening tests do not have the 10-minute transfer time; this is a paper-only feature. Candidates should verify the format at their chosen centre.

A previous-year record is not a deterministic indicator of a future test item. The British Council and IDP refresh the question bank annually, calibrated against the same difficulty curve.

### Pro Tips — IELTS Listening Part 2

1. **Read ahead.** Use the 30-60 seconds before each recording starts to read every question. The binding constraint in Part 2 is reading speed, not listening speed.
2. **Locate map/plan candidates first.** Before the recording starts, identify the likely target locations on the plan (entrance, reception, main hall, cafe, toilets). The answer is almost always one of the obvious spots.
3. **Listen for self-corrections.** A self-correction marker ("actually", "wait", "no, sorry", "let me re-check") is your cue to revise the answer. About 25% of Part 2 items contain one.
4. **Match the function, not the description.** For map/plan labelling, the recording may describe the cafe as "where you can get coffee and snacks" — match the function (cafe), not the description.
5. **Write legibly.** The marking is on what you write, not what you hear. If the marker cannot read the answer, it is wrong. Use the 10-minute transfer time (paper-based) to rewrite unclear numbers.
6. **Manage word limits.** "NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS" is strict. Count words, including numbers ("5th" = 1 word; "the fifth" = 2 words).
7. **Use the time between sections.** Part 2 ends, Part 3 begins — use the gap to re-read your Part 2 answers.
8. **Practise with Qatari-context scenarios.** Doha-based hotel, museum, conference, university and shopping-mall scenarios cut cognitive load on test day.
9. **Don't panic on a missed item.** Move on. The next item is independent. Two or three lost Part 2 items does not change your overall band.
