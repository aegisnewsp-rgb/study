# Mensuration: Areas and Volumes

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your NECO SSCE Mathematics paper.

Mensuration covers the numerical measurement of **perimeter** (1-D, in metres), **area** (2-D, in m²) and **volume** (3-D, in m³) for plane shapes and solids. Every NECO SSCE question on this topic reduces to selecting the right formula, converting all lengths to the same unit, and using π = 22/7 when 7 divides a dimension exactly (otherwise 3.142).

| Shape | Area or Volume formula |
| --- | --- |
| Triangle | A = ½ × base × height |
| Circle | A = πr²; circumference = 2πr |
| Cylinder | V = πr²h; TSA = 2πr(r + h) |
| Cone | V = ⅓πr²h; CSA = πrl, where l = √(r² + h²) |
| Sphere | V = 4/3 πr³; surface area = 4πr² |

- Always check whether the question gives **diameter** (halve it) or **radius** before using r.
- For pyramids and cones, volume is exactly **one-third** of the matching prism or cylinder.
- State the **unit** (m² or m³) on every final answer; NECO marks are lost for bare numbers.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students revising over days to weeks before the exam.

#### Plane-figure formulas

The triangle, trapezium and circle carry separate rules. For a triangle, A = ½ × b × h, where the base b and perpendicular height h must share the same unit. For a trapezium, A = ½(a + b)h, with a and b as the two parallel sides. When the three sides a, b, c of a triangle are known but the height is not, use **Heron's formula**: s = (a + b + c)/2, then A = √[s(s − a)(s − b)(s − c)].

#### Standard 3-D solids

Every solid on the NECO syllabus is built from a base area multiplied by a height. A **prism** has V = base area × length; a **cylinder** is a circular prism with V = πr²h. A **cone** or **pyramid** is one-third of the corresponding cylinder or prism, giving V = ⅓ × base area × h.

| Solid | Curved / Lateral Surface Area | Total Surface Area |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cylinder | 2πrh | 2πr² + 2πrh = 2πr(r + h) |
| Cone | πrl (l = slant height) | πr² + πrl |
| Sphere | 4πr² | 4πr² (same — no separate base) |

#### Composite shapes

For compound figures (e.g. a triangle sitting on a rectangle, or a cylinder capped with a hemisphere), **add the areas or volumes of the component parts**. Always convert all dimensions to metres before substituting; mixing cm with m gives a 10 000-times error in area and a 1 000 000-times error in volume.

- Use π = 22/7 whenever a length given is a multiple of 7 (e.g. r = 7 cm, h = 14 cm).
- Use π = 3.142 when no length is divisible by 7, or when the answer is required in decimal form.
- For cones and pyramids, slant height l = √(r² + h²), **not** the vertical height h.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline; includes traps, edge cases, and exam strategy.

#### The four highest-weight traps

Examiners consistently test the same slips. Guard against them by underlining each length before substitution.

1. Diameter vs radius — diameter d = 2r; halving it is mandatory before any πr², πr²h, 4πr², or 4/3 πr³ calculation.
2. The "silent factor" — students routinely drop the ½ in triangle area and the ⅓ in cone/pyramid volume, producing an answer exactly three or two times too large.
3. Mixed units — converting cm to m by dividing by 100 (length), but forgetting to **square** it for area (÷10 000) or **cube** it for volume (÷1 000 000).
4. Slant vs vertical height — for cones, l = √(r² + h²); using l where h is needed inflates πr²h by the wrong factor.

#### Worked micro-example

A closed cylindrical tank has radius 7 cm and height 21 cm. Find its total surface area.

1. TSA = 2πr(r + h) = 2 × 22/7 × 7 × (7 + 21) = 44 × 28 = **1232 cm²**.
2. Volume check: V = πr²h = 22/7 × 49 × 21 = **3234 cm³** — note m³ would require 0.0003234.

#### Edge cases and linked topics

- A hemisphere's volume is ½ × 4/3 πr³ = 2/3 πr³, and its curved surface is 2πr².
- Volumes of similar shapes scale with the **cube** of the linear ratio; areas scale with the **square**. This links Mensuration directly to Similarity.
- Frustum of a cone: V = ⅓ πh(R² + Rr + r²), where R and r are the two radii.
- Surface area of a pyramid = ½ × base perimeter × slant height + base area.

#### NECO SSCE exam strategy

Mensuration contributes roughly **4%** of the total Mathematics marks, split between Paper 1 (objectives) and Paper 2 (theory). Target **2–3 minutes per structured question**: read for the missing dimension, convert units, pick the formula, then substitute. Always end with the **unit** — examiners withhold one mark for answers written as "3234" instead of "3234 cm³".

#### Practice prompts

1. A cone of radius 6 cm and vertical height 8 cm is filled with water. Find the capacity of the cone in litres (1 litre = 1000 cm³). (Answer uses π = 3.142 → V ≈ 0.3016 L.)
2. A rectangular tank 2 m × 1.5 m × 1 m holds water to a depth of 0.6 m. How many litres must be added to fill it? (Answer: 1200 L.)

> **Scoring tip:** Write the formula in full before substituting numbers — NECO markers award the method mark even when the final arithmetic slips.
