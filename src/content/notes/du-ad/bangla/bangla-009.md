# Contemporary Bengali Literature

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Contemporary Bengali Literature (সমকালীন বাংলা সাহিত্য) covers Bengali literary output from roughly the mid-20th century onward, defined by post-1947 partition sensibilities, the 1952 Language Movement, the 1971 Liberation War, modernist experimentation, feminist and subaltern voices, and the rise of Bangladeshi national literature after independence.

- **Time scope:** mid-20th century → present (post-Kallol era, 1923–31 onward; especially post-1947 and post-1971).
- **Core genres tested:** kobita (poetry), gan (song), chhara (lyric), golpo (short story), uponyash (novel), natok (drama), probandho-nibandha (essay), smritikatha (memoir).
- **Key movements to recognise:** Kallol Yug, Progoti Yug, Parba Yug, Naxalbari Andolon Sahitya, Sontushti Sahitya, Biswa-shilpi Kabita tradition.
- **Mandatory author–work pairs (DU Bangladesh admission):**
  - Jibanananda Das — *Banalata Sen* (poem), *Bisher Banshi* (collection)
  - Shamsur Rahman — *Bondona o Ontoral* (1965)
  - Abu Zafar Obaida — *Deshanto* (poem)
  - Al-Mahmud — *Lokayata*, *Swapnabhanga*
  - Selina Hossain — *Hangor Nadi Grenade* (1976)
  - Akhtaruzzaman Elias — *Khowabnama* (1996)
  - Selim Al-Deen — *Khongajoner Satiganja*
  - Munier Chowdhury — *Nuruldin* (post-1972 staging)
- **Recurring themes:** anti-fascist resistance, language-martyr consciousness, muktijuddho, displacement and identity (1947), nation-building, fragmented poetics, urban experience.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"JP-SM-BD-AE-SA-MC"** — Jibanananda, Probasi-era poets (Sudhindra Nath Datta, Shamsur Rahman), Bangladesh quartet (Al-Mahmud, Abu Zafar Obaida, Selina Hossain, Akhtaruzzaman Elias), Selim Al-Deen, Munier Chowdhury. Read as **"Just Past Shots — Bangladesh Answers: Selim Al-Deen, Munier Chowdhury"** for exam recall.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Canonical Movements and Their Editors

The contemporary canon is periodised by journals more than by reigns. *Kallol Patra* (1923) inaugurated the Kallol Yug; *Parichay* carried the Progoti Yug; *Kritibas* and *Probasi* hosted the Parba Yug; *Khepar Gaan* and *Sangbed* (Dhaka) shaped post-1971 Bangladesh writing; *Notuner Kabita* and *Sutradhar* sustained the Biswa-shilpi line of Samar Sen.

- **Kallol Yug (c. 1923–1931):** rebellion against Sabuj-Sabujpatra romanticism; led by Achintya Kumar Sengupta, Premendra Mitra, Buddhadeva Bose, Achintya Kumar, Sudhindra Nath Datta.
- **Progoti Yug (1930s–40s):** politically engaged, anti-fascist; Prithwindra Mukherjee, Hasan Hafizur Rahman, Nisar Ahmed.
- **Parba Yug (1950s–70s West Bengal):** Sankha Ghosh, Utpal Kumar Basu, Binoy Majumdar, Nirmalendu Goon; forms the bridge to Naxalbari-era writing.
- **Sontushti Sahitya / Naxalbari Sahitya (1968–75):** protest poetry in journals like *Khepar Gaan*; Bengali peasant uprising as central metaphor.
- **Swadhinata Uttor Bangla Sahitya (post-1971):** Liberation War, Language Movement, nation-building; Selina Hossain, Rizia Rahman, Hasan Manzar, Mahmudul Hasan.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** Match the following works to their authors:
(A) *Bondona o Ontoral* (1965)
(B) *Hangor Nadi Grenade* (1976)
(C) *Khongajoner Satiganja*
(D) *Khowabnama* (1996)

Options for authors: (1) Selina Hossain, (2) Akhtaruzzaman Elias, (3) Shamsur Rahman, (4) Selim Al-Deen.

#### Solution:
- (A) → (3) Shamsur Rahman. His 1965 collection fuses devotional and erotic registers; the title means "Salutation and Separation."
- (B) → (1) Selina Hossain. The 1976 novel documents the 1971 Liberation War from a rural woman's viewpoint.
- (C) → (4) Selim Al-Deen. A landmark 1980s Bangladeshi drama staging history through folk-Pala-gaan conventions.
- (D) → (2) Akhtaruzzaman Elias. A modernist novel of post-independence Bangladesh published posthumously in 1996.
- Final matching: A-3, B-1, C-4, D-2.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students frequently swap *Hangor Nadi Grenade* (Selina Hossain) with *Kholni Upakhyan* (Selim Al-Deen) because both dramatise rural Bengal. Verify the **publication year**: Hossain 1976, Khowabnama 1996. Also, do not confuse Abu Zafar Obaida the **poet** (*Deshanto*) with Zahir Raihan the **filmmaker** (*Jibon Theke Neya*).

#### Advanced Traps and Edge Cases

1. **Period boundary dispute:** Is Manik Bandyopadhyay's *Padma Nadir Majhi* (1936) "contemporary"? Strictly **no** — it precedes the mid-20th-century scope, though DU questions may test whether you know its publication year.
2. **Nation boundary dispute:** Shamsur Rahman and Al-Mahmud are **Bangladeshi**, not West Bengali. Confusing them with Calcutta-based poets like Sankha Ghosh is a common error.
3. **Genre misclassification:** Munier Chowdhury is **playwright-and-translator**, not a novelist. Selim Al-Deen is playwright, not a lyric poet.
4. **Date drift:** Some syllabi list *Nuruldin* as 1960s writing but its major post-1972 staging belongs to the Liberation-War aesthetic; cite both the writing and staging dates.
5. **One-vs-many authors:** "Biswa-shilpi Kabita" is a **tradition** founded by Samar Sen, not a single poem — questions asking "who wrote *Biswa-shilpi Kabita*?" expect the answer "the tradition of Samar Sen," not a single author.
6. **Language Movement mis-tagging:** Poems commemorating the 1952 martyrs (Abu Zafar Obaida's *Deshanto*) are not Liberation-War poems; the war is 1971.

> 📌 **Formula Check:** There are no quantitative formulas, but the **mental substitution** is: Movement + Editor + Author + Work + Year = one exam MCQ. Memorise pairs as a 5-tuple.
