# Blood Relations

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Blood Relations** questions ask you to decode how members of a family are connected using short statements, then answer "How is X related to Y?" The topic hinges on two families of relation: **consanguinity** (blood ties — father, mother, son, daughter, brother, sister, uncle, aunt, nephew, niece, cousin) and **affinity** (marriage ties — husband, wife, father-in-law, son-in-law, brother-in-law, sister-in-law). The fastest method is the **symbol notation**: a **square = male**, **circle = female**, a **horizontal line** joins siblings, and a **vertical line** drops to children. CLAT typically gives **1–2 questions** (1 mark each) solvable in 60–90 seconds. Watch the qualifiers **"only"**, **"either-or"**, and **"no male/female member"** — they collapse the family into one valid structure.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Vocabulary
Every Blood Relations problem reduces to a chain of these atomic relations. Memorise them with their gender markers:

| Relation | Type | Symbol Hint |
|---|---|---|
| Father, Son, Brother, Uncle, Nephew, Cousin (male side) | Consanguinity | Square (♂) |
| Mother, Daughter, Sister, Aunt, Niece, Cousin (female side) | Consanguinity | Circle (♀) |
| Husband, Wife, Father/Mother-in-law, Son/Daughter-in-law, Brother/Sister-in-law | Affinity | Linked by marriage line |
| Grandfather/Grandmother, Grandson/Granddaughter | Consanguinity, 2 generations apart | Two vertical jumps |

#### The Diagramming Method
1. **Identify gender** from the statement — "Mr." → male, "Mrs./Ms." → female, "son/daughter/brother/sister" → explicit, "only daughter" → female and unique.
2. **Place symbols on a vertical stack** generation by generation: grandparents on top, parents in the middle, the subject's generation next, children at the bottom.
3. **Join siblings** with a horizontal line; **drop a vertical** to children; **draw a double-horizontal** (marriage line) between husband and wife.
4. **Trace the path** from person A to person B and translate the path into a relation ("father's father" = paternal grandfather).

#### Pointing / Gesture Variants
When a person says, *"Pointing to a photograph, A says, 'He is the son of my mother's only son'"*, treat **A as a separate node**. A's mother's only son is A himself (or A's brother if A is female); the person in the photo is therefore A's son or nephew. Always clarify the speaker's gender first — a female speaker with a brother makes "only son" the brother, not the speaker.

#### Typical CLAT Formats
- **Direct decoding** — three to four statements, one question.
- **Coded relations** — P+Q means "P is the father of Q", P–Q means "P is the sister of Q", etc.
- **Single-statement puzzles** — "A introduces B as the son of the only brother of my mother's husband." → B is A's cousin (mother's husband's only brother = mother's brother).

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Traps
- **"Either-or" conditions** arise when the speaker's gender is unknown. "A is the child of B, and B is the only son of C" — B's siblings are unknown, so A is *either* C's grandson *or* C's granddaughter. Answer as "C's grandchild."
- **In-laws overlap with blood** — your sister's husband is your brother-in-law, but he is unrelated by blood. Do not call him your brother. Similarly, your uncle's wife is your aunt only by marriage.
- **"No male member" / "only daughter"** forces every person in that generation to be female, which can flip the answer key in coded puzzles.
- **Cousin precision** — your father's brother's son is your cousin; your father's sister's son is also your cousin. The route is *parent → sibling → child*, never *parent → child* (that would be your sibling).

#### Worked Micro-Example
*Statement:* A is the son of B. B is the only daughter of C. D is the son of E, and E is the sister of B. *Question:* How is A related to D?
**Solution.** B is C's only daughter → C's only child shown is B. E is B's sister → E is also C's daughter, so A and D are children of sisters. A and D are therefore **cousins**.

#### CLAT-Specific Strategy
The Logical Reasoning section carries ~22–26 questions worth 1 mark each, with **negative marking of –0.25**. A Blood Relations set is a guaranteed scoring slot if you diagram cleanly. Budget **90 seconds per question**: spend 30 seconds drawing the tree, 20 seconds tracing the relation, 40 seconds re-reading the question to confirm direction ("A related to B" vs "B related to A"). Skip and return if a generation count exceeds three — CLAT almost never uses four-level chains.

#### Practice Prompts
1. *A says, "He is the son of the woman who is the mother of my husband's mother." Who is "he" to A?*
2. *Pointing to a man in a photo, Reena says, "His mother is the only daughter of my mother." How is the man related to Reena?*
