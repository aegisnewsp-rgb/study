# Nutrition, Environmental Health, and Toxicology

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

- **Balanced diet:** ~2400 kcal/day for a sedentary adult male, ~1900 kcal/day for a sedentary adult female; add **+20% for moderate activity, +40% for heavy work**.
- **BMI formula:** weight (kg) ÷ height² (m²), with **Asian-specific cut-offs** (overweight ≥23, obese ≥25 kg/m²).
- **PEM spectrum:** **Kwashiorkor** = protein deficit with oedema, flag sign, fatty liver; **Marasmus** = total calorie deficit with muscle wasting, no oedema.
- **Vitamin A prophylaxis (India):** **1,00,000 IU** at 9 months; **2,00,000 IU** every 6 months till 5 years.
- **Iron-folic acid in pregnancy:** **100 mg elemental Fe + 500 µg folic acid daily** for ≥180 days.
- **Reference Dose (RfD):** NOAEL ÷ (UF × MF), expressed in mg/kg/day.
- **Fluorosis:** optimal water fluoride **0.5–0.8 ppm**; defluoridation by **Nalgonda technique**.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Macronutrients and Protein Quality

Carbohydrates supply 4 kcal/g, proteins 4 kcal/g, and fats 9 kcal/g. SDA (Specific Dynamic Action) is the extra heat produced during digestion — proteins have the highest (~30%), carbohydrates ~6%, fats ~4%. Reference protein is **egg protein**, with a chemical score of 100 and biological value (BV) of 94; limiting amino acids vary by source (lysine in cereals, methionine in pulses).

#### Protein-Energy Malnutrition

PEM is graded by **Gomez (weight-for-age)**, **Waterlow (weight-for-height, height-for-age)**, and the **Welcome classification**. Kwashiorkor presents at 1–4 years after weaning onto starchy gruels; oedema, sparse hair, hepatomegaly, and skin fissures dominate. Marasmus shows "old man" facies, gross wasting, and irritability without oedema. **Marasmic-kwashiorkor** carries features of both and carries the worst prognosis.

#### Micronutrient Deficiency — The High-Yield Numbers

| Deficiency | Key clinical sign | Prophylaxis dose |
|---|---|---|
| Vitamin A | Bitot's spots, keratomalacia | 2,00,000 IU oral every 6 months (12–59 mo) |
| Iron | Hb <11 g/dL (pregnant), pallor | 100 mg Fe + 500 µg FA daily |
| Iodine | Goitre, cretinism | Salt iodisation ≥15 ppm at consumption |
| Fluoride (>1.5 ppm) | Mottled enamel, skeletal fluorosis | Nalgonda defluoridation |

#### Environmental Health — Water and Air

WHO drinking-water standard is **0 E. coli per 100 mL**. MPN (Most Probable Number) coliform counts classify potability. Air-quality standards use **PM2.5, PM10, SO₂, NO₂**; NAAQS in India sets 24-hour PM2.5 at 60 µg/m³ (revised 2009 onward).

#### Toxicology Fundamentals

Paracelsus' dictum — **"the dose makes the poison"** — governs risk assessment. **LD50** is the dose killing 50% of test animals; **NOAEL** is the highest dose with no observable adverse effect; **LOAEL** is the lowest dose producing an adverse effect. The **RfD = NOAEL ÷ (UF × MF)**, where UF (uncertainty factor) is typically 10–100 and MF (modifying factor) 1–10, yielding safe lifetime exposure in mg/kg/day.

#### Common Exam Traps

- Confusing Asian vs Western BMI cut-offs.
- Quoting "zero coliforms" instead of "zero E. coli".
- Mixing **TLV** (occupational threshold, mg/m³ air) with **ADI** (oral intake, mg/kg/day).

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Dose-Response and Risk Assessment in Practice

For **threshold toxicants** (most chemicals), a safe exposure exists below the NOAEL; for **non-threshold carcinogens** (asbestos, benzene, ionising radiation), regulators use linear extrapolation to a risk-specific dose (e.g., 1 in 10⁶ lifetime cancer risk). **ADI** = NOAEL ÷ safety factor (usually 100) for food additives and pesticides. **TLV-TWA** (Threshold Limit Value – Time-Weighted Average, ACGIH) refers to airborne occupational exposure over an 8-hour day. **Bioaccumulation** describes tissue build-up over time (lipophilic agents like DDT); **biomagnification** is rising concentration up the food chain (DDT in fish → raptors).

#### Landmark Disasters and Mechanisms

The **Bhopal 1984** leak released **methyl isocyanate (MIC)**, causing >3,800 deaths from pulmonary oedema and corneal injury; treatment was largely supportive. **Chernobyl 1986** released radioactive iodine-131, raising paediatric thyroid carcinoma 30-fold in exposed Belarus children — prevented today by **KI prophylaxis** and sheltering. **Minamata** (Japan, 1956) was methylmercury from acetaldehyde plant effluent in fish → cerebellar ataxia, sensory deficits. **Itai-itai** (Toyama) was cadmium contamination of rice → renal tubular failure and osteomalacia.

#### Food Adulteration Under FSSAI 2006

The Prevention of Food Adulteration Act (PFA, 1954) was repealed and replaced by the **Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006**, which created FSSAI. Classic adulterants: **metanil yellow** in turmeric and sweets, **argemone oil** in mustard oil (epidemic dropsy), **urea/starch** in milk, **brick powder** in chilli, **caustic soda** in flour. Field test kits (e.g., DAD/MAS Kit) detect common adulterants.

#### Biomedical Waste Management

The **BMW Rules 2016** (amended 2018) classify waste into **10 colour-coded categories**: yellow (anatomical/soiled), red (contaminated recyclable plastics), white (sharps), blue (glassware), with specific treatment — **incineration** for human tissue, **autoclaving** for microbiology waste, **deep burial** for anatomy waste.

#### Practice Prompts

1. A 2-year-old presents with bilateral pitting oedema, sparse hypopigmented hair, and hepatomegaly. Weight-for-age is 55%. Classify the PEM and list two differentiating signs from marasmus.
2. Calculate the RfD for a pesticide with NOAEL = 30 mg/kg/day in rats, using UF = 100 and MF = 5. Comment on whether a daily intake of 0.05 mg/kg/day is acceptable.
