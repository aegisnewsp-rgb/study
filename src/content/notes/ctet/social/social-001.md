# The Earth and the Solar System

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your CTET Social Studies paper.

The **Solar System** is a gravitationally bound system centred on the **Sun**, a G2V yellow-dwarf star containing roughly 99.8% of the system's mass. It formed about **4.6 billion years ago** from a rotating solar nebula and contains **eight planets**, five recognised **dwarf planets**, natural satellites, asteroids, and comets.

- **Planet order from the Sun:** Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune (mnemonic: *My Very Educated Mother Just Served Us Noodles*).
- **Earth's two motions:** **rotation** on its axis in ~24 hours (causes day and night) and **revolution** around the Sun in ~365.25 days (causes the year and seasons, because the axis is tilted **23.5°**).
- **Eclipses:** **Solar eclipse** = Moon between Sun and Earth (New Moon); **Lunar eclipse** = Earth between Sun and Moon (Full Moon).
- **Pluto** is a **dwarf planet** (reclassified by the IAU in 2006), not one of the eight planets.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Structure of the Solar System
The Solar System is held together by the Sun's gravity. Planets are divided into two groups based on composition:

| Group | Planets | Key Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Terrestrial (inner)** | Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars | Rocky surfaces, thin or no atmospheres, smaller size, higher density |
| **Gas & ice giants (outer)** | Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune | Gaseous envelopes; Jupiter and Saturn are hydrogen-helium gas giants, Uranus and Neptune are ice giants |

Beyond Neptune lies the **Kuiper belt** (home to Pluto, Haumea, Makemake, Eris), and far outside, the hypothesised spherical **Oort cloud** of icy bodies. The **asteroid belt** sits between Mars and Jupiter.

#### Earth — Size, Shape and Motion
Earth's approximate shape is a **geoid** — an oblate spheroid slightly flattened at the poles (equatorial radius ≈ 6,371 km, polar ≈ 6,357 km). Its surface gravity follows:

$$g = \frac{GM}{r^2} \approx 9.8 \text{ m/s}^2$$

where *G* = 6.674×10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg², *M* (Earth) ≈ 5.972×10²⁴ kg, *r* ≈ 6.371×10⁶ m.

Two simultaneous motions govern Earth's behaviour:
1. **Rotation** — spinning on its axis west-to-east once in ~24 hours → day and night.
2. **Revolution** — orbiting the Sun once in ~365.25 days; combined with the **23.5° axial tilt**, this produces **seasons**, **solstices** (longest/shortest days) and **equinoxes** (equal day and night). The extra ~0.25 day each year is corrected by a **leap year** every four years.

#### The Moon and Eclipses
The **Moon** is Earth's only natural satellite. It rotates and revolves in a **tidally locked** configuration — ~27.3 days sidereal, ~29.5 days synodic (the **lunar month** that drives **phases**). Eclipses arise from the Sun–Earth–Moon geometry:

- **Solar eclipse** — Moon passes between Sun and Earth during a **New Moon**.
- **Lunar eclipse** — Earth passes between Sun and Moon during a **Full Moon**.

> CTET trap: students mark solar eclipse at Full Moon. Memorise: "Solar = Sun-side blocking = New Moon."

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Planetary Highlights and Common Misconceptions
Each planet has textbook-tested distinctions that examiners love:

| Planet | Distinctive Fact Often Tested |
|---|---|
| Mercury | No atmosphere; closest to Sun; extreme temperature swings |
| Venus | **Hottest planet** — runaway **greenhouse effect** from thick CO₂ atmosphere |
| Earth | Only known planet supporting life; one natural satellite (the Moon) |
| Mars | Largest volcano (**Olympus Mons**) and deepest canyon (**Valles Marineris**) |
| Jupiter | Largest planet; **Great Red Spot** (giant persistent storm); most massive |
| Saturn | Most prominent **ring system** of ice and rock particles |
| Uranus | Tilted ~98° — rotates on its **side** |
| Neptune | Strongest **winds** (~2,100 km/h) in the Solar System |

#### Orbit Mechanics — Kepler's Laws in Context
For any planet of mass *m* orbiting the Sun (mass *M*) at distance *r*, the gravitational pull supplies centripetal force:

$$\frac{mv^2}{r} = \frac{GMm}{r^2} \;\Rightarrow\; v = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}}$$

**Kepler's Third Law** relates the orbital period *T* to the semi-major axis *a*:

$$T^2 = \frac{4\pi^2}{GM} a^3$$

This is why outer planets have such long years — Neptune (~30 AU) takes ~165 Earth-years per revolution.

#### Common Mistakes and Exam Strategy
- Confusing **rotation** (own axis) with **revolution** (around another body) — note their different time scales (~24 h vs ~365.25 d).
- Believing Saturn is the *only* ringed planet; Jupiter, Uranus and Neptune also bear fainter rings.
- Treating Pluto as the ninth planet — IAU 2006 reclassification established the **dwarf planet** category (Ceres, Pluto, Haumea, Makemake, Eris).
- Assuming Mercury is hottest because it is nearest — **Venus** is hotter due to greenhouse warming.
- CTET weightage is modest (~3%) but reliable: expect **1–2 MCQs** on planet order, eclipse geometry, or rotation/revolution effects.

#### Practice Prompts
1. A Full Moon night shows Earth between the Sun and Moon. Which eclipse is *not* possible that night — and why?
2. If Earth's axial tilt were 0°, what two seasonal phenomena would disappear, and which two would remain?
