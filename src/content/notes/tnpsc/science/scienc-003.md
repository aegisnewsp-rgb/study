# Key Relationships

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> One sentence to anchor you: every TNPSC Science question on relationships reduces to a proportionality law with named variables and SI units.

Relationships in the Samacheer Kalvi Std IX–XII framework fall into two families: **direct proportionality** (y ∝ x) and **inverse proportionality** (y ∝ 1/x). Memorise the sign of proportionality — that single symbol decides three marks in any Group 1 assertion–reason item.

| Relationship | Formula | Constant of proportionality |
|---|---|---|
| Force–Acceleration | F = m × a | mass m (kg) |
| Ohm's Law | V = I × R | resistance R (Ω) |
| Gravitation | F = G m₁m₂ / r² | G = 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg² |
| Gay-Lussac's Law | P/T = constant | volume, moles held fixed |
| Photosynthesis | 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ | light energy (chlorophyll) |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** "**DIRT-VIP**" — **D**irect = **I**ncrease together, **R**everse = **T**ogether opposite. Use it for **V**olume, **I**deal gas, **P**ressure questions on the spot.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Target audience: a candidate 2 days to 2 months out, building working fluency with derivation chains and the recurring trap patterns.

#### Proportionality as the Master Idea

Three shapes cover roughly 80 % of TNPSC Group 1 relationship questions: linear (y = kx), inverse (y = k/x), and power-law (y = kxⁿ, often n = 2). The constant k carries units that you can derive from dimensional analysis — a frequent 2-mark item asks "what are the units of k in y = kx²?".

#### Newton's Second Law and Mechanical Chains

F = m × a is the gateway. From it: W = F × s = m × a × s gives kinetic energy at displacement s under uniform a (when starting from rest, a = v²/2s). Pressure follows as P = F/A = m·a/A. Each derived equation inherits SI units from F (kg·m/s²).

| Quantity | Derivation from F = ma | SI unit | Dimensional formula |
|---|---|---|---|
| Momentum p | p = mv | kg·m/s | MLT⁻¹ |
| Kinetic Energy KE | KE = ½mv² | joule (J) | ML²T⁻² |
| Power P | P = Fv | watt (W) | ML²T⁻³ |
| Pressure P | P = F/A | pascal (Pa) | ML⁻¹T⁻² |

#### Comparing Easily-Confused Gas Laws

| Law | Held constant | Variable pair | Relationship form |
|---|---|---|---|
| Boyle's Law | T | P, V | P ∝ 1/V (inverse) |
| Charles's Law | P | V, T | V ∝ T (direct, in K) |
| Gay-Lussac's Law | V, n | P, T | P ∝ T (direct, in K) |
| Avogadro's Law | P, T | V, n | V ∝ n (direct) |

#### Worked Solution: Group 1 Style Numerical

A sealed rigid container holds 4 mol of gas at 303 K and 2 atm. The temperature rises to 606 K. Find the new pressure, and identify the trap candidates.

Step 1 — Identify the law. Rigid container ⇒ volume fixed; moles fixed ⇒ **Gay-Lussac's Law**: P₁/T₁ = P₂/T₂. Temperatures MUST be in kelvin — already given, no conversion needed.
Step 2 — Substitute: P₂ = P₁ × (T₂/T₁) = 2 atm × (606/303) = 2 × 2 = **4 atm**.
Step 3 — Dimensional check: atm/K × K = atm, units consistent.
Step 4 — Pick the answer: 4 atm.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Candidates lose this mark by using 303 °C and 606 °C (without adding 273.15) and getting 4.01 atm — wrong by the 273 offset. Another common error is applying Charles's Law (V/T) when volume is fixed, producing a nonsense volume figure.

#### Energy and Mass Conservation

ΔU = Q − W (First Law) and the photosynthesis stoichiometry 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ are tested as statement-comparison items. Reaction equations must balance atom-by-atom; molar ratios of 6:6:1:6 are the standard answer key.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Audience: candidates polishing edge cases and linking the Std IX–XII topics into a single mental network.

#### Edge Cases the Examiner Loves

1. **Non-ohmic conductors.** Ohm's Law V = IR is linear only for ohmic devices at constant temperature. Filament bulbs (R rises with T), diodes (asymmetric I–V curve), and thermistors (R drops as T rises) all violate it. TNPSC frames this as "current is directly proportional to potential difference" — true only for ohmic resistors.
2. **Galilean free-fall.** g ≈ 9.8 m/s² is mass-independent in vacuum. Air resistance breaks this: a feather and a bowling ball dropped in air do not share acceleration. TNPSC includes this in the "common misconception" assertion–reason cluster.
3. **Mendel's segregation vs independent assortment.** Segregation operates on a single gene pair during meiosis I; independent assortment operates across gene pairs on different chromosomes. The two laws are not interchangeable — a frequent wrong-option distractor.
4. **Series vs parallel current.** In series, I is identical at every node (Kirchhoff's Current Law). In parallel, voltage is identical across branches. Candidates regularly reverse these in mains-circuit reasoning.
5. **Periodic trends.** Atomic radius decreases across a period (Z effective increases) but increases down a group (new shell added). Ionisation energy generally rises across a period but shows dips at half-filled and fully-filled subshell boundaries (Be→B, N→O).

#### Adjacent Topic Links

- Newton's Second Law feeds rotational dynamics (τ = Iα) and fluid mechanics (Bernoulli's equation).
- Gas laws feed the ideal gas equation PV = nRT and Dalton's Law of partial pressures.
- Photosynthesis stoichiometry feeds cellular respiration (reverse pathway, ATP yield).
- Ohm's Law feeds Kirchhoff's Laws and the power dissipation formula P = I²R = V²/R.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. Two resistors of 6 Ω and 12 Ω are connected first in series, then in parallel, across the same 24 V battery. Compute (a) total current drawn in each configuration, (b) power dissipated by each resistor in the parallel case, and (c) explain why the same battery delivers more total power in parallel even though the voltage across each resistor is unchanged.
2. A 2 kg block slides down a frictionless incline of 30° from a height of 5 m. Using energy conservation, find its speed at the bottom and the normal force from the incline during the slide. Comment on why the answer is mass-independent.
