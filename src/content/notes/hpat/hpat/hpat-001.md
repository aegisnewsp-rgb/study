# Logical Reasoning and Problem Solving

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute HPAT-Ireland revision before the ACER sitting.

**Logical Reasoning and Problem Solving** — Quick Facts for Irish medical applicants (RCSI, UCD, TCD, UL)

**What is Section 1?**
Section 1 is the first of three separately-timed HPAT-Ireland sections for standard applicants. It carries the most weight of any section by both the number of items (44 multiple-choice questions) and the longest time allocation (65 minutes). The section does not draw on any specific academic curriculum - it tests general reasoning skills acquired over time: comprehension and analysis of written passages, tables, charts and graphs; drawing conclusions; identifying assumptions; evaluating arguments; and solving quantitative and qualitative problems. It is administered by ACER (Australian Council for Educational Research) on behalf of the participating universities.

**At a glance:**
- **44 multiple-choice questions** with four or five response alternatives and a single correct answer
- **65 minutes** strictly timed (about 88 seconds per question)
- **Reasoning skills** - reading comprehension, argument analysis, assumption identification, quantitative and qualitative problem solving
- **No curriculum content** - revision is about question-style familiarity and pacing, not memorisation
- **Largest section by mark weight** - deserves the largest revision block in any 3- or 6-month plan

**HPAT Section timing reference (standard applicants):**
| Section | Skill | Questions | Duration | Mins/Q |

| 1 | Logical Reasoning & Problem Solving | 44 | 65 min | ~88 s |
| 2 | Interpersonal Understanding | 36 | 45 min | ~75 s |

**Time budget rule:** Plan 65 minutes of uninterrupted, timed practice for Section 1 alone before each full-length mock. Section 1 carries roughly 39% of total HPAT question count (44 of 112 across Sections 1-3) and is the longest in time, so under-revising it is the most common score-leak pattern.

### 🟡 Standard — Detailed Review (1d–2w)
> A working understanding for steady preparation across a few weeks.

**What Section 1 actually measures**
Section 1 assesses reasoning skills that are independent of any specific academic curriculum. The productive way to think about the section is as **three sub-categories** blended inside a single 65-minute block:

1. **Reading and information extraction** - dense written passages (clinical scenarios, scientific or social-science excerpts, occasionally tables and charts). The first task is to locate the question-relevant sentence(s); the second is to interpret what the passage says versus what it implies.
2. **Critical reasoning** - identifying an argument's structure, the assumption that links premise to conclusion, an alternative explanation for the same evidence, or a flaw in the chain. These questions are not about whether you agree with the conclusion - they are about whether the *reasoning* supports it.
3. **Quantitative and qualitative problem solving** - applying simple arithmetic, ratios, percentages, unit conversions, and logical operators to small data sets or written problems. The math is deliberately low-floor (no algebra beyond ratios and percentages); the difficulty is in parsing the wording and identifying what is asked.

**Why curriculum revision does not help**
HPAT-Ireland is built on the principle that preparation should not be limited to those with a particular academic background. ACER publishes a small number of official Practice Tests - these are the only published real past questions - and additional official preparation materials. Third-party HPAT prep books and online banks exist, but they offer practice items of varying alignment and the only authoritative source of section style and pacing is the official material. Spending prep time on Leaving Certificate content (biology, chemistry, physics) for the *purpose* of HPAT Section 1 is wasted - none of it is tested.

**What does improve Section 1 scores**
The pattern that improves Section 1 scores, in order of leverage:
1. **Timed full-section runs** - 65-minute practice sets with strict stop, scored afterwards. Pacing under timed conditions is the single largest improvement lever.
2. **Official ACER Practice Tests** - the only published real questions; work through them after the practice-question and practice-placement stages.
3. **Section-specific drill** - sub-sections where you keep missing (e.g. argument-evaluation questions) for 20-30 minute focused blocks, then return to full-section runs.
4. **Error-log review** - keep a one-line note per missed practice question: type, reason (misread, ran out of time, wrong assumption, miscalculated), and the correction. Patterns in the error log identify the actual weak sub-skill.
5. **Wide and critical reading** - regular reading of long-form journalism (feature articles, opinion essays, science explainers) builds the underlying comprehension and inference skills tested in Section 1.

### High-Yield Topics

**Topics to drill down in detail, in priority order:**

1. **Argument structure and assumption identification** - the largest single question-type cluster in published ACER materials. The skill is to read the passage, then identify the *unstated* link between the premise and the conclusion. Common wrong answers add a plausible-sounding but logically disconnected claim.

2. **Drawing conclusions and identifying alternatives** - questions that ask "which of the following can be *concluded* from the passage" test careful reading; "which of the following *most weakens the argument*" tests understanding of what makes a claim stronger or weaker. The two question types are mirror images; mastery of one transfers to the other.

3. **Quantitative reasoning with short data sets** - small tables, percentages, ratios, unit conversions. The math is deliberately low-floor (no algebra beyond ratios and percentages); the difficulty is in parsing the wording, ignoring irrelevant numbers, and identifying what the question is asking.

4. **Tables, charts and graphs** - reading values, comparing values, identifying trends and anomalies. Practice always involves reading the data once carefully before attempting any item, because ACER's items are deliberately designed to misdirect on a quick read.

5. **Inference beyond the text** - "which of the following can be *inferred*", "which statement is *most strongly supported*". These test the difference between what is *said* and what is *implied*. The wrong answers paraphrase the text; the correct answer is one step beyond.

6. **Pacing and time management** - 88 seconds per question is tight. The productive pattern is to skip-and-flag a hard question within 30 seconds, attempt all remaining questions in order, then return to flagged questions in any remaining time. A blank answer scores zero; an attempted answer has a 20-25% chance of being correct by elimination.

### Previous Year Patterns

**Recurring Section 1 patterns in ACER practice materials (qualitative, not quantitative):**

1. **Long-form passage with 4-6 items** - one dense written passage (often 400-600 words) followed by 4-6 multiple-choice items. Items test different reasoning skills (assumption, conclusion, inference, evaluation). The pattern rewards reading the passage *once* carefully then answering items in sequence without re-reading.

2. **Short passage (1 paragraph) with 1-2 items** - one short paragraph (often 80-150 words) with one or two items. The skill is to read the passage, identify the *single* reasoning move it makes, then apply it. They are designed to be finished in <90 seconds per pair.

4. **Quantitative reasoning with small tables** - a small data table (5-8 rows) with one or two items testing arithmetic, ratios, percentages. Common wrong answers confuse rows or misread units.

5. **Argument evaluation with no data** - a passage presenting an argument (premise → conclusion) with items asking for the assumption, an alternative explanation, or the strongest objection. The skill is to identify the *unstated* link and what would break it.

6. **Mixed-format sets** - some sets combine a short passage with a table or graph. Items test whether the writer's claim is supported by the data. The skill is to ignore the framing and check the numbers.

**Pattern to watch:** ACER practice materials rotate the *types* of reasoning across items within a passage, so a single long passage may test assumption, conclusion, evaluation and quantitative reasoning across 4-6 items. Plan to spend 10-12 minutes per long passage with 4-6 items, and <90 seconds per item in a short-answer pair.

### Pro Tips

1. **Use the official ACER Practice Tests first.** They are the only published real past questions and the only authoritative source of section style. Work through them after the practice-question and practice-placement stages described on the ACER preparation page.

2. **Pacing under timed conditions is the single largest improvement lever.** Section 1's 65-minute allocation is tight (88 seconds/item). Run all Section 1 practice sets under strict 65-minute timing; never extend the timer because the section is finite in 100 minutes.

3. **Don't skip-and-flag inside a 10-question mini-block.** Within a single passage's set of 4-6 items, attempting them in order under 90 seconds each is faster than building a flag list and returning. Reserve skip-and-flag for items you cannot parse at all.

4. **Skip only on full blank.** If you can eliminate one or two alternatives, mark your best guess and move on. A 25% chance beats 0%. The cost of leaving an item blank is guaranteed zero; the cost of a fast-but-educated guess is at most 0.75 of one.

5. **Section 1 is your biggest score lever.** It has the longest time allocation (65 minutes) and the most questions (44) of any HPAT section. Under-revising it - the 1:1:1 plan that gives equal time to Sections 1, 2 and 3 - is the most common score-leak pattern. A 2:1:1 plan (Section 1:Section 2:Section 3) reflects the actual time and question weighting.

6. **Read the data before asking the question.** ACER deliberately designs items to misdirect on a first scan of the data. Read the table or chart carefully before reading the item; if you skim the item first, you'll project your own framing onto the data.

7. **Wide reading compounds.** Long-form journalism (feature articles, opinion essays, science explainers, e.g. The Atlantic, New Yorker, Aeon, grump) builds the underlying comprehension and inference skills tested in Section 1. The single best non-HPAT habit for Section 1 is reading two or three long-form articles per week for the six months before the test.

8. **Don't conflate Section 1 with what you know.** Section 1 invites you to use prior knowledge - "of course the heart pumps blood". The right answer is rarely the one that requires outside knowledge. Strip the item to what the passage actually says.

10. **Confirm Section 1 in the live Information Booklet before each cycle.** ACER updates the booklet annually. Verify the live question count, time allocation, response format and the participating universities' score-combination rules on hpat-ireland.acer.org before committing a revision plan to a student.
