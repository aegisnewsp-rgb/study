# Important Days and International Observances (Bangladesh & World)

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Bangladesh observes **six core national days** plus two cultural festivals, while the **UN General Assembly** and **UNESCO** declare world observances. For DU Admission (GK section, ~3% weight), questions typically ask: *date of observance*, *issuing body*, *historical event*, or *UN theme-year* pair.

#### Bangladesh National Days (must-memorise)

- **21 February** — *Shaheed Dibas / International Mother Language Day* (1952 Language Movement; UN adopted 1999, first observed 2000).
- **26 March** — *Independence Day* (1971 declaration of sovereignty).
- **25 March** — *Genocide Day / Black Night* (Operation Searchlight, 1971).
- **17 April** — *Mujibnagar Day* (provisional Govt.-in-exile formed at Baidyanathtala, Meherpur).
- **15 August** — *National Mourning Day* (assassination of Bangabandhu Sheikh Mujibur Rahman, 1975).
- **16 December** — *Victory Day* (Pakistani surrender at Ramna Race Course, Dhaka).
- **14 February** — *Pahela Falgun* (spring festival); **14 April** — *Pahela Boishakh* (Bengali New Year).

> Common trap: 25 March (Genocide) vs 26 March (Independence) are consecutive dates — examiners exploit this swap regularly.

Three UN dates appear almost every year in MCQs: **8 March** (Women's Day), **1 May** (Labour Day), **10 December** (Human Rights Day).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### How Observances Are Declared

**National days of Bangladesh** are notified through Acts of Parliament, Government Gazette circulars, or cabinet division declarations — for example, 21 February was declared *Shaheed Dibas* by the Bangladesh government in 1952's aftermath, and *International Mother Language Day* was proclaimed by **UNGA Resolution A/RES/54/250** on 17 November 1999.

**International observances** fall into three categories:

- **UN General Assembly days** — adopted by resolution of the 193 member states (e.g., Human Rights Day, 10 December, marking the 1948 Universal Declaration).
- **UNESCO days** — declared by the General Conference of UNESCO (e.g., World Book Day, 23 April; World Heritage Day, 18 April, coordinated with ICOMOS).
- **UN agency-specific observances** — WHO (World Health Day, 7 April), ILO (Labour Day, 1 May), UNEP (Environment Day, 5 June).

#### Key Personalities & Events

The **Language Movement of 21 February 1952** saw students Salmin, Barkat, Rafiquddin, Jabbar, and Shafiur Rahman killed near Dhaka Medical College. Bangladesh's proposal at UNESCO (1998) and later at the UNGA led to worldwide recognition.

The **provisional Mujibnagar government** was sworn in at Baidyanathtala, Meherpur on 17 April 1971, with **Syed Nazrul Islam** as Acting President and **Tajuddin Ahmad** as Prime Minister-in-exile.

> Examiner trap: World Press Freedom Day (3 May) is often credited solely to UNESCO — actually it was proclaimed by the **UNGA** in 1993, with UNESCO coordinating observances.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Method of Declaration & Edge Cases

UN observances originate from one of three routes: (1) **UNGA resolution** requiring a plenary vote, (2) **UNESCO General Conference** decisions adopted every two years, or (3) **specialised agency** mandates (WHO, ILO, UNEP). Bangladesh's six national days are entrenched in state commemoration practice and reinforced through school curricula — questions on issuing authority are common.

#### Theme-Year Pairings (Past Examples)

Each UN day carries an annual theme. Recent themes that appeared in Bangladesh admission papers include:

- **International Women's Day** — theme rotates yearly (e.g., "Gender equality today for a sustainable tomorrow").
- **World Environment Day** — host-country-led (India, Pakistan, Sweden have hosted).
- **World Press Freedom Day** — UNESCO awards the Guillermo Cano World Press Freedom Prize on 3 May.

A frequent MCQ variant asks: *"In 2021, World Press Freedom Day theme focused on…?"* — students must remember theme-year pairs, not just dates.

#### Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Pahela Falgun (14 Feb)** vs **Pahela Boishakh (14 April)** — Falgun celebrates the first day of spring (Bengali month Falgun), Boishakh marks the Bengali New Year.
- The **Mujibnagar government** formed at Meherpur, **not** Dhaka (Dhaka fell on 16 December 1971).
- **21 February 1952** is the martyrdom event; the **UN recognition** came in **1999** — never write 1952 as the UN declaration year.
- **Victory Day (16 Dec)** and **Independence Day (26 March)** are sometimes swapped by careless students.

#### Practice Prompts

1. Identify the day, date, and historical event: *"On this day in 1971, Pakistani forces launched a brutal crackdown at Dhaka University, beginning the genocide."* Expected answer: *Genocide Day / Black Night, 25 March 1971, Operation Searchlight.*
2. State the issuing body and resolution year for International Mother Language Day — and list two named 1952 martyrs. Expected answer: *UNGA Resolution A/RES/54/250 (1999), first observed 2000; Salmin, Barkat.*
