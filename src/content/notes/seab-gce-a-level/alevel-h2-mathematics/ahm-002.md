# Sequences and Series — H2 Mathematics (9758) Notes

Sequences and Series is the second content strand in the H2 Mathematics 9758 syllabus. The strand covers arithmetic and geometric progressions, sum of first n terms and sum to infinity, the binomial expansion of (1 + x)^n for any rational n, and (at HL) the method of differences for summation. Sequences and Series items appear on Paper 1 alongside Functions, Calculus and Complex Numbers; a JC2 student aiming at an A or B grade must be able to switch fluently between sequence notation, closed-form formulas and summation techniques.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### The seven facts every JC2 student needs

1. **Sequence**: an ordered list of numbers a₁, a₂, a₃, ….
2. **Arithmetic progression (AP)**: a sequence with constant common difference d. aₙ = a + (n − 1)d.
3. **Geometric progression (GP)**: a sequence with constant common ratio r. aₙ = a r^(n−1).
4. **Sum of an AP**: Sₙ = n/2 × (2a + (n − 1)d) = n/2 × (a + l), where l is the last term.
5. **Sum of a GP** (finite): Sₙ = a (1 − rⁿ) / (1 − r) for r ≠ 1.
6. **Sum to infinity of a GP**: S_∞ = a / (1 − r) for |r| < 1.
7. **Binomial expansion** of (1 + x)^n for any rational n: (1 + x)^n = 1 + nx + n(n−1)/2! x² + n(n−1)(n−2)/3! x³ + … (the series is infinite when n is not a positive integer; for |x| < 1 the series converges).

#### Key notations

- aₙ — the nth term of a sequence
- Sₙ — the sum of the first n terms
- r — the common ratio of a GP
- d — the common difference of an AP
- l — the last term of an AP or GP
- ∞ — infinity (in the sense of sum to infinity, which exists only when the terms approach zero)

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Arithmetic progressions

An **arithmetic progression** (AP) is a sequence where the difference between consecutive terms is constant: aₙ₊₁ − aₙ = d for all n.

The nth term: aₙ = a + (n − 1)d, where a is the first term and d is the common difference.

Sum of the first n terms: Sₙ = n/2 × (2a + (n − 1)d) = n/2 × (a + l), where l is the nth term.

#### Geometric progressions

A **geometric progression** (GP) is a sequence where the ratio between consecutive terms is constant: aₙ₊₁ / aₙ = r for all n.

The nth term: aₙ = a r^(n−1), where a is the first term and r is the common ratio.

Sum of the first n terms: Sₙ = a (1 − rⁿ) / (1 − r) for r ≠ 1. For r = 1, Sₙ = na.

Sum to infinity: S_∞ = a / (1 − r) exists when |r| < 1 (the terms approach zero).

#### Sigma notation

Σ is the Greek capital sigma and denotes summation. Σ_{k=1}^n a_k means a₁ + a₂ + … + aₙ.

Useful formulas:

- Σ_{k=1}^n k = n(n+1)/2
- Σ_{k=1}^n k² = n(n+1)(2n+1)/6
- Σ_{k=1}^n k³ = [n(n+1)/2]²

#### Binomial expansion for positive integer n

For a positive integer n, the binomial expansion of (a + b)^n is:

(a + b)^n = Σ_{k=0}^n C(n, k) a^(n−k) b^k

For (1 + x)^n specifically:

(1 + x)^n = Σ_{k=0}^n C(n, k) x^k

The coefficient of x^k is C(n, k) = n! / [k! (n − k)!].

For a general binomial (a + b)^n with a ≠ 1, factor out a: (a + b)^n = a^n (1 + b/a)^n = a^n Σ_{k=0}^n C(n, k) (b/a)^k = Σ_{k=0}^n C(n, k) a^(n−k) b^k.

#### Binomial expansion for rational n (HL)

For a rational n (not a positive integer) and |x| < 1, the binomial expansion of (1 + x)^n is an infinite series:

(1 + x)^n = 1 + nx + n(n−1)/2! x² + n(n−1)(n−2)/3! x³ + …

For n a negative integer or a fraction, the series is infinite. The general term is:

T_{k+1} = C(n, k) x^k = [n(n−1)(n−2)…(n−k+1) / k!] x^k

The series converges when |x| < 1 (or at the boundary for some values of n).

This expansion is used to find series for 1/(1 + x), 1/√(1 + x), √(1 + x), and similar functions.

#### Method of differences (HL)

The **method of differences** is used to find the sum of a series whose general term can be written as a difference of consecutive terms of another series.

Example: find Σ_{k=1}^n [1 / (k(k+1))]. Note that 1 / (k(k+1)) = 1/k − 1/(k+1). The series telescopes:

Σ_{k=1}^n [1/k − 1/(k+1)] = (1 − 1/2) + (1/2 − 1/3) + … + (1/n − 1/(n+1)) = 1 − 1/(n+1) = n / (n + 1).

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where Sequences and Series appears across the H2 Mathematics papers

- **H2 Mathematics 9758 Paper 1**: short to medium structured items on AP, GP, and the binomial expansion for positive integer n. The HL-only infinite-series content appears on Paper 3.
- **H2 Mathematics 9758 Paper 3**: longer structured items on the binomial expansion for rational n and on the method of differences for summation.
- **Across the papers**: writing the nth-term formula explicitly before summing is the highest-leverage exam technique for sequence-and-series items.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "The sum to infinity of a GP always exists." It exists only when |r| < 1; for |r| ≥ 1, the terms do not approach zero and the sum diverges.
- "The nth term of a GP is aₙ = arⁿ." The nth term is aₙ = a r^(n−1), not a rⁿ (unless the indexing starts at 0).
- "The binomial expansion of (1 + x)^n always has n + 1 terms." For positive integer n, yes. For rational or negative integer n, the series is infinite.
- "C(n, k) = n! / k!" No — C(n, k) = n! / [k! (n − k)!].
- "The method of differences works for any series." It works only for series whose general term can be written as a difference of consecutive terms.

#### Exam technique

- For AP / GP questions, write the values of a and d (or a and r) explicitly before computing. The parameter statement is the method mark.
- For sigma questions, write out the first few terms of the sum before applying the formula. The first few terms are the method mark.
- For binomial-expansion questions, write the general term T_{k+1} before substituting. The general term is the method mark.
- For method-of-differences questions, write the difference form of the general term explicitly. The difference form is the method mark.
