# Introduction to Organic Chemistry and Hydrocarbons

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Organic chemistry** is the study of carbon-containing compounds, primarily those with C–H bonds; **hydrocarbons** are the simplest subset, built only from carbon and hydrogen. They split into **aliphatic** (straight or branched chains and rings) and **aromatic** (benzene-type) families.

| Family | Bonding | Hybridisation | General formula |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alkanes | C–C single | sp³ | CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ (n ≥ 1) |
| Alkenes | C=C double | sp² | CₙH₂ₙ (n ≥ 2) |
| Alkynes | C≡C triple | sp | CₙH₂ₙ₋₂ (n ≥ 2) |
| Cycloalkanes | ring single | sp³ | CₙH₂ₙ (n ≥ 3) |

- **Degree of unsaturation** DoU = (2C + 2 − H)/2 lets you deduce rings + π-bonds from a molecular formula.
- **Alkanes** undergo **free-radical substitution** (UV/Cl₂); **alkenes** undergo **electrophilic addition** (Br₂, HBr, H₂O/H⁺).
- For HAAD, expect 1–2 MCQs on IUPAC naming of C₁–C₆ chains, functional-group identification, and reaction-type matching.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Catenation and Hybridisation
Carbon's tetravalency and **catenation** (self-linking into long chains and rings) make complex molecules possible. The type of C–C bond fixes the geometry: sp³ is tetrahedral at 109.5°, sp² is trigonal planar at 120°, and sp is linear at 180°. A double bond contains one **σ bond** plus one **π bond**; a triple bond has one σ plus two π.

#### IUPAC Nomenclature Rules
1. Identify the longest carbon chain that contains the **principal functional group** and the maximum number of C=C/C≡C bonds.
2. Number from the end giving the **lowest locant** to the first point of unsaturation or substituent.
3. Use prefixes **di-, tri-, tetra-** for multiple identical groups; combine locants as commas (e.g. 2,3-dimethylbutane).
4. Suffixes encode the family: **-ane, -ene, -yne, -ol, -oic acid**.

#### Isomerism
**Structural isomers** share a molecular formula but differ in connectivity (chain, position, or functional-group). **Stereoisomers** (cis/trans around a C=C) require each alkene carbon to bear two different groups.

#### Reaction Types
Alkanes are saturated and undergo **combustion** or **free-radical substitution**. Alkenes are unsaturated and undergo **electrophilic addition**; **Markovnikov's rule** predicts that H⁺ from HX adds to the carbon already bearing more hydrogens, generating the more stable carbocation intermediate. Benzene resists addition because of **delocalised π electrons** and instead undergoes **electrophilic aromatic substitution**.

> **Trap:** Alkenes (CₙH₂ₙ) and cycloalkanes (CₙH₂ₙ) share the same general formula but are **not** the same class — a question giving only the formula cannot distinguish them.

#### Worked Example
Propene (CH₃–CH=CH₂, C₃H₆) + HBr → 2-bromopropane as the major product, because H⁺ adds to C1 (more H), placing Br on C2 (more substituted carbocation). Numbering from the alkene end gives the locant 2; adding H from the other carbon restores a single bond on each carbon.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Degree of Unsaturation in Practice
For C₆H₆, DoU = (12 + 2 − 6)/2 = **4** — three π-bonds plus one ring, confirming the aromatic structure. The extended formula DoU = (2C + 2 + N − H − X)/2 adds nitrogen as +½ contribution and halogens as −½, which matters for HAAD items involving halogenated hydrocarbons or amines.

#### Benzene Behaviour
Benzene does **not** decolourise bromine water under standard conditions, unlike cyclohexene. Under FeBr₃ catalysis it undergoes **halogenation**; HNO₃/H₂SO₄ gives **nitration**; fuming H₂SO₄ gives **sulfonation**; RCl/AlCl₃ yields **Friedel–Crafts alkylation**, and RCOCl/AlCl₃ gives acylation. All proceed through a **Wheland (sigma-complex) intermediate**.

#### Common Pitfalls in MCQs
- Drawing **1,3,5-cyclohexatriene** instead of the delocalised ring — this costs marks because Kekulé structures misrepresent bond equivalence.
- Counting C=C as **two bonds** in DoU calculations; each double bond contributes **DoU = 1**.
- Forgetting that combustion of an alkane needs **(3n+1)/2 O₂** to balance to CO₂ and H₂O.
- Confusing **substitution** (alkane + Cl₂/UV) with **addition** (alkene + Br₂), a frequent HAAD distractor.

#### Adjacent Topics to Link
Hydrocarbons feed directly into **functional-group families** (halogenoalkanes, alcohols, aldehydes, carboxylic acids) and into **polymer chemistry** (ethene → polyethene; propene → polypropene). Combustion stoichiometry recurs in **energy-chemistry** calculations for HAAD general-science questions.

#### Practice Prompts
1. A compound has molecular formula C₄H₆. Calculate DoU, name two possible structures, and state how you would distinguish them chemically with bromine.
2. Predict the major product of CH₃CH=CHCH₃ + HCl and justify using Markovnikov's rule, naming any stereoisomer produced.
