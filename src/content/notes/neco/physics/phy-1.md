# Physical Quantities, Units and Measurements

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

A **physical quantity** is anything in physics that can be measured, written as a **numerical magnitude × unit** (e.g. 5 m, 12 kg). Seven **fundamental quantities** — length [L], mass [M], time [T], current [I], temperature [Θ or K], amount of substance [N or mol], luminous intensity [J or cd] — form the SI base. Every other quantity is **derived** from these (e.g. velocity = LT⁻¹, force = MLT⁻²). The SI base units are the **metre (m)**, **kilogram (kg)**, **second (s)**, **ampere (A)**, **kelvin (K)**, **mole (mol)** and **candela (cd)**. Common prefixes: **kilo (10³)**, **centi (10⁻²)**, **milli (10⁻³)**, **micro (10⁻⁶)**. Measurement instruments include the **metre rule** (MA = 0.1 cm), **vernier caliper** (MA = 0.01 cm) and **micrometer screw gauge** (MA = 0.01 mm). Remember: apply **zero error correction** before recording any final reading.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Fundamental vs Derived Quantities
The SI system rests on **seven base quantities** from which all other quantities are mathematically constructed. A **derived quantity** is obtained by multiplication or division of base quantities; its unit (a derived SI unit) follows the same algebraic operations. Example derivations:

- **Velocity** = length / time → unit m s⁻¹, dimensional form **[L T⁻¹]**
- **Acceleration** = velocity / time → m s⁻², dimension **[L T⁻²]**
- **Force** = mass × acceleration → kg m s⁻² = **newton (N)**, dimension **[M L T⁻²]**
- **Work/Energy** = force × distance → **joule (J) = N m**
- **Pressure** = force / area → **pascal (Pa) = N m⁻²**

Knowing these dimensions lets you check the correctness of physics formulas.

#### Dimensional Analysis
An equation is **dimensionally homogeneous** when the dimensions of every term on both sides are identical. To check whether `v = u + at` is valid: left side is [L T⁻¹], right side gives [L T⁻¹] + [L T⁻²][T] = [L T⁻¹] + [L T⁻¹] — both sides match, so the equation can be dimensionally correct. Dimensional analysis can also **derive relationships**: if a pendulum's period T depends only on length l, mass m and gravity g, then T = k·lᵃ mᵇ gᶜ; solving yields T = 2π√(l/g).

#### Errors and Uncertainty
**Accuracy** = closeness to the true value; **precision** = closeness of repeated readings to each other. Random errors scatter readings around the mean and are reduced by repeating and averaging. **Systematic errors** shift every reading in one direction (e.g. zero error, parallax) and are removed by **calibration**. Uncertainty is reported as ± absolute uncertainty; **percentage error = (|error|/true value) × 100%**.

#### Instruments and Least Count
Always quote a reading to the **least count** of the instrument and apply any **zero error** correction:

| Instrument | Quantity measured | Least count | Reading rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Metre rule | Length | 0.1 cm | Read at eye level, avoid parallax |
| Vernier caliper | Length (internal/external) | 0.01 cm | Main scale + (coincidence × 0.01 cm) |
| Micrometer screw gauge | Small thickness/diameter | 0.01 mm | Main scale + (thimble × 0.01 mm), ± zero error |

#### Exam Patterns in NECO SSCE
Paper 1 (Objective) typically tests: identification of base/derived quantities, SI units, dimensional homogeneity, and identification of instruments. Paper 2 (Essay) commonly asks students to **measure the diameter of a wire** using a micrometer, **determine the zero error of a vernier caliper**, or **state precautions** taken during measurement (e.g. avoid parallax, ensure instrument is at zero before use).

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Reading Mechanics — Worked Numbers

**Vernier caliper:** main scale reads 2.4 cm, vernier coincidence at the 7th division. Zero error = +0.02 cm.
True reading = (2.4 + 7 × 0.01) − 0.02 = **2.45 cm**.

**Micrometer screw gauge:** main scale = 7.5 mm, thimble scale = 23, zero error = −0.03 mm.
True reading = (7.5 + 23 × 0.01) − (−0.03) = 7.73 + 0.03 = **7.76 mm**.

#### Significant Figures and Scientific Notation
A **significant figure** is every digit known with certainty plus the first uncertain digit. Zeros between non-zeros are significant (305 → 3 s.f.); leading zeros are never significant (0.0042 → 2 s.f.). NECO often reports answers to the least count of the measuring instrument — a vernier reading should be given to 2 decimal places in cm.

#### Common Traps
- **Confusing accuracy and precision** — a set of readings may be precise (clustered) yet inaccurate (biased instrument).
- **Forgetting zero error correction** — examiners present "raw" micrometer readings; applying the sign correctly (+ or −) is the differentiator.
- **Mixing units** — adding 4 cm to 60 mm without converting to a single unit (4 cm + 6 cm = 10 cm).
- **Dimensional misuse** — quantities with different dimensions can never be added (e.g. joules + metres is meaningless).
- **Parallax** is a *systematic* error; minimised by aligning the eye perpendicular to the scale.

#### Connection to Other Topics
Dimensional analysis underpins the derivation of formulae in **mechanics** (kinematics, projectiles, SHM), **waves** (v = fλ), and **electricity** (V = IR has dimension [M L² T⁻³ I⁻¹]). Every subsequent formula in SSCE Physics should pass the dimensional-homogeneity test.

#### Practice Prompts
1. A student measures a block's length three times and obtains 5.42 cm, 5.40 cm and 5.43 cm using a vernier caliper. Calculate the **mean**, the **absolute uncertainty**, and the **percentage error** of the measurement.
2. The period T of a simple pendulum is suspected to depend on length l, mass m and gravitational acceleration g. Using **dimensional analysis**, show that T ∝ √(l/g) and hence determine the constant k when T = 2.0 s, l = 1.0 m, g = 9.8 m s⁻².
