# Fractions, Decimals and Percentages

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

A **fraction** a/b represents *a* parts out of *b* equal parts, with **denominator** *b* ≠ 0. A **decimal** is the base-10 equivalent written with a decimal point, and a **percentage** is the value scaled per hundred. All three describe the same rational number.

- **Percentage formula:** % = (Part / Whole) × 100
- **Part from percent:** Part = (% / 100) × Whole
- **Decimal from percent:** shift the decimal point two places left (25% → 0.25)
- **Fraction to percent:** multiply numerator by 100, then divide by denominator

Expect 1–2 MCQs in the HAT-UG Quantitative Reasoning section, usually a percentage-change word problem or a conversion question.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Definitions

A **fraction** a/b has **numerator** *a* (parts taken) and **denominator** *b* (total equal parts). When *a* < *b* it is *proper*; when *a* ≥ *b* it is *improper* and convertible to a *mixed* number (whole part + proper fraction). Two fractions a/b and c/d are **equivalent** when ad = bc (cross-multiplication test). A **terminating decimal** finishes after finitely many digits (denominator's only prime factors are 2 and 5); a **recurring decimal** repeats a digit block indefinitely.

#### Conversion Pathways

| From → To | Procedure |
| --- | --- |
| Fraction → Decimal | Divide numerator by denominator (long division) |
| Fraction → Percentage | (a ÷ b) × 100, or multiply numerator by 100 then divide by denominator |
| Decimal → Percentage | Shift decimal point two places right (0.25 → 25%) |
| Percentage → Fraction | Write over 100, then reduce to lowest terms (25% → 25/100 → 1/4) |

#### Operations and Comparison

To **add or subtract** fractions, find the **LCM** of denominators (the LCD), convert each fraction, then combine numerators. To **multiply**, multiply numerators and denominators directly, then reduce. To **divide** by a fraction, **invert-and-multiply** (multiply by the reciprocal). To **compare** a/b vs c/d, compute ad vs cd without computing the actual decimals.

#### Exam Traps

- **Percentage points ≠ percentage change.** A rise from 5% to 7% is a 2-percentage-point shift but a 40% relative increase ((7−5)/5 × 100).
- **Wrong base.** Percentage change uses the *original* value in the denominator, never the new value.
- **Decimal-point slip.** 25% means 0.25, not 25.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Worked Example

A jacket priced at Rs 4,000 receives a 15% discount, then a 5% tax is added to the discounted price. What is the final amount paid?

1. Discount = 15% × 4000 = 0.15 × 4000 = Rs 600
2. Sale price = 4000 − 600 = Rs 3,400
3. Tax = 5% × 3400 = 0.05 × 3400 = Rs 170
4. Final amount = 3400 + 170 = **Rs 3,570**

#### Successive Percentage Changes

Two successive changes of +a% and +b% on the same base do **not** add to (a + b)%. The correct combined multiplier is (1 + a/100)(1 + b/100) = 1 + (a + b + ab/100)/100. A 20% rise followed by a 20% fall is **not** zero; it is a net 4% decrease because 1.20 × 0.80 = 0.96.

#### Adjacent Topics and Edge Cases

Fractions connect directly to **ratio and proportion** (a:b is a fraction in disguise) and to **time–rate–work** problems where parts of a job are expressed as fractions. **Recurring decimals** like 1/3 = 0.333… are exact; treat them as the original fraction, never round prematurely in multi-step chains. A common HAT-UG phrasing asks "X is what percent of Y?" — invert and multiply: (X/Y) × 100.

#### Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
| --- | --- |
| Using the *new* value as base in % change | Always divide by the *original* value |
| Treating 25% as 25 when multiplying | Convert to 0.25 first |
| Adding fractions without a common denominator | Find LCM, convert, then combine |
| Saying "5% to 7% is a 2% increase" | Clarify: 2 percentage points, 40% relative change |

#### Practice Prompts

1. If 3/8 of a number is 180, what is 5/6 of the same number? (Answer: 400)
2. A population grows from 12,000 to 13,500. Express the change as a percentage and as a fraction in lowest terms. (Answer: 12.5% = 1/8)
