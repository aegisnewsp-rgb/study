# Trial Balance

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

A **Trial Balance** is a statement (never an account) prepared on a particular date listing every debit and credit ledger balance to test the **arithmetical accuracy** of double-entry postings. CMA Foundation Paper 1 sets roughly 3% weight on this topic, mostly through MCQs on purpose, methods, and suspense figures.

- **Total Method:** Σ(debit column totals) = Σ(credit column totals). Each side sums opening balance + period entries.
- **Balance Method:** Σ(debit balances) = Σ(credit balances). Only closing balances appear.
- **Total & Balance Method:** Combines both — totals columns plus a derived balance column, totals always larger than or equal to balances.
- **Suspense Account:** Holds the difference when the two sides disagree; balance in ₹ (INR).

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** "**T-B-S**" — **T**est arithmetical accuracy, **B**alances from ledger, **S**uspense for the difference. Trial Balance tallies ≠ error-free; it only catches posting/totaling mistakes, not errors of principle.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Definition and Purpose

A Trial Balance is a working paper drawn from ledger balances on a specific date. Its sole arithmetical purpose is to verify that, for every transaction recorded, the debit equals the credit. It is **not** a financial statement and carries no legal standing, unlike a Balance Sheet which it superficially resembles.

#### Three Methods of Preparation

The Total Method posts both opening balances and the full period totals of every ledger account; this method is rare in practice because it doubles work. The Balance Method extracts only closing balances and is the textbook choice for CMA Foundation MCQs. The Total-and-Balance Method shows four columns: debit total, credit total, debit balance, credit balance — balances being derived as the difference of totals per account.

| Method | Columns shown | What is summed | Practical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total | Dr total, Cr total | Opening balance + period entries | Verification only |
| Balance | Dr balance, Cr balance | Closing balances only | Most common |
| Total & Balance | Dr total, Cr total, Dr balance, Cr balance | Both totals and balances | Detailed working paper |

#### Errors Disclosed vs Not Disclosed

A non-tallying Trial Balance always points to one or more posting/totaling errors. A tallying one does **not** prove the books are correct — several classes of error remain invisible.

| Disclosed by Trial Balance | Not Disclosed by Trial Balance |
|---|---|
| Posting to wrong side (debit as credit) | Error of principle (e.g., capital exp. treated as revenue) |
| Partial omission of a double entry | Compensating errors (two opposite mistakes cancel) |
| Wrong amount posted to ledger | Error of original entry (wrong amount in both books) |
| Omission of a balance entirely | Complete omission of entry |
| Incorrect totaling or balancing | Posting to wrong account but on the correct side |

> 📌 **Formula Check:** Suspense figure = |Σ(Debit column) − Σ(Credit column)|, expressed in ₹. Placed on the shorter side to make the Trial Balance agree before rectification.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** From the following ledger balances of M/s. Karan & Co. on 31-03-2026, prepare a Trial Balance and compute the Suspense figure (in ₹): Capital ₹3,00,000; Sundry Debtors ₹1,80,000; Sundry Creditors ₹1,20,000; Purchases ₹6,50,000; Sales ₹8,40,000; Wages ₹45,000; Salaries ₹60,000; Rent ₹24,000; Cash in Hand ₹18,000; Bank Overdraft ₹35,000; Closing Stock ₹42,000; Drawings ₹25,000; Machinery ₹1,90,000.

#### Solution:

#### Step 1 — Classify each balance as debit or credit.

| Account | Nature | Dr (₹) | Cr (₹) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capital | Liability | — | 3,00,000 |
| Sundry Debtors | Asset | 1,80,000 | — |
| Sundry Creditors | Liability | — | 1,20,000 |
| Purchases | Expense | 6,50,000 | — |
| Sales | Income | — | 8,40,000 |
| Wages | Expense | 45,000 | — |
| Salaries | Expense | 60,000 | — |
| Rent | Expense | 24,000 | — |
| Cash in Hand | Asset | 18,000 | — |
| Bank Overdraft | Liability | — | 35,000 |
| Closing Stock | Asset | 42,000 | — |
| Drawings | Capital reduction | 25,000 | — |
| Machinery | Asset | 1,90,000 | — |
| **Totals** | | **12,34,000** | **12,95,000** |

#### Step 2 — Compute the Suspense figure.

Suspense = |12,34,000 − 12,95,000| = **₹61,000** placed on the debit side (shorter side).

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students often (a) put Closing Stock on the credit side as a "liability" or omit it entirely — it is a **debit balance** when given outside the books; (b) treat Drawings as a credit balance — it is debit because it reduces capital; (c) forget that Bank Overdraft is a liability (credit balance), not an asset. Any of these will flip your suspense figure and lose marks.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Boundary Conditions

The Trial Balance is a snapshot at a single date, so adjustments for accrued income, prepaid expenses, outstanding expenses, and depreciation are usually reflected through the adjusted ledger balances, not added as separate rows. When closing stock appears in the Trial Balance after being transferred from the Trading Account, it shows a debit balance; when supplied outside the books, the accountant inserts it as a debit balance before final accounts. Contra entries between Cash and Bank columns of the Cash Book must net out before balances are extracted.

#### Common Traps and Exceptions

1. **Tallied ≠ error-free:** Compensating errors (e.g., a purchase of ₹5,000 overcast as ₹6,000 alongside a sales overcast of ₹1,000) leave totals equal but books wrong.
2. **Suspense is temporary:** Once the underlying error is located, rectification entries are passed through Suspense until its balance becomes zero.
3. **Single-entry failure:** Under single-entry, Trial Balance cannot be drawn directly because only personal and cash accounts are complete.
4. **Wrong-side posting of equal amount:** A ₹10,000 sale posted to Purchases creates a ₹20,000 imbalance, not ₹10,000 — debit ₹10,000 to Sales, debit ₹10,000 to Purchases = total debits inflated by ₹20,000 vs credits.
5. **Opening vs closing balance duplication:** Including both opening capital and current year's net profit as separate credit balances double-counts equity.

> 💡 **High-Yield Revision Hook:** "**DPCO**" — the four error classes trial balance **c**atches: **D**ebit-credit wrong side, **P**artial omission, **C**ast/total mistakes, **O**mission of a balance. Anything outside DPCO slips through.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. A Trial Balance tallies but the auditor suspects an error of principle (₹50,000 furniture purchase debited to Repairs). Identify the rectification entry and show how Suspense remains unaffected.
2. Given debit column total ₹8,42,500 and credit column total ₹8,39,200, with opening Suspense balance ₹3,300 (credit), compute the closing Suspense balance after rectification of a ₹6,600 purchase posting on the wrong side.
