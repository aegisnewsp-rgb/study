# Karnataka Polity: State Legislature, Governor, Council of Ministers, and Local Government

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The State Legislature of Karnataka is bicameral, comprising the **Vidhan Sabha** (224 directly elected + 1 nominated Anglo-Indian member) and the **Vidhan Parishad** (75 members). The **Governor**, appointed under Article 153, is the constitutional head; the **Chief Minister** heads the real executive (Council of Ministers). Local self-governance operates through a three-tier **Panchayati Raj** (Zilla, Taluk, Gram Panchayat) and **Urban Local Bodies** (Municipal Corporations, City Municipal Councils, Town Panchayats).

- **Vidhan Sabha strength:** 224 elected + 1 nominated (Article 333) = **225** total members.
- **Vidhan Parishad strength:** Fixed at **75** members (Article 171); one-third retire every 2 years.
- **Council of Ministers ceiling:** ≤**15% of Assembly** strength (91st Amendment) ≈ **33 ministers** in Karnataka.
- **Panchayati Raj tiers:** Gram → Taluk → Zilla Panchayat (Karnataka Act 1993; 73rd Amendment).
- **Governor's pardoning power:** Article 161 — **cannot** pardon death sentence or court-martial.
- **State Finance Commission:** Constituted every **5 years** under Article 243-I.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"G-M-C-S-F"** for Governor's role — **G**uardian constitution, **M**inisterial advice bound (Art 163), **C**onstitutes SFC, **S**ummons House, **F**irst citizen of state protocol. For Panchayat tiers remember **"G-T-Z" = Gram-Taluk-Zilla** (village to district, ascending population).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Composition of the Two Houses

Karnataka's legislature is one of roughly six Indian states with a bicameral system. The **Vidhan Sabha** has 224 members directly elected from single-member territorial constituencies and 1 member nominated by the Governor from the Anglo-Indian community under Article 333. Its normal term is 5 years, extendable by Parliament during an Emergency (Article 352). The **Vidhan Parishad** has a fixed strength of 75, distributed as 28 local authority, 25 Assembly electorate, 7 Governor-nominated (across literature, science, art, cooperative movement, and social service), 14 graduates', and 1 teacher's constituency under Article 171. It is a permanent body with no fixed term; one-third of members retire every 2 years.

#### The Governor and Council of Ministers

The Governor, appointed by the President under Article 153, acts on the **aid and advice** of the Council of Ministers (Article 163) — making the **Chief Minister the de facto executive head**. Key powers include summoning/proroguing the Houses, assenting to bills, reserving bills for the President (Article 200), and pardoning sentences (Article 161), **except** death and court-martial. The CM is appointed by the Governor (Article 164), and the Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Vidhan Sabha (Article 164(2)). The 91st Constitutional Amendment caps the Council's size at **15% of Assembly strength** — roughly 33 ministers in Karnataka.

| Feature | Governor (De jure) | Chief Minister (De facto) |
|---|---|---|
| Constitutional basis | Article 153 | Article 164 |
| Executive authority | Acts on aid and advice (Art 163) | Head of Council of Ministers |
| Appointment power | Appoints CM and Ministers | Recommends ministers, allocation of portfolios |
| Responsibility | Not answerable to Assembly | Answerable collectively (Art 164(2)) |
| Term | Pleasure of the President (Art 156) | Pleasure of the Governor / majority in House |

#### Local Self-Government Framework

Karnataka implements the **73rd Amendment** via the Karnataka Panchayati Raj Act, 1993, creating a three-tier structure with **5-year tenure**, reservations for SC/ST proportional to population, and 50% reservation for women (raised from the original 33%). Urban bodies operate under the Karnataka Municipal Corporations Act, 1976, and the Karnataka Municipalities Act, 1964. The **Karnataka State Election Commission** conducts elections for both Panchayats and Municipal bodies, while the **State Finance Commission** (constituted every 5 years under Article 243-I) reviews financial position.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** The maximum strength of the Council of Ministers in Karnataka (including the Chief Minister) under the 91st Constitutional Amendment is closest to:
(a) 28 (b) 33 (c) 40 (d) No statutory limit

#### Solution:
1. The 91st Amendment (2003) inserted the 15% ceiling in Article 164(1A).
2. Karnataka Vidhan Sabha total members = 224 elected + 1 nominated = **225**.
3. Ceiling = 15% × 225 = **33.75**, rounded down to **33**.
4. Hence the maximum is approximately 33 — answer **(b)**.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students often choose (d) "No statutory limit" assuming only the Lok Sabha ceiling applies, or pick (a) 28 confusing the figure with Vidhan Parishad partial retirement. Memorise: **15% rule applies to every state Assembly uniformly**.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Constitutional Nuances

1. **Article 333 Anglo-Indian Nomination:** The 104th Constitutional Amendment (2020) abolished the Anglo-Indian nominated seat in the Lok Sabha, but Article 333 (States) was **not simultaneously amended** in the same notification. The seat remains constitutionally permissible in State Assemblies until Parliament specifically amends Article 333 — a frequently missed nuance.
2. **Governor's Discretion:** Outside Article 163 aid-and-advice cases, the Governor exercises individual judgment in: (a) Article 356 report on breakdown of constitutional machinery, (b) administering Scheduled Areas, (c) deciding on a hung assembly where no clear majority emerges, and (d) seeking information from the CM under Article 167.
3. **Reservation Dynamics in Karnataka:** Karnataka raised women's reservation in Panchayats and Municipalities from 33% to **50%** by State legislation — beyond the 73rd/74th Amendment floor but constitutionally permissible. The same applies to OBC reservation, which is set against the State OBC population proportion as per census data.
4. **District Planning Committee (DPC):** Inserted by the **74th Amendment** under Article 243-ZD, the DPC consolidates plans from Panchayats and Municipalities — students often misattribute it to the 73rd Amendment or to the Panchayati Raj Act alone.
5. **Vidhan Parishad vs Rajya Sabha:** Parishad strength is **fixed at 75**; Rajya Sabha is variable (currently 245) up to 250. Money Bills **cannot originate** in the Parishad; Ordinary Bills can.
6. **State Finance Commission's Recommendations:** Unlike the Central Finance Commission, the SFC's recommendations are **not binding** on the State Government, though they guide Panchayat fund devolution.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. *"Is the Governor a rubber stamp?"* — Examine the tension between Article 163's aid-and-advice rule and discretionary powers (Art 356, hung verdicts). Reference the **Sarkaria Commission** (1988) and **Punchhi Commission** (2010) for guidance on Governor–CM relations.
2. *"Compare the 73rd and 74th Amendments in their application to Karnataka."* — Address mandatory features: tenure (5 years), reservations, SEC, SFC, DPC; highlight Karnataka-specific statutory innovations (women at 50%, separate State Acts of 1993 and 1964).

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** For pardoning power limits, recall **"President pardons Death + Court Martial; Governor pardons Everything Else"** — a one-line differentiator that eliminates MCQ confusion between Articles 72 and 161.
