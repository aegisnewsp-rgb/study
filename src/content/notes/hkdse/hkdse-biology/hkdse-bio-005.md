# V. Human Physiology — Regulation and Control (Elective) — HKDSE Biology Notes

This topic covers Elective Topic V of the HKDSE Biology Elective Part. Candidates sit any two of the four electives on Paper 2. The topic addresses regulation of water content (osmoregulation in the kidney); regulation of body temperature (mechanisms in mammals); regulation of gas content in blood (oxygen and carbon dioxide transport, ventilation control); and hormonal control of the reproductive cycle (menstrual cycle, oestrogen, progesterone, FSH, LH).

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Osmoregulation**: the kidney regulates blood water and salt content; the nephron filters blood at the glomerulus, reabsorbs water and useful solutes along the tubule, and excretes waste in urine. ADH (anti-diuretic hormone) increases water reabsorption in the collecting duct.
- **Thermoregulation**: mammals maintain a constant body temperature; the hypothalamus monitors blood temperature and triggers responses — sweating, shivering, vasodilation, vasoconstriction, behavioural changes.
- **Blood gas regulation**: oxygen is transported by haemoglobin (oxyhaemoglobin); carbon dioxide is transported as bicarbonate ions (HCO₃⁻), dissolved CO₂, and carbamino compounds. The medulla oblongata monitors blood CO₂ and adjusts ventilation rate.
- **Reproductive cycle**: the menstrual cycle (28 days average) involves changes in the uterine lining regulated by FSH, LH, oestrogen and progesterone; the ovarian cycle (follicular, ovulation, luteal) is coordinated with the menstrual cycle.

#### Examiner traps

- Treating the kidney as just a filter — the kidney actively regulates water and salt balance through reabsorption and secretion.
- Confusing ventilation and respiration — ventilation is the mechanical movement of air; respiration is the cellular process of energy production.
- Forgetting that FSH stimulates follicle development; LH triggers ovulation and the development of the corpus luteum.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Osmoregulation in the kidney

The nephron is the functional unit of the kidney. Its parts:

- **Glomerulus**: capillary network; filters blood under pressure.
- **Bowman's capsule**: collects the filtrate.
- **Proximal convoluted tubule**: reabsorbs glucose, amino acids, salts, and water.
- **Loop of Henle**: creates a salt gradient in the medulla; the descending limb is permeable to water; the ascending limb is impermeable to water but actively transports salts.
- **Distal convoluted tubule**: reabsorbs salts under hormonal control.
- **Collecting duct**: reabsorbs water under ADH control.

ADH (anti-diuretic hormone, from the posterior pituitary) increases the permeability of the collecting duct to water, increasing water reabsorption. High blood osmolarity triggers ADH release; low blood osmolarity inhibits ADH release.

Aldosterone (from the adrenal cortex) increases sodium reabsorption in the distal tubule and collecting duct, indirectly increasing water reabsorption.

#### Thermoregulation

Mammals maintain a constant body temperature (endothermy). The hypothalamus is the thermoregulation centre; it monitors blood temperature and integrates input from skin thermoreceptors.

Responses to high temperature: sweating (evaporative cooling), vasodilation of skin blood vessels (increased heat loss by radiation), behavioural changes (seeking shade, reducing activity).

Responses to low temperature: shivering (muscular heat production), vasoconstriction of skin blood vessels (reduced heat loss), behavioural changes (seeking warmth, increasing activity), increased thyroid hormone (long-term increase in metabolic rate).

#### Blood gas regulation

**Oxygen transport**: haemoglobin in red blood cells binds oxygen to form oxyhaemoglobin. The oxygen-haemoglobin dissociation curve shows the relationship between partial pressure of oxygen (pO₂) and haemoglobin saturation. Factors that shift the curve to the right (decreased affinity, increased O₂ release to tissues): high pCO₂, low pH, high temperature, high 2,3-BPG.

**Carbon dioxide transport**: ~70% as bicarbonate ions (HCO₃⁻) in plasma; ~20% bound to haemoglobin as carbamino compounds; ~10% dissolved in plasma.

**Ventilation control**: the medulla oblongata contains the respiratory centre; chemoreceptors in the medulla and carotid/aortic bodies monitor blood pCO₂, pO₂ and pH. High pCO₂ is the primary driver of increased ventilation rate.

#### Hormonal control of the reproductive cycle

The menstrual cycle has three phases, coordinated with the ovarian cycle:

1. **Menstrual phase (days 1-5)**: the uterine lining (endometrium) sheds; menstruation occurs.
2. **Follicular phase (days 1-13)**: FSH from the pituitary stimulates follicle development in the ovary; the developing follicle secretes oestrogen, which thickens the endometrium.
3. **Ovulation (day 14)**: a surge of LH triggers ovulation (release of the egg from the ovary).
5. **Luteal phase (days 15-28)**: the corpus luteum (remains of the follicle) secretes progesterone, which maintains the endometrium. If fertilisation does not occur, the corpus luteum degenerates, progesterone falls, and the cycle restarts.

**Hormonal contraception**: combined oral contraceptives contain synthetic oestrogen and progesterone; they suppress FSH and LH release, preventing ovulation and thickening cervical mucus.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-topic links

- **III. Organisms and Environment**: homeostasis (negative feedback) is the regulatory principle.
- **IV. Health and Diseases**: kidney disease and hormonal disorders connect to clinical medicine.
- **V. Applied Ecology (Elective)**: water balance in ecosystems connects to physiology.

#### Exam technique

- **For kidney questions**: trace the path of blood (afferent arteriole → glomerulus → efferent arteriole → peritubular capillaries → renal vein) and the path of filtrate (glomerulus → Bowman's capsule → proximal tubule → loop of Henle → distal tubule → collecting duct → renal pelvis).
- **For menstrual cycle questions**: draw a graph of hormone levels (FSH, LH, oestrogen, progesterone) across the cycle. This is a common HKDSE question.
- **For thermoregulation questions**: identify the stimulus, the receptor, the coordinator (hypothalamus), the effector, and the response.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "The kidney removes waste." The kidney regulates water and salt balance AND removes waste; both are important.
- "Oxygen and carbon dioxide are exchanged in the lungs." They are exchanged between alveolar air and pulmonary capillary blood; gas exchange in the tissues occurs in the systemic capillaries.
- "The menstrual cycle is always 28 days." It varies; 21-35 days is a typical range.
