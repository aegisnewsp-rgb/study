# Capacitors and Capacitance

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your NECO SSCE Paper II.

A capacitor stores electrical energy in the electric field between two conductors separated by a dielectric (insulator). **Capacitance** is the charge held per volt across the plates: **C = Q / V**, measured in farads (F). For a parallel-plate capacitor, **C = ε₀ εᵣ A / d**, where ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² F/m.

- **Series:** 1/C = 1/C₁ + 1/C₂ + 1/C₃ — equivalent is smaller than the smallest.
- **Parallel:** C = C₁ + C₂ + C₃ — equivalent is larger than any single one.
- **Energy stored:** U = ½ QV = ½ CV².
- **Dielectric:** inserting one multiplies C by εᵣ and reduces the voltage for the same Q.

The farad is enormous, so expect μF, nF or pF in NECO numerics.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students working through the syllabus over weeks.

#### Defining Capacitance
A capacitor consists of two conductors (plates) separated by an insulating dielectric. When connected to a battery, equal and opposite charges +Q and −Q accumulate on the plates, producing a potential difference V between them. Capacitance is the proportionality constant: **C = Q / V**. Doubling V does not change C — it only doubles Q.

#### Parallel-Plate Geometry
For the standard geometry tested by NECO, **C = ε₀ εᵣ A / d**. Three relationships matter:

| Change made to capacitor | Effect on capacitance |
| --- | --- |
| Plate area A doubled | C doubles |
| Separation d halved | C doubles |
| Dielectric with εᵣ = 4 inserted | C becomes 4× original |

Note the units: A in m², d in m, ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² F/m. Substituting cm or mm is the most common arithmetic error.

#### Combinations of Capacitors
The rules are opposite to those for resistors, which trips up many candidates:

- **In series**, each capacitor carries the same charge; voltages add. **1/C_total = 1/C₁ + 1/C₂ + 1/C₃**, and the result is always **less than the smallest** individual C.
- **In parallel**, each capacitor has the same voltage; charges add. **C_total = C₁ + C₂ + C₃**, always **greater than the largest** single value.

#### Energy Stored
The energy sits in the electric field between the plates, not on the metal itself. Three equivalent expressions — pick whichever matches the quantities given:
**U = ½ QV = ½ CV² = Q² / (2C)**, with U in joules.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for deeper mastery and tricky edge cases.

#### Behaviour in DC and AC
A fully charged capacitor blocks steady DC — no further current flows once Q stops changing. With AC, the plates continuously charge and discharge, allowing current to "pass" through the circuit in the displacement-current sense. NECO sometimes asks candidates to explain this contrast in 2–3 marks.

#### Worked Numerical
A parallel-plate capacitor has A = 200 cm² = 2.0 × 10⁻² m², d = 2 mm = 2.0 × 10⁻³ m, and an air gap (εᵣ ≈ 1).
C = (8.85 × 10⁻¹²)(1)(2.0 × 10⁻²) / (2.0 × 10⁻³) = 8.85 × 10⁻¹¹ F ≈ **88.5 pF**.
If a mica dielectric (εᵣ ≈ 6) is inserted, C rises to ≈ 531 pF, and the voltage across the plates (with charge held constant on an isolated capacitor) drops by the same factor of 6.

#### Common Mistakes and Traps
- **Swapping series/parallel formulas** — the single most common loss in NECO Paper II.
- **Assuming dielectric increases Q at fixed V** — actually Q stays the same and V drops.
- **Unit slip in C = ε₀εᵣA/d** — always convert cm² to m² and mm to m.
- **Treating 1 F as a "normal" size** — practical capacitors are μF, nF or pF; the farad is enormous.

#### Exam-Specific Strategy
Capacitors contribute ~4% of NECO SSCE Physics marks. Expect one structured calculation (often mixing series/parallel with the parallel-plate formula) and a 2-mark definition item on dielectrics or energy storage. Memorise all three energy forms and the dielectric-multiplying effect before the paper.
