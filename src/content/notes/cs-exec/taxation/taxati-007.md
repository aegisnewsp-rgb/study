# Tax Audit & International Taxation

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your CS Executive exam.

- **Section 44AB** mandates tax audit when business **turnover exceeds Rs 1 crore** (Rs 10 crore if cash ≤ 5%) or professional **gross receipts exceed Rs 50 lakh** (Rs 75 lakh from AY 2026-27).
- Auditor reports in **Form 3CA-3CD** (statutory audit already done) or **Form 3CB-3CD** (no other audit); due date is **30 September**, extended to **15 October** for transfer-pricing cases.
- **Transfer Pricing (Sections 92–92F)** applies to international transactions between **Associated Enterprises**; **Arm's Length Price (ALP)** is determined using CUP, RPM, CPM, TNMM, PSM or Other Method.
- **Residential status** decides tax incidence: Resident → global income; Non-Resident → Indian income only; RNOR is a resident sub-class with restricted global income.
- **DTAA under Section 90/90A** overrides domestic law when more favourable; taxpayer must furnish **TRC** and **Form 10F**.
- **Withholding on non-resident payments** falls under Section 195 (20% general) or Section 115A (10% for royalty/FTS to specified foreign companies).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months of preparation.

#### Section 44AB: Who, When, By Whom

A Chartered Accountant must audit books of account and upload **Form 3CD** (with 44 reporting clauses) where turnover crosses the prescribed threshold. The audit ensures that the income declared in the return matches the books and that TDS/TCS provisions have been correctly applied. Penalty for default is **0.5% of turnover**, capped at Rs 1.5 lakh, under Section 271B.

#### Forms, Due Dates and Presumptive Linkage

| Situation | Applicable Form | Due Date |
|---|---|---|
| Assessee already audited under another law | **3CA + 3CD** | 30 September (15 October if TP applicable) |
| Assessee not otherwise audited | **3CB + 3CD** | Same as above |
| Opting out of 44AD/44ADA and declaring lower income | Must get tax audit done | Original 30 September |

Presumptive schemes under **44AD (8%/6% of turnover)** and **44ADA (50% of gross receipts)** reduce compliance burden; opting out triggers full books-of-account maintenance and tax audit.

#### International Taxation Framework

Cross-border taxation in India rests on **residential status** (Sections 6, 7) and **source rules**. A foreign company is taxed only if it has a **Permanent Establishment (PE)** — fixed place PE, agency PE, or service PE (90 days in 12 months for the latter).

> Trap: "Not Ordinarily Resident" is NOT the same as "Non-Resident". RNOR is taxed on Indian income plus foreign business income only if the business is controlled from India.

**DTAAs** are bilateral agreements based on UN or OECD Model Conventions. Where DTAA is silent, **Section 91** unilateral relief applies to doubly-taxed Indian residents.

#### Withholding on Payments to Non-Residents

Section 195 requires withholding on every payment chargeable to tax in India. The payer can apply to AO for a **Section 195(2)/(3) certificate** fixing lower/nil TDS. Section 115A offers a concessional **10% rate** on royalty/FTS received by specified foreign companies, provided TRC is furnished.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Transfer Pricing — Mechanism and Safe Harbour

Sections 92–92F apply when an **international transaction** value exceeds **Rs 1 crore** or a **Specified Domestic Transaction** exceeds Rs 20 crore. The assessee must keep **contemporaneous documentation** and file **Form 3CEB** with the return. The **Transfer Pricing Officer (TPO)** determines ALP; the Assessing Officer applies the resulting adjustment.

**Methods (Section 92C)** are to be applied in the prescribed order, with CUP being most preferred.

| Method | Best Suited For |
|---|---|
| Comparable Uncontrolled Price (CUP) | Commodity sales, interest on loans |
| Resale Price Method (RPM) | Marketing/distribution entities |
| Cost Plus Method (CPM) | Contract manufacturing, service providers |
| Transactional Net Margin Method (TNMM) | Functions with limited comparables |
| Profit Split Method (PSM) | Highly integrated operations |
| Other Method | Unique transactions, e.g., intangibles |

**Safe Harbour rules (Section 92CB)** notify margin ranges (e.g., operating margin of 12–18% for IT/ITeS) exempting qualifying assessees from detailed scrutiny — useful for routine service exports.

#### DTAA, MFN and Equalisation Levy

A **Most Favoured Nation (MFN)** clause (e.g., India-Singapore, India-Lithuania DTAA) automatically grants the lowest rate extended to a third country. Students must check whether the relevant DTAA contains MFN and whether the third-state treaty post-dates the relevant trigger date.

**Equalisation Levy** captures digital transactions outside the PE net:
- **6%** on online advertisement paid to non-resident e-commerce operators (Section 165A, Finance Act 2016).
- **2%** on e-commerce supply of specified services by non-residents (Section 165AA, Finance Act 2020).

Withdrawal of the 6% levy on advertisement and adoption of Pillar One Amount A by India alters the compliance picture — track current CBDT notifications.

#### Common Mistakes and Exam Traps

1. **Threshold confusion**: Rs 1 crore vs Rs 50 lakh; new Rs 75 lakh professional limit is **AY 2026-27 onwards** per Finance Act 2024, not earlier years.
2. **Wrong form**: Filing 3CA where 3CB is required = defective report under Section 144BA.
3. **DTAA claim without TRC**: Tax residency certificate is mandatory; absence defeats the treaty claim.
4. **Treating royalty/FTS as 20% flat**: Section 115A's 10% concession requires specified-foreign-company status.
5. **Ignoring Form 3CEB**: Required even when ALP is at arm's length.

#### Practice Prompts

- **Q1**: A firm has turnover of Rs 9.8 crore with cash receipts of Rs 4.6 lakh. Is tax audit required? What if cash receipts were Rs 50 lakh?
- **Q2**: An Indian software company pays USD 2,00,000 royalty to a US company. Compute withholding tax citing both Sections 195 and 115A, and state when a lower rate under DTAA applies.
