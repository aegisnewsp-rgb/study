# Cytology — ACSEE (Tanzania)

Cytology is the heaviest single block on the ACSEE Biology syllabus scope. The NECTA 133/1 paper draws roughly one fifth of its total weight from cell structure, organelle function, membrane transport, and the two divisions (mitosis and meiosis). Exam questions mix definitions ("distinguish between prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells"), labelled diagrams (draw a mitochondrion and label four parts), short structured calculations (water potential of plant tissue by the Chardakov method), and longer essays that ask you to compare meiosis I and meiosis II or to explain why mitosis produces clones while meiosis generates variation.

> Verify the live syllabus, examination format, and any in-year changes on https://necta.go.tz before planning around the figures below.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)
> Re-anchor the cell vocabulary the night before the paper.

**Cell theory in three lines (the version ACSEE marks against):**
1. All living organisms are made of cells.
2. The cell is the basic unit of structure and function.
3. All cells come from pre-existing cells (Virchow, 1855 — Schleiden and Schwann had earlier versions).

**Eukaryote vs prokaryote — minimal contrast table:**
| Feature | Prokaryote | Eukaryote |
|---|---|---|
| Size | 1–10 µm | 10–100 µm |
| True nucleus | absent (nucleoid) | present |
| Membrane-bound organelles | absent (except in a few special cases) | present |
| DNA | circular, in cytoplasm | linear, in nucleus |
| Ribosomes | 70S (smaller) | 80S in cytoplasm; 70S in organelles |
| Examples | bacteria, cyanobacteria | animals, plants, fungi, protists |
| Cell wall (if present) | peptidoglycan | cellulose (plants), chitin (fungi) |

**Eight organelles you must draw and label:**
- Nucleus (with nucleolus and nuclear envelope).
- Mitochondrion (cristae, matrix, inner and outer membrane).
- Chloroplast (thylakoid, grana, stroma, double membrane).
- Endoplasmic reticulum — rough (with ribosomes) and smooth.
- Golgi apparatus (cisternae, vesicles).
- Ribosomes.
- Lysosomes.
- Centrioles (animal cells, near the nucleus).

Plant-only additions: cell wall (cellulose), large central vacuole, plasmodesmata, chloroplasts.
Animal-only: centrioles, lysosomes are more prominent.

**Plasma membrane — the fluid mosaic model (Singer–Nicolson, 1972):**
- Phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins.
- Cholesterol (animal cells) modulates fluidity.
- Glycoproteins and glycolipids on the outer face — cell recognition, ABO blood groups.
- Functions: selective permeability, signalling, anchoring, transport.

**Two transport modes you must distinguish:**
- **Passive** (no ATP): diffusion, facilitated diffusion, osmosis. Down the gradient.
- **Active** (ATP required): active transport, endocytosis, exocytosis. Against the gradient.

Definitions to know cold:
- Osmosis = net movement of water across a selectively permeable membrane from high water potential to low water potential.
- Water potential $\Psi$ = solute potential $\Psi_s$ + pressure potential $\Psi_p$ (in MPa).
- Plasmolysis = cell membrane pulls away from the cell wall as the cell loses water in a hypertonic solution.
- Turgid = firm, swollen (plant cell in distilled water — high turgor pressure).
- Flaccid = limp (isotonic — neither net gain nor loss).
- Haemolysis = red blood cell bursts in distilled water.

⚡ **Exam tip:** For diagram questions, draw a pencil line for each labelled structure — the marker needs a line, not just a name floating in space.

### 🟡 Standard — Exam Prep (3d-3w)
> Reach the B-grade band on NECTA 133/1.

**Mitochondrion and chloroplast — the energy organelles.** Both have a double membrane and their own DNA and 70S ribosomes, supporting the endosymbiotic theory (engulfed prokaryote became organelle).

- Mitochondrion: cristae (folded inner membrane) increase surface area for the electron transport chain; matrix contains enzymes of the Krebs cycle. Site of oxidative phosphorylation; produces most of the cell's ATP (a typical eukaryote makes ~1 × 10⁷ ATP per glucose; rough textbook number).
- Chloroplast: thylakoids stack into grana; light-dependent reactions happen on the thylakoid membrane (ATP and NADPH produced); the Calvin cycle (light-independent) happens in the stroma and fixes $CO_2$ into triose phosphate using Rubisco.

**Endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi — the secretion pipeline.**
- Rough ER: synthesises membrane proteins and secreted proteins on its ribosomes.
- Smooth ER: synthesises lipids, detoxifies drugs (liver), stores $Ca^{2+}$ (muscle).
- Golgi: modifies (glycosylation, phosphorylation), sorts, packages into vesicles for secretion or for delivery to lysosomes / plasma membrane.

**Nucleus — control centre.**
- Nuclear envelope: double membrane with pores; pores control export of mRNA and import of nuclear proteins.
- Nucleolus: site of rRNA synthesis and ribosome subunit assembly.
- Chromatin: DNA + histones; condenses into chromosomes during mitosis.

**Mitosis vs meiosis — the comparison ACSEE marks against.**

| Feature | Mitosis | Meiosis |
|---|---|---|
| Number of divisions | one | two |
| Daughter cells from one parent | 2 | 4 |
| Ploidy of daughters | diploid (2n) → diploid (2n) | diploid (2n) → haploid (n) |
| Genetic variation | none (clones) | new combinations by crossing over and independent assortment |
| Where it happens | somatic cells, growth, repair | germ cells in ovary / testis |
| Stages | P, M, A, T (one round) | P, M, A, T, P, M, A, T (two rounds) |
| Crossing over | no | yes (prophase I) |
| Homologous pairing | no | yes (synapsis, prophase I) |
| Function | growth, asexual reproduction, tissue repair | gamete formation; halving chromosome number for sexual reproduction |

**Stage-by-stage names (memorise in order):**
- Mitosis: prophase → metaphase → anaphase → telophase (followed by cytokinesis).
- Meiosis I: prophase I → metaphase I → anaphase I → telophase I.
- Meiosis II: prophase II → metaphase II → anaphase II → telophase II.

**Worked example — mitosis calculation.** A root tip cell completes mitosis in 24 hours. How many cells after 5 rounds if every daughter enters the next round?
- After $n$ rounds from one cell: $2^n$ cells.
- $2^5 = 32$ cells.

**Worked example — meiosis calculation.** A primary spermatocyte has 46 chromosomes. After meiosis II, how many chromosomes per spermatid?
- Spermatid is the post-meiosis-II cell; chromosome number is halved: 23 chromosomes.
- Each spermatid also carries one chromatid of each chromosome (sister chromatids have separated).

**Osmosis and water potential — plant cells.**
- $\Psi = \Psi_s + \Psi_p$ (units MPa, usually negative or zero).
- $\Psi_s = -iCRT$ (Morse equation; $C$ in mol/L, $R = 0.00831$ L·MPa/(mol·K), $T$ in K, $i$ = number of dissolved ions).
- $\Psi_p$ inside a turgid plant cell is positive; outside is zero (atmospheric).
- Incipient plasmolysis: $\Psi_p = 0$, so $\Psi_{cell} = \Psi_s$.

**Membrane transport — kinetics you must read.**
- Diffusion rate $\propto$ (surface area) × (concentration gradient) / (membrane thickness).
- Facilitated diffusion uses a carrier or channel — saturates at high substrate because carriers are finite.
- Active transport: $V_{max}$ is reached when all carriers are saturated; ATP hydrolysis by the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase maintains the 3 Na⁺ out / 2 K⁺ in gradient used for nerve signalling and for secondary active transport (e.g. glucose uptake in the intestine).

**Practice set:**
1. Name three structures present in a plant cell but absent in an animal cell.
2. Calculate the solute potential of a 0.1 mol/L NaCl solution at 25 °C. ($i = 2$.)
3. State two differences between rough ER and smooth ER.
4. A cell has 16 chromosomes. After mitosis, how many chromosomes does each daughter have? After meiosis?
5. Explain why oxygen consumption of a mitochondrion-rich tissue (e.g. cardiac muscle) is higher than that of a mitochondrion-poor tissue.

Answers:
1. Cell wall, chloroplasts, large central vacuole (also plasmodesmata). Accept any three.
2. $\Psi_s = -iCRT = -2 \times 0.1 \times 0.00831 \times 298 \approx -0.495$ MPa.
3. RER has ribosomes (synthesises proteins); SER has no ribosomes (synthesises lipids and detoxifies). RER is continuous with the nuclear envelope; SER is not always. RER buds vesicles to the Golgi; SER may not.
4. Mitosis: 16 chromosomes each daughter. Meiosis: 8 chromosomes each daughter.
5. Cardiac muscle has more mitochondria per cell than e.g. cartilage or epidermis. Oxidative phosphorylation in the inner mitochondrial membrane is the dominant $O_2$ consumer, so more mitochondria → higher $O_2$ consumption.

### 🔴 Deep — Mastery (1mo+)
> Build the molecular picture behind the syllabus.

**Origin of eukaryotic cells — endosymbiosis.** Mitochondria and chloroplasts have their own circular DNA (no introns, no histones in the same form as the nucleus), 70S ribosomes, and double membranes. Sequence data place mitochondrial DNA closer to α-proteobacteria and chloroplast DNA closer to cyanobacteria. The leading hypothesis (Lynn Margulis, 1967, then widely accepted from the 1980s) is that an ancestral archaeal cell engulfed an aerobic bacterium (~mitochondrion) and later a cyanobacterium (~chloroplast), without digesting them.

**Cytoskeleton — three filament systems.**
- Actin microfilaments (~7 nm): cell shape, cytokinesis (contractile ring), cell crawling, microvilli.
- Intermediate filaments (~10 nm): mechanical strength (keratin in skin, neurofilaments in axons, lamins in the nucleus).
- Microtubules (~25 nm): tracks for vesicle transport (kinesin, dynein), mitotic spindle, centrioles, cilia and flagella (9+2 axoneme).
- Motors: kinesin walks toward the (+)-end of microtubules (anterograde in axons); dynein walks toward the (−)-end (retrograde).

**Cell cycle controls — why cancers go wrong.**
- G1 checkpoint (restriction point): if conditions are wrong or DNA is damaged, the cell does not enter S phase. p53 is the gatekeeper; mutations in TP53 are common in many human cancers.
- G2 checkpoint: confirms DNA replication is complete before mitosis.
- M checkpoint (spindle assembly checkpoint): each chromosome must be attached to spindle fibres from both poles before anaphase begins; securin and separase regulate cohesin cleavage.
- Cyclin-dependent kinases (CDKs) pair with cyclins to drive the cycle; CDK inhibitors (p21, p27) pause the cycle.

**Meiosis in depth — where the variation comes from.**
1. **Crossing over (prophase I, pachytene):** non-sister chromatids of homologous chromosomes exchange segments at chiasmata. The further apart two genes are on a chromosome, the more often crossovers fall between them — this is the basis of genetic mapping in centimorgans (1 cM ≈ 1% recombinant offspring).
2. **Independent assortment (metaphase I):** each pair of homologues lines up independently at the equator; for $n$ pairs, there are $2^n$ possible combinations of maternal/paternal chromosomes per gamete (in humans, $n = 23$, so $2^{23} \approx 8.4 \times 10^6$ possibilities ignoring crossing over).
3. **Random fertilisation:** any sperm fuses with any egg; $2^{23} \times 2^{23} > 7 \times 10^{13}$ possible zygote chromosome combinations from one couple (still ignoring crossing over).

**Membrane transport energetics.** Glucose enters red blood cells via GLUT1, a facilitated-diffusion carrier (passive, down a gradient). In intestinal epithelial cells, the same glucose is taken up by SGLT1, a sodium-glucose cotransporter that uses the Na⁺ gradient (set up by Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase on the basal side) to drag glucose uphill — secondary active transport. From the enterocyte, glucose exits across the basolateral membrane by GLUT2 into the blood.

**Water potential in real plant cells.** Incipient plasmolysis begins when the protoplast just loses contact with the wall. To estimate $\Psi$ of a tissue, equilibrate tissue pieces in a series of sucrose solutions of known $\Psi_s$; the solution in which 50% of cells just show incipient plasmolysis has $\Psi$ equal to the cell's $\Psi_s$ at the moment $\Psi_p = 0$. The Chardakov method uses density change of an external sucrose solution to detect whether cells gained or lost water.

**Worked example — Chardakov by numbers.** A 1 cm potato cylinder is placed in 0.3 M sucrose ($\Psi_s = -iCRT \approx -0.3 \times 0.00831 \times 2 \times 298 \approx -1.49$ MPa). After 30 minutes the cylinder length is unchanged. Estimate the cell $\Psi$.
- No net water movement → the cell $\Psi$ equals the external $\Psi$ (since at equilibrium $\Psi_{inside} = \Psi_{outside}$, with $\Psi_p = 0$ in the incipient plasmolysis limit).
- Cell $\Psi \approx -1.49$ MPa.

### Common traps
1. **Calling mitosis "for growth and reproduction".** Mitosis is for growth, repair, and asexual reproduction. Sexual reproduction uses meiosis, not mitosis. The exam marks this distinction strictly.
2. **Treating "cell wall" as a single structure.** Plant cell wall = cellulose plus pectin plus hemicellulose. Fungal wall = chitin. Bacterial wall = peptidoglycan. ACSEE occasionally asks for the wall composition; defaulting to "cellulose" loses marks on a fungus or bacterium.
3. **Saying "glucose is transported by diffusion in all cells".** Intestinal uptake is active (SGLT1). Kidney reabsorption uses Na⁺-coupled transport. Liver uptake of glucose uses GLUT2 (facilitated diffusion). The mechanism depends on the tissue.
4. **Confusing turgid and turgor pressure.** Turgid is the adjective (a turgid cell). Turgor pressure is the variable $\Psi_p$ inside the cell.
5. **Halving the chromosome number at the wrong stage of meiosis.** Homologous chromosomes separate in anaphase I; sister chromatids separate in anaphase II. Halving happens at the end of meiosis I, not meiosis II. Some textbooks describe the ploidy as "n" after meiosis I and confirm "n" after meiosis II — but the chromatid count changes from 2N to N equivalents between the two meiotic divisions, which is the trick.
6. **Writing "ATP is made in the mitochondria".** The citric acid cycle in the matrix makes NADH and FADH₂; ATP is made on the inner membrane by ATP synthase using the proton gradient (oxidative phosphorylation). The substrate-level ATP from the matrix is a small fraction. Markers want you to localise the steps.
