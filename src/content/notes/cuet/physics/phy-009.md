# Fluid Mechanics

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Fluid mechanics studies how liquids and gases behave at rest (fluid statics) and in motion (fluid dynamics). The chapter carries about **3%** weightage in CUET UG Physics, with 1–2 MCQs per paper drawn mostly from NCERT Class 11 Chapter 10.

| Term | Meaning | SI Unit |
|---|---|---|
| Pressure (P) | Force per unit area, P = F/A | pascal (Pa = N/m²) |
| Density (ρ) | Mass per unit volume | kg/m³ |
| Viscosity (η) | Internal friction of a fluid | Pa·s |
| Surface tension (S) | Force per unit length along a surface | N/m |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"PBA BVS"** — **P**ascal's law, **B**ernoulli, **A**rchimedes, **B**uoyancy, **V**iscosity, **S**urface tension. These six laws cover ~90% of CUET questions from this chapter.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Laws and Their Meaning

**Pascal's law** states that pressure applied to an enclosed incompressible fluid is transmitted undiminished in every direction. The hydraulic lift uses F₁/A₁ = F₂/A₂, where a small force on a small piston produces a large force on a larger piston.

**Archimedes' principle** says the buoyant force on a submerged object equals the weight of fluid displaced: **F_b = ρ·V·g**, where ρ is fluid density, V is displaced volume, g ≈ 9.8 m/s². An object floats when ρ_object < ρ_fluid.

#### Comparison Matrix: Drop vs Bubble vs Jet

| System | Excess pressure (ΔP) | Reason | Formula |
|---|---|---|---|
| Liquid drop | 2S/R | One surface | ΔP = 2S/R |
| Soap bubble | 4S/R | Two surfaces (inner + outer) | ΔP = 4S/R |
| Liquid jet | S/R | Single cylindrical surface | ΔP = S/R |

#### Continuity + Bernoulli

For an incompressible fluid in a pipe, mass conservation gives **A₁v₁ = A₂v₂** (equation of continuity). Bernoulli's theorem then expresses energy conservation along a streamline:

#### P + ½ρv² + ρgh = constant

where P is pressure [Pa], ρ density [kg/m³], v speed [m/s], h height [m]. All three terms must be in compatible SI units.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** Water flows through a horizontal pipe of cross-section 6 cm² at 4 m/s. The pipe narrows to 2 cm². Find (a) the speed in the narrow section, and (b) the pressure difference between the wide and narrow sections. Take ρ_water = 1000 kg/m³.

#### Solution:
(a) Continuity: A₁v₁ = A₂v₂ → v₂ = (6 × 4)/2 = **12 m/s**

(b) Bernoulli (horizontal, h constant): P₁ + ½ρv₁² = P₂ + ½ρv₂²
ΔP = P₁ − P₂ = ½ρ(v₂² − v₁²) = ½ × 1000 × (144 − 16) = **64,000 Pa = 64 kPa**

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students often use A₂/A₁ instead of A₁/A₂ in the continuity ratio, or forget to subtract (v₂² − v₁²) the correct way round — narrowing sections have lower pressure, not higher.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Viscosity and Temperature — Counter-Intuitive Behaviour

A frequently missed CUET point: viscosity of **liquids decreases** with temperature (oil thins when heated), while viscosity of **gases increases** with temperature (air thickens slightly). Newton's law of viscous flow is **F = −η·A·(dv/dx)**, with η having SI units of Pa·s and dimensions [M L⁻¹ T⁻¹]. Stokes' law gives the drag on a sphere: **F = 6πηrv**.

#### Reynolds Number and Flow Regime

The Reynolds number **Re = ρvd/η** classifies flow: Re < 1000 is laminar (streamline), Re > 2000 is turbulent. CUET numericals often give η, ρ, v, d and ask the student to compute Re and pick the regime.

#### Advanced Traps and Edge Cases

1. **Gauge vs absolute pressure** — atmospheric pressure (≈ 1.013 × 10⁵ Pa) must be added to gauge pressure to get absolute; barometers measure absolute, tyre gauges measure gauge.
2. **Capillary rise** — h = 2S cosθ/(ρgr) assumes a contact angle θ; mercury gives *depression* (θ > 90°, cosθ negative), not rise.
3. **Torricelli's law** — efflux speed v = √(2gh) is a special case of Bernoulli when the top surface is large (v ≈ 0) and exit is open to atmosphere.
4. **Bernoulli fails for viscous fluids** — real pipes need the Hagen–Poiseuille correction; CUET sometimes offers this as a "which assumption fails?" MCQ.
5. **Continuity and compressible gases** — A₁v₁ = A₂v₂ holds only when ρ is constant; for gases moving faster than ~0.3 × speed of sound, density changes break the equation.

#### Practice Prompts

1. A soap bubble of radius 1 cm has surface tension 0.03 N/m. Calculate the excess pressure inside it (expected: ΔP = 4S/R = 12 Pa).
2. Glycerine (η = 1.5 Pa·s) flows in a tube of radius 1 mm at average velocity 0.2 m/s. Compute Re given ρ = 1260 kg/m³ (expected: Re ≈ 168 → laminar).
