# Trigonometry — Identities, Equations and Applications — Qatar Secondary Certificate Mathematics Notes

Trigonometry is half of the Geometry and Measurement strand at MoEHE Grade 12. The MoEHE 2018 Mathematics Curriculum Standards publish trigonometric ratios in degrees and radians, the Law of Sines and Law of Cosines, Heron's formula for area, sum/difference and double-angle identities, and solving trigonometric equations at advanced track (MoEHE pp. 53, 60). Trigonometry is the bridge between algebra and applied geometry, and the prerequisite for the calculus of trigonometric functions.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Unit circle definitions:** cos theta = x / r, sin theta = y / r, tan theta = y / x.
- **Pythagorean identity:** sin^2 theta + cos^2 theta = 1.
- **Radian measure:** arc length s = r theta; sector area A = (1/2) r^2 theta.
- **Law of Sines:** a / sin A = b / sin B = c / sin C.
- **Law of Cosines:** c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab cos C.
- **Heron's formula:** Area = sqrt(s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)) where s = (a + b + c) / 2.
- **Double-angle:** sin 2 theta = 2 sin theta cos theta; cos 2 theta = cos^2 theta - sin^2 theta = 1 - 2 sin^2 theta = 2 cos^2 theta - 1.

#### Examiner traps

- Mixing degree and radian mode on the calculator — always check.
- Forgetting the angle range when using inverse trig functions — arcsin gives values in [-pi/2, pi/2] only.
- Using the wrong Law of Sines vs Law of Cosines — Sines for AAS/ASA, Cosines for SAS/SSS.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Trig identities

Pythagorean: sin^2 + cos^2 = 1, 1 + tan^2 = sec^2, 1 + cot^2 = cosec^2.

Sum/difference: sin(A +/- B) = sin A cos B +/- cos A sin B. cos(A +/- B) = cos A cos B -/+ sin A sin B. tan(A + B) = (tan A + tan B) / (1 - tan A tan B).

Double-angle from sum: sin 2 theta = 2 sin theta cos theta. cos 2 theta = cos^2 theta - sin^2 theta.

To prove an identity, work from one side and convert using the identities above until you reach the other side. Keep expressions factored where possible.

#### Trig equations

Solve sin 2x = 1/2 for x in [0, 2 pi]. General solution: 2x = pi/6 + 2 pi k or 2x = 5 pi/6 + 2 pi k. So x = pi/12 + pi k or x = 5 pi/12 + pi k. Restrict to [0, 2 pi]: x = pi/12, 5 pi/12, 13 pi/12, 17 pi/12.

For cos^2 x = 3/4, cos x = +/- sqrt(3)/2, x = pi/6, 5 pi/6, 7 pi/6, 11 pi/6 in [0, 2 pi].

#### Law of Sines and Cosines applied

Find side a in a triangle with A = 50 degrees, B = 70 degrees, b = 10. Then a / sin 50 = 10 / sin 70, so a = 10 sin 50 / sin 70 ~= 7.66.

Find side c in a triangle with a = 5, b = 7, C = 60 degrees. Then c^2 = 25 + 49 - 2 * 5 * 7 * cos 60 = 74 - 35 = 39, so c = sqrt(39) ~= 6.24.

Find area by Heron's formula for a = 5, b = 7, c = 9: s = (5 + 7 + 9) / 2 = 10.5. Area = sqrt(10.5 * 5.5 * 3.5 * 1.5) = sqrt(303.1875) ~= 17.41.

#### Radian measure and arc / sector

A sector of radius 5 and central angle 60 degrees = pi/3 rad. Arc length s = r theta = 5 * pi/3. Sector area A = (1/2) r^2 theta = (1/2) * 25 * pi/3 = 25 pi / 6.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### Why trigonometry is a feeder strand

Trigonometric functions are part of the MoEHE Functions and Graphs sub-topic of the Algebra and Calculus strand (~69 percent of the Grade 12 advanced paper). Trig identities are tested both in pure algebra questions and as part of integration (e.g. integral of sin^2). The Law of Sines and Cosines plus Heron's formula appear in applied geometry and surveying.

#### Exam technique

- On a calculator, check radian/degree mode before every trig computation.
- For trig equations, write the general solution first, then restrict to the given domain.
- For Heron's formula, double-check that s > a, s > b, s > c (otherwise the radicand is negative and there is no such triangle).

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "sin^2 x = (sin x)^2." Yes, the notation means squared. Same for cos^2 x.
- "cos(2 pi - x) = cos x." Yes — cosine is even. But sin(2 pi - x) = -sin x — sine is odd about pi.
- "Law of Sines has two solutions for the angle case." SSA (the ambiguous case) does; AAS, ASA, SAS, SSS each have a unique solution (or none).
