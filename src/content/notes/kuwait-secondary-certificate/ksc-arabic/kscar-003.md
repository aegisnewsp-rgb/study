# Morphology (Sarf) — Kuwait Secondary Certificate Arabic Notes

Arabic morphology (Sarf / الصرف) is the third terminal-level domain on the Kuwait MOE Arabic curriculum. At terminal level, the curriculum covers verb forms (mujarrad and mazeed fihi), derived nouns (masdar, mufa''al, mifa''al), weak verbs (muda''af, mu'tall, mahmuz), and how Arabic word patterns map to grammatical function.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Mujarrad (bare form):** three-consonant root verb (فعل). The three root letters are fa, 'ayn, lam (ف ع ل). Conjugations follow predictable patterns.
- **Mazeed fihi (augmented forms):** add letters to the root. Common patterns: Form II (فعّل - "to make X do"), Form III (فاعل - "to do with X"), Form V (تفعّل - reflexive of II), Form VI (تفاعل - reflexive of III), Form VII (انفعل - passive of I), Form VIII (افتعل - reflexive of I), Form X (استفعل - "to ask for X").
- **Weak verbs:** muda''af (doubled, e.g. مدّ), mu'tall middi (hollow, e.g. قال), mu'tall naqis (defective, e.g. رمى), mahmuz (glottal-stop, e.g. قرأ).
- **Derived nouns:** masdar (verbal noun), fa'il (active participle), maf'ul (passive participle), mifa''al (place/time instrument).
- **Sound plural:** masculine -un (معلمون), feminine -at (معلمات).
- **Broken plural:** fa''il (كتب), af''al (أقلام), f'ila (مساجد), afa'il (عصافير), fa'a'il (سحائب), fu'ul (شهور), fu'aal (كهوف), mafa'il (منازل), mafaa'il (مصائب).

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing mujarrad and mazeed — augmented forms have an extra letter not in the root.
- Applying sound plural rules to weak verbs — defective verbs change vowel patterns.
- Forgetting the mu'tall middi (hollow) changes — قال becomes قول in the masdar.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Verb forms in detail

The ten Arabic verb forms (الأوزان) follow a template system:

| Form | Pattern | Meaning | Example (root: كتب) |
|------|---------|---------|---------------------|
| I    | فَعَلَ / يَفْعُلُ | simple past/present | كَتَبَ / يَكْتُبُ |
| II   | فَعَّلَ / يُفَعِّلُ | causative / intensive | كَتَّبَ / يُكَتِّبُ |
| III  | فَاعَلَ / يُفَاعِلُ | reciprocal / attempt | كَاتَبَ / يُكَاتِبُ |
| IV   | أَفْعَلَ / يُفْعِلُ | causative | أَكْتَبَ / يُكْتِبُ |
| V    | تَفَعَّلَ / يَتَفَعَّلُ | reflexive of II | تَكَتَّبَ / يَتَكَتَّبُ |
| VI   | تَفَاعَلَ / يَتَفَاعَلُ | reflexive of III | تَكَاتَبَ / يَتَكَاتَبُ |
| VII  | اِنْفَعَلَ / يَنْفَعِلُ | passive of I | اِنْكَتَبَ / يَنْكَتِبُ |
| VIII | اِفْتَعَلَ / يَفْتَعِلُ | reflexive of I | اِكْتَتَبَ / يَكْتَتِبُ |
| IX   | اِفْعَلَّ / يَفْعَلُّ | colours/defects | — |
| X   | اِسْتَفْعَلَ / يَسْتَفْعِلُ | "to ask/request X" | اِسْتَكْتَبَ / يَسْتَكْتِبُ |

Each form has its own active/passive, past/present/command/verbal noun (masdar) conjugations. Memorise the form table and the masdar pattern for each form.

#### Weak verb changes

Weak verbs undergo vowel and consonant modifications when conjugated:

- **Muda''af (doubled, e.g. مدّ):** the doubled consonant becomes a single consonant with shadda on the next consonant in some conjugations (e.g. يمدّ).
- **Mu'tall middi (hollow, e.g. قال):** the middle radical becomes a long vowel (و or ي or ا) when conjugated.
- **Mu'tall naqis (defective, e.g. رمى):** the final radical becomes a long vowel, and the vowel patterns shift.
- **Mahmuz (hamza-bearing, e.g. قرأ):** the hamza (ء) is treated as a regular consonant but changes writing rules.

For terminal items, identify the verb type first; then apply the standard conjugation rules to predict the form.

#### Derived nouns

Derived nouns map forms to functions:

- **Masdar (verbal noun):** each form has its own masdar pattern. Form I masdar is unpredictable per root; Forms II-X have predictable patterns.
- **Fa'il (active participle):** Form I فاعل, Form II مُفَعِّل, Form III مُفَاعِل, Form IV مُفْعِل, Form V مُتَفَعِّل, Form VI مُتَفَاعِل, Form VII مُنْفَعِل, Form VIII مُفْتَعِل, Form X مُسْتَفْعِل.
- **Maf'ul (passive participle):** Form I مَفْعُول, Form II مُفَعَّل, Form III مُفَاعَل, Form IV مُفْعَل, Form V مُتَفَعَّل, Form VI مُتَفَاعَل, Form VII مُنْفَعَل, Form VIII مُفْتَعَل, Form X مُسْتَفْعَل.

#### Broken plurals

The broken plural (جمع التكسير) follows internal vowel changes rather than suffixes. The pattern determines the plural:

- فَعَل → أَفْعَال (kutub)
- فَعِيل → أَفْعُلَة (qisasi), فِعْلان → فَعَالَى, etc.

Memorise the common patterns. The broken plural cannot be predicted from the singular alone.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where Morphology (Sarf) appears across the terminal Arabic exam

- **Form identification items:** identify which form a given verb belongs to.
- **Masdar derivation:** produce the masdar of a given verb form.
- **Conjugation items:** conjugate a weak verb or augmented form.
- **Plural formation:** derive the broken plural of a given singular noun.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Form IX verbs are rare." They are, but they appear in classical Arabic (e.g. احمرّ = to turn red). Memorise them.
- "All triliteral roots follow Form I." No — augmented forms have additional letters.
- "The masdar is always regular." No — Form I masdar varies per root (e.g. كتب → كتابة, ذهب → ذهاب).

#### Exam technique

- For form-identification items, count the consonants in the verb — three = Form I, four = Forms II-X.
- For masdar derivation, recall the form-specific pattern, then apply the root letters.
- For weak verb conjugations, identify the weak letter first, then apply the change rules.
