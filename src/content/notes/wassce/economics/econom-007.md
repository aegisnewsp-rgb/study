# Market Structures

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Market structures describe how buyers and sellers are organised in a market, setting the rules of competition, pricing power, and profit outcomes. WASSCE Economics tests **four structures**: perfect competition, monopoly, monopolistic competition, and oligopoly.

| Structure | Sellers | Product | Entry | Price Control |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Perfect competition | Many | Homogeneous | Free | None (price taker) |
| Monopoly | One | No close substitutes | Blocked | Full (price maker) |
| Monopolistic competition | Many | Differentiated | Free | Slight |
| Oligopoly | Few dominant | Homogeneous or differentiated | Difficult | Interdependent |

The universal profit-maximisation rule across all four is **MR = MC**, where MR is marginal revenue and MC is marginal cost, both measured in GHS per unit.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"PM-MO"** for the four structures in this order — **P**erfect, **M**onopolistic, **M**onopoly, **O**ligopoly. Pair it with the rule **"MR = MC, AR = P"** so you write the right curve labels on every diagram.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Definitions and Revenue–Cost Relationships

A firm's profit in any market is **Π = TR − TC**, where TR = P × Q. From TR we derive **Average Revenue (AR) = TR ÷ Q = P** and **Marginal Revenue (MR) = ΔTR ÷ ΔQ**. Total cost splits into **AC = TC ÷ Q** and **MC = ΔTC ÷ ΔQ**.

Supernormal profit under any structure equals **(AR − AC) × Q** measured in Ghana cedis. Normal profit is recorded when **AR = AC**, meaning Π = 0; the implicit cost of the owner's resources is already absorbed into AC.

#### Perfect Competition vs Monopoly

| Feature | Perfect competition | Monopoly |
|---|---|---|
| Number of sellers | Very many | One |
| Product | Homogeneous (identical) | No close substitute |
| Entry and exit | Free | Blocked by legal, technical, or cost barriers |
| Demand curve facing firm | Perfectly elastic (horizontal) | Downward-sloping market demand |
| AR vs MR | AR = MR = P | MR < AR = P |
| Long-run profit | Normal profit only | Supernormal profit persists |
| Efficiency | Allocative (P = MC) and productive (AC at minimum) | Allocative inefficiency (P > MC) |

In perfect competition, the firm's demand curve is horizontal because the individual seller is too small to influence price. In monopoly, the firm faces the entire downward-sloping market demand, so to sell one more unit it must lower price on every unit, which is why **MR < P**.

#### Monopolistic Competition and Oligopoly

Monopolistic competition blends many sellers with differentiated products (branding, packaging, location). Free entry erodes supernormal profit, so in long-run equilibrium the firm's demand curve is **tangent to its AC curve**, yielding only normal profit.

Oligopoly is dominated by a few large, mutually interdependent firms. The **kinked demand curve** explains sticky prices: if a firm raises price, rivals do not follow (demand is elastic above the kink), but if it cuts price, rivals match (demand is inelastic below the kink). The MR curve has a discontinuity at the kink, leaving profit-maximising output stable.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

#### Question:
A firm in monopolistic competition sells 200 units of a beverage at GHS 18 each. Average cost is GHS 14, marginal cost is GHS 16, and marginal revenue is GHS 16. Calculate the firm's total profit and state whether it is currently earning normal profit, supernormal profit, or a loss.

#### Solution:

1. Compute Total Revenue: TR = P × Q = GHS 18 × 200 = **GHS 3,600**.
2. Compute Total Cost: TC = AC × Q = GHS 14 × 200 = **GHS 2,800**.
3. Compute Profit: Π = TR − TC = GHS 3,600 − GHS 2,800 = **GHS 800**.
4. Confirm profit-maximising condition: MR = GHS 16 and MC = GHS 16, so MR = MC ✓.
5. Since AR (GHS 18) > AC (GHS 14), the firm earns **supernormal profit**, suggesting it is operating in the short run before free entry erodes the surplus.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Many students write "monopolistic competition earns supernormal profit in the long run." It does not — free entry wipes it out, leaving only normal profit (AR = AC). Award yourself the mark only when you specify **short run** for supernormal profit and **long run** for normal profit.

#### Common Mistakes in Standard Questions

- Confusing **normal profit** (an implicit cost) with economic loss; the owner still earns a return on capital.
- Drawing AR and MR as separate horizontal lines under monopoly; for a monopoly the demand curve (AR) slopes downward and MR lies below it.
- Forgetting that the **shut-down point** in the short run is **P < AVC**, not P < AC.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Collusion, Cartels, and Ghana's Industrial Examples

In oligopoly, firms often **collude** to fix prices or restrict output, forming a **cartel** that behaves like a collective monopoly. OPEC is the textbook example; in Ghana, attempts at price-fixing in cement and poultry industries have been investigated under the **Ghana Competition Act, 2018 (Act 960)**. Cartels raise joint profit but face an internal incentive to cheat by secretly cutting price to capture market share.

| Real Ghanaian Industry | Market Structure | Key Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Cocoa export purchasing | Monopsony (single buyer) | Ghana Cocoa Board (COCOBOD) is the sole legal buyer of cocoa from farmers |
| Mobile telecoms | Oligopoly | MTN, Vodafone, AirtelTigo dominate; barriers include spectrum licences and tower networks |
| Open-air retail markets (e.g. Makola) | Monopolistic competition | Many traders sell differentiated goods (tomatoes vs. dried peppers) with free entry |
| Electricity transmission | Natural monopoly | Ghana Grid Company (GRIDCo) is the sole national transmitter |
| Foreign exchange bureau | Monopolistic competition | Differentiated by rates, location, customer service |

#### Efficiency Frontiers and Welfare Loss

Perfect competition achieves **allocative efficiency** because **P = MC** — the price consumers pay equals the marginal cost of producing the last unit. It also achieves **productive efficiency** because firms produce at the **minimum point of AC**. Under monopoly, **P > MC**, generating a **deadweight welfare loss**: units that consumers value above their production cost are never produced.

The **Herfindahl–Hirschman Index (HHI)** measures market concentration as **HHI = Σ(sᵢ)²**, where sᵢ is each firm's percentage market share. HHI below 1,500 indicates an unconcentrated market; above 2,500 indicates high concentration. This tool appears in Ghana's Competition Commission merger reviews.

#### Edge Cases and Advanced Traps

1. **Monopsony vs monopoly** — a monopsony is a single buyer (COCOBOD), not a single seller. Examiners reward clear distinction.
2. **Natural monopoly with economies of scale** — average cost falls over the relevant output range, so a single firm is cheapest; splitting output raises AC.
3. **Price discrimination under monopoly** — requires different PEDs across markets and no resale; the firm charges the higher PED market a higher price.
4. **Kinked demand curve and the discontinuity trap** — the kink explains *price rigidity*, not the *profit-maximising output*; the firm still chooses output where MR = MC at the discontinuous segment.
5. **Long-run vs short-run equilibrium** — only monopoly can sustain supernormal profit in the long run; monopolistic competition converges to normal profit via entry.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. Using a clearly labelled diagram, explain how a Ghanaian mobile telecom oligopolist would determine output when MR = MC within the kinked demand curve's MR gap. State one Ghanaian policy measure that limits collusion in this sector.
2. The Ghana Cocoa Board (COCOBOD) is a monopsony buyer. Discuss, with reference to consumer sovereignty and producer surplus, whether monopsony purchasing power harms Ghanaian cocoa farmers in the long run.

> 📌 **Formula Check:** Always write GHS as the currency unit. AR and MR are GHS per unit; TR and TC are in GHS; Q is in units; PED and HHI are dimensionless.
