# Final Accounts

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

> A 3-paper sweep through Final Accounts lands 3% in CA Foundation Paper 1, but mastery here unlocks Inter and Final group papers.

Final Accounts are the three statements drawn up at period-end from the Trial Balance: **Trading Account** (gross profit), **Profit & Loss Account** (net profit), and **Balance Sheet** (financial position). Every adjustment — closing stock, depreciation, bad debts, provisions, accruals — must touch both an account and the Balance Sheet or the answer scores zero.

**Memory Hook — "TDS-PB":** **T**rading (direct), **D**ebt side of P&L (indirect expenses), **S**ubtract Closing Stock on credit side, then **P**rovision on Debtors, **B**ad Debts recovered on credit side of P&L.

| Item | Where it goes | Side |
|---|---|---|
| Opening Stock | Trading A/c | Debit |
| Purchases (less Returns) | Trading A/c | Debit |
| Sales (less Returns) | Trading A/c | Credit |
| Closing Stock | Trading A/c & Balance Sheet | Credit / Asset |
| Carriage Inward | Trading A/c | Debit |
| Office Rent, Salaries | P&L A/c | Debit |
| Discount Received | P&L A/c | Credit |
| Provision for Doubtful Debts | P&L A/c | Debit |
| Capital, Loan | Balance Sheet | Liability |
| Debtors, Stock, Cash | Balance Sheet | Asset |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** Closing Stock appears **twice** — credit of Trading A/c (as closing inventory reducing COGS) and asset side of Balance Sheet — but it is **never** inside the Trial Balance list when adjustments carry it in.

Gross Profit = Net Sales − COGS, where COGS = Opening Stock + Net Purchases + Direct Expenses − Closing Stock. Net Profit = Gross Profit + Other Income − Indirect Expenses (all figures in ₹). Working Capital = Current Assets − Current Liabilities (₹).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

> Stop treating Final Accounts as memorised debit-credit slots; each adjustment is a transaction with two postings, and ICAI examiners hunt the missing second posting.

#### The Three-Statement Architecture

Trading Account matches direct revenue against direct cost for one accounting period. Direct cost includes Opening Stock, Purchases (net of Purchase Returns), Carriage Inward, Wages, Manufacturing Expenses, and Factory Expenses. Closing Stock on the credit side closes the COGS loop.

Profit & Loss Account absorbs everything that Trading leaves behind: indirect expenses (rent, salaries, printing, depreciation), financial charges (interest on loan, discount allowed), and non-operating income (discount received, commission, interest on investments). The bottom line — Net Profit — is then added to Capital in the Balance Sheet.

Balance Sheet is a **statement**, not an account; it lists assets and liabilities at a single date, never over a period. Marshalling picks the order: assets by liquidity (Cash first) or permanence (Land & Building first); liabilities opposite.

| Comparison Axis | Trading Account | Profit & Loss Account | Balance Sheet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Compute Gross Profit | Compute Net Profit | Show financial position |
| Time frame | For the period | For the period | As on a date |
| Closing Stock | Credit side | Not shown | Asset side |
| Carriage Inward | Debit side | Not shown | Not shown |
| Depreciation | Not shown | Debit side | Deducted from asset |
| Provision for Bad Debts | Not shown | Debit side | Deducted from Debtors |
| Capital | Not shown | Net Profit added | Liability side |
| Drawings | Not shown | Not shown | Deducted from Capital |

#### Adjustment Posting Logic

Every ICAI adjustment touches two places. Outstanding Expense debits the concerned expense account (P&L) and appears as a current liability in the Balance Sheet. Prepaid Expense credits the expense account and becomes a current asset. Accrued Income credits P&L and is shown as an asset; Income Received in Advance debits P&L and sits as a liability.

Depreciation: under SLM, debit P&L and deduct the accumulated figure from the concerned asset in the Balance Sheet. Bad Debts: debit P&L (new bad debts) and deduct from Debtors; the old Provision balance is credited to P&L for the amount written off, then a fresh Provision is created on the reduced Debtors and debited to P&L.

#### Worked Illustration

Trial Balance extract (₹): Opening Stock 50,000; Purchases 3,00,000; Sales 5,50,000; Wages 80,000; Salaries 60,000; Debtors 1,20,000; Provision for Doubtful Debts (old) 6,000; Bad Debts 4,000; Closing Stock (in adjustment) 70,000; New Provision required 5% on Debtors after further bad debts.

**Trading A/c:** Opening Stock 50,000 + Purchases 3,00,000 + Wages 80,000 = 4,30,000. Less Closing Stock 70,000 → COGS 3,60,000. Net Sales 5,50,000 − COGS 3,60,000 = Gross Profit ₹1,90,000.

**P&L A/c (Bad Debt treatment):** Bad Debts 4,000 + New Provision required 5% on (1,20,000 − 4,000) = 5,800, less Old Provision 6,000 → Net P&L charge = 4,000 + 5,800 − 6,000 = ₹3,800.

**Balance Sheet:** Debtors shown as 1,20,000 − 4,000 − 5,800 = ₹1,10,200; Closing Stock ₹70,000 under Current Assets.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students debit the full new provision 5,800 without crediting the old provision 6,000 to P&L — that double-counts the bad debt risk and inflates expenses. Always net old provision against new provision on the P&L debit side.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

> Edge cases in Final Accounts decide whether you score 7 or 8 out of 8 on the adjustment-heavy question ICAI repeats every cycle.

#### Manufacturing Account Branch

When raw materials are converted into finished goods, a Manufacturing Account sits **before** the Trading Account. It opens with Raw Materials Consumed (Opening Stock + Purchases − Closing Stock of raw material), adds Wages, Factory Power, Factory Rent, Depreciation on Plant, and Manufacturing Expenses, then yields Cost of Goods Manufactured, which replaces Purchases in the Trading Account. ICAI tests this when trial balance shows "Work-in-Progress" or "Finished Goods" as separate ledgers.

#### Advanced Trap Catalogue

1. **Sale of Old Furniture** — credit the Furniture Account at book value, post the balancing profit or loss to P&L credit or debit side respectively; never route through Trading Account.
2. **Interest on Capital** — debit P&L, add to Capital Account on the liability side; it is not a separate liability.
3. **Partner's Drawings in Goods** — credit Purchases Account with the cost, debit Drawings Account, then close Drawings against Capital.
4. **Provision for Discount on Debtors** — calculated on **good debtors** (Debtors − Further Bad Debts − New Provision for Doubtful Debts); it is purely a P&L debit with no balance-sheet deduction confusion because it is shown as a separate current liability.
5. **Depreciation on Additions** — under WDV, full-year depreciation only on assets held for the full year; pro-rate additions by months of use, or follow the policy given in the question.
6. **Goods in Transit** — added to Purchases in Trading A/c (if purchase) and shown as a separate current asset if the risk has passed; otherwise a contingent note.
7. **Bills Receivable dishonoured** — reverse to Debtors with a note; never treat as bad debt.

#### Practice Prompts

1. A trial balance shows Purchases ₹6,00,000, Sales ₹9,00,000, Opening Stock ₹1,20,000, Closing Stock (adjustment) ₹1,50,000, and Carriage Inward ₹40,000 wrongly placed in P&L. Recast the Trading Account and quantify the original Gross Profit error.
2. Debtors stand at ₹2,00,000 with an old Provision of ₹10,000. Further Bad Debts of ₹20,000 are confirmed. New Provision is to be 5% on remaining debtors, and an additional Provision for Discount on Debtors at 2% is required. Compute the net P&L debit and the Balance Sheet figure for Debtors.

> 📌 **Formula Check:** New Provision on Debtors = Percentage × (Debtors − Further Bad Debts − New Bad Debts already written off but not yet adjusted); Provision for Discount = Percentage × (Debtors − Further Bad Debts − New Provision for Doubtful Debts).

#### Connection Map

Final Accounts sit between the Trial Balance and the financial statements covered at Inter (Ind AS adjustments, consolidated balance sheet). A weak grip on marshalling and adjustment postings here directly lowers scores in Paper 5 of Inter, where every adjustment carries an Ind AS footnote.

| Adjuster | P&L side | Balance Sheet posting | Common slip |
|---|---|---|---|
| Outstanding Interest | Debit | Current Liability | Forgetting second posting |
| Prepaid Insurance | Credit | Current Asset | Treating as expense cut |
| Income Received in Advance | Debit (reverse) | Current Liability | Routing to liability only |
| Accrued Interest | Credit | Current Asset | Double-crediting |
| Bad Debts Recovered | Credit | Reduce Provision or add to Cash | Putting in Trading A/c |
