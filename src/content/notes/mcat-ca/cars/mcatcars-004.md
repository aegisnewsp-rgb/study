# Humanities Passages — MCAT CARS

Humanities passages are one of the three passage categories on the MCAT CARS section. They are drawn from philosophy, literature, art history, music, religion, and ethics. The MCAT frames Humanities passages as testing interpretation of narrative voice, symbolic meaning, and historical or cultural context. About one-third of the CARS passages are Humanities.

> Verify the live CARS section length and passage distribution on https://students-residents.aamc.org/ before planning around the figures below.

---

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### What Humanities passages look like

- **Philosophy:** arguments about ethics, knowledge, existence, justice, or meaning. The author defends a position or critiques one.
- **Literature:** excerpts from novels, short stories, essays, or memoirs. The author explores character, theme, voice, or setting.
- **Art history:** analyses of a work of art, an artistic movement, or a critic's response to art.
- **Music:** analyses of a musical work, a composer's style, or a listener's response.
- **Religion:** analyses of religious belief, practice, history, or text.
- **Ethics:** arguments about moral reasoning, applied ethics, or moral dilemmas.

#### What Humanities items test

- Interpretation of the author's central theme or claim.
- Identification of the author's tone and purpose.
- Reasoning within the text (Skill 2) and beyond (Skill 3).
- Recognition of symbolic or figurative language.
- Understanding of historical or cultural context.

#### One-pass drill

1. Read the passage in 4 minutes. Identify the discipline (philosophy, literature, etc.).
2. Identify the author's central claim or theme.
3. Answer the items. Pay attention to the author's voice, not the reader's view.

#### Examiner traps

- Treating a literary passage as if it were an argument. Literature items often ask about theme, tone, or character, not about the truth of a claim.
- Confusing the author's view with the narrator's view (for fiction passages).
- Ignoring the historical or cultural context. Humanities passages often assume knowledge of the relevant period or tradition.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Strategy for philosophy passages

- Identify the author's position: what does the author argue for?
- Identify the premises: what reasons does the author give?
- Identify the counter-arguments: does the author address opposing views?
- For items, focus on the structure of the argument and the specific claims.

The MCAT tests philosophy passages for argument evaluation, claim identification, and assumption recognition.

#### Strategy for literature passages

- Identify the genre: novel, short story, essay, memoir.
- Identify the narrator or speaker: who is telling the story?
- Identify the central theme or character.
- For items, focus on the literary devices: imagery, metaphor, symbolism, narrative voice.

The MCAT tests literature passages for theme identification, tone, narrative perspective, and figurative language.

#### Strategy for art history and music passages

- Identify the work, artist, or movement discussed.
- Identify the critic's claim: what does the author argue about the work?
- For items, focus on the author's argument, not on whether the argument is correct.

#### Strategy for religion and ethics passages

- Identify the tradition, text, or moral framework discussed.
- Identify the author's central claim.
- For items, focus on the author's reasoning and the implications of the argument.

#### Practice routine

- Take one Humanities passage from each discipline (philosophy, literature, art history, religion, ethics). Read each in 4 minutes.
- Answer the items. Identify the discipline-specific features that each item tests.
- Time yourself: under 8 minutes per passage.
- Take 5 passages in a row. Repeat the routine.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How Humanities passages connect to the other CARS passage categories

- **Social Sciences passages** share the argument-evaluation skill with philosophy passages.
- **Natural Sciences passages for non-scientists** share the general-audience framing with some Humanities passages (e.g., literature for a general audience).
- **All four CARS skills** apply across the three passage categories, but the emphasis varies by discipline.

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"Literature passages have no argument."** Literature passages have a central theme or claim; items test interpretation of that theme.
- **"I need to know the literary work."** The MCAT uses excerpts from real works, but the items can be answered from the passage alone.
- **"Art history is about memorising works."** Art history passages test the critic's argument about a work or movement, not the work itself.

#### Exam technique

- Read the passage in 4 minutes. Identify the discipline and the central claim or theme.
- For literature passages, focus on theme, tone, and narrative voice.
- For philosophy passages, focus on the structure of the argument.
- For art history and music passages, focus on the critic's argument.
- Use the platform's flag-for-review function for items you cannot answer.
