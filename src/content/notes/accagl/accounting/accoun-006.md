# Final Accounts

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Final Accounts** are the year-end financial statements that close the bookkeeping cycle by converting the Trial Balance into reports on trading performance and financial position. For sole traders and partnerships preparing for ACCA/CA Pakistan papers, three statements are mandatory: the **Trading Account**, the **Profit and Loss Account**, and the **Balance Sheet**, with a **Manufacturing Account** inserted before the Trading Account whenever production activity is involved.

The must-know formula chain is:
**Gross Profit = Net Sales − (Opening Stock + Net Purchases + Direct Expenses − Closing Stock)**, then **Net Profit = Gross Profit + Other Income − Operating Expenses**, and finally **Working Capital = Current Assets − Current Liabilities** on the Balance Sheet side.

High-yield pointers: closing stock is **never** an expense — it is credited to Trading and carried to the Balance Sheet as a current asset. Drawings reduce owner's capital; capital introduced increases it — both bypass the P&L. Depreciation, bad debts written off, and movements in the provision for doubtful debts are all P&L charges.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Sequence of Preparation
The Trial Balance is the launch point. Adjustments listed in the question (and any additional note) are posted via journal entries, after which the statements are drafted in a fixed order: **Manufacturing → Trading → P&L → Balance Sheet**. The vertical (statement) format is the CA Pakistan and ACCA preferred presentation.

#### Manufacturing Account
Debit side: **Opening Raw Materials + Purchases + Carriage Inward + Wages/Factory Labour + Manufacturing Overheads (factory rent, factory power, depreciation on plant)**. Credit side: **Sales of Scrap + Closing Raw Materials + Cost of Goods Manufactured** (balancing figure transferred to Trading A/c).

#### Trading Account
Debits include Opening Stock of Finished Goods, Net Purchases (Purchases less Purchase Returns, plus carriage inward), and Direct Expenses such as customs duty and wages of sales staff only if production-related. Credits are Net Sales and **Closing Stock of Finished Goods** — both must be brought down into the Balance Sheet.

#### Profit and Loss Account
Indirect expenses land here: office salaries, rent, rates, insurance (time-apportioned), depreciation on non-factory assets, bad debts written off, and the *increase* in the provision for doubtful debts. Discounts allowed and interest on loan appear on the debit; discounts received and commission earned on the credit.

#### Adjustments That Trip Students Up
- **Accruals** (outstanding expenses) are added to the relevant expense and shown as a current liability.
- **Prepayments** are deducted from expense and shown as a current asset.
- **Depreciation** is split: factory portion → Manufacturing; non-factory → P&L.
- **Provision for doubtful debts**: specific provision is a direct write-down of a named debtor; general provision covers the remainder. Only the *movement* (increase) hits the P&L.

#### Balance Sheet Ordering
Fixed assets at top in order of permanence; current assets listed by liquidity (stock, debtors, cash). On the liabilities side, capital (with adjustments) sits above long-term loans, with current liabilities (creditors, accruals, bank overdraft, provision for taxation) last.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases in Practice
- **Goods taken by the proprietor (drawings of stock)**: debit Drawings, credit Purchases — never touch the P&L. The Trading Account's purchase figure falls, and the gross profit rises commensurately.
- **Goods received but not invoiced** (goods in transit at year-end) are added to closing stock and to creditors under the accruals concept.
- **Sale on sale-or-return basis** is excluded from sales until the customer accepts; stock remains with the seller.
- **Mark-up vs margin questions**: in CA Pakistan papers, students are routinely asked to compute closing stock from a known gross profit margin — the algebra is *Closing Stock = (Sales × (100 − Margin%)) − (Opening Stock + Net Purchases + Direct Expenses)*.
- **Partnership flavour**: the P&L appropriation account splits net profit between partners' salaries, interest on capital, interest on drawings, and the residual share — these items do not appear in the Trading or P&L accounts themselves.

#### Common Mistakes
- Posting closing stock only to the Trading Account and forgetting to list it under Current Assets on the Balance Sheet.
- Treating the *total* provision for doubtful debts as the P&L charge instead of the *movement* (closing − opening + specific write-offs).
- Forgetting to deduct drawings and add capital from the Capital Account on the Balance Sheet.
- Splitting a combined expense (e.g. rent of premises used for both factory and office) without the ratio given in the question.

#### Worked Micro-Example
Opening Stock ₹20,000; Purchases ₹150,000; Carriage Inward ₹5,000; Sales ₹260,000; Closing Stock ₹30,000; Wages (60% factory, 40% office) ₹40,000.
#### COGS = 20,000 + 150,000 + 5,000 + 24,000 − 30,000 = 169,000
**Gross Profit = 260,000 − 169,000 = 91,000** (margin 35%). Office wages of ₹16,000 sit in the P&L.
