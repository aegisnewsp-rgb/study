# Topic 1

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your UPPSC RO/ARO Prelims.

The Indian Constitution is the **lengthiest written constitution** in the world, enacted on **26 November 1949** and enforced on **26 January 1950**. It blends **rigidity and flexibility** through three amendment routes under **Article 368**. The **Preamble** declares India a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic; it is **not enforceable** but guides interpretation. **Fundamental Rights (Part III, Articles 12–35)** are justiciable; **Directive Principles (Part IV, Articles 36–51)** are non-justiciable; **Fundamental Duties (Part IVA, Article 51A)** were inserted by the **42nd Amendment, 1976** and apply only to citizens.

- **Article 32** — "heart and soul" of the Constitution (Dr. B.R. Ambedkar), writ jurisdiction of the Supreme Court.
- **44th Amendment, 1978** — removed **Right to Property** as a Fundamental Right; **86th Amendment, 2002** — added **Article 21A**, making education a right for ages 6–14.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months of prep.

#### Salient Features of the Constitution
The Constitution draws from multiple sources: the **parliamentary system** from Britain, **fundamental rights and judicial review** from the USA, the **concept of Directive Principles** from Ireland, and the **federal with unitary bias** structure is indigenous. It originally contained **395 Articles** and **8 Schedules**; today it has **470+ Articles** and **12 Schedules**.

#### Part III, IV, and IVA — The Triad
**Part III (Articles 12–35)** grants six Fundamental Rights: Right to Equality (14–18), Right to Freedom (19–22), Right against Exploitation (23–24), Right to Freedom of Religion (25–28), Cultural and Educational Rights (29–30), and Right to Constitutional Remedies (32). **Article 32** allows direct petition to the Supreme Court through writs — *habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, certiorari, quo warranto*.

**Part IV (Articles 36–51)** lists Directive Principles — social, economic, and political goals. They are **non-justiciable**; the state cannot be sued for not implementing them, though Article 37 makes them "fundamental in governance."

**Part IVA (Article 51A)** lists 11 Fundamental Duties, applicable **only to citizens**, added on the recommendation of the **Swaran Singh Committee, 1976**.

#### Preamble and Constitutional Interpretation
The **Berubari Union case (1960)** held the Preamble is not part of the Constitution; the **Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973)** 13-judge bench later held it **is** a part and a source of the **basic structure doctrine**.

|---|---|---|
| Fundamental Rights | Yes (Article 32/226) | USA |
| Directive Principles | No | Ireland |

#### Emergency and Federalism
**Article 352** (National Emergency), **Article 356** (President's Rule), and **Article 360** (Financial Emergency) convert the federal structure into a **unitary** one. The **44th Amendment (1978)** restricted National Emergency to **armed rebellion** (replacing "internal disturbance") and made its declaration subject to **written advice** of the cabinet.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for long-term mastery and edge cases.

#### The Basic Structure Doctrine
In **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)**, the Supreme Court held that Parliament's amending power under **Article 368** is not unlimited — it cannot destroy the **basic structure** of the Constitution. This was reaffirmed in **Indira Nehru Gandhi v. Raj Narain (1975)**, **Minerva Mills v. Union of India (1980)**, and **I.R. Coelho v. State of Tamil Nadu (2007)**. The basic structure includes judicial review, federalism, secularism, and the harmony between Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles.

#### Schedules You Must Number
The **First Schedule** lists states and territories; the **Seventh Schedule** contains the three lists (Union, State, Concurrent); the **Eighth Schedule** names 22 scheduled languages (last added: **Bodo, Dogri, Maithili, Santhali** via 92nd Amendment, 2003); the **Ninth and Tenth Schedules** deal with land reforms and anti-defection matters. The **Twelfth Schedule** (added by 74th Amendment, 1992) covers **Municipalities**.

#### Article 370 and Recent Amendments
**Article 370** granted special autonomous status to Jammu & Kashmir and was abrogated in **August 2019** through a Presidential Order under Article 367 and the **Constitution (Application to Jammu and Kashmir) Order, 2019**. The **J&K Reorganisation Act, 2019** bifurcated the state into two Union Territories. The **103rd Amendment, 2019** introduced **10% Economically Weaker Section (EWS) reservation** in government jobs and educational institutions.

#### Common Traps in the Exam Hall
- **Money bills** originate **only in Lok Sabha**; Rajya Sabha can recommend amendments within **14 days** but Lok Sabha may accept or reject them.
- The **anti-defection law (52nd Amendment, 1985; 10th Schedule)** was inserted on the recommendation of the **Dinesh Goswami Committee**.
- The **Speaker's casting vote** (Lok Sabha) exists even after the 2015 amendment; the **National Commission for Scheduled Castes (Article 338)** and **National Commission for Scheduled Tribes (Article 338A)** were bifurcated by the **89th Amendment, 2003**.

#### Practice Prompts
1. The **44th Amendment, 1978** restricted the scope of **National Emergency (Article 352)** by replacing the phrase *"internal disturbance"* with **"armed rebellion."** Critically analyse its implications for federalism.
2. Compare the **justiciability** of Fundamental Rights, Directive Principles, and Fundamental Duties, citing relevant constitutional articles and at least one Supreme Court judgment.
