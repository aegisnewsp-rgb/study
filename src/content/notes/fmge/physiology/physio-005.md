# Respiratory Physiology

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Respiratory physiology** describes how O₂ reaches tissues and how CO₂ is cleared, covering ventilation, alveolar exchange, blood gas transport, and breathing regulation. The four lung capacities are split into **eight volumes**: tidal volume (TV ≈ 500 mL), inspiratory reserve volume (IRV ≈ 3000 mL), expiratory reserve volume (ERV ≈ 1100 mL), and residual volume (RV ≈ 1200 mL). Vital capacity = IRV + TV + ERV; **TLC = VC + RV**; FRC = ERV + RV.

The alveolar gas equation **PAO₂ = PiO₂ − (PaCO₂ / R)** gives an alveolar PO₂ near 100 mmHg at sea level. About **98.5% of O₂ rides on haemoglobin**, with only 1.5% dissolved; one Hb tetramer binds four O₂ molecules cooperatively. CO₂ travels mainly as **HCO₃⁻** (~70%) via carbonic anhydrase, requiring the chloride (Hamburger) shift.

- **Central chemoreceptors**: ventrolateral medulla, sense CSF pH (↑PaCO₂ → ↓pH → ↑ventilation).
- **Peripheral chemoreceptors**: carotid and aortic bodies, respond to ↓PaO₂, ↑H⁺, ↑CO₂.
- **V/Q ratio = 0.8** normally; zero in shunt, infinite in dead space.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Mechanics of ventilation
Inspiration is active: the diaphragm contracts, external intercostals lift the ribs, and intrapleural pressure falls from about −5 cmH₂O (at FRC) toward −8 cmH₂O. Expiration at rest is passive recoil of lung and chest wall. **Surfactant** from type II pneumocytes lowers alveolar surface tension, preventing collapse of small alveoli and reducing the work of breathing. Lung compliance (C = ΔV/ΔP) falls in pulmonary fibrosis and rises in emphysema.

#### Lung volumes on spirometry
Spirometry measures TV, IRV, ERV, VC, FVC, and FEV₁, but **RV, FRC, and TLC** require helium dilution, nitrogen washout, or body plethysmography. An obstructive pattern shows **FEV₁/FVC < 0.7** (asthma, COPD); a restrictive pattern shows **low FVC with normal or high FEV₁/FVC** (interstitial lung disease).

#### Gas exchange and transport
Fick's law governs diffusion: V̇gas = (A · D · ΔP) / T. A thicker membrane (fibrosis) or smaller area (emphysema) lowers DLCO. The O₂–Hb dissociation curve is sigmoidal; **right shift** (↑H⁺, ↑PaCO₂, ↑temperature, ↑2,3-BPG, e.g., exercising muscle) promotes O₂ unloading, while **left shift** (foetal Hb, alkalosis, hypothermia, ↓2,3-BPG) impairs it. The **Bohr effect** describes pH/CO₂ influence on O₂ binding, while the **Haldane effect** describes how deoxygenated blood carries more CO₂.

#### Regulation of breathing
Dorsal and ventral respiratory groups in the medulla generate rhythm. The pontine pneumotaxic centre limits inspiration, while the apneustic centre prolongs it. Central chemoreceptors dominate under normal conditions; **peripheral chemoreceptors provide the hypoxic drive** in chronic hypercapnia (e.g., long-standing COPD), which is why high-flow oxygen must be given cautiously there.

| Parameter | Typical value | Clinical note |
|---|---|---|
| PaO₂ | 95–100 mmHg | < 60 mmHg = respiratory failure |
| PaCO₂ | 35–45 mmHg | > 50 mmHg = hypercapnia |
| TV | 500 mL | Dead space ≈ 150 mL |
| V/Q | 0.8 | Low in airway obstruction, high in PE |
| FEV₁/FVC | > 0.7 | < 0.7 = obstruction |

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Alveolar ventilation vs minute ventilation
Minute ventilation (V̇E = f × Vt) can stay constant while alveolar ventilation (V̇A = f × (Vt − Vd)) collapses if breathing becomes shallow. Doubling respiratory rate with halved tidal volume cuts alveolar ventilation roughly in half — a classic trap when reviewing a tachypnoeic, shallow-breathing patient.

#### V/Q pathology in detail
A **shunt** (V/Q = 0, e.g., atelectasis, ARDS) does not correct with supplemental O₂ because perfused blood never meets ventilated alveoli. **Dead space** (V/Q = ∞, e.g., pulmonary embolism) raises the A–a gradient but PaCO₂ often normalises via compensatory hyperventilation. The **alveolar–arterial (A–a) gradient** for O₂ is normally 5–15 mmHg and rises with age; a widened gradient in hypoxaemia points to V/Q mismatch, shunt, or diffusion defect.

#### Periodic breathing patterns
- **Cheyne–Stokes**: crescendo–decrescendo cycles with apnoea; heart failure, high altitude, raised ICP.
- **Kussmaul**: deep, rapid, laboured; diabetic ketoacidosis.
- **Biot (ataxic)**: irregular, unpredictable; medullary damage.
- **Apneustic**: prolonged inspiration, short expiration; pontine lesion.

#### Common mistakes
- Stating that **residual volume can be measured by spirometry** — it cannot, by definition.
- Confusing **Bohr and Haldane effects** (Bohr = CO₂/H⁺ shifts O₂ curve; Haldane = O₂ unloading shifts CO₂ carrying).
- Forgetting the **chloride shift** during CO₂ transport; without it, RBCs would alkalinise and HCO₃⁻ export would fail.
- Treating **hypoxic drive as the primary stimulus** in healthy people; central CO₂/pH drive dominates, and hypoxic drive is a backup unmasked in chronic CO₂ retainers.
- Applying the alveolar gas equation with R = 0.8 universally; pure carbohydrate metabolism gives R = 1.0, pure fat ≈ 0.7.

#### Practice prompts
1. A patient breathes at 20/min with a tidal volume of 300 mL. Given anatomical dead space of 150 mL, calculate alveolar ventilation. *(Answer: 20 × (300 − 150) = 3000 mL/min.)*
2. A COPD patient has PaO₂ 55 mmHg, PaCO₂ 55 mmHg, and Hb 15 g/dL. Use CaO₂ = (1.34 × Hb × SaO₂) + (0.003 × PaO₂) and an SaO₂ of about 88% to estimate arterial O₂ content. *(Answer: ≈ 17.7 + 0.17 ≈ 17.9 mL O₂/dL.)*
