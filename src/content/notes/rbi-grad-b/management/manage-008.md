# SWOT Analysis

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

SWOT Analysis is a strategic planning framework that audits an organisation across four quadrants: **Strengths**, **Weaknesses**, **Opportunities**, and **Threats**. Strengths and Weaknesses are internal, manager-controlled attributes (finance, HR, brand, processes). Opportunities and Threats are external, uncontrollable factors originating from PESTEL (Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Environmental, Legal) and Porter's Five Forces.

The framework is shown as a 2×2 matrix pairing Internal vs External with Helpful vs Harmful. It is a *diagnostic*, not a *prescription* — the analysis lists factors, and the matching into actionable strategy is done by the separate **TOWS matrix** (SO, WO, ST, WT cells).

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"SWOT = Snapshot; TOWS = Tactics."** Remember S→O, W→O, S→T, W→T pairing: **"SOW STO"** — internal-positive + external-positive = Growth (SO); internal-negative + external-positive = Re-engineer (WO); internal-positive + external-negative = Defend (ST); internal-negative + external-negative = Retreat (WT).

#### Key Term Glossary

| Term | Definition | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Strength | Resource or capability giving competitive edge | Internal, Helpful |
| Weakness | Internal limitation reducing performance | Internal, Harmful |
| Opportunity | External favourable trend to exploit | External, Helpful |
| Threat | External unfavourable trend endangering position | External, Harmful |
| TOWS Matrix | Cross-pairs SWOT factors into four strategy types | Strategy generator |
| IE Matrix | Internal-External Matrix for portfolio prioritisation | Quantitative complement |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

SWOT Analysis was popularised in the 1960s–70s through the Stanford Research Institute and the work of management scholars Albert Humphrey, C. Ronald Christenson, and Kenneth Andrews. It structures an internal audit (resources, value chain activities, core competencies) against an external environmental scan (macro PESTEL forces plus industry-level Porter's Five Forces). The four quadrants are populated through tools such as the **Value Chain Analysis** (internal), **Resource-Based View** (internal), and **PESTEL** (external).

A critical distinction RBI examiners test is that **SWOT is qualitative and non-weighted**. It cannot tell a PSU bank that "low digital penetration" outweighs "strong CASA franchise." Quantitative complements such as the **Internal-External (IE) Matrix**, the **BCG Matrix**, the **QSPM (Quantitative Strategic Planning Matrix)**, and the **Ansoff Matrix** are required to convert the SWOT inventory into prioritised, executable strategy.

#### Comparison Matrix — SWOT vs TOWS vs QSPM vs IE Matrix

| Tool | Purpose | Output | Weighted? | RBI Test Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SWOT | Diagnose internal/external position | 4-quadrant list of factors | No | Very High |
| TOWS | Match factors into strategy alternatives | SO / WO / ST / WT strategy cells | No | Very High |
| IE Matrix | Plot divisions on a 9-cell grid | Hold / Harvest / Divest call | Yes (scores) | High |
| QSPM | Rank alternative strategies numerically | Final strategy priority list | Yes (scores) | Medium |
| Ansoff | Choose growth direction | Market-penetration / development / diversification | No | High |

#### TOWS Strategy Pairings

1. **SO (Maxi-Maxi):** Use Strengths to capture Opportunities — e.g., a PSU bank's strong CASA base deployed into digital lending.
2. **WO (Mini-Maxi):** Overcome Weaknesses by exploiting Opportunities — e.g., partner with a fintech to fix weak digital infrastructure.
3. **ST (Maxi-Mini):** Use Strengths to mitigate Threats — e.g., leverage government ownership to absorb regulatory shocks.
4. **WT (Mini-Mini):** Defensive retreat, divest, or liquidate — e.g., exit an unprofitable foreign subsidiary.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** *RBI Bank, a hypothetical public-sector bank, identifies (i) 42% CASA ratio, (ii) aged core-banking software, (iii) RBI's digital lending guidelines permitting fintech-bank partnerships, and (iv) aggressive entry of private-sector digital lenders. Construct a 2×2 SWOT, then derive the most defensible TOWS strategy and the quantitative tool that should follow.*

#### Solution:

#### Step 1 — Classify the four factors:
- (i) 42% CASA ratio → **Strength** (internal, helpful).
- (ii) Aged core-banking software → **Weakness** (internal, harmful).
- (iii) RBI digital lending guidelines → **Opportunity** (external, helpful).
- (iv) Entry of private digital lenders → **Threat** (external, harmful).

**Step 2 — Populate the 2×2 SWOT matrix** with Internal (top) vs External (bottom) and Helpful (left) vs Harmful (right).

#### Step 3 — Match in TOWS:
- SO: Use CASA strength to lend against low-cost deposits to fintech partners.
- WO: Use the RBI opportunity (fintech-bank tie-ups) to overcome aged CBS weakness.
- ST: Use CASA franchise to defend market share against private digital lenders.
- WT: Avoid retreating from retail — instead, modernise.

**Step 4 — Recommend follow-up tool:** the **QSPM** (Quantitative Strategic Planning Matrix) should rank SO/WO/ST/WT alternatives using IFE and EFE scores before final selection.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Candidates routinely write *"Use strengths to overcome threats"* as the universal answer. That is the ST cell — only valid when an external threat exists. If the question gives an opportunity, the correct pair is SO or WO. Also, do **not** weight SWOT factors numerically — SWOT is qualitative; weighting belongs to QSPM or the IE Matrix.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Advanced Traps and Exceptions

1. **Static-snapshot problem:** SWOT captures a single point in time. A bank's NIM looked healthy in FY24 but compressed in FY25 as deposit rates repriced faster than loan rates — SWOT would not flag this dynamic lag unless refreshed quarterly.
2. **Listing effects rather than causes:** Writing "declining market share" as a weakness is an *effect*; the *cause* (e.g., slower digital onboarding than private peers) is the actionable weakness. RBI examiners award higher marks when candidates dig to causes.
3. **Confusing internal with external:** "Skilled workforce" is a strength (internal), not an opportunity (external). "Industry growth of 12% CAGR" is an opportunity, not a strength. Mixing these categories breaks the SWOT logic.
4. **Strategic vs operational factors:** "Branch network of 5,000" is operational; "ability to cross-sell insurance through branch footfall" is strategic. The latter belongs in SWOT.
5. **SWOT as groupthink artefact:** Brainstorming sessions often produce uniformly positive or uniformly negative lists. Best practice — used in RBI's own internal strategy reviews — is to validate each factor against a published source (RBI Annual Report, SEBI filings, IBA data).
6. **No link to vision/mission:** SWOT outputs must feed back to the firm's stated objectives. A TOWS strategy irrelevant to the mission (e.g., entering crypto trading for a conservative PSU bank) is academically valid but practically rejected.
7. **Combining with Ansoff and Porter:** In RBI Phase-II descriptive questions, the highest-scoring answers integrate SWOT with **Ansoff's growth matrix** and **Porter's generic strategies** to show fit between internal capability and external positioning.

#### Quantitative Bridges — When to Use What

| Situation | Recommended Tool | Why SWOT Alone Fails |
|---|---|---|
| 5 business units, choose which to fund | BCG Matrix / IE Matrix | SWOT cannot compare units |
| 4 alternative strategies, pick one | QSPM | SWOT cannot rank |
| Decide market vs product vs diversification | Ansoff Matrix | SWOT cannot pick direction |
| Long-term competitive positioning | Porter's Generic Strategies | SWOT misses industry logic |
| Macro-trend mapping | PESTEL → EFE Matrix | SWOT omits quantification |

#### Two Advanced Practice Prompts

1. *You are the Chief Strategy Officer of a small finance bank. RBI has just issued prompt correction action (PCA) guidelines tightening capital adequacy. Draft a SWOT (with at least three factors per quadrant) and a TOWS that is consistent with the bank's PCA constraints.*
2. *Critically evaluate why SWOT alone is insufficient for RBI's 2024-25 supervisory strategy framework. Which two quantitative complements would you recommend, and how would you sequence them after the SWOT?*

> 📌 **Strategy Tip:** In Phase-II, allocate ~12 minutes for a 15-mark SWOT-cum-TOWS question. Use a clearly drawn 2×2 matrix in the answer sheet; examiners scan for quadrant labels before reading prose. Always close with a one-line statement linking the chosen strategy to the firm's stated mission.
