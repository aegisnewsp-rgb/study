# British Raj in Sindh

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The British Raj in Sindh covers **1843–1947 CE**, beginning with Charles Napier's conquest of the Talpur Mirs and ending with Sindh's merger into Pakistan. Two decisive battles sealed Talpur rule: the **Battle of Miani (17 February 1843)** and the **Battle of Dubba (24 March 1843)**. From 1843 to 1936, Sindh was administered as part of the **Bombay Presidency** through the office of the **Commissioner-in-Sindh**, then separated on **1 April 1936** under the Government of India Act, 1935, becoming a Governor's Province in 1937.

- **Conquest:** Napier's 'Annexation' justified by treaty breaches and strategic Indus access.
- **Administration:** Three divisions — Karachi, Hyderabad, Sukkur — under Bombay until 1936.
- **Separation:** G.M. Syed, Allah Bakhsh Soomro led the demand; achieved 1 April 1936.
- **Economy:** Bartle Frere's **Sindh Cotton Boom (1860s)** linked Sindh to Manchester via the **Indus Steam Flotilla**.
- **Education:** **Sindh Madressatul Islam** founded in Karachi, 1885, by **Hassanally Effendi**.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"M-D-S-F-K"** for the four pillars — **M**iani 1843, **D**ubba 1843, **S**eparation 1936, **F**rere's Cotton Boom, **K**arachi as new capital. Recall order = chronological conquest timeline.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Causes and Conquest (1843)
Napier invaded Sindh citing the Talpur Mirs' failure to honour the 1809 treaty, Russian frontier influence (the **Great Game**), and the strategic Indus corridor to Punjab and Afghanistan. He defeated Mir Sher Muhammad Khan Talpur's forces at Miani on 17 February 1843, then suppressed the Mirs of Hyderabad at Dubba on 24 March 1843. Mir Nasir Khan Talpur of Khairpur signed a separate treaty and retained the title of **Nawab**, while the upper-Sindh Mirs were deposed and exiled.

#### Administrative Structure
Sindh was never an independent Presidency — it was bolted onto the **Bombay Presidency** via the Commissioner-in-Sindh. Three divisions were carved: **Karachi**, **Hyderabad**, and **Sukkur** (later incorporating Shikarpur). This 'double government' produced persistent grievances because revenue and policy decisions sat with Bombay, not with local commissioners. The structure persisted until the separation movement succeeded.

#### Comparison: Bombay Presidency vs. Chief Commissioner's Province

| Feature | Under Bombay Presidency (1843–1936) | Chief Commissioner's Province (1 Apr 1936) |
|---|---|---|
| Supervising authority | Governor of Bombay, via Commissioner-in-Sindh | Chief Commissioner, directly under Viceroy |
| Divisions | Karachi, Hyderabad, Sukkur | Same three divisions retained |
| Capital | Karachi (administrative seat) | Karachi declared provincial capital |
| Political agency | Limited; Sindhi members sat in Bombay Council | Separate Sindh legislature established |
| Trigger | Post-annexation convenience | Government of India Act, 1935 |

#### Economic Transformation
**Sir Henry Bartle Frere** (Commissioner-in-Sindh, 1850–1859) launched the **Sindh Cotton Boom** by integrating Sindhi cotton with the **Indus Steam Flotilla Company** and Manchester textile mills. The **Karachi-Kotri railway** opened in 1861, and **Karachi Port** was deepened from the 1850s. These interventions converted Sindh from a subsistence agrarian economy into a major raw-cotton exporter routed through Bombay.

#### Key British Personalities and Institutions

- **Sir Charles Napier** — Conqueror; authored the 'Annexation of Sindh' memorandum.
- **Sir John Jacob** — Founded **Jacobabad** (1847) as a military cantonment; raised the **Sindh Frontier Force** (later **Upper Sindh Frontier Force**) for the Baluchistan border.
- **Sir Bartle Frere** — Engineered the Cotton Boom; promoted Karachi as a port.
- **Sir Robert Sandeman** — Border policy architect (worked in Baluchistan, distinct from Sindh administration).
- **Hassanally Effendi** — Founded **Sindh Madressatul Islam**, Karachi, 1885.

#### Separation Movement
During the 1920s–30s, the demand for a separate Sindh province intensified. Leaders including **G.M. Syed**, **Allah Bakhsh Soomro**, **Shaikh Ghulam Hussain**, and **Harchandrai Vishandas** argued that Sindh's identity was being erased within Bombay. The **Government of India Act, 1935** enabled separation, and Sindh became a **Chief Commissioner's Province on 1 April 1936**, then a **Governor's Province in 1937**.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem
**Question:** In which year was Sindh separated from the Bombay Presidency, and under which Act was this separation enacted?
(a) 1935 under the Government of India Act, 1919
(b) 1 April 1936 under the Government of India Act, 1935
(c) 1940 under the Government of India Act, 1935
(d) 1947 under the Indian Independence Act

**Solution:** The correct answer is **(b) 1 April 1936 under the Government of India Act, 1935**. The 1935 Act received royal assent and came into force with provincial autonomy beginning April 1936; Sindh became a Chief Commissioner's Province on that date and a Governor's Province in 1937.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Candidates frequently write **1935** as the separation year because the Act bears that date. The Act was *enacted* in 1935, but *commencement* of Sindh's separation was **1 April 1936**. Read the option's wording carefully — "under the Act" vs. "by the Act."

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Advanced Traps and Exception Cases

1. **Karachi vs. Hyderabad as capital:** Hyderabad was the Talpur capital; Karachi only became Sindh's capital **after 1936 separation**. Before that, Karachi was an administrative port under Bombay's framework, not a provincial capital.
2. **Sindh Frontier Force vs. Sandeman System:** The Sindh Frontier Force was raised by John Jacob in the 1840s–50s for the Baluchistan border. **Robert Sandeman** later applied his own 'Sandeman System' inside Baluchistan itself — distinct command, distinct province.
3. **Khairpur exception:** Mir Nasir Khan Talpur of Khairpur signed a treaty with Napier in 1843 and retained princely status as **Nawab of Khairpur** until 1955 — Khairpur was therefore **never directly annexed**.
4. **Sindh Madressatul Islam founded before Pakistan Movement:** Established in 1885 by Hassanally Effendi — predates the Muslim League's 1906 founding and Allama Iqbal's 1930 Allahabad address by decades.
5. **Indus Flotilla vs. Sukkur Barrage:** The Flotilla (1850s) was a *steamship company* on the Indus; the **Sukkur Barrage** was planned later (construction began 1923, completed 1932) and reshaped agriculture, not trade.

#### Connection to Pakistan Movement and SPSC Syllabus
The Sindhi Muslim middle class produced by Sindh Madressatul Islam and government schools supplied the cadre of the Pakistan Movement in Sindh. **Allah Bakhsh Soomro** became the first Prime Minister of Sindh (1937) and a Unionist leader; **G.M. Syed** later founded the Jeay Sindh movement. SPSC examiners frequently test the bridge between British-era institutional development and post-1947 Sindhi politics.
