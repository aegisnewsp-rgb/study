# Income Tax Act Basics

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The **Income Tax Act, 1961** is the central statute that charges, collects, and recovers income tax in India, administered by the **CBDT** under the Department of Revenue. It applies to every **assessee** (individual, HUF, firm, LLP, company, AOP/BOI, AJP, local authority) on income earned, received, or deemed to accrue/arise in India during the **Previous Year (PY)**, assessed in the next **Assessment Year (AY)** — the 12-month period from 1 April to 31 March.

Total Income flows through a fixed pipeline: **GTI** = sum of five heads of income under Sec 14 → minus **deductions u/s 80C–80U** → **Total Income** → slab tax + surcharge + 4% Health & Education Cess, minus **rebate u/s 87A** (if TI ≤ ₹5,00,000).

#### Must-know anchors
- **Charging Section 4** levies tax at rates in the **Finance Act** of each year.
- **Five heads (Sec 14)** are mutually exclusive: Salaries, House Property, PGBP, Capital Gains, Other Sources.
- **Sec 80C cap** for individuals/HUF: ₹1,50,000; **Sec 80CCD(1B)** (NPS) additional ₹50,000.

> **Exam tip:** Always state PY and AY explicitly — examiners mark the distinction.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Structural framework of the Act
The Act works on a layered model. **Section 4** is the charging section — tax is levied on the **total income** of every assessee at rates prescribed by the annual **Finance Act**, which overrides the rate schedule in the Act itself. Income is first classified under one of the **five heads** (Sec 14), then aggregated as **Gross Total Income (GTI)**. Deductions under **Chapter VI-A (Sec 80C–80U)** are subtracted from GTI to arrive at **Total Income** — the base for tax computation.

#### Previous Year vs Assessment Year
**PY** is the 12-month period (usually 1 April to 31 March, but may differ for newly set up business) in which income is earned. **AY** is the 12-month period immediately following PY in which that income is assessed and taxed (Sec 3). For income earned in PY ending 31 March 2025, the AY is 2025-26.

#### Heads of income at a glance
| Head | Governing Sections | Typical receipts |
|---|---|---|
| Salaries | 15–17 | Pension, wages, gratuity, perquisites |
| House Property | 22–27 | Rental income from any property owned |
| PGBP | 28–44 | Business/profession profits |
| Capital Gains | 45–55 | Sale of capital asset |
| Other Sources | 56–59 | Residuary, interest, dividends |

#### Exemptions, deductions, rebates — keep them separate
**Exemptions u/s 10** are removed before computing any head. **Deductions u/s 80C–80U** reduce GTI to reach Total Income. **Rebate u/s 87A** reduces the final tax liability (not income) when TI ≤ ₹5,00,000, capping tax at nil.

#### Typical CS Executive questions
- Compute **Total Income** of an individual with salary, rental income, and bank interest (5–8 marks).
- Determine **residential status** of an NRI based on days of stay and apply Sec 5 scope.
- Short note on **PY vs AY** or on any one head of income (3 marks).

> **Trap:** The Health & Education Cess of 4% applies on (tax + surcharge), not on tax alone. Forgetting it loses one mark in every computation.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Residential status — the scope trigger
Section 6 decides **what** is taxable, not **how much**. A **Resident and Ordinarily Resident (ROR)** is taxed on global income; a **Non-Resident (NR)** is taxed only on Indian income; a **Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR)** falls in between. From AY 2021-22, an Indian citizen earning Indian income above **₹15 lakh** who is not liable to tax in any other country qualifies as **Deemed Resident** (Sec 6(1A)).

#### Deemed income and clubbing
Sections 68–69D treat unexplained cash credits, investments, and hundi borrowings as **income of the year in which they are found**, even when no source is disclosed. **Sec 2(14)** defines capital asset; agricultural income (Sec 2(1A)) is partially integrated with non-agricultural income using the IT-Rule formula when the basic and partial integration conditions are met.

#### Corporate dimension for CS Executive
For companies, the default rate is **25%/30%** based on turnover. **Sec 115BAA** offers an optional **22%** rate (no deductions/exemptions except 80JJAA); **Sec 115BAB** offers **15%** for new manufacturing companies (set up after 1 Oct 2019, commenced before 31 Mar 2024). **MAT u/s 115JB** kicks in when regular tax < **15% of book profit**, ensuring a minimum tax floor.

#### Common pitfalls in exam answers
| Mistake | Correction |
|---|---|
| Allowing 80C on exempt income | Deductions apply only on taxable GTI |
| Treating HRA full as exempt | Apply minimum of three tests u/s 10(13A) |
| Ignoring surcharge on incomes above ₹50 lakh | SC is computed before cess |
| Missing cess rounding | Round tax + SC + cess to nearest ₹10 |

#### Practice prompts
1. Mr. A, a resident aged 45, earned ₹9,00,000 salary, ₹1,20,000 rental income (municipal value ₹1,00,000; standard rent ₹1,40,000; interest on loan ₹60,000), ₹40,000 savings-bank interest, and deposited ₹1,50,000 in PPF. Compute **Total Income** under the old regime for AY 2025-26 and tax liability before cess.
2. Explain with reasons whether an Indian citizen who stays 90 days in India in the current PY, was present for 365 days in each of the four preceding PYs, and earns ₹18 lakh Indian income is **ROR, RNOR, or Deemed Resident**.

> **Strategy:** In any 8-mark computation, draw a vertical pipeline — five heads → GTI → Chapter VI-A → Total Income → slab tax → surcharge → cess → rebate. Examiners award step-marks.
