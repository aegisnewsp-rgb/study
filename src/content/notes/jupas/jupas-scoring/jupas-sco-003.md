# Weighted Subjects — English, Mathematics and Electives — JUPAS Notes

This topic covers weighted subject scoring in JUPAS programmes. Many programmes weight specific subjects above 1 in their scoring formulae. Typical weightings: English × 1.5 (English-medium programmes); Mathematics × 1.5 (sciences and engineering); M1/M2 × 1.5 or × 2 (maths-heavy programmes); Biology/Chemistry/Physics × 1.5 in some medical and life-science programmes. In HKUST's expected-score tables, the highest score of at most TWO weighted electives is taken in the Best 5 calculation.

> Verify the live weighted scoring formulae on each university's admissions page before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **English weight**: typically × 1.5 in English-medium programmes.
- **Mathematics weight**: × 1.5 in sciences, engineering, and business programmes.
- **M1/M2 weight**: × 1.5 or × 2 in maths-heavy programmes (especially at HKUST and CUHK).
- **Science elective weights**: Biology/Chemistry/Physics × 1.5 in some medical and life-science programmes.
- **HKUST rule**: the highest score of at most TWO weighted electives is taken in the Best 5 calculation.
- **Source**: published on each programme's admissions page and in the 2025 JUPAS Admissions Scores report.

#### Examiner traps

- Treating English × 1.5 as a fixed weighting — it varies by programme.
- Assuming all HKUST science programmes weight M2 × 2 — they vary.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Common weightings

**English × 1.5**: used by many programmes that emphasise English language ability (e.g. humanities, social sciences, business, law, some science programmes). The English subject is multiplied by 1.5 in the scoring formula.

**Mathematics × 1.5**: used by many science and engineering programmes. The Compulsory Part Mathematics is multiplied by 1.5 in the scoring formula.

**M1/M2 × 1.5 or × 2**: used by maths-heavy programmes (engineering, physical sciences, computer science, actuarial science). M1 (Calculus and Statistics) and M2 (Algebra and Calculus) may be weighted differently. At HKUST, M2 may be weighted × 2 in some programmes.

**Biology/Chemistry/Physics × 1.5**: used by some medical and life-science programmes. The specific science elective required for the programme is often weighted.

#### Examples from JUPAS participating institutions

**HKU** (from the 2026 JUPAS Expected Score document):

- International Research Enrichment programme: English + Math + Best 2 from Biology/Chemistry/Physics/M1/M2 + Best 1 other subject.
- Many programmes weight English × 1.5 and Math × 1.5.

**HKUST** (from the 2026 Expected Scores document):

- English × 1.5 + Math + Best from Biology/Chemistry/Physics/M1/M2 + Best 2 other subjects.
- Some programmes weight Physics/M2 × 2 and Biology/Chemistry × 1.5.
- The highest score of at most TWO weighted electives is taken in the Best 5 calculation.

**CityU, PolyU**: programme-specific weightings, published on their admissions pages.

#### How weighted scoring affects your ranking

**Example**: A candidate has the following JUPAS scores:

- English: 7 (5*)
- Chinese: 5.5 (5)
- Math (Compulsory): 5.5 (5)
- M2: 5.5 (5)
- Physics: 4 (4)
- Biology: 4 (4)
- Chemistry: 3 (3)

**Without weighting** (Any Best 5): 7 + 5.5 + 5.5 + 5.5 + 4 = 27.5

**With weighting** (English × 1.5, M2 × 2, Physics × 1.5): 7×1.5 + 5.5 + 5.5×2 + 4×1.5 + 4 = 10.5 + 5.5 + 11 + 6 + 4 = 37.0

The weighted scoring rewards candidates who excel in the weighted subjects.

#### How to use weighted scoring

1. Identify the weighted subjects for each programme you apply to.
2. If you have a strong grade in a weighted subject, that programme is more favourable to you.
3. If you have a strong grade in a non-weighted subject, that programme is less favourable.
4. Compare your weighted scores across programmes to estimate your relative ranking.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-topic links

- **"Best 5" and "Best 6" Formulae**: the weighting is applied within the formula.
- **Subject-Specific Requirements**: the weighting applies to the scoring formula, not the prerequisites.

#### Exam technique

- **For "explain the weighted scoring" questions**: state the weighted subjects and the multiplier; calculate the weighted score.
- **For "compare two programmes" questions**: identify the weighted subjects; compare the weighted scores.

#### Common misconceptions

- "All programmes weight English × 1.5." No — some programmes weight other subjects or none at all.
- "The highest-weighted subject always gives the highest contribution." No — the highest-weighted subject gives the highest contribution per JUPAS point, but the overall score depends on the grades too.
