# Hadith — Ittqa' al-Shubuhat and Qimat al-Ata' — Qatar Secondary Certificate Islamic Studies Notes

The MoEHE Grade 12 Islamic Studies Hadith (الحديث الشريف) domain opens in Term 1 with the hadith "Ittqa' al-Shubuhat" (avoiding doubts, اتقاء الشبهات) — the famous narration from al-Nu'man ibn Bashir on halal, haram, and shubha (doubtful). Term 2 continues with "Qimat al-Ata'" (value of giving, قيمة العطاء) — the hadith literature on charity, generosity, and social giving.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Ittqa' al-Shubuhat:** the hadith in Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim, narrated by al-Nu'man ibn Bashir: "الحلال بيّن والحرام بيّن وبينهما أمور مشتبهات لا يعلمهن كثير من الناس، فمن اتقى الشبهات استبرأ لدينه وعرضه، ومن وقع في الشبهات وقع في الحرام" — the lawful is clear, the unlawful is clear, and between them are doubtful matters unknown to most people. Whoever avoids doubts protects his religion and honour.
- **Qimat al-Ata':** the hadith literature on charity — including "ما نقصت صدقة من مال" (charity does not decrease wealth), the hadith of the seven whom Allah will shade, and the Prophet's teachings on sadaqah jariyah (ongoing charity).
- **Hadith sciences (ulum al-hadith):** the methodology of evaluating narrations — isnad (chain of transmission), matn (text), sahih, hasan, da'if, mawdu'.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing shubha (doubtful) with haram (unlawful) — shubha is the grey area, not the forbidden.
- Quoting a hadith without naming the source (Sahih al-Bukhari, Sahih Muslim, etc.).

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Ittqa' al-Shubuhat — full text and explanation

The full hadith (Sahih al-Bukhari 52, Sahih Muslim 1599) reads: "الحلال بيّن والحرام بيّن، وبينهما أمور مشتبهات لا يعلمهن كثير من الناس، فمن اتقى الشبهات استبرأ لدينه وعرضه، ومن وقع في الشبهات وقع في الحرام، كالراعي يرعى حول الحمى يوشك أن يرتع فيه، وإن لكل ملك حمى، وإن حمى الله محارمه، وإن في الجنة لمجرة، وإن في النار لمهرة"

The lawful is clear, the unlawful is clear. Between them are doubtful matters that most people do not know. Whoever avoids doubts has protected his religion and his honour. Whoever falls into doubts falls into the unlawful. Like a shepherd who grazes near a private pasture — he will soon graze in it. Every king has a protected area; Allah's protected area is what He has forbidden. In Paradise there is a tree, and in Hell there is a tree.

Classical scholars identified three benefits: (1) it is the principle of legal caution (ihtiyat); (2) it teaches the path of piety (taqwa) beyond the strict letter of the law; (3) it is the foundation of the discipline of halal-sourcing in trade and consumption.

#### Application in modern context

A Muslim trader who finds a transaction whose permissibility is unclear (mixed-interest, ambiguous ownership, contractual grey area) is called to abstain even if technically permissible. The hadith establishes "wara'" (scrupulous avoidance) as a virtue.

#### Qimat al-Ata' — hadith literature on charity

Key narrations:

- Sahih Muslim 2588: "ما من يوم يصبح العباد فيه إلا ملكان ينزلان، فيقول أحدهما: اللهم أعط منفقا خلفا، ويقول الآخر: اللهم أعط ممسكا تلفا" — Every morning two angels descend. One says: O Allah, compensate the giver. The other says: O Allah, destroy the withholder.
- Sahih Muslim 1004: "سبق درهم مئة ألف درهم" — A dirham of charity in the path of Allah can outweigh 100,000 dirhams elsewhere (the context is the sacrifice of companions who stayed behind with their families).
- Sahih al-Bukhari 1414, Sahih Muslim 1016: The seven whom Allah will shade on the Day of Resurrection include "رجل تصدق بصدقة فأخفاها حتى لا تعلم شماله ما تنفق يمينه" — a man who gave charity so secretly that his left hand did not know what his right hand gave.

#### Hadith evaluation methodology

Classical Sunni methodology: (1) examine the isnad (chain of transmission) for continuity, integrity of narrators, and accuracy; (2) examine the matn (text) for consistency with the Quran, other hadith, and established reason; (3) classify as sahih (sound), hasan (good), da'if (weak), or mawdu' (fabricated); (4) apply the hadith to legal rulings (ahkam) or spiritual teachings (ta'lim).

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### Why hadith study matters at the MoEHE terminal

The Hadith domain is one of the five pillars of the MoEHE Islamic Studies curriculum alongside Quran, Aqidah, Fiqh, and Seerah. The Grade 12 Hadith modules (Term 1 Ittqa' al-Shubuhat, Term 2 Qimat al-Ata') are the most direct entry to the ethical teachings of the Sunnah.

#### Exam technique

- For Ittqa' al-Shubuhat questions, distinguish the three categories (halal, haram, shubha) clearly.
- For Qimat al-Ata' questions, link each hadith to the wider ethical principle — charity, secret-giving, generosity as worship.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "All charity is financial." Charity (sadaqah) in the hadith literature includes a smile, a kind word, removing harm from the road, and giving advice — broader than financial giving.
- "Wara' means avoiding everything." No — wara' means avoiding the doubtful; the halal remains halal and should be enjoyed gratefully.
