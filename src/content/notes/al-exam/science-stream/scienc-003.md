# Plant Kingdom: Taxonomy and Classification

> Sri Lanka A/L Biology examiners love this topic because it rewards students who can name a system AND justify it. Walk in with Whittaker 1969, ICN rules, and a clean alternation-of-generations diagram, and half the paper's plant section is already settled.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Plant taxonomy sorts every green organism into a strict ladder: **Kingdom → Division → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species**. In botany the rank is written as **Division**, not Phylum. Sri Lanka's A/L Biology syllabus tests three classification systems — **Artificial (Linnaeus, 1735)**, **Natural (Bentham & Hooker, 1862–1883)**, and **Phylogenetic (APG IV, 2016)** — and the **Five-Kingdom system (Whittaker, 1969)** that places plants in **Kingdom Plantae**, split into Algae, Bryophyta, Pteridophyta, Gymnospermae, and Angiospermae.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook (Memory Hook — "KDC OF GAS"):** K**ingdom → D**ivision → C**lass → O**rder → F**amily → **G**enus → **A** + **S**pecies. Run through "**K**ing **D**avid's **C**ourt **O**rdered **F**air **G**enerals **A**nd **S**oldiers" and you will never invert the hierarchy again.

| Rank | Ends in | Example for *Oryza sativa* (rice) |
|---|---|---|
| Kingdom | — | Plantae |
| Division | — | Angiospermae (Magnoliophyta) |
| Class | — | Monocotyledonae |
| Order | — | Poales |
| Family | — | Poaceae |
| Genus | — | *Oryza* |
| Species | — | *sativa* |

**Binomial nomenclature** (Linnaeus): Genus (capitalised) + specific epithet (lowercase), both italicised or underlined, both Latinised.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

> Working understanding for the A/L Biology paper, with examples you can adapt in any structured answer.

#### Three Systems of Classification

Sri Lanka's Department of Examinations syllabus asks you to **state, contrast, and exemplify** the three systems. Memorise the *basis* of each — that single line is the highest-yield two marks on the paper.

| System | Author & Date | Basis | Strength | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Artificial | Linnaeus, 1735 | One or two morphological characters (mainly stamens) | Quick identification key | Ignores natural affinity |
| Natural | Bentham & Hooker, 1862–1883 | Overall morphological similarity | Reflects visible relationships | Still pre-evolutionary |
| Phylogenetic | APG IV, 2016 | Evolutionary + molecular (rbcL, matK, ITS) data | Reflects true ancestry | Continually revised |

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Linnaeus's system is **not "wrong"** — it is a valid *identification* tool. Write that it "does not reflect evolutionary relationships" rather than calling it incorrect. Examiners mark this distinction strictly.

#### Cryptogams vs Phanerogams

Plants below the seed habit reproduce by spores; seed-bearing plants reproduce by ovules and pollen. The split goes deeper, and examiners expect you to write at least one example per group.

| Group | Seed/Vascular Status | Examples | Key Feature |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thallophyta (algae) | No embryo, no vascular tissue | *Spirogyra*, *Sargassum* | Thallus body, aquatic |
| Bryophyta | Embryo present, no vascular tissue | *Funaria*, *Marchantia* | Gametophyte dominant |
| Pteridophyta | Vascular cryptogam | *Nephrolepis*, *Selaginella* | First true vascular plants |
| Gymnospermae | Naked seeds | *Cycas*, *Pinus* | No fruit, cone-bearing |
| Angiospermae — Monocots | Enclosed seed, 1 cotyledon | *Oryza sativa*, *Cocos nucifera* | Parallel venation, fibrous root |
| Angiospermae — Dicots | Enclosed seed, 2 cotyledons | *Mangifera indica*, *Phaseolus vulgaris* | Reticulate venation, taproot |

> 📌 **Formula Check (Nomenclature):** A valid binomial = *Genus species*, both Latinised, Genus capitalised. ICN rules: priority of publication, typification (holotype), no tautonyms (e.g. *Mangifera mangifera* is forbidden), authority cited as *Mangifera indica* L.

#### Worked Problem — Spot the Correct Binomial

A structured question gives four written forms of a plant name and asks which is **ICN-compliant**. The student's job is to scan four formatting and grammar rules in under a minute.

#### Resolving it:

1. Look first at the Genus — it must begin with a capital letter and be either italicised (print) or underlined (handwritten).
2. Check the specific epithet — entirely lowercase, never capitalised even if derived from a place.
3. Confirm both words are Latinised — no English vernacular words slip in.
4. Reject tautonyms and ensure the authority (if cited) is not in italics.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Writing *Mangifera Indica* with both words capitalised costs the format mark. So does leaving the name in plain Roman type with no italics or underline — paper-setters test this every year.

#### Alternation of Generations

Every plant life cycle toggles between a **sporophyte (2n)** and a **gametophyte (n)**. The placement of meiosis defines the cycle type, and examiners draw these as labelled diagrams worth 4–5 marks.

| Life-Cycle Type | Meiosis Location | Dominant Phase | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Haplontic | Zygote | Gametophyte (n) | *Spirogyra* (alga) |
| Diplontic | Gamete mother cell | Sporophyte (2n) | *Cycas*, angiosperms |
| Haplodiplontic | Spore mother cell | Both (intermediate) | *Funaria*, *Nephrolepis* |

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

> Mastery material: edge cases, ambiguities the textbook dodges, and the kind of "compare and contrast" question that separates A from B grade.

#### Edge Cases Worth One Mark Each

1. **Division vs Phylum:** Both are equivalent in rank. The ICN formally uses "Division" for plants; zoology uses "Phylum". Examiners accept either but penalise the inconsistency if you swap mid-answer.
2. **Myxomycetes (slime moulds):** Once taught under Thallophyta; Whittaker moved them to Kingdom Protista. If the question references "modern classification", place them in Protista — older texts still list them under Fungi or Plantae.
3. **Progametangial gymnosperms vs angiosperms:** *Cycas* has a **motile spermatozoid**, an oddity inside seed plants — flagellated male gametes are otherwise a bryophyte/pteridophyte trait. Examiners occasionally ask why *Cycas* is classed with gymnosperms despite this.
4. **Heterospory in *Selaginella*:** Micro- and megaspores mark an evolutionary step toward the seed habit. Linking heterospory to the origin of seed plants scores bonus marks.
5. **APG IV vs Bentham & Hooker for dicot families:** Polypetalae, Gamopetalae, and Monochlamydeae (Bentham & Hooker) have largely dissolved under molecular data — the former "Cruciferae" is now **Brassicaceae**, "Compositae" is **Asteraceae**, and "Palmae" is **Arecaceae**. If the question says "modern" classification, use APG.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Bilingual synoptic questions often quote a traditional family name (Cruciferae, Compositae, Labiatae) and ask students to map it to the APG family. Treat those eight conserved family names as mandatory vocabulary.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. **Compare the gametophyte of *Marchantia* with the gametophyte of *Funaria* under the sporophyte in each case.** Your answer should explicitly contrast the thalloid vs leafy gametophyte, rhizoid function, and dependence on the sporophyte for nutrition. A labelled diagram with 2n/n markings earns full marks.
2. **Discuss why Whittaker's 1969 Five-Kingdom system replaced the older Two-Kingdom classification of Linnaeus.** Focus on cell organisation (prokaryote vs eukaryote), mode of nutrition (autotroph vs heterotroph vs absorptive), and thallus structure. Name the criteria Whittaker used — they appear verbatim in marking schemes.
