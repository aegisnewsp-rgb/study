# Major World Events

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Major World Events are the political, military, economic, and humanitarian turning points that reshaped borders, treaties, and international law. LAT examiners use them to test whether you can connect a date to the organisation or treaty it produced.

- **WWI (1914–1918)** ended with the **Treaty of Versailles (1919)** and created the failed **League of Nations**.
- **WWII (1939–1945)** produced the **United Nations (1945)**, the **Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948)**, and the **Geneva Conventions (1949)**.
- The **Cold War** (USA vs USSR) ended through the **Belavezha Accords (1991)**, leaving the **NPT (1968)** as its key nuclear arms treaty.
- **Brexit (2020)** and the **Paris Agreement (2015)** are the most repeated recent-event MCQs.

| Organisation / Event | Year | Core legal or political outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Treaty of Versailles | 1919 | Redrew European borders, war reparations, League of Nations Charter |
| United Nations | 1945 | Replaced League; Charter-based international legal order |
| UDHR | 1948 | Foundation of modern human rights law (non-binding declaration) |
| Geneva Conventions | 1949 | Codified protection of civilians and POWs in armed conflict |
| Paris Agreement | 2015 | Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs) on climate change |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Foundational events and their legal legacy

Two World Wars dominate the syllabus. The Treaty of Versailles (signed 28 June 1919) imposed reparations on Germany and redrew Central European borders, but its harsh terms helped enable WWII. The League of Nations, born from Versailles, failed because the United States never joined and the organisation lacked enforcement power.

WWII produced a stronger architecture. The United Nations Charter (signed 26 June 1945, entered into force 24 October 1945) replaced the League and gave the Security Council binding enforcement power. The Universal Declaration of Human Rights (adopted by the UN General Assembly on 10 December 1948) is non-binding but became the moral source for the binding ICCPR and ICESCR covenants adopted in 1966.

#### Cold War to multipolarity

The Cold War (≈1947–1991) divided the world into US-led NATO (founded 4 April 1949) and USSR-led Warsaw Pact blocs. Its end came through the **Belavezha Accords (8 December 1991)** and the **Alma-Ata Protocol (21 December 1991)**, dissolving the USSR on 26 December 1991. Key arms-control outputs were the **Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty (1968)** and START treaties.

#### Recent and contemporary events

- **Brexit**: the UK voted to leave the EU on 23 June 2016; formal withdrawal completed 31 January 2020 under the **Withdrawal Agreement**, with a transition phase ending 31 December 2020.
- **Paris Agreement (2015)**: a treaty framework under the **UNFCCC (1992)** with NDCs, not binding emission caps.
- **9/11 (2001)**: triggered UN Security Council **Resolution 1373** and reshaped counter-terrorism law.

#### Common traps

- The UDHR is a declaration, not a treaty; only the 1966 covenants create binding obligations.
- NATO (military, 1949) is routinely confused with the EU (economic/political, 1993 under the Maastricht Treaty).
- The ICJ (1945) settles **state** disputes; the ICC (2002) prosecutes **individuals** for war crimes.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Connections across topics

Major World Events tie directly to Pakistan and South Asian law. The **Partition of India (1947)** is a foundational case study in self-determination and minority rights, referenced in UDHR Article 15. The **Simla Agreement (1972)** and **UN Security Council resolutions on Kashmir** test your grasp of how Cold War geopolitics still shapes contemporary international disputes. LAT frequently asks which body has jurisdiction over inter-state matters — the answer is the **International Court of Justice (ICJ)**, not the ICC or the UN General Assembly.

#### Worked mini-example

*Question pattern*: "The Treaty of Versailles (1919) is most directly associated with the formation of which organisation?"
*Approach*: identify the event → match to organisation. Versailles → League of Nations; UN Charter 1945 → United Nations; Maastricht Treaty 1993 → European Union; Belavezha Accords 1991 → dissolution of USSR.

#### High-yield mistakes to avoid

1. Saying Brexit ended UK membership in **all** international treaties — only EU treaties ceased; WTO, NATO, and UN memberships are independent.
2. Treating the Paris Agreement as binding **targets** — it obliges parties to submit and pursue NDCs.
3. Dating the EU to 1957 (Treaty of Rome, EEC) rather than 1993 (Maastricht, EU).
4. Confusing the UDHR (1948) with the binding **International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (1966)**.

#### Practice prompts

1. Which 1948 UN document is the foundation of modern human rights law, and why is it not directly enforceable in domestic courts?
2. Explain how the Belavezha Accords (1991) formally ended the Cold War and what treaty architecture it left behind.
