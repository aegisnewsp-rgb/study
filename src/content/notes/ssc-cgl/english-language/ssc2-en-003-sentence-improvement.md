# Sentence Improvement

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Sentence Improvement** in SSC CGL Tier-2 tests one specific editing skill: a sentence contains an underlined phrase that is grammatically wrong, awkward, or idiomatically off, and you pick the option (out of four) that makes it correct while **preserving the original meaning**.

- **Question format:** one underlined segment + four replacement choices (A/B/C/D).
- **Weightage:** ~2–4 questions under the Vocabulary/Grammar block of Tier-2 English (200 marks), 0.25 negative marking per wrong answer.
- **High-frequency rule categories to master:**
  1. Tense consistency across main + subordinate clause
  2. Subject–verb agreement across long subjects
  3. Voice (Active preferred over Passive)
  4. Correct comparative/superlative form
  5. Parallel structure in lists and correlative pairs
  6. Idioms, phrasal verbs, and gerund/infinition usage
  7. Article placement and preposition choice

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **T-A-V-I-P-C-M-S** — the eight traps examiners reuse: **T**ense, **A**greement, **V**oice, **I**diom, **P**reposition, **C**omparison, **M**odal, **S**tyle/redundancy. Read the sentence, name the trap, then match the option.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### How the Question Works

You get a sentence with **one** underlined segment. Three of the four options either repeat the same error, introduce a new error, or alter the original meaning. Only one option corrects the error **without** changing the intended sense. The skill is less about "finding any wrong part" and more about **comparing four rewrites against the author's meaning**.

#### Step-by-Step Solving Method

1. **Read the whole sentence first**, ignoring the underline. Understand the meaning, tense frame, and subject of the verb.
2. **Locate the likely error category** using T-A-V-I-P-C-M-S.
3. **Eliminate options that change meaning** (e.g., swapping "refused" for "denied" — opposite senses).
4. **Eliminate options that look shorter/modern** but break grammar (e.g., dropping "has" before a past participle).
5. **Pick the option that fixes the rule and preserves meaning.** If two options seem correct, the one preserving tense sequence and idiom wins.

#### Commonly Confused Categories

| Trap | Wrong Pattern | Correct Pattern | Memory Cue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tense sequence | He said that he **is** happy. | He said that he **was** happy. | Reported speech shifts back one tense |
| Subject–verb agreement | The list of items **are** ready. | The list of items **is** ready. | Head noun (singular) governs verb |
| Voice preference | The match **was won by** India. | India **won** the match. | Active unless agent is unknown |
| Comparative form | She is **more better** than him. | She is **better** than him. | "better/worse" are already comparative |
| Parallelism | He likes **reading, to swim, and jogging**. | He likes **reading, swimming, and jogging**. | Same -ing form across list |
| Article misuse | He is **the** best player. | He is **the** best player. *(correct)* vs. He wants **the** money. *(also correct)* | Drop "the" before generic plurals |
| Gerund vs infinitive | He is **used to work** hard. | He is **used to working** hard. | "used to" = habitual (gerund); "used to" past = infinitive |

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** *"The teacher asked the student why he has not submitted his assignment on time."* Which replacement for **"has not submitted"** is correct?

- (A) had not submitted
- (B) did not submit
- (C) was not submitting
- (D) no improvement

#### Solution:
Apply the **tense-sequence rule**: the main verb "asked" is simple past, so the reported clause must also sit in a past-tense frame.

- (A) **had not submitted** — past perfect, correctly back-shifted from present perfect; sits inside a past reporting verb. ✔
- (B) "did not submit" — simple past, but acceptable only if the event and reporting are simultaneous in past narration; weaker than (A) here.
- (C) "was not submitting" — past continuous, wrong aspect for a one-time completed action.
- (D) "no improvement" — wrong, because "has not submitted" breaks tense sequence.

#### Answer: (A) had not submitted.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students who pick (B) confuse *back-shift optionality* with *back-shift obligation*. With "asked why …," the past perfect (A) is the safer, examiner-preferred rewrite; (B) is technically defensible in casual speech but loses marks in SSC's rule-based key.

> 📌 **Formula Check:** Reported speech rule — if the main verb is past tense, the subordinate verb shifts one step back: present → past, present perfect → past perfect, will → would.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases That Decide Top Ranks

- **"Make" vs "Let":** *He made me do it* (force) ≠ *He let me do it* (allow). SSC often swaps these to test causative meaning.
- **"Lend" vs "Borrow":** *Lend* takes the recipient as object (*He lent me a book*); *borrow* takes the source (*I borrowed a book from him*). Mixing them is a classic.
- **"Sick of" vs "Sick with":** *Sick of* = fed up with (idea); *sick with* = physically ill (disease). Same spelling, different prepositions.
- **"Since" vs "For":** *Since* anchors a point in time (since 2020); *for* measures duration (for five years). Mixing them breaks time expressions.
- **"Between" vs "Among":** *Between* for two parties, *among* for more than two. SSC has tested this with three-party lists.
- **Double negative avoidance:** "He did **not** do **nothing**" → "He did **not** do **anything**" or "He did **nothing**."

#### Advanced Trap Map

| # | Trap | Wrong Example | Correction | Why Examiners Reuse It |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Modal sequence | He **should** have **went**. | He **should** have **gone**. | Past participle after "have" |
| 2 | Adverb placement | He **never has** gone there. | He **has never** gone there. | Mid-position rule with have/has |
| 3 | Intensifier misuse | She is **very** delighted. | She is **much** delighted. | "very" before adjectives, not past participles |
| 4 | "any other" comparison | He is **best** than Rohit. | He is **better than any other** player. | Member vs rest of class |
| 5 | Phrasal verb split | He **picked up** it. | He **picked it up**. | Pronoun goes between, noun goes after |
| 6 | Preposition idiom | He is **good in** maths. | He is **good at** maths. | Fixed collocation |

#### Two Advanced Practice Prompts

1. *"Neither the principal nor the teachers **was** present at the meeting."* — Identify the rule, list two plausible distractors, and justify the correct rewrite.
2. *"She is one of those writers who **has** won the award thrice."* — Explain why the singular verb in the underlined part is **wrong** despite "one of" feeling singular, and write the correct form.

> 💡 **High-Yield Revision Hook:** When the subject phrase starts with **"one of those who/what"**, the relative clause's verb takes the **plural** of the antecedent noun (*writers who have won*), not the singular of "one."

#### Exam Strategy Notes

- **Time-per-question target:** 30–45 seconds; if two options both seem correct, choose the one matching **tense + idiom + meaning preservation** simultaneously.
- **Negative marking discipline:** at 0.25 per wrong answer, skip only if all four options look identical in plausibility — rare in SSC.
- **Paper position:** Sentence Improvement clusters with Spotting Errors and Sentence Correction in Tier-2 Paper-II English; the rule categories overlap, so revising this note also reinforces the other two formats.
- **Source-grounded rule check:** SSC's official Tier-2 syllabus lists "Grammar" and "Vocabulary" as testable; the T-A-V-I-P-C-M-S framework covers ~90% of the items in the 2022–2025 paper cycles as published on ssc.gov.in.
