# Current Affairs and General Knowledge

### 🟢 Lite

This topic is built on one question that recurs in General Awareness: *which body is this, and where does it sit?* Learn headquarters cities and the veto/expansion facts, because that is where most options are made to differ.

#### Headquarters tell you the function

| City | Body | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **New York** | United Nations | General Assembly, Security Council |
| **Geneva** | WHO, WTO | Global health; trade rules |
| **Washington, D.C.** | IMF, World Bank | Balance-of-payments lending; development finance |
| **The Hague** | ICJ | Settles disputes between states |
| **Brussels** | NATO | Collective defence |

**Memory hook — "New York talks, Geneva heals and trades, Washington lends, The Hague judges, Brussels defends."**

#### The five numbers worth carrying

- **UN**: 193 member states; Security Council has **15** members — **5 permanent with veto** (US, UK, France, Russia, China) plus 10 rotating for two-year terms.
- **WTO**: **166 members.** The Doha Round, launched in 2001, is still unfinished.
- **NATO**: **32 members** after Finland (2023) and Sweden (2024).
- **G20**: 19 countries plus the EU, roughly 85% of world GDP.
- **ASEAN**: 10 members. India is a **Dialogue Partner**, not a member.

### 🟡 Standard

#### United Nations

Founded in 1945 after the Second World War, with 193 member states and its headquarters in New York.

- **General Assembly** — every member, one vote each; the "parliament of nations".
- **Security Council** — 15 members. The P5 hold veto power, so any one of them can block a substantive resolution; the other 10 rotate for two-year terms.
- **Secretariat** — headed by the Secretary-General, António Guterres since 2017.
- **International Court of Justice** — sits at The Hague and settles legal disputes between states.

#### WHO

Headquartered in Geneva, founded in 1948. It coordinates pandemic response, sets standards, and provides technical assistance. Current priorities are universal health coverage, health emergencies, and healthier populations. Its COVID-19 response — origin investigation and vaccine distribution — was debated worldwide.

#### WTO

Headquartered in Geneva with 166 members. It governs trade rules, negotiates agreements, and hears disputes. The **Doha Round**, begun in 2001, remains incomplete; current work concentrates on e-commerce, investment, and the trade-related aspects of intellectual property (TRIPS).

#### IMF and World Bank

Both sit in Washington, D.C. The **IMF** lends to members facing balance-of-payments trouble, and its loans carry conditions. Its **World Economic Outlook** appears twice a year, and its quota system sets voting power — the United States holds roughly 17%.

The **World Bank** lends and grants to developing countries for infrastructure, education, and health, through the **IDA** (concessional loans to the poorest countries) and the **IFC** (private-sector investment). The **New Development Bank**, created by BRICS, was intended as an alternative lender.

#### NATO

A defence alliance of **32** North American and European members, founded in 1949 and headquartered in Brussels.

**Article 5** is the provision to know: an attack on one member is treated as an attack on all. Since Russia's invasion of Ukraine in 2022 the alliance has expanded eastward — Finland joined in 2023, Sweden in 2024.

#### Summits and blocs

**G20** — 19 countries plus the EU, about 85% of global GDP and 75% of international trade. India hosted the 2023 summit at New Delhi as the first G20 presidency held by India, under the theme "One Earth, One Family, One Future", producing the Delhi Declaration, the Global Biofuels Alliance, and the Initiative for Critical and Emerging Technologies (ICET).

**BRICS** — formed as BRIC in 2006, expanded to include South Africa in 2010, and now a group of **ten**: Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa, Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, the UAE and Indonesia. The new members were formally admitted from 2024, with Indonesia joining as the tenth. Its stated aim is a more multipolar order and reduced dependence on the dollar; the **New Development Bank** is its lending arm.

**ASEAN** — ten members: Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, Brunei, Vietnam, Laos, Myanmar and Cambodia. It promotes economic integration and regional stability. India holds the status of **ASEAN Dialogue Partner** — a standing invitation, not membership. India opted out of **RCEP** in November 2019 and has not ratified it.

### 🔴 Extended

#### Worked item

**Question.** How many permanent members does the UN Security Council have, and what distinguishes them from the other ten?

- (A) Ten permanent, chosen by the General Assembly
- (B) Five permanent, each able to veto a substantive resolution
- (C) Fifteen permanent, each with a two-year term
- (D) Five permanent, but only on economic matters

**Resolution.**
1. The Council has **15 seats**.
2. **Five** are permanent — China, France, Russia, the United Kingdom and the United States.
3. A permanent member may **veto** a substantive resolution, which is what makes those five different in kind, not merely in term.
4. The other **ten** are elected by the General Assembly for **two-year** terms and are not permanent, ruling out (A) and (C). The veto is not limited to economic questions, ruling out (D).

**Answer: B**

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** the commonest error is answering "15 permanent". Fifteen is the size of the Council; five is the number of permanent members. A second trap is describing the rotating ten as elected by the Security Council — they are elected by the **General Assembly**.

#### Edges that change the answer

- **BRICS membership is a moving target.** Older material says five members; the group now stands at ten. Saudi Arabia is frequently listed as a member in press coverage, but it has not been formally confirmed — do not count it.
- **India is not an ASEAN member and never ratified RCEP.** "Dialogue Partner" and "opted out in 2019" are the precise phrases.
- **The P5 veto does not apply to procedural matters.** On procedure, nine votes carry.
- **The economics prize is not a Nobel in the original sense** — it is the Sveriges Riksbank Prize in Economic Sciences in Memory of Alfred Nobel, which is why 2024 had none.
- **G20 presidency rotates annually and the summit is a leaders' summit.** Membership of the format is countries plus the EU, not the AU, despite AU joining as a permanent member of the format.

#### CMAT strategy

Volatile numbers are exactly where an exam goes stale, so anchor on the things that change slowly — headquarters cities, the veto, Article 5, Article 21A-style structural facts — and treat membership counts as "approximately", since they move. Where a question hinges on a recent summit, read the year carefully: the distinction between the 2023 New Delhi G20 and the 2023 Johannesburg BRICS meeting is a reliable discriminator.
