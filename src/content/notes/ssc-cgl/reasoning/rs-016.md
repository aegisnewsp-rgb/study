# Cube

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

In SSC CGL non-verbal reasoning, a **cube** is a regular hexahedron: 6 square faces, 12 equal edges, 8 vertices. The standard question paints some faces of a large cube and cuts it into **n³ unit cubes** of side 1. Candidates must count how many small cubes show **0, 1, 2, or 3 painted faces**.

- **3 painted faces** (corners): always **8**, independent of *n* (only when *n* ≥ 2).
- **2 painted faces** (edge cubes, not corners): **12 × (n − 2)**.
- **1 painted face** (face-centre cubes): **6 × (n − 2)²**.
- **0 painted faces** (fully interior): **(n − 2)³**.
- **Diagonals:** face diagonal = *a*√2, body diagonal = *a*√3 (where *a* = side length).
- **Dice rule:** opposite faces of a standard die always sum to **7**.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Vertex | Corner point where 3 edges meet |
| Face diagonal | Across one square face (*a*√2) |
| Body diagonal | Connects two opposite vertices through interior (*a*√3) |
| Unit cube | Smallest 1×1×1 cube after cutting |
| Corner cube | Small cube with 3 painted faces |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"8 Corners Cry, 12 Edges Weep, 6 Faces Peek, Inside Sleep."** Translates to **8** cubes with 3 faces painted, **12(n−2)** with 2 faces, **6(n−2)²** with 1 face, **(n−2)³** with 0 faces. The numbers 8, 12, 6 also equal the cube's own corners, edges, faces — memorise once, use forever.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Painting-Cut Formulas (All 6 Faces Painted)

When every face of an *n* × *n* × *n* cube is painted and sliced into 1×1×1 unit cubes, the count splits cleanly into four categories. The total must always satisfy the **verification identity**:

> n³ = 8 + 12(n − 2) + 6(n − 2)² + (n − 2)³

This identity is the fastest way to check your arithmetic on exam day. For *n* = 4: 64 = 8 + 24 + 24 + 8 ✓.

#### Variation Patterns by Painting Scheme

| Painting scheme | 3-faced | 2-faced | 1-faced | 0-faced |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All 6 faces | 8 | 12(n−2) | 6(n−2)² | (n−2)³ |
| 2 opposite faces (top + bottom) | 0 | 4(n−2) | 2(n−2)² | (n−2)³ + 4(n−2) |
| 2 adjacent faces | 1 | 4(n−1) | 2(n−2)² + (n−2) | (n−2)³ + (n−2) |
| 3 faces meeting at a corner | 1 | 3(n−2) | 3(n−2)² | (n−2)³ + (n−2) |
| 4 faces (one unpainted) | 4 | 8(n−2) + 4(n−2) | 4(n−2)² + (n−2)² | (n−2)³ + 2(n−2) |

> 📌 **Formula Check:** Every variable *n* is dimensionless (number of unit cubes per edge). The formulas assume *n* ≥ 2; for *n* = 2, only corners exist and 12(n−2) = 6(n−2)² = (n−2)³ = 0.

#### Steps to Solve Any Paint-and-Cut Question

1. Extract *n* from the stem (often given directly, or derived from total cubes via n = ∛N).
2. Identify the painting scheme (all 6, opposite pair, adjacent pair, 3 at a corner, 4 faces).
3. Pick the matching row from the matrix above.
4. Compute each term and verify the sum equals *n*³.
5. Answer only what is asked — many questions trap you with "exactly 2" vs "at least 2".

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** A cube of side 5 cm is painted red on all six faces and then cut into 1 cm³ unit cubes. How many small cubes have **exactly two** faces painted?

(A) 8 &nbsp;&nbsp; (B) 24 &nbsp;&nbsp; (C) 36 &nbsp;&nbsp; (D) 54

**Solution:** Here *n* = 5 (since 5 cm ÷ 1 cm = 5 unit cubes per edge). All 6 faces are painted, so apply the standard formula:

> 2-faced cubes = 12 × (n − 2) = 12 × (5 − 2) = 12 × 3 = **36**

#### Answer: (C) 36

Quick verification: 3-faced = 8, 2-faced = 36, 1-faced = 6 × 9 = 54, 0-faced = 27. Sum = 8 + 36 + 54 + 27 = 125 = 5³ ✓.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students often answer **54** (the 1-faced count) because they misread "exactly two" as "at least two" or pick the largest count. Re-read the qualifier — *exactly* 2 means edge cubes excluding corners, which is **12(n−2)**, not 6(n−2)².

#### Dice and Net Shortcuts

- **Standard die:** opposite faces sum to 7 (1↔6, 2↔5, 3↔4).
- **Net rule:** in an unfolded cube net, two faces sharing an edge are **adjacent**, not opposite. Opposite faces are separated by exactly one square in a straight row, or sit in specific "Z" and "L" configurations.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Boundary Cases and Edge Conditions

| Condition | What changes | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| *n* = 1 | Whole cube is the unit cube | No cutting possible; all faces painted means it has 6 painted faces |
| *n* = 2 | Only corner cubes exist | (n−2) = 0 kills edge, face-centre, and inner counts; only 8 corners remain |
| Only 2 opposite faces painted | 2-faced count drops to 4(n−2) | Only the 4 vertical edges carry two painted sides; top/bottom rims are 1-faced |
| Question gives N = total cubes | n = ∛N, not √N | 64 unit cubes ⇒ n = 4, not 8 — the most common extraction error |

#### Connection to Dice Problems and Rotational Views

Cube problems in SSC CGL often chain **paint-cut counting** with **rotated-view identification**. The technique: from any corner view, exactly 3 faces are visible, so cubes with at least one painted face on those three adjoining surfaces dominate the count. For a fully painted cube viewed from a corner, the visible painted small cubes equal **3 × (n−2)² + 3 × (n−2) + 1**, which simplifies to **3n² − 9n + 7** for *n* ≥ 2.

When a question asks for the **colour of the face opposite** a given symbol in a rotated cube, apply the dice rule first (sum = 7) — if the symbols are dots 1–6. For symbolic faces (A, B, C, D, E, F), use the net folding rule: pick any face as the base, fold the four side faces around it, and the remaining face lands opposite.

#### Common Advanced Traps

1. **The "(n−2)³ trap":** Students report (n−2)³ as "cubes with at least one painted face". It actually gives fully interior cubes with **zero** painted faces.
2. **The 8-corner trap:** Writing 3-faced count as 8*n* or 8(n−2). The count is a constant 8 once *n* ≥ 2 — independent of size.
3. **The diagonal swap:** Mixing body diagonal (*a*√3) with face diagonal (*a*√2). Body diagonal cuts through the cube's interior and is always longer.
4. **Net adjacency illusion:** In an unfolded net, two faces sharing a row of one square appear "far apart" but are actually adjacent when folded. Trust geometry, not visual distance.
5. **Partial painting math error:** For 4 faces painted (one unpainted), students forget to subtract the unpainted face's contribution from both the 1-faced and 0-faced terms.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. A 4×4×4 cube has exactly **two opposite faces** painted and is then cut into unit cubes. How many cubes have **no paint**? (Use the second row of the matrix.)
2. An unfolded cube net shows faces labelled A, B, C, D, E, F where A and C share an edge, B and D are separated by one square in a row, and F sits beside A. Which face is opposite E? Apply the net-folding rule stepwise.

> 💡 **High-Yield Revision Hook:** Before submitting any cube answer, run the **n³ check**: 8 + 12(n−2) + 6(n−2)² + (n−2)³ must equal the total unit cubes. If it doesn't, your formula row or your extraction of *n* is wrong — never trust the answer without this verification.
