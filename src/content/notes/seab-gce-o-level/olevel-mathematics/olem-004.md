# Ratio, Rate, Proportion and Percentages — O-Level Mathematics (4052) Notes

Ratio, Rate, Proportion and Percentages is one of the three strands of the O-Level Mathematics 4052 syllabus (alongside Algebra and Geometry / Measurement / Statistics). It is the heaviest practical-application load on Paper 2 because most of the long structured questions in this strand are built around money, time, distance and other real-world contexts. A Sec 4 student who is fluent in ratio and proportion will find that the applied paper questions become straightforward; a student who is not will lose marks on the highest-load section of the paper.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### The seven facts every Sec 4 student needs

1. **Ratio** compares two or more quantities of the same kind; a ratio of 3 : 5 means the first quantity is 3 parts and the second is 5 parts.
2. **Equivalent ratios**: multiply or divide each term of a ratio by the same non-zero number. 3 : 5 = 6 : 10 = 9 : 15.
3. **Rate** compares two quantities of different kinds; "speed" is a rate, "wage" is a rate.
4. **Direct proportion**: when one quantity increases, the other increases in the same ratio.
5. **Inverse proportion**: when one quantity increases, the other decreases in the inverse ratio.
6. **Percentage** is a fraction out of 100; "x%" means x/100 or 0.0x.
7. **Compound units**: speed is measured in km/h or m/s; density is measured in g/cm³.

#### Key notations

- Speed = Distance ÷ Time (s = d / t)
- Density = Mass ÷ Volume (ρ = m / V)
- Average speed = Total distance ÷ Total time
- Percentage change = (Change / Original) × 100%

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Ratio

A ratio compares two or more quantities of the same kind. The ratio 3 : 5 means that for every 3 units of the first quantity there are 5 units of the second. The two quantities must be in the same units before forming a ratio (e.g. both in metres, both in dollars).

To divide a quantity in a given ratio, add the parts of the ratio to get the total number of parts, then multiply the quantity by each part over the total. Divide $80 in the ratio 3 : 5: total parts = 8; first share = $80 × 3/8 = $30; second share = $80 × 5/8 = $50.

To find an unknown in a given ratio, set up a proportion. The ratio of boys to girls in a class is 5 : 7; if there are 35 boys, how many girls are there? 5/7 = 35/g, so g = 35 × 7/5 = 49.

To express a ratio in its simplest form, divide each term by the HCF. 18 : 24 → 18/6 : 24/6 → 3 : 4.

#### Rate

A rate is a comparison of two quantities of different kinds. Speed is a rate (distance per unit time); wage is a rate (dollars per hour); density is a rate (mass per unit volume).

To find speed, divide distance by time. A car travels 240 km in 3 hours; speed = 240/3 = 80 km/h.

To find average speed, divide total distance by total time. The car travels 100 km at 50 km/h and another 100 km at 100 km/h. Total distance = 200 km; total time = 100/50 + 100/100 = 2 + 1 = 3 h; average speed = 200/3 ≈ 66.7 km/h (NOT the average of 50 and 100).

To convert units of rate, use the unit-conversion factors. 1 km/h = 1000 m / 3600 s = 5/18 m/s. So 90 km/h = 90 × 5/18 = 25 m/s.

#### Direct and inverse proportion

**Direct proportion**: y = kx, where k is a positive constant. As x doubles, y doubles; as x is halved, y is halved.

Example: y is directly proportional to x; when x = 4, y = 12. Find y when x = 7. k = 12/4 = 3; y = 3 × 7 = 21.

**Inverse proportion**: y = k/x, where k is a positive constant. As x doubles, y halves; as x is halved, y doubles.

Example: y is inversely proportional to x; when x = 3, y = 10. Find y when x = 5. k = 3 × 10 = 30; y = 30/5 = 6.

Direct and inverse proportion can be combined. y is directly proportional to x² and inversely proportional to z. Then y = k x² / z. To find k, use a known pair of (x, y, z). To find an unknown y, substitute the new x and z.

#### Percentages

A percentage is a fraction with denominator 100. To convert a fraction to a percentage, multiply by 100. 3/5 = 60%. To convert a percentage to a fraction, divide by 100. 60% = 0.6 = 3/5.

**Percentage of a quantity**: 25% of 80 = 0.25 × 80 = 20.

**Percentage change**: percentage change = (change / original) × 100. A price goes from $80 to $100; change = $20; percentage change = 20/80 × 100 = 25%.

**Percentage increase**: new value = original × (1 + rate). A 10% increase on $50 = $50 × 1.10 = $55.

**Percentage decrease**: new value = original × (1 − rate). A 20% discount on $50 = $50 × 0.80 = $40.

**Reverse percentage**: an item is sold at $40 after a 20% discount. What was the original price? The discount removed 20%, so $40 is 80% of the original. Original = $40 / 0.80 = $50.

**Simple interest**: I = Prt, where P is the principal, r is the rate (per year as a decimal), and t is the time in years. $1000 at 5% per year for 3 years: I = 1000 × 0.05 × 3 = $150. Total = $1150.

**Compound interest**: A = P(1 + r)^n. $1000 at 5% per year compounded annually for 3 years: A = 1000 × 1.05³ = $1157.63. The compound interest is $157.63, more than the simple interest of $150.

#### Map scales and similar figures

A map scale of 1 : 25 000 means 1 cm on the map represents 25 000 cm (= 250 m) in real life. A distance of 5 cm on the map represents 5 × 25 000 = 125 000 cm = 1.25 km in real life.

Similar figures have the same shape but different sizes; corresponding lengths are in the same ratio. The ratio of areas is the square of the ratio of lengths; the ratio of volumes is the cube of the ratio of lengths.

If a rectangle 4 cm by 6 cm is enlarged by a scale factor of 3, the new rectangle is 12 cm by 18 cm; the area is enlarged by 3² = 9 times.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where Ratio, Rate and Proportion appears across the papers

- **O-Level Mathematics 4052 Paper 1**: short structured items on ratio, simple percentage, and direct / inverse proportion.
- **O-Level Mathematics 4052 Paper 2**: long structured items (5 to 10 marks) that combine ratio or rate with a money / motion / population / map context. Often the highest-mark structured items on the paper.
- **Across both papers**: writing "amount = rate × base" explicitly before substituting is the single biggest differentiator between a B-grade and an A-grade answer.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Average speed is the average of the two speeds." It is not. Average speed is total distance divided by total time; the average of two speeds is only equal to the average speed if the time spent at each speed is the same.
- "Divide the quantities by the sum of the ratio." To divide in a ratio, divide by the SUM of the parts to get the value of one part, then multiply each part by its share.
- "Compound interest is always more than simple interest." On a single compounding period (n = 1), they are equal. Beyond that, compound interest grows faster.
- "20% off and then 20% on returns to the original." A 20% decrease on $50 gives $40; a 20% increase on $40 gives $48, not $50.
- "Direct proportion passes through the origin." Yes — but inverse proportion does not; it is asymptotic to both axes.

#### Exam technique

- For ratio questions, lay out the parts in a table. The table is the method mark.
- For rate questions, write the rate equation before substituting. "Distance = speed × time" is the method mark.
- For percentage change questions, state the original value, the change and the formula. The formula line is the method mark.
- For compound interest questions, write out each year's amount. The year-by-year table is the method mark.
