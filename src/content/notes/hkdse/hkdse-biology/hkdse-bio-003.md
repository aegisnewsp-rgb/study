# III. Organisms and Environment — HKDSE Biology Notes

This topic covers Topic III of the Compulsory Part of HKDSE Biology. It addresses essential life processes in plants (photosynthesis, transport, transpiration, plant hormones); essential life processes in animals (nutrition, digestion, circulation, gas exchange); reproduction, growth and development (human reproductive systems, flowering plant reproduction); coordination and response (nervous system, sense organs, hormones in animals); homeostasis (temperature regulation, osmotic and glucose regulation); and ecosystems (energy flow, biogeochemical cycles, population dynamics).

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Plant life processes**: photosynthesis (light-dependent and light-independent reactions); transport (xylem carries water and minerals upward; phloem carries sugars); transpiration (evaporation of water from leaves, driven by water potential gradient); plant hormones (auxins, gibberellins, cytokinins, abscisic acid, ethylene).
- **Animal life processes**: nutrition (autotrophs vs. heterotrophs); digestion (mechanical and chemical, in alimentary canal); circulation (open vs. closed, heart structure); gas exchange (gills, lungs, tracheal systems).
- **Reproduction**: human male and female reproductive systems; menstrual cycle and hormonal control; flowering plant reproduction (pollination, fertilisation, seed and fruit formation).
- **Coordination and response**: nervous system (central and peripheral, sensory and motor neurons, synapses, reflexes); sense organs (eye, ear); hormones in animals (endocrine glands, target cells, feedback loops).
- **Homeostasis (negative feedback)**: thermoregulation (sweating, shivering, vasodilation/constriction); osmoregulation (kidney function, ADH); glucose regulation (insulin, glucagon).
- **Ecosystems**: energy flow (food chains, food webs, trophic levels, 10% rule); biogeochemical cycles (carbon, nitrogen, water); population dynamics (growth curves, carrying capacity, factors affecting population size).

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing xylem and phloem — xylem carries water and minerals upward (from roots); phloem carries sugars in both directions.
- Forgetting that gas exchange in plants occurs through stomata, mostly on the underside of leaves.
- Treating the menstrual cycle as a 28-day cycle — the cycle length varies (typically 21-35 days), and hormonal regulation is the key to understanding it.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Plant life processes

**Photosynthesis**: occurs in chloroplasts; 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ (light energy, chlorophyll). Light-dependent reactions (in thylakoids): photolysis of water, ATP and NADPH production. Light-independent reactions / Calvin cycle (in stroma): CO₂ fixation using ATP and NADPH.

**Transport in plants**:

- **Xylem**: dead, hollow tubes; transport water and minerals from roots to shoots; driven by transpiration pull, root pressure, and cohesion-tension.
- **Phloem**: living sieve tubes with companion cells; transport sugars (sucrose) and amino acids in both directions (translocation); pressure-flow hypothesis.

**Transpiration**: evaporation of water from leaves through stomata. Affected by light intensity, temperature, humidity, wind, and water availability. Function: pulls water and minerals up; cools the plant.

**Plant hormones**:

- **Auxins** (e.g. IAA): promote cell elongation, apical dominance, phototropism, gravitropism.
- **Gibberellins**: promote stem elongation, seed germination.
- **Cytokinins**: promote cell division.
- **Abscisic acid (ABA)**: inhibits growth, promotes stomatal closure, dormancy.
- **Ethylene**: fruit ripening, leaf abscission.

#### Animal life processes

**Nutrition**: autotrophs (photosynthesis); heterotrophs (consume other organisms); saprotrophs (decompose dead matter).

**Digestion in humans**:

- **Mechanical**: chewing, churning in the stomach.
- **Chemical**: enzymes — amylase (saliva, pancreas) digests starch to maltose; pepsin (stomach) digests protein; lipase (pancreas, small intestine) digests lipids; bile (liver) emulsifies fats. Absorption occurs in the small intestine (villi increase surface area).

**Circulation**: humans have a closed double circulation — pulmonary (heart → lungs → heart) and systemic (heart → body → heart). Heart has four chambers; blood flows through arteries (away from heart), capillaries (exchange), and veins (to heart).

**Gas exchange**: in humans, gas exchange occurs in the alveoli of the lungs. Oxygen diffuses from alveolar air into the blood; carbon dioxide diffuses from the blood into the alveolar air. The large surface area, thin walls, moist surface and rich blood supply of alveoli make gas exchange efficient.

#### Reproduction

**Human reproduction**: males produce sperm in the testes; females produce eggs in the ovaries. The menstrual cycle (28 days average) is regulated by FSH, LH, oestrogen and progesterone.

**Flowering plant reproduction**: pollination (transfer of pollen from anther to stigma); fertilisation (sperm fuses with egg in the ovule); seed and fruit formation.

#### Coordination and response

**Nervous system**: central (brain, spinal cord) and peripheral (sensory and motor neurons). Sensory neurons carry impulses from receptors to the CNS; motor neurons carry impulses from the CNS to effectors. Synapses transmit impulses via neurotransmitters.

**Hormones**: endocrine glands (pituitary, thyroid, adrenal, pancreas, ovaries, testes) secrete hormones into the bloodstream. Hormones act on target cells with specific receptors.

#### Homeostasis

**Negative feedback**: a change in a physiological variable triggers a response that reverses the change.

**Temperature regulation**: hypothalamus monitors blood temperature; responses include sweating, shivering, vasodilation, vasoconstriction, behavioural changes.

**Osmoregulation**: kidney filters blood, reabsorbs water and useful solutes, excretes waste. ADH (anti-diuretic hormone) regulates water reabsorption in the collecting duct.

**Glucose regulation**: insulin (from β-cells of islets of Langerhans) lowers blood glucose by promoting uptake into cells and glycogen synthesis; glucagon (from α-cells) raises blood glucose by promoting glycogen breakdown.

#### Ecosystems

**Energy flow**: food chains (producer → primary consumer → secondary consumer → tertiary consumer); food webs (interlinked food chains); trophic levels; 10% rule (only 10% of energy at one trophic level is transferred to the next; the rest is lost as heat, in faeces, or in undigested material).

**Biogeochemical cycles**: carbon cycle (photosynthesis, respiration, decomposition, combustion); nitrogen cycle (nitrogen fixation, nitrification, assimilation, ammonification, denitrification); water cycle (evaporation, transpiration, condensation, precipitation).

**Population dynamics**: sigmoid growth curve (lag, log, stationary, decline phases); carrying capacity; limiting factors (food, space, water, predation, disease).

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-topic links

#### Exam technique

- **For diagram questions**: label every structure and use arrows to indicate flow direction (e.g. xylem transport, blood flow, nerve impulses).
- **For process questions**: state inputs and outputs, then describe the process step by step.
- **For comparison questions**: use a table with two columns to lay out the differences clearly.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Plants get their food from the soil." Plants get water and minerals from the soil; they make their own food (carbohydrates) by photosynthesis.
- "The menstrual cycle and the ovarian cycle are the same." They are coordinated but distinct — the menstrual cycle refers to the changes in the uterine lining; the ovarian cycle refers to the changes in the ovary.
- "Negative feedback is bad for the body." Negative feedback is a regulatory mechanism that maintains homeostasis — it is essential.
