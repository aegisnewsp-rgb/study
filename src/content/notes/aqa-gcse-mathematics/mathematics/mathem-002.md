# Algebra — GCSE Mathematics (AQA 8300) Notes

Algebra is the largest single content strand at AQA GCSE Mathematics higher tier by marks. It spans Paper 1 (non-calculator) and Paper 2/3 (calculator), with questions ranging from basic substitution at foundation tier up to algebraic proof, iteration, and the use of functions at higher tier. Mastery of Algebra is the single highest-return investment for a student aiming at grade 7–9.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### The seven moves every Algebra question needs

1. **Substitute** a value into an expression.
2. **Collect like terms** by adding coefficients: 3x + 5x = 8x; 4x² − x² = 3x².
3. **Expand** a single bracket: a(b + c) = ab + ac.
4. **Factorise** by taking out a common factor: 6x² + 9x = 3x(2x + 3).
5. **Solve** a linear equation by inverse operations.
6. **Solve** a quadratic equation by factorising, completing the square, or the quadratic formula.
7. **Rearrange** a formula to make a different variable the subject.

#### Formulae to remember (higher tier)

- Quadratic formula: x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / 2a
- Difference of two squares: a² − b² = (a − b)(a + b)
- Perpendicular lines have gradients whose product is −1.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Expressions, equations and formulae

An **expression** contains variables but no equals sign: 3x + 5. An **equation** states two expressions are equal: 3x + 5 = 14. A **formula** is a rule relating variables: A = πr².

**Substitution** replaces the variable with a number. If y = 2x² − 3x and x = 4, then y = 2(16) − 12 = 32 − 12 = 20.

**Rearranging formulae.** To make x the subject of v = u + at, isolate x by inverse operations in reverse order:

- Subtract u: v − u = at
- Divide by t (assuming t ≠ 0): x = a = (v − u) / t

For formulae with the subject appearing on both sides (e.g., make t the subject of v = u + at), treat t as a single quantity and collect: v − u = at, so t = (v − u) / a.

#### Brackets and factorising

**Expanding a single bracket.** Multiply every term inside by the term outside:

- 3(x + 4) = 3x + 12
- −2(5y − 6) = −10y + 12
- x(x − 7) = x² − 7x

**Expanding two brackets (FOIL).** Multiply every term in the first bracket by every term in the second:

- (x + 3)(x + 5) = x² + 5x + 3x + 15 = x² + 8x + 15
- (2x − 1)(3x + 4) = 6x² + 8x − 3x − 4 = 6x² + 5x − 4

**Factorising.** Start with the largest common factor:

- 6x² + 9x = 3x(2x + 3)
- x² − 9 = (x − 3)(x + 3)  (difference of two squares)
- x² + 5x + 6 = (x + 2)(x + 3)  (quadratic factorisation)

#### Linear and quadratic equations

**Linear equations** are solved by inverse operations applied equally to both sides:

- 3x + 7 = 22 → 3x = 15 → x = 5
- 5(x − 2) = 3x + 1 → 5x − 10 = 3x + 1 → 2x = 11 → x = 5.5

**Simultaneous equations.** Two equations in two unknowns are solved by elimination or substitution:

- y = 2x + 1 and y + x = 10
- Substitute: 2x + 1 + x = 10 → 3x = 9 → x = 3 → y = 7

**Quadratic equations** (higher tier). Three methods:

1. **Factorising** (when the discriminant b² − 4ac is a perfect square): x² − 5x + 6 = 0 → (x − 2)(x − 3) = 0 → x = 2 or 3.
2. **Completing the square**: x² + 6x + 5 = (x + 3)² − 4 → (x + 3)² = 4 → x + 3 = ±2 → x = −1 or −5.
3. **Quadratic formula**: ax² + bx + c = 0 → x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / 2a.

The **discriminant** b² − 4ac tells you how many real roots:

- b² − 4ac > 0: two distinct real roots
- b² − 4ac = 0: one repeated root
- b² − 4ac < 0: no real roots

#### Inequalities

Inequalities use ≤, <, ≥, > instead of equals. The rules:

- Add or subtract the same number from both sides.
- Multiply or divide both sides by the same positive number.
- **Reverse the inequality** if you multiply or divide by a negative number.

For graphical solutions, treat the inequality as a line first, then decide which side of the line to shade.

#### Graphs of linear and quadratic functions

**Linear graphs** y = mx + c have gradient m and y-intercept c. Draw by plotting the y-intercept, then using the gradient to find a second point.

**Quadratic graphs** y = ax² + bx + c are parabolas. The vertex (turning point) is at x = −b/(2a), with y-value found by substitution. Roots are where the graph crosses the x-axis.

**Cubic and reciprocal graphs** (higher tier) extend the visual vocabulary: y = x³ has a single S-shape; y = k/x has two branches and never touches the axes.

#### Sequences

A **sequence** is a list of numbers following a rule. **Arithmetic sequences** have a common difference d: nth term aₙ = a₁ + (n − 1)d. **Geometric sequences** have a common ratio r: nth term aₙ = a₁ × rⁿ⁻¹.

#### Functions and iteration (higher tier)

A **function** is a rule mapping one number to another, written f(x) = 2x + 1. **Composite functions** combine two functions: fg(x) = f(g(x)). **Inverse functions** reverse the mapping: f⁻¹(x) = (x − 1) / 2.

**Iteration** uses one formula to generate successive values: x_{n+1} = √(2 + xₙ). Convergence depends on the formula; some iterations diverge.

#### Algebraic proof (higher tier)

Proof questions require you to derive a result from a general algebraic identity. The two most-used results:

- Sum of first n positive integers: 1 + 2 + … + n = n(n + 1) / 2
- Sum of first n odd numbers: 1 + 3 + 5 + … + (2n − 1) = n²

Proofs typically use the identity (k + 1)² − k² = 2k + 1 and sum over k = 0 to n − 1.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where Algebra appears across the papers

- **Paper 1 (non-calculator):** simple factorisation, substitution, expanding single brackets, solving linear equations.
- **Paper 2 and Paper 3 (calculator):** simultaneous equations, quadratic equations by all three methods, completing the square, iteration, algebraic proof, function composition.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Expand (x + 3)² as x² + 9." It is x² + 6x + 9. Squaring a sum is not the same as summing the squares.
- "Move the 5 across the equals sign." The correct statement is "subtract 5 from both sides". Moving a term is shorthand; the formal operation is the same inverse on both sides.
- "A quadratic equation always has two solutions." It can have 0, 1 or 2 real solutions, decided by the discriminant.
- "y = mx + c has a unique gradient for any pair of points." The gradient is unique between two distinct points, but the y-intercept c is set independently.

#### Exam technique

- Show every algebraic step. Partial marks on 4- and 5-mark questions depend on visible working.
- For rearranging formulae, write the inverse operations in reverse order on a separate line each time.
- When factorising, always take out the largest common factor first — leaving 2x² + 4x as 2x(x + 2) is correct, but 2x² + 4x as x(2x + 4) is incomplete and loses a mark for "not fully factorised".
