# Explore data using a statistical enquiry process (91944) — NCEA Level 1 Mathematics and Statistics Notes

Achievement Standard 91944 is the first of four NCEA Level 1 Mathematics and Statistics standards. It is internally assessed (the school marks it) and carries 5 credits toward the NCEA Level 1 certificate. The standard asks students to plan a statistical enquiry, collect and manage data, analyse patterns, and report findings — all in response to a research question chosen in context. Most schools run 91944 across Terms 1 and 2 so that the data-collection work is finished well before the end-of-year external exams on 91946 and 91947.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Statistical enquiry process** at Level 1 has a published sequence: pose a question, plan, collect data, manage data, analyse, report. NZQA's "Unpacking" document lists each step.
- **5 credits**, internally assessed by the school, with moderation by NZQA.
- **Grade descriptors** at Level 1 use the four-grade scale: Not Achieved, Achieved, Merit (relational thinking), Excellence (extended abstract thinking).
- **Plan** includes the population of interest, the variable(s), how data will be collected, and why the chosen method is appropriate.
- **Manage** covers cleaning the data, dealing with missing or outlying values, and presenting tables ready for analysis.
- **Analyse** includes appropriate summary statistics, a relevant display, and a sentence interpreting what the data shows in context.
- **Report** is a written or verbal summary that links back to the original question.

#### Examiner traps

- Skipping the "plan" step — NZQA markers look for evidence that the student thought about the population and method before collecting anything.
- Using the wrong summary statistic (mean with skewed data, mode with continuous data) — pick the statistic that matches the variable type.
- Drawing a graph that doesn't answer the research question — a bar chart for two-group comparison is fine; a pie chart for trend data is not.
- Stating conclusions without linking back to the original context — Merit and Excellence specifically require interpretation in the chosen context, not generic comments.
- Confusing relational thinking (Merit) with extended abstract thinking (Excellence) — Merit requires choosing the right method; Excellence requires justifying why that method is the best fit.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### The statistical enquiry process

NZQA publishes a six-step enquiry process for 91944. Each step appears in the marking schedule, and skipping one drops the grade.

1. **Pose a question.** A research question that can actually be answered with data, in a context the student cares about (a school context, a community context, or a sports context). Avoid questions that cannot be answered numerically — "what is your favourite colour?" is not a 91944 question; "how long do Year 11 students spend on homework per night?" is.
2. **Plan.** Identify the population, the sample (and whether it is representative), the variables to measure (categorical, discrete numerical, continuous numerical), and the data collection method (survey, observation, measurement, experiment). Justify the choice of method briefly.
3. **Collect.** Carry out the plan. Record data carefully; use a tally sheet, spreadsheet or similar tool. Note the sample size.
4. **Manage.** Clean the data (remove or correct obvious errors), handle missing values, decide whether outliers should be excluded, and lay the data out in a table.
5. **Analyse.** Choose an appropriate display (bar chart, column chart, dot plot, box plot, stem-and-leaf, histogram, scatter plot) and an appropriate summary statistic (mean, median, mode, range, IQR). Calculate and display.
6. **Report.** Summarise findings in a written report or oral presentation. State the answer to the original question in context, with reference to the data.

#### Variable types drive the analysis

The variable type determines both the display and the summary statistic that should be used.

| Variable type | Example | Display | Summary statistic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Categorical | Eye colour, school subject | Bar chart, column chart | Mode, frequency table |
| Discrete numerical | Number of siblings, goals scored | Bar chart, dot plot | Mode, median, range |
| Continuous numerical | Height, reaction time | Histogram, stem-and-leaf, box plot | Median, mean, range, IQR |

Picking the wrong pair — say, a pie chart for a continuous numerical variable — is a common reason Merit-grade work slips back to Achieved.

#### Comparing two groups

91944 often asks for a comparison (Year 10 vs Year 12, two classes, boys vs girls). For a comparison of two groups on a numerical variable:

- Use **parallel box plots** or **back-to-back stem-and-leaf plots** for the display.
- Compare **medians** (centre), **IQRs** (spread) and **overall range**.
- State the comparison in context — "Year 12 students tend to score higher than Year 10 students" is weak; "Year 12 students had a median of 78 versus Year 10's median of 64, but Year 10 had a wider spread (IQR 18 vs Year 12's IQR 12)" is strong.

#### Internal assessment logistics

- The school submits results to NZQA by a fixed date (usually November).
- The school provides at most one resubmission opportunity per student per standard; further resubmissions are at the teacher's discretion.
- A random sample of student work is sent to NZQA for external moderation to check that school marking is consistent with the national standard.
- A student who misses an internal assessment due to genuine illness can apply for a derived grade (a grade based on other evidence) — apply through the school.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Picking a context that lifts the grade

Most 91944 reports land at Achieved because the context is generic ("How tall are Year 11 students?") and the conclusion is a one-line read-off. To reach Merit or Excellence:

- Pick a context with a **comparison** built in. "How does daily screen time differ between Year 11 boys and Year 11 girls?" gives you a comparison structure that NZQA markers can score higher than a single-group survey.
- Pick a context that allows a **justification** of method. "Why is the median the better summary than the mean for the variable you chose?" is the Excellence question, and you should be able to answer it for whatever variable you pick.
- Pick a context where the conclusion can be **qualified**. "The data shows … but the sample was small / non-random / from one school, so the conclusion might not hold for all Year 11 students." That sentence moves an otherwise Achieved-grade report into Merit territory.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Plan means write a list." No — plan means identify population, sample, variable, method, and justify the method.
- "Mean is always best." No — median is more robust to outliers; use median for skewed continuous data.
- "Excel will pick the right graph." No — Excel guesses. You pick the graph, then Excel draws it.
- "Conclusion can be one sentence." No — Excellence requires a conclusion that is linked to context, refers to the data, and notes a limitation.
- "I can resubmit as many times as I want." No — at most one resubmission per standard; further resubmissions are at teacher discretion.
