# Preamble, Fundamental Rights, and DPSP

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The **Preamble** declares India as a sovereign, socialist, secular, democratic republic and identifies the people as the source of constitutional authority. **Fundamental Rights** in Part III (Articles 12–35) are enforceable through constitutional remedies, while **Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP)** in Part IV (Articles 36–51) guide governance but are ordinarily non-justiciable under Article 37.

- **Six Fundamental Rights:** Right to Equality (14–18), Freedom (19–22), Against Exploitation (23–24), Freedom of Religion (25–28), Cultural and Educational (29–30), and Constitutional Remedies (32).
- **Article 32** makes the Supreme Court the guardian of Fundamental Rights; **Article 226** gives the High Court parallel jurisdiction.
- **Five writs:** habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, certiorari, quo warranto.
- **Article 31C** shields laws implementing specified DPSP (Articles 39(b), (c), 39A, 42) from challenge on Articles 14 or 19 grounds.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### The Preamble as Interpretive Guide

The Preamble is not an independent source of enforceable rights — the Supreme Court held it is part of the Constitution and a key to the minds of the makers, but the operative provisions lie in Articles 14, 19, 21 and the rest of Part III. The 42nd Amendment, 1976 added **socialist**, **secular** and **integrity** to the text. In *Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala* (1973), the Preamble was used to identify the **basic structure** of the Constitution.

#### Scope of Article 12 and Article 14

Article 12 expands "State" to include the Government and Parliament of India, State Governments and Legislatures, and all **local or other authorities** within Indian territory. Judicial decisions (e.g. *Ajay Hasia*, *Chander Mohan*) decide whether a body is a "State" by looking at funding, control and function.

Article 14 promises equality before the law **and** equal protection of the laws. Permissible classification must rest on an **intelligible differentia** and have a **rational nexus** to the object of the law — the test from *State of West Bengal v. Anwar Ali Sarkar* (1952).

| Article | Right | Holders |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 14 | Equality before law | Persons |
| 19 | Six freedoms (speech, assembly, association, movement, residence, profession) | Citizens only |
| 20 | Protection against conviction for offences | Persons |
| 21 | Life and personal liberty | Persons |

#### Constitutional Remedies and Writs

- **Article 32** — direct petition to the Supreme Court for Fundamental Rights enforcement.
- **Article 226** — wider High Court jurisdiction over Fundamental Rights **and** other rights.
- **Habeas corpus** — production of a detained person before the court.
- **Mandamus** — order to a public authority to perform a duty.
- **Prohibition** — issued to an inferior court preventing usurpation of jurisdiction.
- **Certiorari** — transfer of a pending case to a superior court for legality review.
- **Quo warranto** — inquiry into the authority by which a person holds a public office.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Article 21 and the Maneka Gandhi Expansion

In *Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India* (1978), the Supreme Court read **procedure established by law** under Article 21 as requiring fairness, non-arbitrariness and non-discrimination. The judgment linked Articles 14, 19 and 21, and produced a long line of derivative rights: livelihood (*Olga Tellis*), clean environment (*MC Mehta*), education (*Unni Krishnan*), privacy (*Puttaswamy*, 2017), and dignified death (*Common Cause*, 2018).

#### DPSP Categorisation and Conflict Resolution

DPSP are classified into four groups for KPSC answers: **Socialist** (Articles 38, 39, 39A, 41, 42, 43, 43A), **Gandhian** (Articles 40, 43, 46, 47, 48), **Internationalist** (Article 51), and **Miscellaneous/Agrarian** (Articles 44, 45, 48, 48A, 49, 50).

The classic conflict is between Part III and Part IV. The Supreme Court in *Minerva Mills v. Union of India* (1980) struck down the Clause (4) and Clause (5) inserted into Article 31C by the 42nd Amendment, holding that **harmony between Fundamental Rights and DPSP** is a basic feature. The **Champakam Dorairajan** (1951) doctrine — DPSP must yield to Fundamental Rights — has been diluted in practice; Parliament now routinely invokes Article 31C to protect land reform and reservation statutes.

#### Common Traps in KPSC Prelims

1. Calling the Preamble a source of enforceable Fundamental Rights — it is interpretive, not operative.
2. Granting Article 19 freedoms to foreigners — only citizens hold Article 19.
3. Saying Article 32 can be suspended during Emergency — only Articles 20 and 21 survive suspension under Article 359; Article 32 itself remains on the statute book, though access is restricted.
4. Confusing Fundamental Duties (Article 51A) with DPSP — duties bind **citizens**, principles bind the **State**.
5. Treating DPSP as completely toothless — Article 37 makes them fundamental in governance, and Article 31C, statutory backing and judicial directions give them operational force.

**Practice prompts:** (i) Explain why the 42nd Amendment's insertion of "socialist" and "secular" did not convert India into a theocracy or a command economy. (ii) Discuss whether DPSP override Fundamental Rights when Parliament legislates under Article 31C, citing at least two Supreme Court decisions.
