# Research Methods, Statistics and Ethics in Psychology — MCAT Psych/Soc

Research methods, statistics, and ethics in psychology span AAMC Foundational Concepts 6 and 8 of the Psych/Soc section and tie directly into the AAMC Scientific Inquiry and Reasoning Skills (SIRS). The MCAT tests experimental versus correlational designs, variables and controls, sampling, descriptive and inferential statistics, effect size, confidence intervals, and research ethics. These topics underpin every Psych/Soc passage that describes a study.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### Core concepts

- **Experimental design:** manipulate IV, measure DV, control for confounds. Allows causal inference.
- **Correlational design:** measure variables as they naturally occur. Cannot establish causation.
- **Variables:** independent (manipulated), dependent (measured), confounding (alternative explanation).
- **Controls:** placebo control, standard-of-care control. Reduces expectancy effects.
- **Sampling:** random sampling (every member has equal chance) versus convenience sampling (easily accessible subjects).
- **Descriptive statistics:** mean, median, mode, range, standard deviation.
- **Inferential statistics:** t-test, ANOVA, chi-square, correlation, regression. Test whether results are likely due to chance.
- **Effect size:** Cohen's d, r². Measures the magnitude of the effect, not just whether it is significant.
- **Confidence interval:** range that likely contains the true value with a given probability (typically 95%).
- **Ethics:** informed consent, confidentiality, minimal harm, debriefing, IRB approval.

#### One-pass drill

1. Identify the topic: design, variable, statistics, or ethics.
2. Recall the relevant principle.
3. Apply to the specific scenario.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing correlation with causation. Correlational studies cannot establish causation.
- Treating statistical significance as practical significance. A significant result may have a tiny effect size.
- Forgetting that random assignment (to conditions) is different from random sampling (of subjects).

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Research designs

**Experimental:** manipulate IV, measure DV, control confounds. Random assignment to conditions. Allows causal inference.

**Quasi-experimental:** compare pre-existing groups (e.g., treatment vs no treatment). Cannot establish causation as cleanly as true experiments.

**Correlational:** measure the relationship between two variables. Cannot establish causation; directionality and third variables are concerns.

**Longitudinal:** measure the same participants over time. Allows study of development and change.

**Cross-sectional:** measure different participants of different ages at one time. Less informative about change than longitudinal.

**Case study:** detailed study of one individual or small group. Useful for rare conditions; cannot generalise.

#### Variables and controls

- **Independent variable (IV):** what the researcher manipulates.
- **Dependent variable (DV):** what the researcher measures.
- **Confounding variable:** alternative explanation for the results.
- **Control:** condition that isolates the effect of the IV (placebo, standard of care, no treatment).
- **Random assignment:** ensures groups are equivalent at baseline.

#### Statistics

**Descriptive:** mean (average), median (middle), mode (most frequent), range (max - min), standard deviation (spread).

**Inferential:**
- **t-test:** compare two group means.
- **ANOVA:** compare three or more group means.
- **Chi-square:** test association between categorical variables.
- **Correlation (r):** measure linear relationship between two variables (-1 to +1).
- **Regression:** predict one variable from another.

**Effect size:** Cohen's d (difference between means in standard deviation units); r² (variance explained). Important for practical significance.

**Confidence interval (CI):** the range likely to contain the true value. A 95% CI means that if the study were repeated 100 times, 95 of the CIs would contain the true value. A wider CI = less precise estimate.

#### Ethics

- **Informed consent:** participants must understand the study and agree to participate.
- **Confidentiality:** participant data must be kept private.
- **Minimal harm:** physical and psychological harm must be minimised.
- **Debriefing:** after the study, participants must be told the true purpose (especially in deception studies).
- **IRB approval:** institutional review board must approve the study before it begins.
- **Vulnerable populations:** special protections for children, prisoners, pregnant women, persons with cognitive impairment.

#### Practice routine

- Take 5 design items. Distinguish experimental, correlational, longitudinal, case study.
- Take 5 variable items. Identify IV, DV, confound.
- Take 5 statistics items. Choose the right test for the data type.
- Take 5 ethics items. Identify the principle being violated.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How this topic connects to the other Psych/Soc topics

- **All Psych/Soc passages** describing a study depend on research-methods literacy.
- **Social interaction and socialisation** are studied using correlational and experimental designs.
- **Biology of behaviour** depends on neuroscience methods (lesion studies, fMRI, EEG).

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"Random sampling and random assignment are the same."** Random sampling selects subjects from the population; random assignment assigns subjects to conditions. Both are important but serve different purposes.
- **"A significant p-value means a large effect."** A significant p-value only means the effect is unlikely due to chance. Effect size measures magnitude.
- **"All research requires informed consent."** Most research does, but archival research using public data may be exempt.

#### Exam technique

- For design items, identify the design by its key feature (manipulation, comparison, time frame).
- For variable items, identify IV (manipulated) and DV (measured).
- For statistics items, identify the data type (categorical, continuous) before choosing the test.
- For ethics items, identify the principle being violated.
- Use the platform's calculator for any arithmetic.
