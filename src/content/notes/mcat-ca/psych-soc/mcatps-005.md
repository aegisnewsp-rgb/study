# Self-Concept, Social Interaction and Socialisation — MCAT Psych/Soc

Self-concept, social interaction, and socialisation form the core of AAMC Foundational Concept 8 of the Psych/Soc section. The MCAT tests self-concept, self-esteem, self-efficacy, identity formation, attitudes, persuasion, conformity (Asch, Milgram), obedience, group dynamics, prejudice, discrimination, and stereotyping. These topics underpin most social-psychology passages on the exam.

---

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### Core concepts

- **Self-concept:** the overall set of beliefs a person holds about themselves.
- **Self-esteem:** the affective evaluation of the self-concept (how much you like yourself).
- **Self-efficacy:** the belief in one's ability to perform a specific task (Bandura).
- **Identity:** the sense of who one is, including personal identity (unique traits) and social identity (group memberships).
- **Attitudes:** evaluations of people, objects, or ideas. Three components: affective (feelings), behavioural (actions), cognitive (beliefs).
- **Persuasion:** central route (logical arguments) versus peripheral route (cues like attractiveness, credibility).
- **Conformity:** changing behaviour to match a group (Asch's line-judgement experiment).
- **Obedience:** following orders from an authority figure (Milgram's shock experiment).
- **Group dynamics:** groupthink, group polarization, social facilitation, social loafing.
- **Prejudice, discrimination, stereotyping:** prejudice (attitude), discrimination (behaviour), stereotype (cognitive belief).

#### One-pass drill

1. Identify the topic: self, attitude, conformity, group, or prejudice.
2. Recall the relevant theory or experiment.
3. Apply to the specific scenario.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing self-esteem and self-efficacy. Self-esteem is global; self-efficacy is task-specific.
- Treating conformity and obedience as the same. Conformity is peer pressure; obedience is authority pressure.
- Confusing prejudice, discrimination, and stereotypes. They are different (attitude, behaviour, belief).

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Self-concept and identity

**Self-concept:** the overall set of beliefs about the self. Develops through social interaction and self-reflection.

**Self-esteem:** the affective evaluation of the self. Influenced by social comparison, success/failure, and feedback from others.

**Self-efficacy (Bandura):** the belief in one's ability to perform a specific task. Sources: mastery experiences, vicarious experiences, verbal persuasion, physiological state.

**Identity:** personal identity (unique traits) and social identity (group memberships). Identity formation is a key task of adolescence (Erikson).

#### Attitudes and persuasion

**Attitude components:** affective (feelings), behavioural (actions), cognitive (beliefs). The MCAT tests how attitudes are formed and changed.

**Cognitive dissonance (Festinger):** discomfort from holding contradictory beliefs. People reduce dissonance by changing attitudes, beliefs, or behaviours.

**Persuasion:**
- **Central route:** logical arguments; leads to strong, lasting attitude change.
- **Peripheral route:** cues like source attractiveness or credibility; leads to weaker attitude change.

#### Conformity and obedience

**Conformity (Asch):** participants judged line lengths in a group. When confederates gave the wrong answer, about one-third of participants conformed. Conformity increases with group size (up to ~3-4 people), unanimity, and cohesion.

**Obedience (Milgram):** participants delivered (fake) shocks to a learner when ordered by an authority. About 65% delivered the maximum shock. Obedience increases with authority proximity and uniformity.

#### Group dynamics

- **Groupthink:** desire for harmony leads to poor decisions (Janis).
- **Group polarization:** group decisions are more extreme than individual decisions.
- **Social facilitation:** performance improves on simple tasks in the presence of others.
- **Social loafing:** individuals exert less effort in a group than alone.

#### Prejudice, discrimination, stereotypes

- **Prejudice:** negative attitude toward a group.
- **Discrimination:** negative behaviour toward a group.
- **Stereotype:** cognitive belief about a group's characteristics.
- **Sources:** social learning, social categorisation, social identity (in-group/out-group bias).
- **Reducing prejudice:** contact hypothesis (positive contact under equal status), education, perspective-taking.

#### Practice routine

- Take 5 self items. Distinguish self-concept, self-esteem, self-efficacy, identity.
- Take 5 persuasion items. Identify the route (central or peripheral).
- Take 5 conformity/obedience items. Identify the experiment (Asch or Milgram).
- Take 5 group items. Identify the dynamic (groupthink, polarisation, facilitation, loafing).
- Take 5 prejudice items. Distinguish prejudice, discrimination, stereotype.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How this topic connects to the other Psych/Soc topics

- **Development** shapes self-concept and identity (Erikson's stages).
- **Cognition** underlies attitudes and persuasion.
- **Social structure** (Foundational Concept 9) affects intergroup relations.

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"Self-esteem and self-efficacy are the same."** Self-esteem is global; self-efficacy is task-specific.
- **"Stereotypes are always negative."** Stereotypes can be positive or negative; prejudice and discrimination are typically negative.
- **"Groupthink improves decisions."** Groupthink impairs decisions by suppressing dissent.

#### Exam technique

- For self items, identify the construct by its definition.
- For persuasion items, identify the route by the type of argument.
- For conformity/obedience items, identify the experiment by the setup.
- For group items, identify the dynamic by the group behaviour.
- For prejudice items, identify the construct by its definition.
