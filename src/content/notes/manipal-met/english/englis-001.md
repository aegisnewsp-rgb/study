# Grammar and Usage — Manipal MET (MET)

Manipal MET treats English as a tested skill rather than a literature subject. Grammar and Usage provides the sentence-level foundation for error detection, sentence improvement, cloze choices, comprehension, and the logical connections used in longer passages. A question may look like vocabulary, but the answer can depend on whether a noun is countable, whether a verb follows a modal, or which noun a pronoun refers to.

Build accuracy from structure: locate the subject and finite verb, check tense and agreement, inspect the relationship between parts of a sentence, and then check punctuation. Work with complete sentences instead of isolated rules, because a form that is acceptable in one context may be wrong in another. Confirm the current syllabus and question presentation on the official MAHE website before applying paper-level advice.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### Eight rules to retain

1. **Modal + base verb:** Use **can/could/may/might/must/should/would + verb** and **to + verb** after infinitives.
2. **Subject–verb agreement:** Make the verb agree with the subject’s head noun, not with a plural noun inside an intervening phrase.
3. **Tense and aspect:** Use simple past for a completed past time and present perfect when a past event has present relevance.
4. **Parallel structure:** Keep nouns with nouns, verbs with verbs, and adjective forms with adjective forms.
5. **Articles and determiners:** A/an requires a singular countable noun, and the article must agree with singular or plural reference.
6. **Modifier placement:** Attach each adjective, adverb, or participial phrase to the word it describes and identify the doer of the action.
7. **Conditionals and prepositions:** Use the correct verb form after **if/unless** and fixed pairs such as **interested in**, **capable of**, and **discuss** without *about*.
8. **Boundaries and pronoun case:** Do not join independent clauses with a comma alone, and use **me** after a preposition such as **between**.

### 🟡 Standard — Exam Prep (3d-3w)

#### 1. Modal verbs and infinitives

A modal auxiliary does not change for person or number. The verb after **can, could, may, might, must, should, would**, or **shall** is the base form without **-s**, **-ed**, or **-ing**. An ordinary infinitive also uses **to + base verb**.

- **Incorrect:** The candidates should submits their identities before the entrance examination.
- **Correct:** The candidates should submit their identities before the entrance examination.

- **Incorrect:** The technician must to isolate the circuit before repairing it.
- **Correct:** The technician must isolate the circuit before repairing it.

- **Incorrect:** The committee may to postpone the review if the documents remain incomplete.
- **Correct:** The committee may postpone the review if the documents remain incomplete.

**Have to** and **need to** behave differently because they are ordinary verb forms and do change.

- **Incorrect:** Every participant have to submit a feedback form before leaving the campus.
- **Correct:** Every participant has to submit a feedback form before leaving the campus.

#### 2. Subject–verb agreement

Find the head of the subject before deciding on the verb. A long phrase such as **of the candidates** or **on the notice** does not control agreement when the head is singular. **Each**, **every**, and **one of** also take a singular verb.

- **Incorrect:** The number of applications received by the portal have increased since the new deadline was announced.
- **Correct:** The number of applications received by the portal has increased since the new deadline was announced.

- **Incorrect:** The quality of the answers submitted by the candidates are likely to affect the final result.
- **Correct:** The quality of the answers submitted by the candidates is likely to affect the final result.

- **Incorrect:** Each of the trainees need to complete the safety module before the laboratory visit.
- **Correct:** Each of the trainees needs to complete the safety module before the laboratory visit.

In an **either ... or** construction, the verb normally agrees with the subject nearest to it.

- **Incorrect:** Either the manager or the assistants was responsible for the delayed announcement.
- **Correct:** Either the manager or the assistants were responsible for the delayed announcement.

#### 3. Tense and aspect

Use the simple past for a completed event with a finished time expression such as **yesterday, last month,** or **on Friday**. Use the present perfect when the past event is connected with the present without naming a finished time. In a past narrative, an event that happened before another past event usually takes the past perfect.

- **Incorrect:** The research team has completed its survey last month.
- **Correct:** The research team completed its survey last month.

- **Incorrect:** When the auditors arrived, the accounts have not yet been checked.
- **Correct:** When the auditors arrived, the accounts had not yet been checked.

- **Incorrect:** The application form is filling since the applicant started it.
- **Correct:** The application form has been filling since the applicant started it.

Do not change tense mechanically. A sequence of past actions can move between simple past, past perfect, and present perfect when the time relationship changes.

- **Incorrect:** The committee approved the proposal, and then it has announced the decision publicly.
- **Correct:** The committee approved the proposal, and then it announced the decision publicly.

#### 4. Parallel structure

Items joined in a list or comparison should have the same grammatical role. A reader should be able to match the items by form as well as by meaning.

- **Incorrect:** The training aims to develop analytical reasoning, communication skills, and to solve unfamiliar problems.
- **Correct:** The training aims to develop analytical reasoning, strengthen communication skills, and solve unfamiliar problems.

- **Incorrect:** The scholarship is awarded on the basis of academic performance, financial need, and how innovative the applicant is.
- **Correct:** The scholarship is awarded on the basis of academic performance, financial need, and the applicant’s innovativeness.

- **Incorrect:** The new method is not only faster but also it is easier to operate.
- **Correct:** The new method is not only faster but also easier to operate.

- **Incorrect:** The course helps students to analyse evidence, construct arguments, and writing conclusions.
- **Correct:** The course helps students to analyse evidence, construct arguments, and write conclusions.

#### 5. Articles, determiners, and countability

The choice of **a, an, the, some, many,** or **much** depends on countability, number, sound, and whether the reference is specific. **A/an** follows pronunciation rather than spelling.

- **Incorrect:** The tutor offered us a useful advice about revising the syllabus.
- **Correct:** The tutor offered us some useful advice about revising the syllabus.

- **Incorrect:** The scientist waited for an university laboratory to become available.
- **Correct:** The scientist waited for a university laboratory to become available.

- **Incorrect:** The researchers gathered many evidences before beginning the analysis.
- **Correct:** The researchers gathered much evidence before beginning the analysis.

- **Incorrect:** These kind of solutions require careful testing.
- **Correct:** These kinds of solutions require careful testing.

- **Incorrect:** The visitors were asked to wait for a hour before entering the examination hall.
- **Correct:** The visitors were asked to wait for an hour before entering the examination hall.

#### 6. Modifier placement

An adjective normally modifies a noun, and an adverb modifies a verb, adjective, or another adverb. A participial phrase containing **-ing** must have a clear subject. If the subject of the main clause is not the doer of the participial action, the phrase is dangling.

- **Incorrect:** Having reviewed the proposal, the conclusion was accepted by the interview panel.
- **Correct:** After the panel had reviewed the proposal, the panel accepted the conclusion.

- **Incorrect:** Walking towards the laboratory, the rain made the researcher change direction.
- **Correct:** While the researcher was walking towards the laboratory, the rain made the researcher change direction.

- **Incorrect:** The professor notified the student with the scholarship that the schedule had changed.
- **Correct:** The professor notified the student who had the scholarship that the schedule had changed.

- **Incorrect:** The committee sent the file to the clerk after checking it.
- **Correct:** After checking the file, the committee sent it to the clerk.

#### 7. Conditionals and fixed prepositions

After **if** and **unless**, use the verb form required by the time relationship. In an ordinary first conditional, use **if + simple present** and **will + base verb**. Prepositions are selected by the verb, adjective, or noun around them; they are not freely interchangeable.

- **Incorrect:** If the applicants will upload the documents by midnight, the portal will accept their requests.
- **Correct:** If the applicants upload the documents by midnight, the portal will accept their requests.

- **Incorrect:** Unless you will receive the notification, you should check your spam folder.
- **Correct:** Unless you receive the notification, you should check your spam folder.

- **Incorrect:** The candidates discussed about the proposed changes during the meeting.
- **Correct:** The candidates discussed the proposed changes during the meeting.

- **Incorrect:** The new rule is applicable to every applicant who is capable to follow it.
- **Correct:** The new rule is applicable to every applicant who is capable of following it.

- **Incorrect:** She is interested on improving her presentation skills.
- **Correct:** She is interested in improving her presentation skills.

#### 8. Sentence boundaries and pronoun case

A comma can separate extra information or introduce a clause, but it cannot by itself join two independent clauses. Use a period, semicolon, or coordinating conjunction. Pronouns change form according to their job in the sentence: subject, object, possessive, or object of a preposition.

- **Incorrect:** The experiment began at noon, the first reading appeared at one o’clock.
- **Correct:** The experiment began at noon; the first reading appeared at one o’clock.

- **Incorrect:** Between you and I, the final draft needs another revision.
- **Correct:** Between you and me, the final draft needs another revision.

- **Incorrect:** The coordinator sent the instructions to we after the meeting.
- **Correct:** The coordinator sent the instructions to us after the meeting.

- **Incorrect:** The team worked hard, it completed the task before the deadline.
- **Correct:** The team worked hard and completed the task before the deadline.

### 🔴 Deep — Mastery (1mo+)

#### 1. The finite-verb skeleton and agreement

A complete clause normally contains a subject and a finite verb. The verb carries information about person, number, tense, or mood, while participles and infinitives add detail but do not replace the finite verb. Agreement is controlled by the grammatical head of the subject, even when several nouns appear after it.

A student who sees a plural noun near the verb may choose the wrong form. Reduce each noun phrase to its head before deciding. In **the number of**, **the number** is singular; in **a number of**, the phrase means several items and takes a plural verb.

- **Incorrect:** The list of names on the notice are posted outside the examination room.
- **Correct:** The list of names on the notice is posted outside the examination room.

- **Incorrect:** There is several reasons for the delay in publishing the results.
- **Correct:** There are several reasons for the delay in publishing the results.

- **Incorrect:** Neither the principal nor the teachers was informed about the change in policy.
- **Correct:** Neither the principal nor the teachers were informed about the change in policy.

- **Incorrect:** The committee, together with its advisers, were unable to reach a decision.
- **Correct:** The committee, together with its advisers, was unable to reach a decision.

**Common student error:** Treating every noun in a prepositional phrase as the subject. **Trap pattern:** Put a long phrase between the subject and verb, such as **The quality of the answers submitted by the candidates ...**, and test the verb against **quality**. This mechanism is useful in **Reading Comprehension** because it separates a passage’s claim from supporting detail. In **Paragraph Formation**, agreement errors can make a pronoun or repeated noun appear to refer to the wrong participant. In **Sentence Improvement**, locate the smallest faulty span rather than rewriting the entire sentence.

#### 2. Modals, auxiliaries, and meaning

Modal verbs carry a combination of attitude, ability, possibility, obligation, or time. Their form is fixed, so the main decision is often the base verb that follows them. **Must** can express obligation or strong deduction, **may** and **might** express possibility, and **should** commonly gives advice. Context determines the intended meaning; the modal should not be replaced merely because another modal seems more familiar.

- **Incorrect:** The instructions require each applicant to must submit a photograph before the interview.
- **Correct:** The instructions require each applicant to submit a photograph before the interview.

- **Incorrect:** If the battery is weak, the phone will emits a warning signal.
- **Correct:** If the battery is weak, the phone will emit a warning signal.

- **Incorrect:** The candidate may to choose either the morning or the evening session.
- **Correct:** The candidate may choose either the morning or the evening session.

Auxiliaries also combine with participles in fixed forms.

- **Incorrect:** The submitted documents were not reviewed until the committee had received them.
- **Correct:** The submitted documents were not reviewed until the committee had received them.

**Common student error:** Adding **to** after a modal or adding **-s** to the following verb. **Trap pattern:** A sentence such as **The instructor asked the students to revise their answers** is already correct because **to revise** contains the base verb. Another trap is changing the modal’s meaning while repairing its form; keep the original level of certainty unless context clearly requires otherwise. This rule appears directly in **Sentence Improvement**, in **Cloze Test** selection after **can, must, should,** or **would**, and in **Vocabulary in Context**, where modal words provide clues about attitude and probability.

#### 3. Tense, aspect, and viewpoint

Tense places an event in relation to a point in time; aspect presents the event as complete, ongoing, habitual, or connected to another event. The adverbial phrase is evidence, not a replacement for grammatical judgment. **Yesterday** and **last week** usually call for simple past in ordinary exam English. **Since** and **for** often point to a situation continuing into the present. A past event that precedes another past event commonly takes **had + past participle**.

- **Incorrect:** The committee has approved the budget on Friday.
- **Correct:** The committee approved the budget on Friday.

- **Incorrect:** When the visitor entered the laboratory, the technician has already calibrated the instrument.
- **Correct:** When the visitor entered the laboratory, the technician had already calibrated the instrument.

- **Incorrect:** The application form is filling since the applicant started it.
- **Correct:** The application form has been filling since the applicant started it.

- **Incorrect:** The engineer says that the machine works properly yesterday.
- **Correct:** The engineer says that the machine worked properly yesterday.

Keep a consistent point of view in a passage. A writer may deliberately shift from past to present for a general truth, but an unexplained shift often creates a timeline error. In **Reading Comprehension**, tense helps identify what happened first and what remains true. In **Summary and Conclusion Skills**, changing **may** to **will**, or changing a completed event into an ongoing one, changes the writer’s claim. **Paragraph Formation** also depends on tense continuity when a paragraph describes a process from beginning to end.

#### 4. Parallelism as logical mapping

Parallelism is not only a surface style preference. It tells the reader that two or more items have the same logical status. When items name abilities, make the forms match: **to analyse, to construct, and to write**, not **to analyse, construct, and writing**. When items name qualities, use comparable nouns or adjectives. In a comparison, the two sides should be measured on the same scale.

- **Incorrect:** The course develops analytical ability, communication skills, and the ability of solving unfamiliar problems.
- **Correct:** The course develops analytical ability, communication skills, and problem-solving ability.

- **Incorrect:** The award recognises achievement, originality, and students who demonstrate perseverance.
- **Correct:** The award recognises achievement, originality, and perseverance.

- **Incorrect:** The proposal is more detailed and has more practical examples than the previous proposal.
- **Correct:** The proposal is more detailed and more practical than the previous proposal.

- **Incorrect:** The new system not only reduces delays but also improves the reporting process.
- **Correct:** The new system not only reduces delays but also improves the reporting process.

- **Incorrect:** The scientist explained that the samples were preserved carefully, labelled clearly, and analysed in the laboratory.
- **Correct:** The scientist explained that the samples were carefully preserved, clearly labelled, and analysed in the laboratory.

**Common student error:** Correcting a verb while leaving the other list items in a different form. **Trap pattern:** Mix **to improve**, **improving**, and **improved** in one series. Check the shared role of every item after identifying the first one. Parallel signals such as **not only ... but also**, **either ... or**, **both ... and**, and **from ... to ...** also signal a relationship. **Summary and Conclusion Skills** benefits from this check because a compressed conclusion should preserve the same list logic. **Paragraph Formation** uses parallel structures to connect reasons, examples, and consequences. In **Verbal Reasoning — Analogies**, recognising shared relationships is also central, even when the item is not a complete sentence.

#### 5. Determiners, noun classes, and reference

Determiners depend on what the noun means. Countable nouns have singular and plural forms; uncountable nouns such as **advice, information, equipment, furniture,** and **evidence** normally do not. **A/an** is selected by the first sound, so spelling alone is not a reliable guide. **The** can refer to a specific person, object, institution, or previously mentioned item, but it is not simply a signal for the second mention.

- **Incorrect:** The committee asked for a evidence that would support the conclusion.
- **Correct:** The committee asked for some evidence that would support the conclusion.

- **Incorrect:** The visitor waited for a hour before the gate opened.
- **Correct:** The visitor waited for an hour before the gate opened.

- **Incorrect:** The researchers collected many evidences during the field survey.
- **Correct:** The researchers collected many pieces of evidence during the field survey.

- **Incorrect:** The manager assigned these kinds of responsibilities to the new volunteers.
- **Correct:** The manager assigned these kinds of responsibilities to the new volunteers.

- **Incorrect:** The university opened a centre for students who need advice and a library for those who prefer quiet study.
- **Correct:** The university opened an advice centre for students who need guidance and a library for those who prefer quiet study.

**Common student error:** Treating an uncountable noun as though every modifier can use **a** or **many**. **Trap pattern:** A sentence may use **an** before a noun beginning with a consonant letter, or no article before a plural noun. Check pronunciation, countability, and reference together. **Vocabulary in Context** becomes easier when word class is identified: a noun’s countability predicts its article and quantifier options. **Reading Comprehension** benefits from noticing whether **the** points to a specific example or a general idea. **Cloze Test** questions often use a noun’s grammatical class to decide between **an advice**, **some advice**, and **a piece of advice**.

#### 6. Modification and pronoun resolution

A modifier should have one clear referent. A misplaced participial phrase can make it appear that the wrong noun performs the action. A dangling participle is more severe because the grammatical subject of the main clause cannot logically perform the participial action. Pronouns require a similar check: identify the antecedent, then decide whether the pronoun must be subjective, objective, possessive, or a form such as **its**, **their**, or **who’s**.

- **Incorrect:** After reviewing the data, the conclusion seemed convincing to the research team.
- **Correct:** After the researcher had reviewed the data, the conclusion seemed convincing to the research team.

- **Incorrect:** The manager sent the proposal to the reviewer after reading it.
- **Correct:** After reading the proposal, the manager sent it to the reviewer.

- **Incorrect:** The committee asked the candidates whether they had submitted their assignment or not.
- **Correct:** The committee asked the candidates whether they had submitted their assignment.

- **Incorrect:** Between you and I, the schedule appears to have changed.
- **Correct:** Between you and me, the schedule appears to have changed.

- **Incorrect:** The teacher asked who’s proposal had been selected for publication.
- **Correct:** The teacher asked whose proposal had been selected for publication.

- **Incorrect:** The company lost it’s account after the payment was delayed.
- **Correct:** The company lost its account after the payment was delayed.

A modifier need not be moved every time an adverb is flexible. The real question is whether the reader can identify the intended relationship. In **Reading Comprehension**, dangling modifiers and unclear pronouns can change the apparent meaning of a statement. In **Paragraph Formation**, correct reference prevents a pronoun from attaching to the wrong noun across sentences. **Sentence Improvement** often tests this issue through a misplaced **-ing** phrase, while **Cloze Test** may require **whose**, **its**, or **their** rather than a similarly spelled form.

#### 7. Conditional logic and prepositional patterns

Conditionals combine time, reality, and expectation. In ordinary first-conditional sentences, **if** is followed by a present-tense clause and the main clause uses **will** or another modal. In a hypothetical past situation, formal English commonly uses **if + past** and **would + base verb**. A past unreal condition uses **if + had + past participle** and **would have + past participle**. These structures express different relationships, so a tense change is not merely a stylistic preference.

- **Incorrect:** If I would have accepted the offer, I would have joined the research team.
- **Correct:** If I had accepted the offer, I would have joined the research team.

- **Incorrect:** Unless the applicant does not submit the form, the portal will reject the request.
- **Correct:** Unless the applicant submits the form, the portal will reject the request.

- **Incorrect:** The course is suitable for students who are interested about environmental policy.
- **Correct:** The course is suitable for students who are interested in environmental policy.

- **Incorrect:** The applicant must be eligible for submit an application before the deadline.
- **Correct:** The applicant must be eligible to submit an application before the deadline.

- **Incorrect:** The proposal can be discussed about during the next committee meeting.
- **Correct:** The proposal can be discussed during the next committee meeting.

Prepositions should be learned as parts of stable expressions: **depend on**, **consist of**, **capable of**, **aware of**, **responsible for**, **interested in**, and **discuss** in its direct sense. In **Vocabulary in Context**, a nearby noun or adjective can identify the required preposition. **Cloze Test** rewards collocation knowledge, and **Reading Comprehension** often repeats such phrases. In **Verbal Reasoning — Analogies**, knowing whether a word expresses function, means, or category helps preserve the exact relationship instead of relying on a loose association.

#### 8. Punctuation as structural grammar

Punctuation marks the relationships between clauses. A comma splice occurs when two independent clauses are joined by a comma alone. A semicolon can join closely related independent clauses, while a coordinating conjunction can join them with a comma before it. A colon introduces a list, explanation, or result. Apostrophes mark possession, plurals, and contractions.

- **Incorrect:** The samples were transported carefully, the instruments were calibrated before use.
- **Correct:** The samples were transported carefully, and the instruments were calibrated before use.

- **Incorrect:** The device is efficient, it also reduces the energy consumed by the motor.
- **Correct:** The device is efficient; it also reduces the energy consumed by the motor.

- **Incorrect:** The report gives three recommendations: improve ventilation, train staff, and to update the emergency plan.
- **Correct:** The report gives three recommendations: improve ventilation, train staff, and update the emergency plan.

- **Incorrect:** The teachers meeting was scheduled for Monday afternoon.
- **Correct:** The teachers’ meeting was scheduled for Monday afternoon.

- **Incorrect:** The laboratory issued a warning, the equipment must be switched off before maintenance.
- **Correct:** The laboratory issued a warning; the equipment must be switched off before maintenance.

Do not add a comma simply because a sentence feels long. A comma between a subject and its verb is usually incorrect unless the subject is interrupted, and a comma cannot repair every run-on. **Reading Comprehension** becomes more accurate when clauses are separated properly, since punctuation signals a change in time, cause, contrast, or addition. **Paragraph Formation** uses commas and semicolons to connect ideas without creating false sentence boundaries. **Sentence Improvement** may contain punctuation errors alongside agreement or word-form errors, so identify the error type before editing.

#### 9. A repeatable correction routine

Use the same sequence whenever a sentence appears faulty:

1. **Find the subject head and finite verb.** Ignore the first plural noun you see and test the actual subject.
   - **Incorrect:** The number of students enrolled in the course have increased.
   - **Correct:** The number of students enrolled in the course has increased.

2. **Locate time and reference words.** Check **now, yesterday, since, before, after, already,** and **by** before deciding on tense.
   - **Incorrect:** The team has submitted its proposal last Monday.
   - **Correct:** The team submitted its proposal last Monday.

3. **Check the word after an auxiliary or infinitive marker.** Look for an unnecessary **to**, an extra suffix, or a missing base verb.
   - **Incorrect:** The candidates should submits their documents before the deadline.
   - **Correct:** The candidates should submit their documents before the deadline.

4. **Mark every item in a coordinated series.** Give each item the same grammatical form.
   - **Incorrect:** The task requires reading carefully, writing clearly, and to edit the final draft.
   - **Correct:** The task requires reading carefully, writing clearly, and editing the final draft.

5. **Resolve modifiers and pronouns.** Ask which noun performs the action or supplies the possessor.
   - **Incorrect:** Having checked the measurements, the panel approved the proposal.
   - **Correct:** After the panel had checked the measurements, the panel approved the proposal.

6. **Inspect the sentence boundary.** If two independent clauses meet, use a full stop, semicolon, or coordinating conjunction rather than a comma alone.
   - **Incorrect:** The lecture began at ten, the students entered after the speaker sat down.
   - **Correct:** The lecture began at ten; the students entered after the speaker sat down.

Make the smallest change that repairs the error. A short correction is often more reliable than a complete rewrite because it preserves the sentence’s original meaning.

#### 10. Transfer across the MET English syllabus

**Vocabulary in Context.** Part of speech predicts syntax. If a target noun is countable, check its article and quantifier; if a target word is an adjective, check whether it needs an adverb before a verb. Collocation also signals the correct preposition and complement.

- **Incorrect:** The candidates were asked to explain their answers in clearly language.
- **Correct:** The candidates were asked to explain their answers in clear language.

**Reading Comprehension.** Grammar helps identify the subject, the time sequence, the cause-and-effect relation, and the noun to which a pronoun refers. A reader who tracks these structures is less likely to confuse a detail with the main claim.

- **Incorrect:** The passage argues that the new policy have reduced waiting times for applicants.
- **Correct:** The passage argues that the new policy has reduced waiting times for applicants.

**Paragraph Formation.** A paragraph needs consistent reference, sensible pronouns, parallel reasons, and clear linking transitions. Replacing a repeated noun with an unclear pronoun can break cohesion.

- **Incorrect:** The committee approved the plan after the students presented it because they were confident.
- **Correct:** The committee approved the plan after the students presented it because the committee was confident.

**Sentence Improvement.** Treat each option as a diagnosis: agreement, verb form, tense, parallel structure, modifier placement, preposition, article, pronoun, or punctuation. Avoid changing several parts when only one is faulty.

- **Incorrect:** The applicant must to submit the original document before the interview.
- **Correct:** The applicant must submit the original document before the interview.

**Cloze Test.** Syntax narrows the answer before vocabulary does. Identify the missing word’s part of speech, the required agreement, the likely collocation, and the surrounding tense.

- **Incorrect:** The applicants were asked to ___ their documents before the deadline.
- **Correct:** The applicants were asked to **submit** their documents before the deadline.

**Verbal Reasoning — Analogies.** Analogy items often depend on a stable relationship such as function, means, part and whole, cause and effect, or category. Grammar can prevent a learner from pairing words merely because they belong to the same broad topic.

- **Incorrect reasoning:** An analyst is to a report as a pilot is to an aircraft because both words are nouns.
- **Correct reasoning:** An analyst is to a report as a pilot is to an aircraft because an analyst produces a report and a pilot operates an aircraft.

**Summary and Conclusion Skills.** A summary must preserve the original actor, action, time, degree of certainty, and main contrast. Changing a possibility into a certainty changes the passage’s conclusion.

- **Original sentence:** The new procedure may reduce waiting time, although the pilot study has not confirmed a reduction.
- **Incorrect summary:** The new procedure will reduce waiting time.
- **Correct summary:** The new procedure may reduce waiting time, but the reduction has not been confirmed.

### Common traps

1. **Taking the nearest noun as the subject.** A plural noun inside **of**, **with**, or **among** may not control agreement.
   - **Incorrect:** The quality of the answers submitted by the candidates are likely to affect the result.
   - **Correct:** The quality of the answers submitted by the candidates is likely to affect the result.

2. **Adding an infinitive marker after a modal.** Modals already take the base form and do not take **to** in the ordinary positive form.
   - **Incorrect:** The technician must to disconnect the power supply before repairing the device.
   - **Correct:** The technician must disconnect the power supply before repairing the device.

3. **Using present perfect with a finished time.** Expressions such as **yesterday, last week,** and **in 2024** normally call for simple past.
   - **Incorrect:** The committee has announced the winners last Friday.
   - **Correct:** The committee announced the winners last Friday.

4. **Leaving a dangling participial phrase.** The subject after **having** or **-ing** must be the person or thing performing that action.
   - **Incorrect:** Having reviewed the proposal, the conclusion was accepted by the panel.
   - **Correct:** After the panel had reviewed the proposal, the panel accepted the conclusion.

5. **Joining independent clauses with a comma alone.** Use a conjunction, semicolon, or full stop to show the relationship.
   - **Incorrect:** The train was delayed, the passengers reached the venue after the presentation began.
   - **Correct:** The train was delayed; the passengers reached the venue after the presentation began.

6. **Breaking parallelism in a list.** Every coordinated verb should have the same form and level of the sentence.
   - **Incorrect:** The course teaches students to analyse data, interpret graphs, and writing conclusions.
   - **Correct:** The course teaches students to analyse data, interpret graphs, and write conclusions.

7. **Treating an uncountable noun as countable.** **Advice, information, equipment, furniture,** and **evidence** normally need a quantifier or a phrase such as **a piece of**.
   - **Incorrect:** The mentor gave the students many useful advices about revising their projects.
   - **Correct:** The mentor gave the students much useful advice about revising their projects.

8. **Choosing an article by spelling instead of sound.** The letter and sound of the first part of a word must be considered separately.
   - **Incorrect:** The scientist waited for an university laboratory to become available.
   - **Correct:** The scientist waited for a university laboratory to become available.

9. **Using a preposition that does not fit the expression.** Learn the verb or adjective together with its usual preposition rather than translating word by word.
   - **Incorrect:** The students are capable to solve the problem independently.
   - **Correct:** The students are capable of solving the problem independently.

10. **Putting the wrong verb form in an ordinary conditional.** Use a present-tense clause after **if** or **unless** for a future condition in the usual first conditional.
    - **Incorrect:** If the weather will remain clear, the experiment will begin outdoors.
    - **Correct:** If the weather remains clear, the experiment will begin outdoors.

11. **Confusing subject and object pronoun case.** A pronoun after **between, to, for,** or **with** normally has the object form.
    - **Incorrect:** Between you and I, the proposed schedule appears difficult to maintain.
    - **Correct:** Between you and me, the proposed schedule appears difficult to maintain.

12. **Leaving a possessive apostrophe or a modifier unresolved.** Decide whether the mark shows possession, a plural, or a contraction, and identify the noun that the modifier describes.
    - **Incorrect:** The teacher asked who’s proposal had received the highest evaluation.
    - **Correct:** The teacher asked whose proposal had received the highest evaluation.
