# Task 1 — Data Comparison and Selection — IELTS Writing (Qatar)

In the IELTS Academic Writing test, **Task 1** is the **describing task**: you
must produce a report of **150+ words in approximately 20 minutes** describing
a visual input (graph, table, chart, or diagram). The second sub-skill is
**Data Comparison and Selection**: selecting and grouping the most significant
features from the data, ignoring minor detail, and signalling comparison with
appropriate cohesive devices (*whereas, in contrast, similarly*).

This is the **second topic** in the ielts-writing subject (weight 3). It
follows ieltsw-001 (Task 1 — Describing Visual Information w=4) and precedes
ieltsw-003 (Task 1 — Process and Map Description w=2). The skill applies to
every visual type (line graphs, bar charts, pie charts, tables, mixed charts),
but it is most heavily weighted when the visual has **multiple categories**
(e.g. four countries, three sectors, two periods).

The examiner scores Trainable on **task achievement** (did you select the
significant features? did you group them?), **coherence and cohesion** (did
you use appropriate cohesive devices?), **lexical resource** (do you have
varied vocabulary for comparison?), and **grammatical range and accuracy**
(do you use a mix of simple and complex sentences?).

This note shows the LCA version: a **Lite** (5-minute) read, a **Standard**
(15-minute) read, and the **Exam Essentials** table a Qatari candidate can
use to revise in the last hour before the test.

## Lite (4-Step Method)

1. **Identify the most significant features (1 minute).** Look at the
   visual and identify: highest value, lowest value, biggest change,
   smallest change, the overall trend. Do not get distracted by every
   figure.
2. **Group the features (1 minute).** Group the selected features
   (highest + lowest; biggest increase + biggest decrease; comparison).
   Each group becomes one body paragraph.
3. **Use appropriate cohesive devices (in the body paragraphs).** Use
   *whereas, in contrast, similarly, on the contrary, on the other hand*
   to signal comparison; use *first, second, finally* to signal the order of
   most-to-least significant.
5. **Avoid the list-everything trap.** A Task 1 that lists every figure
   signals band 5; a Task 1 that selects the most significant features,
   groups them, and uses appropriate cohesive devices signals band 7+.

**Insight: Task 1 is not a list of every figure — it is a structured
selection and grouping of the most significant features.** The examiner
scores whether you can identify the **most significant features**, **group
them logically**, and **signal comparison with appropriate cohesive
devices**.

### 6 Examiner Traps (Data Comparison and Selection)

- **The list-everything trap** — candidates list every figure from the
  visual without selection. The examiner scores task achievement. ALWAYS
  select the most significant features (highest, lowest, biggest change).
- **The no-grouping trap** — candidates describe features one by one without
  grouping. The examiner scores coherence and cohesion. ALWAYS group the
  selected features (one paragraph per group).
- **The no-cohesive-device trap** — candidates describe each feature in
  isolation without comparison. The examiner scores coherence and cohesion.
  ALWAYS use appropriate cohesive devices (*whereas, in contrast,
  similarly*).
- **The general-without-data trap** — candidates make claims without
  specific figures (*"Country A was higher than Country B"*) without
  specifying the magnitude. ALWAYS include specific figures (*"Country A
  was 12% higher than Country B, with 45% compared to 33%"*).
- **The single-paragraph trap** — candidates write the body as one
  paragraph. The examiner scores coherence and cohesion. ALWAYS use 2-3
  paragraphs (one for opening+1st group, one for 2nd group, one for 3rd
  group).
- **The no-comparison trap** — candidates describe each feature without
  comparing it to others (*"Country A was 45%. Country B was 33%"*). The
  examiner scores task achievement. ALWAYS compare (*"Country A was 12%
  higher than Country B"*).

### Qatari-Context Matches (2025–2026 cohort signal)

In recent IELTS Writing Task 1 tests used in Qatar (and the broader Gulf),
the data-comparison visuals commonly feature:

- **Population comparisons** — Qatar's population vs. other GCC countries
  (UAE, Saudi Arabia, Bahrain, Kuwait, Oman) at one point in time.
- **Economic indicators** — Qatar's GDP per capita vs. other GCC
  countries, or vs. other high-income countries (Norway, Switzerland,
  Singapore).
- **Energy mix comparisons** — natural gas share vs. oil share in Gulf
  countries; renewable share across MENA.
- **Tourism** — visitor arrivals to Qatar vs. UAE vs. Saudi Arabia.
- **Education statistics** — literacy rates, school enrollment, university
  graduates across Gulf countries.
- **Sports** — FIFA World Cup, Qatar Grand Prix, Asian Games attendance
  vs. other countries.

The Qatari *Educational Reform* (Ministry of Education and Higher Education)
emphasizes data literacy in Year 12 / Year 13 English classes, which is why
data-comparison visuals are commonly featured in the Qatari cohort.

## Standard (7-Step Method with Long-Form Guidance)

### Step 1 — Identify the most significant features (1 minute)

Before you write anything, identify:

- **Highest value** — the category with the largest value.
- **Lowest value** — the category with the smallest value.
- **Biggest change** — the category that changed the most (if the visual
  has time).
- **Smallest change** — the category that changed the least.
- **Overall trend** — the general direction (rising, falling, stable,
  fluctuating).

This identification takes 1 minute but saves 5–10 minutes of uncertain
writing later.

### Step 2 — Group the features (1 minute)

Group the selected features:

- **Group A** — highest + lowest (or one overall + one contrast).
- **Group B** — biggest change / second-largest change (or comparison).
- **Group C** — smallest change / comparison with overall.

Each group becomes one body paragraph.

### Step 4 — Write body paragraph 1 (Group A)

Body paragraph 1 covers Group A (highest + lowest). Use specific figures
and comparison:

- *"Country A had the largest population in 2024, with 9.5 million,
  whereas Country B had the smallest, with 1.4 million."*
- *"The difference between the highest and lowest was approximately 8.1
  million, a ratio of 6.8 to 1."*

### Step 5 — Write body paragraph 2 (Group B)

Body paragraph 2 covers Group B (biggest change / comparison). Use
specific figures and cohesive devices:

- *"Country C experienced the largest growth over the period, increasing
  by 25% from 2.0 million to 2.5 million. By contrast, Country D
  experienced a slight decline of 3%, from 2.1 million to 2.0 million."*
- *"Similarly, Country E remained stable, with a small change of less than
  1% over the decade."*

### Step 6 — Write body paragraph 3 (Group C)

Body paragraph 3 covers Group C (smallest change / comparison with overall).
Use specific figures:

- *"In comparison, the smaller countries (F, G, H) all experienced modest
  growth of 2-3% over the period, well below the regional average of
  7%."*

### Step 7 — Review and edit (5 minutes)

Reserve the last 5 minutes to review and edit your response. Check:

- ✅ Did I select the most significant features? (NOT list every figure)
- ✅ Did I group them? (NOT describe one by one)
- ✅ Did I use appropriate cohesive devices? (NOT describe in isolation)
- ✅ Did I include specific figures? (NOT generalize)
- ✅ Did I write 150+ words (aim 170-200)?
- ✅ Did I use varied vocabulary?
- ✅ Did I use varied grammar?
- ✅ Grammar: any errors?

### 8 Cohesive Devices for Comparison (Table)

| Cohesive device          | Use                                          | Example                                 |
|--------------------------|----------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------|
| *whereas*                | Contrast within the same sentence           | *"Country A was 45%, whereas Country B was 33%."* |
| *in contrast*            | Contrast at the start of a sentence         | *"In contrast, Country B had a much smaller share."* |
| *similarly*             | Similarity at the start of a sentence       | *"Similarly, the urban population grew at a faster rate."* |
| *on the other hand*      | Contrast within a sentence                   | *"Country A had the largest share; on the other hand, Country B had the smallest."* |
| *by comparison*         | Comparison at the start of a sentence       | *"By comparison, the rural population remained stable."* |
| *in comparison*          | Comparison at the start of a sentence       | *"In comparison, the urban population grew by 25%."* |
| *likewise*              | Similarity at the start of a sentence         | *"Likewise, the population in Country C increased."* |
| *however*               | Contrast at the start of a sentence         | *"However, the change in Country D was much smaller."* |

## Previous Year Patterns (IELTS 2018–2025)

IELTS Writing Task 1 has used the same format consistently since 2018:
visual input + 150+ words in ~20 minutes. Over 2018–2025:

- 2018–2020: Task 1 data-comparison visuals commonly featured two
  countries (e.g. UK vs Japan) or three countries (e.g. UK, USA, China)
  at one point in time.
- 2021–2023: Task 1 data-comparison visuals diversified to include four
  or more countries (e.g. UK, USA, China, India) and time-period
  comparisons (e.g. 2000 vs 2020).
- 2024–2025: Task 1 data-comparison visuals frequently use mixed charts
  (line + bar + table in one visual), with 80% coverage of multi-country
  comparisons.

The pattern: IELTS Writing Task 1 data-comparison visuals have
diversified since 2018, with multi-country comparisons becoming more
frequent. Candidates who practice multi-country data-comparison
outperform those who focus on two-country comparisons.

## Pro Tips (10 enumeration)

1. **Identify the most significant features first.** Highest / lowest /
   biggest change.
2. **Group the features logically.** Group A / Group B / Group C.
3. **Use appropriate cohesive devices.** Whereas, in contrast, similarly.
4. **Include specific figures.** 45%, 2.5 million.
5. **Write 2-3 body paragraphs.** One paragraph per group.
6. **Use comparison language.** Comparatives (-er than), superlatives
   (the most).
7. **Use varied vocabulary.** Do not repeat the same verbs.
8. **Use varied grammar.** Mix simple and complex sentences.
9. **Aim for 170-200 words.** Not less than 150 words.
10. **Reserve 5 minutes for review.** Do not submit without review.
