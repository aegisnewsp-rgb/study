# Renal Physiology: Glomerular Filtration

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Glomerular filtration is the **first step of urine formation**: a protein-free ultrafiltrate of plasma is forced from glomerular capillaries into Bowman's capsule across a three-layered barrier (fenestrated endothelium, basement membrane, podocyte slit diaphragm). The driving force is **Net Filtration Pressure (NFP)**, computed from Starling forces: **(PGC − PBS) − (πGC − πBS) ≈ 10 mmHg** (45 − 15 − 25 + 0). GFR = **Kf × NFP**, normally **~125 mL/min** (~180 L/day), with >99% reabsorbed downstream.

- **Inulin clearance = GFR**; **creatinine clearance ≈ GFR**; **PAH clearance ≈ effective RPF**; **FF = GFR/RPF ≈ 0.16–0.20**.
- **Angiotensin II constricts the efferent arteriole**, preserving GFR during hypovolaemia — a classic INI CET trap.
- Autoregulation holds GFR constant between MAP ≈ 80–180 mmHg via myogenic response + tubuloglomerular feedback.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Defining Starling Forces Across the Filtration Barrier

The barrier selects by **size (~7 kDa cut-off) and charge** (negatively charged glycocalyx repels albumin). Filtration is passive and depends on four pressures:

| Force | Symbol | Typical value | Direction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Glomerular capillary hydrostatic | PGC | ~45 mmHg | Promotes filtration |
| Bowman's space hydrostatic | PBS | ~15 mmHg | Opposes filtration |
| Glomerular capillary oncotic | πGC | ~25 mmHg | Opposes filtration |
| Bowman's space oncotic | πBS | ~0 mmHg | Negligible |

**NFP = (PGC − PBS) − (πGC − πBS) ≈ 10 mmHg.** GFR = Kf × NFP. Raising PGC (afferent dilation) or lowering efferent resistance tilts the balance toward filtration; the opposite tilts it toward cessation.

#### Filtration Fraction and Peritubular Consequences

Filtration fraction (**FF = GFR/RPF**, normally 0.16–0.20) is the single most-misunderstood INI CET derivative. **Efferent constriction raises FF**: oncotic pressure rises in leaving glomerular blood, which then enters peritubular capillaries and **pulls more fluid back from the proximal tubule**, increasing reabsorption.

- Efferent arteriolar constriction → FF ↑
- Afferent arteriolar constriction → FF ↓
- Sympathetic tone and noradrenaline target the afferent side, dropping GFR
- Angiotensin II targets the efferent side, shielding GFR

#### Clearance Framework

- **Inulin**: exogenous, freely filtered, **not reabsorbed, not secreted** → Cx = GFR
- **Creatinine**: endogenous, slight secretion → Cx ≈ GFR (routinely used clinically)
- **Urea**: partly reabsorbed → Cx < GFR
- **PAH**: filtered + secreted, almost completely cleared → Cx ≈ effective RPF

#### Autoregulation

Two mechanisms keep GFR stable from MAP 80–180 mmHg: the **myogenic response** (afferent smooth muscle contracts to MAP stretch) and **tubuloglomerular feedback** (macula densa senses distal NaCl delivery via NKCC2 and adjusts afferent tone). Outside this range, GFR becomes pressure-passive.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Hormonal and Pharmacological Modulation of GFR

| Agent | Site of action | Effect on GFR | Effect on FF |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Angiotensin II | Efferent arteriole (preferential) | Preserved/↑ | ↑ |
| Noradrenaline / sympathetic | Afferent arteriole | ↓ | ↑ |
| Prostaglandins (PGE2, PGI2) | Afferent arteriole (vasodilator) | ↑ | ↓ |
| Atrial natriuretic peptide (ANP) | Afferent arteriole + mesangial relaxation | ↑ | ↓ |
| ACE inhibitors | Blocks efferent constriction | ↓ (esp. in bilateral RAS) | ↓ |

> **Trap to remember:** In **renal artery stenosis**, ACE inhibitors can precipitate ARF — loss of efferent tone drops GFR when the kidney is perfusion-dependent.

#### Common Mistakes in INI CET Numericals

1. Forgetting **πBS ≈ 0** and adding a non-existent opposing force.
2. Computing FF backwards (RPF/GFR) — it is **GFR/RPF**.
3. Using creatinine clearance as a perfect GFR marker — secretion causes a small (~10–20%) overestimation.
4. Stating that all amino acids are reabsorbed; in **cystinuria**, the dibasic transporter is defective, producing calculi.

#### Worked Micro-Example

A patient has MAP 100 mmHg, Hct 0.45, RBF 1100 mL/min. Calculate RPF, expected GFR, and FF.

- **RPF = RBF / (1 − Hct) = 1100 / 0.55 = 2000 mL/min**
- **Expected GFR ≈ 125 mL/min** (normal adult)
- **FF = 125 / 2000 = 0.0625** (low — suggests efferent dilation or afferent constriction)

#### Practice Prompts

1. A patient on lisinopril presents with rising creatinine after dehydration. Predict the directional change in FF and the most likely mechanism.
2. If PGC rises to 55 mmHg while πGC, PBS, and Kf are unchanged, by what percentage does NFP increase?
