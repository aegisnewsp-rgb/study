# Topic 2

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The UAE University CAT Science section evaluates **scientific literacy** through the scientific method, foundational physics, matter, and life science basics. Master the vocabulary: **hypothesis** (a testable prediction), **independent variable** (the factor you change), **dependent variable** (the factor you measure), and **controlled variables** (factors kept constant). Know these formulas cold:

- **F = m·a** (Newton's second law: force in newtons = mass in kg × acceleration in m/s²)
- **ρ = m/V** (density in kg/m³ = mass ÷ volume)
- **KE = ½mv²** (kinetic energy in joules)
- **V = I·R** (Ohm's law: voltage in volts = current in amps × resistance in ohms)

**Exam pointers:** (1) Always identify which variable is independent before designing an experiment. (2) Use **SI units** (metre, kilogram, second, kelvin, ampere) — inconsistent units cost marks. (3) **Mass is not weight**: mass (kg) measures matter quantity; weight (N) is a force.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### The Scientific Method
The **scientific method** is the structured procedure behind every CAT Science item. Steps: observation → question → **hypothesis** (an *if…then* statement that must be falsifiable) → experiment → analysis → conclusion. A valid hypothesis generates a measurable prediction; statements like "the plant grew because of nature" are not hypotheses because they cannot be tested.

#### Variables in Experiments
Every controlled experiment manipulates one **independent variable** to observe its effect on a **dependent variable**, while all **controlled variables** stay constant. Example: testing how light intensity (independent) affects photosynthesis rate (dependent), holding temperature, CO₂, and water constant. Confusing which variable is which is the single most common source of lost marks in CAT data-interpretation items.

#### Units and SI Standards
The **SI (Système International)** system standardises measurement. The seven base units are metre (m) for length, kilogram (kg) for mass, second (s) for time, kelvin (K) for temperature, ampere (A) for current, mole (mol) for amount, and candela (cd) for luminous intensity. Derived units follow: newton (N = kg·m/s²), joule (J = kg·m²/s²), pascal (Pa = N/m²).

#### Force, Motion, and Energy
**Newton's second law** F = m·a governs linear motion. A 2 kg object accelerating at 3 m/s² experiences F = 6 N. **Kinetic energy** KE = ½mv² scales with the *square* of velocity — doubling speed quadruples KE, a key idea behind braking distance. **Potential energy** for gravity near Earth's surface is PE = mgh.

#### Matter and Its States
**Matter** exists in three common states — **solid** (fixed shape and volume), **liquid** (fixed volume, variable shape), and **gas** (variable both). Phase changes involve energy transfer without temperature change (latent heat). The **ideal gas law** PV = nRT links pressure (P), volume (V), amount (n), and temperature (T in kelvin); always convert °C to K by adding 273.15.

#### Cells and Ecosystems
**Cell theory** holds that (1) all living things are made of cells, (2) the cell is the basic unit of life, and (3) all cells come from pre-existing cells. In **ecosystems**, energy flows one-way from sun → producers (plants) → consumers (animals) → decomposers, while matter cycles through biogeochemical loops (carbon, nitrogen, water).

#### Common Question Formats
CAT Science uses scenario-based MCQs: read a short experiment description and select the correct variable identification, predict an outcome, identify a flaw, or compute a missing quantity using one of the core formulas.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Reading Graphs and Experimental Data
CAT Science items frequently present a line graph, bar chart, or data table and ask for interpretation. Always check: (1) axis labels and units, (2) the trend direction (linear vs. exponential vs. inverse), (3) whether the relationship is **correlation** or **causation**. A classic trap: ice-cream sales and drowning deaths both rise in summer — they correlate but share no causal link; the hidden variable is temperature.

#### Edge Cases and Advanced Traps
- **Mass vs. weight on different planets:** mass stays constant; weight changes with gravitational field strength (W = mg). On the Moon (g ≈ 1.62 m/s²), a 10 kg object weighs 16.2 N, not 98 N.
- **Ohm's law limits:** V = IR applies to ohmic conductors at constant temperature; filament bulbs deviate because resistance rises with temperature.
- **Density as identification:** since ρ = m/V is intrinsic to a substance, measuring density identifies unknown materials — a frequent lab-based question.
- **Conservation of energy:** in free fall, KE gained equals PE lost; ignore air resistance and total mechanical energy stays constant.

#### Connections to Adjacent Topics
Newton's laws (Topic 2) underpin almost all classical mechanics. **Einstein's E = mc²** links matter to energy, connecting chemistry (bond energies) with physics (nuclear reactions). Cell biology links to ecosystem dynamics through the concept that **producers convert solar energy into chemical energy** — the same energy conservation principle expressed biologically.

#### Common Mistakes Examined
1. Treating "control group" as identical to "controlled variables" — the control group is the *untreated* baseline; controlled variables are *kept constant across all groups*.
2. Reading the dependent variable from the **y-axis**, not the x-axis.
3. Using °C where K is required in the ideal gas law.
4. Forgetting that weight is a vector while mass is a scalar.

#### Practice Prompts
1. A student tests how salt concentration affects water uptake in potato strips (mass change after 30 min). Identify the independent, dependent, and two controlled variables, then state a falsifiable hypothesis in *if…then* form.
2. A 1500 kg car travelling at 20 m/s brakes to 10 m/s. Using KE = ½mv², find the kinetic energy lost and explain in one sentence why doubling the speed from 20 to 40 m/s would require losing *four times* this energy, not twice.
