# Information and Ideas — Command of Evidence — SAT R&W Notes

Command of Evidence is part of the SAT R&W Information and Ideas content domain. It tests the candidate's ability to cite paired textual evidence to support a claim and to evaluate the relationship between a quantitative chart or graph and an accompanying passage.

> Verify the live SAT specifications on https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/ before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Citing paired evidence:** selecting the lines from the passage that best support a claim.
- **Evaluating quantitative evidence:** relating a chart or graph to an accompanying passage.
- **Skills tested:** locating specific evidence, integrating textual and visual information.
- **Format:** each item has a passage (and a chart) and a question.
- **Common distractor:** the passage can mention all options; the question asks for the most accurate.

#### Examiner traps

- Choosing lines that are relevant but do not specifically support the claim.
- Misreading the chart or graph.

---

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Citing paired evidence

When a question asks which lines support a claim:

1. Identify the claim in the question stem.
2. Scan the passage for lines that directly relate to the claim.
3. Choose the lines that specifically support the claim, not just any relevant lines.

#### Evaluating quantitative evidence

When a question asks about a chart or graph:

1. Read the chart title, axis labels, and legend.
2. Identify the trend or comparison the chart shows.
3. Match the chart's content to the question.
4. Choose the option that best describes the relationship.

---

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where Command of Evidence appears across the SAT R&W section

Command of Evidence items appear across the SAT Reading and Writing section, in many papers with one paired-evidence item per passage.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Any relevant lines support a claim." No — only lines that specifically support the claim.
- "The chart is always literal." No — the chart can include inferred data.

#### Exam technique

- For paired-evidence questions, choose the lines that specifically support the claim.
- For quantitative-evidence questions, read the chart carefully and match the trend.

#### Step-by-step worked College Board test example

**Passage & Data Context**:
Biologists monitored the population recovery of two native fish species in a protected estuary following restoration of seagrass beds. Species A grew from 120 individuals in Year 1 to 450 individuals in Year 5. Over the same interval, Species B grew from 85 individuals to 190 individuals. A research report claims that although both species benefited from habitat restoration, Species A demonstrated a significantly greater relative rate of growth than Species B.

**Question**:
Which choice best uses data from the study to support the researchers' claim?
- (A) Species A reached a final population of 450 individuals in Year 5, whereas Species B reached only 190 individuals.
- (B) Species A's population more than tripled between Year 1 and Year 5, whereas Species B's population slightly more than doubled over the same timeframe.
- (C) Species A was already more abundant than Species B at the start of the study in Year 1.
- (D) The total combined population of both species exceeded 600 individuals by Year 5.

**Analysis**:
- Claim to support: "Species A demonstrated a significantly greater *relative rate of growth* (percentage change) than Species B."
- Calculate percentage growth:
 - Species A: (450 - 120) / 120 = 330 / 120 = 275% increase (population grew by 3.75x).
 - Species B: (190 - 85) / 85 = 105 / 85 = 123.5% increase (population grew by 2.23x).
- Choice (B) precisely isolates relative growth rate (tripling vs doubling). Choice (A) addresses only absolute counts, not growth rate. Correct selection is (B).
