# Hemodynamics and Blood Pressure Regulation

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Hemodynamics** is the physics of blood flow through the cardiovascular tree; **blood pressure regulation** is the integrated neural–hormonal–renal system that holds arterial pressure within a narrow perfusion range.

- **Cardiac output (CO):** CO = HR × SV (L/min). Normal ≈ 5 L/min at rest.
- **Mean arterial pressure:** MAP = DBP + ⅓(SBP − DBP), the true perfusion pressure.
- **Ohm's law analog:** Q = ΔP / R. Arterial BP = CO × SVR; SVR ∝ 1/r⁴ (Poiseuille).
- **Pulse pressure (PP):** SBP − DBP; reflects stroke volume and aortic compliance.
- **Short-term control:** baroreceptors (carotid sinus, aortic arch) → NTS → sympathetic/parasympathetic outflow.
- **Long-term control:** kidneys via pressure natriuresis and the **RAAS** (renin → Ang II → aldosterone).
- **Hypertension cutoff (ICMR/AIIMS):** SBP ≥ 140 and/or DBP ≥ 90 mmHg.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Determinants of Arterial Pressure

Arterial pressure is the product of how much blood the heart pumps (CO) and how much the systemic vasculature resists that flow (SVR). CO itself splits into heart rate and stroke volume, while SVR is governed chiefly by **arteriolar radius** — a fourth-power relationship described by **Poiseuille's law** (R = 8ηL / πr⁴), which is why small calibre changes dominate over viscosity (η) or length (L) effects in normal physiology.

| Parameter | Formula | Normal value |
|---|---|---|
| Cardiac output | CO = HR × SV | 5 L/min |
| Mean arterial pressure | MAP = DBP + ⅓(SBP − DBP) | 70–100 mmHg |
| Systemic vascular resistance | SVR = (MAP − CVP) / CO | 700–1600 dyn·s·cm⁻⁵ |
| Pulse pressure | PP = SBP − DBP | 30–40 mmHg |
| Ohm's law analog | Q = ΔP / R | — |

#### Short-Term Neural Regulation

The **baroreceptor reflex** senses stretch in the carotid sinus and aortic arch. Afferents travel via CN IX and X to the **nucleus tractus solitarius (NTS)**, which inhibits rostral ventrolateral medulla output and excites the dorsal motor nucleus of the vagus — yielding reduced sympathetic tone and increased parasympathetic outflow when pressure rises. The reflex resets within 1–2 days, so it buffers acute swings but does **not** set long-term pressure.

#### Hormonal and Renal Regulation

Medium-term controllers include **ADH (vasopressin)** from the supraoptic nuclei and **catecholamines** from the adrenal medulla. Long-term pressure is set by the kidneys through **pressure natriuresis** and the **RAAS**: juxtaglomerular cells release renin in response to low renal perfusion pressure, low Na⁺ at the macula densa, or β1 stimulation. Angiotensin II raises SVR via AT1 receptors and stimulates aldosterone, expanding blood volume. **ANP/BNP** from stretched atria/ventricles oppose RAAS.

> **Exam pointer:** In shock questions, calculate MAP first. MAP < 65 mmHg compromises coronary and cerebral autoregulation — that is the threshold INI CET vignettes use to start vasopressors.

#### Capillary Exchange

Net filtration across capillaries follows **Starling forces**: Jv = Kf[(Pc − Pi) − σ(πc − πi)]. Interstitial fluid returns to circulation mainly via the **lymphatic system**; failure of lymphatic drainage produces edema and explains low-protein states (e.g., nephrotic syndrome, kwashiorkor) on INI CET Medicine questions.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Clinical Correlations

**Cushing reflex** (raised intracranial pressure → cerebral ischemia → sympathetic surge → **hypertension with reflex bradycardia**) is a high-yield INI CET trap — it mimics hypertensive emergency but is neurogenic, requiring ICP lowering, not antihypertensives. **Coarctation of the aorta** produces upper-limb hypertension with rib notching and radio-femoral delay; PP widens centrally, narrows peripherally. **Aortic regurgitation** widens PP via reduced diastolic runoff (Hill's sign, popliteal SBP > brachial by ≥ 60 mmHg).

#### Autoregulation

**Cerebral** and **coronary** circulations maintain constant flow across MAP ≈ 60–150 mmHg via myogenic (Bayliss) and metabolic (adenosine, pO₂) mechanisms. Outside this window, flow becomes pressure-dependent — the basis for hypotensive hypoperfusion injury and hypertensive encephalopathy.

#### Capillary and Venous Dynamics

CVP (normal 2–8 mmHg) approximates right atrial pressure and is the downstream back-pressure for venous return. **Venous compliance** (~24× arterial) makes the venous system the main capacitance reservoir; sympathetic venoconstriction recruits volume to maintain preload.

| Disturbance | Primary mechanism | Compensation |
|---|---|---|
| Acute hemorrhage | ↓ preload → ↓ SV | Baroreflex tachycardia, RAAS activation |
| Heart failure | ↓ contractility → ↓ CO | RAAS, sympathetic, ventricular remodeling |
| Essential HTN | ↑ SVR, abnormal natriuresis | Lifestyle, ACEi/ARB, CCB, diuretics |
| Cushing reflex | ↑ ICP | Treat ICP, not BP directly |

#### Common Mistakes

> **Trap 1:** MAP is **not** (SBP+DBP)/2 — it is DBP + ⅓ PP because diastole lasts longer than systole.

> **Trap 2:** Pulse pressure ≠ stroke volume alone. Aortic stiffening (age, atherosclerosis) widens PP without changing SV.

> **Trap 3:** Baroreceptors reset in 1–2 days; chronic hypertension is a **renal** problem, not a baroreceptor failure.

> **Trap 4:** RAAS pharmacology — ACE inhibitors raise **bradykinin** (cough, angioedema); ARBs do not.

> **Trap 5:** Cushing triad = hypertension + bradycardia + irregular respiration. Don't conflate with Conn's syndrome (primary hyperaldosteronism → hypertension + hypokalemia).
