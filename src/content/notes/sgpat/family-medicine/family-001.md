# The SCFHS Training Pathway

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> The 60-second recall anchor every candidate should keep in their head before the exam.

The SCFHS Training Pathway is the **Saudi Board of Family Medicine (SBFM)** residency, run by the Saudi Commission for Health Specialties. Two tracks exist: a **4-year program** (the standard junior-to-senior route after internship) and a **3-year accelerated program** (for candidates with prior recognised training or ARAB/EU board certification).

| Element | What it is |
|---|---|
| Certifying body | **SCFHS** (Saudi Commission for Health Specialties) |
| Board | **SBFM** — Saudi Board of Family Medicine |
| Standard duration | **4 years** (R1 → R4) |
| Accelerated duration | **3 years** (eligibility criteria apply) |
| Final exit exam | Two parts: Part I written + Part II clinical/OSCE |
| Outcome | Title: *Saudi Board Certified Family Medicine Consultant* |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook (Memory Hook — "SCFHS-4-3-2"):** **S**CFHS = sole certifier; **4** = standard years; **3** = accelerated years; **2** = two-part exit exam (Part I written, Part II clinical).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> The working framework: who runs it, what the years look like, and how promotion actually happens.

#### The Regulatory Body vs The Board

SCFHS is the umbrella commission that accredits every health-specialty training programme in Saudi Arabia and issues the final consultant certificate. The SBFM is one of SCFHS's specialty boards, with its own curriculum, examinations, and scientific council. Board exams cannot be sat in isolation — they sit inside an SCFHS-accredited training centre, with progress tracked through formal In-Training Evaluation Reports (ITER).

#### The Two Parallel Tracks

The 4-year track is the default route: graduates enter at R1 after completing their internship (post-internship entry), progress through R2 and R3, and finish as senior residents at R4. The 3-year accelerated pathway shortens training for candidates who have already completed recognised prior training (e.g., a recognized ARAB Board or certain EU certificates) and meet the SBFM's exemption criteria. Eligibility for acceleration is decided by the SBFM scientific council on a case-by-case basis — current criteria must be confirmed against the latest SCFHS admission call before applying.

#### CanMEDS-FM Competency Framework

Assessment throughout the pathway is mapped to seven CanMEDS-FM roles: **Family Medicine Expert, Communicator, Collaborator, Manager, Health Advocate, Scholar, and Professional.** The ITER scores residents against these roles each rotation, not just clinical knowledge.

#### Comparison Matrix — 4-Year vs 3-Year Track

| Feature | 4-Year Standard Pathway | 3-Year Accelerated Pathway |
|---|---|---|
| Eligibility | After internship completion | Prior recognised training / ARAB or EU board |
| Levels | R1 → R2 → R3 → R4 | Compressed R-equivalent levels |
| Typical rotations | IM, Paediatrics, OB-GYN, EM, Surgery, Psychiatry + continuity clinic | Same rotation set, fewer total months per block |
| Promotion gate | ITER + rotation completion + scholarly project | Same criteria, compressed timeline |
| Final certification | Saudi Board Certified Family Medicine Consultant | Identical — same SBFM exit exam |

#### Promotion Requirements (R-to-R Progression)

1. Satisfactory ITER for every completed rotation in the academic year.
2. Completion of mandated rotations (medicine, paediatrics, OB-GYN, EM, surgery, psychiatry, family medicine continuity).
3. Approved scholarly project — research, audit, or quality-improvement work.
4. No unresolved professionalism or leave-of-absence flags in the training file.

#### Exit Examination

The final SBFM certificate requires passing **two sequential parts**: Part I is the written MCQ; Part II is the clinical / OSCE. Part I typically sits only after rotation and ITER requirements are met, and Part II sits after Part I is cleared — they cannot be taken in the wrong order.

> 💡 **High-Yield Revision Hook:** Rotate → Record (ITER) → Research (scholarly project) → **two** exams. Think of it as a four-step residency rhythm every Saudi GP Board candidate should memorise.

#### Worked Practice Question

> A candidate applying to the SBFM wants to sit Part II *before* Part I to "save time." Which single rule disqualifies this plan?

Working it through: SBFM regulations require candidates to clear Part I (the written MCQ) **before** sitting Part II (the clinical/OSCE). Even if all rotations, ITERs, and the scholarly project are complete, Part II eligibility is gated by a Part I pass certificate. The plan fails at the eligibility check.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** The trap is the assumption that OSCE readiness (clinical skill) substitutes for written MCQ readiness. They are sequential gates, not alternatives — and they test different CanMEDS-FM roles.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Edge cases, eligibility nuances, and the questions examiners ask when they want to separate candidates who memorised a slide from candidates who read the regulations.

#### Promotion Edges and Failure Modes

Promotion between R-levels is **competency-gated**, not time-only. A resident who has spent 12 months in a rotation but scored "borderline" ITERs on the Scholar or Professional role cannot progress until remediation is documented. The ITER rubric weights the seven CanMEDS-FM roles, and a single unsatisfactory role triggers a remediation cycle even when clinical scores look fine.

Failure modes that block promotion or exit-exam eligibility:

- Unsatisfactory ITER in any single CanMEDS-FM role (not just clinical).
- Incomplete scholarly project at the time of Part I application — many candidates miss that the project must be **approved**, not merely started.
- Excess leave of absence beyond the SCFHS-allowed percentage of the training year.
- Rotation gaps in the mandated list (e.g., missing OB-GYN or psychiatry blocks).

#### Eligibility for the 3-Year Track — Where Candidates Slip Up

The accelerated pathway is not awarded by examination score. It is awarded by **documented prior training** that the SBFM scientific council maps against the standard curriculum. A candidate with non-Arab, non-EU qualifications usually has to go through the standard 4-year route. The latest list of accepted prior certificates (and any updated exemption criteria) is published in each annual SCFHS admission cycle — always check the live admission call rather than relying on the previous year's list.

#### Adjacent Topic Connections

- **CanMEDS-FM as an assessment framework** — the same seven roles are reused in the CPD/revalidation cycle after certification. Memorising them once pays off in two parts of a Saudi GP Board career.
- **ITER language and remediation** — the ITER vocabulary (satisfactory, borderline, unsatisfactory, remediation) recurs in clinical governance after consultancy.
- **Scholarly project taxonomy** — research vs clinical audit vs QI — the same taxonomy is used in SBFM Part I MCQ stems.

#### Concept Comparison — SCFHS vs SBFM vs SCFHS-Accredited Centre

| Term | What it actually is | Common confusion |
|---|---|---|
| SCFHS | National commission; accredits programmes, awards final consultant title | Mistaken for "the exam itself" |
| SBFM | Specialty board inside SCFHS; owns curriculum and exit exam | Mistaken for the certifying commission |
| SCFHS-accredited training centre | A hospital/group that delivers the rotations under SBFM curriculum | Mistaken for "any hospital that trains residents" |
| ITER | Per-rotation in-training evaluation report mapped to CanMEDS-FM | Mistaken for a single annual score |
| Part I | Written MCQ — first of two sequential exit exams | Mistaken as interchangeable with Part II |
| Part II | Clinical / OSCE — must be taken **after** Part I pass | Mistaken as optional or order-free |

#### Common Mistakes Candidates Make on the Saudi GP Board

1. Treating SCFHS and SBFM as synonyms — the commission accredits, the board examines.
2. Assuming the SBFM is "just 3 years" — the 4-year route is the current default after internship.
3. Believing Part I and Part II can be attempted in either order.
4. Overlooking the scholarly project requirement until it blocks Part I eligibility.
5. Confusing regional training certificates with SCFHS national accreditation — only SCFHS issues the consultant title valid across the Kingdom.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts
