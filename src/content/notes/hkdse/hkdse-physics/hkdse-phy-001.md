# I. Heat and Gases — HKDSE Physics Notes

This topic covers Topic I of the Compulsory Part of HKDSE Physics. It addresses temperature, heat and internal energy (thermometric properties, specific heat capacity, latent heat); transfer processes (conduction, convection, radiation); change of state and the gas laws (Boyle's law, Charles' law, the ideal gas equation); and kinetic theory of gases.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Temperature**: a measure of the average kinetic energy of particles in a system. Units: °C, K (Kelvin). K = °C + 273.15.
- **Thermometric properties**: vary linearly with temperature (length of liquid column, electrical resistance of a metal, pressure of a gas at constant volume, EMF of a thermocouple). Used to define temperature scales.
- **Specific heat capacity c**: the energy required to raise the temperature of 1 kg of a substance by 1 K. Units: J/(kg·K). Q = mcΔT.
- **Specific latent heat L**: the energy required to change the state of 1 kg of a substance without changing its temperature. Q = mL.
- **Conduction**: transfer of heat through a material by molecular vibrations and electron collisions. Metals are good conductors; non-metals and gases are poor conductors.
- **Convection**: transfer of heat by bulk movement of fluid (liquid or gas). Hot fluid rises (less dense), cool fluid sinks.
- **Radiation**: transfer of heat by electromagnetic (infrared) waves. All objects emit and absorb radiation; good absorbers are good emitters. Black surfaces are best.
- **Gas laws**:
  - Boyle's law: pV = constant at constant T (for fixed mass of gas).
  - Charles' law: V/T = constant at constant p.
  - Gay-Lussac's law: p/T = constant at constant V.
  - Ideal gas equation: pV = nRT, where n is moles, R is the gas constant (8.314 J/(mol·K)), T in K.
- **Kinetic theory of gases**: gas consists of a large number of small particles in random motion; collisions are elastic; no forces between particles except during collisions; the volume of particles is negligible compared with the container.
- **Absolute zero**: 0 K = -273.15 °C; the temperature at which the average kinetic energy of particles is zero (theoretically).

#### Examiner traps

- Using °C instead of K in gas law calculations — always convert to Kelvin.
- Forgetting that latent heat is the same whether melting or freezing (numerically equal; sign differs by convention).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Temperature and thermometric properties

A thermometer is calibrated by choosing two fixed points (e.g. ice point and steam point) and a thermometric property that varies linearly with temperature. Common thermometric properties:

- Length of a liquid column (mercury, alcohol).
- Pressure of a gas at constant volume (constant-volume gas thermometer).
- Electrical resistance of a metal or thermistor.
- EMF of a thermocouple.

Different thermometers give slightly different temperature scales; the thermodynamic temperature scale (Kelvin) is independent of the thermometric property.

#### Specific heat capacity and latent heat

**Specific heat capacity c**: energy required to raise 1 kg of a substance by 1 K.

- Q = mcΔT (heat added, mass, specific heat capacity, temperature change).

Examples: water has c = 4,180 J/(kg·K) (very high); copper has c = 385 J/(kg·K).

**Specific latent heat L**: energy required to change the state of 1 kg of a substance without temperature change.

- Q = mL (heat added, mass, specific latent heat).

Examples: specific latent heat of fusion of ice is 3.34 × 10⁵ J/kg; specific latent heat of vaporisation of water is 2.26 × 10⁶ J/kg.

#### Heat transfer

**Conduction**: heat flows through a material from hot to cold. The rate depends on the material's thermal conductivity, the cross-sectional area, and the temperature gradient.

**Convection**: heat transfer by bulk fluid movement. The hot fluid becomes less dense and rises; cool fluid sinks to replace it, setting up a convection current. Used in domestic hot-water systems, sea and land breezes, atmospheric circulation.

**Radiation**: heat transfer by electromagnetic waves (mainly infrared). All objects above absolute zero emit radiation. Good absorbers are good emitters; black surfaces are best. Applications: solar panels, infrared cameras.

#### Gas laws and ideal gas equation

**Boyle's law**: pV = constant at constant T. As p increases, V decreases.

**Charles' law**: V/T = constant at constant p. As T increases, V increases.

**Gay-Lussac's law**: p/T = constant at constant V. As T increases, p increases.

**Ideal gas equation**: pV = nRT.

For a fixed mass of gas, combining Boyle's and Charles' laws: p₁V₁/T₁ = p₂V₂/T₂ (always use Kelvin for T).

#### Kinetic theory of gases

The kinetic theory explains macroscopic gas behaviour in terms of microscopic motion:

- A gas consists of a large number of particles in random motion.
- Collisions between particles and with the walls are elastic (no net loss of kinetic energy).
- The time of collision is negligible compared to the time between collisions.
- No forces between particles except during collisions.
- The volume of the particles is negligible compared with the container volume.

The average kinetic energy of a particle is (3/2)kT, where k is Boltzmann's constant. This gives the relationship between temperature and microscopic kinetic energy.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-topic links

- **II. Force and Motion**: kinetic energy and work link to thermodynamic concepts.
- **IV. Electricity and Magnetism**: resistance thermometers measure temperature.
- **VIII. Energy and Use of Energy (Elective)**: energy efficiency in buildings uses heat transfer.

#### Exam technique

- **For gas law calculations**: always convert temperature to Kelvin first.
- **For calorimetry problems**: identify the energy transfers, then apply Q = mcΔT and Q = mL.
- **For heat transfer questions**: distinguish conduction (in materials), convection (in fluids), radiation (in vacuum and through space).

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Heat and temperature are the same." Heat is energy transferred due to temperature difference; temperature is a measure of average kinetic energy.
- "Cold flows." Cold is the absence of heat; only heat flows, from hot to cold.
- "All gases are ideal." Real gases deviate from ideal behaviour at high pressures and low temperatures; the ideal gas model assumes point particles with no intermolecular forces.
