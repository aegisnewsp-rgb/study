# Legal News

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Legal News in CLAT tests your awareness of **Indian judicial pronouncements**, **legislative changes**, and **constitutional developments** reported in the 12 months preceding the exam. It sits inside the Current Affairs & General Knowledge section and contributes roughly 5% of the total paper weightage. Mastery here is less about memorising headlines and more about recognising the **legal issue**, the **bench strength**, and the **statutory provisions interpreted**.

- **Three new criminal codes** — BNS, BNSS, BSA — replaced the IPC, CrPC and Indian Evidence Act from **1 July 2024**; cite BNS sections, not IPC, in answers.
- **Article 142**, **Article 32**, and **Article 226** are the most-tested constitutional provisions in recent judgment-based questions.
- Read at least one national daily (**The Hindu** or **Indian Express**) and one law portal (**LiveLaw**, **Bar & Bench**, **Supreme Court Observer**) weekly.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### What CLAT Tests Under Legal News
The Legal News slice of Current Affairs expects three distinct competencies: identifying a **landmark judgment**, distinguishing between **ratio decidendi** (the binding reason) and **obiter dicta** (persuasive observations), and mapping a news item to its correct statutory provision. Questions appear either as factual MCQs (e.g. "Which Article was invoked in case X?") or as **passage-based comprehension** sets drawn from a recent Supreme Court order.

#### Core Reading Strategy
Consistent reading of one national daily and one law-specific outlet beats bulk revision. The Hindu's "Law & Society" column, Indian Express explainers, and LiveLaw's daily SC tracker cover roughly 90% of testable items. For constitutional developments, follow notifications from the **Ministry of Law and Justice**, **Election Commission of India**, and **NALSA**.

#### Mapping News to Subject Heads
A reliable way to organise notes is by legal domain rather than by date. The table below summarises the highest-frequency heads in CLAT's last five years:

| Subject Head | Typical Testable Item | Recent Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Criminal Law | New BNS section interpretation | Section 302 BNS death-penalty rulings |
| Constitutional | Article 32 / 226 PIL outcomes | Article 370 abrogation follow-ups |
| Commercial / Corporate | IBC, Competition Act amendments | Supreme Court NCLT rulings |
| Environmental | MPCB, Forest Conservation Act | Western Ghats, stubble burning orders |
| Family / Personal | Live-in relationship, same-sex marriage | Recognition of marriage equality pleas |
| Cyber / IPR | IT Act, Copyright amendments | AI-generated content rulings |

- Always note **bench strength** (Coram) and **judge names** in your answer.
- Match the **date of judgment** to the 12-month reference window — older items are not tested.
- Distinguish a **Law Commission Report** or **Standing Committee recommendation** from an enacted law.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Examiner Traps
The single most penalised mistake is quoting **IPC sections** (e.g. 302, 376, 420) for matters now governed by **BNS sections** (302, 63, 318 respectively). Since **1 July 2024**, all criminal-law questions in CLAT reference the new codes. A second common trap: students treat an **interim order** or **obiter dicta** as binding ratio and write it as "the law declared." Only the **ratio decidendi** of a coordinate or larger bench binds lower courts.

#### Connections to Adjacent Topics
Legal News overlaps directly with **Constitutional Law** (basic structure doctrine, Article 142 completeness powers), **Polity** (ordinance route under Article 123, Money Bill classification), and **Quantified Legal Reasoning** passages where the same judgment is recycled. Linking these notes prevents redundant revision.

#### Worked Micro-Example
Suppose a news item reads: "Supreme Court refuses to stay the amended BNS provision on attempted suicide." A scoring answer would name (i) the bench (e.g. 2-Judge), (ii) the date, (iii) the **specific BNS section** (Section 309 BNS / Section 226 BNSS for procedural bail), (iv) the **Article invoked** (typically Article 32 for writ jurisdiction), and (v) the brief reason in the order. A weak answer would merely write "SC denies stay" — the court, section, and relief are missing.

#### Two Practice Prompts
1. List three recent Supreme Court rulings where **Article 142** was used to do "complete justice," and identify the case type (criminal, civil, matrimonial).
2. Identify the **BNS section** corresponding to the old **IPC Section 420** (cheating) and cite one 2025 judgment interpreting it.
