# Each Category I Drug Targets a Discrete Mycobacterial

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Chemotherapy of specific infections** in INI CET tests first-line regimens, mechanisms, and the drug interactions behind them. Anti-tubercular drugs follow the RNTCP Category I backbone (2 HRZE / 4 HR), while Category II (2 HRZES / 1 HRZE / 5 HRE) handles relapse and failure. Anti-malarials split into chloroquine-sensitive *P. vivax/ovale* (chloroquine + 14-day primaquine for radical cure of hypnozoites) and chloroquine-resistant *P. falciparum* treated with artemisinin-based combinations (ACT). First-line ART combines two NRTIs with either an NNRTI (efavirenz) or a boosted PI (lopinavir/ritonavir).

- **INH** — mycolic acid synthesis inhibitor; co-prescribe **pyridoxine (B6)** to prevent neuropathy
- **Rifampicin** — bactericidal; potent **CYP3A4 inducer** (lowers OCP, warfarin, PI levels)
- **Primaquine / dapsone / sulfonamides** — screen for **G6PD deficiency** before prescribing

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Mechanisms of First-Line Anti-TB Drugs

Each Category I drug targets a discrete mycobacterial process. **Isoniazid** is a pro-drug activated by KatG to form an INH-NAD adduct that blocks **InhA (enoyl-ACP reductase)**, halting mycolic acid synthesis. **Rifampicin** binds the β-subunit of DNA-dependent RNA polymerase, blocking transcription. **Pyrazinamide** requires PncA-mediated conversion to pyrazinoic acid, which acidifies and disrupts membrane energetics at acidic pH. **Ethambutol** inhibits **arabinosyl transferase (EmbCAB)**, blocking arabinogalactan cell-wall assembly. Streptomycin binds the 30S ribosomal subunit, producing misreads.

#### RNTCP Regimens and Indications

| Regimen | Phase composition | Indication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Category I | 2 HRZE / 4 HR | New sputum-positive / severe extra-pulmonary TB |
| Category II | 2 HRZES / 1 HRZE / 5 HRE | Relapse, failure, or default |
| Category IV (MDR-TB) | 6–9 months intensive (kanamycin/amikacin + levofloxacin + ethionamide + cycloserine + PAS + PZA) → continuation | MDR-TB confirmed on DST |

#### Malaria: Species-Stratified Therapy

- *P. vivax / ovale* — chloroquine 25 mg/kg over 3 days + **primaquine 0.25 mg/kg × 14 days** (radical cure of hepatic hypnozoites); *always* check G6PD before primaquine
- Chloroquine-resistant *P. falciparum* — **ACT** (artesunate + sulfadoxine-pyrimethamine, or artemether-lumefantrine); single-dose primaquine 0.75 mg/kg added for gametocidal clearance
- Severe / cerebral malaria — **IV artesunate 2.4 mg/kg** at 0, 12, 24 h, then daily until oral intake

#### ART Initiation Logic

The WHO-preferred first line is **TDF + 3TC (or FTC) + dolutegravir**, but INI CET legacy stems still test **zidovudine + lamivudine + nevirapine (or efavirenz)**. NNRTI failure is rescued with a boosted PI regimen (lopinavir/ritonavir); ritonavir serves only as a CYP3A4 pharmacokinetic booster.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Drug Interactions: The CYP450 Minefield

Rifampicin induces CYP3A4 (and to a lesser extent CYP2B6, CYP2C9), accelerating metabolism of protease inhibitors, OCPs, warfarin, methotrexate, and calcineurin inhibitors — patients on ATT who continue OCPs risk contraceptive failure and unwanted pregnancy. Isoniazid does the opposite: it **inhibits CYP2C19 and CYP3A4**, elevating phenytoin, carbamazepine, and diazepam levels. Fluconazole, a potent CYP2C9 and CYP3A4 inhibitor, prolongs warfarin half-life and causes INH-rifampicin hepatotoxicity to worsen when all three are co-administered.

#### Pharmacokinetic Dose Adjustment

For ATT in renal impairment, dose the **aminoglycosides (streptomycin, amikacin, kanamycin)** by creatinine clearance using Cockcroft-Gault:

> **CrCl (mL/min) = [(140 − age) × weight (kg)] / (72 × serum creatinine mg/dL)** — multiply by 0.85 in females.

A 60-year-old, 60 kg woman with creatinine 2.0 mg/dL has CrCl ≈ 21 mL/min, mandating dose reduction or avoidance of streptomycin because of ototoxicity risk.

#### Edge Cases and High-Yield Traps

- **Dapsone** — oxidised by N-hydroxylation to a metabolite that causes methaemoglobinaemia and haemolysis in G6PD deficiency; the **sulfone syndrome** (fever, rash, hepatitis, lymphadenopathy) appears 2–6 weeks into therapy
- **Ethambutol** — dose-dependent **retrobulbar optic neuritis** presenting as red-green colour blindness; the Snellen chart misses this — Ishihara plates are mandatory monthly
- **Clofazimine** is *not* first-line — MDT multibacillary = **rifampicin + dapsone + clofazimine** (12 months), paucibacillary = **rifampicin + dapsone** (6 months)
- Primaquine does **not** treat acute falciparum malaria — its role is *P. vivax* radical cure and falciparum gametocidal clearance

#### Practice Prompts

1. A 28-year-old pregnant woman with active TB is started on Category I. Which drug should you add, and which laboratory test is mandatory before prescribing primaquine to her husband for vivax malaria?
2. An HIV-positive patient on lopinavir/ritonavir begins ATT. Which anti-TB drug is most likely to cause PI levels to fall, and what is the recommended pharmacokinetic rescue?
