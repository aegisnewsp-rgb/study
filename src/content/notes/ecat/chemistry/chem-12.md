# Transition Metals and Coordination Chemistry

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Transition metals** are d-block elements whose atoms or common ions carry **partially filled d-orbitals**; Zn, Cd, and Hg (d¹⁰) are excluded. They show **variable oxidation states**, form **coloured complexes**, exhibit **paramagnetism**, and act as catalysts. A **coordination compound** has a central metal atom/ion bonded to **ligands** through **coordinate (dative) covalent bonds**.

- **Coordination number (CN)** = total number of donor atoms attached to the metal (a bidentate ligand counts as 2).
- **Spin-only magnetic moment:** μ = √[n(n+2)] BM, where n = number of unpaired electrons.
- **ECAT focus:** IUPAC naming, oxidation state from formula, geometry, and μ calculation.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Defining the d-Block

A transition metal has an **incomplete d-subshell** either in its neutral atom or in any of its commonly encountered ions. Scandium ([Ar]3d¹4s²) qualifies through Sc³⁺ (d⁰), while **Zn²⁺ (d¹⁰) is excluded** because no common ion of zinc is partially filled. This narrow definition is what ECAT multiple-choice items probe when they offer Zn, Cd, or Hg as distractors.

#### Coordination Compounds and Ligands

A **ligand** is a Lewis base donating a lone pair to a metal centre (Lewis acid). Ligands are classified by denticity:

| Type | Example | Donor atoms |
|------|---------|-------------|
| Monodentate | NH₃, H₂O, Cl⁻ | 1 |
| Bidentate | ethylenediamine (en), oxalate (C₂O₄²⁻) | 2 |
| Polydentate | EDTA⁴⁻ | 6 |

#### IUPAC Naming Rules

Order: **cation → anion**; within the complex ion, **ligands alphabetically first, metal last** with its oxidation state in Roman numerals. Anionic ligands drop the -ide/-ate ending and gain -o (chloride → chloro, sulfate → sulfato); neutral ligands keep their name except H₂O (aqua) and NH₃ (ammine). Prefix counts use di-, tri-, tetra-, hexa-.

#### Worked Example

For **[Co(NH₃)₆]Cl₃**:
- Outer-sphere anions = 3 Cl⁻ → outer charge −3.
- Six neutral NH₃ contribute 0.
- Therefore Co oxidation state = +3, written as cobalt(III).
- IUPAC name: **hexaamminecobalt(III) chloride**.

#### Bonding: VBT and CFT

**Valence Bond Theory** assigns d²sp³ (inner-orbital, low-spin) for strong-field ligands and sp³d² (outer-orbital, high-spin) for weak-field ligands. **Crystal Field Theory** splits the five d-orbitals: in an **octahedral** field, t₂g lies **0.4Δₒ below** and e_g lies **0.6Δₒ above** the barycentre; in a **tetrahedral** field the labels reverse and Δₜ = (4/9)Δₒ.

#### Common ECAT Traps

- Forgetting that a bidentate ligand contributes 2 to CN but is **one ligand** for naming.
- Reporting Δₜ as greater than Δₒ; tetrahedral splitting is always smaller.
- Using n = total d-electrons instead of unpaired electrons in μ calculations.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Crystal Field Stabilisation Energy (CFSE)

For an octahedral complex, CFSE = [−0.4x + 0.6y]Δₒ, where x = electrons in t₂g and y = electrons in e_g. Pairing energy (P) must be added when low-spin configurations force extra pairing. Choose **high-spin** when Δₒ < P (weak-field ligands such as F⁻, Cl⁻, H₂O), and **low-spin** when Δₒ > P (strong-field ligands such as CN⁻, CO, en).

#### Isomerism Map

- **Structural:** linkage, coordination (ionisation), hydrate (solvate).
- **Stereoisomerism:** geometrical (cis-trans) in square planar [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] and octahedral [Ma₄b₂]; **optical** in [M(en)₃]ⁿ⁺, which lacks a plane of symmetry and exists as Δ/Λ enantiomers.

#### Chelate and Thermodynamic Stability

Polydentate ligands produce markedly more stable complexes than equivalent monodentate ligands (chelate effect), because entropy rises sharply when one polydentate replaces several monodentate ligands, releasing multiple particles into solution.

#### Worked Magnetic Moment

For [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻ (Fe²⁺, d⁶, strong-field CN⁻): all six electrons occupy t₂g → n = 0 → μ = √[0(2)] = **0 BM** (diamagnetic). Compare with [FeF₆]⁴⁻ (weak-field F⁻): high-spin d⁶ → n = 4 → μ = √[4·6] = **4.90 BM** (paramagnetic).

#### Colour and the Spectrochemical Series

The wavelength absorbed corresponds to Δₒ via Δₒ = hν. The observed colour is the **complementary** colour of the absorbed light. The spectrochemical ordering I⁻ < Br⁻ < Cl⁻ < F⁻ < H₂O < NH₃ < en < CN⁻ < CO explains why [Ni(H₂O)₆]²⁺ (green) and [Ni(en)₃]²⁺ (purple) differ in hue.

#### Practice Prompts

1. Name [Cr(NH₃)₄Cl₂]Cl and determine its CN, oxidation state, and expected geometry.
2. A complex of Co³⁺ (d⁶) with NH₃ is low-spin and diamagnetic. Identify the hybridisation and predict whether the same metal with F⁻ would behave identically.
