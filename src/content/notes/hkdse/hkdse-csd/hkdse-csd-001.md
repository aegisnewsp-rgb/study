# Theme 1 — Hong Kong under "One Country, Two Systems" — HKDSE CSD Notes

This topic covers the first of the three published themes in the HKDSE Citizenship and Social Development (CSD) curriculum. Theme 1 addresses the meaning and implementation of "one country, two systems"; the contemporary situation of the country and a sense of national identity; and the characteristics of cultural diversity of Hong Kong society. CSD replaced Liberal Studies as a HKDSE core subject from the 2024 examination. The HKEAA assessment is a 2-hour paper (100% of the subject mark) testing all three themes, with data-response and short-essay items.

> Verify the live CSD Assessment Framework and the National Security Education framework (updated 2025) on https://www.hkeaa.edu.hk/ before planning revision.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Topic 1 — meaning and implementation of "one country, two systems"**: the origin of the Hong Kong question (three unequal treaties and background); the process of Hong Kong's return to China; the constitutional relationship between the country and the HKSAR; the legal basis of "one country, two systems" and the Basic Law; the National Security Law and the holistic approach to national security; the political structure of the HKSAR; the significance of rule of law; and the fundamental rights and duties of Hong Kong residents under the Basic Law.
- **Topic 2 — national identity and cultural diversity**: the contemporary situation of the country (political structure, the Chinese nation, nationality, national symbols [flag, emblem, anthem]); the country's achievements; benefits and contributions of the HKSAR's participation in national affairs; characteristics of cultural diversity and its positive significance.
- **Assessment**: tested through data-response items (interpretation of source material) and short-essay items (250-400 words per essay) on the 2-hour CSD paper.

#### Examiner traps

- Treating "one country, two systems" as a slogan — the question requires specific knowledge of the constitutional basis, the Basic Law, and the National Security Law.
- Confusing the HKSAR political structure (Chief Executive, Executive Council, Legislative Council, Judiciary) with the country's political structure (National People's Congress, State Council, Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference).
- Ignoring the National Security Law — it is a core part of the curriculum from 2021 onwards.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Topic 1 — meaning and implementation of "one country, two systems"

**Origin of the Hong Kong question**: Hong Kong became a British colony through three unequal treaties — the Treaty of Nanking (1842, ceding Hong Kong Island), the Convention of Peking (1860, ceding Kowloon) and the Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory (1898, leasing the New Territories for 99 years). The 99-year lease's expiry in 1997 led to the negotiation of Hong Kong's return.

**Constitutional basis**: The Sino-British Joint Declaration (1984) set out the principles for the return. The Basic Law of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (promulgated 1990) is the constitutional document for the HKSAR. It codifies "one country, two systems", Hong Kong's status as part of China, and the HKSAR's high degree of autonomy in most areas except foreign affairs and defence.

**National security**: The National Security Law (NSL), enacted in June 2020 by the Standing Committee of the National People's Congress, criminalises acts of secession, subversion, terrorist activities and collusion with foreign forces. The NSL establishes the Committee for Safeguarding National Security of the HKSAR and the Office for Safeguarding National Security of the CPG in the HKSAR. The holistic approach to national security covers traditional security (military, political) and non-traditional security (economic, social, technological, ecological).

**HKSAR political structure**: The Chief Executive is the head of the HKSAR, appointed by the Central People's Government on the basis of election results. The Executive Council assists the Chief Executive in policy decisions. The Legislative Council (LegCo) enacts laws and approves taxation and public expenditure. The Judiciary is independent, with the Court of Final Appeal as the highest court.

**Rule of law**: equality before the law, judicial independence, fair and open trials, law-abidingness. The legal system in Hong Kong continues to follow the common law tradition inherited from British rule.

#### Topic 2 — national identity and cultural diversity

**The country's contemporary situation**: political structure (people's congress system, multi-party cooperation and political consultation led by the Communist Party of China); the Chinese nation as a unified concept; Chinese nationality; national symbols (the five-star red flag, the national emblem with Tiananmen Gate, the March of the Volunteers as the national anthem).

**The country's achievements**: new high-end technologies (5G, space exploration, AI); medical care and public health (e.g. response to COVID-19); culture and education (international student mobility); infrastructure (high-speed rail, the Belt and Road Initiative); poverty eradication (declared in 2021).

**HKSAR's participation in national affairs**: benefits include the country's policies supporting Hong Kong (the Greater Bay Area development, CEPA — Closer Economic Partnership Arrangement, support for Hong Kong as an international financial centre); contributions include Hong Kong promoting development and exchange with the Mainland (financial, legal, professional services), donations for natural disasters, support for the developing regions of the Mainland.

**Cultural diversity**: Hong Kong society has Chinese culture as the mainstay, with influences from the West and other parts of Asia. The positive significance includes promoting inclusion of cultures, facilitating cultural innovations and reflections, promoting harmony among different ethnic groups, and nurturing citizenship rooted in Chinese culture with a global perspective.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-topic links

- **Theme 2**: the development plans (Five-Year Plans, Greater Bay Area, CEPA) connect to Topic 2 of Theme 1.
- **Theme 3**: economic globalisation (Topic 1) and international affairs (Topic 3 of Theme 2) are integrated with Hong Kong's role under "one country, two systems".
- **National Security Education framework (2025 update)**: the CSD curriculum is the primary vehicle for national security education at senior secondary level.

#### Exam technique

- **For data-response items**: read the source carefully, identify the issue, distinguish fact from opinion, and structure the response (e.g. identify the issue, describe the context, analyse the causes, propose or evaluate solutions).
- **For short-essay items**: plan a 3-paragraph structure (introduction with a clear thesis, body paragraph with analysis, conclusion with a balanced view). 250-400 words is the typical range.
- **Quote the Basic Law or the National Security Law** where relevant — examiners reward specific citation.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Hong Kong is independent under 'one country, two systems'." No — Hong Kong is an inalienable part of China under the Basic Law. The HKSAR has a high degree of autonomy in most areas except foreign affairs and defence.
- "The Joint Declaration is still legally binding." The Joint Declaration was a bilateral treaty between China and the UK on the question of Hong Kong. The UK has no residual role in Hong Kong's administration after 1997.
- "National security is only about the military." No — the holistic approach includes economic security, social security, technological security and ecological security in addition to traditional political and military security.
