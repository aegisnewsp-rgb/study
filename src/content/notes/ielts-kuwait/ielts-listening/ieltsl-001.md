### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Format:** 10 questions. Question types: form completion, note completion, table completion, multiple choice, matching.
- **Speakers:** Two speakers in an everyday social context — for example introductions, booking travel, or finding accommodation.
- **Recording length:** Around 3–4 minutes. Played once. You hear the recording while reading the question paper.
- **Word limit:** Most answers are limited to **NO MORE THAN ONE WORD AND/OR A NUMBER**. Some questions allow up to three words.
- **Order:** Questions follow the order of the recording. If you miss an answer, move on — the next answer is still ahead of you.
- **Test centre delivery in Kuwait:** Computer-delivered IELTS is standard at British Council Kuwait (Salmiya) and IDP IELTS Kuwait (Mishref). Paper-based IELTS is offered on selected sittings — check availability for your test date.

#### The 5 question families in Part 1

1. **Form completion** — fill in blanks on a form (name, address, postcode, telephone, etc.).
2. **Note completion** — fill in blanks in a set of notes (often with a heading + sub-points).
3. **Table completion** — fill in blanks in a table (categories + missing details).
4. **Multiple choice** — choose the correct option (3 options A/B/C or 5 options A-E).
5. **Matching** — match a list of items to a list of categories, speakers, or places.

#### 6 examiner traps students fall into in Part 1

1. **Spelling trap:** A word is spelled aloud but the speaker corrects themselves partway through. Write the **second (corrected) spelling**, not the first.
2. **Plural trap:** The speaker says "ticket" but the answer blank requires "tickets". Listen for the plural marker.
3. **Number trap:** The speaker says "two" but the form has a number slot. Write **2** as a digit, not the word.
4. **Same word trap:** Two blanks in a row require words that sound similar (e.g. "post code" vs "postcard"). Slow down for the second blank.
5. **Distractor trap:** The speaker gives two answers — only the second one fits the blank. The first answer is a distractor (a wrong answer given on purpose to test listening).
6. **Capitalisation trap:** Proper nouns (place names, street names) require capital letters. IELTS accepts either capital or lower case, but consistency helps.

### 🟡 Standard — 1 Week Study Plan

#### Method: a 7-step routine for Part 1 mastery

1. **Read the questions first (30 seconds).** Before the recording plays, skim the questions + identify blanks + predict the word type (noun / number / name).
2. **Highlight signal words.** Listen for: *and*, *sorry*, *actually*, *I meant*, *let's see*, *oh wait* — these signal corrections and the real answer.
3. **Write what you hear, not what you understand.** If the speaker says "September 25th", write "September 25" — do not paraphrase.
4. **Use the word limit strictly. If the instruction says NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS, write 1 or 2 words, not 3.** Going over the limit = mark wrong.
5. **Check plurals and articles.** "an" vs "a" matters for grammar, but IELTS accepts either spelling. Focus on plurals.
6. **Use the 10-second gap between questions.** Each answer is followed by a pause — use it to check spelling + word limit + move to the next blank.
7. **Re-check answers in the final 2 minutes.** The 10-minute transfer time at the end is for copying answers from the question paper to the answer sheet (paper-based) or for final review (computer-delivered).

#### 9-item pre-submit checklist for Part 1

- [ ] Every blank filled (or skipped to move on).
- [ ] Spelling matches the speaker's pronunciation (NOT your dialect).
- [ ] Plural markers are correct (tickets, not ticket).
- [ ] Numbers are written as digits (25, not twenty-five).
- [ ] Word limit is respected (NO MORE THAN ONE WORD AND/OR A NUMBER).
- [ ] Capitalisation is consistent (street names, place names).
- [ ] No paraphrasing (write exactly what you hear).
- [ ] No changed answers in doubt (first instinct is usually right).
- [ ] No skipped answers at the end (re-check every blank in the final 2 minutes).

### 💡 Pro Tips

1. **Practice with UK, US, AU, and NZ accents.** IELTS uses a range of English accents — including non-native accents. Familiarity with all four major native accents is essential.
2. **Practice with the official IELTS Listening practice tests.** Cambridge IELTS 15/16/17 Academic contains 4 full Listening tests with answer keys.
3. **Don't memorise scripts.** Part 1 is conversational and unscripted in the test — practice with authentic audio.
4. **Listen to BBC Radio 4 and ABC Radio National.** These are common sources of IELTS-style Part 1 audio.
5. **Listen to podcasts at 0.8x speed.** Slowing down a recording lets you catch details you might miss at normal speed.
6. **Predict the answer type before listening.** This primes your ear and reduces surprise.
7. **Use the pause between questions wisely.** Each answer is followed by a pause — use it to check spelling + word limit + move on.
8. **Re-check answers in the final 2 minutes.** The 10-minute transfer time at the end is for paper-based, or for final review (computer-delivered).
9. **Don't change answers in doubt.** First instinct is usually right — changing answers raises questions.
10. **Bring your own headphones (if allowed).** Some test centres allow personal headphones; check with the test centre in advance.
