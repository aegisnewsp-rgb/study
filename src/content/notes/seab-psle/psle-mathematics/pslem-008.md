# Problem Solving with Heuristics — PSLE Mathematics Notes

Problem Solving with Heuristics is the cross-strand topic that runs through every PSLE Mathematics paper. The MOE Primary Mathematics syllabus names a specific set of ten heuristics — draw a model, make a list, look for pattern, work backwards, guess and check, before-after, part-whole, comparison, supposition, and simultaneous-equations form — and the PSLE assessment explicitly tests a student's ability to choose and apply the right heuristic to a non-routine word problem. Heuristic word problems are the heaviest single paper-2 load at Primary 6 and the most reliable source of marks lost to mis-classification.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### The MOE heuristics set

1. **Draw a model** — bar model, number-line model, or part-whole model.
2. **Make a list / make a systematic list** — enumerate the possibilities.
3. **Look for a pattern** — extend the pattern and apply the rule.
4. **Work backwards** — undo each step from the final state to find the start.
5. **Guess and check** — start with a reasonable guess, then refine.
6. **Before-after** — compare the state before and after a change.
7. **Part-whole** — identify a part, the whole and the rest.
8. **Comparison** — compare two quantities by ratio or difference.
9. **Supposition** — assume a value, work out the difference, then adjust.
10. **Simultaneous-equations form** — set up two unknowns and two equations.

#### Key heuristic selection rules

- "Had some, gave away Y, received Z more" → **before-after** or **part-whole**.
- "Find all possibilities" → **make a systematic list**.
- "Reverse the steps to find the start" → **work backwards**.
- "Total is X, A is more than B by Y" → **comparison** or **simultaneous-equations form**.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Bar model

The bar model is the workhorse heuristic of the MOE syllabus. Each bar represents a quantity; each labelled segment represents a known or unknown part. Two common forms:

**Part-whole form**: a whole bar split into parts. If a whole = sum of parts, the parts add to the whole.

**Comparison form**: two bars, one longer than the other, with the difference labelled. Used when "A is more than B by X" or "B is less than A by X".

**Before-after form**: two side-by-side bars, one for the state before the change and one for the state after.

Example: Ali has 24 more marbles than Bala. After Ali gives Bala 18 marbles, Ali has 6 more marbles than Bala. How many marbles did each have at first?

- Draw the before-state bar: Ali = Bala + 24.
- Draw the after-state bar: Ali' = Bala' + 6, where Ali' = Ali − 18 and Bala' = Bala + 18.
- Total of marbles is unchanged: Ali + Bala = Ali' + Bala'.
- Substituting: (Ali − 18) + (Bala + 18) = Ali + Bala ✓ (the total does not move).
- Use the after-state: Ali − 18 = Bala + 18 + 6 → Ali − Bala = 42 → Ali = Bala + 42.
- But Ali = Bala + 24 at the start — contradiction unless the given numbers are read carefully.
- Re-read: "After Ali gives Bala 18 marbles, Ali has 6 more marbles than Bala." At start, Ali has 24 more. Giving 18 to Bala moves 18 from Ali to Bala, so the difference shrinks by 36. Starting difference 24 → ending difference 24 − 36 = −12. So Bala has 12 more than Ali at the end, not 6.

This kind of careful re-reading is the heuristic skill the PSLE paper tests. A confident bar model with a wrong reading is still a wrong answer; the model supports the reasoning, it does not replace it.

#### Make a systematic list

When the question asks "find all possibilities", enumerate them. Use a table or tree diagram to avoid missing cases. Example: a 3-digit code uses digits 1, 2, 3 with no repeats. List all possible codes. There are 3 × 2 × 1 = 6 codes: 123, 132, 213, 231, 312, 321.

#### Look for a pattern

Identify a repeating relationship and extend it. Example: a sequence starts 2, 5, 11, 23, … What is the 10th term? Each term is "double the previous, plus 1". The 5th term is 47, the 6th is 95, … the 10th is 1 535. Write the pattern rule as a formula (e.g. aₙ = 2 aₙ₋₁ + 1) before extending.

#### Work backwards

Undo each step in reverse to find the starting state. Example: a number is halved, then 6 is subtracted, then the result is 10. What was the original number? Work backwards: 10 + 6 = 16, then 16 × 2 = 32. The original number was 32.

#### Guess and check

Useful when the answer is constrained but not obvious. Make an initial guess, compute, then refine. Example: a rectangle has length 5 cm more than its width, and its perimeter is 50 cm. Guess width 10 cm, length 15 cm, perimeter 50 cm. The guess is correct; width = 10 cm, length = 15 cm.

#### Supposition and simultaneous-equations form

Use supposition when the totals are mixed (e.g. a basket of apples and oranges). Assume all items are apples, compute the difference, then adjust. The algebra form is simultaneous-equations: a + b = total; a − b = difference → solve.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where Heuristics appears across the paper

- **PSLE Mathematics Paper 2**: every open-ended word problem is a heuristic question. The longer the problem statement, the more likely it tests heuristic selection rather than arithmetic.
- **PSLE Mathematics Paper 1**: heuristic questions appear as short structured items, often disguised as a two-step word problem.
- **Across both papers**: choosing the wrong heuristic is the most common reason a confident student loses marks. Re-read the question after the model is drawn.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "The bar model is the answer." The bar model is the method. The arithmetic and the conclusion are the answer.
- "Work backwards means reverse the order of the words." It means reverse the order of the operations.
- "Make a list is for counting problems only." It is also for problems where the constraints rule out possibilities one by one (constraint-satisfaction problems).
- "Look for a pattern means find the next term." It often means find the general rule (e.g. the nth-term formula) and apply it.
- "Supposition is guessing." It is structured guessing with a fixed assumption; the difference between the assumed total and the actual total is then distributed by a unit-rate correction.

#### Exam technique

- For bar-model questions, draw the bars first, label the parts, then write the arithmetic. The model is the method mark.
- For "find all possibilities" questions, lay out the cases in a table or tree diagram before counting. The marker can then award partial credit for the enumeration even if one case is missed.
- For work-backwards questions, write each undo-step on a separate line. The marker can then distinguish a "wrong direction" error from a "computational" error.
- For pattern questions, state the pattern rule in words before extending. The marker can then award partial credit even if the extension slips.
