# Pakistan's International Relations

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Pakistan's International Relations covers the country's foreign policy, bilateral ties, and multilateral memberships since 1947. The foreign-policy trinity is **national security, economic development, and ideological solidarity with the Muslim world**.

- **Founding memberships:** United Nations (1947), OIC (1969/1974), SAARC (1985, Dhaka Declaration).
- **All-weather partnership:** China — formalized 1950, deepened by the **China-Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC)** signed in 2013 and operationalized from 2015, anchored at **Gwadar Port**.
- **Nuclear milestone:** Public nuclear tests conducted on **28–30 May 1998**, following India; doctrine of **minimum credible deterrence**.
- **Kashmir dispute:** Resolution anchored in **UN Security Council resolutions** and the Simla Agreement (1972).
- **FATF:** Placed on grey list June 2018, exited in **October 2022** after action-plan compliance.
- **Common exam traps:** SAARC HQ is **Kathmandu**, OIC HQ is **Jeddah**, Pakistan is **not** a permanent UNSC member.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Foundational principles

Pakistan's foreign policy is constitutionally and ideologically rooted in the **Objectives Resolution (1949)**, embedded in the Constitution, which anchors support for Muslim causes globally. Three objectives consistently guide diplomacy: territorial security (vis-à-vis India), economic development (aid, trade, energy), and **Muslim-world solidarity** (Palestine, Kashmir, intra-OIC coordination).

#### Key bilateral relationships

- **India:** Adversarial since 1947; pivots around Kashmir, the **Indus Waters Treaty (1960)**, the **Simla Agreement (1972)**, and the 1998 nuclear standoff. Composite Dialogue framework has been intermittently suspended.
- **China:** Described as a "**strategic cooperative partnership**"; CPEC is the flagship of the Belt and Road Initiative (BRI), with routes through **Gwadar, Kashgar, Khunjerab Pass**, and special economic zones.
- **United States:** Alliance-driven by Cold War pacts (**SEATO 1954, CENTO 1955** — both dissolved), post-2001 cooperation on counter-terrorism, and recurrent civilian/military aid packages.
- **Saudi Arabia and UAE:** Labour remittances, oil on deferred payment, and coordination in OIC; close defence ties include the **Joint Ministerial Commission**.
- **Afghanistan:** Strained by the **Durand Line (1893)** dispute, the 2021 Taliban takeover, and cross-border TTP activity.

#### Multilateral platforms

Pakistan operates through the **UN General Assembly, OIC, SAARC, ECO, SCO, and the Non-Aligned Movement (NAM)**. Its OIC role has repeatedly translated into resolutions on Kashmir and Palestine.

> Tip: NLT often asks "founder of which body?" — memorise 1947 (UN), 1969 (OIC), 1985 (SAARC), and 2017 (SCO full membership).

#### Common exam mistakes

- Mixing up **SEATO/CENTO** (dissolved, Western-backed) with **SCO** (active, Russia–China-led).
- Attributing SAARC's founding solely to Pakistan — it was a **seven-nation Dhaka Declaration**.
- Confusing CPEC's 2013/2015 launch with the broader **BRI** (also 2013, by China).

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Nuclear doctrine and deterrence

Pakistan's nuclear doctrine, never formally codified, rests on **full-spectrum deterrence** with **minimum credible deterrence (MCD)** as the public articulation. The 1998 tests (Chagai-I on 28 May, Chagai-II on 30 May) made Pakistan the **seventh declared nuclear state**. Civilian oversight sits with the **PAEC**; selected facilities are under **IAEA safeguards**, distinguishing them from unsafeguarded weapons-grade assets. **Nasr (Hatf-IX)** and **Shaheen** series missiles underpin tactical and strategic counter-strike capability.

#### Economic diplomacy and FATF trajectory

Trade diplomacy includes **GSP Plus status** from the EU (granted 2014, valid through 2027 contingent on 27 conventions), FTAs with China and Malaysia, and preferential access under **SAFTA**. The **FATF grey-listing (June 2018)** imposed compliance costs estimated at billions in lost GDP; remediation through 38 action-plan items led to **removal in October 2022**. Pakistan remains on the **EU's list of high-risk third countries** for AML/CFT.

#### Strategic depth and the Afghanistan file

The doctrine of **"strategic depth"** — influence in Kabul via a friendly government — shaped Pakistan's Afghan policy from the 1990s. The **2021 Taliban takeover** reopened debates on recognition, refugee management (over **1.4 million Afghan refugees** documented by UNHCR), and counter-terror coordination with the TTP.

#### Defence and multilateral exercises

Joint exercises include **Shaheen (China), Friendship (China-Navies), AMAN (multinational, Karachi)**. Pakistan has been a **major non-NATO ally** of the US since 2004 and conducts defence purchases from China, Turkey, and the US.
