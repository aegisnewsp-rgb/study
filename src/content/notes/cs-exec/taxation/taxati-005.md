# TDS and TCS

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**TDS** (Tax Deducted at Source) is the obligation under **Chapter XVII-B (Sections 192–194N)** of the Income-tax Act, 1961 cast on the **deductor** to withhold income-tax at prescribed rates while making payments like salary, rent, contractor fees, commission, interest, lottery winnings, and immovable property transfers. **TCS** (Tax Collected at Source) under **Section 206C** is the mirror obligation on the **seller (collector)** to collect tax from the **buyer (collectee)** at the time of sale of specified goods, motor vehicles above ₹10 lakh, overseas tour packages, or goods above ₹50 lakh under Section 206C(1H).

#### Must-know formulas

| Transaction | Formula | Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Salary u/s 192 | Tax on estimated taxable income ÷ months remaining | Every salary payment |
| Contractor u/s 194C | Payment × 1% (individual/HUF) or 2% (others) | ₹30,000 single / ₹1,00,000 aggregate |
| Immovable property u/s 194-IA | Consideration × 1% | Above ₹50 lakh |
| Purchase of goods u/s 194Q | Purchase value × 0.1% | Buyer turnover > ₹10 cr; aggregate > ₹50 lakh |
| Sale of goods u/s 206C(1H) | Sale consideration × 0.1% | Seller turnover > ₹10 cr; aggregate > ₹50 lakh |
| Non-filer higher rate u/s 206AB/206CCA | Higher of 2× prescribed rate OR rate in force + 5% | TDS > ₹50,000 / TCS > ₹10,000 |

> **Exam tip:** For overlapping purchase-and-sale situations, only **one** provision applies — the one that triggers first in point of time (CBDT Circular 17/2020).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Architecture of the regime

TDS and TCS together form a *pay-as-you-earn* mechanism that shifts tax collection to the point of payment. Section 4 read with Sections 190–206C creates a parallel liability on the payer/seller even though the ultimate incidence falls on the recipient. For the company secretary, this translates into operational duties: obtaining **TAN (Section 203A)**, deducting/collecting at the right rate, depositing through **challan 281**, and issuing quarterly statements plus annual returns.

#### Time of deduction, deposit, and return filing

The deductor must deduct tax at the **earlier of credit or actual payment** (Section 191 read with rule). Deposit with the central government is due by the **7th of the following month** for non-government deductors, the **same day** using challan-cum-statement for government deductors, and **30 April** for March transactions of non-government deductors. Quarterly statements go in via **Form 24Q (salary), 26Q (others resident), 27Q (non-resident), and 27EQ (TCS)**; annual return by **31 May**.

| Section | Who is deductor/collector | Common exam trap |
|---|---|---|
| 192 | Employer | Forgetting standard deduction of ₹50,000 |
| 194C | Payer of contractor fees | Mixing 1% vs 2% and threshold limits |
| 194-IA | Transferee (buyer) | Applies above ₹50 lakh only |
| 194Q | Buyer (turnover > ₹10 cr) | First trigger rule with 206C(1H) |
| 194M | Individual/HUF paying contractor/professional | Cash payments above ₹1 cr/₹50 lakh aggregate |
| 206C(1H) | Seller (turnover > ₹10 cr) | Overlap with 194Q — only one survives |

#### Non-filer higher rate

Where the deductee/collectee has **not filed returns** for the relevant previous year and TDS/TCS exceeds ₹50,000/₹10,000, the deductor/collector must apply the higher of **twice the prescribed rate** or **rate in force + 5%** under Sections 206AB and 206CCA.

> **Exam pointer:** A 5–15 mark question frequently asks the student to compute TDS/TCS across two or three sections and reconcile the deposit due date — memorise the 7th-of-next-month rule and the 30 April March carve-out.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Interest, penalty, and disallowance mechanics

Failure to deduct or deposit attracts **interest under Section 201(1A)** at 1% per month (or part) on TDS and 1.5% per month on TCS, calculated from the date of default until actual payment. Late filing of the quarterly statement triggers **fee under Section 234E** at ₹200 per day (capped at the TDS amount). Failure to issue TDS certificates (Form 16/16A/27D) within the statutory window exposes the deductor to penalty under Section 272A.

Where TDS is deductible but not deducted or deposited, **Section 40(a)(ia)/(ib)** disallows **100%** of the expenditure in the year of payment — a frequent miscalculation in practice (students often wrongly restrict disallowance to 30%). The corresponding addition to income is reversed in the year the deductor finally complies.

#### Interplay between 194Q and 206C(1H)

CBDT Circular 17/2020 resolved ambiguity: where the same transaction could attract both provisions, **only one applies — the one that comes into operation first in point of time**. Sequence depends on whether credit, payment, or sale occurred earlier. Practitioners must document the chronology to defend the chosen section.

#### Worked micro-example

A Ltd (turnover ₹18 cr) sells goods worth ₹80 lakh to B Pvt Ltd on 15 August. B has filed returns for the prior year; A has filed for two years but skipped FY 2022-23, and A's TCS collected from B will be ₹35,000.

**Step 1** — Section 194Q trigger: B's turnover exceeds ₹10 cr and aggregate purchase crosses ₹50 lakh → TDS u/s 194Q at 0.1% on ₹80 lakh = **₹8,000**.

**Step 2** — Section 206C(1H) trigger: A's turnover exceeds ₹10 cr → A must collect TCS at 0.1% on the same ₹80 lakh = **₹8,000**.

**Step 3** — Apply first-in-time test. If B paid on 18 August (credit earlier on 15 August), 194Q was triggered on 15 August → **only 194Q applies**, and 206C(1H) is not attracted. A need not collect TCS.

**Step 4** — Deposit due by **7 September** through challan 281 with the correct TAN and nature of payment code.

> **Practice prompts:**
> 1. Compute TDS where a resident individual contractor is paid ₹3,50,000 in one lump sum and ₹4,00,000 in instalments during the year — apply Section 194C thresholds.
> 2. Mr X buys a flat for ₹72 lakh from a resident individual. State the deductor, rate, threshold, deposit date, and the form in which the certificate must be issued.
