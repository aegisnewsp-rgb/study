# Neoplasia

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Neoplasia** is an abnormal, autonomous, uncontrolled, progressive proliferation of cells that serves no physiological purpose and persists after removal of the initiating stimulus. A **neoplasm** is the resulting mass of tissue; **benign** tumours remain localised, while **malignant** tumours invade and metastasise. INI CET (AIIMS PG) commonly tests nomenclature, the hallmark features of malignancy, and the carcinogen-cancer associations.

- **Definition triad**: autonomous + uncontrolled + progressive growth.
- **Key dichotomies**: benign vs malignant; differentiation vs anaplasia; grading vs staging; invasion vs metastasis.
- **Malignant suffixes**: carcinoma (epithelial), sarcoma (mesenchymal), and exceptions — melanoma, seminoma, lymphoma, leukaemia, glioma, hepatoma.

| Feature | Benign | Malignant |
|---|---|---|
| Growth rate | Slow, expansile | Rapid, infiltrative |
| Differentiation | Well-differentiated | Poorly differentiated / anaplastic |
| Metastasis | Absent | Present |
| Capsule | Often present | Usually absent |
| Nuclear features | Mild atypia | Pleomorphism, hyperchromasia, atypical mitoses |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook — "MASSP of Malignancy":** **M**etastasis, **A**naplasia, **S**tructural atypia, **S**econdary effects (cachexia, paraneoplastic), **P**oor differentiation. If any one is missing, think benign first. Nomenclature trick: **"Malignant -oma"** exceptions → **M**elanoma, **S**eminoma, **L**ymphoma, **L**eukaemia, **G**lioma, **H**epatoma — remember **"MSLLGH never benign."**

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Nomenclature — Suffixes and Exceptions

The suffix encodes tissue of origin and behaviour. **Benign** mesenchymal and epithelial tumours end in **-oma** preceded by the cell type (lipoma, chondroma, adenoma, papilloma). **Malignant epithelial** tumours are **carcinomas** (squamous cell carcinoma, adenocarcinoma); **malignant mesenchymal** tumours are **sarcomas** (osteosarcoma, leiomyosarcoma, liposarcoma).

Six malignant tumours break the rule by retaining the **-oma** suffix: **melanoma, seminoma, lymphoma, leukaemia, glioma, and hepatoma**. In addition, mixed tumours (pleomorphic adenoma) and the misnamed "benign" sounding lesions such as **invasive mole** and **hydatidiform mole** (gestational trophoblastic disease) carry malignant potential.

#### Hallmarks of Malignancy on Histology

**Anaplasia** = lack of differentiation. Cytologic features include **pleomorphism** (variable cell/nuclear size and shape), **hyperchromatic nuclei**, **high nuclear-to-cytoplasmic (N:C) ratio**, **prominent nucleoli**, **bizarre giant cells**, and **atypical mitotic figures** (tripolar, ring forms). Architectural disorganisation accompanies these cytologic changes. **Dysplasia** is disordered growth that is *pre-neoplastic* and potentially reversible; once basement membrane is breached, it becomes **carcinoma in situ → invasive carcinoma**.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix

| Concept | Definition | Key Distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Differentiation | How closely tumour cells resemble parent tissue | Grading parameter |
| Anaplasia | Complete loss of differentiation | Hallmark of malignancy |
| Dysplasia | Disordered, atypical but non-autonomous growth | Pre-neoplastic, reversible |
| Carcinoma in situ | Dysplastic cells above intact basement membrane | Non-invasive; cannot metastasise |
| Invasion | Breach of basement membrane by malignant cells | Required before metastasis |
| Metastasis | Spread to a distant, discontinuous site | Strictly a malignant property |

#### Grading vs Staging

- **Grading** = histologic assessment of differentiation. Broders (I–IV) for squamous cell carcinoma; Nottingham (tubule formation, nuclear pleomorphism, mitotic count) for breast carcinoma; Gleason for prostate adenocarcinoma.
- **Staging** (TNM) = anatomic extent. **T** = size/local invasion, **N** = regional lymph node involvement, **M** = distant metastasis. Stage grouping I–IV. **Staging outweighs grading in prognosis.**

#### Routes of Metastasis

| Route | Vehicle | Common Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Lymphatic | Tumour emboli in lymphatics | Carcinomas (breast → axillary nodes) |
| Haematogenous | Blood-borne tumour emboli | Sarcomas, renal cell carcinoma, hepatocellular carcinoma |
| Transcoelomic | Seeding across serosal surfaces | Ovarian carcinoma → peritoneum; gastric carcinoma → Krukenberg to ovary |
| Perineural | Along nerve sheaths | Prostatic adenocarcinoma, pancreatic adenocarcinoma |

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students swap lymphatic and haematogenous spread — **carcinomas go via lymphatics first, sarcomas via blood**. Sentinel lymph node biopsy is relevant only for carcinomas.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** A 55-year-old chronic hepatitis B carrier develops a liver mass. Serum alpha-fetoprotein is markedly elevated. Histology shows trabeculae of polygonal cells with bile plugs, traversed by sinusoidal capillaries. Which of the following is the most likely aetiological agent and the gene most commonly mutated by this agent?

(A) Aflatoxin B1 — *KRAS* mutation
(B) Hepatitis B virus — *TP53* mutation
(C) Aflatoxin B1 — *TP53* mutation at codon 249
(D) Hepatitis C virus — *RB1* mutation
(E) Aflatoxin B1 — *APC* mutation

#### Solution:
Step 1 — Identify the tumour: trabecular architecture with bile and sinusoidal capillaries is **hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)**.
Step 2 — Identify the risk factor: chronic **HBV** carrier status plus elevated **AFP** strongly supports HCC.
Step 3 — Recall the classical association: **aflatoxin B1** (from *Aspergillus flavus* contaminating stored grains/peanuts) causes a characteristic **G→T transversion mutation at codon 249 of *TP53***, synergising with HBV in hepatocarcinogenesis.
Step 4 — Eliminate distractors: KRAS is mutated in pancreatic/colorectal cancers; RB1 in retinoblastoma/osteosarcoma; APC in colorectal FAP.
**Answer: (C) Aflatoxin B1 — *TP53* mutation at codon 249.**

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Many candidates link aflatoxin with HCC correctly but forget the *codon 249* specificity; without it, option (B) becomes a tempting distractor. Memorise **"Aflatoxin → codon 249 of p53 → HCC."**

#### Carcinogen–Cancer Map (must memorise)

- **HPV** (E6 inactivates p53, E7 inactivates RB) → cervical, oropharyngeal, anal squamous carcinoma.
- **EBV** → Burkitt lymphoma (t8;14 *MYC*), Hodgkin lymphoma, nasopharyngeal carcinoma, post-transplant lymphoproliferative disease.
- **H. pylori** → gastric **MALT lymphoma**, gastric adenocarcinoma.
- **HBV/HCV** → hepatocellular carcinoma.
- **HTLV-1** → adult T-cell leukaemia/lymphoma.
- **HIV** → Kaposi sarcoma (HHV-8 co-factor), CNS lymphoma.
- **Aflatoxin B1** → HCC (*TP53* codon 249).
- **Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons** (tobacco smoke, soot) → lung, skin, bladder.
- **Nitrosamines** (smoked foods) → gastric carcinoma.
- **Asbestos** → mesothelioma, bronchogenic carcinoma.
- **UV-B** → pyrimidine dimers → skin (BCC, SCC, melanoma).

#### Paraneoplastic Syndromes

Remote effects unrelated to invasion or metastasis, caused by tumour-secreted hormones, antibodies or cytokines.

| Syndrome | Tumour | Mediator |
|---|---|---|
| SIADH (hyponatraemia) | Small cell lung carcinoma | ADH |
| Cushing syndrome | Small cell lung carcinoma | ACTH |
| Hypercalcaemia | Squamous cell lung, breast, RCC, myeloma | PTHrP / osteolysis |
| Trousseau sign (migratory thrombophlebitis) | Pancreatic adenocarcinoma | Mucins activating coagulation |
| Lambert-Eaton myasthenic syndrome | Small cell lung carcinoma | Anti-voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channel Ab |
| Polycythaemia | Renal cell carcinoma, HCC | EPO |

#### Tumour Markers Snapshot

**AFP** → HCC, yolk sac tumour, mixed germ cell tumour. **β-hCG** → choriocarcinoma, seminoma (some). **CEA** → colorectal carcinoma (monitoring). **PSA** → prostatic carcinoma. **CA-125** → ovarian carcinoma. **CA 19-9** → pancreatic carcinoma. **LDH** → lymphoma, germ cell tumours (prognostic).

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Molecular Carcinogenesis in Depth

Cancer arises from accumulated **non-lethal genetic damage** (DNA mutations, amplifications, translocations, deletions, epigenetic silencing). Two functional classes of genes dominate:

- **Oncogenes** — gain-of-function, dominant. Examples: *KRAS, NRAS* (GTPase signalling), *MYC* (transcription factor), *ERBB2/HER2, EGFR* (receptor tyrosine kinases), *BCL2* (anti-apoptotic, t(14;18) in follicular lymphoma), *ABL* (t(9;22) BCR-ABL in CML). Activation by **point mutation, translocation, amplification**.
- **Tumour suppressor genes** — loss-of-function, generally recessive (two-hit hypothesis, Knudson). Examples: ***TP53*** ("guardian of the genome," most frequently mutated gene in human cancer), ***RB1*** (retinoblastoma, osteosarcoma), *BRCA1/2* (breast/ovarian), *APC* (colorectal/FAP), *NF1* (neurofibromin, neurofibromatosis 1), *WT1* (Wilms tumour), *VHL* (renal cell, von Hippel-Lindau), *DCC, SMAD4/DPC4*.

Hanahan and Weinberg's **eight hallmarks of cancer** (2011 update) — sustained proliferative signalling, evasion of growth suppressors, resistance to cell death, replicative immortality, angiogenesis, invasion/metastasis, deregulated cellular energetics, and evasion of immune destruction — should be quoted verbatim when a 5-mark essay is asked.

#### Tumour Kinetics

Three parameters govern bulk growth:

1. **Doubling time** — interval for tumour cell mass to double.
2. **Growth fraction (GF)** — proportion of cells actively cycling (high in early disease and aggressive tumours).
3. **Rate of cell loss** — through apoptosis, necrosis, differentiation.

A clinically detectable tumour (~1 cm³ ≈ 10⁹ cells) has usually completed **~30 doublings**. Total life-span before host death ≈ 40 doublings. Early lesions have high GF; chemotherapy sensitivity correlates with high GF.

#### Cachexia and Systemic Effects

**Cachexia** = anorexia, weight loss, muscle wasting, anaemia. Mediated by tumour and host cytokines: **TNF-α ("cachectin"), IL-1, IL-6**, and proteolysis-inducing factor. Independent of nutritional intake; not reversed by feeding.

#### Advanced Traps and Edge Cases

1. **Tumour heterogeneity** — subclonal evolution during progression means a single biopsy may not represent the whole lesion; explains treatment resistance.
2. **Dormancy** — micrometastases can remain clinically silent for years before angiogenic switch (a 2nd hallmark).
3. **Carcinoma in situ cannot metastasise** because it has not breached the basement membrane; lymphovascular invasion marks the transition.
4. **Tumour immunity paradox** — tumour antigens (TSA, TAA) elicit CD8+ and NK responses, yet tumours escape via MHC-I downregulation, antigen loss variants, and immunosuppressive cytokines (TGF-β, IL-10).
5. **Germ cell tumour exceptions** — seminoma (malignant despite "-oma") and the embryonal carcinoma/yolk sac/choriocarcinoma triad (markers AFP, β-hCG, LDH).
6. **Mixed tumours** — pleomorphic adenoma (salivary) is benign but has malignant potential; carcinosarcoma shows both epithelial and mesenchymal malignant components.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. A 60-year-old smoker presents with weight loss, proximal muscle weakness, and a lung mass. Serum sodium is 124 mmol/L (low), potassium 3.0 mmol/L (low), and morning cortisol is elevated with suppressed ACTH. Identify the tumour, the syndrome, and the mediator.
2. Explain why a basal cell carcinoma of the skin rarely metastasises despite being histologically malignant, and contrast this with squamous cell carcinoma and melanoma of the same region.
