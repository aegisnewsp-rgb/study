# Political Science Concepts

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Politics** is the authoritative allocation of values for a society (David Easton). The **State** is a political community founded on four elements — population, territory, government, and **sovereignty** — and is permanent. **Government** is the temporary agency through which the State acts. **Sovereignty** has two faces: internal supremacy over all authorities within, and external independence from outside powers.

**Legality** means accordance with law; **legitimacy** means moral acceptance by the people (Max Weber). The Indian Constitution is **written, rigid, federal, and parliamentary**, with quasi-federal tilt in normal times and strong unitary bias during emergencies (Articles 352, 356, 360). **Fundamental Rights** (Part III) are justiciable; **Directive Principles** (Part IV) are non-justiciable but fundamental in governance.

- Highest-yield pairs for UPPSC Prelims: *State vs Government*, *Legality vs Legitimacy*, *FR vs DPSP*.
- Indian secularism = principled distance, not strict Western separation.
- K.C. Wheare labelled India's structure **quasi-federal**.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Vocabulary

Politics, State, Government, Sovereignty, Power, Authority, and Legitimacy form the foundational grammar of Indian Polity. David Easton defined politics as the **authoritative allocation of values** for an entire society, distinguishing it from power struggles within a family or firm. Aristotle's classical definition of the State requires population, territory, government, and sovereignty. The State is permanent and impersonal; the Government is its temporary agent — change the ruling party and you change the government, not the state.

#### Sovereignty and Authority

| Concept | Core Idea | Key Thinker |
|---|---|---|
| Legal Sovereignty | Whose command ultimately obeys law | John Austin |
| Political Sovereignty | Effective power behind the legal form | Harold Laski |
| Traditional Authority | Ruled by long-established custom | Max Weber |
| Rational-Legal Authority | Ruled by codified rules and procedures | Max Weber |
| Charismatic Authority | Ruled by personal magnetism of leader | Max Weber |

**Legality** (lawful in procedure) and **legitimacy** (accepted as just) often diverge — a lawfully passed Act may lack legitimacy if citizens view it as unjust, and vice versa.

#### Constitution vs Constitutionalism

A **Constitution** is the supreme written law (India's runs across the Preamble, 395 Articles, and 12 Schedules). **Constitutionalism** is a principle of limited government — rulers must operate within constitutional bounds, and an independent judiciary can strike down excesses. India is parliamentary (executive drawn from legislature), federal in form (Seventh Schedule division of powers), but **quasi-federal** in spirit (K.C. Wheare).

#### Liberty, Equality, Rights

**Liberty** has two strands: **negative liberty** (Berlin — absence of external restraint, Articles 19–20) and **positive liberty** (capacity to act effectively, Article 21). **Equality** spans legal (Article 14), political (Article 325), social, and economic dimensions. **Fundamental Rights** are justiciable through writs under Article 32/226; **Directive Principles** (Articles 36–51) guide policy but cannot be enforced directly.

> **Exam trap:** "FRs are absolute" is wrong. Articles 19(2), 25(4), and 31A allow reasonable restrictions. DPSPs are non-justiciable but, per Article 37, "fundamental in the governance of the country."

#### UPPSC Question Patterns

- **Direct definition:** "Define political sovereignty" / "Define legitimacy."
- **Match-the-following:** State (permanent) vs Government (temporary) elements.
- **Statement-based MCQ:** "Consider the following statements about Directive Principles…" — typically tests justiciability, Article 37 status, or relationship with FRs.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Adjacent Concepts and Subtle Distinctions

**Power** is the raw ability to influence outcomes; **authority** is that power cloaked in recognized right; **influence** is informal persuasion without command. The triad shapes how a welfare state like India (Article 38: social, economic, and political justice) translates policy into compliance.

**Civil society** (Hegel, Gramsci) is the non-state sphere of voluntary associations — trade unions, NGOs, media, religious bodies — that mediates between citizen and state. A strong civil society is the informal check on majoritarian excess and complements constitutionalism.

#### Federalism, Secularism, Welfare State

India's **federalism** is asymmetric — Articles 371, special status under Article 370 (now abrogated), and Sixth Schedule tribal areas create uneven autonomy. In emergencies, Articles 352, 356, and 360 convert the Union into a near-unitary actor, contradicting the textbook federal ideal.

**Indian secularism** differs from the American strict-separation model: there is no state religion, but the state intervenes to reform religious practice (Hindu Marriage Act, 1955; abolition of untouchability under Article 17). This is called **principled distance** — equal respect plus active reform.

A **Welfare State** commits the government to economic security, social safety nets, and public goods. Article 38, 39, 41, and 42 translate this commitment into DPSP language; Article 21's right to life has been read to include livelihood, shelter, and education through landmark cases like *Unni Krishnan v. State of AP* (1993).

#### Common Mistakes Candidates Make

| Mistake | Correction |
|---|---|
| Equating Constitution with Constitutionalism | Constitution = document; Constitutionalism = practice of limited government |
| Calling India a "pure" federation | K.C. Wheare: quasi-federal; Granville Austin: a federation with a strong unitary bias |
| Saying DPSPs override FRs | Minerva Mills (1980): both are part of the basic structure; neither swallows the other |
| Treating Liberty as one idea | Berlin's negative and positive liberty are not interchangeable |
| Assuming Sovereignty is absolute in India | Sovereignty rests with the people (Preamble); constitutional limitations exist |

#### Practice Prompts

1. **Distinguish** between *Legality* and *Legitimacy*, illustrating with one example each from Indian constitutional practice. Which concept does Max Weber's typology of authority belong to?
2. **Explain** why India's federalism is described as *quasi-federal*. List two constitutional provisions that strengthen the Union and two that preserve federal character.

> **Strategy for UPPSC PCS:** In Prelims, this cluster yields 1–3 questions (3% weightage). In Mains GS-II, examiners award marks for precise distinctions and constitutional article references, not vague generalities. Memorize the Article numbers (14, 19, 21, 32, 38, 256, 352, 356) — they are the quickest path to a full-mark answer.
