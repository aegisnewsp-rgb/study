# Indian History

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Indian History** for UPSC CSE GS1 traces the subcontinent's political, economic, social, and cultural evolution across **Ancient**, **Medieval**, and **Modern** periods. The Ancient phase begins with the **Indus Valley Civilization (c. 2600–1900 BCE)** at Harappa and Mohenjo-Daro, marked by grid streets, the **Great Bath**, and standardized burnt-bricks. The **Vedic Period (c. 1500–600 BCE)** documents the shift from pastoral Rigvedic tribes to settled Later Vedic society with **varna**, **gotra**, and **sabha/samiti**. The 6th century BCE produced heterodox challenges — **Buddhism** (Hinayana→Mahayana) and **Jainism** (Digambara/Shvetambara) — to ritual Brahmanism. **Mauryan** centralization under Chanakya and Ashoka's **dhamma** edicts, the **Gupta golden age** (zero, decimal numerals, Kalidasa), and **Sangam Tamil** polities anchor the classical phase. Medieval India is shaped by five **Delhi Sultanate** dynasties (Mamluk→Lodi), the **Mughal Mansabdari** and **zabt/nasaq** revenue systems, and the syncretic **Bhakti-Sufi** traditions (Kabir, Ramanuja, Chishti silsila). Modern history is dominated by British colonialism (**Drain Theory, Permanent Settlement, Doctrine of Lapse**), the **Indian National Congress**, Gandhian mass movements (**Non-Cooperation 1920, Civil Disobedience 1930, Quit India 1942**), **Partition 1947**, and the **Constituent Assembly** drafting a republican Constitution. Expect **3–5 Mains questions/year** with a tilt toward modern and post-independence themes.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Ancient: Sources and Civilizations
**Sources** of reconstruction fall into three buckets: **archaeological** (inscriptions like Ashoka's rock edicts, punch-marked and Gupta gold coins, monuments such as Sanchi and Aihole), **literary** (Vedas, Puranas, Buddhist *Jatakas* and *Tripitaka*, Jain *Angas*, Sangam *Pathupattu*/*Akananuru*, and foreign accounts — Megasthenes' *Indica*, Fa-Hien, Xuanzang, Alberuni's *Kitab-i-Hind*, Ibn Battuta), and **oral folk traditions**. The **Harappan** phase (Rakhigarhi, Dholavira, Lothal) is distinguished by **drainage systems**, granaries, citadel-lower town planning, and an undeciphered script of ~400 signs. The **Vedic shift** is best tracked through *gotra* exogamy, increasing rigidity of *varna*, and the rise of *samskaras*. **Mahajanapadas** (16 kingdoms, c. 600 BCE) and the **First Buddhist Council at Rajagriha** set the stage for Mauryan unification.

#### Mauryan and Post-Mauryan Polity
Chanakya's *Arthashastra* codifies espionage (**gumhaptas**), treasury (**kosa**), and army (**bala**). Ashoka's **dhamma** — non-violence, religious toleration, animal welfare — is propagated across 14 rock and 7 pillar edicts. The **Sangam Age** documents **Pandya, Chola, Chera** kings, Indo-Roman trade through Puhar and Muziris, and early **bhakti** hymns by Appar, Sundarar, and Sambandar. The **Gupta period** (Samudragupta's *Allahabad Pillar* prashasti, Chandragupta II's *Meherauli Iron Pillar*) is dated by the **Gupta era (319 CE)** and is canonical for the **decimal system, zero, chess (chaturanga)**, and Kalidasa's *Abhijnanashakuntalam*.

#### Medieval: Sultanate and Mughal
**Delhi Sultanate** (1206–1526): five dynasties, **Iqta** land grants, **Alauddin Khilji's** market-reform ordinances (price control, grain levies, espionage), and **Muhammad bin Tughlaq's** failed experiments (token currency, capital shift to Daulatabad). **Mughal** governance rests on the **Mansabdari** (numerical ranking of *zat* = personal status, *sawar* = cavalry contingent), **Ain-i-Dashunnah** salary schedules, **Todar Mal's zabt** survey in the 1580s, and the imperial *diwan-i-wazarat*. **Religious policy** moves from Akbar's *Sulh-i-Kul* and Ibadat Khati debates to Aurangzeb's re-imposition of *jizya* (1679).

#### Modern: Colonialism and Freedom
British revenue experiments — **Permanent Settlement (1793, Cornwallis)**, **Ryotwari (1792, Munro)**, **Mahalwari (1822, Holt Mackenzie)** — restructured rural India. The **Drain Theory** of Dadabhai Naoroji (*Poverty and Un-British Rule in India*, 1876) quantified wealth outflow. **Indian National Congress (1885)** evolved from moderate "prayer politics" to Extremists (Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal, Lala Lajpat Rai) post-1905 Partition of Bengal, then to Gandhian mass mobilization. **Drain Theory**, **drain of wealth**, and the **Lucknow Pact (1916)** mark the WW1 phase.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases, Causal Chains and Common Traps
- **Harappan script** remains undeciphered; the **Asko Parpola** and **S. R. Rao** proposals are speculative, so Mains answers must avoid attributing meanings to seals. A safer formulation: *"The Harappan script, comprising about 400–600 signs on seals and tablets, is generally accepted as logo-syllabic but remains undeciphered."*
- **Varna vs Jati**: in early Vedic sources, the *chaturvarnya* is fluid and *jati* is guild-based; in the **Dharmasutras** and **Manusmriti**, both harden. Examiners test this transition.
- **Mughal decline** is *not* a single cause. A model answer integrates the **Aurangzeb–Maratha** long war (1681–1707), **Nadir Shah's** invasion (1739), **Najm-ud-Daulah's** 1719 puppet-king turnover, and the rise of successor states (Hyderabad, Awadh, Mysore, Bengal, Maratha Confederacy).
- **Gandhian phases** have precise anchors: Champaran (1917, indigo), Kheda (1918, peasant), Rowlatt (1919), Jallianwala Bagh (13 April 1919), Chauri Chaura (5 Feb 1922) **ending** Non-Cooperation, Dandi (6 April 1930), Irwin March 1931 Pact, and the **August Kranti (8 August 1942)**. Mixing years is a high-frequency trap.
- **Drain of wealth** in Prelims: revenue surplus, home charges, pensions, private remittances, railway "drain" — cite *Naoroji's £2–3 crore* annual estimate.

#### Connections to Adjacent GS1 Topics
Indian History links directly to **Art & Culture (GS1)** via temple architecture (Khajuraho, Brihadisvara, Dilwara), to **Society** via Bhakti's anti-caste thrust, and to **Post-Independence India (GS1)** via the **Nehruvian** template of **mixed economy, non-alignment, panchayati raj, Hindu Code Bills** and **Linguistic Reorganisation of States (1956)**. Constitution-makers (Ambedkar, Nehru, Rajendra Prasad) bridge History and **Polity (GS2)**.

#### Worked Micro-Example
Q: "Critically examine the role of Bhakti and Sufi movements in shaping composite Ganga-Jamuni culture."
Model scaffold: *(i) Definitions and timelines — 12th–17th C. (ii) Saguna–Nirguna split; Chishti, Suhrawardi, Naqshbandi silsilas. (iii) Mechanisms — shared vernaculars (Hindi, Punjabi, Bengali), shared shrines (Ajmer, Pandharpur, Varanasi), *qawwali* and *bhajan* synthesis. (iv) Tensions — Varanasi's temple-mosque disputes, Aurangzeb's 1669 orders, but also the **Nizamuddin Auliya–Amir Khusrau** cultural fusion. (v) Limiters — did not dissolve caste or gender hierarchy, but created a public sphere of *mehfil*s and *kirtan*s that prefigured anti-colonial mobilization.*
