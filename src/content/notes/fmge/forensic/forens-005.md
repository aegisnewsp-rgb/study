# Legal Medicine — IPC Sections Relevant to Medical Practice, Medical Negligence

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your FMGE.

**Medical negligence** is the breach of a legal **duty of care** owed by a registered medical practitioner (RMP) to a patient, causing foreseeable harm with a direct causal link. Four legal components must coexist: (1) duty, (2) breach, (3) damage, (4) causation. The **Bolam test** (modified by **Bolithus**) asks whether the doctor's conduct withstands logical scrutiny of a responsible body of medical opinion.

| Section | Offence | Punishment |
|---|---|---|
| IPC 304-A / BNS 106 | Death by negligence | Up to 2 yrs + fine |
| IPC 299 / BNS 100 | Culpable homicide | Up to life |
| IPC 300 / BNS 101–103 | Murder | Death / life |
| IPC 320, 326 | Grievous hurt (by dangerous means) | Up to life |
| IPC 84 / BNS 22 | Act of unsound mind | No offence |

> **Trap:** 304-A needs only *rashness/negligence*, no intention to kill — never confuse with culpable homicide.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Elements of Negligence & Landmark Case
A plaintiff must prove all four **ingredients of negligence** — *Donoghue v Stevenson* laid down the neighbour principle in tort; in India, **Jacob Mathew v State of Punjab (2005)** crystallised the criminal standard. The court held that a doctor is **not criminally liable** merely because something went wrong; the negligence must be gross, amounting to a criminal act. **Bolam** is the shield, but **Bolithus** is the sword — peer opinion must withstand logical scrutiny by the court.

#### Key IPC & BNS Sections for Doctors
- **Sec 304-A IPC / Sec 106 BNS**: death by any *rash or negligent act not amounting to culpable homicide*.
- **Sec 299 / 300 IPC**: culpable homicide (BNS 100) and murder (BNS 101–103) — distinguished by *intention to cause death* vs *knowledge that the act is imminently dangerous*.
- **Sec 320 / 326 IPC**: eight kinds of *grievous hurt*, including permanent privation of sight, hearing, limb, or fracture/dislocation.
- **Sec 88 / 92 IPC** (BNS 26 / 29): acts done in **good faith** with consent (88) or without consent in good faith for the patient's benefit (92).
- **Sec 84 IPC / BNS 22**: acts of a person of *unsound mind* at the time of the act — judged by the **McNaghten Rules** (cognitive + volitional incapacity).

#### Procedural Duties (CrPC)
Every RMP must report under **Sec 39 CrPC** any case of wounds, burns, poisoning, suspected suicide, or death in custody. **Sec 174** mandates intimation of unnatural/suspicious death; **Sec 176** triggers a magistrate inquest for custodial death.

#### Consent
Consent must be **informed, specific, and free**. Implied consent covers routine examination; expressed written consent is mandatory for operative procedures. Minors (<12 yrs: no valid consent; 12–18 yrs: only for procedures beneficial to their health) require guardian consent. Emergency overrides consent under the **doctrine of necessity**.

#### Liability Doctrines
- **Res ipsa loquitur**: speaks for itself (e.g., swab retained in abdomen).
- **Novus actus interveniens**: intervening act breaks causation.
- **Vicarious liability**: hospital is liable for *employed* doctors in course of employment — independent consultants are exempt.
- **Contributory negligence**: damages reduced proportionally; distinct from *volenti non fit injuria*.

#### Professional Misconduct (NMC)
Adultery with a patient, false certificate, fee-splitting, dual practice without declaration, breach of professional secrecy — all are infamous conduct under the **NMC Code of Ethics Regulations, 2002** (now read with the **NMC Act, 2019**).

> **FMGE pearl:** Expect a clinical vignette — *"A surgeon leaves a mop inside the abdomen; patient sues"* → answer *Res ipsa loquitur + Sec 304-A IPC*.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Negligence vs Misconduct — The Fine Line
Negligence is judged by the **standard of care**; misconduct is judged by the **standard of conduct** prescribed by the NMC/SMC. A doctor can be sued civilly, prosecuted criminally, and summoned before the State Medical Council *simultaneously* — and acquittal in one forum does not automatically absolve liability in another. **Jacob Mathew (2005)** also introduced the protection of **"reasonable care, skill, and judgment"** — a doctor acting per accepted protocol is protected even if the outcome is poor.

#### Brain-Stem Death & Euthanasia
Under the **Transplantation of Human Organs Act, 1994**, brain-stem death is legal death in India **only** after certification by a Board of four doctors (RMO, treating specialist, neurologist/neurosurgeon, and an independent doctor nominated by the hospital). The **Common Cause v Union of India (2018)** ruling legalised **passive euthanasia** through advance directives and a two-tier procedure; **active euthanasia** remains unlawful under Sec 302 IPC / BNS 101.

#### Confidentiality & Medical Records
Professional secrecy is protected by the **Indian Medical Council (Professional Conduct, Etiquette and Ethics) Regulations**. Exceptions: court order, public interest, communicable disease notification, and Sec 39 CrPC intimation. Records must be preserved — **3 years** for inpatient, **2 years** for outpatient, and **lifelong** for medico-legal documents.

#### Worked Vignette
A 45-year-old man admitted for cholecystectomy dies on the OT table after the anaesthetist fails to notice a kinked endotracheal tube. Police register a case. *Which IPC section applies?* **Sec 304-A IPC / BNS 106** — death by negligence, not culpable homicide (no intention). The anaesthetist's conduct must be tested by **Bolam–Bolithus**: was it accepted practice? Did it withstand logical scrutiny? Hospital faces **vicarious liability** because the anaesthetist was employed.

#### Common Pitfalls
- Forgetting to map IPC → BNS when answering post-July 2023 questions.
- Assuming advance directive alone legalises withdrawal of life support — the high court/SDM must approve.
- Citing *volenti* for patients who ignore medical advice (wrong — that's *contributory*).
- Believing Sec 88 protects experimental or research procedures — the act must be in *good faith* for the *patient's benefit*, with informed consent.

> **Strategy for FMGE:** This 3% block yields 1–2 questions; spend 30 minutes memorising section numbers 84, 88, 92, 299–304-A, 320/326 and CrPC 39/174/176 — they appear verbatim in recall MCQs.

#### Practice Prompts
1. A surgeon operates on the wrong kidney. Discuss liability under IPC, BNS, and the doctrine of *Res ipsa loquitur*.
2. A patient refuses amputation despite gangrene and dies. Is the doctor liable under Sec 304-A? Justify using *contributory negligence* and *novus actus interveniens*.
