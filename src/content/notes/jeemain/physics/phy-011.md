# Thermodynamics

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision.

**Thermodynamics** — Key Facts for JEE Main
• **Core formula or law — write the equation:** ΔQ = ΔU + ΔW (First Law: ΔU = ΔQ − ΔW)
• **Core concept — one sentence explanation:** Heat energy supplied to a system equals the change in its internal energy plus the work done by the system on the surroundings.
• **Most common application of this topic:** Calculating work done, efficiency of heat engines, and change in entropy for quasi-static processes.
• **Key numerical value or constant to memorise:** Universal gas constant R = 8.314 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹; for diatomic gases γ = Cp/Cv = 1.4
• **Most tested concept in JEE Main:** Work done in an isothermal reversible process: **W = nRT ln(V₂/V₁)** and adiabatic condition: **PV^γ = constant**
• **Common mistake students make here:** Confusing signs — students often forget that when gas expands (ΔV > 0), work done **by** gas is positive, but from the system's perspective W done on the system is negative. Also mixing up Cp and Cv relationships.
⚡ **Exam tip:** For any cyclic process, ΔU = 0, so ΔQ = ΔW. In P-V diagram area enclosed = net work done. Always check if the process is quasi-static before applying **PV = nRT** at every step.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

**Thermodynamics** — JEE Main / Advanced Study Guide

Thermodynamics deals with the **interconversion of heat and other forms of energy** without violating the laws of thermodynamics. It describes macroscopic properties rather than microscopic behaviour.

**Core Concepts and Key Equations**

The **Zeroth Law** establishes temperature as a fundamental property — if two systems are each in thermal equilibrium with a third, they are in thermal equilibrium with each other, forming the basis of temperature measurement. The **First Law of Thermodynamics** is essentially the law of conservation of energy expressed as **ΔQ = ΔU + ΔW**, where ΔQ is heat added, ΔU is change in internal energy, and ΔW is work done by the system. For an ideal gas, **ΔU = nCvΔT**, independent of the process. Work done by a gas in a general quasi-static process is **W = ∫PdV**.

Four fundamental processes are critical:

- **Isothermal (T = constant):** For ideal gas, **W = nRT ln(V₂/V₁)**, ΔU = 0, so ΔQ = ΔW
- **Adiabatic (Q = 0):** **PV^γ = const**, **TV^(γ−1) = const**, **W = (P₁V₁ − P₂V₂)/(γ−1)**
- **Isochoric (V = constant):** W = 0, ΔQ = ΔU
- **Isobaric (P = constant):** W = P(V₂ − V₁)

**Mayer's relation** connects heat capacities: **Cp − Cv = R**, and **Cp/Cv = γ**. For monoatomic gas, Cv = (3/2)R, Cp = (5/2)R, γ = 5/3. For diatomic (rigid), Cv = (5/2)R, Cp = (7/2)R, γ = 1.4.

**Second Law of Thermodynamics** states that heat cannot spontaneously flow from a cold body to a hot body (Clausius statement) and that no heat engine can have 100% efficiency (Kelvin–Planck statement).

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

> For the last stretch before the exam: efficiency ceilings, sign conventions, and the three process families.

#### The Carnot Ceiling — Why No Engine Beats 1 − T₂/T₁

For any engine running between a hot reservoir at absolute temperature T₁ and a cold reservoir at T₂, the second law fixes the ceiling:

$$\eta_{max} = 1 - \frac{T_2}{T_1}$$

Temperatures here are in **kelvin**, never Celsius. An engine absorbing heat at 500 K and rejecting to 300 K cannot exceed 1 − 300/500 = **40%**, no matter how clever the mechanism. Real engines fall well short of that ceiling; a petrol engine runs near 30%.

#### Worked Example: Work Done in Isothermal Expansion

One mole of an ideal gas expands isothermally at 300 K from 10 L to 30 L. Work done **by** the gas:

$$W = nRT \ln\left(\frac{V_2}{V_1}\right) = 1 \times 8.314 \times 300 \times \ln(3) \approx 2{,}736\ \text{J}$$

Since temperature is constant, internal energy depends only on T, so **ΔU = 0**. The first law then forces ΔQ = W ≈ 2.74 kJ — every joule of heat absorbed leaves as work. Push the endpoints to 30 L → 90 L and the work triples, because ln scales rather than the difference between volumes.

#### The Sign Convention Question That Costs Marks

Examiners mix two conventions, and the safest response is to state which one you are using and then stay consistent.

| Quantity | Physics convention | Chemistry convention |
|----------|--------------------|---------------------|
| Work done **by** gas | +W | −W |
| ΔU relation | ΔU = Q − W | ΔU = Q + W |
| Exothermic process | — | Q negative, ΔU < 0 |

#### Isochoric, Isothermal, Adiabatic — The Only Three You Need

| Property | Isochoric | Isothermal | Adiabatic |
|----------|-----------|------------|-----------|
| Constant | V | T | Q = 0 |
| ΔU | Can be non-zero | **0** | Non-zero |
| ΔW | **0** (dV = 0) | Non-zero | Non-zero |
| PV | Rises with T | Constant (Boyle) | Falls, TV^(γ−1) = const |
| P against T | Slope = nR/V | P constant | P ∝ T^γ |

#### Cyclic Processes — Internal Energy Returns to Zero

Over a complete cycle the system ends exactly where it started, so **ΔU = 0** and therefore **W = Q_net**. This is why an engine's efficiency collapses to η = W_net / Q_in with no internal-energy term at all.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** "Heat is absorbed but no work is done in an isochoric process." The first clause is right and the inference is wrong — with volume fixed the gas cannot push the piston, but the absorbed heat still raises internal energy, so it is absorbed and *stored*, never converted.

#### Where This Shows Up

Thermodynamics is a reliable 1–2 mark cluster in JEE Main physical chemistry: usually one numerical on efficiency, one conceptual on processes. Budget three to four minutes. The numericals almost always test Carnot, the sign convention, or a single-step first-law balance, and all three appear above.
