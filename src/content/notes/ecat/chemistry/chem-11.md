# p-Block Elements

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

P-block elements occupy **Groups 13 to 18** of the periodic table, with valence-shell configuration **ns² np¹⁻⁶**. Six families — **boron, carbon, nitrogen, oxygen, fluorine, and noble gases** — span the most diverse chemistry of any block because the **ns² electrons, np electrons, and (n−1)d electrons** (in heavier members) all participate in bonding, allowing multiple oxidation states.

Three must-know effects:
- **Inert pair effect**: the ns² pair resists bonding down a group, stabilising lower oxidation states (e.g. **Tl⁺ over Tl³⁺**, **Pb²⁺ over Pb⁴⁺**, **Bi³⁺ over Bi⁵⁺**).
- **Anomalous behaviour of the 2nd-period elements** (B, C, N, O, F): small size, high charge density, **no d-orbitals**, and strong **pπ–pπ multiple bonding**.
- **Catenation** falls sharply down **Group 14** because the **E–E bond energy decreases C > Si > Ge > Sn > Pb**.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Electronic Configuration and Position
Elements of the p-block have their **outermost electrons in a p-orbital**, configuration **ns² np¹⁻⁶**. They occupy six groups on the right side of the periodic table; together with the s-block they make up the **representative (main-group) elements**. Because three p-orbitals can hold six electrons, p-block elements show a range of oxidation states equal to the group number minus 10.

#### Group 13 — The Boron Family
General configuration **ns² np¹**, common oxidation state **+3**. **Boron** is a non-metal with a very high ionisation enthalpy; its compounds (e.g. **BF₃, BCl₃**) are **electron-deficient** (fewer than 8 valence electrons around B) and behave as **Lewis acids**. Hydroxides become progressively more basic down the group: **B(OH)₃** is acidic, **Al(OH)₃ amphoteric**, **Ga(OH)₃ amphoteric**, **In(OH)₃/Tl(OH)₃ basic**.

#### Group 14 — The Carbon Family
Configuration **ns² np²**, oxidation states **+4 and +2**. Carbon alone forms strong **pπ–pπ** bonds (C=C, C≡C), which silicon cannot because its larger 3p orbitals overlap poorly. This is why **CO₂ is a discrete molecule but SiO₂ is a covalent network solid**. Allotropy is dramatic in carbon — **diamond, graphite, fullerene, graphene** — and bond-energy data explain the rapid fall in catenation down the group.

#### Group 15 — The Nitrogen Family
Configuration **ns² np³**. The **half-filled np³** gives extra stability, raising ionisation enthalpy above Group 14. Nitrogen shows oxidation states from **−3 (in NH₃) to +5 (in HNO₃)**. Basic strength of trihydrides falls sharply: **NH₃ ≫ PH₃ > AsH₃ > SbH₃ > BiH₃**.

#### Group 16 and 17 Highlights
Group 16 (chalcogens, ns² np⁴): oxygen shows only **−2, −1, 0**, while S, Se, Te, Po exhibit **+4, +6** due to d-orbital participation. Group 17 (halogens, ns² np⁵): highly electronegative, oxidising agents whose reactivity decreases down the group, with characteristic **interhalogen compounds** (ClF, BrF₃, IF₅).

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Periodic Trends Across the Block
- **Electronegativity**: rises left-to-right across a period (B 2.04 → F 3.98), falls down a group. The most electronegative element, **fluorine**, sits in the top-right corner.
- **Ionisation enthalpy**: generally rises from Group 13 to 17 with two notable dips — at Group 16 (np⁴, easy to lose the unpaired p electron) and a smaller anomaly at Group 13 (np¹). The np³ half-filled stability of N, P, As produces local maxima.
- **Oxidising power** of halogens: **F₂ > Cl₂ > Br₂ > I₂**, mirrored by decreasing standard reduction potentials.

#### Cross-Block Phenomena
- **Diagonal relationship** between B and Si arises because both have similar charge/size ratios; **B₂O₃ and SiO₂** are both acidic oxides, **BCl₃ and SiCl₄** are both readily hydrolysed.
- **Inert pair effect** intensifies down Groups 13–15 because the ns² pair is held more tightly with poor shielding from d and f electrons.
- **Allotropy** in p-block elements is a recurring tested theme: **O₂ / O₃, P (white/red/black), S (rhombic/monoclinic), Sn (grey/white)**.

#### Common ECAT Traps
- Confusing **electron-deficient** (B) with **electron-rich** (N, halogens) compounds — only B-compounds are Lewis acids in MCQs.
- Assuming all Group 14 elements catenate equally — carbon is exceptional because of small size and pπ–pπ overlap.
- Forgetting that **Pb prefers +2** and **Tl prefers +1** in MCQs about stable oxidation states.

#### Practice Prompts
1. Arrange BF₃, BCl₃, BBr₃ in order of Lewis acid strength and justify using back-bonding.
2. Why is BiH₃ the strongest reducing agent among Group 15 hydrides while NH₃ is the strongest base?
