# Indian Polity & Constitution

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your MAT slot.

The Constitution of India is the longest written national charter in the world, adopted on **26 November 1949** and enforced on **26 January 1950**. For MAT, you only need the framework, not the case law.

- **Three pillars of the state:** Legislature, Executive, Judiciary — separately for the Union and the States.
- **Parts you must name:** Part III (Fundamental Rights, Articles 12–35, justiciable), Part IV (Directive Principles, Articles 36–51, non-justiciable), Part IVA (Fundamental Duties, added 1976).
- **Schedules that get asked:** Seventh (three lists), Eighth (22 languages), Tenth (anti-defection law), Twelfth (salaries). Total = 12.
- **Emergency trio:** Article 352 (National), Article 356 (President's Rule), Article 360 (Financial).
- **One quote to remember:** Dr. B.R. Ambedkar called **Article 32** the "heart and soul" of the Constitution.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook (Memory Hook):** "**PDS = Three Parts, Three Lists**" — **P**art III (Rights) + **D**PSP (Part IV) + **S**chedule 7 (**L, C, U** = List). The 12 Schedules read as "**1, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12**" — memorise them as the only six MAT ever tests.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> The working understanding — mechanisms, contrasts, and one solved MCQ.

#### The Preamble in One Line
"Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic" securing Justice, Liberty, Equality, Fraternity. The words **Socialist, Secular, Integrity** were inserted by the **42nd Amendment, 1976**. The Preamble is part of the Constitution but cannot be amended to destroy the basic structure (*Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala*, 1973).

#### Fundamental Rights vs Directive Principles vs Fundamental Duties

| Feature | Fundamental Rights (Part III) | Directive Principles (Part IV) | Fundamental Duties (Part IVA) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Articles | 12–35 | 36–51 | 51A (ten duties) |
| Nature | Justiciable | Non-justiciable | Non-enforceable |
| Source | USA (Bill of Rights) | Ireland (Constitution) | Swaran Singh Committee (1976) |
| Added by | Original Constitution, 1950 | Original Constitution, 1950 | 42nd Amendment, 1976 |
| Role | Limit state action | Direct state policy | Moral obligation of citizens |

#### Federalism and the Three Lists

The Seventh Schedule splits legislative subjects between Union and States. **Residuary powers** sit with Parliament under **Article 248**. For MAT recall:

- **Union List:** Defence, Atomic Energy, Foreign Affairs, Railways, Banking, Currency.
- **State List:** Police, Public Order, Land, Local Government, Agriculture.
- **Concurrent List:** Criminal Law (IPC), Education, Electricity, Drugs.

#### Three Kinds of Majority (confused in every paper)

| Majority type | What it means | Where used |
|---|---|---|
| Simple | More votes than opposing side | Ordinary legislation |
| Absolute | >50% of **total sanctioned strength** of House | Confidence motions, Speaker removal |
| Special | ≥2/3 of members **present and voting** | Article 368 amendments, resolution to remove Vice President |
| Effective | Special + Absolute combined | Ratification of constitutional amendments under Article 368 |

> 📌 **Formula Check (units):** All four majorities are dimensionless ratios. "Present and voting" excludes abstentions — a frequent MAT trap.

#### Money Bill Mechanics (Article 109)
A Money Bill originates **only in the Lok Sabha**. The Rajya Sabha can only **recommend** changes within **14 days**; it cannot amend, reject, or delay it. The Speaker certifies whether a Bill is a Money Bill, and that certificate is **final**.

#### Worked Question (the kind MAT actually asks)

> AIMA sample-style MCQ: *The words 'Socialist' and 'Secular' were added to the Preamble by the…*
> (a) 44th Amendment (b) 42nd Amendment (c) 86th Amendment (d) 1st Amendment

- **Step 1 — Recall the landmark:** The 42nd Amendment, 1976, is called the "Mini-Constitution." It inserted Socialist, Secular, Integrity into the Preamble.
- **Step 2 — Eliminate decoys:** 44th Amendment (1978) **restored** some of what the 42nd had taken away; 86th (2002) made education a Fundamental Right for children aged 6–14; the 1st (1951) added the Ninth Schedule.
- **Answer:** **(b) 42nd Amendment**.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Option (a) 44th is the favourite decoy — students pick it because both amendments touch the 42nd era. The 44th *removed* "Internal disturbance" as ground for emergency and trimmed some 42nd additions, it did not add the Preamble words.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Edge cases, cross-links, and the kind of trap MAT sets when it tries to look harder than it is.

#### Article 368 vs Article 249 — Which Amendment Path?
Most MAT questions test only Article 368, but the **22nd Amendment (1969)** and **24th Amendment (1971)** use Article 249 to bypass state ratification for specific subjects in the State List. Don't confuse the two: Article 368 changes the Constitution; Article 249 lets Parliament legislate temporarily on a State List subject for **one year** with Rajya Sabha's two-thirds resolution.

#### Schedules Students Misattribute

| Schedule | Common wrong belief | Correct content |
|---|---|---|
| First | Languages | Lists of States and Territories with boundaries |
| Eighth | Anti-defection | Recognised languages (22 originally; 92nd Amendment added four more) |
| Ninth | Land reforms | Land reforms and certain other laws shielded from judicial review |
| Tenth | Languages | Anti-defection law (52nd Amendment, 1985) |
| Twelfth | Emergency powers | Salaries, allowances, privileges of MPs, Speaker, Judges |

#### The Basic Structure Doctrine — Why It Matters for MAT
*Kesavananda Bharati* (13-judge bench, 7:6 majority, 1973) held that Parliament **cannot amend the basic structure** even under Article 368. The Court has never closed this list, but accepted features include: supremacy of the Constitution, federalism, secularism, democracy, separation of powers, judicial review, and the unity and integrity of India. MAT may phrase a question as: *"Which case limited Parliament's power to amend the Constitution?"* — the answer is always *Kesavananda Bharati*, never *Minerva Mills* (which struck down parts of the 42nd but came later).

#### Emergency Provisions — Boundary Conditions
- **Article 352** National Emergency: grounds are now only "**armed rebellion**" (44th Amendment, 1978 — earlier term was "internal disturbance"); must be approved by both Houses within **one month**; maximum duration **six months**, extendable.
- **Article 356** President's Rule: a State can be put under it if the constitutional machinery has broken down. **Lok Sabha must approve within two months**; otherwise it lapses.
- **Article 360** Financial Emergency: declared on the ground of threat to financial stability or credit of India. **Never invoked so far** — this is a frequent MAT factual-recall question.

#### Cross-Topic Edges That Get Tested
- The **anti-defection law** (52nd Amendment, 1985, Tenth Schedule) was inserted by the **52nd Amendment** but moved to the **Tenth Schedule** by the **91st Amendment, 2003**, which raised the split-defection threshold to two-thirds. Linking 52nd to Tenth is a high-value trap.
- **Article 21** (Right to Life and Personal Liberty) has been read to include the right to livelihood, education (post 86th Amendment), privacy (*Puttaswamy*, 2017). MAT may frame this as *"Right to Privacy is a Fundamental Right under which Article?"*
- **Emergency vs President's Rule:** Article 352 affects the whole country; Article 356 affects only a State. Confusing these is the single most common MAT error.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts
1. *"Rajya Sabha rejects a Money Bill. What happens next?"* — The Lok Sabha may pass it again with or without Rajya Sabha's recommendations. The Bill does not lapse. A joint sitting is **not** permitted on Money Bills (Article 108 carve-out).
2. *"A constitutional amendment touches the State List and requires ratification. How many State Legislatures must ratify, and what majority in each?"* — At least **half the State Legislatures** (not half the states), and in each a **simple majority** of members present and voting.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Watch the wording carefully: "half the State Legislatures" means half the total number, not half the number that actually ratify. The 44th, 73rd, 74th and 86th Amendments each used this ratification pathway.
