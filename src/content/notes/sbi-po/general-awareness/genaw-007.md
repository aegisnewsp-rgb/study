# Priority Sector Lending and Financial Inclusion

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Priority Sector Lending (PSL) is a **mandatory credit allocation framework** mandated by the Reserve Bank of India under its Master Directions on Priority Sector. Every scheduled commercial bank, regional rural bank, small finance bank, and foreign bank with 20+ branches must deploy a defined slice of its loan book to identified growth-critical sectors. The aggregate target is **40% of Adjusted Net Bank Credit (ANBC)**, computed on the previous financial year's closing balance of 31 March.

The eight priority sectors are: agriculture and allied activities, micro and small enterprises, export credit, education, housing, social infrastructure, renewable energy, and weaker sections. Sub-targets sit inside the 40% ceiling: agriculture 18% (small and marginal farmers 10%), weaker sections 10%, micro enterprises 7.5%, export credit 2% (only where ANBC exceeds ₹100 crore), and renewable energy 1% scaling to 7% by FY2030.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"A-W-M-E-R"** — the 40% PSL pie, recall the sub-targets in order: **A**griculture 18% → **W**eaker Sections 10% → **M**icro Enterprises 7.5% → **E**xport Credit 2% → **R**enewable Energy 1% (rising). For RIDF shortfall: think **"RIDF = RBI's Parking Lot"** at bank rate + 2%.

- **ANBC (Adjusted Net Bank Credit):** Net bank credit minus intra-bank exposures, used as the PSL denominator.
- **DRI (Differential Rate of Interest):** A separate 4% per annum loan scheme for the poorest households; cannot offset PSL shortfall.
- **RIDF (Rural Infrastructure Development Fund):** NABARD-managed deposit where banks park the PSL shortfall at bank rate + 2%.
- **LDM/DCC:** Lead District Manager and District Consultative Committee certify PSL achievement after field verification.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Definition and Regulatory Basis

PSL originates from the **1972 RBI directive** and is presently governed by the **Master Direction on Priority Sector Lending – Targets and Classification** (updated periodically, latest consolidation 2024–25). It obliges banks to direct credit to sectors considered priority from a policy standpoint — agriculture, MSMEs, weaker sections, and emerging green-economy segments. Non-achievement does not invite a penalty in the strict sense; instead, the deficit is parked in **RIDF with NABARD** at the prevailing bank rate plus 2%, earning interest but losing yield upside.

#### How the 40% Target is Computed

The denominator is **ANBC** plus the **Credit Equivalent of Off-Balance Sheet Exposure (CEOBE)** such as acceptances, guarantees, and documentary credits. For FY25, the formula reads:

**Target PSL = 0.40 × (ANBC + CEOBE)**, measured on the 31 March closing balance of the previous year.

Inside this 40%, sub-targets act as inner ceilings that the bank must independently meet:

| Sub-Target | Quantum (% of ANBC) | Eligible Beneficiaries |
|---|---|---|
| Agriculture overall | 18% | Farmers, allied activities (dairy, fisheries, forestry) |
| Small & Marginal Farmers (within Agriculture) | 10% | Landholders with ≤2 hectares |
| Micro Enterprises (within MSE) | 7.5% | Investment in plant & machinery ≤ ₹1 cr; turnover ≤ ₹5 cr |
| Weaker Sections | 10% | SC/ST, minorities, women, disabled, small road/water transport operators |
| Export Credit | 2% | Only where ANBC > ₹100 crore in 4 of last 5 years |
| Renewable Energy | 1% → 7% (phased by FY30) | Solar, wind, biomass, small hydro ≤25 MW |

> 📌 **Formula Check:** PSL target = 0.40 × (ANBC + CEOBE). Each sub-target is independently calculated on ANBC alone, not on the 40% pie.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix — PSL vs DRI vs RIDF

Students routinely mix up these three instruments. Use the table below to lock the distinction.

| Feature | Priority Sector Lending (PSL) | Differential Rate of Interest (DRI) | RIDF (Shortfall Deposit) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Issuer | RBI mandate on banks | RBI social-interest scheme | NABARD-administered fund |
| Purpose | Mandated lending to priority sectors | 4% p.a. loans to poorest households | Park PSL shortfall |
| Interest to bank | Earns asset yield | Earns 4% (subsidised) | Earns bank rate + 2% |
| Counts towards PSL? | Yes (it IS PSL) | No — over and above PSL | No — penalty parking |
| Default consequence | Transfer shortfall to RIDF | No penalty; outreach measure | Loss of deployment flexibility |

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** A bank's ANBC is ₹50,000 crore and CEOBE is ₹5,000 crore. Compute (a) the overall PSL target, (b) the agriculture sub-target, and (c) the weaker-sections sub-target. If the bank achieves PSL credit of ₹21,500 crore, agriculture lending of ₹8,000 crore, and weaker-sections lending of ₹4,200 crore, find each shortfall. State which shortfall moves to RIDF.

#### Solution:

- (a) Overall PSL target = 0.40 × (50,000 + 5,000) = 0.40 × 55,000 = **₹22,000 crore**.
- (b) Agriculture sub-target = 0.18 × 50,000 = **₹9,000 crore**.
- (c) Weaker Sections sub-target = 0.10 × 50,000 = **₹5,000 crore**.

Shortfalls:
- Overall PSL shortfall = 22,000 − 21,500 = **₹500 crore** → moved to RIDF.
- Agriculture shortfall = 9,000 − 8,000 = **₹1,000 crore** → moved to RIDF.
- Weaker Sections shortfall = 5,000 − 4,200 = **₹800 crore** → moved to RIDF.

The total RIDF deposit = 500 + 1,000 + 800 = **₹2,300 crore** at bank rate + 2%.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students often apply 18% and 10% on (ANBC + CEOBE) instead of ANBC alone. The **outer 40% uses ANBC + CEOBE**, but **sub-targets are calculated on ANBC only**. Confusing these two denominators inflates the apparent shortfall and turns a correct answer into a wrong one.

#### Priority Sector Categories at a Glance

- **Agriculture & allied:** Crop loans, dairy, poultry, fisheries, post-harvest infrastructure.
- **Micro & Small Enterprises:** Manufacturing MSMEs (investment ≤ ₹50M) and service MSMEs (investment ≤ ₹20M).
- **Education:** Loans to individuals for studies in India and abroad; loans to institutions.
- **Housing:** Loans up to ₹35 lakh in metros, ₹25 lakh in non-metros (as per current classification).
- **Social Infrastructure:** Schools, health centres, drinking water, sanitation facilities.
- **Renewable Energy:** Grid-connected solar, wind, biomass, small hydro up to 25 MW.
- **Export Credit:** Pre- and post-shipment credit in rupees/foreign currency.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Policy Loopholes

Five traps appear repeatedly in the Mains and Interview rounds:

1. **Housing loan cap trap:** A loan of ₹40 lakh in a metro is **not** eligible PSL housing; only the first ₹35 lakh qualifies, leaving ₹5 lakh outside PSL classification. Banks sometimes classify the entire ticket — examiners flag this.
2. **DRI does NOT offset PSL:** The 4% DRI advances are over and above the 40% target. Counting them as PSL is the most common descriptive-paper error.
3. **Renewable Energy phase-in:** Renewable Energy starts at 1% (currently) and rises to 7% by FY2030. The intermediate schedule is 1%→2%→3%→4.5%→6%→7%. Recalling "1 to 7 by 2030" is safer than quoting any single year.
4. **Foreign bank 32% rule:** Foreign banks with ≤20 branches historically had a 32% target (instead of 40%). Under the revised RBI framework, this distinction is being phased out — verify the latest RBI circular before quoting in interview.
5. **Udyam Registration mandatory for MSME PSL:** Collateral-free institutional credit to MSMEs requires a valid Udyam Registration. Treating registration as optional misclassifies the asset.

#### Connection to Adjacent Topics

PSL sits inside the larger **Financial Inclusion** architecture. PMJDY (Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana) creates the account; MUDRA (refinance to NBFCs for micro-units) leverages PSL refinance windows; Stand-Up India and PMSBY are linked through weaker-sections lending. In the SBI PO interview, panel members often ask how PSL complements these schemes — knowing the **refinance channel through NABARD, SIDBI, and NHB** completes the answer.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. *Numerical:* A bank's ANBC is ₹80,000 crore and CEOBE is ₹10,000 crore. Compute the total PSL target and the maximum possible RIDF deposit if the bank achieves zero PSL credit in a year. (Answer: Target = 0.40 × 90,000 = ₹36,000 crore → RIDF deposit = ₹36,000 crore.)
2. *Conceptual:* Explain why the RBI allows shortfall to be parked in RIDF rather than imposing a cash penalty. Discuss the moral-hazard angle if RIDF interest exceeded the bank's asset yield.

> 📌 **Formula Check:** RIDF parking rate = prevailing bank rate + 2%. NABARD on-lends RIDF proceeds to state governments and rural infrastructure projects, recycling bank liquidity into rural assets.
