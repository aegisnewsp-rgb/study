# Problem Solving and Data Analysis — Ratios, Rates and Percentages — SAT Math Notes

Ratios, rates and percentages are part of the SAT Math Problem Solving and Data Analysis content domain. The SAT tests ratio and proportion, percentage change, unit rates, rate-time-distance problems, and direct/inverse variation in real-world contexts.

> Verify the live SAT specifications on https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/ before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Ratio:** a comparison of two quantities (e.g. 3:4).
- **Proportion:** an equation stating that two ratios are equal (a/b = c/d).
- **Unit rate:** a rate expressed per one unit (e.g. $5 per hour, 60 miles per hour).
- **Percentage change:** "Increase by r%" means multiply by (1 + r/100); "Decrease by r%" means multiply by (1 - r/100).
- **Direct variation:** y = kx.
- **Inverse variation:** y = k/x.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing rate with ratio (rate has units; ratio does not).
- Treating percentage change as a single percentage rather than a multiplication.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Solving proportions

To solve a proportion (a/b = c/d):

1. Cross-multiply: ad = bc.
2. Solve for the unknown variable.

#### Percentage change

"Increase by r%" means multiply by (1 + r/100). Example: $100 increased by 20% = $100 × 1.20 = $120.

"Decrease by r%" means multiply by (1 - r/100). Example: $100 decreased by 20% = $100 × 0.80 = $80.

For a repeated percentage change, multiply the factors. Example: $100 increased by 20%, then decreased by 10% = $100 × 1.20 × 0.90 = $108.

#### Rate-time-distance

The relationship is: Distance = Rate × Time. Given any two, you can find the third.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where Ratios, Rates and Percentages appears across the SAT Math section

Ratios, rates and percentages items appear throughout the SAT Math section. They test the candidate's ability to apply these concepts to real-world contexts.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Rate and ratio are the same." No — rate has units; ratio does not.
- "Percentage change is a percentage." No — percentage change is a multiplication factor.

#### Exam technique

- For proportion problems, cross-multiply.
- For percentage change, write the multiplication factor (1 + r/100 or 1 - r/100).
- For rate problems, identify the formula (D = R × T) and the units.

#### Step-by-step worked College Board test example

**Problem**:
What is the sum of the solutions to the quadratic equation 2x^2 - 14x + 11 = 0?

**Solution**:
1. Recall Vieta's formulas for any quadratic ax^2 + bx + c = 0: the sum of the roots is given by -b/a, and the product of the roots is given by c/a.
2. Identify the coefficients: a = 2, b = -14, c = 11.
3. Compute the sum directly:
   Sum = -(-14) / 2 = 14 / 2 = 7.
4. Methodological check via the quadratic formula:
   x = (14 +- sqrt((-14)^2 - 4(2)(11))) / 4 = (14 +- sqrt(196 - 88)) / 4 = (14 +- sqrt(108)) / 4.
   Adding the two conjugate roots: (14 + sqrt(108) + 14 - sqrt(108)) / 4 = 28 / 4 = 7.
   Vieta's formula eliminates five arithmetic steps and prevents radical calculation errors.
