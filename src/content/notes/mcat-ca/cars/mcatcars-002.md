# Reasoning Within the Text — MCAT CARS Skill 2

Reasoning Within the Text is CARS Skill 2 in the AAMC CARS framework. It is the ability to follow the author's argument: identifying claims and evidence, distinguishing strengthen/weaken statements, recognising assumptions, and evaluating the logic of the argument as it is presented. This skill builds on Foundations of Comprehension (Skill 1) and is the most-tested CARS skill.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### What Skill 2 tests

- **Claims and evidence:** the author's main assertions and the support offered for them.
- **Strengthen and weaken:** items that ask which choice would strengthen or weaken the author's argument.
- **Assumptions:** unstated beliefs the argument depends on. Items ask "the argument assumes that..."
- **Logical reasoning:** evaluating whether the argument's conclusions follow from its premises.
- **Inference within the passage:** items that ask "according to the passage..." or "the author would most likely agree that..."

#### One-pass drill

1. Read the passage once for the main idea (Skill 1).
2. Identify the central claim and the main pieces of evidence.
3. For each Skill 2 item, ask: "what would strengthen or weaken this claim?" or "what assumption is the author making?"

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing strengthen with weaken. Strengthen items ask for evidence that supports the argument; weaken items ask for evidence that undermines it.
- Picking an answer that is true in real life but not supported by the passage. Skill 2 items ask about the author's argument, not the world.
- Confusing assumption with conclusion. Assumptions are unstated beliefs; conclusions are stated claims.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Identifying claims and evidence

A claim is a statement the author makes. Evidence is the support offered for the claim. The MCAT often frames Skill 2 items as:

- "The author's main claim is that..."
- "Which of the following, if true, would most strengthen the author's argument?"
- "Which of the following, if true, would most weaken the author's argument?"

To answer, identify the central claim, then evaluate each answer choice against that claim.

#### Strengthen and weaken items

**Strengthen:** the choice that provides additional support for the author's claim. The right answer is consistent with the passage AND adds to the argument.

**Weaken:** the choice that undermines the author's claim. The right answer is consistent with the passage's other claims AND contradicts the central claim.

#### Assumption items

An assumption is an unstated belief the argument depends on. To find it:

- State the claim.
- State the evidence.
- Identify the missing link between evidence and claim. That missing link is the assumption.

The MCAT asks "the argument assumes that..." or "the author's reasoning depends on which of the following assumptions?"

#### Practice routine

- Take one CARS passage. Identify the central claim and the main evidence.
- Answer the Skill 2 items. For each item, explain to yourself why the right answer is right and the wrong answers are wrong.
- Time yourself: under 8 minutes per passage (4 minutes reading + 4 minutes answering).
- Take 5 passages in a row. Repeat the routine.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How Skill 2 connects to the other CARS skills

- **Skill 1** identifies what the passage says. Skill 2 evaluates how well the passage says it.
- **Skill 3 (Reasoning Beyond the Text)** extends Skill 2 to new situations — applying the author's reasoning outside the passage.
- **Skill 4 (Quantitative Reasoning)** often uses Skill 2 reasoning to evaluate numerical claims.

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"Strengthen and weaken are the same."** No — strengthen supports the argument; weaken undermines it. The opposite of a strengthener is a weakener.
- **"I should use my own knowledge."** CARS items ask about the author's argument, not the world. The right answer is grounded in the passage.
- **"The assumption is stated in the passage."** Assumptions are unstated. The right answer is the missing link between evidence and claim.

#### Exam technique

- For strengthen items, ask: "which choice adds support for the author's claim?"
- For weaken items, ask: "which choice contradicts the author's claim?"
- For assumption items, ask: "what must be true for the argument to work?"
- Eliminate choices that contradict the passage or are off-topic.
- Use the platform's flag-for-review function for items you cannot answer.
