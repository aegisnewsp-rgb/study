# History reading and writing standards (Level 2 and Level 3) — NCEA UE Literacy Notes

History achievement standards at Level 2 and Level 3 contribute to the University Entrance literacy requirement. The standards examine primary and secondary sources, communicate historical understanding, and develop research and essay-writing skills. A student taking Level 2 or Level 3 History typically earns several UE literacy credits through the subject.

> Verify the live UE literacy standards spreadsheet on https://www2.nzqa.govt.nz/ncea/understanding-secondary-quals/university-entrance/literacy-for-ue/ before planning revision around the figures below.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Reading and writing credits** available through Level 2 and Level 3 History standards.
- Common Level 2 standards: AS 91229 (Examine primary sources), AS 91230 (Examine a significant historical event), and others.
- Common Level 3 standards: AS 91434 (Research a historical event), AS 91435 (Analyse a historical interpretation), AS 91436 (Examine a significant historical event/place), and others.
- All contribute to the UE literacy requirement (per the UE literacy spreadsheet).

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing "primary source" with "first draft" — a primary source is a document from the historical period being studied, not a draft of the student's own writing.
- Treating historical interpretation as opinion — historical interpretations are evidenced arguments that can be evaluated.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### The History standards contributing to UE literacy

The NZQA UE literacy spreadsheet lists which History standards contribute to UE literacy and how the credits are split between reading and writing contributions. Common patterns:

- AS 91229 (Examine primary sources): typically reading credits.
- AS 91230 (Examine a significant historical event): typically reading and writing credits (essay-based).
- AS 91434 (Research a historical event): typically writing credits (research report).
- AS 91435 (Analyse a historical interpretation): typically reading and writing credits.

A typical Level 2 History programme might contribute 6-10 UE literacy credits; a Level 3 History programme can contribute similarly.

#### Reading primary sources

Primary sources are documents from the period being studied (letters, diaries, official records, photographs, artefacts). The student:

1. Identifies the source (author, date, context).
2. Examines the content (what does the source say?).
3. Evaluates the source (reliability, perspective, bias).
4. Connects the source to broader historical context.

#### Writing in History

History writing at Level 2 and Level 3 is evidence-based argument. The student:

1. Makes a clear claim (thesis).
2. Supports the claim with evidence (primary and secondary sources).
3. Analyses the evidence (what does it show? how does it support the claim?).
4. Acknowledges counter-arguments and limitations.

This is the same structure as a university humanities essay.

#### Research in History

AS 91434 (and similar) require independent research:

- Choose a topic.
- Locate relevant primary and secondary sources.
- Evaluate the sources.
- Synthesise into a coherent essay or report.

The research process teaches source evaluation and synthesis skills, both of which are transferable to university study.

#### Preparation strategy

1. **Practice reading sources critically** — primary and secondary, with attention to perspective and bias.
2. **Practice essay writing** — clear thesis, evidence-based argument, analytical paragraphs.
3. **Practice research** — library and online search skills, source evaluation, citation.
4. **Use exemplars** — NZQA publishes student work at each grade level.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Excellence in source analysis

The biggest gap between Merit and Excellence in source analysis is the integration of content and context.

For example, on AS 91229 (Examine primary sources):

- Merit: Describes what the source says and explains its reliability based on the author's perspective.
- Excellence: Describes what the source says, explains its reliability, AND connects the source to broader historical context (e.g., the political situation of the time, the intended audience, the rhetorical strategies).

The Excellence analyst treats the source as a window into the past AND as a text shaped by its author's context.

#### Excellence in historical argument

Merit in historical writing presents evidence in support of a thesis. Excellence engages with historiography (the history of how historians have interpreted the event) and reflects on the construction of historical knowledge.

For Excellence, the student might:

- Acknowledge competing interpretations.
- Evaluate the strengths and weaknesses of each.
- Justify their own interpretation with reference to evidence and methodology.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "History is just memorising dates." No — it is analysing evidence, evaluating interpretations, and constructing arguments.
- "Primary sources are automatically reliable." No — they are subject to the biases of their authors and the limitations of their context.
- "One source is enough." Usually not — historical arguments are typically built on multiple sources.
- "UE literacy from History is a side benefit." It is — but the skills are central to History learning.
