# Group Elements

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Group elements** (s- and p-block) are the main-group representatives of the periodic table whose valence electrons occupy only **s** or **s and p** orbitals, in contrast to d-block transition metals.

- **Groups 1 & 2 (s-block):** Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs, Fr (alkali metals, +1) and Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba, Ra (alkaline earth metals, +2). Soft, low-density, reactive metals forming basic oxides.
- **Groups 13–18 (p-block):** include the **halogens** (Group 17, X₂, –1) and **noble gases** (Group 18, closed shells, inert).
- **Down a group:** atomic radius ↑, **ionization energy (IE₁) ↓**, metallic character ↑, electronegativity ↓, electron affinity generally becomes less negative.
- **Anomalous first element:** F, O, N, C and Li, Be behave differently from the rest of their group because of small size, high charge density, and lack of available d-orbitals.
- **MDCAT pointer:** expect 1–2 direct questions — usually on trends (IE, atomic radius) or identifying the group of a given element.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Classification of Group Elements
Group elements occupy the **s-block (Groups 1, 2)** and **p-block (Groups 13–18)**. Every member of a group has the **same valence-shell configuration** (ns¹, ns², ns²np¹…ns²np⁵, ns²np⁶), which is why they show recurring chemistry.

| Block | Groups | Valence electrons | Common oxidation state |
|-------|--------|-------------------|------------------------|
| s-block | 1 (alkali), 2 (alkaline earth) | ns¹, ns² | +1, +2 |
| p-block | 13–18 | ns²np¹ to ns²np⁶ | variable; –1 (halogens), 0 (noble gases) |

#### Periodic Trends Down a Group
Each step down adds one full electron shell, increasing distance from the nucleus and the **shielding constant S** in Zeff = Z − S.

- **Atomic radius:** ↑ because of added shells and greater shielding.
- **Ionization energy (IE₁):** ↓ — the outermost electron is held less tightly, so less energy (kJ mol⁻¹) is needed to remove it.
- **Electronegativity and electron affinity:** generally become less negative (weaker pull for an extra electron).
- **Metallic character:** ↑; non-metallic character ↓.
- **Melting/boiling points:** mostly decrease in Group 1 (weaker metallic bonding) but show complex patterns in p-block groups.

#### Group 1 vs Group 2 vs Group 17 at a Glance
- **Alkali metals (Li → Cs):** stored under oil, react vigorously with water (2M + 2H₂O → 2MOH + H₂), form ionic +1 salts, produce characteristic flame colours (Li crimson, Na yellow, K lilac).
- **Alkaline earth metals (Be → Ba):** +2 cations, less reactive; hydroxides are basic except **Be(OH)₂** which is amphoteric. BeO is also amphoteric.
- **Halogens (F₂ → I₂):** coloured diatomic molecules (F pale yellow, Cl green, Br red-brown, I violet), oxidising power ↓ down the group, displacement reactions (Cl₂ + 2KBr → 2KCl + Br₂).

#### Exam Patterns for MDCAT
- Numericals: comparing IE₁ of Na vs Cs, or radius of Cl⁻ vs I⁻, using **Coulombic attraction F ∝ Zeff / r²**.
- Statement-type MCQs: "Down Group 1, metallic character ___ / IE₁ ___ / atomic radius ___."
- Anomalous behaviour questions on Li, Be, B, or F.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Anomalous Behaviour of the First Element
The first member of each main group differs sharply from its congeners because:
1. **Very small atomic radius** → high charge density (charge / ionic radius).
2. **High polarizing power** of the cation → greater covalent character in bonds (Fajans' rules). LiCl is more covalent than NaCl; BeCl₂ is covalent while CaCl₂ is ionic.
3. **Absence of d-orbitals** → first-row elements cannot expand their octet, so PF₅ and SF₆ exist but NF₅ and OF₆ do not.

#### Diagonal Relationships
Period-2 elements resemble their **right-down neighbour** in Period 3 because of comparable charge-to-size ratios:
- **Li ↔ Mg:** both form somewhat covalent chlorides, give nitride on heating in N₂, and form sparingly soluble carbonates.
- **Be ↔ Al:** both are amphoteric (BeO, Al₂O₃ react with both acids and NaOH) and form covalent halides.
- **B ↔ Si:** both form acidic oxides and covalent halides.

#### Hydration Energy and Solubility
Hydration enthalpy of an ion ∝ (charge² / ionic radius). Going **down Group 1**, lattice enthalpy drops faster than hydration enthalpy, so salts of larger cations (K⁺, Rb⁺, Cs⁺) tend to be more soluble than those of Li⁺ and Na⁺ for many anions — a frequent MDCAT trap when comparing solubility of, say, LiOH vs CsOH.

#### Common Mistakes
- Confusing **electron affinity** (energy released when an electron is added to a gaseous atom, usually negative) with **electronegativity** (Pauling scale, dimensionless tendency in a bond).
- Assuming IE₁ always decreases smoothly down a group — small irregularities occur (e.g., IE₁ of N > O because of half-filled 2p stability in N).
- Equating "noble gas" with "always inert": Xe forms XeF₂, XeF₄, XeF₆; Kr forms KrF₂ under special conditions.

#### Worked Micro-Example
**Q:** Arrange Na, Mg, Al in order of increasing first ionization energy.
**Reasoning:** IE₁ generally rises across a period (Na < Al < Mg expected), but Mg has a filled 3s² sub-shell, so removing an electron from 3s² requires more energy than removing one from Al's 3s²3p¹. Order: **Na < Al < Mg**.

#### Practice Prompts
1. Explain, using Zeff and atomic radius, why the IE₁ of potassium is lower than that of lithium.
2. Why is Be(OH)₂ amphoteric while Ba(OH)₂ is strongly basic? Answer in terms of charge density and polarizing power.
