# Departmentalisation

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Departmentalisation** is the second step of the *Organising* function of management. It groups similar activities into distinct units called **departments**, each placed under a **departmental head** accountable for results. The sequence inside Organising is *Division of Work → Departmentation → Delegation → Span of Control* → reporting relationships on the org chart.

| Term | Meaning | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| Division of Work | Splitting total work into tasks | Pre-departmental |
| Departmentation | Grouping tasks into departments | Horizontal (structural) |
| Delegation | Transferring authority to subordinates | Operational, vertical |
| Span of Control | Subordinates per manager | Vertical (managerial) |

- **Functional** — by activity (Production, Marketing, Finance, HR).
- **Divisional/Product** — by product line, each division self-contained.
- **Geographic** — by territory/region; standard in branch banking.
- **Customer-based** — by client segment (retail, corporate, NRI).
- **Process/Equipment** — by machine or production stage.
- **Matrix** — dual reporting (functional + project manager).

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"FD-GC-PM-M"** — *Functions, Divisions, Geography, Customers, Process, Matrix*. Recall order matches frequency in RBI Grade B situational MCQs.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Why Departmentation Sits Inside 'Organising'
Henri Fayol's *Organising* function converts the plan into structure. Division of work first fragments the job; Departmentation then **clusters** those fragments so that specialisation, accountability and coordination become possible. Only after departments exist can authority be *delegated downward* and a *span of control* be fixed. RBI Phase-II examiners routinely test this order — placing Departmentation under "Planning" is a guaranteed half-mark loss.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix — Commonly Confused Pairs

| Feature | Division of Work | Departmentation | Delegation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nature | Analytical (breaking down) | Structural (grouping) | Operational (transfer) |
| Direction | Horizontal, task-focused | Horizontal, unit-focused | Vertical, superior→subordinate |
| Pre-requisite | Objectives/Plan | Division of work | Departmentation |
| Output | Task list | Org chart departments | Authority assignments |
| Linked concept | Specialisation | Coordination, accountability | Span of control, decentralisation |

#### Bases of Departmentation — When Each Fits

1. **Functional** — best for single-product, small/medium firms; achieves deep specialisation but suffers silo bias.
2. **Divisional/Product** — large multi-product firms (e.g., a bank with Retail, Corporate, Treasury, International divisions).
3. **Geographic** — organisations with dispersed branches; classic Indian scheduled commercial banks (Zonal → Regional → Branch).
4. **Customer-based** — distinct service needs per segment (priority-sector, NRI, corporate, retail).
5. **Process/Equipment** — manufacturing units where machinery dictates flow (assembly, machining, finishing).
6. **Matrix** — project-based firms where an employee reports to *both* a functional and a project manager; tested almost every RBI cycle.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** *A large public-sector bank has set up separate divisions for Retail Banking, Corporate Banking, Treasury and International Banking, each headed by a Chief General Manager. Which basis of departmentation is the bank using, and what is its primary advantage?*
(a) Functional departmentation; deep specialisation in marketing
(b) Geographic departmentation; regional autonomy
(c) Divisional/Product departmentation; accountability for profit per product line
(d) Customer departmentation; better service to retail clients
*(Choose the correct option and justify.)*

#### Solution:
- The four divisions mirror **product/business lines**, not regions or customer types alone.
- Hence the basis = **Divisional (Product) Departmentation**, i.e., option **(c)**.
- Each CGM is accountable for **profit, revenue and costs of that division**, allowing performance appraisal per product.
- Divisional heads act as profit-centre managers, similar to Strategic Business Units.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students pick (a) Functional because the divisions *look* like functions. The giveaway is the word "Retail, Corporate, Treasury, International" — these are **product lines**, not internal support functions like HR or Finance. Functional departmentation groups similar *activities*; divisional departmentation groups complete *value chains* around a product.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases, Traps and Adjacent Links

| Scenario / Trap | Correct Treatment |
|---|---|
| "Departmentation eliminates the need for coordination" | False — it *increases* the need for inter-departmental coordination, liaison roles and integration mechanisms. |
| Treating Matrix as just another basis | Matrix is a **structure overlay** that combines functional + project reporting; it produces dual authority, hence higher conflict potential. |
| Equating Span of Control with Departmentation | Span = subordinates a manager supervises; it is determined *after* departments are formed. They are sequential, not synonymous. |
| Choosing only one ideal basis | No basis is ideal universally — it must match product diversity, geography, customer mix and technology. |
| Branch-vs-Zonal confusion in banks | Bank branches = Geographic unit; Zonal/Regional offices = higher-tier Geographic divisions, not a different basis. |
| Over-decentralisation pushback | Excessive divisional autonomy can weaken top-management control — a classic limitation cited in RBI descriptive answers. |

#### Two Advanced Practice Prompts

1. **Descriptive (10 marks):** *"Departmentation is the foundation of delegation and span of control." Discuss in the context of a nationalised bank.* — Expected points: structural step, link to delegation/span, banking examples (Zonal, Regional, Branch), Matrix in IT projects, accountability and decentralisation benefits, plus limitation of inter-branch conflict.
2. **Case MCQ:** A fintech firm assigns every analyst to a product squad *and* a functional guild (Risk, Tech, Analytics). Identify the structure: **Matrix departmentation**. Common wrong answer: Functional — because squads *look* like functions.

> 💡 **High-Yield Revision Hook:** For RBI descriptive answers, use the **3-link chain** sentence: *"Departmentation groups activities → enables delegation downward → fixes span of control, thereby completing the structural design."* Examiners reward this exact linkage.
