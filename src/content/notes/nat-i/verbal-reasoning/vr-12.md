# Grammar: Agreement and Usage

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Grammar: Agreement and Usage** on NAT-I (NTS) Verbal Reasoning tests whether you can spot the grammatically correct sentence among four options. Roughly 1–2 items appear, each carrying ~1% of the total score. Three rules decide almost every answer.

- **Subject–verb agreement**: a verb's number (singular/plural) is dictated by the true grammatical subject, never by a nearby noun buried inside a prepositional phrase.
- **Pronoun–antecedent agreement**: a pronoun must match its antecedent in **number**, **gender**, and **person** (1st/2nd/3rd).
- **Parallelism & tense**: list items share one grammatical form, and verbs stay in one tense unless time logically shifts.

| Term | What it controls | Singular marker |
|---|---|---|
| Subject | Number/person of the verb | "The committee **is** voting" |
| Antecedent | Number/gender of pronoun | "Each student brings **his/her** book" |
| Collective noun | Singular when unit, plural when individuals | "The team **is** united" |
| Indefinite pronoun | Always singular verb | "Everyone **is** ready" |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"Find the real subject, ignore the noise."** Circle the verb, then trace backward word-by-word through prepositional phrases (of, with, along with, as well as) until you hit the doer of the action. That word — not the nearest noun — controls agreement.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Rule Set

NAT-I agreement items reward grammatical precision, not intuition. Each option typically violates a single rule while sounding colloquially fine. Memorise the rule set below; every worked item routes through one of these.

| Rule | Pattern | Correct example |
|---|---|---|
| Subject–verb number | Verb matches true subject, not intervening noun | "The box of chocolates **is** on the table" |
| Indefinite pronoun | each, every, either, neither, someone, anyone, everybody, nobody → singular verb | "Each of the players **has** a jersey" |
| Collective noun | Singular when group acts as one; plural when individuals act separately | "The jury **is** deliberating" / "The jury **disagree** among themselves" |
| Compound subject with *and* | Usually plural | "Ali and Sara **are** coming" |
| Compound subject with *or/nor* | Verb agrees with nearest subject | "Either she or they **are** coming" |
| Relative pronoun | *who* → persons; *which* → things; *that* → either | "The man **who** called is my uncle" |
| Pronoun case | *who* = subject; *whom* = object | "**Whom** did you call?" |
| Parallelism | List items share form (all -ing, all infinitives, all nouns) | "She likes **reading, writing, and hiking**" |
| Tense consistency | Stay in one tense unless time shifts | "He **walked** to the shop and **bought** milk" |
| Modifier placement | Modifier must be next to the word it modifies | "**Running quickly**, he crossed the line" |

#### Comparison Matrix: Easily Confused Pairs

| Feature | Subject–verb agreement | Pronoun–antecedent agreement |
|---|---|---|
| What must match | Subject and verb in **number** and **person** | Pronoun and antecedent in **number, gender, person** |
| Tested with | Indefinite pronouns, collective nouns, intervening phrases | Singular *they*, *his/her*, compound antecedents |
| Classic NAT-I trap | Plural verb after "each of the…" | "their" with singular *each student* |
| Diagnostic question | "Who/what performs the action?" | "Whom does this pronoun replace?" |

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

#### Question:
Choose the grammatically correct sentence:
(a) Each of the students have submitted their assignment.
(b) The committee are divided in their opinions.
(c) The bag of apples are rotten.
(d) Either the manager or his assistants is attending the meeting.

#### Solution:
Evaluate each option against the agreement rules.

- (a) **Each** is an indefinite pronoun → singular. Verb must be *has*; pronoun must be *his/her/its*. "Each of the students **has** submitted **his or her** assignment." → ❌
- (b) "The committee" is a collective noun acting as individuals divided in opinion. When members act separately, the plural verb is acceptable: "**are** divided in **their** opinions." → ✅
- (c) The true subject is *bag* (singular), not *apples*. Correct: "The bag of apples **is** rotten." → ❌
- (d) Compound subject with *or*: verb agrees with the nearer subject, *assistants* (plural). Correct: "Either the manager or his assistants **are** attending the meeting." → ❌

#### Answer: (b)

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Option (b) trips students because Pakistani English textbooks often teach that collective nouns are *always* singular. In formal NAT-I phrasing, "the committee **are** divided" is acceptable because the emphasis is on individual members disagreeing. Watch for cues like "divided," "disagreeing," "among themselves" that signal plural usage.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Boundary Conditions

Advanced NAT-I items exploit the grey zones of agreement rules. Master these five traps before attempting mock papers.

1. **Indefinite pronoun + prepositional phrase**: "Each of the five hundred tickets **has** been sold." The plural noun *tickets* sits inside the phrase; the subject remains the singular *each*.
2. **Singular *they***: Increasingly accepted when gender is unknown — "A student must bring **their** ID." NAT-I still favours **his or her**, but recognise *their* as an emerging acceptable form.
3. **Subject after verb**: In inverted clauses ("There **are** three reasons…"), the verb agrees with the *following* noun, not *there*. With existential *there*, the noun decides.
4. **Measure words and units**: "Five kilometres **is** a long distance." Units of measurement, money, and time take a singular verb when treated as a single quantity.
5. **Gerunds vs infinitives as subject**: A gerund phrase takes a singular verb: "**Swimming** is fun." A list of gerunds remains singular: "**Swimming, diving, and surfing** are popular."

#### Common Mistakes Catalogue

| # | Mistake | Correction | Memory cue |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Less people attended" | "**Fewer** people" (countable) | Fewer = countable, Less = uncountable |
| 2 | "Whom called you?" | "**Who** called you?" (subject) | Substitute *he/him*: "**He** called" → *who* |
| 3 | "She likes swim, run, and to hike" | "…**swimming, running, and hiking**" | All three end in -ing |
| 4 | "Running quickly, the finish line was crossed" | "Running quickly, **he** crossed the finish line" | Dangling modifier needs the doer nearby |
| 5 | "He done it" / "He do it" | "He **did** it" / "He **does** it" | Past simple needs *-ed* / irregular form |

#### Connections to Adjacent Verbal Topics

Agreement questions overlap with **Sentence Completion** (the correct option must be both logical and grammatically consistent) and **Error Identification** (which asks you to locate, not fix, the same agreement faults). Strong agreement mastery therefore lifts your accuracy across roughly 8% of the Verbal section, not just the headline 3%.

#### Two Advanced Practice Prompts

1. **Pick the correct option** for: "The data [is/are] inconclusive, and [it/they] suggest [further/further] study." Justify each verb choice using the measure-word rule and pronoun-antecedent agreement.
2. **Rewrite for parallelism**: "The lecturer explained the theory, demonstrated the experiment, and was discussing results." Identify the broken form and supply a corrected version using parallel -ing verbs.

> 💡 **High-Yield Revision Hook:** On test day, read the underlined portion aloud. If the verb number doesn't match the subject (ignoring the phrase clutter between them), eliminate. Repeat for each pronoun and for any list — that single auditory sweep clears ~80% of NAT-I agreement distractors.
