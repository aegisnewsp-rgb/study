# Question Types — Form, Note, Table, Flow-Chart Completion — IELTS Listening (Hong Kong) Notes

The five "completion" question families used across IELTS Listening: form, note, table, flow-chart and summary completion. These require the candidate to fill in blanks using words from the recording within a stated word limit. Common in Parts 1 and 4 of the Listening test.

> Verify the live Listening question types on https://ielts.org/ before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Form completion:** fill in blanks on a form (registration form, application form, booking form).
- **Note completion:** fill in blanks in informal notes.
- **Table completion:** fill in blanks in a table (categories, dates, names, prices).
- **Flow-chart completion:** fill in blanks in a process diagram.
- **Summary completion:** fill in blanks in a summary of the recording.
- **Word limit:** typically "ONE WORD AND/OR A NUMBER", "NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS", "NO MORE THAN THREE WORDS".
- **Source of answers:** words from the recording (no synonyms).

#### Examiner traps

- Exceeding the word limit — the answer is wrong.
- Using synonyms instead of the exact word from the recording.
- Forgetting to use plural forms correctly.
- Missing the answer because the recording moves on quickly.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Form completion

The candidate is given a form with blanks. The recording provides the information to fill in the blanks.

**Example**:

```
Booking Form
Name: _______________
Date of arrival: _______________
Number of guests: _______________
```

The recording:
- "Could I have your name, please?" "Sure, it's John Smith."
- "When are you arriving?" "On the 15th of March."
- "How many in your party?" "Just two — me and my wife."

**Answers**: John Smith, 15 March, 2 (or "two").

#### Note completion

The candidate is given informal notes with blanks. The recording provides the information.

**Example**:

```
Notes on Library Membership

- Bring: photo ID
- Membership fee: _______________
- Opening hours: _______________
```

**Tips**:

- Read the notes to predict what kind of information is needed (number, date, name, etc.).
- Listen for keywords that match the structure of the notes.
- Use the word limit strictly.

#### Table completion

The candidate is given a table with categories and missing entries.

**Example**:

```
Library Sections
| Section | Floor | Special Features |
|---------|-------|------------------|
| Fiction | 1st | _______________ |
| Reference | 2nd | Computer access |
| Periodicals | 3rd | _______________ |
```

**Tips**:

- Follow the columns and rows carefully.
- Listen for the order of items (typically the order of the recording).
- Use the word limit.

#### Flow-chart completion

The candidate is given a process diagram with blanks.

**Example**:

```
Recycling Process
  Step 1: Collect paper
  Step 2: _______________
  Step 3: _______________
  Step 4: Distribute
```

**Tips**:

- Follow the order of the process.
- Use the word limit.
- Note any arrows or labels that indicate flow.

#### Summary completion

The candidate is given a summary of the recording with blanks. The summary typically uses the main structure of the recording.

**Example**:

```
The library offers a wide range of services. Members can borrow up to
ten books at a time, with a borrowing period of _______________ weeks.
The library also provides _______________ access for those with
disabilities. Membership fees are _______________
```

**Tips**:

- Read the summary to predict the structure of the recording.
- Listen for the keywords that match the summary.
- Use the word limit.

#### General tips for completion items

1. **Predict:** read the question and predict what kind of information is needed.
2. **Listen actively:** focus on the keywords from the question.
3. **Note:** write the answer as soon as you hear it. Don't wait.
4. **Check:** after the recording ends (and during transfer time on paper), check spelling and word limits.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-paper links

- **Part 1**: form and table completion are common.
- **Part 4**: note, table, flow-chart, and summary completion are common.
- **Reading**: similar completion techniques.

#### Exam technique

- Always check the word limit. One word too many is wrong.
- Use words from the recording, not synonyms.
- For completion items, predict the type of information before listening.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "I can use synonyms in completion items." No — you must use words from the recording.
- "Numbers don't count as words." Numbers do count; "two" or "2" is one word/number; "twenty-one" or "21" is one word/number.
- "I can leave blanks and come back." You can, but it's risky.
