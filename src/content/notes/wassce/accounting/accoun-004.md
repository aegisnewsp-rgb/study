# Trial Balance

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

A **Trial Balance (TB)** is a statement — never an account — extracted from the ledger at the end of an accounting period to verify that **total debit balances equal total credit balances**.

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Arithmetic check on the double-entry system |
| Format | Three columns: Particulars, Debit (GH¢), Credit (GH¢) |
| Core rule | Σ Debit Balances = Σ Credit Balances |
| If unequal | Difference posted to a **Suspense Account** on the shorter side |

#### Exam pointers:
- A balanced TB does **not** prove the books are error-free — only that totals agree.
- **Closing stock** appears only on the credit side as an adjustment; it is not a ledger debit balance.
- Errors like *error of principle* and *error of original entry* leave the TB balanced and are **not** disclosed.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Definition and Purpose

A **Trial Balance** is a working paper drawn up after posting all ledger entries and balancing the accounts. It lists every ledger balance on a given date and tests the arithmetic accuracy of the double-entry recording process. When the two column totals differ, the imbalance is transferred to a **Suspense Account** on the shorter side so the books still balance for the preparation of final accounts.

#### Format and Extraction

The TB is presented with three columns: the account name in the **Particulars** column, debit balances in the middle, and credit balances on the right, all in **Ghana Cedis (GH¢)**. Balances are listed in a conventional order — assets, liabilities, capital, income and expenditure — before revenue items.

| Account | Balance Type | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Cash, Stock, Debtors, Fixed Assets | Debit | GH¢15,000 |
| Creditors, Capital, Sales, Accrued Expenses | Credit | GH¢22,000 |
| Purchases, Expenses, Drawings | Debit | GH¢8,500 |
| Purchases Returns, Income, Prepaid Expenses | Credit | GH¢1,500 |

#### Errors Revealed vs. Errors Not Revealed

The TB discloses **one-sided errors** such as a posting to the wrong side, a casting error in a ledger account, or the omission of one half of a double entry. It does **not** disclose *errors of original entry* (same wrong amount on both sides), *errors of principle* (correct amount, wrong class of account), compensating errors, or complete omission of a transaction.

#### Common Exam Patterns

WASSCE questions typically ask candidates to (i) extract a TB from a given list of ledger balances, (ii) insert missing figures given a difference and identify the Suspense Account entry, or (iii) redraft a TB after passing correction journal entries. Expect 10–20 marks per paper on this skill.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Adjustments

Several year-end adjustments alter TB totals even though they are not ledger balances. **Closing stock** is inserted on the credit side as the closing entry of the Trading Account. **Accrued income** and **prepaid expenses** sit on the debit and credit sides respectively after adjustment. **Depreciation**, **bad debts** and **provision for doubtful debts** reduce the value of fixed assets and debtors on the debit side, while **discounts allowed/received** sit on the debit/credit sides as expenses or income.

#### Suspense Account Mechanics

When the TB fails to agree, the difference is parked in a Suspense Account so that final accounts can still be prepared. The Suspense balance is then cleared by journal entries that correct the underlying errors. A frequent trap is **placing the Suspense Account on the wrong side** during correction, which leaves a residual imbalance.

#### Connections to Final Accounts

The adjusted TB is the direct source of figures for the **Trading Account**, **Profit and Loss Account** and **Balance Sheet**. Every WASSCE final accounts question is therefore preceded by a TB question — mastering extraction and adjustment is non-negotiable.

#### Common Mistakes

- Treating the balanced TB as proof of accuracy (it only confirms totals).
- Posting accrued expenses to the debit side; they are **liabilities**.
- Forgetting **contra entries** (e.g., cash banked directly) when extracting balances.
- Confusing the debit balance of the Bank Account with that of the Cash Account in contra situations.

#### Practice Prompts

1. Given ledger balances totalling GH¢48,200 debit and GH¢47,650 credit, calculate the Suspense Account figure and state on which side it appears.
2. From the following balances, extract a Trial Balance and show the effect of closing stock of GH¢3,400: Capital GH¢20,000; Debtors GH¢6,500; Creditors GH¢4,800; Purchases GH¢18,200; Sales GH¢25,600; Stock (opening) GH¢5,000; Rent GH¢1,700.
