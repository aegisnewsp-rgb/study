# Legal Maxims

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Legal Maxims** are short Latin or English phrases that compress a whole legal doctrine into a few words. In CLAT they appear as either stand-alone MCQs or inside passages, so you need both the meaning and the right application.

- **Mens Rea** = guilty mind; **Actus Reus** = guilty act. Both are needed for most crimes under Section 6 IPC.
- **Audi Alteram Partem** = hear the other side; pairs with **Nemo Judex In Causa Sua** (no one should be judge in their own cause). Together they form natural justice.
- **Caveat Emptor** = let the buyer beware; offset by Section 14 of the Sale of Goods Act, 1930 and the Consumer Protection Act, 2019.
- **Ignorantia Juris Non Excusat** = ignorance of law is no excuse, codified in the maxim's general acceptance.
- **Volenti Non Fit Injuria** = to a willing person, no injury is done. Applies only to free, informed consent to risk.

> Tip: In CLAT, scenario questions test application, not translation. Always match the fact pattern, not just the wording.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### What counts as a "maxim"

A maxim is a distilled rule expressed in Latin (or occasionally English) that judges cite as shorthand for a doctrine. In Indian common-law courts, maxims survive even after codification because the IPC, the Indian Contract Act, 1872, and the Constitution, 1950 often restate, rather than replace, them.

#### Three families of maxims

CLAT groups the most-tested maxims into three clusters. Each cluster demands a different reading skill.

| Family | What it governs | Representative maxims |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Substantive law — Criminal | Mens rea, fault, and excuses | Mens Rea, Actus Reus, Ignorantia Juris Non Excusat, Volenti Non Fit Injuria, in pari delicto |
| Substantive law — Contract | Interpretation and enforceability | Pacta Sunt Servanda, Expressio Unius Est Exclusio Alterius, Noscitur a Sociis, Ejusdem Generis, Caveat Emptor |
| Evidence & Procedure | Fair trial and proof | Res Ipsa Loquitur, Audi Alteram Partem, Nemo Judex In Causa Sua, Habeas Corpus, Ubi Jus Ibi Remedium |

#### How CLAT tests the topic

- **Meaning recall**: "Identify the meaning of 'Pacta Sunt Servanda'." Direct, one-line answers win these.
- **Principle-to-maxim match**: A short principle is given, and you select the Latin phrase that states it.
- **Fact-to-maxim match**: A 4–6 line scenario describes consent, negligence, bias, or buyer grievance. Pick the maxim that resolves the legal outcome.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Mapping maxims to Indian statutes

Knowing the statutory anchor turns a recalled maxim into an applied answer. The table below links each high-frequency maxim to the provision a CLAT passage will most often invoke.

| Maxim | English meaning | Statutory anchor in Indian law |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Actus Non Facit Reum Nisi Mens Sit Rea | An act alone does not make one guilty without a guilty mind | Section 6 IPC, 1860; General Exceptions, Chapter IV |
| Ignorantia Juris Non Excusat | Ignorance of law is no excuse | Section 76 IPC (limited exception); accepted as a general rule of criminal liability |
| Caveat Emptor | Let the buyer beware | Sale of Goods Act, 1930 (read with Section 14 implied conditions); Consumer Protection Act, 2019 |
| Volenti Non Fit Injuria | No injury to one who consents | Tort doctrine; limited by Sections 23 and 25 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872 |
| Audi Alteram Partem | Hear the other side | Article 14, Constitution of India; natural justice in administrative law |
| Nemo Judex In Causa Sua | No one should be a judge in their own cause | Article 14; Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India (1978) line of cases |
| Res Ipsa Loquitur | The thing speaks for itself | Evidence Act, 1872 — Sections 3 and 101 read with negligence pleadings |
| Pacta Sunt Servanda | Agreements must be kept | Section 37, Indian Contract Act, 1872 (obligation to perform) |
| Ejusdem Generis | Of the same kind | General Clauses Act, 1897; interpretation of statutes |
| Ubi Jus Ibi Remedium | Where there is a right, there is a remedy | Article 32 and Article 226, Constitution of India |

#### Common traps examiners set

- Mixing **Audi Alteram Partem** (hear the other side) with **Nemo Judex In Causa Sua** (no bias). A question describing a biased tribunal tests the second, not the first.
- Applying **Caveat Emptor** to e-commerce or processed food sales, where Section 14 of the Sale of Goods Act imposes implied conditions on merchantability and fitness.
- Treating **Volenti** as a blanket defence in negligence; it fails when consent was not informed or the injury arose from a different risk than the one accepted.

#### Practice prompts

1. A passenger signs a waiver and is injured during a stunt at an amusement park. Identify the maxim that the defendant's counsel will raise, and the counter-argument grounded in the Indian Contract Act.
2. A municipal officer suspends a shopkeeper's licence without giving him a chance to be heard. Name the two maxims of natural justice violated, and the constitutional article that protects this right.

> Tip: For every maxim you memorise, write one line of application. That single application line is what CLAT's passage-based questions reward.
