# Environmental and Nutritional Pathology

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your FMGE paper.

Environmental and nutritional pathology covers disease from **xenobiotics**, **physical insults**, and **nutrient imbalance**. The high-yield triad is protein-energy malnutrition (marasmus vs kwashiorkor), heavy-metal poisoning, and pneumoconioses.

- **Marasmus** = total calorie deficiency → muscle wasting, no edema, "old man" facies.
- **Kwashiorkor** = protein deficiency with adequate calories → edema, fatty liver, flag-sign hair, skin lesions.
- **Lead poisoning** → microcytic anemia with **basophilic stippling**, gingival **lead line**, encephalopathy in children.
- **Aflatoxin B1** → hepatocellular carcinoma via **TP53 R249S** mutation (synergy with hepatitis B).
- **Silicosis** → silicotic nodules, predisposes to TB; **asbestosis** → **ferruginous bodies**, mesothelioma.
- **Acetaminophen** overdose → centrilobular hepatic necrosis via **NAPQI** after glutathione depletion.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with weeks to months before FMGE.

#### Mechanisms of Cell Injury
Environmental agents damage cells via **free radical–mediated lipid peroxidation**, DNA adduct formation, and enzyme inhibition. Reactive oxygen species (ROS) are quenched by **vitamin E, vitamin C, and glutathione**. Excess ROS leaves a residual **lipofuscin** ("wear-and-tear" pigment) in long-lived cells like cardiac myocytes and neurons.

#### Protein-Energy Malnutrition

| Feature | Marasmus | Kwashiorkor |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | Total calorie deficiency | Protein deficiency with near-normal calories |
| Edema | Absent | Present (hypoalbuminemia) |
| Liver | Normal | **Fatty infiltration** |
| Skin/hair | Dry, sparse | **Flag sign**, flaky paint dermatosis |
| Age group | <18 months (infants) | Older weaned children |

#### Vitamin Deficiency High-Yield Table

| Vitamin | Deficiency syndrome | Key clue |
|---|---|---|
| A (retinol) | Night blindness, Bitot spots, xerophthalmia | Squamous metaplasia |
| B1 (thiamine) | Beriberi, Wernicke-Korsakoff | **Give thiamine before glucose** |
| B12 (cobalamin) | Megaloblastic anemia, **subacute combined degeneration** | Methylmalonyl-CoA accumulation |
| C (ascorbic) | Scurvy | Perifollicular hemorrhages, corkscrew hairs |
| D (cholecalciferol) | Rickets (children), osteomalacia (adults) | ↓ Calcium, ↑ PTH |

#### Toxicology Snapshots
- **Arsenic**: raindrop skin pigmentation, palmar-plantar hyperkeratosis, Mees lines on nails.
- **Mercury**: acrodynia (pink disease), Minamata disease (cerebellar ataxia from contaminated fish).
- **Cadmium**: **Itai-Itai disease** — proximal renal tubular damage, osteomalacia.
- **Carbon tetrachloride (CCl₄)**: classic free-radical hepatotoxin, centrilobular steatosis → fibrosis.

#### Pneumoconioses Comparison

| Disease | Exposure | Pathognomonic finding |
|---|---|---|
| Anthracosis | Carbon dust (benign) | Black pigment in lung |
| Coal worker pneumoconiosis | Coal dust | Centrilobular nodules, progressive massive fibrosis |
| Silicosis | Silica (mining, sandblasting) | **Silicotic nodules**, birefringent crystals, ↑ TB risk |
| Asbestosis | Asbestos | **Ferruginous bodies**, lower-lobe fibrosis, mesothelioma |

#### Exam Pattern
FMGE Pathology contributes ~3% weight; environmental and nutrition sections typically yield 1–2 MCQs that pair a clinical vignette with a specific toxin or vitamin. Image-based questions on **ferruginous bodies**, **basophilic stippling**, or **Bitot spots** appear regularly.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for deeper mastery.

#### Drug-Induced Injury Pathways
**Acetaminophen** is metabolized to a toxic intermediate **NAPQI**, normally detoxified by glutathione. Overdose depletes glutathione, allowing NAPQI to bind hepatocyte proteins and cause **centrilobular (zone 3) necrosis**. The antidote is **N-acetylcysteine**, which replenishes glutathione.

**Thalidomide** caused phocomelia in the 1960s by inhibiting **angiogenesis** (ceramide pathway, anti-TNFα), demonstrating a teratogen acting during limb-bud formation (days 20–36 post-fertilization).

#### Carcinogen–Mutation Pairings
- **Aflatoxin B1** → G→T transversion at **codon 249 of TP53** → hepatocellular carcinoma.
- **Vinyl chloride** → hepatic **angiosarcoma**.
- **Benzene** → **acute myeloid leukemia** (not lymphoid), via marrow metabolite hydroquinone.
- **Asbestos + tobacco smoke** → synergistic 50-fold ↑ lung cancer risk; asbestos alone preferentially drives mesothelioma.

#### Antioxidant Defense and Cancer Caveats
Selenium, vitamin E, and beta-carotene were tested as chemopreventive agents. The **CARET** and **ATBC** trials showed beta-carotene supplementation **increased** lung cancer incidence in smokers and asbestos-exposed workers — a high-yield negative result.

#### Adjacent Topic Links
Lead poisoning links to **heme synthesis** (inhibits ALA dehydratase + ferrochelatase, with elevated **protoporphyrin IX**), explaining microcytic anemia with normal iron stores. Chronic alcoholism links Wernicke encephalopathy to **mammillary body hemorrhages** and links folate deficiency to megaloblastic anemia.

#### Common Exam Traps
1. Confusing **aflatoxin** (hepatotoxic, TP53) with **ochratoxin** (nephrotoxic, Balkan endemic nephropathy).
2. Equating **anthracosis** (harmless carbon pigment) with **coal worker pneumoconiosis** (fibrotic disease).
3. Forgetting that **silicosis** — not silicosis + TB — is associated with **Caplan syndrome** (rheumatoid pneumoconiosis).

#### Practice Prompts
1. A weaned 3-year-old presents with pitting edema, hepatomegaly, and reddish, sparse hair. Identify the deficiency state and the hepatic histologic change.
2. A battery-recycling worker develops microcytic anemia, wrist drop, and a bluish gingival line. Name the toxin, the inhibited enzymes, and the antidote principle.
