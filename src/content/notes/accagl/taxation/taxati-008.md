# International Taxation & Transfer Pricing

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**International Taxation & Transfer Pricing (TP)** governs how profits are allocated between associated enterprises (AEs) operating across Pakistan and foreign jurisdictions, anchored in **Section 108 of the Income Tax Ordinance, 2001 (ITO 2001)** and Rules 18A–21 of the Income Tax Rules, 1982. The governing standard is the **Arm's Length Principle (ALP)** — prices between related parties must mirror prices between independent parties in comparable transactions.

Pakistan follows the **five OECD methods** (Rule 18A): CUP, Resale Price, Cost Plus, TNMM, and Profit Split. A multinational with consolidated group revenue ≥ **PKR 50 billion** must file a **Country-by-Country Report (CbCR)** alongside a Master File and Local File.

- **FAR analysis** (Functions, Assets, Risks) drives method selection — not rule-of-thumb mark-ups.
- **Section 152 WHT** on payments to non-resident AEs applies the lower of domestic rate or **DTA rate** when a Tax Residency Certificate (TRC) is furnished.
- **Penalty under Section 182AA** for missing documentation reaches the higher of **1% of turnover or PKR 5 million**, even with no tax shortfall.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Legal Framework and Core Definitions

Pakistan's TP regime sits on a three-pillar structure: **ITO 2001 Section 108** (power to adjust AE transactions), **Rules 18A–21** (methodology and documentation), and the **OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines (2022)** for interpretative support. An **Associated Enterprise** exists where one person participates directly or indirectly in management, control, or capital of another — including common ownership of ≥ 50% across a chain.

A **controlled transaction** covers goods, services, intangibles, financing, and cost-sharing arrangements between AEs. Each transaction must be benchmarked against an arm's length range using one of the OECD methods.

#### Method Selection and Comparability

Selection follows **rule of best method** — pick the method that produces the most reliable result given comparability. Comparability rests on five factors: **property, services, contractual terms, economic circumstances, and business strategies**.

| Method | Tested Variable | When Preferred |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CUP | Price itself | Identical/similar goods traded with third parties |
| Resale Price | Gross margin | Marketing/distribution entities |
| Cost Plus | Gross mark-up on costs | Contract manufacturing or routine services |
| TNMM | Net profit margin (Operating profit/Net sales) | Complex intangibles; routine tested party |
| Profit Split | Combined profit allocation | Highly integrated AEs, unique intangibles |

#### Documentation and BEPS Alignment

A multinational operating in Pakistan must maintain a **Master File** (global group overview), **Local File** (Pakistan-specific controlled transactions), and — if consolidated revenue exceeds **PKR 50 billion** — a **CbCR**. BEPS Actions 8–10 align returns with **value creation** using the **DEMPE framework** (Development, Enhancement, Maintenance, Protection, Exploitation) for intangibles.

- TP exam questions typically test method justification, not arithmetic complexity.
- Penalty exposure under Section 182AA applies even when no tax is understated.
- **Advance Pricing Agreement (APA)** under Section 100AA locks pricing for 5 prospective years.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Worked Example — TNMM Benchmark

A Pakistani subsidiary resells imported electronics from its Hong Kong parent. Cost of purchase: PKR 80m. Resale revenue: PKR 100m. Operating costs (excluding purchases): PKR 8m. Operating profit: **PKR 12m**.

TNMM Tested Margin = 12 / 100 = **12%**. Comparable distributors of similar branded goods show operating margins between **9% (Q1) and 14% (Q3)**. The 12% result falls inside the **arm's length range** — no adjustment. If the margin were 17%, FBR could adjust upward to the 75th percentile under Rule 21.

#### Common Mistakes and Examiner Traps

| Mistake | Why It Fails | Correct Approach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Applying flat 10% mark-up | Ignores FAR; no comparables | Use comparables dataset for industry-specific margin |
| Selecting CUP for unique intangibles | No reliable uncontrolled price exists | Apply Profit Split or TNMM |
| Forgetting TRC for DTA relief | Section 152(2A) denies treaty rate | Obtain TRC before payment due date |
| Treating APA as permanent | APAs cover future years only | Apply for renewal before expiry |

#### Adjacent Topics and Exam Strategy

TP integrates directly with **Permanent Establishment (Section 2(41) ITO 2001)**, **WHT on cross-border payments (Section 152)**, and **Mutual Agreement Procedure (MAP)** under Pakistan's ~70 DTAs. ICAP PE-II Taxation and ACCA ATX/APM both allocate **10–15 marks** to a structured TP scenario: candidates should spend 18–22 minutes outlining ALP, performing a brief FAR, justifying one method, and computing an arm's length range.

#### Practice prompts:
1. A Pakistani software subsidiary licenses IP from a UAE parent for 4% of turnover. Justify whether this royalty rate satisfies the ALP given the parent's DEMPE functions.
2. A Lahore manufacturer supplies goods to a UK sister company below market. Compute the adjustment required using CUP data from an unrelated domestic buyer paying PKR 1,200 per unit while the controlled sale is at PKR 950.
