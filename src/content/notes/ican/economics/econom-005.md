# Theory of Production

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your ICAN Economics paper.

The **Theory of Production** explains how a firm turns factors of production (labour **L**, capital **K**) into goods using a given technology. The single output relationship is captured by **TP = f(L, K)**, where TP is total physical product in units of output. From this one function every product and cost curve in the syllabus is derived.

| Term | Formula | What it tells you |
|------|---------|-------------------|
| Total Product (TP) | TP = f(L, K) | Total output in units, given inputs L (workers) and K (capital units) |
| Average Product (AP) | AP = TP / L | Output per worker; units: output per worker, dimension [Q]/[L] |
| Marginal Product (MP) | MP = ΔTP / ΔL | Extra output from one more worker; units: output per worker |
| Total Cost (TC) | TC = FC + VC | Total spend in Naira (₦); FC fixed, VC variable |
| Marginal Cost (MC) | MC = ΔTC / ΔQ | Extra cost per extra unit, ₦ per unit |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"MAP the TP curve"** — when **M**P cuts **A**P from above, **A**P peaks; when **M**P hits zero, **T**P peaks. Memorise MP as the slope of TP, AP as the height of TP divided by L, and MC as the mirror image of MP on the cost side.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students working through the ICAN ATS or Skills Economics syllabus.

#### Product Curves and Their Relationships

In the short run, at least one factor (K) is fixed, so output responds only to changes in the variable factor (L). Three curves trace this response: TP (total output), AP (output per worker) and MP (the slope of TP). The AP–MP linkage is the most tested relationship on the ICAN paper.

> 📌 **Formula Check:** AP and MP share the same units (output per worker). MP is the *first derivative* of TP with respect to L in the continuous form: MP = dTP/dL.

The three geometric relationships to draw on a sketch are:

- **MP > AP** ⇒ AP is rising (TP curve convex to the L-axis).
- **MP = AP** ⇒ AP is at its **maximum**.
- **MP < AP** ⇒ AP is falling (TP curve concave to the L-axis).
- **MP = 0** ⇒ TP is at its **maximum**.
- **MP < 0** ⇒ TP falls as more labour is added.

#### Stages of Production

| Stage | Range of L | AP behaviour | MP behaviour | Producer's action |
|-------|------------|--------------|--------------|--------------------|
| Stage I | 0 → L₁ | Rising | MP > AP, MP rising | Irrational — too little labour relative to fixed capital |
| Stage II | L₁ → L₂ | Falling but positive | MP > 0 and falling | **Rational stage**; profit-maximising output lies here |
| Stage III | Beyond L₂ | Falling | MP negative | Irrational — adding workers reduces total output |

#### Law of Diminishing Returns vs Returns to Scale

These two principles are routinely confused in MCQs. The ICAN paper tests the distinction directly.

| Feature | Law of Diminishing Returns | Returns to Scale |
|---------|----------------------------|------------------|
| Time horizon | **Short run** | **Long run** |
| What changes | One factor (usually L); others fixed | **All** factors vary proportionately |
| Trigger | Adding successive units of the variable factor | Scaling all inputs up or down together |
| Outcome | MP eventually falls | **IRS**, **CRS** or **DRS** |
| Typical MCQ trap | Saying it applies when all factors change | Saying it applies when only one factor changes |

#### Cost Curves from Product Curves

Because TC = wL + rK (where w = wage rate in ₦/worker, r = rental rate of ₦/unit of capital), the cost curves are the supply-side mirror of the product curves.

> 📌 **Formula Check:** AC is minimised at the output where **MC = AC**, not where MP is maximised. MC equals w₦ divided by MP_L (in the short run with only labour variable): **MC = w / MP_L**.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

#### Question:
A firm uses only labour (L) with a wage rate of ₦5,000 per worker. Output at different employment levels is:

| L (workers) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|-------------|---|---|---|---|---|
| TP (units)  | 20 | 50 | 75 | 90 | 95 |

(a) Calculate AP and MP for each level of L. (b) Identify the stage of production where the firm should operate. (c) If the firm sells at ₦600 per unit, how many workers maximise profit?

#### Solution:

(a) Using AP = TP/L and MP = ΔTP/ΔL:

| L | TP | AP = TP/L | MP = ΔTP/ΔL |
|---|----|-----------|--------------|
| 1 | 20 | 20.00 | 20 |
| 2 | 50 | 25.00 | 30 |
| 3 | 75 | 25.00 | 25 |
| 4 | 90 | 22.50 | 15 |
| 5 | 95 | 19.00 | 5 |

(b) AP rises then falls, and MP is positive throughout. Production is therefore in **Stage II** for L between 2 and 5. The rational range begins where AP is maximised (L = 2 or 3) and ends just before MP turns zero (L just below 5).

(c) Hire labour up to the point where **MRP_L = w**. With constant price ₦600, MRP_L = 600 × MP_L. The wage cost per extra worker is ₦5,000, so hire while 600 × MP_L ≥ 5,000, i.e. while MP_L ≥ 8.33.

- L = 4: MP = 15; MRP = 600 × 15 = ₦9,000 > ₦5,000 → hire.
- L = 5: MP = 5; MRP = 600 × 5 = ₦3,000 < ₦5,000 → do **not** hire.

The profit-maximising workforce is **4 workers**, giving TP = 90 units and revenue = ₦54,000.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students often answer "L = 5 because TP is highest." Total revenue is not profit — the firm must also pay the wage of the fifth worker (₦5,000) against only ₦3,000 of extra revenue. Always equate MRP to the input price, not maximise TP.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for ICAN ATS candidates building full Microeconomic fluency.

#### Producer's Equilibrium with Isoquants and Isocosts

In the long run both L and K vary. An **isoquant** shows all (L, K) combinations producing the same output Q₀. It is downward sloping and convex to the origin because inputs are substitutable but imperfect substitutes. The slope of an isoquant is the **Marginal Rate of Technical Substitution (MRTS)**: how many units of K the firm gives up to gain one more unit of L while keeping output constant.

$$\text{MRTS}_{LK} = -\frac{\Delta K}{\Delta L} = \frac{MP_L}{MP_K}$$

The **isocost line** joins all (L, K) bundles costing the same total outlay C: $C = wL + rK$, giving slope $-w/r$ in the (L, K) plane.

The cost-minimising input bundle satisfies the tangency condition:

$$\text{MRTS}_{LK} = \frac{w}{r} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \frac{MP_L}{MP_K} = \frac{w}{r}$$

| Concept | Definition | Units / Slope |
|---------|------------|----------------|
| Isoquant | Locus of equal-output bundles (L, K) | Slope = MRTS_LK = MP_L/MP_K, dimensionless |
| Isocost | Locus of equal-cost bundles | Slope = −w/r (₦ per ₦ = dimensionless ratio) |
| Producer's equilibrium | Tangency of isoquant to lowest reachable isocost | MRTS_LK = w/r |
| Expansion path | Locus of cost-minimising bundles as output rises | Curves upward as K becomes relatively cheaper |

#### Edge Cases and Boundary Conditions

| Edge case | What happens | Why it matters for ICAN |
|-----------|--------------|--------------------------|
| Perfect substitutes (L and K are linear) | Isoquants are straight lines; corner solution, no tangency | MRTS is constant; firm uses only the cheaper input |
| Perfect complements (Leontief, e.g. one driver per truck) | Isoquants are L-shaped | No smooth substitution; ratio is fixed, MRTS undefined inside |
| Constant returns to scale (CRS) | Doubling all inputs doubles TP | Long-run AC is flat; perfect competition long-run supply is horizontal |
| Negative marginal product | TP falls with extra L | Defines the boundary of Stage III — never produce here |

#### Connections to Adjacent Topics

The product curves feed straight into **cost curves** (MC, AC, AVC), which feed into **revenue curves** (TR, AR, MR), which close the loop with **profit maximisation** (MC = MR). The Theory of Production is therefore the upstream half of the firm's optimisation problem; without mastering TP, AP and MP, the cost-minimisation and profit-maximisation derivations later in the syllabus cannot be derived from first principles.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. A firm's production function is **Q = 10√L** with K fixed and wage w = ₦2,000. Derive the MC curve and confirm the relationship MC = w/MP_L. (Hint: MP_L = dQ/dL = 5/√L, so MC = w√L / 5.)
2. Isoquants for two technologies cross. Explain, using the convexity and non-intersection properties, why this is impossible under a single production function — and what it implies about assuming a **single, well-defined** technology for ICAN exam questions.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"MRTS = Money Ratio at the Tangency"** — physical ratio of inputs on the left, money ratio (w/r) on the right, and they meet exactly where the isocost just kisses the isoquant. Read it as "**M**arginal **R**ate equals the **T**angent's **S**lope."
