# VI. Microscopic World II — HKDSE Chemistry Notes

This topic covers Topic VI of the Compulsory Part of HKDSE Chemistry. It addresses chemical cells (simple cells, electrochemistry, electrode potentials, the electrochemical series); non-electrolyte solutions and concentration; molar concentration and its calculations; dilution; and volumetric analysis.

> Verify the live HKDSE Chemistry Assessment Framework on https://www.hkeaa.edu.hk/ before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Chemical cells**: a cell that converts chemical energy into electrical energy. A simple cell has two different metals (electrodes) in an electrolyte solution.
- **Electrochemical series**: lists metals in order of their tendency to lose electrons (form cations). More reactive metals are higher in the series (more negative standard electrode potential).
- **Electrode potential (E°)**: the voltage produced when a half-cell is connected to the standard hydrogen electrode under standard conditions.
- **Concentration**: c = n/V (mol/dm³ or M). n = moles, V = volume in dm³.
- **Dilution**: c₁V₁ = c₂V₂ when diluting a solution.
- **Volumetric analysis**: titration — a precise method for determining the concentration of a solution using a standard solution of known concentration.
- **Acid-base titration**: indicator (or pH meter) detects the equivalence point.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing the anode with the cathode in an electrolytic vs. galvanic cell — in both cases, oxidation occurs at the anode.
- Forgetting that standard electrode potentials are defined against the standard hydrogen electrode (E° = 0 V by convention).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Chemical cells

**Simple galvanic cell**: two different metals (e.g. zinc and copper) in an electrolyte (e.g. dilute sulfuric acid). The more reactive metal (zinc) is the anode (oxidation: Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻); the less reactive metal (copper) is the cathode (reduction: 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂). Electrons flow from anode to cathode through the external circuit.

**Cell potential**: E°cell = E°cathode - E°anode. A positive E°cell indicates a spontaneous reaction.

**Standard hydrogen electrode (SHE)**: E° = 0 V by definition. Used as the reference for measuring standard electrode potentials.

#### Electrochemical series

The standard electrode potentials (E°) for common half-cells:

- F₂ + 2e⁻ → 2F⁻: +2.87 V (strongest oxidising agent)
- O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ → 2H₂O: +1.23 V
- Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu: +0.34 V
- 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂: 0.00 V (reference)
- Pb²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Pb: -0.13 V
- Fe²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Fe: -0.44 V
- Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Zn: -0.76 V
- Mg²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Mg: -2.37 V (strongest reducing agent)

A half-cell with a more positive E° has a stronger tendency to be reduced (acts as an oxidising agent). A half-cell with a more negative E° has a stronger tendency to be oxidised (acts as a reducing agent).

#### Concentration calculations

Molarity c = n/V (mol/dm³). Common conversions:
- 1 dm³ = 1000 cm³ = 1000 mL
- 1 mol/dm³ = 1 M
- moles = mass (g) / molar mass (g/mol)

For dilution: c₁V₁ = c₂V₂. When diluting, the number of moles stays constant.

For mixing: c_final × V_total = Σ c_i × V_i.

#### Volumetric analysis (acid-base titration)

**Equipment**: burette, pipette, conical flask, indicator.

**Procedure**:
1. Rinse the burette with the standard solution; fill and read the initial volume.
2. Pipette a fixed volume of the unknown solution into a conical flask.
3. Add a few drops of indicator (e.g. phenolphthalein).
4. Titrate by adding the standard solution slowly, swirling the flask, until the indicator changes colour (endpoint).
5. Record the final burette reading; calculate the titre volume.
6. Repeat until concordant titres (within 0.10 cm³) are obtained.

**Calculation**: c_aV_a × n_a = c_bV_b × n_b (where n is the stoichiometric coefficient from the balanced equation).

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-topic links

- **III. Metals**: the reactivity series is related to standard electrode potentials.
- **VII. Redox reactions, chemical cells and electrolysis**: electrolysis extends the concept of electrodes.
- **X. Chemical Equilibrium**: the Nernst equation relates E to concentration.

#### Exam technique

- **For cell potential questions**: identify the more reactive metal (more negative E°), write the half-equations, and apply E°cell = E°cathode - E°anode.
- **For titration questions**: write the balanced equation, identify the stoichiometric ratio, then apply c_aV_a × n_a = c_bV_b × n_b.
- **For dilution questions**: state c₁V₁ = c₂V₂ explicitly before solving.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "The cathode is always positive." In a galvanic cell the cathode is positive; in an electrolytic cell the cathode is connected to the negative terminal of the power supply.
- "Standard electrode potentials are measured in isolation." They are measured against the SHE by convention.
- "Molarity and molality are the same." Molarity is moles per litre of solution; molality is moles per kilogram of solvent.
