# International Organizations (UN, WTO, etc.)

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

An **international organization** is a treaty-based body of sovereign states created to pursue shared goals — peace, trade, health, human rights. The **United Nations (UN)**, founded **24 October 1945** under the Charter signed at San Francisco, succeeded the League of Nations and is the principal universal organization. The **World Trade Organization (WTO)**, established **1 January 1995** by the Marrakesh Agreement, regulates global trade under its own treaty and is *not* part of the UN system.

The **UN Security Council** has 15 members: 5 permanent (**P5** — China, France, Russian Federation, UK, US) holding **veto power**, plus 10 non-permanent members elected for two-year terms. WTO operates on **Most-Favoured-Nation (MFN)** treatment (GATT Article I), **National Treatment** (Article III), and **tariff bindings**.

- UN Charter is the constitutive treaty; WTO Agreement is the constitutive treaty of the WTO.
- ICJ sources of law: conventions, custom, general principles, judicial decisions (subsidiary).
- P5 abstention ≠ veto; reverse consensus rule applies in WTO dispute settlement.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Six Principal UN Organs (UN Charter, Chapters IV–X)

The UN Charter establishes six principal organs, each with a defined mandate. The **General Assembly (UNGA)** is deliberative and recommendatory (Article 10), budgetary (Article 17), and elects non-permanent Security Council members. The **Security Council (UNSC)** enforces peace and security, with binding decisions under Chapter VII (Article 25). **ECOSOC** coordinates economic and social work; the **Trusteeship Council** is suspended since 1994. The **ICJ** settles legal disputes and issues advisory opinions. The **Secretariat**, headed by the Secretary-General, carries out administrative duties.

#### UN Security Council Voting

Substantive decisions need 9 affirmative votes *including* the concurring votes of all P5 members. Procedural votes need 9 affirmatives with no veto applicability.

| Feature | UNSC | UNGA |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Members | 15 (5 permanent + 10 elected) | 193 member states |
| Voting on substance | 9 incl. all P5 | Simple majority (or 2/3 on key issues) |
| Decision effect | Binding under Art. 25 | Recommendatory, except budget |

#### WTO Principles (GATT 1994)

- **MFN (Article I)** — any advantage given to one member must be extended to all WTO members.
- **National Treatment (Article III)** — foreign goods, once past the border, must be treated no less favourably than domestic goods (no internal taxes/regulations protecting local products).
- **Tariff bindings** — members commit not to raise tariffs above negotiated ceilings.
- **Prohibition of quantitative restrictions (Article XI)** — bans import quotas and similar non-tariff barriers except in defined exceptions.

#### VCLT Treaty Interpretation (1969)

Article 31 sets the **general rule**: a treaty is interpreted in good faith per the ordinary meaning of its terms in context and in light of object and purpose. Article 32 permits recourse to preparatory work (**travaux préparatoires**) as a supplementary means to confirm meaning or resolve ambiguity.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### ICJ Jurisdiction and Sources of Law

The **International Court of Justice** hears contentious cases only with **state consent** — through a **compromis** (special agreement), a **jurisdictional clause** in a treaty, or **forum prorogatum** (subsequent acceptance). Under Article 38(1) of its Statute, the ICJ applies: (1) international conventions, (2) international custom as evidence of state practice accepted as law, (3) general principles of law, and (4) judicial decisions and the doctrines of highly qualified publicists as subsidiary means. The ICJ cannot prosecute individuals; that function belongs to the **International Criminal Court (ICC)** under the separate Rome Statute.

#### WTO Dispute Settlement (DSU)

The WTO's two-stage process begins with **consultations** (DSU Article 4). If unresolved, a **panel** is established by the **Dispute Settlement Body (DSB)**, which adopts the panel report unless there is **negative consensus** (the *reverse consensus* rule, Article 16.4) — effectively automatic adoption. Appeals go to the **Appellate Body**. Remedies include retaliation through suspended concessions.

#### Common LAT Traps

- Conflating **ICJ** (UN judicial organ, state consent required) with **ICC** (treaty-based, prosecutes individuals under the Rome Statute).
- Treating WTO as a UN agency — it holds observer status at UNGA but is independent.
- Assuming **P5 abstention** equals a veto; under UNSC practice since 1946, abstention does not block adoption.
- Believing **UNGA resolutions** are binding; they are recommendatory except for budgetary matters under Article 17.
- Confusing **GATT 1947** (provisional protocol) with **GATT 1994** (incorporated into the WTO Agreement with Understandings, Protocols, and Schedules).

#### Worked Scenario

> Question: A WTO member grants a bilateral tariff cut to its neighbour. A third member complains. Which principle is invoked?
> Answer: **MFN treatment under GATT Article I** — any advantage granted to any member must be extended immediately and unconditionally to all WTO members.

#### Practice Prompts

1. State the UNSC voting requirement for substantive decisions and explain whether a P5 abstention counts as a veto.
2. Identify the two stages of WTO dispute settlement and the role of the reverse consensus rule under DSU Article 16.4.
