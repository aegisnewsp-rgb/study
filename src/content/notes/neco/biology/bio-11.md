# Reproduction: Asexual and Sexual

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Reproduction is the biological process by which living organisms produce offspring to ensure continuity of life. **Asexual reproduction** uses one parent and produces genetically identical **clones** via **mitosis**, while **sexual reproduction** fuses male and female **gametes** (formed by **meiosis**) to create a **zygote** with restored **diploid (2n)** number.

- Asexual types to memorise: **binary fission** (Amoeba), **multiple fission** (Plasmodium), **budding** (Hydra, yeast), **spore formation** (Rhizopus, fern), **fragmentation** (Spirogyra, planaria), **vegetative propagation** (yam tuber, onion bulb, cassava cutting).
- Sexual reproduction generates **genetic variation** through **crossing over**, **independent assortment**, and **random fertilisation** — this is its main evolutionary advantage.
- In flowering plants, **pollination** (pollen transfer to stigma) must occur **before** **fertilisation** (gamete fusion); the resulting **endosperm** is **triploid (3n)**, not diploid.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Definitions

**Reproduction** is the production of new individuals of the same species. **Asexual reproduction** involves a single parent, no gametes, and offspring that are genetic copies (clones) produced by mitotic cell division. **Sexual reproduction** requires the fusion of two haploid gametes — a sperm (n) and an ovum (n) — to form a diploid zygote (2n), restoring the chromosome number characteristic of the species.

#### Comparison of the Two Modes

| Feature | Asexual reproduction | Sexual reproduction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Parents involved | One parent | Two parents (or hermaphrodite pair) |
| Gametes | Absent | Present (sperm and ovum) |
| Cell division used | Mitosis only | Meiosis (to form gametes) + mitosis |
| Genetic variation | None — offspring are clones | High — due to crossing over and random fertilisation |
| Rate of population growth | Very rapid | Slower |
| Fertilisation | Not required | Required |

#### Mechanisms You Must Know

- **Binary fission** — parent divides into two equal daughter cells (Amoeba, Paramecium).
- **Budding** — small outgrowth detaches from parent and grows (Hydra, yeast).
- **Spore formation** — reproductive spores from a sporangium germinate into new individuals (Rhizopus bread mould, ferns).
- **Vegetative propagation** — new plants arise from stems, roots, or leaves (cassava stem cutting, grass runner, yam tuber, onion bulb).

#### Key Distinctions to Avoid Traps

- Meiosis produces **four haploid (n)** cells; mitosis produces **two diploid (2n)** cells.
- Pollination ≠ fertilisation; pollination is pollen transfer, fertilisation is gamete fusion.
- Hermaphrodites (earthworm, snail, tapeworm) possess both sexes but most cross-fertilise between two individuals.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Double Fertilisation

Angiosperms exhibit **double fertilisation**, a feature unique to flowering plants. One sperm nucleus (n) fuses with the ovum (n) to produce the **diploid zygote (2n)**; a second sperm nucleus (n) fuses with **two polar nuclei (n + n)** to produce the **triploid endosperm (3n)**. The endosperm nourishes the developing embryo inside the seed. Students frequently mis-state the endosperm as 2n — write **3n** in any NECO question.

#### Comparative Advantages and Disadvantages

| Aspect | Asexual | Sexual |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Best suited to | Stable environments | Changing environments |
| Energy cost | Low | High (mate-finding, gamete production) |
| Disease vulnerability | High — entire clone wiped out | Low — variation provides resistance |
| Adaptability | Poor | Strong, drives evolution |

#### Human Reproduction Link

Spermatogenesis in **testes** produces sperm; oogenesis in **ovaries** releases one ovum monthly. Fertilisation normally occurs in the **fallopian tube (oviduct)**, followed by implantation in the **uterus**, gestation (~9 months), and birth via the **vagina**. This connects Reproduction to **Hormonal Coordination** and **Developmental Biology**, both common NECO follow-up topics.

#### Common Mistakes Examiners Exploit

- Calling budding "binary fission" — budding produces unequal cells.
- Stating asexual reproduction "creates variation" — it does not.
- Treating runners, tubers, and bulbs as sexual reproduction — they are asexual vegetative structures.
