# Order of Course Choices

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your CAO application.

**Order of Course Choices** — Quick Facts for Irish Leaving Certificate students

**What is the order of course choices?**
The order of course choices is the **ranked list of up to ten Level 7 and Level 8 courses** that a candidate enters on their application. CAO uses this list to determine which offer a candidate receives: the candidate receives ONE offer per round, at the **highest preference they're entitled to**.

**Key facts:**
- Candidates may list up to TEN Level 7/8 courses on their CAO application
- Courses can be Level 7 (ordinary bachelor degree, typically 3 years), Level 8 (honours bachelor degree, typically 4 years), or both
- The list must be in **GENUINE preference order** — the most-preferred course goes at #1, the next-most-preferred at #2, etc.
- CAO allocates ONE offer per round at the highest preference the candidate is entitled to
- CAO does NOT consider the order strategic; only its preference matters
- Re-ordering courses strategically (e.g., listing "easier" courses first) is a poor strategy because CAO allocates based on what you actually want

**The allocation logic:**
For each offer round:
1. CAO looks at the candidate's #1 preference
2. If the candidate is entitled to an offer for that course (meets matriculation + cut-off + random-number tiebreaker), they receive the offer
3. If not, CAO looks at #2 preference
4. Continue down the list until an offer is made or the list is exhausted

A candidate who is entitled to multiple courses receives the offer for their HIGHEST preference among those entitled courses.

**Why it matters:**
- If you genuinely prefer a course, list it FIRST — CAO will give you that offer if entitled
- If you list courses by "strategic order" rather than preference, you may receive an offer for a course you don't want, and miss offers for courses you do want

**Practical implications:**
- Order courses by GENUINE preference — not by "chances" or "points"
- A "safety" course with a low cut-off should NOT be at #1 unless you genuinely prefer it
- A "reach" course with a high cut-off SHOULD be at #1 if you genuinely prefer it
- The same rule applies in QLDs (QQI FET/LEAVING Certificate pathways) and other CAO pathways

⚡ **Quick rule:** List courses in GENUINE preference order — most-preferred at #1, least-preferred at #10. CAO allocates ONE offer per round at the highest preference you are entitled to. Don't try to "trick" CAO with strategic ordering — it doesn't work and costs you offers.

**Order of Course Choices** — CAO Points Study Guide

**What is the order of course choices?**
The order of course choices is the **ranked list of courses** that a candidate enters on their CAO application. This is one of the most important decisions in the CAO process because it determines which offer a candidate will receive if they are entitled to multiple courses.

**Why genuine preference order matters:**
CAO's allocation logic operates on a strict "highest preference entitled" basis:

- The candidate lists courses in order of preference (most-preferred first)
- CAO checks each course in order
- The first course for which the candidate is entitled to an offer is the offer they receive
- If the candidate is not entitled to any course in their list, they receive no offer in that round

This means **if a candidate is entitled to courses #1 and #5, they will receive the offer for #1** (because #1 is higher preference). The candidate does NOT receive offers for #5 or below — only the highest-preference offer.

**Strategic ordering — what NOT to do:**
A common mistake is to list courses by "strategic" considerations:

- Listing "safety" courses (low cut-off) at the top so the candidate is "guaranteed" an offer
- Listing "reach" courses at the bottom to avoid "wasting" a preference
- Listing by "points" rather than by preference

This is a **POOR strategy** because:

1. **CAO allocates by preference, not by strategy:** If you genuinely prefer a course but list it lower, you may receive an offer for a less-preferred course instead
2. **You may receive offers for courses you don't want:** If you list a "safety" at #1 but genuinely prefer a "reach" at #5, you may receive the "safety" offer and have to accept it or decline it (with consequences)
3. **You cannot "save" offers for later:** CAO's allocation gives you ONE offer per round at your highest entitled preference — there is no mechanism to "skip" offers
4. **Re-ordering during the cycle is limited:** Once the application cycle starts, you can change your preferences up to a deadline (typically 1 July) — but after that, changes are very limited

**The allocation logic in 3 rounds:**

| Round | Allocation | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Round 1 (mid-August) | Highest preference entitled | Candidate receives offer for #1 (if entitled), or #2, etc. |
| Round 2 (late August) | Same logic, updated preferences if any | Candidate may receive offer for a higher preference if a previous-round offer was declined |
| Round 3 (early September, if applicable) | Same logic, further updated preferences | Final round for most courses; places typically fill by Round 3 |

After Round 1, if a candidate declines an offer, they may be considered for a lower-preference course in subsequent rounds. But this is a specific use case — most candidates do not decline Round 1 offers.

**Worked example 1:** Standard preference order, candidate entitled to multiple courses.

- Candidate list: #1 UCD Medicine, #2 TCD Medicine, #3 UCD Engineering, #4 UCD Science, #5 TCD Business
- Candidate has 580 points
- All courses' cut-offs: UCD Medicine 580, TCD Medicine 580, UCD Engineering 520, UCD Science 470, TCD Business 510

The candidate is entitled to all 5 courses. CAO allocates the offer for #1 (UCD Medicine) because it is the highest preference the candidate is entitled to. The candidate does NOT receive offers for TCD Medicine, UCD Engineering, etc.

**Worked example 2:** Mixed entitlements.

- Candidate list: #1 UCD Medicine, #2 TCD Medicine, #3 UCD Engineering, #4 UCD Science, #5 TCD Business
- Candidate has 510 points
- All courses' cut-offs: UCD Medicine 580, TCD Medicine 580, UCD Engineering 520, UCD Science 470, TCD Business 510

The candidate is NOT entitled to UCD Medicine (cut-off 580 > 510) or TCD Medicine. The candidate IS entitled to TCD Business (cut-off 510). CAO allocates the offer for TCD Business (#5 preference) because it's the highest preference they're entitled to. The candidate does NOT receive offers for UCD Engineering (cut-off 520 > 510) or UCD Science (cut-off 470 < 510 but lower preference than TCD Business — wait, #3 is higher preference than #5, but candidate's points are below the cut-off, so they aren't entitled to #3).

Let me restate: candidate is entitled to TCD Business (cut-off 510, points 510 — at the cut-off). Not entitled to UCD Engineering (cut-off 520 > 510). The highest-preference course they're entitled to is #5 TCD Business. Offer for TCD Business.

But wait — if candidate is also entitled to UCD Science (cut-off 470 < 510), then the highest-preference entitled course is #4 UCD Science, not TCD Business. The candidate receives the UCD Science offer.

**Worked example 3:** Strategic ordering gone wrong.

- Candidate list: #1 TCD Arts (cut-off 380, "safety"), #2 UCD Engineering (cut-off 520, "reach"), #3 UCD Medicine (cut-off 580, "dream")
- Candidate has 510 points
- Candidate genuinely prefers: #3 UCD Medicine > #2 UCD Engineering > #1 TCD Arts

Round 1: Candidate is entitled to TCD Arts (cut-off 380 < 510). NOT entitled to UCD Engineering (cut-off 520 > 510) or UCD Medicine (cut-off 580 > 510). Candidate receives the TCD Arts offer.

Round 2 (if applicable): UCD Engineering cut-off might drop to 500 due to withdrawals. Candidate IS entitled to UCD Engineering. CAO checks preference order: UCD Engineering is #2. Candidate already accepted TCD Arts in Round 1, so they cannot receive an additional offer in Round 2 unless they declined the Round 1 offer.

Lesson: This candidate "wasted" their #1 preference on a "safety" they didn't really want, and missed the chance to be considered for UCD Engineering or UCD Medicine in Round 1.

If the candidate had listed their preferences GENUINELY:

- #1 UCD Medicine, #2 UCD Engineering, #3 TCD Arts

Round 1: Candidate is entitled to TCD Arts. NOT entitled to UCD Medicine or UCD Engineering. CAO checks #3 TCD Arts — candidate is entitled. Offer for TCD Arts.

Same outcome in Round 1 in this case. But the difference shows up in Round 2 if UCD Engineering cut-off drops: with the GENUINE list, CAO would consider UCD Engineering for a Round 2 offer (if the candidate declined Round 1). With the STRATEGIC list, the candidate would also be considered for UCD Engineering in Round 2 (since #2 is UCD Engineering).

Actually in this specific example, the outcome is similar. But consider this scenario:

**Worked example 4:** Strategic ordering matters when a candidate is entitled to multiple courses.

- Candidate list: #1 TCD Arts (cut-off 380, "safety"), #2 UCD Engineering (cut-off 520, "match"), #3 UCD Medicine (cut-off 580, "dream"), #4 TCD Business (cut-off 510, "match")
- Candidate has 510 points
- Candidate genuinely prefers: UCD Engineering > UCD Medicine > TCD Business > TCD Arts

Round 1: Candidate is entitled to TCD Arts (cut-off 380 < 510). NOT entitled to UCD Engineering (520 > 510), UCD Medicine (580 > 510), or TCD Business (510 = at cut-off, may or may not be entitled depending on random number). Candidate receives TCD Arts offer.

If the candidate had listed GENUINELY:

- #1 UCD Engineering, #2 UCD Medicine, #3 TCD Business, #4 TCD Arts

Round 1: Candidate is entitled to TCD Arts (cut-off 380). NOT entitled to UCD Engineering, UCD Medicine, or TCD Business (depending on tie). Same outcome — TCD Arts offer.

The difference emerges if the candidate declines the TCD Arts offer (e.g., they really want engineering). In the STRATEGIC list, the candidate is now eligible for UCD Engineering in Round 2 if cut-off drops — but they would also be eligible in the GENUINE list.

**Worked example 5:** Strategic ordering fails badly.

- Candidate list: #1 TCD Arts (cut-off 380, "safety"), #2 UCD Engineering (cut-off 520, "match"), #3 UCD Medicine (cut-off 580, "dream")
- Candidate has 510 points
- Candidate genuinely prefers: UCD Engineering > UCD Medicine > TCD Arts

Round 1: Cut-offs: TCD Arts 380, UCD Engineering 520, UCD Medicine 580. Candidate entitled to TCD Arts (510 > 380). NOT entitled to UCD Engineering (510 < 520) or UCD Medicine (510 < 580). Offer for TCD Arts.

The candidate now has a Round 1 offer for TCD Arts. They must accept or decline by the Round 1 reply date.

If they ACCEPT: They are committed to TCD Arts. Subsequent rounds may offer higher preferences if they change their mind and decline (subject to deadlines).

If they DECLINE: They become eligible for subsequent rounds. They may receive a Round 2 offer for UCD Engineering if cut-off drops below 510.

Now imagine the GENUINE list:

- #1 UCD Engineering (cut-off 520), #2 UCD Medicine (cut-off 580), #3 TCD Arts (cut-off 380)

Round 1: Same entitlements. Same offer for TCD Arts (#3, the highest preference entitled).

Same outcome in this scenario. The strategic vs. GAOH difference emerges when the candidate IS entitled to multiple courses.

**Worked example 6:** GAOH list works when candidate entitled to multiple courses.

- Candidate list: #1 UCD Engineering (cut-off 520, "match"), #2 UCD Medicine (cut-off 580, "dream"), #3 TCD Business (cut-off 510, "match"), #4 TCD Arts (cut-off 380, "safety")
- Candidate has 580 points
- Candidate genuinely prefers: UCD Medicine > UCD Engineering > TCD Business > TCD Arts

Round 1: Candidate is entitled to UCD Engineering (cut-off 520 < 580), UCD Medicine (cut-off 580 = at cut-off — random number decides), TCD Business (cut-off 510 < 580), TCD Arts (cut-off 380 < 580).

If candidate is entitled to UCD Medicine (random number wins): Offer for UCD Medicine (#1 preference).

If candidate is NOT entitled to UCD Medicine (random number loses): Check #2 UCD Engineering — entitled. Offer for UCD Engineering.

Now imagine the STRATEGIC list:

- #1 TCD Arts (cut-off 380), #2 UCD Engineering (cut-off 520), #3 UCD Medicine (cut-off 580), #4 TCD Business (cut-off 510)

Round 1: Candidate entitled to TCD Arts. Offer for TCD Arts.

The candidate JUST MISSED THE OFFER FOR UCD MEDICINE — they listed TCD Arts (a course they don't really want) at #1 instead of UCD Medicine (their dream). This is a strategic failure.

Lesson: Genuine preference order is critical when you are entitled to multiple courses.

**The "Liking and Entry" (GAO) versus "Strategic Ordering" (SAO) debate:**
Many candidates wonder: "Should I list courses by preference, or by strategy?"

The answer is always: **list by genuine preference**. CAO's allocation logic gives you the highest-preference offer you're entitled to. Strategic ordering cannot "trick" CAO into giving you a better offer — it can only cause you to receive offers for courses you don't want.

**Changing your order after submission:**
Candidates can change their course preferences up to a deadline (typically 1 July for the following September's intake). After that, changes are very limited:

- Adding a course: generally not allowed after the deadline
- Removing a course: generally allowed (candidate withdraws from that course)
- Re-ordering: generally allowed before the deadline, very limited after

If you need to change your order, do so before the deadline. After the deadline, your order is locked.

**The order of choices is independent of course cut-offs:**
A common misconception is that candidates should list courses by "chances of getting in." This is incorrect:

- Course cut-offs are HISTORICAL, not predictive
- Your points are based on your actual LC results
- The order of choices should reflect YOUR preference, not your "chances"

**Order of choices and random number allocation:**
Order of choices does NOT affect random number allocation (see cao-006). The random number is a property of your application, not of any individual course choice. When candidates are tied at the cut-off, the random number decides regardless of where they listed the course.

**Order of choices and matriculation:**
Order of choices does NOT affect matriculation (see cao-005). The matriculation is a property of the course, not of your preference order. A course that requires H6 in Chemistry will reject you regardless of where you listed it.

**Order of choices and Round 2/3 offers:**
Candidates who decline a Round 1 offer may receive a Round 2 offer for a higher preference. The order of choices is RE-RATED in each round based on:

- Whether the candidate accepts/declines previous-round offers
- Updated cut-offs (lower in subsequent rounds)
- Updated matriculation/entitlement checks

If you decline a Round 1 offer for a course you genuinely don't want, you may receive a Round 2 offer for a higher preference.

**Order of choices for mature applicants:**
Mature applicants (23+ years old, no recent LC) follow a similar preference-ordering logic but with separate cut-offs and matriculation requirements. The order of choices still matters and should reflect genuine preference.

**Order of choices for QQI FET applicants:**
QQI FET applicants have separate pathways and cut-offs, but the order of choices still reflects genuine preference. QQI FET courses are typically listed alongside Level 7/8 courses on the CAO application.

**Order of choices for HEAR/DARE applicants:**
HEAR (Higher Education Access Route) and DARE (Disability Access Route) applicants have additional admissions based on socio-economic or disability status. These admissions do not change the order-of-choices logic — preference order still matters.

**Frequently asked questions:**
- "Should I list courses by preference or by 'points'?" — By preference. CAO allocates by preference, not by points.
- "Can I re-order my choices after submission?" — Generally yes, before the deadline (typically 1 July). After that, very limited changes allowed.
- "Does my order affect random number allocation?" — No. The random number is independent of preference order.
- "Does my order affect matriculation?" — No. The matriculation is a property of the course, not of your preference order.
- "Can I list the same course twice?" — No. Each course can appear only once on your list.
- "Can I list courses from different HEIs?" — Yes. Your list can include courses from multiple HEIs (UCD, TCD, UCC, NUIG, UL, Maynooth, DCU, TU Dublin, etc.).
- "Can I list both Level 7 and Level 8 courses?" — Yes. The list can mix Level 7 (ordinary bachelor, typically 3 years) and Level 8 (honours bachelor, typically 4 years).
- "Can I change my choices after receiving an offer?" — Yes, before the offer acceptance deadline. You may decline an offer and be considered for a higher preference in subsequent rounds.

**Practical tips:**
- List courses in GENUINE preference order, from most-preferred (#1) to least-preferred (#10)
- Use the balanced approach: 2 reach + 3-4 match + 2-3 safety courses (see cao-007)
- Re-order your list before the deadline (typically 1 July) if needed
- After the deadline, your order is locked
- Decline Round 1 offers only if you have a clear preference for a higher-ranked course (and that course is realistic)
- Don't try to "trick" CAO with strategic ordering — it doesn't work and reduces your working budget
