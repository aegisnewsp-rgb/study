# Question Types — Form, Note, Table, Flow-Chart Completion — IELTS Listening Notes

The five "completion" question families appear across all four parts of the IELTS Listening test. They share a common structure: a text with gaps, instructions about the word limit, and answers drawn from the recording. Mastery of these families is the single highest-leverage preparation for the Listening test.

> Verify the live Listening format on https://ielts.org/ before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Form completion:** a registration or booking form. Answers are typically personal details (name, address, phone, email).
- **Note completion:** informal notes with gaps. Often includes abbreviations or shortened phrases.
- **Table completion:** a table with categories and missing entries. Information flows in the same order as the recording.
- **Flow-chart completion:** a process flow with gaps. Each step is a noun phrase or short clause.
- **Summary completion:** a paragraph summarising the recording. Gaps are key terms.
- **Word limits:** "NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER" is the most common instruction. One word over is wrong.
- **Capitalisation:** names of places, organisations, and proper nouns must be capitalised.

#### Examiner traps

- Writing more words than the limit — even "and" or "the" counts.
- Lowercase when capitalisation is required (e.g. "Dr Smith" not "dr smith").
- Spelling errors on names that are spelled out in the recording.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Form completion

The form usually contains structured personal details:

- Name (first/last)
- Address (street, city, postcode)
- Phone number
- Email
- Date of birth
- Reference number

For numbers and dates, write them as the question requires (digits or words). For names, capitalise the first letter of each part. For emails, write the full address including the @ symbol.

#### Note completion

Notes are informal, often with abbreviations (e.g. "min" for minimum, "k" for thousand). The instruction may say "complete the notes" or "complete the form." The word limit is strict.

#### Table completion

Tables organise information by category. The columns or rows usually follow the recording in order. Before listening, scan the table to predict what each column needs (a name, a date, a price, an opinion).

#### Flow-chart completion

Flow-charts show a process with arrows. Each gap is a step. The recording describes the process step by step, usually in the same order as the chart.

#### Summary completion

A summary paragraph condenses the recording into 100-200 words. Each gap is a key term. The summary's structure mirrors the recording's structure.

#### Word limit discipline

The instruction "NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS" means exactly that — one or two words, never three. The instruction "NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER" allows two words plus a number (e.g. "approximately 15 minutes"). Read the instruction before listening.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where the completion families appear across IELTS Listening

The five completion families appear across all four parts, but with different frequency:

- Part 1: form, note, table (transactional context).
- Part 2: note, table, summary (monologue context).
- Part 3: flow-chart, summary (discussion context).
- Part 4: summary, sentence, note (lecture context).

Master all five — they appear in different mixes in different tests.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Paraphrasing the answer is fine." No — write what you hear, within the word limit. Paraphrasing usually loses the mark.
- "Numbers can be written as words." Sometimes — follow the question's format. If the form has boxes for digits, write digits.
- "Abbreviations are acceptable." Sometimes — follow the question's instruction. If the instruction says "NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS," an abbreviation counts as one word.

#### Exam technique

- For all completion items, write the answer as soon as you hear it. Don't wait — you'll miss the next one.
- For form items, double-check the spelling of names against the recording.
- For table items, follow the row order — the recording matches the table row by row.
