# Cardiovascular System

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The cardiovascular system is a closed circulatory loop of the **heart** (four chambers — two atria, two ventricles) and a **vascular tree** (arteries → arterioles → capillaries → venules → veins) that moves blood, gases, hormones, and wastes. In NEET PG Anatomy, the focus is structural: chambers, valves, conducting system, coronary vessels, pericardium, and fetal shunts.

- **Cardiac Output (CO)** = SV × HR, where SV = EDV − ESV (all in mL, HR in beats/min)
- **Ejection Fraction (EF)** = (SV / EDV) × 100; normal ≈ 55–70%
- **MAP** = Diastolic BP + ⅓(Systolic − Diastolic), in mmHg
- **Conducting order**: SA node → AV node (0.3 s delay) → Bundle of His → bundle branches → Purkinje fibres
- **Fetal shunts**: foramen ovale, ductus arteriosus, ductus venosus — all functionally close at birth
- **Valve auscultation** (not anatomical position): aortic — right 2nd ICS; pulmonary — left 2nd ICS; tricuspid — lower left sternal border; mitral — apex, 5th ICS mid-clavicular

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Chambers, Septa, and Valves
The heart sits in the middle mediastinum wrapped by a **fibrous pericardium** externally and a **serous pericardium** (parietal + visceral/epicardium) internally. The right atrium receives the **SVC, IVC, and coronary sinus**; the left atrium receives four pulmonary veins. The interventricular septum has a thin **membranous part** (just below the aortic valve — common site of VSDs) and a thick muscular part. AV valves (tricuspid right, mitral/bicuspid left) are anchored by **chordae tendineae** to **papillary muscles**; semilunar valves (aortic, pulmonary) have **no chordae**.

#### Conducting System and Coronary Supply
The **SA node** (pacemaker, near SVC–right atrium junction) initiates each impulse. After a 0.1 s AV-node delay (≈0.3 s total at AV node), the **Bundle of His** divides into right and left bundle branches that terminate as **Purkinje fibres** — these are modified cardiomyocytes, not nerves. Coronary supply is asymmetric:

| Artery | Main territory supplied |
| --- | --- |
| Left coronary (LAD + circumflex) | Left atrium, left ventricle, anterior 2/3 of interventricular septum |
| Right coronary (RCA) | Right atrium, right ventricle, posterior 1/3 septum, SA node (~60%), AV node (~80%) |
| Posterior interventricular (PDA) | Defines dominance — RCA gives PDA in ~70–80% (right-dominant) |

#### Fetal Circulation and Clinical Pearls
Fetal gas exchange occurs at the placenta, not the lungs. Three shunts bypass the non-functional pulmonary circuit: **foramen ovale** (right atrium → left atrium), **ductus arteriosus** (pulmonary trunk → aortic arch), and **ductus venosus** (umbilical vein → IVC, bypassing the liver). All three close functionally within hours to days of birth; anatomic closure forms the fossa ovalis, ligamentum arteriosum, and ligamentum venosum respectively. Referred cardiac pain travels via sympathetic fibres from **T1–T5** to the same dermatomes — classically the left arm and jaw in MI.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Pericardial Sinuses — Surgical Landmarks
Two sinuses are tested repeatedly. The **transverse pericardial sinus** lies between the aorta/pulmonary trunk anteriorly and the SVC/atria posteriorly; surgeons pass a clamp through it to occlude both great vessels during coronary surgery. The **oblique sinus** is a blind pouch posterior to the left atrium, bounded by the pulmonary veins.

#### Auscultation vs Anatomical Position (Classic Trap)
The aortic valve sits in the left side of the heart anatomically but is best heard at the **right 2nd intercostal space, parasternal**. The pulmonary valve sits anteriorly but is heard at the **left 2nd intercostal space**. Remember: auscultation sites project sound toward the chest wall along flow direction, not anatomical location.

#### Common Mistakes and Edge Cases
- Confusing **ductus arteriosus** (pulmonary trunk → aortic arch) with a connection to the descending aorta or pulmonary veins
- Treating **Purkinje fibres as nerves** — they are modified ventricular myocytes allowing rapid conduction (~2–4 m/s vs 0.3 m/s in contractile myocardium)
- Forgetting that RCA supplies the **SA node in ~60%** and **AV node in ~80%** — making RCA occlusion a common cause of nodal ischaemia and bradyarrhythmias
- Mistaking the **epicardium** for the pericardium — epicardium is the visceral serous layer and the outermost tunic of the heart wall itself
- Aorta coarctation typically occurs **just distal to the left subclavian artery** (post-ductal in adults, pre-ductal in infants) — produces radio-femoral delay

#### Practice Prompts
1. An infant has a continuous machine-like murmur; which fetal shunt failed to close, and what structure does it become?
2. A posterior MI shows ST elevation in V1–V2 with right-sided involvement — which coronary artery is most likely occluded, and which node is at highest risk?
