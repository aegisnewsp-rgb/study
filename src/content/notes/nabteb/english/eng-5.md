# Active and Passive Voice

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Active voice** places the doer in the subject slot: *The chef (subject) prepared (verb) the meals (object).* **Passive voice** shifts the receiver into the subject slot and uses a form of *be* + past participle: *The meals were prepared by the chef.* Only **transitive verbs** (verbs that take a direct object) can be passivised.

| Voice | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Active | Subject + Verb + Object | *The students wrote the essays.* |
| Passive | Subject + be + past participle (+ by + agent) | *The essays were written by the students.* |
| Modal Passive | Subject + modal + be + V3 | *The rules must be obeyed.* |
| Impersonal Passive | It + be + V3 + that-clause | *It is believed that he lied.* |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **SOB ↔ PSB** — **S**ubject–**O**bject–**B**y-agent becomes **P**assive **S**ubject–**B**e–past participle. Also remember the gatekeeper: **No Object, No Passive**. Spot the direct object first; if none exists, stop.

For NABTEB Paper I objective items, expect at least one question asking you to pick the passive equivalent of a given active sentence, and one cloze requiring the correct *be* + V3 form.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Mechanism of Voice Conversion

Voice conversion is a **syntactic rearrangement**, not a tense change. The tense of the original verb is preserved exactly; only the roles of noun phrases shift. The active **object** rises to become the passive **subject**, and the active **subject** demotes to a *by-phrase* agent (in many papers deleted).

- Step 1 — Identify the tense of the active verb (e.g., simple past → *wrote*).
- Step 2 — Locate the direct object; confirm the verb is transitive.
- Step 3 — Move the object to the subject position.
- Step 4 — Insert the correct form of *be* matching the new subject in **number** and matching the original tense in **time**.
- Step 5 — Change the main verb to its past participle (V3).
- Step 6 — Add *by + agent* only when the doer is known and relevant.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix

| Feature | Active Voice | Passive Voice |
|---|---|---|
| Default usage | Yes — preferred in 70%+ of English prose | Optional, used for emphasis or when agent is unknown |
| Sentence focus | On the doer (agent) | On the action or the receiver |
| Verb requirement | Any verb (transitive or intransitive) | Only **transitive** verbs |
| *By*-phrase | Absent | In many papers optional; mandatory only for emphasis |
| Length | In standard papers shorter | In standard papers longer (extra auxiliary) |
| Typical NABTEB signal | Short, punchy statements in essays | Scientific or report-style writing in comprehension passages |

#### Tense-by-Tense Passive Forms

| Tense | Active Example | Passive Equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| Simple Present | *She writes the letter.* | *The letter is written by her.* |
| Simple Past | *She wrote the letter.* | *The letter was written by her.* |
| Present Continuous | *She is writing the letter.* | *The letter is being written by her.* |
| Past Continuous | *She was writing the letter.* | *The letter was being written by her.* |
| Present Perfect | *She has written the letter.* | *The letter has been written by her.* |
| Past Perfect | *She had written the letter.* | *The letter had been written by her.* |
| Future (will) | *She will write the letter.* | *The letter will be written by her.* |
| Modal | *She can write the letter.* | *The letter can be written by her.* |

> 📌 **Formula Check:** In *has/have/had + been + V3*, two auxiliaries sandwich **been** between the perfect auxiliary and the past participle. Dropping *been* is the single most common NABTEB cloze-test error.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** Choose the option that best expresses the sentence *“The committee has approved the new curriculum.”* in the passive voice.

- A. The new curriculum has approved by the committee.
- B. The new curriculum has been approved by the committee.
- C. The new curriculum is approved by the committee.
- D. The new curriculum was approved by the committee.

#### Solution:

1. Identify the tense: **present perfect active** (*has approved*).
2. Locate the direct object: *the new curriculum*.
3. Promote the object to subject position.
4. Conjugate *be* in the present perfect: **has been** (singular subject takes *has*).
5. Attach the past participle: **approved** (already V3).
6. Add the agent: *by the committee*.
7. Final answer: ***The new curriculum has been approved by the committee.*** — **Option B**.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Option A drops *been*, a classic 2023–2025 NABTEB distractor. Option C loses the perfective aspect by switching to *is*; Option D shifts tense into the simple past. Always mirror the original tense exactly when passivising.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Stative Constructions

Some sentences look passive but are not, and vice versa. Distinguishing them sharpens your NABTEB comprehension-passage answers.

| Surface Form | True Status | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *The cloth feels soft.* | **NOT passive** — linking verb | *Feel* here is copular, not transitive |
| *He is known for honesty.* | **True passive** — stative | *Known* is a past participle sharing the auxiliary *be* |
| *She had her hair cut.* | **Causative passive** | *Have* + object + V3 = passive sense without *by* |
| *It is said that he is rich.* | **Impersonal passive** | Reporting verbs use dummy *it* |
| *He is said to be rich.* | **Personal passive** | Subject of subordinate clause promoted |

#### Advanced Traps in NABTEB Items

1. **Intransitive blockage:** *arrive, happen, occur, sleep, die, lie (recline)* cannot be passivised. Watch for the trick option *“The accident was happened.”*
2. **Stative *be + V3*:** Sentences such as *The window is broken* can be **passive** (action) **or adjectival** (state). Context decides; NABTEB in most keys signals action with a *by*-phrase.
3. **Two-object verbs:** With verbs like *give, send, teach*, the indirect object can become the passive subject: *He was given a book* or *A book was given to him.*
4. **Modal subtlety:** *“The work must complete”* is wrong; correct is *“The work must be completed.”* Modal passive always inserts **be**.
5. **Wh-question passives:** *Who wrote this?* → *By whom was this written?* — note *whom*, not *who*.

#### Worked Micro-Example

Convert: *“People believe that the witness escaped.”*

- Step 1: Tense — simple present active (*believe*).
- Step 2: Object — *that the witness escaped* (a that-clause, which can be passivised as a finite clause).
- Step 3: Apply impersonal pattern: ***It is believed that the witness escaped.***
- Step 4: Or the personal form: ***The witness is believed to have escaped.***

> 💡 **High-Yield Revision Hook:** When the active subject is vague (*people, they, someone*) and the matrix verb is a reporting verb (*say, believe, think, report, consider*), reach for **It + be + V3 + that-clause** first; only convert to the personal passive if the exam asks for a single-clause rewrite.

#### Common Mistakes in NABTEB Scripts

- Writing *The thief was stolen* instead of *Something was stolen* (no agent available).
- Mixing *was/were* with plural subjects: *The students was punished* ✗ → *were punished* ✓.
- Using base form after *be*: *The match is play* ✗ → *is played* ✓.
- Forgetting the passive infinitive **to be + V3** after modals or perception verbs: *He wants to promote* ✗ → *to be promoted* ✓.

#### Exam Strategy for NABTEB (3% Weight)

- Paper I objective: 1–2 items per session on voice identification/conversion.
- Paper II essay: prefer active voice in narration; switch to passive for formal or report passages to vary rhythm.
- Paper III comprehension/summary: rewriting instructions in many papers demand a passive paraphrase; underline the verb tense before writing.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts:

1. Rewrite *“They have not yet completed the bridge.”* into (a) an active sentence beginning with the agent, and (b) a passive sentence beginning with *The bridge*.
2. Identify and correct the error: *“The decision were made yesterday by the board.”*
