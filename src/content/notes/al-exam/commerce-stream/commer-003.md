# National Income and Economic Indicators

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

> Three numbers decide almost every question: **GDP at MP**, **GNP**, and **NNP at FC**. Memorise how each is built from the previous one.

National Income = monetary value of all **final** goods and services produced in an economy during one year. The A/L examiner at the Department of Examinations Sri Lanka tests five building blocks, listed below with their units.

- **GDP at MP** = C + I + G + (X − M), in Rs.
- **GNP at MP** = GDP + NFIA, in Rs.
- **NNP at MP** = GNP − Depreciation, in Rs.
- **NNP at FC** = NNP at MP − Net Indirect Taxes, in Rs.
- **Per Capita Income** = National Income ÷ Total Population, in Rs. per person.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook (Mnemonic):** "**C**ook **I**n **G**reen **X**tra **M**asala" → **C + I + G + (X − M)** = GDP at MP. Then "**G**randpa **N**eeds **D**entures" → **GNP = GDP + NFIA**, and **NNP = GNP − Depreciation**.

The single most-tested idea: distinguish **domestic territory** (counted in GDP) from **normal residents** (counted in GNP). A Sri Lankan worker earning in Qatar raises GNP, not GDP.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

> Each layer of the national accounts pyramid depends on the one below it. Skip a step, and the entire calculation collapses.

#### The Five-Step Conversion Chain

National Income accounting uses two price bases — **Market Price (MP)** and **Factor Cost (FC)** — and two capital treatments — **Gross** (includes depreciation) and **Net** (excludes depreciation). Every aggregate can sit in four positions on this grid.

| Aggregate | Formula | What is added/subtracted |
|---|---|---|
| GDP at MP | C + I + G + (X − M) | Final expenditure on domestically produced goods |
| NDP at MP | GDP at MP − Depreciation | Removes Consumption of Fixed Capital |
| GNP at MP | GDP at MP + NFIA | Adds factor income earned abroad by residents, minus that earned by foreigners at home |
| NNP at MP | GNP at MP − Depreciation | Net of capital wear-and-tear |
| NNP at FC | NNP at MP − Net Indirect Taxes | Strips off indirect taxes, adds back subsidies |

#### Comparison Matrix: Concepts the Examiner Relies On

| Confused pair | Correct distinction | Examiner-tested scenario |
|---|---|---|
| GDP vs GNP | GDP counts production inside **domestic territory**; GNP counts income of **normal residents** wherever they work | Foreign-owned factory in Colombo contributes to GDP only |
| Intermediate vs Final goods | Final goods reach the end-user; intermediates are re-used in production | Counting both flour and the bread → double-counting |
| Transfer payments vs Factor income | Transfers (pensions, subsidies, scholarships) do **not** arise from production of goods/services | Old-age pension must be **excluded** from National Income |
| CPI vs GDP Deflator | CPI tracks a fixed basket of consumer goods; Deflator covers **all** final goods in the current period | A new product (e.g. mobile phones) appears in Deflator but distorts CPI |
| Nominal vs Real GDP | Nominal uses current-year prices; Real uses base-year prices | 15% nominal growth with 10% inflation = only 4.5% real growth |
| Market Price vs Factor Cost | MP includes Net Indirect Taxes; FC is what factors of production actually receive | Question asks for "at factor cost" → subtract NIT |

#### Worked Resolution: From Raw Data to Per Capita Income

A closed economy in 2025 records: Private consumption Rs. 800 bn, Investment Rs. 250 bn, Government expenditure Rs. 180 bn, Exports Rs. 200 bn, Imports Rs. 220 bn. Factor income earned abroad by residents = Rs. 40 bn; earned domestically by non-residents = Rs. 25 bn. Depreciation = Rs. 60 bn. Net Indirect Taxes = Rs. 30 bn. Population = 22 million.

- **Step 1 — GDP at MP:** 800 + 250 + 180 + (200 − 220) = Rs. 1,210 bn.
- **Step 2 — NFIA:** 40 − 25 = Rs. 15 bn.
- **Step 3 — GNP at MP:** 1,210 + 15 = Rs. 1,225 bn.
- **Step 4 — NNP at MP:** 1,225 − 60 = Rs. 1,165 bn.
- **Step 5 — NNP at FC:** 1,165 − 30 = Rs. 1,135 bn.
- **Step 6 — Per Capita Income:** 1,135 bn ÷ 22 m = **Rs. 51,591 per person** (≈ Rs. 51,591).

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Dividing GDP (Rs. 1,210 bn) by 22 million gives Rs. 55,000 — close, but wrong. The marker expects **NNP at FC ÷ population**, not GDP.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

> Sri Lanka's DCS publishes a single headline number; the steps hidden inside it decide whether your answer scores full marks.

#### Where Each Aggregate Comes From in Practice

The Department of Census and Statistics (DCS) releases National Accounts figures that mirror the textbook chain but with three Sri-Lanka-specific twists:

- The DCS report separates **"production approach"** output from **"expenditure approach"** output and reconciles the gap as "statistical discrepancy" — worth flagging in 15-mark essays.
- Factor income from abroad is dominated by **migrant remittances from the Middle East**, which entered GNP but not GDP — a recurring A/L question since the 1970s.
- The base year for real-GDP rebasing shifts roughly every decade; older papers reference 2002 prices, recent ones 2015 prices.

#### Advanced Traps and Boundary Conditions

1. **Subsidies on production** are subtracted from indirect taxes before the NIT calculation — never ignore them.
2. **Imputed rent** of owner-occupied housing is included in GDP, even though no cash changes hands.
3. **Second-hand goods** sales are excluded; only the broker's margin enters GDP.
4. **Stock appreciation** (unrealised inventory gains) is removed from business profits before the income method is applied.
5. **Goods produced by households for own consumption** are partly included (e.g. food) and partly excluded (e.g. personal services).
6. **Losses from natural disasters** reduce GDP in the year of occurrence but rebuilding activity adds to it later — net effect over time is small but volatile.

#### Connection to Adjacent Topics

National Income flows directly into **fiscal policy** (tax buoyancy calculations), **balance of payments** (the income account mirrors NFIA), and **monetary policy** (CPI feeds inflation targeting by the Central Bank of Sri Lanka). A student who masters this topic handles roughly 12–15 marks across the A/L Commerce Stream Economics paper, plus every cross-over question on monetary policy and trade.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

- A coastal economy suffers a Rs. 50 bn tsunami in year T. Show how GDP, GNP, and NNP at FC move across years T, T+1, and T+2, assuming rebuilding expenditure of Rs. 80 bn is spread evenly.
- Construct a numerical example where Nominal GDP growth is 14%, Inflation is 9%, and the Deflator change is 4.6%. Verify the identity: Real growth = (1 + Nominal)/(1 + Inflation) − 1.
