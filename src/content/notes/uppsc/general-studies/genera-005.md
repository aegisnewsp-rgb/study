# Defining Scope of the Topic

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

- **Topic 5** of the UPPSC RO/ARO General Studies paper covers the **Indian National Movement (1857–1947)**, the historical stream that has carried consistent 2–3 question weight in Prelims.
- The arc runs from the **Revolt of 1857** and the rise of the **Indian National Congress (1885)** through **Gandhian mass satyagraha**, the **Constitutional reforms (1909, 1919, 1935)**, the **Quit India Movement (1942)**, and the **Partition/Independence settlement of 1947**.
- Three high-yield anchors: (1) the **Slogan–Leader–Year** triad (e.g. *“Inquilab Zindabad”* – Iqbal/Hasrat Mohani – 1929–30), (2) the **Session–President–Venue** triad of the INC, and (3) the **Acts–Provisions–Year** triad of British reforms.
- Watch the four most-tested sub-themes: **Rise of Communal Politics**, **Revolutionary Terrorism (HSRA, Bengal)**, **Peasant/Tribal Upsurges (Champaran, Tebhaga)**, and **Subhas Chandra Bose / INA**.

> Mnemonic: *“SRP”* — **S**logan, **R**eform, **P**erson; build any answer around these three columns and you cover 70% of the question.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Defining Scope of the Topic
The Indian National Movement in the UPPSC RO/ARO syllabus spans the **political, social, and ideological currents** that culminated in independence. Examiners frame questions on three planes: **chronological events**, **ideologies (moderate vs. extremist, Gandhian, socialist, communal)**, and **legislative milestones** that re-defined the colonial state.

#### Core Phases and Key Acts
| Period | Defining Feature | Key Act/Event | Strategic Shift |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1857–1885 | Armed resistance and political associations | Revolt of 1857; formation of INC | Indigenous to organised |
| 1885–1905 | Early Congress / Moderates | Councils not included | Constitutional methods |
| 1905–1919 | Extremism & Gandhian entry | Morley–Minto (1909), Rowlatt | Mass mobilisation begins |
| 1919–1939 | Mass satyagraha era | Government of India Act 1919; Poona Pact | Civil disobedience cycles |
| 1939–1947 | Negotiations & Partition | August Offer, Quit India (1942), INA trials | Transfer of power |

#### Slogans and Their Authors
- *“Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it”* — **Bal Gangadhar Tilak**, 1907.
- *“Inquilab Zindabad”* — popularised by **Hasrat Mohani** and **Subhas Chandra Bose**.
- *“Do or Die”* — **Mahatma Gandhi**, Quit India speech, 8 August 1942.
- *“Jai Jawan Jai Kisan”* — **Lal Bahadur Shastri**, 1965 (often confused with Bose-era slogans).

#### Standard Question Patterns
1. **Match the following** – Congress sessions with presidents and venues (e.g. **Lahore 1929 – Jawaharlal Nehru – Purna Swaraj**).
2. **Statement-based MCQs** – asserting that a particular Act, Commission, or revolt happened in a given year; expect **chronology traps** (e.g. mixing Cripps with Cabinet Mission dates).
3. **Assertion–Reason** on ideological splits: Moderate–Extremist, Gandhian–Subhasite, Hindu–Muslim political streams.

> Exam tip: Memorise the **four Acts** (1909, 1919, 1935, 1947 Independence Act) along with their **communal electorates, dyarchy, provincial autonomy, and dominion status** provisions — these are the most-tested columns.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Examiner Traps
- **Date overlap**: 1929 is a trap year — **Nehru Report (1928)** and **Lahore Session (Dec 1929)** are often swapped.
- **Personalities in disguise**: A question framed as “who was the Viceroy when the Jallianwala Bagh incident occurred?” expects **Lord Chelmsford**, not Irwin.
- **Communal politics in detail**: the **Shuddhi–Sanghathan movement (Arya Samaj)** vs. **Tabligh movement** predates the Muslim League’s separate-electorate push and is occasionally asked.
- **Revolutionary terror**: HSRA members (Bhagat Singh, Rajguru, Sukhdev) and the **Kakori Conspiracy (1925)** belong to UP’s own political geography — the UPPSC expects local awareness.

#### Connections to Adjacent Topics
- Links directly to **Indian Polity (Topic 4)** through the **Government of India Act 1935** – the constitutional bedrock of modern India’s federal structure.
- Links to **Modern Indian History (Topic 6)** for **post-independence integration** (princely states, reorganisation) since some Prelims sets test continuity.
- Links to **Current Affairs** because **partition historiography** resurfaces whenever communal tensions make news.

#### Practice Prompts
1. “The Morley–Minto Reforms of 1909 institutionalised communal representation in India.” Discuss the long-term consequences on the National Movement. *(Answer in 150 words; focus on: separate electorates → Muslim League consolidation → Partition.)*
2. Compare the **ideological content** of the **Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–22)**, the **Civil Disobedience Movement (1930–34)**, and the **Quit India Movement (1942)**. Highlight the underlying shifts in Congress strategy. *(Use the “SRP” matrix: Slogan, Reform, Person.)*

> Strategy: Allocate **6–8 minutes per question** on this topic; aim to attempt at least **2 of the 3 expected MCQs**. Carry an **Acts–Year sheet** and a **Congress–Session table** in your last-mile revision folder.
