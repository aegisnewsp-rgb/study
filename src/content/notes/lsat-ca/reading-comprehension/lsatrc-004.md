# Inference and Implication — LSAT Reading Comprehension

Inference items ask the test-taker to draw a conclusion that follows from the passage even when not stated explicitly. They are the single most-tested RC question type.

> Verify the live section count, item count, and any in-year changes on https://www.lsac.org/ before planning around the figures below.

---

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### What inference items test

- The conclusion that follows necessarily or most strongly from the passage.
- The author's likely view on a related issue.
- The implication of a specific passage detail.

#### One-pass drill

1. Read the passage. Identify the main idea and the supporting details.
2. For each inference item, ask: "what must follow from the passage?"
3. Eliminate choices that contradict the passage, that add unsupported information, or that are too strong.

#### Examiner traps

- Picking a choice that is true in real life but not supported by the passage.
- Picking a choice that is too strong ("must" or "always" when the passage supports "could" or "often").
- Picking a choice that requires new information.

---

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Strategy for inference items

1. **Identify the main idea** and the supporting details.
2. **For each inference item, ask: "what must follow?"**
3. **Eliminate choices that:**
   - Contradict the passage.
   - Add unsupported information.
   - Are too strong ("must" or "always" when "could" or "often" is supported).
   - Are too weak (consistent with the passage but not implied).
4. **Pick the choice that is most strongly supported** by the passage.

#### Common inference patterns

- **Numerical inference:** "the passage says X. Y is greater than X. Therefore Y is greater than X." Direct calculation.
- **Causal inference:** "the passage says A causes B. Therefore B is evidence of A." Careful with directionality.
- **Categorical inference:** "the passage says all X are Y. Z is X. Therefore Z is Y." Categorical reasoning.
- **Analogical inference:** "the passage compares X and Y. Therefore Z (similar to X) is likely to behave like X." Careful with relevance.

#### Practice routine

- Take 10 RC inference items. For each, identify the type of inference before evaluating the choices.
- Time yourself: under 1.5 minutes per item.

---

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How inference items connect to the other RC skills

- **Main idea** is the foundation for inference; the inference should be consistent with the main idea.
- **Strengthen/weaken** items often test whether a new piece of evidence supports the main idea or an inference from it.
- **Method items** describe how the author argues; inference items describe what the author concludes.

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"Inference means reading between the lines."** Inference means following the passage carefully; the conclusion is in the passage.
- **"Must be true means proved in real life."** Must be true means it follows from the passage; it does not need to be proved in the real world.
- **"Could be true means probably true."** Could be true means it is consistent with the passage; it does not need to be the most likely.

#### Exam technique

- For inference items, eliminate choices that contradict the passage or add unsupported information.
- Pick the choice that is most strongly supported.
- Use the platform's flag-for-review function for items you cannot answer.
