# Living Things: Characteristics and Classification

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Living things** are organisms that carry out the seven life processes, remembered by the acronym **MRS GREN**: **M**ovement, **R**espiration, **S**ensitivity (response to stimuli), **G**rowth, **R**eproduction, **E**xcretion, **N**utrition. Every organism on Earth — from a bacterium to an elephant — performs all seven. The cell is the basic unit of life, and every living thing is made of one or more cells. Living things are sorted into **five kingdoms**: **Monera** (bacteria), **Protista** (amoeba, algae), **Fungi** (mushrooms, moulds), **Plantae** (plants), and **Animalia** (animals). Animals with a backbone are **vertebrates** (mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, fish); those without are **invertebrates** (insects, worms, jellyfish). Plants are **autotrophs** that make food by **photosynthesis**; animals are **heterotrophs** that eat other organisms. NCEE candidates should memorise MRS GREN and the five-kingdom scheme — these appear directly in Section A objective questions.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### The Seven Characteristics of Life (MRS GREN)

1. **Movement** — A whole-body change of position (animals walk, swim, fly) or a localised response (plants show tropisms: shoots bend toward light, roots grow toward gravity).
2. **Respiration** — A **cellular** chemical process: glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + energy. It happens in every living cell, day and night. Breathing is only the mechanical movement of air in animals; respiration is the underlying chemistry.
3. **Sensitivity (Irritability)** — Detection of stimuli such as **light, heat, touch, chemicals, and gravity**, followed by a response. Examples: the pupil shrinks in bright light; a sunflower tracks the sun; a woodlouse hides from light.
4. **Growth** — A **permanent** increase in dry mass and size, brought about by cell division (mitosis) and enlargement.
5. **Reproduction** — Production of new individuals. **Sexual reproduction** uses two parents and fuses gametes; **asexual reproduction** uses one parent (binary fission in Amoeba, budding in yeast, vegetative propagation in cassava).
6. **Excretion** — Removal of metabolic waste products (urea, carbon dioxide, excess salts) that would otherwise poison the body.
7. **Nutrition** — Intake and use of food. **Autotrophs** (green plants) photosynthesise; **heterotrophs** (animals, fungi) consume other organisms.

#### The Five-Kingdom Scheme

| Kingdom | Cell type | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Monera** | Prokaryotic (no true nucleus) | Bacteria, blue-green algae |
| **Protista** | Eukaryotic, mostly unicellular | Amoeba, Paramecium, Euglena |
| **Fungi** | Eukaryotic, cell wall of chitin | Mushroom, Rhizopus, yeast |
| **Plantae** | Eukaryotic, cell wall of cellulose, chloroplasts | Maize, fern, mango tree |
| **Animalia** | Eukaryotic, no cell wall | Human, goat, earthworm |

#### Animal Sub-Groups

**Vertebrates** possess a vertebral column (backbone) and are divided into **Mammals** (hair, mammary glands, give birth to live young — whales, bats), **Birds** (feathers, beak, lay hard-shelled eggs), **Reptiles** (dry scaly skin, lay eggs on land — lizard, snake), **Amphibians** (moist skin, dual life — frog, toad), and **Fish** (fins, gills, scales). **Invertebrates** lack a backbone. **Insects** have three body parts (head, thorax, abdomen) and six legs; **arachnids** (spiders, scorpions) have two body parts and eight legs; **annelids** are segmented worms; **molluscs** have soft bodies, often with shells (snail, slug).

#### Typical NCEE Question Patterns

Section A tests direct recall: *"Which of the following is NOT a characteristic of living things?"* Section B uses process-of-elimination matching (kingdom ↔ organism, vertebrate class ↔ features). NCEE often disguises respiration as breathing in distractors — pick the option describing **cellular energy release**, not air movement.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Common Misconceptions

- **Respiration ≠ breathing.** Plants respire continuously through stomata and root hairs; they do not breathe. Distractors that say "respiration means taking in air" should be rejected.
- **Movement in plants.** Though slow and often invisible, plants move (tropisms, nastic movements in *Mimosa pudica*, growth of tendrils). Saying "plants do not move" is wrong.
- **Whales and dolphins are mammals, not fish**, despite having fins and living in water. They lack gills, breathe through lungs, and suckle their young.
- **Insects vs. arachnids:** legs and body segments are the deciding features, not habitat or size. A spider has 8 legs → arachnid; an ant has 6 legs → insect.
- **Autotroph vs. heterotroph:** fungi and many bacteria are heterotrophs but are not animals. Do not assume "heterotroph = animal".
- **Growth vs. reproduction:** a seed swelling in water is *imbibition*, not true growth. True growth increases **dry mass** (water uptake alone does not).

#### Classification Hierarchy

From broadest to most specific: **Kingdom → Phylum → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species**. The full classification of *Homo sapiens* (human) is: Kingdom Animalia → Phylum Chordata → Class Mammalia → Order Primates → Family Hominidae → Genus *Homo* → Species *sapiens*. **Binomial nomenclature** (introduced by Carl Linnaeus) writes the genus name with a capital letter and the species in lowercase, both italicised, e.g., *Zea mays* (maize).

#### Worked Example

**Question:** A pupil observes an organism with a backbone, moist skin, and lives partly in water and partly on land. To which class does it belong?

**Solution:** Moist skin + dual life → **Amphibian** (Class Amphibia, e.g., frog). It is a vertebrate because of the backbone, ruled out of fish (no scales/gills only), reptiles (dry scaly skin), and mammals (no hair/mammary glands).

#### Practice Prompts

1. A farmer finds an organism with no backbone, a segmented body, and eight legs. State its class and justify by listing two features that rule out Insecta.
2. State two differences between respiration and breathing, and explain why plants must respire at night when photosynthesis stops.
