# India & International Trade

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> One-screen recap for the desk — read this on exam morning and you have the spine of the topic.

India runs a managed-floating rupee, settles its external accounts through a **Balance of Payments (BoP)** identity that must sum to zero, and frames trade policy through **DGFT's Foreign Trade Policy** and **WTO** obligations. The **Current Account** records goods, services, transfers and income; the **Capital Account** records FDI, FPI, ECBs and NRI deposits that finance any current account gap.

The single equation that decides a descriptive answer:

> **BoP = Current Account + Capital Account + Errors & Omissions = 0**

The **Trade Balance** is the goods-only slice (Exports of goods − Imports of goods, both in USD). **CAD** is the sum of trade balance plus net invisibles.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook (MASC-DI):** the Current Account holds **M**erchandise, **A**nd **S**ervices, **C**urrent transfers, **D**eficit, plus **I**ncome. Anything you cannot touch — shipping tickets, software bills, remittances, profit inflows — sits here.

| Account | What it captures | Currency unit | Surplus means |
|---|---|---|---|
| Current Account | Goods, services, transfers, income | USD | Net foreign earnings |
| Capital Account | FDI, FPI, ECBs, NRI deposits | USD | Net foreign liabilities rising |
| Trade Balance | Exports − Imports of goods only | USD | Competitiveness signal |

A weaker rupee does **not** automatically fix a deficit — the **Marshall-Lerner condition** (|εx| + |εm| > 1) gates the outcome, and the **J-curve** says it worsens before it improves.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Build the mechanism layer: how BoP identity works, why RBI manages but does not fix the rupee, how trade policy instruments interact with WTO rules.

#### BoP Architecture

The Balance of Payments is an accounting identity, not a profit-and-loss statement. Three sub-accounts must, by construction, sum to zero whenever a country settles its external transactions. The "deficit" you read in newspapers is almost always a **Current Account Deficit (CAD)** — a sub-component imbalance, not a BoP failure.

| Sub-account | Inflows (credit) | Outflows (debit) | 2024 character for India |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trade in goods | Exports of merchandise | Imports of merchandise | Persistent deficit (crude oil driven) |
| Trade in services | IT/software exports, BPO | Foreign-sourced services | Persistent surplus |
| Primary income | Investment income received | Outward investment income | Mixed |
| Secondary transfers | Remittances from NRIs | Outward transfers | Large surplus |
| Capital account | FDI, FPI, ECBs, NRI deposits | Outward FDI | Usually a surplus financing CAD |
| Errors & omissions | Statistical plug | Statistical plug | Residual balancer |

> 💡 **High-Yield Revision Hook:** if CAD widens, look at the Capital Account — it must widen in tandem, otherwise FX reserves fall. RBI's intervention **is** the balancing entry in many months.

#### Exchange Rate Mechanics

India operates a **managed-float** since March 1993. RBI does not peg, but it steps in through the forex market to lean against disorderly volatility. Two indices frame competitiveness:

- **NEER** = trade-weighted geometric average of the rupee against a basket of partner currencies; nominal, dimensionless.
- **REER** = NEER adjusted for relative inflation between India and partners; dimensionless, >100 means rupee is overvalued vs base year.

REER appreciation = exports costlier in foreign currency = competitive loss, even if headline GDP looks healthy. This is a recurring Phase-II trap.

#### Trade Policy Stack

India's tariff regime sits on four pillars:

1. **Customs Tariff Act, 1975** sets the legal schedule.
2. **Annual Finance Act Notifications** revise basic customs duty and cess rates.
3. **FTP (Foreign Trade Policy)** notified by DGFT under the **Foreign Trade (Development & Regulation) Act, 1992** governs licensing, export incentives, Advance Authorisation, EPCG and EOU/SEZ schemes. Current FTP took effect from **1 April 2023**.
4. **Anti-dumping and countervailing duties** flow from the **Customs Tariff (Identification, Assessment and Collection of Anti-dumping Duty on Dumped Articles and for Determination of Injury) Rules, 1995**, administered by the Directorate General of Trade Remedies.

On top of domestic rules, India is bound by WTO principles:

| WTO Principle | Effect on India |
|---|---|
| MFN (Most Favoured Nation) | Same tariff to all WTO members unless an FTA carve-out applies |
| National Treatment | Once goods enter, they must be treated like domestic goods |
| Bound tariff ceiling | India committed not to exceed scheduled rates for specified lines |
| TRIPS / TRIMS / GATS | IPR, investment-measure and services disciplines |

India grants MFN status to most WTO members; the well-known exception is non-MFN treatment of imports from specific neighbouring jurisdictions, which examiners love to test.

#### A Comparison Matrix That Eliminates Common Confusions

| Concept | Confused with | Actual distinction |
|---|---|---|
| BoP deficit | Current Account Deficit | BoP always balances; CAD is a sub-balance |
| REER | NEER | REER adjusts NEER for inflation differential |
| MFN | FTA preferential tariff | MFN is non-discriminatory across WTO members; FTA is a discriminatory carve-out |
| Trade Deficit | Current Account Deficit | Trade = goods only; CAD = goods + services + income + transfers |
| Full CAC | Partial CAC under FEMA 1999 | India still restricts capital outflows; no timeline for full CAC |
| RBI fixing rupee | RBI managing volatility | Float since 1993; RBI smooths, does not peg |

#### Worked Walk-Through on the J-Curve and Marshall-Lerner

Suppose the rupee depreciates 10% against the dollar. Imports of crude oil — priced in USD — instantly cost more in rupee terms, widening the trade deficit in the first quarter. By quarter four, export volumes respond and import volumes contract. Net improvement only materialises when export-price elasticity plus import-price elasticity (in absolute value) exceeds 1. If εx ≈ 0.6 and εm ≈ 0.4, you fail Marshall-Lerner and the trade balance stays worse even after adjustment — a real risk when exports are dollar-denominated services like IT.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** answer keys flag any answer claiming "rupee depreciation always improves trade balance" — that is wrong unless Marshall-Lerner is satisfied and J-curve time has elapsed.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Edge cases, regulatory nuances, and the kind of trick questions that show up in Phase-II descriptive items.

#### Capital Account Convertibility: Where India Actually Stands

The Tarapore Committee laid out a roadmap, but full CAC is still off the table. Under **FEMA, 1999**, residents face caps on outward remittances (the Liberalised Remittance Scheme permits a defined annual ceiling per individual — verify the current cap in the RBI notification), and corporates borrowing abroad route through the ECB framework. This partial-CAC regime lets RBI defend reserves without surrendering the monetary policy rate.

#### Terms of Trade and the Index Trap

**Net Barter Terms of Trade** = (Index of Export Price ÷ Index of Import Price) × 100. A reading above 100 means export prices are outpacing import prices — favourable for exporters if volume holds. A rising **Income Terms of Trade** (Net Barter × Export Volume Index) tells a kinder story than Net Barter alone because India simultaneously grew export volumes.

#### REER Construction Nuance

The RBI publishes six-currency and forty-currency REER baskets. The six-currency index is more volatile; the forty-currency index is the more credible competitiveness gauge. Watch for questions quoting a single index — answer should specify which basket and base year before commenting.

#### DGFT Instruments That Show Up in Questions

- **Advance Authorisation** — duty-free import of inputs for export production, with an export obligation.
- **EPCG (Export Promotion Capital Goods)** — zero or concessional import duty on capital goods, with an export performance condition over a multi-year period.
- **EOU / SEZ / EHTP / STP / BTP** — enclave-based schemes with net foreign exchange earning (NFE) obligations.
- **MEIS / RoDTEP** — incentive scrips on exports; RoDTEP replaced MEIS to comply with WTO subsidy rules.

Each is a WTO-sensitive subsidy design, and examiners frequently test why MEIS was discontinued and what RoDTEP replaced it.

#### Advanced Traps and Edge Cases

1. A BoP surplus in the capital account means **liabilities rose**, not that India "earned" anything — the economy is selling claims on itself.
2. Treating remittances as capital account — remittances are **secondary transfers** in the current account.
3. Assuming SEZ exports are double-counted in merchandise exports — they are counted once, with a separate SEZ-specific data series.
4. Believing depreciation is always inflationary — pass-through depends on the share of imported intermediates in the consumption basket.
5. Confusing FDI with FPI under volatile flows — FDI is equity + control intent (more stable); FPI is portfolio hot money (volatile).

#### Two Practice Prompts for Self-Assessment

1. *"Critically examine whether a weakening rupee is an unalloyed blessing for India's external sector."* Use BoP identity, Marshall-Lerner, J-curve, and CAD financing through capital inflows. Conclude with the managed-float posture.
2. *"Discuss how India's WTO commitments constrain its foreign trade policy choices."* Anchor in MFN, National Treatment, bound tariffs, TRIPS flexibilities (compulsory licensing history), and the move from MEIS to RoDTEP.
