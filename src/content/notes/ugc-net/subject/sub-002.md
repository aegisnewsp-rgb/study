# Subject-Specific Topics

> UGC NET Paper II carries subject-specific content that differs entirely depending on the candidate's chosen subject. This note explains the structure, not a single syllabus module.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

> Two papers, one exam: Paper I is shared across all subjects; Paper II changes with every chosen subject.

UGC NET has **two papers** of **100 marks each**. Paper I is a 50-question **General Aptitude** paper covering Teaching & Research Aptitude, Reasoning, Comprehension, Data Interpretation, and Communication. Paper II tests the **subject** the candidate has chosen at the time of application — it is fixed per subject and is not interchangeable across subjects.

| Slot | Paper | Questions | Marks | Nature |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| First session | Paper I (General Aptitude) | 50 | 100 | Common to all 83+ subjects |
| Second session | Paper II (Subject) | 100 | 100 | Changes with chosen subject |

The 5% planning weight on a topic called "Subject-Specific Topics" is a preparation heuristic, not a number UGC or NTA publishes. UGC does not release topic-wise weightage for Paper II.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** "**TRI-DC**" for Paper I recall — **T**eaching & Research Aptitude, **R**easoning, **I**nterpretation (Data), **D**ata Interpretation + **C**ommunication. For Paper II, the only mnemonic is the subject name itself.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### How the two-paper split actually works

Paper I and Paper II are scheduled on the same day in two timed sessions of **1 hour and 30 minutes each**, with no overlap in content. A candidate who opted for **Commerce** cannot answer questions from the **Computer Science & Applications** Paper II, even on a related topic like accounting software, because the syllabus boundary is the chosen subject.

#### Comparison matrix: Paper I vs Paper II

| Feature | Paper I (General Aptitude) | Paper II (Subject-Specific) |
|---|---|---|
| Mandatory? | Yes, for every candidate | Yes, but the *content* depends on subject |
| Common across subjects? | Identical paper for all | Different paper per subject (83+ syllabi) |
| Number of questions | 50 | 100 |
| Marks | 100 | 100 |
| Syllabus source | Single unified UGC syllabus | Separate UGC syllabus PDF per subject |
| Tested skills | Teaching, research methodology, reasoning | Subject mastery |

#### Where the official syllabus lives

UGC publishes each subject syllabus as a separate PDF on **ugc.ac.in**. NTA hosts the registration and question-paper archive on **ugcnet.nta.ac.in**. The two bodies work in tandem — UGC designs the syllabus, NTA conducts the test. Candidates who study from third-party "important topics" lists without cross-checking against the official PDF regularly miss units that UGC added or restructured.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

A candidate's coaching material lists "**Research Aptitude**" under Paper I and claims it carries 10–12 questions. The candidate assumes this weightage is official.

**Step 1 — Identify the claim.** The coaching note treats topic-wise numbers as fixed.

**Step 2 — Check the official source.** UGC publishes Paper I as a single combined unit list; it does **not** assign question counts per unit. NTA releases past papers, but the distribution varies year to year.

**Step 3 — Apply the rule.** Treat any per-topic question count as a planning heuristic, not an official allocation. Use UGC syllabus units for **coverage** and past 10-year papers for **frequency estimation**.

**Step 4 — Conclude.** The candidate should study every Paper I unit but rely on past papers for frequency, not on a third-party number.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Coaching notes often quote "5 to 10 questions per unit" as if it were a UGC rule. UGC does not fix this. Candidates who skip a unit because a third-party list rated it "low yield" lose marks whenever NTA shifts emphasis.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

#### Edge cases that change the strategy

1. **JRF vs Assistant Professor only.** UGC NET has two qualifying outcomes: **Junior Research Fellowship (JRF)** eligibility, and **Assistant Professor (AP)** eligibility. The score cutoffs differ, and JRF cutoffs are uniformly higher within the same subject.
2. **Subjects with overlapping syllabi.** Economics, Commerce, and Public Administration share units on Indian economy and public policy. Candidates sometimes switch subject mid-preparation — this resets Paper II entirely and is rarely worth the loss.
3. **Subjects discontinued or added.** UGC occasionally revises the list of 83+ subjects. Confirm the subject exists on the current application form before buying a Paper II book two years old.

#### Mathematical and structural limits

There is no negative marking in UGC NET. Each correct answer carries **2 marks** out of the **100** per paper. The aggregate qualifying score across both papers determines JRF or AP eligibility, not the per-paper score alone.

#### Advanced practice prompts

1. Pull the official UGC syllabus PDF for **your** subject. Count the units. Cross-check against the previous three years' Paper II papers and mark which units NTA actually tested. Does the third-party "important topics" list match the official unit coverage?
2. Take one full Paper I mock and one full Paper II mock back to back. Compare the time pressure. Paper II on an unfamiliar subject almost always overruns; Paper I rarely does. Adjust the order you attempt them in your real exam.
