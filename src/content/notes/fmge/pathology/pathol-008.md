# Infectious Diseases

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your FMGE.

Infectious diseases arise when **pathogenic microorganisms** — bacteria, viruses, fungi, parasites, or prions — invade a host, multiply, and produce illness through **direct tissue damage**, **toxin secretion**, or **immune-mediated injury**. Pathogen power is described by **virulence**, **toxigenicity** (secreted exotoxins, e.g. diphtheria, tetanus, botulinum) and **invasiveness**; Gram-negative **endotoxin (LPS)** activates TLR4 → TNF-α/IL-1 → fever, shock, DIC.

> High-yield: Exotoxins are protein, heat-labile, convertible to **toxoids**; endotoxin is LPS, heat-stable, not neutralised by toxoid.

Bacterial load quantifies as **CFU/mL = colonies ÷ (volume × dilution)**. Disease stages run **incubation → prodrome → acute → decline → convalescence**, with outcomes ranging from full recovery through **carrier state** to death. Granulomatous inflammation (TB = caseating with Langhans giant cells) is a recurring image-based MCQ.

| Feature | Exotoxin | Endotoxin |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Living Gram ± | Gram-negative outer membrane |
| Chemistry | Protein | LPS (lipid A) |
| Heat stability | Labile | Stable |
| Toxoid vaccine | Yes | No |
| Classic effect | Neurotoxic/cytotoxic | DIC, shock, fever |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Pathogenesis mechanisms
Bacteria adhere via **pili/adhesins**, then invade or secrete toxins. **Exotoxins** (A–B subunit architecture) act enzymatically — diphtheria toxin inhibits EF-2, tetanus toxin blocks inhibitory interneurons (Renshaw cells), botulinum cleaves SNARE proteins. **Endotoxin (LPS, lipid A)** binds CD14/TLR4, triggering MyD88 signalling and TNF-α release, producing the **septic shock** triad: hypotension, DIC, multiorgan failure.

Viruses cause disease through **cytolysis** (poliovirus), **non-cytolytic persistence** (HIV), **immune-mediated injury** (hepatitis B), and **transformation** (EBV → lymphoma; HPV → cervical cancer; HTLV → leukaemia).

#### Morphological tissue responses
- **Acute suppurative:** neutrophilic abscesses (Staphylococcus, Streptococcus).
- **Chronic:** lymphocytes, plasma cells, macrophages.
- **Granulomatous:** epithelioid macrophages, **Langhans giant cells**, lymphocytes ± central **caseous necrosis** (TB) or non-caseating (sarcoidosis).

#### Disease patterns tested in FMGE
| Disease | Hallmark lesion | Key diagnostic |
|---|---|---|
| Tuberculosis | Caseating granuloma, Langhans giant cells | AFB on Ziehl–Neelsen, LJ medium |
| Leprosy (lepromatous) | Foamy **lepra/Virchow cells** packed with AFB | Slit-skin smear, Fite stain |
| Typhoid | **Typhoid nodules** in Peyer's patches, ileal ulcers | Widal (rising titre), blood culture |
| Amoebiasis | **Flask-shaped** colonic ulcers; trophozoite with ingested RBCs | Fresh wet mount, serology |
| Malaria | Ring forms, schizonts, haemozoin | Giemsa-stained peripheral smear |
| Syphilis (tertiary) | **Gumma**, endarteritis obliterans | VDRL, TPHA confirmation |
| HIV/AIDS | CD4 depletion, opportunistic infections | ELISA → Western blot; CD4 count |

#### Diagnostic test performance
**Sensitivity = TP / (TP + FN)** and **Specificity = TN / (TN + FP)**. Positive predictive value rises with prevalence — critical when interpreting Widal or VDRL in endemic India.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer timeline.

#### Edge cases and micro-mechanisms
**Koch's postulates** assume pure culture is achievable; modern molecular **modified postulates** accommodate viruses (HIV, HCV), prions (CJD), and uncultivable organisms (Treponema pallidum, Mycobacterium leprae). Recognise **bacteraemia** (transient blood presence) versus **septicaemia** (systemic LPS/toxin-mediated illness with shock and DIC) versus **pyemia** (metastatic abscesses, e.g. *S. aureus*).

#### Worked micro-example
A blood culture plate shows 150 colonies on a 0.1 mL loop inoculated from a 1:10⁵ dilution.
**CFU/mL = 150 ÷ (0.1 × 10⁻⁵) = 1.5 × 10⁸ CFU/mL**, consistent with significant bacteraemia.

#### Adjacent links and traps
The **inoculum effect** (MIC_dense ÷ MIC_standard ≥ 4) flags β-lactamase hyperproducers — a tested concept in antibiotic stewardship. **Mantoux induration** thresholds shift with risk: **≥5 mm** in HIV/close contacts, **≥10 mm** in endemic adults (Indian default), **≥15 mm** in low-prevalence countries. Latency (HSV, VZV, HIV, TB) versus carrier state (typhoid, hepatitis B) is frequently confused.

#### Common FMGE traps
> Mistaking **Entamoeba histolytica** (ingests RBCs, flask ulcers) for non-pathogenic **E. dispar** (never ingests RBCs) costs marks.

Sterilisation parameters are distinct: **autoclave 121 °C × 15 psi × 15 min** versus **dry heat 160 °C × 1 h** versus **2 % glutaraldehyde** high-level disinfection for endoscopes. Transmission-based precautions: **TB → airborne (N95, negative pressure)**, influenza/diphtheria → **droplet**, MRSA/*C. difficile* → **contact**.

#### Practice prompts
1. A 30-year-old has a Mantoux induration of 12 mm in Delhi. He is HIV-negative, asymptomatic, with no TB contact. Interpret the result and recommend next steps.
2. Compare and contrast lepromatous and tuberculoid leprosy on immunity, histology, and bacillary load — and explain why lepromatous patients show foamy macrophages.
