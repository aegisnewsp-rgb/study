# Interpret and apply mathematical and statistical information in context (91946) — NCEA Level 1 Mathematics and Statistics Notes

Achievement Standard 91946 is the first of two NCEA Level 1 Mathematics and Statistics externals. It carries 5 credits and is assessed by an end-of-year NZQA examination in November/December. The standard asks students to interpret given mathematical and statistical information, select and apply appropriate methods, and solve problems in real-world contexts. Unlike 91944 and 91945 (the internals), 91946 provides all the data and the context in the question; the student's job is to choose methods and apply them.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **5 credits**, external NZQA examination at the end of Year 11.
- The exam provides **all the data** — students choose methods, not data sources.
- Questions cover **number, algebra, geometry, measurement, statistics, and probability** applied in context.
- The exam runs for **three hours** with multiple questions.
- **Merit** requires relational thinking — choosing the most appropriate method for the context, communicating the reasoning clearly.
- **Excellence** requires extended abstract thinking — insight into why a particular result holds, generalisation, reflection on the model.

#### Examiner traps

- Writing only the answer — Excellence-level answers require the reasoning that leads to the answer.
- Using a calculator when the question is non-calculator (NZQA papers explicitly mark which questions allow calculators).
- Picking a method that works but is not the most efficient — Merit and Excellence reward method selection.
- Missing units — NZQA markers expect units on every numerical answer with a unit.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Exam structure

The 91946 paper has four or five questions, each worth a different mark allocation. The questions combine multiple topics — a single question might require algebra to set up, a calculator-based computation, and a statistical interpretation. Total marks across the paper are around 40-50, scaled to the 5-credit value.

The exam is **three hours** long and **calculator-active** (specific calculator models are approved by NZQA; check the current list on nzqa.govt.nz).

#### Topic-by-topic preparation

**Number.** Fractions, decimals, percentages, ratio, rates, best buys, percentage change (including compound change), standard form, indices. Practice percentages in context (GST, discount, inflation) and ratio problems (recipe scaling, map scales, unit conversions).

**Algebra.** Substitute into formulae; simplify algebraic expressions; solve linear equations; rearrange formulae; solve simple simultaneous equations by substitution; solve simple quadratic equations by factorising. Practice selecting the right approach: factorise when the equation has obvious factors, use the quadratic formula when it does not.

**Geometry.** Angle properties (corresponding, alternate, co-interior), interior angles of polygons, similar shapes and scale factor, areas of triangles and composite shapes, volumes of prisms, Pythagoras' theorem. Practice 3D problems that combine area and volume.

**Measurement.** Unit conversions within and between metric units; perimeter, area, volume of common shapes; speed-distance-time; density; currency conversion. Practice the relationship between speed, distance and time, and check units carefully.

**Statistics.** Reading and interpreting graphs (bar, line, scatter, stem-and-leaf, box plot); summary statistics (mean, median, mode, range, IQR); comparing two groups; time-series reading and trend identification.

**Probability.** Sample space diagrams; two-way tables; tree diagrams for independent and dependent events; expected value; experimental probability compared to theoretical.

#### A worked approach to a context question

A typical 91946 question gives a real-world scenario and asks for analysis. For example:

> "A school orders 30 calculators for a class. The supplier offers two prices: $240 for all 30, or $8.50 each. The school can also buy an insurance policy for $15 that covers any calculator that fails during the year. Historically, 1 in 12 calculators fail. Compare the two purchase options."

Steps:

1. **Read the question** and identify what is being asked (compare the two purchase options).
2. **Identify the data** (30 calculators, $240 or $8.50 each, $15 insurance, 1 in 12 failure rate).
3. **Calculate option 1:** $240 flat. **Calculate option 2:** 30 × $8.50 = $255, which is already more than option 1. So option 1 is cheaper without insurance.
4. **Add insurance:** Option 1 with insurance = $240 + $15 = $255. Option 2 with insurance = $255 + $15 = $270. Option 1 with insurance is still cheaper.
5. **Conclusion:** Option 1 with insurance is the cheapest at $255.

Notice the explicit reasoning at each step. NZQA markers look for visible working, not just final numbers.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Method selection

The single biggest gap between Achieved and Excellence on 91946 is method selection. Achieved-grade students pick the first method that gives an answer; Excellence-grade students compare two methods and justify the choice.

For example, on a quadratic equation x² − 5x + 6 = 0:

- Achieved approach: trial and error to find x = 2 or x = 3. Works here.
- Merit approach: factorise as (x − 2)(x − 3) = 0 and solve. Faster and more general.
- Excellence approach: factorise, then compare with the quadratic formula result to show the methods agree, and comment on which is more efficient when the discriminant is small vs large.

For a percentage change problem (a price goes from $80 to $92):

- Achieved approach: subtract to get $12, divide by $80, get 0.15, multiply by 100, get 15%.
- Merit approach: state "the price increased by 15% of the original $80, so the new price is $80 × 1.15 = $92".
- Excellence approach: recognise that percentage change depends on which value is the base (here $80 is the base), and explain why $80 is the correct base rather than $92 or some midpoint.

#### Time management on the exam

- Spend the first 10 minutes reading all questions and noting which method you will use for each.
- Tackle the highest-mark questions first to ensure the biggest pay-off per minute.
- Leave 15 minutes at the end for checking units, calculator entry, and missing working.
- NZQA penalises careless errors more than method choice — a clean Achieved answer with all units and visible working scores higher than an Excellence answer with arithmetic slips.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "I don't need to write the question back." NZQA markers need to see what your answer refers to.
- "Calculator entry doesn't matter." It does — entering 8.5 instead of 8.50 or pressing ÷ when you meant × loses the mark.
- "Geometry is only shapes." 91946 geometry questions usually embed the geometry in a real-world context (a building, a field, a fence). Practice translating word problems into geometry.
- "Statistics means drawing graphs." It also means reading them — many 91946 marks come from interpreting a graph correctly.
