# Te Reo Matatini equivalents (US32413, US32415) — NCEA UE Literacy Notes

For students working in te reo Māori, the University Entrance literacy requirement can be met through Te Reo Matatini (Māori-medium literacy) unit standards instead of the English-medium equivalents. US32413 is the Te Reo Matatini reading standard, and US32415 is the Te Reo Matatini writing standard. Both carry 5 credits at Level 1 or above and contribute to UE literacy in the same way as US32403 and US32405.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **5 credits each**, internally assessed by the school or approved provider.
- US32413 (Te Reo Torohū, reading) and US32415 (Te Reo Whakaputa, writing) are the Te Reo Matatini equivalents of US32403 (reading) and US32405 (writing).
- For students on a Māori-medium pathway, these standards substitute for the English-medium dedicated co-requisite unit standards.
- Both standards are part of the dedicated co-requisite framework introduced from 2024 onwards (transition period 2024-2027).

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing Te Reo Matatini with Te Reo Māori subject standards — Te Reo Matatini standards test literacy in te reo Māori; Te Reo Māori subject standards test language proficiency at various levels.
- Assuming these standards are only for fluent speakers — the standards are designed for students on a Māori-medium pathway, with appropriate support.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### The Te Reo Matatini framework

The Te Reo Matatini framework is the Māori-medium equivalent of the English-medium literacy and numeracy co-requisite framework. The framework was developed to recognise Māori-medium education as a valid pathway for UE and NCEA Level 1.

The Te Reo Matatini standards:

- US32413: Te Reo Torohū (Māori-medium reading)
- US32415: Te Reo Whakaputa (Māori-medium writing)
- US32412: Te Pāngarau (Māori-medium numeracy)

The corresponding Te Reo Pāngarau standards are part of the same framework.

#### How US32413 and US32415 fit into UE

Together, US32413 and US32415 satisfy the full 10-credit UE literacy requirement (5 reading + 5 writing), just as US32403 + US32405 do for English-medium students.

A student on a Māori-medium pathway can use either set of standards to meet UE.

#### What the standards test

US32413 tests reading comprehension and analysis of te reo Māori texts. The student reads texts in te reo Māori and demonstrates understanding, including analysis of language features and structure.

US32415 tests writing in te reo Māori for specific purposes and audiences. The student produces written texts that communicate ideas effectively in te reo Māori.

#### Internal assessment logistics

The standards are internally assessed by the school or approved provider. The materials are in te reo Māori. Marking is moderated by NZQA, with cultural advisors typically involved to ensure cultural responsiveness.

#### Preparation strategy

For students on a Māori-medium pathway:

1. **Read widely in te reo Māori** — engage with diverse texts in te reo Māori.
2. **Practice analysis** — language features and structure in te reo Māori texts.
3. **Practice writing** — for different purposes and audiences in te reo Māori.
4. **Use whānau and kaiako support** — the Māori-medium pathway is supported by the school and community.

#### Cultural considerations

The Te Reo Matatini framework was developed with input from iwi and Māori-medium education experts. The standards recognise te reo Māori as a taonga (treasure) and a valid medium for assessment.

For Excellence-level work, students engage with the cultural context of te reo Māori, including tikanga (customs), kawa (protocols), and the relationship between language and identity.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Why Te Reo Matatini matters

The Te Reo Matatini framework recognises the right of students to be assessed in their language of instruction. For many Māori-medium students, this is essential for fair assessment — being assessed in English would not reflect their actual literacy skills.

The framework also supports the revitalisation of te reo Māori by providing a pathway for students to achieve NCEA and UE through te reo Māori.

#### Excellence in Te Reo Matatini

The Excellence grade requires perceptive engagement with te reo Māori texts, including:

- Analysis of language features (e.g., metaphor, repetition, rhythm) specific to te reo Māori.
- Connection to cultural context (e.g., the significance of whakapapa, kōrero tuku iho, mōteatea).
- Personal response grounded in te ao Māori.

For Excellence writing, the student demonstrates sophisticated control of te reo Māori, including appropriate register (formal vs informal) and tikanga-appropriate expression.

#### Bilingual pathways

Some students take both the English-medium and Te Reo Matatini standards. This can result in more than 10 literacy credits, which is fine — the UE requirement is a minimum of 10 credits with at least 5 reading and 5 writing.

For students aiming to study at a Māori-medium tertiary institution (e.g., Te Wānanga o Aotearoa, Te Wānanga o Raukawa), the Te Reo Matatini standards are particularly valuable.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Te Reo Matatini is only for fluent speakers." No — it is for students on a Māori-medium pathway, with appropriate support.
- "Te Reo Matatini standards are easier than English standards." They are different, not easier or harder.
- "I can use both Te Reo Matatini and English standards." Yes — they both count toward UE literacy.
- "Cultural context doesn't matter." It does — the standards are grounded in te ao Māori.
