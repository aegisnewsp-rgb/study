# Task 1 — Describing Visual Information — IELTS Writing (Qatar)

In the IELTS Academic Writing test, **Task 1** is the **describing task**: you are
given a visual input (graph, table, chart, or diagram) and you must write a
**report** describing the data in **150+ words** in approximately **20 minutes**.
The report must include an **opening paraphrase**, an **overview of the key
trend**, **supporting detail with data**, and **accurate comparison across
categories**.

This is the **first task** in the ielts-writing subject, and it is **worth
one-third** of the Writing band score (Task 2 is worth two-thirds). Despite
being the shorter task, Task 1 requires a **specific academic register** that
many Qatari candidates struggle with. The examiner scores Task 1 on
**task achievement** (did you write 150+ words? did you cover the key features
of the visual?), **coherence and cohesion** (is your report well-paragraphed and
logically ordered?), **lexical resource** (do you have varied vocabulary for
describing data?), and **grammatical range and accuracy** (do you use a mix
of simple and complex sentences?).

The most common Task 1 question types are:

- **Line graphs** — showing change over time (e.g. population growth in
  Qatar from 2014 to 2024).
- **Bar charts** — comparing categories at one point in time (e.g. GDP by
  sector in Qatar in 2024).
- **Pie charts** — showing proportions (e.g. energy mix in Qatar in 2024).
- **Tables** — showing multiple categories across multiple dimensions (e.g.
  population, GDP, and life expectancy across GCC countries).
- **Process diagrams** — showing a process (e.g. desalination process in
  Qatar).
- **Maps** — showing change over time (e.g. development of Lusail City from
  2010 to 2025).

This note shows the LCA (Long-Read Cohort Appetite) version: a **Lite**
(5-minute) read, a **Standard** (15-minute) read, and the **Exam Essentials**
table a Qatari candidate can use to revise in the last hour before the test.

## Lite (4-Step Method)

1. **Identify the visual type and time range.** Is it a line graph, bar
   chart, pie chart, table, process, or map? What is the time range (if
   any)? What are the units (percentage, millions, thousands)?
2. **Write the opening paraphrase.** Do NOT copy the visual's title
   verbatim. Rephrase it: *"The line graph illustrates the population of
   Qatar from 2014 to 2024"* (NOT *"The graph shows the population of
   Qatar from 2014 to 2024"*).
3. **Write the overview (1–2 sentences) highlighting the KEY trend.** The
   overview is the most important part of Task 1 — it is where the
   examiner sees whether you have identified the key feature. ALWAYS write
   an overview. NEVER omit it.
4. **Write 1–2 body paragraphs with specific figures.** Group the data:
   highest, lowest, biggest change, smallest change, comparison. Use
   specific figures (e.g. *"Population grew from 2.2 million in 2014 to
   2.9 million in 2024, an increase of approximately 700,000"*).

**Insight: Task 1 is not a list of numbers — it is a structured report.**
The examiner scores whether you can identify the **key trend** and
**support it with grouped data**. A Task 1 response that lists every
single figure without grouping signals band 5; a Task 1 response that
identifies the key trend, supports it with grouped data, and uses
varied vocabulary signals band 7+.

### 6 Examiner Traps (Task 1)

- **The verbatim-copy trap** — candidates copy the visual's title or labels
  verbatim. The examiner scores paraphrasing. ALWAYS paraphrase the title.
- **The no-overview trap** — candidates omit the overview. The examiner
  scores task achievement. ALWAYS write an overview.
- **The no-data trap** — candidates describe the trend without specific data.
  The examiner scores task achievement. ALWAYS include specific figures.
- **The list-everything trap** — candidates list every figure from the
  visual without grouping. The examiner scores coherence and cohesion.
  ALWAYS group the data (highest, lowest, biggest change, comparison).
- **The over-generalization trap** — candidates make claims without data
  (*"Population increased significantly"*) without specifying the magnitude.
  ALWAYS include specific figures (*"Population increased by approximately
  700,000, from 2.2 million to 2.9 million"*).
- **The wrong-tense trap** — candidates use the wrong tense (present for
  past, past for present). Pair correctly to the time range: past tense
  for completed periods (*"In 2014, the population was 2.2 million"*), present
  perfect for ongoing trends (*"The population has grown steadily over
  the past decade"*).

## Standard (7-Step Method with Long-Form Guidance)

### Step 1 — Identify the visual type and time range

Before you write anything, identify:

- **Visual type** — line graph / bar chart / pie chart / table / process
  diagram / map.
- **Time range** — past (1900–2000), present (2024), future (2025–2030),
  or none (single point in time).
- **Units** — percentage, millions, thousands, currency (USD / QAR),
  specific counts.
- **Categories** — what is being compared (countries, age groups,
  sectors, products, processes).

This identification takes 1–2 minutes but saves 5–10 minutes of
uncertain writing later.

### Step 2 — Write the opening paraphrase (1 sentence)

The opening sentence should paraphrase the visual's title. Use varied
vocabulary:

- *"The line graph illustrates..."* (NOT *"The graph shows..."*)
- *"The bar chart depicts..."* (NOT *"The chart shows..."*)
- *"The pie chart presents..."* (NOT *"The chart shows..."*)
- *"The table summarizes..."* (NOT *"The table shows..."*)
- *"The diagram outlines..."* (for process diagrams)
- *"The two maps depict..."* (for map comparisons)

Avoid mechanical paraphrases (*"The given graph shows..."*). Use varied
vocabulary.

### Step 3 — Write the overview (1–2 sentences)

The overview is the **key feature** of the data. It is the most important
1–2 sentences in your Task 1 response. Examples:

- *"Overall, the population of Qatar grew steadily over the period."*
- *"Overall, Qatar's energy mix is dominated by natural gas."*
- *"Overall, the two maps show significant urban development in Lusail
  City."*

The overview must NOT contain specific figures (those are in the body
paragraphs). The overview must state the **key trend** or **main
comparison**.

### Step 4 — Identify 2–3 main groups of data

Identify the main groups:

- **Highest / lowest values** — the categories with the largest /
  smallest values.
- **Biggest change** — the categories that changed the most.
- **Comparison** — the categories that are similar / different.
- **Trends over time** — the overall direction (rising, falling, stable,
  fluctuating).

### Step 5 — Write body paragraph 1 (highest / lowest / biggest change)

Body paragraph 1 covers the highest values, lowest values, and biggest
changes. Use specific figures:

- *"In 2024, the population of Qatar reached 2.9 million, the highest
  figure in the period."*
- *"By contrast, the population in 2014 was 2.2 million, the lowest in
  the period."*
- *"The largest increase occurred between 2018 and 2020, when the
  population grew by approximately 200,000."*

### Step 6 — Write body paragraph 2 (comparison / smaller changes)

Body paragraph 2 covers comparisons and smaller changes. Use specific
figures:

- *"In comparison, the urban population grew at a faster rate than the
  total population, increasing from 2.0 million to 2.8 million over the
  period."*
- *"Rural areas, by contrast, remained relatively stable, with only a
  modest increase of 30,000 over the decade."*

### Step 7 — Review and edit (5 minutes)

Reserve the last 5 minutes to review and edit your response. Check:

- ✅ Did you paraphrase the title? (NOT copy)
- ✅ Did you write an overview? (NOT omit)
- ✅ Did you include specific figures? (NOT generalize)
- ✅ Did you group the data? (NOT list everything)
- ✅ Did you use varied vocabulary? (NOT repeat)
- ✅ Did you use varied grammar? (NOT only simple sentences)
- ✅ Word count: 150+ words? (NOT less)
- ✅ Grammar: any errors?

## Previous Year Patterns (IELTS 2018–2025)

IELTS Writing Task 1 has used the same format consistently since 2018:
visual input + 150+ words in ~20 minutes. Over 2018–2025:

- 2018–2020: Task 1 commonly used line graphs and bar charts (70%+ of
  tests).
- 2021–2023: Task 1 diversified to include pie charts, tables, and process
  diagrams (~15% of tests each).
- 2024–2025: Task 1 frequently uses mixed charts (combining line + bar
  in one visual), with 80% coverage of all visual types.

The pattern: IELTS Writing Task 1 visual types have diversified since
2018, with mixed charts becoming more frequent. Candidates who practice
all visual types outperform those who focus on one type.

## Pro Tips (10 enumeration)

1. **Identify the visual type first.** Line/bar/pie/table/process/map.
2. **Paraphrase the title.** Do not copy the visual's title.
3. **Write an overview (1-2 sentences).** Identify the key trend.
4. **Group the data.** Highest / lowest / biggest change / comparison.
5. **Include specific figures.** Do not generalize without data.
6. **Use varied vocabulary.** Seen (rise, grow, climb, increase, surge).
7. **Use varied grammar.** Mix simple and complex sentences.
8. **Use correct tense.** Past for completed; present perfect for ongoing.
9. **Aim for 170-200 words.** Not less than 150 words.
10. **Reserve 5 minutes for review.** Do not submit without review.
