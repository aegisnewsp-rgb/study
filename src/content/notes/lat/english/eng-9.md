# Spotting Errors

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your LAT English slot.

Spotting Errors tests whether you can locate the grammatically faulty segment inside a sentence — usually one of four underlined parts (a–d), or one of five alternatives. LAT items follow the HEC aptitude pattern where every option carries a phrase and "No error" is the fifth alternative. Aim to scan each sentence for **subject–verb agreement**, **tense consistency**, and **article choice** before checking finer points like prepositions or modals.

- **Read the full sentence first**, then judge each underlined chunk on its own grammatical fit.
- **Mark "No error" only after verifying** every phrase — verb form, article, pronoun, preposition.
- **Common LAT traps**: "a/an" by sound (not spelling), "fewer vs less", parallelism breaks in "either…or" lists.

| Quick-fix checklist | What to look for |
| --- | --- |
| Subject–verb | Singular subject → singular verb, even with a long phrase in between |
| Tense | Same tense unless a time-shift word ("before", "since", "when") is present |
| Article | "a" before consonant sound, "an" before vowel sound |
| Pronoun | Agrees with antecedent in number, gender, person |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### How the question is framed
LAT presents a single sentence with four underlined segments labelled (a), (b), (c), (d), plus option (e) "No error". You select the option that contains the grammatical fault. Sometimes the format inverts — five full sentences are given and you pick the *incorrect* one. Either way, the skill is identical: parse the sentence into subject, verb, object, and modifiers, then test each underlined unit against the rules of standard English.

#### The eight rules examiners exploit
1. **Subject–verb agreement** in number and person — the head noun governs the verb, not the nearest noun ("The box of chocolates **is** on the table").
2. **Tense consistency** — keep one tense within a clause unless a time-shift marker demands otherwise.
3. **Pronoun–antecedent agreement** — singular indefinite pronouns ("each", "everyone", "somebody") take singular pronouns.
4. **Articles** — driven by the *sound* that follows, not the letter: "a university", "an honest man", "a one-rupee coin".
5. **Prepositions** — governed by fixed collocations: *interested in*, *good at*, *depend on*, *angry with* (person) / *angry at* (thing).
6. **Modals** — match meaning to form: *should* (advice), *would* (past habit, polite request), *could* (past ability), *must* (strong obligation).
7. **Parallelism** — items in a list joined by *and*, *or*, *both…and*, *either…or*, *not only…but also* must share grammatical shape.
8. **Voice and speech** — passive constructions require *be + past participle*; indirect speech back-shifts tense ("He said he **was** tired").

#### Standard LAT problem types
- **Type A:** Underlined segments (a–d) with "No error" — choose the faulty letter.
- **Type B:** Five sentences, pick the one containing the error.
- **Type C:** A sentence with four bold words, identify the wrongly used word.

| Error category | Typical LAT signal | Quick test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Verb form | "has went", "is belong" | Replace with base form mentally |
| Article | "a apple", "an university" | Say it aloud — listen for the first sound |
| Preposition | "good in", "depend of" | Recall the fixed phrase |
| Parallelism | "to read, writing, and to paint" | All three forms must match |

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge cases that decide top scores
Most LAT aspirants lose marks on questions where the error is **stylistic** rather than blatantly ungrammatical. Watch for dangling modifiers ("Walking through the park, the trees were beautiful"), faulty comparison degrees ("more better", "most unique" treated as non-comparable), and the misuse of *who* versus *whom* in object position. Conditionals are another minefield: a Type 2 sentence ("If I **was** rich, I would travel") is technically acceptable in informal speech, but HEC items prefer the subjunctive "*were*".

#### Worked micro-example
**Sentence:** "Each of the students (a) / have submitted (b) / their assignments (c) / before the deadline. (d) / No error (e)"
The head of the subject phrase is the singular pronoun **Each**, so the verb must be *has submitted*, not *have submitted*. Option **(b)** is the error. Note also that *their* agrees with *students* (plural antecedent in the prepositional phrase), which is acceptable — the trap is the verb.

#### Exam-specific strategy
LAT English contributes roughly 3% to the overall score, but Spotting Errors often appears as 8–12 items in the verbal cluster, making it one of the higher-yield sub-tests per minute spent. Allocate **45–60 seconds per item**: 15 seconds to read the whole sentence, 20 seconds to test each underlined segment, 10 seconds to confirm or pick "No error". Skip-and-return works poorly here because the sentence is short and the reasoning is fresh only on first read.

| Priority drill | Daily reps | Expected gain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Subject–verb agreement with intervening phrases | 10 sentences | Catches 25–30% of items |
| Article by sound, not spelling | 8 sentences | Catches 15% of items |
| Parallelism in correlative lists | 6 sentences | Catches 12% of items |
| Preposition collocations | 6 sentences | Catches 10% of items |

#### Practice prompts
1. Identify the error: "The sceneries of Kashmir are more beautiful than any other place in Pakistan."
2. Find the faulty segment: "Neither the principal nor the teachers (a) / was present (b) / when the inspector (c) / arrived. (d) / No error (e)"
