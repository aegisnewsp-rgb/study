# Legal Maxims

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Legal maxims are short Latin or English phrases that codify a rule of law used in Indian and common-law jurisprudence. CLAT tests them in three forms: direct meaning recall, maxim-to-principle matching, and scenario application. Mastering 25–30 high-frequency maxims covers roughly **4–6 questions** in the Legal Reasoning section.

- **Mens Rea** = guilty mind; **Actus Reus** = guilty act. Both needed for most crimes (Section 6 IPC, Section 10 Indian Contract Act).
- **Audi Alteram Partem** = hear the other side; **Nemo Judex In Causa Sua** = no one should be a judge in their own cause (natural justice).
- **Volenti Non Fit Injuria** = to a willing person, no injury is done — valid only with free and informed consent, never for negligence or trespass.

| Maxim | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Res Ipsa Loquitur | The thing speaks for itself |
| Caveat Emptor | Let the buyer beware |
| Ignorantia Juris Non Excusat | Ignorance of law is no excuse |
| Stare Decisis | Stand by things decided |
| Pacta Sunt Servanda | Agreements must be kept |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Origin and Indian Anchors
Most CLAT-tested maxims descend from English common law, re-imported into India via the IPC 1860, the Indian Contract Act 1872, and the Constitution. Section 6 IPC and Section 10 of the Contract Act operationalise **Mens Rea** and free consent respectively, while Articles 14 and 19 read **Audi Alteram Partem** into administrative action. The Consortium of NLUs lists legal propositions, including maxims, as part of the Legal Reasoning syllabus.

#### Categories of Maxims Tested
CLAT does not ask definitions in isolation — it pairs maxims with fact patterns. Three functional clusters dominate:

| Cluster | Key Maxims | Legal Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Criminal liability | Mens Rea, Actus Reus, Ignorantia Juris Non Excusat, Volenti Non Fit Injuria | Determines guilt and available defences |
| Contract interpretation | Pacta Sunt Servanda, Expressio Unius Est Exclusio Alterius, Noscitur a Sociis, Ejusdem Generis | Governs how clauses and lists are read |
| Evidence & procedure | Res Ipsa Loquitur, Audi Alteram Partem, Nemo Judex In Causa Sua, Habeas Corpus | Controls proof and fair hearing |

#### Application Pattern
Read the fact pattern, isolate the legal issue (guilt? consent? interpretation? fair hearing?), then match the cluster. A facts-pattern mentioning a contract dispute triggers the contract cluster; one mentioning a police confession without a lawyer triggers the criminal and procedural clusters together.

- Always distinguish **Expressio Unius** (exclusion by inclusion) from **Ejusdem Generis** (general words limited by specific words preceding them).
- **Stare Decisis** binds lower courts to High Court and Supreme Court precedent under Article 141.
- **Bona Fide** and **Mala Fide** appear frequently in service and administrative law passages — note that bona fide means in good faith, mala fide means its opposite.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Statutory Overrides
Several maxims survive in textbooks but have been narrowed by statute. **Caveat Emptor**, once the default rule of sale, now yields to implied conditions under Sections 14–16 of the Sale of Goods Act 1930 and the Consumer Protection Act 2019. Similarly, **Volenti Non Fit Injuria** is a complete defence only where consent is free, informed, and not against public policy — it fails against negligence by a professional, against trespass to person, and where consent is obtained by fraud.

#### Worked Application
A factory discharges untreated effluents into a river downstream. Fishermen downstream sue. The river, on its own, cannot speak — but the consistent discharge and fish death create a presumption under **Res Ipsa Loquitur**: the thing speaks for itself, shifting the evidentiary burden to the factory. If the factory argues the fishermen knew of the discharge, **Volenti Non Fit Injuria** fails because consent to economic risk does not extend to toxic contamination, and public policy under Article 21 overrides private waiver.

#### High-Yield Traps and Adjacent Topics
- **Mens Rea vs Actus Reus**: in strict-liability offences (Section 325, 326 IPC and motor-accident cases under Section 304-A), only the actus reus matters — picking Mens Rea here is a classic wrong answer.
- **Audi Alteram Partem vs Nemo Judex In Causa Sua**: the first is a hearing rule, the second a bias rule. They often appear together in administrative-law passages on natural justice.
- **Ubi Jus Ibi Remedium** (where there is a right, there is a remedy) frequently anchors Article 32 and 226 writ jurisdiction questions in CLAT.

#### Practice Prompts
1. A passenger consents to a stunt ride knowing it is risky. Is **Volenti Non Fit Injuria** a complete defence if the operator skipped a safety check? Justify with one statutory reason.
2. A statute lists "cars, motorcycles, and other vehicles." Apply **Ejusdem Generis** to decide whether "other vehicles" includes an electric scooter.
