# A JUPEB Subject Combination

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

A JUPEB Subject Combination is the fixed set of three Advanced Level subjects a candidate registers for, tied to a target university course. Each combination carries designated trade subjects — for example, **ABE** (Accounting, Business Studies, Economics) for accounting/business degrees, or **GEM** (Government, Economics, Mathematics) for economics and political science pathways. Grades A–F carry 5–0 points respectively, and the aggregate is used for Direct Entry admission via JAMB. The combination must align with the DE brochure of the target university; misalignment is the top cause of denied DE admission.

- A/L grade points: **A=5, B=4, C=3, D=2, E=1, F=0**.
- Exactly **three trade subjects** per candidate, plus General Studies where applicable.
- DE admission runs through **JAMB**, so the combination must satisfy both JUPEB and JAMB UTME rules.

#### What a Combination Means

A JUPEB combination is not a free pick. The Joint Universities Preliminary Examinations Board publishes an approved list pairing each combination with the faculties it feeds — Accounting/Business/Economics (ABE), Sciences (PCM/PBC), Humanities (GEM, GKL), and so on. The combination locks the candidate into a specific DE admission lane for the next two to three years, so it must match both the intended **course** and the **target university**.

#### Common Combinations and Their Target Courses

| Combination | Subjects | Typical DE Target Courses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ABE** | Accounting, Business Studies, Economics | Accounting, Business Admin, Banking & Finance, Economics |
| **GEM** | Government, Economics, Mathematics | Economics, Political Science, Public Administration, Sociology |
| **PCM** | Physics, Chemistry, Mathematics | Engineering, Computer Science, Physics, Mathematics |
| **PBC** | Physics, Biology, Chemistry | Medicine, Pharmacy, Nursing, Biological Sciences |
| **GKL** | Government, CRS/IRS, Literature | Law, Religious Studies, Philosophy, History |

#### Selection Rules Students Routinely Miss

- The combination is fixed at registration — switching mid-programme is rarely allowed and may invalidate results.
- Mathematics is **compulsory** for many science and quantitative courses; substituting it with a non-quantitative trade subject blocks DE eligibility.
- Some universities accept ABE for Economics while others insist on GEM — the DE brochure is the only reliable reference.
- General Studies contributes to the aggregate in some centres and not others; confirm with the study centre coordinator.
- JAMB UTME subjects must also align with the same faculty area; a JUPEB-only check is incomplete.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### How Combinations Connect to DE Eligibility

Direct Entry into Nigerian universities evaluates a candidate on the **JUPEB aggregate**, computed as the sum of the three trade-subject points plus any General Studies contribution, capped at a maximum (commonly 10 points depending on the receiving institution's scale). A candidate offering ABE with grades A (5), B (4), C (3) aggregates 12 points before scale conversion, while GEM with B (4), C (3), C (3) aggregates 10 — the first profile is materially stronger for accounting courses even if both candidates pass. Because the aggregate is the primary DE screen, the choice of combination determines the pool of competitors a candidate is ranked against.

#### Common Pitfalls and Edge Cases

- **Centre limitations:** Not every JUPEB study centre offers every combination. Registering for a non-offered combination forces costly transfers.
- **Cross-faculty ambitions:** A student aiming for both Accounting and Law cannot sit ABE and a humanities set; they must pick one lane or seek a university whose DE policy admits both.
- **O'Level confusion:** Five O'Level credits (including English and Mathematics) are a parallel gate; strong WAEC grades cannot substitute for a missing A/L trade subject.
- **JAMB UTME mismatch:** Sitting JUPEB ABE but choosing JAMB UTME subjects like Government and CRS will trigger a JAMB-side disqualification at DE screening.
- **Resit impact:** A resit in only one trade subject can drag down the aggregate; planning the initial combination to be resit-friendly is a quiet advantage.

#### Worked Selection Example

A candidate targets **Economics at the University of Lagos** via DE. UNILAG's DE brochure lists both GEM and ABE as acceptable for Economics, but GEM is the recommended lane because Mathematics carries higher weighting for quantitative screening. Choosing GEM (Government, Economics, Mathematics) secures the recommendation and aligns with JAMB UTME requirements (English plus three relevant arts/social-science subjects). Choosing ABE instead would still be accepted for Economics but is the weaker strategic pick.

#### Practice Prompts

1. List two JUPEB combinations suitable for a candidate aiming at Mechanical Engineering, and identify which trade subject is non-negotiable.
2. A candidate registered for ABE now wants to switch to GKL after one semester. Identify the likely administrative and aggregate consequences.
