# p-Block

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> One-screen pass before the bell rings: lock these six groups, six shells, six periods into memory.

The differentiating electron enters a p-orbital of the outermost shell: valence configuration **ns²np¹⁻⁶**, spanning Groups **13 to 18** and Periods **2 to 6** (with a few Period-7 heavy congeners). p-Block hands out roughly **4–5 questions (≈16–20 marks)** every NEET paper — the heaviest slice of Inorganic Chemistry.

#### Must-Know at a Glance

| Group | General config. | First element anomaly | Defining test point |
|---|---|---|---|
| 13 (IIIA) | ns²np¹ | B is a non-metal, max covalency 4 | Al amphoteric, Tl⁺ > Tl³⁺ (inert pair) |
| 14 (IVA) | ns²np² | C catenates, forms multiple bonds | SiO₂ covalent 3D, CO₂ discrete linear |
| 15 (VA) | ns²np³ | N≡N bond 941.4 kJ mol⁻¹ | P₄ tetrahedral with 60° strain |
| 16 (VIA) | ns²np⁴ | O shows –2, –1, 0, +1, +2 | H₂O liquid, H₂S gas (H-bonding) |
| 17 (VIIA) | ns²np⁵ | F strongest oxidiser, E° = +2.87 V | I–I weakest X–X bond |
| 18 (VIIIA) | ns²np⁶ | He, Ne truly inert; Xe forms XeF₂, XeF₄, XeF₆ | XeF₆ hybridisation **sp³d³**, not sp³d |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"13–18, p¹–p⁶, Boil Cat Nit Oxy Hal Nob"** — read as the six groups in order. Count the p-electrons from 1 (B) to 6 (Ne core) and you have the whole block sketched.

The single fact that decides a NEET score here: **Period-2 elements (B, C, N, O, F) behave anomalously** because they have no d-orbitals, small size, and high charge density. Every "why-doesn't-this-match-the-trend" question traces back to this.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Group-by-Group Mechanics

**Group 13.** Boron is the only non-metal; aluminium is amphoteric — `2Al + 2NaOH + 2H₂O → 2NaAlO₂ + 3H₂↑`. The **inert pair effect** makes the +1 oxidation state increasingly stable down the group: Tl⁺ is more stable than Tl³⁺, so TlI exists but TlI₃ is really Tl⁺[Tl³⁺I₄]⁻. Borax hydrolyses to boric acid, a **monobasic Lewis acid**: `H₃BO₃ + 2H₂O ⇌ [B(OH)₄]⁻ + H₃O⁺`, pKa = 9.25.

**Group 14.** Carbon's catenation is unmatched — diamond, graphite, fullerene, graphene are allotropes of the same element. CO₂ is linear O=C=O (discrete molecules); SiO₂ is a 3D covalent network (cristobalite/quartz) — that single structural difference explains why CO₂ sublimes at 195 K while SiO₂ melts at 1986 K. Pb²⁺ and Sn²⁺ dominate over Pb⁴⁺/Sn⁴⁺ down the group.

**Group 15.** N₂'s triple bond energy is **941.4 kJ mol⁻¹** — the highest of any diatomic — which is why N₂ is so unreactive at room temperature. White phosphorus P₄ has tetrahedral geometry with 60° bond angles (severe strain), making it the most reactive and toxic allotrope; red phosphorus is polymeric and far safer. HNO₃ behaves primarily as an **oxidising agent**, not an acid, with metals: dilute gives NO, concentrated gives NO₂ — never H₂.

**Group 16.** Oxygen shows O₂ and O₃ (ozone bent, ~117°, V-shaped sp²). Sulphur has rhombic α-S₈ (stable below 369 K) and monoclinic β-S₈ (369–392 K). The contact process for H₂SO₄: `2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃` (ΔH = –196.6 kJ mol⁻¹) over **V₂O₅ at 720 K**, then SO₃ dissolved in H₂SO₄ to give oleum (H₂S₂O₇), finally diluted with water.

**Group 17.** Fluorine is the strongest oxidising agent (E° = +2.87 V). HF etches glass: `SiO₂ + 4HF → SiF₄ + 2H₂O`. **Interhalogens** XY_n follow the rule n = 1, 3, 5, 7 only: ClF₃ is T-shaped (≈87.5°), BrF₅ square pyramidal, IF₇ pentagonal bipyramidal. Acidity of chlorine oxoacids increases with Cl oxidation state: **HClO < HClO₂ < HClO₃ < HClO₄**.

**Group 18.** All have complete octets (ns²np⁶). Only Xe and Kr form true compounds. XeF₂ is linear (sp³d, Xe in +2), XeF₄ square planar (sp³d², Xe in +4), XeF₆ distorted octahedral (sp³d³, Xe in +6) — the lone pair in XeF₆ is the trap.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix

| Pair | Easily confused | How to tell them apart |
|---|---|---|
| H₃PO₄ vs H₃PO₃ vs H₃PO₂ | All called "phosphoric acids" | H₃PO₄ tribasic (3 O–H); H₃PO₃ dibasic (2 O–H + 1 P–H); H₃PO₂ monobasic (1 O–H + 2 P–H) |
| HNO₃ dilute vs concentrated | Both "nitric acid" | Dilute → NO; concentrated → NO₂; never H₂ |
| Al(OH)₃ vs B(OH)₃ | Both group-13 hydroxides | Al(OH)₃ amphoteric, dissolves in NaOH; B(OH)₃ is monobasic Lewis acid |
| XeF₆ hybridisation | Often written sp³d | Correct: **sp³d³**, distorted octahedral (one lone pair) |
| Cl–Cl vs Br–Br vs I–I bond | Strength ordering | I–I is the **weakest** (poor p-overlap), then Br–Br, then Cl–Cl strongest |
| O₃ vs SO₂ | Both bent molecules | O₃ is sp² (≈117°); SO₂ is sp² with one lone pair (≈119°) — different origin of the bend |

#### Process Stages to Memorise

1. **Ostwald process** for HNO₃: NH₃ oxidation over Pt-Rh at 500 K → NO → NO₂ → HNO₃ (68% azeotrope, bp 356 K).
2. **Contact process** for H₂SO₄: S → SO₂ → SO₃ (V₂O₅, 720 K) → H₂S₂O₇ → H₂SO₄.
3. **Chlor-alkali**: electrolysis of brine gives Cl₂, H₂, NaOH simultaneously.
4. **Interhalogen shape ladder**: XX' linear → XX'₃ T-shape → XX'₅ square pyramidal → XX'₇ pentagonal bipyramidal.

#### Worked Solution — Structure Identification

Question: The molecule BrF₅ is best described as:

(A) trigonal bipyramidal with no lone pair  
(B) square pyramidal with one lone pair on Br  
(C) octahedral with one lone pair  
(D) pentagonal bipyramidal with one lone pair

Solution: Count valence electrons on Br: 7 + 5(7) = 42, leaving 5 bond pairs and 1 lone pair on Br. AX₅E₁ in VSEPR gives a **square pyramidal** geometry (base: four F atoms in a square, one F at the apex, lone pair trans to the apex F). Hybridisation is **sp³d²**.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students who memorise "interhalogens = sp³d hybridisation" mark (A) by reflex. Interhalogens need sp³d only up to XY₃; from XY₅ onwards, the hybridisation is sp³d², and for XY₇ it is sp³d³. Match the VSEPR class to the hybridisation every single time.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

#### Edge Cases and Boundary Conditions

The **anomalous behaviour of Period-2** members is not a single anomaly — it stacks: (i) no available d-orbitals, so max covalency caps at 4 for B and C (vs. higher for Si, P, S); (ii) small atomic radii, so pπ–pπ overlap is strong (C=C, C≡C, N=N, O=O, N≡N all multiple-bonded); (iii) high electronegativity, so N and O form hydrogen bonds while their heavier congeners do not; (iv) absence of vacant d-orbitals rules out dπ–pπ back-bonding in B–N compounds, while BX₃ (X = Cl, Br) does show limited pπ–pπ.

The **diagonal relationship** is sharper for the first two periods: Li–Mg (carbonates, hydroxides thermally stable, nitrides), Be–Al (amphoteric oxides/hydroxides, both form complexes), and B–Si (both acidic oxides, both form hydrides that ignite spontaneously, B₂H₆ and SiH₄).

#### Common Mistakes That Drop Marks

| # | Wrong belief | Correct statement |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | H₃PO₃ is tribasic | It is dibasic — one P–H bond means only two replaceable H⁺ (basicity = 2) |
| 2 | All noble gases are completely inert | Xe forms XeF₂, XeF₄, XeF₆, XeO₃, XeOF₄ — a direct NCERT assertion |
| 3 | Dilute HNO₃ produces H₂ with metals | It produces NO (or NH₄⁺ with very active metals); H₂ is never liberated |
| 4 | HF is a strong acid like HCl | HF is weak (H–F bond very strong, F⁻ highly solvated); but it etches glass — unique |
| 5 | XeF₆ is octahedral | It is **distorted octahedral** because of the stereochemically active lone pair |
| 6 | Bond order in PO₄³⁻ is exactly 1.25, so each P–O is the same | Resonance delocalises; measured P–O distance ≈ 154 pm, intermediate between P–O (160 pm) and P=O (150 pm) |
| 7 | Heavier halogens are stronger oxidising agents | Oxidising power falls: F₂ > Cl₂ > Br₂ > I₂ (F–F is weaker than expected due to repulsion but overall still strongest oxidiser) |

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. Predict the products, balance, and identify the oxidised/reduced species when **hot concentrated H₂SO₄** reacts with **copper turnings**. (Hint: SO₂, CuSO₄, H₂O; S in +6 → +4, Cu 0 → +2.)
2. Justify why **NH₃ is a base but NF₃ is not**, even though both have lone pairs on N. (Hint: F withdraws electron density via –I effect, plus N's lone pair in NF₃ is in sp³ hybrid pointing away from the F atoms; in NH₃ the lone pair sits in a less electronegative environment and is available for donation.)
3. Order the following by bond angle and justify: **H₂O, OF₂, SCl₂**. (Hint: central atom electronegativity and lone-pair–lone-pair repulsion; OF₂ < H₂O because F pulls electron density away from O, reducing LP–LP repulsion; SCl₂ ~ H₂O but S is larger so angle shrinks slightly.)

#### How This Topic Is Tested

NEET typically asks 3–5 questions worth 12–20 marks: (i) **structure identification** of XeF₄, P₄O₁₀, SF₄, ClF₃, BrF₅; (ii) **acid strength reasoning** across oxoacids of the same halogen or same-period oxoacids (HClO₄ > HBrO₄ > HIO₄); (iii) **product prediction** in reactions of concentrated vs dilute HNO₃, and of hot vs cold concentrated H₂SO₄; (iv) **explanation** of inert pair effect, anomalous behaviour of Period 2, and absence of allotropy in F; (v) **numerical** Ka values of H₃PO₄ or pKa of oxoacids. Time per question averages 90 seconds; skip the heavy calculation, hit the reasoning.
