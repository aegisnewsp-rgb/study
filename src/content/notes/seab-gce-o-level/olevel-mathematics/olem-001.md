# Numbers and Their Operations — O-Level Mathematics (4052) Notes

Numbers and Their Operations is the arithmetic foundation of the O-Level Mathematics 4052 syllabus. The sub-strand covers integers, rational and real numbers, indices, standard form, surds, bounds, estimation, and the four operations in applied contexts. While Algebra is the heaviest sub-strand by marks on the paper, Numbers and Their Operations underpins every other topic — a Sec 4 student cannot succeed on the algebraic items without arithmetic fluency, and cannot succeed on the applied Paper-2 items without confidence with surds, standard form and bounds.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### The seven facts every Sec 4 student needs

1. **Integer**: a whole number, positive or negative (or zero).
2. **Rational number**: a number that can be written as a fraction of two integers. Includes integers, terminating decimals and recurring decimals.
3. **Real number**: any number on the number line. Includes rationals and irrationals (like √2).
4. **Indices**: a^k means a multiplied by itself k times. a^0 = 1, a^(−n) = 1 / a^n, a^(1/n) = nth root of a, a^(m/n) = (a^m)^(1/n).
5. **Standard form**: a number written as a × 10^n where 1 ≤ a < 10 and n is an integer.
6. **Surd**: an irrational root that cannot be simplified to a rational number, e.g. √2, √3, √5.
7. **Bounds**: the maximum and minimum values a rounded quantity can take.

#### Key notations

- 5.7 × 10⁴ = 57 000
- 3.6 × 10⁻³ = 0.0036
- √12 = 2√3 (simplified surd)
- 1 / (3 − √2) = (3 + √2) / [(3 − √2)(3 + √2)] = (3 + √2) / (9 − 2) = (3 + √2) / 7 (rationalised denominator)

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Integers, rationals and reals

**Integers** are the whole numbers {…, −3, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3, …}. The four operations work as expected, but subtraction can produce a smaller number (e.g. 3 − 7 = −4) and multiplication of two negatives produces a positive.

**Rational numbers** are numbers that can be written as a fraction p/q where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0. All integers, terminating decimals and recurring decimals are rational. The number 0.333… = 1/3 is rational even though it is non-terminating.

**Irrational numbers** are real numbers that cannot be written as a fraction of two integers. √2, √3, π and e are irrational. The decimal expansion of an irrational number is non-terminating and non-recurring.

#### Indices (laws)

- a^m × a^n = a^(m+n)
- a^m / a^n = a^(m−n)
- (a^m)^n = a^(mn)
- a^0 = 1 (provided a ≠ 0)
- a^(−n) = 1 / a^n
- a^(1/n) = nth root of a
- a^(m/n) = (a^m)^(1/n) = (a^(1/n))^m

Example: simplify (2x³)² × (x⁻²) / (4x⁴). (2x³)² = 4x⁶. × x⁻² = 4x⁴. ÷ (4x⁴) = 1.

#### Standard form

A number is in standard form when it is written as a × 10^n where 1 ≤ a < 10 and n is an integer.

- 5 700 = 5.7 × 10³
- 0.0036 = 3.6 × 10⁻³
- 6 020 000 = 6.02 × 10⁶

Standard form makes it easy to compare the sizes of very large or very small numbers and to do arithmetic with them.

Example: (3 × 10⁵) × (4 × 10⁴) = 12 × 10⁹ = 1.2 × 10¹⁰. (Adjust the coefficient to be in [1, 10).)

Example: (8 × 10⁷) / (2 × 10³) = 4 × 10⁴.

#### Surds

A surd is an irrational root that cannot be simplified to a rational number. √2, √3, √5, √7 are typical surds. √8 = √(4 × 2) = 2√2 is a simplified surd.

**Laws of surds**:

- √(ab) = √a × √b
- √(a/b) = √a / √b
- (√a)² = a

**Rationalising the denominator**: to remove surds from the denominator, multiply numerator and denominator by the conjugate of the denominator.

- 1 / (3 + √2): multiply by (3 − √2) / (3 − √2) to get (3 − √2) / (9 − 2) = (3 − √2) / 7.
- 1 / (√a + √b): multiply by (√a − √b) / (√a − √b) to get (√a − √b) / (a − b).

**Adding and subtracting surds**: like surds can be added or subtracted. 3√2 + 5√2 = 8√2. Unlike surds cannot be combined: √2 + √3 stays as √2 + √3.

**Multiplying and dividing surds**: use the laws above. (√2)(√3) = √6. (√12)(√3) = √36 = 6.

#### Bounds

When a quantity is rounded, the rounding introduces an error. The **bounds** of a rounded quantity are the smallest and largest values that would round to the given value.

Example: 5.7 cm is rounded to one decimal place. The actual length could be anywhere from 5.65 cm to 5.75 cm (exclusive of 5.75, since 5.75 would round to 5.8).

Bounds are used to find the maximum or minimum value of a calculation involving rounded quantities. Example: if a = 5.7 (1 d.p.) and b = 3.2 (1 d.p.), then a × b can range from 5.65 × 3.15 = 17.7975 to 5.75 × 3.25 = 18.6875. The maximum is 18.6875; the minimum is 17.7975. The calculation itself does not change — only the bounds on the inputs matter.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where Numbers and Their Operations appears across the papers

- **O-Level Mathematics 4052 Paper 1**: short structured items on indices, standard form, surds, and bounds.
- **O-Level Mathematics 4052 Paper 2**: long structured items that combine surds or standard form with algebra or geometry. Surd simplification is a common step in a 5-mark item.
- **Across both papers**: arithmetic accuracy under time pressure is the single biggest differentiator between a C-grade and a B-grade answer.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Negative indices give negative numbers." a^(−n) = 1 / a^n, not −a^n. The exponent is the reciprocal, not the negation.
- "Standard form is just scientific notation." Standard form has a coefficient a in [1, 10); some definitions of scientific notation allow a in [1, 10) too, but the O-Level convention is the strict [1, 10) range.
- "Surds can be combined like variables." Like surds (same irrational part) can be combined; unlike surds cannot.
- "Rationalising means making the numerator rational." Rationalising means making the denominator rational; the denominator becomes rational after multiplying by the conjugate.
- "Bounds are the same as rounded values." The bounds are the smallest and largest actual values that would round to the given rounded value; the rounded value is the midpoint (approximately).

#### Exam technique

- For index questions, state the law you are applying before simplifying. The law statement is the method mark.
- For standard-form questions, write the answer in standard form even if the calculator gives a non-standard form. The marker awards the standard-form mark separately.
- For surd questions, simplify the surd before the next step. A surd that can be simplified to an integer (e.g. √9 = 3) should be simplified.
- For bounds questions, write the bounds on each input explicitly. The bounds are the method mark.
