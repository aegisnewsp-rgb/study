# Carbohydrates: Classification, Stereochemistry, and Reactions

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> **Memory Hook:** Every student should pin two words to memory — **"polyhydroxy-carbonyl"** — because that single phrase is the textbook fingerprint of a carbohydrate.

A carbohydrate is a **polyhydroxy aldehyde or polyhydroxy ketone**, or anything that hydrolyses to one. Empirical shorthand writes them as Cₙ(H₂O)ₙ, hence the name "hydrate of carbon." The benchmark hexose is glucose, C₆H₁₂O₆, with molar mass **180.16 g mol⁻¹**.

| Basis | Sub-class | Defining feature | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hydrolysis | Monosaccharide | Single sugar unit | Glucose, fructose |
| Hydrolysis | Disaccharide | Two units joined by glycosidic bond | Sucrose, maltose, lactose |
| Hydrolysis | Polysaccharide | Many units, may be branched | Starch, glycogen, cellulose |
| Carbonyl | Aldose | –CHO at C1 | Glucose |
| Carbonyl | Ketose | C=O at C2 | Fructose |

**Quick recall:** α-anomer has the anomeric –OH **below** the ring plane; β-anomer has it **above** (Haworth convention). Mutarotation drives both forms to the same equilibrium specific rotation.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Classification by hydrolysis products

The number of monosaccharide units released on acid hydrolysis partitions carbohydrates cleanly:

- **Monosaccharides** — glucose (aldohexose), fructose (ketohexose), galactose, ribose, 2-deoxyribose. Cannot be hydrolysed further.
- **Disaccharides** — sucrose (glucose + fructose), maltose (glucose + glucose, α-1,4), lactose (galactose + glucose, β-1,4), cellobiose (glucose + glucose, β-1,4).
- **Polysaccharides** — amylose (α-1,4 linear), amylopectin (α-1,4 with α-1,6 branches every 24–30 units), glycogen (α-1,4 with α-1,6 branches every 8–12 units), cellulose (β-1,4 linear).

Sucrose hydrolysis: C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁ + H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ (glucose) + C₆H₁₂O₆ (fructose), ΔH ≈ −19.5 kJ mol⁻¹.

#### Aldose vs ketose — distinguishing the carbonyl

Aldoses carry –CHO at C1; ketoses carry C=O at C2. A Fischer projection of D-glucose reads –OH right at C2, C4, C5 and left at C3. The "D" prefix means the highest-numbered chiral centre (C5 in hexoses) has its –OH on the right.

#### Cyclisation and the anomeric carbon

In aqueous solution, an aldose's C5 –OH attacks the C1 aldehyde to form a six-membered **pyranose** ring. C1 becomes a new stereocentre called the **anomeric carbon**, giving α and β anomers. Ketoses form five-membered **furanose** rings; for fructose the anomeric carbon is **C2**, not C1 — a common student slip.

#### Reducing vs non-reducing sugars

A **reducing sugar** has a free anomeric carbon able to open into an aldehyde. Sucrose is **non-reducing** because the C1 of glucose and C2 of fructose are both locked in the glycosidic bond. Fehling's, Benedict's and Tollens' reagents all detect the open-chain aldehyde.

#### Comparison Table

| Pair | Relationship | Difference | Test that exposes it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Glucose vs Galactose | C4 epimers | –OH at C4 | Different [α]ᴅ, separate chromatographic Rƒ |
| α-D-glucose vs β-D-glucose | Anomers | –OH at C1 (anomeric) | Mutarotation to +52.7° |
| Maltose vs Cellobiose | α- vs β-1,4 linkage | Configuration at glycosidic C | Hydrolysis products same, but enzymatic specificity differs |
| Glucose vs Fructose | Aldohexose vs ketohexose | Carbonyl position | Seliwanoff's test (ketose → cherry red) |
| Sucrose vs Lactose | Reducing status | Free anomeric carbon | Fehling's: lactose brick-red ppt, sucrose no ppt |

#### Stereochemistry of glucose

Glucose has chiral centres at C2, C3, C4 and C5, so 2⁴ = **16 stereoisomers** exist, eight D- and eight L-. Specific rotation is measured by

[α] = α_obs / (l · c)

where α_obs is observed rotation in degrees, l is path length in **dm**, and c is concentration in **g mL⁻¹**. Units simplify to **deg·mL·g⁻¹·dm⁻¹**.

#### Worked problem

**Question (10 marks, KU Organic Chemistry of Biomolecules):** A white crystalline solid (A) has molecular formula C₆H₁₂O₆. It gives a positive Fehling's test, a positive osazone test forming needle-shaped crystals, and mutarotates in aqueous solution to [α]D = +52.7°. Hydrolysis of compound (B), formula C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁, gives (A) plus a ketohexose (C). (B) gives no Fehling's test. Identify A, B, C and draw the Haworth projection of α-A.

#### Resolution:
1. Formula C₆H₁₂O₆ → hexose.
2. Positive Fehling's → free anomeric carbon → **aldohexose**, so (A) is **D-glucose**.
3. Osazone crystals are the classic D-glucose phenylhydrazone (m.p. 205 °C).
4. Mutarotation to +52.7° confirms interconvertible α and β anomers of glucose.
5. (B) is a non-reducing disaccharide that hydrolyses to glucose + a ketohexose → **sucrose**.
6. (C), the ketohexose partner, is **D-fructose**.
7. α-D-glucopyranose Haworth: ring oxygen at upper right, C1 –OH below the plane (axial, α), C2 –OH below, C3 –OH above, C4 –OH below, C5 –CH₂OH above.

> **Examiner Trap:** Students routinely draw the anomeric –OH above the plane and label it α. By Haworth convention, α = **trans** to the CH₂OH group at C5, which sits below the reference carbon — for D-sugars that means **down** for α.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

#### Edge cases that derail marks

Carbohydrates do not always obey the Cₙ(H₂O)ₙ formula. **2-Deoxyribose** is C₅H₁₀O₄, missing one oxygen; **glucosamine** carries an –NH₂ replacing an –OH; **ascorbic acid (vitamin C)** is technically a carbohydrate-derived lactone. Examiners exploit these to test whether you really understood the definition.

| Sugar-like molecule | Formula | Why it is (or is not) a true carbohydrate |
|---|---|---|
| 2-Deoxyribose | C₅H₁₀O₄ | Still a polyhydroxy aldehyde — qualifies |
| Sucrose | C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁ | Not C₁₂(H₂O)₁₂ — one H₂O lost in glycosidic bond |
| Acetic acid | C₂H₄O₂ | Not polyhydroxy — excluded |

#### Reactions worth memorising by mechanism

- **Oxidation** — bromine water (mild) oxidises the aldehyde of an aldose to the aldonic acid; nitric acid goes further to the aldaric acid. Tollens' gives the silver mirror.
- **Reduction** — NaBH₄ reduces the carbonyl to give an **alditol** (glucose → sorbitol).
- **Osazone formation** — three equivalents of phenylhydrazine react at C1 and C2; aldohexoses and ketohexoses sharing C1–C2 stereochemistry give identical osazones, so glucose, fructose and mannose yield the same crystals.
- **Kiliani-Fischer synthesis** — adds HCN across the aldehyde, then reduces and hydrolyses, **lengthening** an aldose by one carbon (ascending the series).
- **Ruff degradation** — calcium-fructosate + H₂O₂/Fe³⁺ **shortens** an aldose by one carbon.

#### Polysaccharide architecture and function

Starch is a mixture: amylose forms a helical α-1,4 chain that gives the deep blue colour with I₂/KI; amylopectin's α-1,6 branches give a lighter reddish-brown. Glycogen is more heavily branched and gives a brown-red colour. Cellulose's β-1,4 linkage produces straight chains that hydrogen-bond into fibres — the reason cows and termites, not humans, digest it.

#### Advanced practice prompts

1. A student measures [α]ᵀ for a freshly dissolved D-glucose sample at +112°, and the value falls over hours to +52.7°. Use the equation K = [α-form]/[β-form] (≈ 36/64 at 25 °C) to confirm the equilibrium ratio and explain why mutarotation halts at +52.7° rather than 0°.
2. Fructose gives a positive Seliwanoff's test but a negative Benedict's test when applied without prior hydrolysis in some procedural notes. Reconcile the apparent contradiction by tracing which functional group is being detected, and predict the outcome if sucrose is treated identically.

> **Practical sanity check:** in the KU organic lab, always report observed rotation with temperature and concentration specified — a bare number invites a mark deduction.
