# Composition and Expression (Insha') — Kuwait Secondary Certificate Arabic Notes

Composition and Expression (Insha' / الإنشاء) is the sixth terminal-level domain on the Kuwait MOE Arabic curriculum. The curriculum covers argumentative, descriptive, narrative and expository essays; letter writing (formal and informal); the introduction-body-conclusion structure; cohesion devices; and stylistic register.

> Verify the live Arabic terminal exam pattern and the 2026/2027 tracks regulation on https://www.moe.edu.kw/ before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Argumentative essay:** states a position and supports it with reasons, evidence, and rebuttal of counter-arguments.
- **Descriptive essay:** uses sensory detail to describe a person, place, event or object.
- **Narrative essay:** tells a story with a clear arc (introduction, rising action, climax, falling action, conclusion).
- **Expository essay:** explains a concept or process in clear, structured terms.
- **Letter writing:** formal letters (to officials, institutions) and informal letters (to family, friends). Each has its own format.
- **Cohesion devices:** conjunctions (و، أو، ثم، ف، لكن، لأن، إذا، بينما), reference words (هذا، تلك، هؤلاء), and logical connectives (بناءً على، نتيجة لـ، فضلاً عن ذلك).
- **Register:** formal Arabic uses MSA, classical vocabulary, third-person voice, long sentences. Informal Arabic uses colloquialisms, second person, short sentences.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing argumentative with expository — argumentative takes a position; expository explains.
- Using informal register in a formal essay or letter.
- Missing the structure — the introduction-body-conclusion is mandatory for a full mark.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Argumentative essay structure

A standard Arabic argumentative essay has:

1. **مقدمة (introduction):** a hook (question, statistic, anecdote, quote), then the thesis statement.
2. **عرض (body):** at least two reasons supporting the thesis, each in its own paragraph, with evidence (a statistic, an example, an expert opinion, a Quranic verse or hadith).
3. **تفنيد (rebuttal):** a paragraph stating the opposing view fairly, then refuting it.
4. **خاتمة (conclusion):** a restatement of the thesis in different words, a summary of the strongest reason, and a forward-looking or call-to-action ending.

Use cohesive devices, but do not overuse. One per paragraph is enough. Vary sentence structure: short sentences for emphasis, longer ones for nuance.

#### Descriptive essay structure

A descriptive essay organises details spatially or thematically:

- **Spatial:** from top to bottom, left to right, near to far, or inside to outside.
- **Thematic:** group details around themes (appearance, behaviour, atmosphere).

Sensory details: sight, sound, smell, taste, touch. Use vivid verbs and adjectives but avoid ornamentation that does not serve the description.

#### Narrative essay structure

A narrative essay tells a story with a clear arc:

1. **Setting:** time, place, context.
2. **Characters:** who is involved.
3. **Conflict:** the issue that drives the story.
4. **Climax:** the turning point.
5. **Resolution:** how the conflict is resolved.
6. **Reflection:** what the narrator learns.

Use past tense, vivid verbs, dialogue (where appropriate), and a clear sequence marker. Avoid flashbacks unless they serve the narrative.

#### Expository essay structure

An expository essay explains a concept or process:

1. **Definition:** what is the concept.
2. **Categories or types:** how it is divided.
3. **Causes / effects / examples:** how it works in practice.
4. **Significance:** why it matters.

Use neutral language, third-person voice, and definitions before examples. Avoid rhetorical questions and personal opinions in a formal expository essay.

#### Letter writing

A formal letter has:

- **Sender address:** top right.
- **Date:** below the sender address.
- **Recipient address:** top left.
- **Salutation:** "السيد/السيدة" + name, or "إلى من يهمه الأمر" (To whom it may concern).
- **Body:** introduction (purpose), main content, conclusion.
- **Sign-off:** "وتفضلوا بقبول فائق الاحترام والتقدير" (Please accept my highest regards).
- **Signature:** name and title.

An informal letter is shorter, uses second person, may use colloquialisms, and has a less rigid format.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where Composition and Expression appears across the terminal Arabic exam

- **One essay item:** the candidate chooses one of several prompts (argumentative, descriptive, narrative, or expository).
- **Optional letter item:** some terminal papers include a formal letter option.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Long essays score higher." No — quality matters more than length. A 500-word essay with clear structure scores higher than a 1000-word essay with no structure.
- "Use classical Arabic everywhere." Modern Standard Arabic is the standard for formal writing; classical Arabic is appropriate for religious and literary contexts.
- "Skip the conclusion." The conclusion is the method mark — without it, the essay loses marks even if the body is strong.

#### Exam technique

- For essay items, plan the structure (intro, body, rebuttal, conclusion) before writing — the plan is the method mark.
- For letter items, follow the formal format strictly — the format is the method mark.
- For cohesive devices, use them sparingly — one per paragraph is enough.
