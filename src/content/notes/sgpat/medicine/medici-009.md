# Topic 9

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Body Surface Area (BSA)** standardizes drug dosing across body sizes. The **Mosteller formula** is the bedside formula of choice:

> **BSA (m²) = √[(Height_cm × Weight_kg) / 3600]**

For burns, the **Rule of Nines** gives rapid adult TBSA percentages (head 9%, each arm 9%, each leg 18%, anterior trunk 18%, posterior trunk 18%, perineum 1%). For **fluid resuscitation**, the **Parkland formula** delivers **4 mL × kg × %TBSA** of Lactated Ringer over 24 hours — half in the first 8 hours.

**Creatinine clearance (Cockcroft-Gault)** adjusts renally-cleared drug doses:

> **CrCl = [(140 − Age) × Weight_kg] / (72 × SCr)**, multiplied by **0.85 in females**.

**High-yield pointers:** memorize Mosteller (not DuBois), use Lund-Browder (not Rule of Nines) in children, give half the Parkland volume in the first 8 hours, and always apply the 0.85 female factor.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

### Body Surface Area Formulas

BSA standardizes doses for chemotherapy, biologics, and pediatric drugs where fixed adult doses would be unsafe. Three formulas coexist, but the **Saudi GP Board** expects the Mosteller formula for rapid calculation MCQs because it requires only height, weight, and a square root.

| Formula | Equation | When Used |
|---|---|---|
| **Mosteller** | √[(H × W) / 3600] | Bedside, exam default |
| **DuBois** | 0.007184 × H^0.725 × W^0.425 | Historical/research |
| **Haycock** | 0.024265 × H^0.3964 × W^0.5378 | Pediatric reference |

> Variables: H = height in cm, W = weight in kg; result in m². Drug dose = mg/m² × BSA.

### Burn Assessment and Resuscitation

The **Rule of Nines** partitions the adult body into 9% regions for rapid TBSA estimation. In children, proportions differ (head ≈ 18% at birth), so the **Lund-Browder chart** is mandatory. The **Parkland formula** then drives crystalloid resuscitation:

> **Volume (mL) = 4 × Weight_kg × %TBSA** of Lactated Ringer over 24 hours; **half in first 8 h**, remainder over next 16 h, measured from the burn time.

### Renal-Based Dose Adjustment

When eGFR is unreliable (extremes of body size, pregnancy, amputees), **Cockcroft-Gault** estimates CrCl from age, weight, and serum creatinine. **Always multiply by 0.85 in females**, or renally cleared drugs are underdosed.

**Typical exam pattern:** a 60 kg patient with 30% burns → Parkland = 7,200 mL/24 h, with 3,600 mL in the first 8 hours.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Worked Numeric Example

A 70 kg, 170 cm adult man with 40% TBSA burns needs resuscitation.

- **BSA (Mosteller):** √[(170 × 70) / 3600] = √3.306 ≈ **1.82 m²**
- **Parkland volume:** 4 × 70 × 40 = **11,200 mL over 24 h**
- **First 8 hours:** 5,600 mL Lactated Ringer
- **Next 16 hours:** 5,600 mL
- **CrCl (SCr 1.2 mg/dL, age 50):** [(140 − 50) × 70] / (72 × 1.2) = **72.9 mL/min** → no dose adjustment needed.

#### Common Pitfalls

- Swapping **height and weight** in Mosteller produces an incorrect BSA — verify H is in cm and W in kg.
- **Rule of Nines in children** overestimates TBSA because the head is proportionally larger; use Lund-Browder until age 14.
- Giving the **entire Parkland volume in the first 8 hours** causes pulmonary edema — the formula splits the total.
- Forgetting the **0.85 female factor** in Cockcroft-Gault underdoses drugs like enoxaparin and aminoglycosides.
- **Obese patients:** use adjusted body weight for chemotherapy dosing, or actual body weight for Parkland — never ideal body weight alone.
- Confusing **mg/m²** (dose intensity) with **m²** (BSA) when computing total dose.

#### Adjacent Topics and Strategy

Topic 9 connects to **oncology dosing** (mg/m²), **pediatric prescribing** (mg/kg), and **renal pharmacology** (dose-interval extension). Saudi GP Board MCQs usually present a short vignette with two numeric inputs and four formulas to choose between — recognizing *which* formula fits the stem (burn → Parkland; renal → Cockcroft-Gault) is half the answer.

**Practice prompts:**

1. A 25 kg child with 20% burns — compute the 8-hour Parkland volume using the **pediatric** 2 mL × kg × %TBSA variant often preferred under age 30 kg.
2. A 65-year-old woman, 60 kg, SCr 1.4 mg/dL — calculate CrCl and decide whether to dose-adjust amikacin.
