# Probability

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

Probability quantifies the likelihood of occurrences within a well-defined sample space $S$. In CAT Quantitative Aptitude, questions evaluate combinatorial counting, conditional dependence, and expected payoff modeling.

Axiomatic Foundations:
- Probability of an event $E$: $P(E) = \frac{n(E)}{n(S)}$, where $0 \le P(E) \le 1$.
- Complement Rule: $P(E') = 1 - P(E)$.
- Addition Rule for arbitrary events $A$ and $B$:
$$P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A \cap B)$$
If $A$ and $B$ are mutually exclusive ($A \cap B = \emptyset$), then $P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B)$.

Conditional and Independent Events:
- Conditional Probability of $A$ given event $B$ has occurred ($P(B) > 0$):
$$P(A|B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)}$$
- Multiplication Rule: $P(A \cap B) = P(B) \cdot P(A|B)$.
- Independent Events: Occurrence of $B$ conveys zero information about $A$:
$$P(A|B) = P(A) \iff P(A \cap B) = P(A) \cdot P(B)$$

Complementary Shortcut for "At Least One":
$$P(\text{at least one successful trial}) = 1 - P(\text{zero successes})$$

⚡ **CAT Exam Strategy:** Beware of sampling context. Drawing items *with replacement* preserves independence across draws ($P = \text{constant}$). Drawing items *without replacement* creates sequential conditional dependence where the sample space denominator decrements at each draw.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

Standard preparation for CAT focuses on multi-stage decision trees, the Theorem of Total Probability, and Bayes' Theorem.

#### 1. Comparison of Core Probability Frameworks

| Framework | Governing Formula | Key Operational Identifier | Typical CAT Application |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Classical Combinatorial** | $P(E) = \frac{\binom{m}{r}\binom{n}{k}}{\binom{m+n}{r+k}}$ | Fixed pool of discrete objects without replacement | Card selections, committee formations |
| **Conditional Probability** | $P(A\|B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)}$ | Restricted sub-sample space given prior outcome | Defective item given factory origin |
| **Total Probability** | $P(B) = \sum_{i=1}^k P(A_i)P(B\|A_i)$ | Partition of sample space into mutually exclusive $A_i$ | Multi-machine output defect rates |
| **Bayes\' Theorem** | $P(A_k\|B) = \frac{P(A_k)P(B\|A_k)}{\sum_i P(A_i)P(B\|A_i)}$ | Reverse conditional inference from observed effect to cause | Medical diagnostic test accuracy |
| **Derangements** | $D_n = n!\sum_{k=0}^n \frac{(-1)^k}{k!}$ | Permutations where no element occupies its original position | Envelopes, secret gifts, hat-check problems |

#### 2. Derangements Formulation

When $n$ items are matched to $n$ destinations, the number of ways where zero items match their correct position is:
$$D_n = n!\left(1 - \frac{1}{1!} + \frac{1}{2!} - \frac{1}{3!} + \dots + \frac{(-1)^n}{n!}\right)$$
Key values for rapid calculation: $D_1 = 0, D_2 = 1, D_3 = 2, D_4 = 9, D_5 = 44, D_6 = 265$.
The probability that no item reaches its correct spot approaches $\frac{1}{e} \approx 0.3679$ as $n \to \infty$.

#### 3. Step-by-Step Worked Example: Bayes' Inverse Probability

**Problem:** Factory plant A manufactures $60\%$ of total components, and plant B manufactures $40\%$. The defect rate is $2\%$ for plant A and $5\%$ for plant B. A randomly inspected component turns out to be defective. Find the probability that it originated from plant B.

**Solution Steps:**
1. Define events:
   - $A_1$: Component manufactured by plant A $\implies P(A_1) = 0.60$.
   - $A_2$: Component manufactured by plant B $\implies P(A_2) = 0.40$.
   - $D$: Component is defective.
2. Given conditional defect rates:
   - $P(D|A_1) = 0.02$.
   - $P(D|A_2) = 0.05$.
3. Compute total probability of selecting a defective component:
$$P(D) = P(A_1)P(D|A_1) + P(A_2)P(D|A_2)$$
$$P(D) = (0.60)(0.02) + (0.40)(0.05) = 0.012 + 0.020 = 0.032$$
4. Apply Bayes' theorem to find $P(A_2|D)$:
$$P(A_2|D) = \frac{P(A_2)P(D|A_2)}{P(D)} = \frac{0.020}{0.032} = \frac{20}{32} = \frac{5}{8} = 0.625$$
5. There is a $62.5\%$ probability that the defective unit came from plant B.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

Extended preparation targets geometric probability distributions, expected value optimization, and binomial trial sequences.

#### 1. Discrete Distributions and Mathematical Expectation

For a discrete random variable $X$ taking values $x_i$ with probabilities $P(x_i)$:
- Expected Value (Mean):
$$E[X] = \mu = \sum_{i=1}^n x_i P(x_i)$$
- Variance:
$$\text{Var}(X) = \sigma^2 = E[X^2] - (E[X])^2 = \sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2 P(x_i) - \mu^2$$

| Distribution | Probability Mass Function | Mean ($E[X]$) | Variance ($\text{Var}(X)$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Binomial ($n, p$)** | $P(X = k) = \binom{n}{k} p^k (1-p)^{n-k}$ | $n p$ | $n p (1-p)$ |
| **Geometric ($p$)** | $P(X = k) = (1-p)^{k-1} p$ | $\frac{1}{p}$ | $\frac{1-p}{p^2}$ |
| **Hypergeometric** | $P(X = k) = \frac{\binom{K}{k}\binom{N-K}{n-k}}{\binom{N}{n}}$ | $n\frac{K}{N}$ | $n\frac{K}{N}\left(1-\frac{K}{N}\right)\left(\frac{N-n}{N-1}\right)$ |

#### 2. Geometric Continuous Probability

When outcomes represent points selected uniformly over continuous geometric regions (lengths, areas, time intervals):
$$P(E) = \frac{\text{Measure of Favorable Region}}{\text{Measure of Total Region}}$$

#### 3. Step-by-Step Worked Example: Rendezvous Problem

**Problem:** Two business executives, Rohan and Priya, agree to meet at a lounge between 12:00 PM and 1:00 PM. Each agrees to wait exactly 15 minutes before leaving. Neither will wait past 1:00 PM. Assuming their arrival times are independent and uniformly distributed throughout the hour, find the probability that they successfully meet.

**Solution Steps:**
1. Let $x$ and $y$ represent the arrival times of Rohan and Priya in minutes past 12:00 PM.
2. The sample space is a square of side $60$ minutes in the Cartesian plane:
$$0 \le x \le 60, \quad 0 \le y \le 60 \implies \text{Total Area} = 60 \times 60 = 3600$$
3. They meet if and only if their arrival difference is at most $15$ minutes:
$$|x - y| \le 15 \iff -15 \le x - y \le 15 \iff x - 15 \le y \le x + 15$$
4. Compute the area of the non-favorable region (where $|x - y| > 15$):
   - Region 1 ($y > x + 15$): Right triangle with vertices $(0, 15), (0, 60), (45, 60)$. Leg length is $45$.
     $$\text{Area}_1 = \frac{1}{2}(45)(45) = 1012.5$$
   - Region 2 ($x > y + 15$): Symmetric right triangle with vertices $(15, 0), (60, 0), (60, 45)$. Leg length is $45$.
     $$\text{Area}_2 = \frac{1}{2}(45)(45) = 1012.5$$
5. Total non-favorable area:
$$\text{Area}_{miss} = 1012.5 + 1012.5 = 2025$$
6. Calculate favorable meeting area:
$$\text{Area}_{meet} = 3600 - 2025 = 1575$$
7. Determine meeting probability:
$$P(\text{Meet}) = \frac{1575}{3600} = \frac{7}{16} = 0.4375$$
There is a $43.75\%$ probability that the two executives meet.
