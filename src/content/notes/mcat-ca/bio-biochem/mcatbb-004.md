# Genetics and Evolution — MCAT Bio/Biochem

Genetics and evolution form the second half of AAMC Foundational Concept 2 of the Bio/Biochem section. The MCAT tests Mendelian genetics, extensions of Mendel, linkage and recombination, gene expression (transcription, translation), gene regulation, DNA replication and repair, and population genetics. These topics underpin many passages on inheritance, genetic disease, and evolutionary biology.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### Core concepts

- **Mendel\'s laws:** law of segregation (alleles separate during gamete formation); law of independent assortment (genes on different chromosomes assort independently).
- **Extensions of Mendel:** incomplete dominance (heterozygote intermediate); codominance (both alleles expressed); multiple alleles (ABO blood groups); polygenic inheritance (skin colour, height).
- **Sex-linked traits:** X-linked recessive (haemophilia, Duchenne muscular dystrophy); X-linked dominant (Rett syndrome).
- **Linkage:** genes close together on the same chromosome tend to be inherited together.
- **DNA replication:** semi-conservative; occurs in S phase; requires DNA polymerase, primase, helicase, ligase, topoisomerase.
- **Transcription:** DNA → mRNA. RNA polymerase; promoter; 5' cap and poly-A tail added.
- **Translation:** mRNA → protein. Ribosome reads codons; tRNA brings amino acids; start codon AUG; stop codons UAA, UAG, UGA.
- **Population genetics:** Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium (p² + 2pq + q² = 1).

#### One-pass drill

1. Identify the topic: Mendelian, molecular, or population genetics.
2. Recall the relevant rule or equation.
3. Apply it to the specific scenario.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing genotype with phenotype. Genotype is the genetic makeup; phenotype is the observable trait.
- Treating all dominances as complete. Incomplete dominance and codominance are different.
- Forgetting that X-linked recessive traits are more common in males (one X chromosome).

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Mendelian genetics

- **Monohybrid cross:** Aa × Aa → 1 AA : 2 Aa : 1 aa (3:1 phenotype ratio for complete dominance).
- **Dihybrid cross:** AaBb × AaBb → 9:3:3:1 phenotype ratio (for genes on different chromosomes).
- **Test cross:** cross with a homozygous recessive (aa) to determine the unknown genotype.
- **Pedigree analysis:** identify the inheritance pattern (autosomal dominant, autosomal recessive, X-linked dominant, X-linked recessive, mitochondrial).

#### Molecular genetics

**DNA replication:** semi-conservative (each daughter molecule has one parental strand and one new strand). Requires DNA polymerase (5' to 3' synthesis), primase (RNA primer), helicase (unwinds the double helix), topoisomerase (relieves supercoiling), ligase (joins Okazaki fragments).

**Transcription:** DNA → mRNA. RNA polymerase reads the template strand 3' to 5', synthesising mRNA 5' to 3'. The mRNA is processed: 5' cap, poly-A tail, splicing (introns removed, exons joined).

**Translation:** mRNA → protein. Ribosome reads codons (3 nucleotides) on the mRNA. tRNA brings the corresponding amino acid. Start codon AUG (methionine). Stop codons UAA, UAG, UGA. The polypeptide is synthesised N-terminus to C-terminus.

**Gene regulation:** operons (lac, trp in prokaryotes); transcription factors, enhancers, silencers in eukaryotes; epigenetic modifications (DNA methylation, histone acetylation).

**Mutations:** point mutations (silent, missense, nonsense); frameshift mutations (insertion or deletion of non-multiples of 3 nucleotides).

#### Population genetics

**Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium:** p² + 2pq + q² = 1, where p is the frequency of one allele and q is the frequency of the other. The conditions for equilibrium are: no mutation, no migration, no selection, random mating, large population.

**Selection:** directional (favours one extreme); stabilising (favours the average); disruptive (favours both extremes).

#### Practice routine

- Take 5 Mendelian items. Predict offspring ratios; identify inheritance patterns from pedigrees.
- Take 5 molecular items. Identify the enzymes in replication, transcription, translation.
- Take 5 mutation items. Classify the mutation; predict the effect on the protein.
- Take 5 Hardy-Weinberg items. Calculate allele frequencies.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How this topic connects to the other Bio/Biochem topics

- **Cellular biology** provides the cell cycle context for DNA replication.
- **Metabolism** depends on gene expression for enzyme production.
- **Evolution** depends on population genetics and selection.

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"All mutations are harmful."** Most mutations are neutral; some are beneficial (source of evolutionary variation).
- **"DNA polymerase can start a new strand."** DNA polymerase needs a primer (RNA primer made by primase). It cannot start a new strand on its own.
- **"Hardy-Weinberg applies to all populations."** Hardy-Weinberg applies only under specific conditions (no selection, no mutation, etc.). Real populations rarely meet all conditions.

#### Exam technique

- For Mendelian items, set up the Punnett square and count the offspring ratios.
- For pedigree items, identify the inheritance pattern (autosomal or X-linked, dominant or recessive) before answering.
- For molecular items, identify the enzyme by its function (polymerase, helicase, ligase, etc.).
- For Hardy-Weinberg items, identify p and q, then calculate.
- Use the platform's calculator.
