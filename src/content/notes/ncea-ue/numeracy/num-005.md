# Numeracy-rich achievement standards from Mathematics (Level 1 and above) — NCEA UE Numeracy Notes

Beyond the dedicated co-requisite numeracy standard US32406 and the Level 1 external standards 91946/91947, a student can satisfy UE numeracy using numeracy-rich achievement standards in Mathematics (Level 1 and above). NZQA publishes a list of these standards in the UE numeracy spreadsheet, available on the UE numeracy page. The standards include selected Level 1, Level 2, and Level 3 Mathematics achievement standards that have sufficient numeracy content.

> Verify the live UE numeracy standards spreadsheet on https://www2.nzqa.govt.nz/about-us/publications/newsletters-circulars/assessment-matters/a2023-17/ before planning revision around the figures below.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Various credits** depending on the specific standard.
- Selected Level 1 and above Mathematics achievement standards are on the "numeracy rich" list published by NZQA.
- Together with US32406, these standards can satisfy the 10-credit UE numeracy requirement.
- The standards include topics like measurement, geometry, statistics, and applied problems with a numeracy focus.

#### Examiner traps

- Assuming all Mathematics standards are on the list — only specific standards are, and the list is updated periodically.
- Confusing numeracy-rich standards with mathematics-for-specialists standards (e.g., Level 3 Calculus, which is more advanced).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### The UE numeracy framework

The 2024 UE numeracy rule is 10 credits at Level 1 or above. The dedicated co-requisite numeracy standard US32406 (5 credits) is the centrepiece. The other 5 credits can come from a variety of sources:

- US32412 (Te Pāngarau, 5 credits).
- NCEA Level 1 external Mathematics standards (91946, 91947).
- Numeracy-rich achievement standards in Mathematics, Science, Geography, and other subjects.

The numeracy-rich list is published as a spreadsheet on the NZQA UE numeracy page.

#### Why numeracy-rich?

A standard is "numeracy rich" if its content involves substantial mathematical and statistical reasoning in applied contexts. This is distinct from mathematics-for-specialists standards (e.g., Level 3 Calculus), which focus on advanced mathematical techniques.

The distinction reflects the purpose of UE numeracy: to ensure that students have the practical numeracy skills needed for university study, not just the abstract mathematical knowledge.

#### Common numeracy-rich standards

The list changes from year to year as NZQA updates the standards. Common entries include:

- AS 91009 (Apply algebraic methods in solving problems, Level 1) — typically on the list.
- AS 91030 (Apply measurement in solving problems, Level 1) — typically on the list.
- AS 91035 (Investigate a given multivariate data set using the statistical enquiry cycle, Level 1) — typically on the list.
- Various Level 2 and Level 3 standards with measurement, statistics, or applied mathematics focus.

The exact list is on the NZQA UE numeracy spreadsheet.

#### Combined pathways

A typical UE numeracy pathway:

- US32406 (5 credits) + AS 91009 (3 credits) + AS 91035 (4 credits) = 12 credits.

Or:

- US32406 (5 credits) + 91946 (5 credits) = 10 credits.

Both pathways satisfy the requirement; the choice depends on the student's other Mathematics standards.

#### Preparation strategy

For each standard on the list:

1. **Read the standard** — know what is being assessed.
2. **Check the credit value** — multiply by the number of standards to meet 10 credits.
3. **Practice the genre** — applied problems, measurement, statistics.
4. **Use exemplars** — NZQA publishes student work at each grade level.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Why the list matters

The numeracy-rich list gives students flexibility in meeting UE numeracy. A student who has not achieved US32406 (perhaps because they attended a school that did not offer the dedicated co-requisite) can still meet UE numeracy through a combination of numeracy-rich achievement standards.

The list also recognises that numeracy is broader than the dedicated co-requisite — students encounter numeracy in many subjects, and the standards on the list reflect that breadth.

#### Excellence in numeracy-rich standards

Merit in numeracy-rich standards demonstrates competent application. Excellence demonstrates:

- Sophisticated method selection.
- Justification of method choice.
- Reflection on the reasonableness of results.
- Connection to the broader context.

The Excellence student treats the numeracy standard as an opportunity to demonstrate applied mathematical thinking, not just calculation.

#### Combining standards

A student can mix and match standards from different subjects to meet UE numeracy. For example:

- US32406 (5 credits, dedicated co-requisite).
- AS 91035 (4 credits, Level 1 Mathematics statistics).
- A Geography standard on the numeracy list (3 credits).
- Total: 12 credits.

This flexibility allows students to design a UE numeracy pathway that aligns with their subject choices.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "All Mathematics standards count for UE numeracy." No — only those on the published numeracy-rich list.
- "UE numeracy is just about arithmetic." No — it includes measurement, geometry, statistics, and applied problems.
- "US32406 is the only way to meet UE numeracy." No — there are multiple pathways.
- "I can claim credits retroactively." Yes — once a standard is on the list and you have achieved it, the credit counts.
