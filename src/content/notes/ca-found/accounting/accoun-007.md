# Company Accounts

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Company Accounts** deals with financial statements of a company registered under the Companies Act, 2013, covering **Share Capital, Debentures, Final Accounts** in Schedule III (Division I) format, and **Valuation of Shares**. The branch differs from partnership accounts mainly because companies follow a statutory format and have separate legal entity status.

- **Issue Price = Face Value + Premium** per share; discount on issue is **prohibited** u/s 53 except for sweat equity.
- **Calls in Arrears** = partly-paid shares × unpaid amount; interest charged at **not exceeding Table F rate**.
- **Calls in Advance** ≤ **12% p.a.** interest allowed, credited to Interest on Calls in Advance A/c.
- **Forfeited amount per share** = amount already paid *excluding* securities premium.
- **Basic EPS** = Profit after tax attributable to equity shareholders ÷ Weighted average equity shares.
- **Securities Premium** use is restricted to 5 purposes u/s 52: bonus issue, preliminary expenses, discount/commission write-off, buy-back premium, redemption premium.
- Final accounts must use **vertical form** under Schedule III; proposed dividend goes under *Current Liabilities → Provisions*, not on the asset side.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Share Capital Classification

A company distinguishes five layers of share capital: **Authorised** (memorandum limit), **Issued** (offered to public), **Subscribed** (actually applied for), **Called-up** (amount demanded so far), and **Paid-up** (actually received). Equity and preference shares are both part of subscribed capital, but preference shares carry a fixed dividend and priority on winding up.

#### Issue, Forfeiture and Reissue

Shares may be issued at **par, premium or discount**. Premium received must be parked in **Securities Premium A/c** and used only for the five Section 52 purposes. On **forfeiture**, the journal entry debits Share Capital (called-up) and Securities Premium (if any premium unpaid), credits Calls in Arrears and Share Forfeiture A/c. On **reissue**, the discount permissible equals the amount already paid per share, ensuring no loss routes through Share Forfeiture A/c.

| Concept | Key treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Calls in Arrears | Debit shareholder; interest charged at Table F rate |
| Calls in Advance | Credit Interest on Calls in Advance A/c; ≤ 12% p.a. |
| Pro-rata Allotment | Excess application money adjusted against allotment/calls |
| Reissue discount | Maximum = amount already paid per share |
| Securities Premium | Usable only for 5 statutory purposes u/s 52 |

#### Debentures and Buy-back

**Debentures** are borrowings secured or unsecured, convertible or non-convertible. **DRR** was phased out for listed non-convertibles via MCA notification; unlisted companies follow Rule 18 of Companies (Share Capital and Debentures) Rules, 2014. On **buy-back** or **preference share redemption** out of free reserves or securities premium, an equivalent amount must transfer to **Capital Redemption Reserve (CRR)** before any dividend declaration.

#### Final Accounts under Schedule III (Division I)

The **Statement of Profit and Loss** starts with Revenue from Operations, while the **Balance Sheet** presents equity and liabilities on one side and assets on the other. Proposed dividend sits under *Provisions* (Current Liabilities), provision for tax under *Short-term Provisions*, and depreciation debited to the respective asset head. Prior period and extraordinary items have separate note disclosure lines.

- Final accounts must follow the **vertical form** prescribed in Schedule III, Division I.
- Managerial remuneration appears in P&L with disclosure in Notes to Accounts.
- Contingent liabilities and commitments are **memorandum items**, not balance-sheet figures.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Valuation of Shares — Intrinsic Methods

Two intrinsic methods appear in CA Foundation questions: **Net Asset Method** and **Yield Method**. The Net Asset Method adjusts total assets for fictitious and intangible assets, deducts external liabilities *and* preference share capital, and divides by the number of equity shares. The Yield Method capitalises expected earnings at the normal rate of return. A combined **Fair Value** equals the average of the two intrinsic values, often used in court-driven valuations.

| Method | Formula | Use case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Net Asset Method | (Assets − Fictitious − External Liabilities − Pref. Capital) ÷ Equity shares | Asset-heavy companies |
| Yield Method | Expected EPS × 100 ÷ Normal Rate of Return (%) | Profit-stable companies |
| Fair Value | Average of Net Asset and Yield Value | Statutory valuation u/s 247 |

#### Edge Cases and Common Traps

Several recurring mistakes appear across ICAI test papers and RTP questions. Including securities premium in the forfeiture debit is wrong — premium must be written off separately to Securities Premium A/c. Charging calls-in-advance interest above 12% p.a. violates Table F. Placing proposed dividend on the asset side of the Balance Sheet contradicts Schedule III, which mandates its disclosure under *Provisions*. Treating DRR as universally applicable ignores the MCA exemption for listed non-convertibles.

Two shareholders holding 100 shares each failed to pay the second call, and their shares were forfeited and later reissued at ₹80 per share.
