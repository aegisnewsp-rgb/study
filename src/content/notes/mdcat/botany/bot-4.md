# Variety of Life

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Single-sentence anchor for the 60-second read before you walk into MCQs.

Living things are sorted into a hierarchy that runs **Kingdom → Division → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species**. Memorise that order first; every classification question in MDCAT Botany reduces to placing an organism somewhere along this chain or naming its two Latin words correctly.

- **Binomial nomenclature** was fixed by Carl Linnaeus. Format: *Genus species* — genus capitalised, species lowercase, both italicised or underlined. Example: *Homo sapiens*, not *Homo Sapiens*.
- **Five Kingdoms** (Whittaker, 1969): Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia — separated on cell type, body organisation, nutrition, and reproduction.
- **Three Domains** (Woese, 1977): Archaea, Bacteria, Eukarya — built on 16S rRNA sequence similarity.
- **Dominant generation rule:** bryophyte = gametophyte dominant; pteridophyte, gymnosperm, angiosperm = sporophyte dominant.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook (Memory Hook — "King Philip"):** *King Philip Came Over For Good Spaghetti* = **K**ingdom → **P**hylum/Division → **C**lass → **O**rder → **F**amily → **G**enus → **S**pecies. Drop "Good" if you want a seven-step chain without filler.

#### Classification systems side by side

| Feature | Five-Kingdom (Whittaker 1969) | Three-Domain (Woese 1977) |
|---|---|---|
| Basis | Cell type, nutrition, reproduction, body organisation | 16S rRNA gene phylogeny |
| Kingdoms | 5 (Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia) | Not used as primary rank |
| Domains | — | Archaea, Bacteria, Eukarya |
| Where viruses fit | Excluded (acellular) | Excluded |
| MDCAT frequency | Direct MCQs on kingdom features | Occasional "who proposed" question |

| Group | Cell type | Cell wall | Nutrition | Reproduction | One example |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| Cyanobacteria | Prokaryote | Peptidoglycan | Photoautotroph (chl *a*, phycocyanin) | Binary fission | *Nostoc* |

#### Monocot vs dicot — the four-line MCQ discriminant

| Trait | Monocotyledon | Dicotyledon |
|---|---|---|
| Cotyledons in seed | 1 | 2 |
| Leaf venation | Parallel | Reticulate |
| Root system | Fibrous (adventitious) | Tap root |
| Floral parts | Multiple of 3 | Multiple of 4 or 5 |
| Vascular bundles in stem | Scattered | Ringed |

#### Resolving a typical MDCAT item

A past-paper stem reads: *"Which organism lacks a true nucleus, has peptidoglycan in its wall, and carries out oxygenic photosynthesis using chlorophyll a?"* Options — (A) *Spirogyra* (B) *Nostoc* (C) *Agaricus* (D) *Funaria*.

Step 1 — Eliminate on cell type. *Spirogyra*, *Agaricus*, *Funaria* are eukaryotes with a true nucleus. Only *Nostoc* qualifies as prokaryote. Step 2 — Confirm photosynthesis. Cyanobacteria such as *Nostoc* run oxygenic photosynthesis using chlorophyll *a* plus phycobiliproteins (phycocyanin gives the blue-green colour) and store nitrogen in specialised cells called **heterocysts**. Peptidoglycan walls confirm Bacteria, not Archaea. The answer is (B) *Nostoc*.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** students often pick *Spirogyra* because it is "green" and "algal" — but *Spirogyra* is eukaryotic (has a nucleus and chloroplasts), so it fails the "no true nucleus" criterion. The diagnostic feature for cyanobacteria is the **prokaryote + chlorophyll a + phycobiliproteins** triple, not colour alone.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

#### Edge cases examiners rotate through

1. **Viruses cross every kingdom rule.** Acellular, no metabolism outside a host, reproduce only by hijacking a host's ribosomes. The Baltimore classification sorts them by genome type: dsDNA, ssDNA, dsRNA, (+)ssRNA, (−)ssRNA, ssRNA-RT, dsDNA-RT — seven classes total. HIV is a retrovirus, class VI (ssRNA-RT); influenza is class V ((−)ssRNA).
2. **Mycoplasma breaks the "bacteria have cell walls" rule.** It is the only bacterium lacking peptidoglycan; its plasma membrane contains sterols, which is why it resists beta-lactam antibiotics such as penicillin.
3. **Lichens are not one organism.** The mycobiont (fungus) gives shape and water retention; the phycobiont (alga or cyanobacterium) fixes carbon. Removing either partner kills the thallus. Growth forms — crustose, foliose, fruticose — are diagnostic at sight and are used as bioindicators of SO₂ pollution (fruticose vanish first).
4. **Alternation of generations shifts dominance.** Bryophytes: gametophyte (n) dominant, sporophyte (2n) reduced and nutritionally dependent. Pteridophytes and seed plants: sporophyte dominant, gametophyte reduced (in angiosperms the male gametophyte is just pollen, the female is the embryo sac — seven cells, eight nuclei).
5. **Heterospory vs homospory.** Selaginella and the seed plants are heterosporous (microspores + megaspores, producing male and female gametophytes separately). *Pteris* and most ferns are homosporous (one spore type, bisexual gametophyte).
6. **Gymnosperm ovules are exposed on megasporophylls** — that is the literal meaning of "naked seed". In *Pinus* these aggregate into female cones; in *Cycas* they sit on loose, leaf-like megasporophylls.

#### Comparison matrix that pays off across multiple MCQs

| Pair often confused | Distinguishing test | Pick this if… |
|---|---|---|
| Cyanobacteria vs algae | Prokaryote vs eukaryote | Cyanobacteria = no nucleus, no chloroplasts, phycobiliproteins |
| Fungi vs plants | Cell wall chemistry | Fungi = chitin; plants = cellulose |
| Gram (+) vs Gram (−) | Stain retention | Thick peptidoglycan = purple; thin layer + outer membrane = pink |
| Bryophyte vs pteridophyte | Dominant generation | Bryophyte = gametophyte; pteridophyte = sporophyte |
| Gymnosperm vs angiosperm | Seed enclosure | Naked ovule vs ovule inside ovary → fruit |
| Division vs Phylum | Botanical vs zoological rank | Both valid; MDCAT prefers **Division** for plants |
| Species vs Genus | Position in binomial | First word = Genus (capital); second = specific epithet (lower-case) |

#### Common mistake log to mark before exam night

- Writing *sapiens Homo* instead of *Homo sapiens* — order matters.
- Treating "algae" as plants because they photosynthesise. Under Whittaker they sit in **Protista**, not Plantae.
- Calling lichens a single species — they are dual organisms with two scientific names historically.
- Forgetting that gymnosperm pollination is anemophilous (wind), so they produce massive pollen quantities and lack showy petals.
- Mixing up stored food in algae: green algae store **starch** like plants; brown store **laminarin + mannitol**; red store **floridean starch**.
- Reading "no cell wall" on a fungus option and selecting it — fungi always have chitinous walls.

#### Two advanced prompts to test yourself

1. A plant has parallel-veined leaves, fibrous roots, floral parts in threes, and a single cotyledon. A student insists it must be an alga because it is "simple". List three reasons why the specimen is a monocot angiosperm, not an alga. (Hint: vascular tissue, true roots, flowers, seed enclosure.)
2. *Rhizopus* reproduces asexually by sporangiospores and sexually by zygospores. (a) Name the fungal class. (b) Identify which of these processes represents the sexual phase and what ploidy the zygospore reaches immediately after karyogamy.
