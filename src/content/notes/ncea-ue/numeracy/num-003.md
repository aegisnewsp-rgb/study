# NCEA Level 1 Mathematics: Interpret and apply mathematical and statistical information in context (91946) — NCEA UE Numeracy Notes

Achievement Standard 91946 is the first of two NCEA Level 1 Mathematics external standards. It is assessed by an end-of-year NZQA examination and carries 5 credits. The standard contributes to UE numeracy when used in conjunction with the dedicated co-requisite numeracy standard US32406. The 2024 UE numeracy rule change means a student can use 91946 (alongside US32406) to satisfy the 10-credit UE numeracy requirement.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **5 credits**, external NZQA examination at the end of Year 11.
- Tests the ability to interpret given mathematical and statistical information, select and apply methods, and solve problems in real-world contexts.
- Counts toward UE numeracy when used in conjunction with US32406.
- The standard is a sister standard to 91947 (Demonstrate mathematical reasoning); both are external Level 1 Mathematics standards.

#### Examiner traps

- Forgetting that the question provides the data — students choose methods, not data sources.
- Skipping the units on numerical answers.
- Using a calculator on a non-calculator question (the paper specifies which questions allow calculators).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### How 91946 contributes to UE numeracy

The 2024 UE numeracy rule changed the requirement to 10 credits at Level 1 or above. Before 2024, the requirement was narrower; now, a wider range of standards contribute.

US32406 is the dedicated co-requisite numeracy standard (5 credits). To satisfy the remaining 5 credits, a student can use:

- US32412 (Te Pāngarau, 5 credits).
- A "numeracy-rich" achievement standard (downloadable spreadsheet on the NZQA UE numeracy page).
- A NCEA Level 1 or above Mathematics achievement standard, including 91946 (5 credits) or 91947 (5 credits).

So a student who achieves both US32406 and 91946 satisfies the full 10-credit UE numeracy requirement.

#### What the standard tests

91946 tests the application of mathematics and statistics in context. The exam provides all the data and the context; the student chooses methods and applies them.

Common topics:

- Number (fractions, percentages, ratio, rates).
- Algebra (linear equations, substitution).
- Geometry (angles, areas, volumes).
- Measurement (unit conversions, speed/distance/time).
- Statistics (interpreting graphs, summary statistics).
- Probability (basic probability, tree diagrams).

#### Preparing for UE numeracy through 91946

Since 91946 contributes 5 credits to UE numeracy, achieving it is a "two-birds-one-stone" moment: the student earns credits toward the NCEA Level 1 certificate AND toward UE numeracy.

Strategy:

1. Take Level 1 Mathematics (which includes 91946 as one of the externals).
2. Achieve the standard (Aim for Merit or Excellence — University Entrance does not require a specific grade, but higher grades indicate stronger numeracy).
3. Also achieve US32406 (typically done at the same time, often through a school-wide numeracy programme).
4. Combine the credits to satisfy UE numeracy.

#### Combined with US32406

The combination of US32406 + 91946 is a common pathway:

- US32406: applied numeracy in everyday contexts (internally assessed).
- 91946: applied mathematics and statistics in exam-style contexts (externally assessed).

Together, they cover the breadth of numeracy expected for university study.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Excellence in 91946

The biggest gap between Merit and Excellence on 91946 is the depth of method selection.

For example, on a question about choosing between two phone plans:

- Merit: Calculate the total cost for each plan over 12 months, identify the cheaper plan.
- Excellence: Calculate the total cost, identify the cheaper plan, AND consider edge cases (e.g., what if usage changes? what if the contract is broken early?), AND justify the choice with a clear comparison.

The Excellence response engages with the context, not just the calculation.

#### Transferable skills

The numeracy skills tested in 91946 are directly transferable to university study:

- Number sense (estimation, percentages, ratios).
- Algebraic reasoning (equations, formulae).
- Statistical literacy (interpreting data).
- Geometric reasoning (spatial thinking).

These skills are valuable across STEM, social science, and many professional courses.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "91946 alone satisfies UE numeracy." No — it provides 5 credits; the dedicated co-requisite US32406 is also needed (or an alternative 5-credit numeracy standard).
- "Numeracy is just arithmetic." No — it is the application of mathematics and statistics in context.
- "The exam is like a maths test." It is more applied and contextualised.
- "UE numeracy is unrelated to NCEA." It is built on NCEA credits — the 10-credit requirement is met through NCEA standards.
