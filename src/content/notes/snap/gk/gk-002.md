# Indian History & Culture

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Indian History & Culture is the heritage slice of the SNAP General Knowledge section, weighted around 3% and usually yielding 1–2 single-statement MCQs. It bundles ancient–medieval–modern political history with classical arts, religious movements, and Constitution basics.

- **Three date anchors to memorise first:** 15 Aug 1947 (Independence), 26 Nov 1949 (Constitution adopted), 26 Jan 1950 (Constitution enforced).
- **Classical dances count is 8**, not 7 — Sattriya (Assam) was added by the Sangeet Natak Akademi in 2000.
- **Fundamental Rights (Art. 12–35)** are justiciable; **Directive Principles (Art. 36–51)** are non-justiciable — examiners love this contrast.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Chronology
SNAP rewards clean recall of dynasties, capitals, and one signature contribution per ruler. The expected timeline spans roughly 3300 years, from the Indus Valley (c. 2500 BCE) through 1947.

#### Constitution Essentials
The Indian Constitution is the single highest-yield Constitution subtopic. Memorise the three lists in the Seventh Schedule, the Preamble keywords (Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic), and the basic structure doctrine (Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala, 1973).

#### Art, Architecture, and Literature
Match each classical dance to its state and remember one architectural style marker: Nagara (North, e.g. Khajuraho), Dravidian (South, e.g. Brihadeeswarar), Vesara (hybrid, e.g. Hoysala temples), Indo-Islamic (Qutub Minar), and Mughal (Taj Mahal). UNESCO World Heritage Sites in India currently number 40+, with the latest additions announced by UNESCO sessions; check the count from the official UNESCO list before the exam.

| Maurya Empire | Founded by Chandragupta Maurya; peak under Ashoka (c. 268–232 BCE); capital Pataliputra |

- **Highest-payoff sub-areas for SNAP:** National Movement milestones, Constitution fundamentals, classical dance–state mapping.
- **Reading time per question** should be under 45 seconds since GK sits before Quant in the paper.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Examiner Traps
The traps below appear repeatedly in MBA entrance papers:

- **Treaty confusion:** Treaty of Lahore (1846) ended the First Anglo-Sikh War and ceded Kashmir; Treaty of Amritsar (1846) handed Kashmir to Gulab Singh. They are NOT the same document.
- **UNESCO Tentative List trap:** Many monuments (e.g., Delhi's signature Mughal gardens) are only *proposed*, not inscribed. Only inscribed sites count for "UNESCO World Heritage Site" questions.
- **Dance count drift:** Older books list 7 classical dances; Sattriya was added by Sangeet Natak Akademi in 2000, making the current total 8.
- **Justiciability:** Fundamental Rights → Supreme Court can enforce; Directive Principles → guidelines, not enforceable. Mixing them up costs a mark.

#### Worked Practice Prompts
1. *Match-the-following style:* "X — capital of the Vijayanagara Empire" — answer: Hampi (also called Vijayanagara). Cross-check: Krishnadevaraya's reign (1509–1529) was the empire's cultural peak, with the Tamil poet-patronage tradition.
2. *Single-statement MCQ style:* "The Indian Constitution came into force on…" — answer: 26 January 1950. Trap option will be 26 November 1949 (adoption) or 15 August 1947 (Independence).
