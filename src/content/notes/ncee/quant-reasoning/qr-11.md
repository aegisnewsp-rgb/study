# Data Interpretation from Simple Tables

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Data interpretation from simple tables** means reading numbers arranged in rows and columns, then answering questions about totals, averages, percentages and comparisons. A *row* runs horizontally across the table; a *column* runs vertically. Each value is found at the **intersection** of a specific row and column.

Core formulas to memorise:

- **Mean** = Sum of values ÷ Number of values
- **Percentage** = (Part ÷ Whole) × 100%
- **Percentage Increase** = ((New − Old) ÷ Old) × 100%
- **Percentage Decrease** = ((Old − New) ÷ Old) × 100%

High-yield pointers for NCEE Quantitative Reasoning:

- Always read the **headings** first to know what each row and column represents.
- Match the unit (₦, kg, pupils) before subtracting or comparing values.
- In percentage change questions, divide by the **old** value, never the new one.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Reading a Table Correctly

Every table has a **title** (telling you the subject — sales, rainfall, exam scores), **column headings** (telling you what is being measured), and **row headings** (telling you the category — months, classes, items). To find a specific value, trace the row heading down a column until it crosses the column heading you want; the cell at that intersection holds the answer.

#### Computing Totals and the Mean

**Totals** are sums along a row or down a column. A frequent NCEE-style question asks: *"What is the total number of pupils in all the classes?"* — you add a whole column. The **mean** then uses Mean = Sum ÷ Number of entries. If six classes recorded 30, 25, 28, 32, 27, 28 pupils, the mean is 170 ÷ 6 ≈ **28.33 pupils**.

#### Percentage Change

Percentage change compares two values from different rows or columns.

- **Increase:** ((New − Old) ÷ Old) × 100%
- **Decrease:** ((Old − New) ÷ Old) × 100%

If a shop sold 200 phones in January and 250 in February, the percentage increase is (250 − 200) ÷ 200 × 100% = **25%**. The base (200) is always the *earlier* value.

#### Comparing Quantities and Ratios

"Difference" means Larger − Smaller. "How many times more" means Larger ÷ Smaller. To express one quantity as a **percentage of another**, divide the part by the whole and multiply by 100. Out of 50 questions, a pupil got 35 correct: 35 ÷ 50 × 100% = **70%**.

#### Typical NCEE Question Patterns

Expect 2–4 table-based questions testing: reading a value, finding a total, computing a mean, calculating a percentage change, and comparing two entries. Tables usually cover familiar topics such as market sales, school enrolment, weather records, or test marks.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Subtleties

Tables sometimes include **sub-totals** that must not be double-counted when summing a column. A row marked *"Total"* should be excluded from further addition. Watch for tables with **missing entries** (a dash or blank); treat them as zero only if the heading confirms no value was recorded, otherwise note the gap in your answer.

Percentages can exceed 100% only when the *new* value is larger than the *old*; if the change is negative, the formula gives a decrease. Also remember that a **percentage point** (e.g., 60% → 70% = 10 percentage points) is different from a **percent change** (≈16.67% increase).

#### Connections to Adjacent Topics

Table interpretation feeds directly into **bar chart reading**, **pie chart angles**, and **line graph trends** — all use the same row/column logic. Mastery here also speeds up later word problems on profit, loss, and simple interest, which often present figures in tabular form.

#### Common Mistakes

- **Wrong base in percentage change:** dividing by the *new* value instead of the *old*.
- **Row–column swap:** answering with a value from the wrong row.
- **Unit mismatch:** subtracting kilograms from litres, or naira from dollars.
- **Average misuse:** dividing by the wrong count (e.g., summing five items but dividing by six).
- **Selective reading:** drawing a conclusion from one cell without checking related rows.

#### Practice Prompts

1. A table shows Ade's monthly savings as ₦2,000, ₦2,500, ₦1,800, ₦2,200, ₦2,500, and ₦3,000 from January to June. Find the **mean monthly savings** and the **percentage increase** from January to June.
2. Two classes recorded test scores: Class A — 45, 50, 55, 60, 65; Class B — 40, 55, 60, 65, 80. Which class has the higher **mean**, and by how many **marks** does it exceed the other?
