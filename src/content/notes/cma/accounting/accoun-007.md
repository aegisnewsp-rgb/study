# Company Accounts

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before CMA Foundation Paper 2.

Company Accounts is the specialised branch of financial accounting dealing with share capital, debentures, underwriting, redemption and Schedule III final statements of a joint-stock company under the Companies Act, 2013.

- **Share capital is split into** Authorised, Issued, Subscribed, Called-up and Paid-up — forfeiture entries depend on called-up, not face value.
- **Securities Premium (Sec. 52)** cannot be treated as revenue; it funds share-issue expenses, writes off preliminary expenses, or issues bonus shares.
- **Capital Redemption Reserve (Sec. 68)** is created only to the extent fresh issue proceeds fall short of the nominal value of preference shares redeemed.
- **Schedule III (Division I)** prescribes the vertical Balance Sheet format with separate heads for Shareholders' Funds, Non-current Liabilities, Current Liabilities, Non-current Assets and Current Assets.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for systematic preparation across Paper 2 syllabus.

#### Issue, Forfeiture and Re-issue of Shares

Shares can be issued at par, at premium, or at discount (discount permitted only on a firm allotment, within the 10% ceiling). When a shareholder defaults on calls, the **Forfeited Shares Account** is debited with the called-up amount unpaid and credited with the capital actually received — securities premium already paid must be transferred to the **Securities Premium Account** and excluded from the forfeiture figure.

#### Amount Payable Refund Rule

On forfeiture the company can refund the amount received (less expenses) or leave it in the books to be transferred to **Capital Reserve** on re-issue, after discounting any loss on re-issue.

| Step | Journal entry (₹) |
| --- | --- |
| Share called-up A/c Dr | Amount unpaid per share |
| To Calls-in-Arrears A/c | (per call basis) |
| Forfeited Shares A/c Dr | Capital actually received |
| To Share Capital A/c | Called-up amount |

#### Debentures — Issue, Classification and Redemption

Debentures are classified as **secured/unsecured**, **convertible/non-convertible**, **redeemable/perpetual**. Issue can be at par, premium or discount; the **Discount on Issue** is written off against securities premium first, then securities premium remains fully available. **Premium on redemption** is a liability disclosed under *Secured Loans* until the year of redemption.

#### Redemption Methods

1. **Lump-sum on maturity** — transfer face value to a Debenture Redemption Reserve (DRR) where applicable.
2. **By draw of lots in instalments** — maintain DRR for un-redemed balance.
3. **Sinking Fund Method** — yearly investment of an equated amount so that the accumulated fund equals the redemption sum on maturity.
4. **Conversion into shares** — books closed by crediting Debenture Account and debiting Share Capital at face value, with Securities Premium capturing any excess.

#### Underwriting of Shares

Underwriters can be given **firm applications** (the underwriter takes up a stated number outright) plus **marked applications** (those procured by them from the public) and **unmarked applications** (defaulted ones not procured by any underwriter). The liability is calculated as *Gross underwriting − firm applications − marked/unmarked applications marked*, never the gross figure alone.

#### Critical exam traps

- Treating Securities Premium as part of forfeiture receipt.
- Computing CRR at 100% of preference capital without netting fresh issue.
- Using Schedule VI instead of Schedule III (Division I) headings.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive treatment for mastery and CMA Inter onward progression.

#### Sinking Fund Mechanics and Worked Numbers

The **Annual Instalment** of the Sinking Fund is calculated as: Instalment = Redemption Amount / [(1 + i)^n − 1] / i, where i is the expected rate of return on investment and n is the remaining life of the debentures. Each year the P&L appropriation entry is **Debit: Statement of Profit and Loss → Debit: Profit and Loss Appropriation A/c (or directly debit to retained earnings)** and **Credit: Sinking Fund A/c**. Interest earned on the sinking fund investment is credited to the Sinking Fund Account, not to the P&L. On actual redemption, the accumulated fund is used to pay the debenture holders; any surplus is transferred back to **General Reserve**.

#### Worked Example

A company issued ₹10,00,000 12% debentures redeemable after 5 years. Required annual deposit at 10% interest on investment (using sinking fund table factor 3.7908): ₹10,00,000 × 1 / 3.7908 ≈ **₹2,63,800 per year**. After five years the accumulated balance plus compounding yields ₹10,00,000, exactly meeting the redemption obligation without straining the operating cash flow.

#### Underwriting Worked Cases

> Trap: under firm underwriting, the underwriter's liability equals the *underwritten portion net of firm applications*, not the gross underwritten amount.

- If A underwrites 4,000 shares firm and 3,000 unmarked, liability = 4,000 + (3,000 total unmarked − 0 marked to A) = 7,000 shares.
- Commission at 2% on **total issue price**, not just the gross liability.

#### Common Mistakes to Eliminate

- Crediting Premium on Redemption to P&L.
- Forgetting to write off Discount on Issue of Debentures over the life of the instrument (permitted under Ind AS).
- Mixing Preference and Equity share capital in CRR computation.

#### Practice prompts

1. 2,000 shares of ₹10 each issued at 20% premium; allotment money not paid; forfeit and re-issue at ₹8 per share as fully paid. Compute transfer to Capital Reserve.
2. ₹5,00,000 debentures at 5% premium on redemption, redeemed out of a sinking fund earning 8% over 4 years. Find the equated annual instalment.
