# Growth and Development

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Growth** is a quantitative, irreversible increase in body mass and dimensions, driven by **hyperplasia** (cell number increase via mitosis) and **hypertrophy** (cell size enlargement). **Development** is the qualitative, coordinated progression of structural and functional maturation from conception to death. The two are interlinked but never synonymous: growth is measurable in cm/kg, while development is assessed by milestones (sitting, puberty, senescence). The **sigmoid growth curve** describes postnatal human growth, with rapid infancy, steady childhood, the pubertal spurt, and plateau at adulthood when **epiphyseal plates** close. Hormonal regulators include **Growth Hormone (GH)** from the anterior pituitary, **thyroxine** (essential for amphibian metamorphosis and CNS maturation), **insulin**, and **sex steroids**. MDCAT typically tests the growth-vs-development distinction, GH–thyroxine roles, metamorphosis triggers, and prenatal stage sequencing — usually a single MCQ worth 1 mark.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Definitions and Distinction
Growth = increase in **size/mass** (quantitative, measurable). Development = increase in **complexity and functional capacity** (qualitative, assessed by skill acquisition and differentiation). A foetus *grows* in size and *develops* organs; the two happen in parallel but are described by different parameters.

#### Cellular Basis
- **Hyperplasia** — rise in cell number by mitotic division; dominant in embryonic and childhood phases.
- **Hypertrophy** — enlargement of existing cells; dominant in muscle and adipose tissue after adolescence.
- **Differentiation** — specialization of unspecialized cells into functional types (neurons, myocytes, erythrocytes).
- **Morphogenesis** — shaping of tissues and organs guided by differential gene expression, cell adhesion molecules, and Hox genes.

#### Prenatal Stages (Human)
| Stage | Approx. Timing | Key Event |
|---|---|---|
| Gametogenesis | Before birth (oogenesis starts) / puberty (spermatogenesis) | Production of haploid gametes |
| Fertilization | Day 0 | Zygote formation in ampulla of oviduct |
| Cleavage | Days 1–4 | Rapid mitosis; morula |
| Blastulation | Days 5–6 | Blastocyst with inner cell mass and trophoblast; **implantation** in endometrium |
| Gastrulation | Weeks 2–3 | Formation of three germ layers (ectoderm, mesoderm, endoderm) |
| Neurulation | Week 3–4 | Neural tube closes → CNS |
| Organogenesis | Weeks 4–8 | Organs form; **embryonic period ends at week 8** |
| Foetal period | Week 9 → birth | Growth and maturation of existing organs |

The embryonic period is the most teratogen-sensitive window.

#### Hormonal Control
- Hypothalamus releases **GHRH** (stimulates) and **somatostatin** (inhibits) → anterior pituitary → **GH** acts on liver to produce **IGF-1**, which stimulates bone and soft-tissue growth.
- **Thyroxine** is permissive for GH action, drives CNS myelination, and triggers **amphibian metamorphosis** (tadpole → frog: tail resorption, limb growth).
- **Insulin** supports anabolic growth; deficiency causes growth retardation.
- **Sex steroids** (testosterone, oestrogen) produce the pubertal growth spurt and ultimately fuse epiphyseal plates, ending linear growth.

#### Postnatal Growth Pattern
Human growth follows a **sigmoid curve**: rapid in infancy, steady in childhood, accelerated at puberty, then plateau. Body proportions follow the **cephalocaudal** (head → feet) and **proximodistal** (centre → periphery) principles — the head is disproportionately large at birth (~1/4 body length) but ~1/8 in adulthood.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Growth Rate Calculations
$$\text{Growth Rate (\%)} = \frac{\text{Final size} - \text{Initial size}}{\text{Initial size}} \times 100$$

$$\text{Relative Growth Rate} = \frac{\text{Growth increment}}{\text{Initial size} \times \text{time}}$$

In **auxology**, the **Hugershoff coefficient (k)** describes the steepness of the growth curve, used in fisheries (von Bertalanffy) and dendrochronology.

#### Determinate vs Indeterminate Growth
- **Determinate growth** — growth ceases at a defined size (humans, most mammals).
- **Indeterminate growth** — continues throughout life (fishes, many reptiles, most plants).
- **Ecdysis** (moulting) in arthropods and nematodes is a developmental event gated by **ecdysone** and **juvenile hormone**; in insects, high JH maintains larval status, while its drop at pupation permits adult differentiation.

#### Metamorphosis Mechanisms
- **Amphibians**: thyroxine surge from the developing thyroid triggers tail resorption (apoptosis), limb emergence, and lung remodelling. Blocking thyroxine arrests metamorphosis — a classic MDCAT experiment (Gudernatsch).
- **Insects**: complete (holometabolous: egg → larva → pupa → adult, e.g. butterfly) vs incomplete (hemimetabolous: nymph → adult, e.g. grasshopper). Juvenile hormone titre determines the type of moult.

#### Aging (Senescence)
Cellular accumulation of damage drives senescence. The **free-radical theory** attributes aging to reactive oxygen species damaging DNA, lipids, and proteins. **Telomere shortening** with each somatic cell division (the Hayflick limit of ~50 divisions) limits replicative capacity; telomerase is active in germ cells and cancer cells but largely absent in somatic cells. Senescence is genetically modulated (e.g., the *age-1* gene in *C. elegans* doubles lifespan when mutated).

#### Common Exam Traps
- Conflating *growth* with *development* — the wording matters; a 10-kg baby has grown but is not necessarily more developed.
- Assuming puberty stops growth entirely — bones stop elongating at epiphyseal closure, but tissues (brain, reproductive system) continue developing into the mid-20s.
- Confusing hyperplasia with hypertrophy — muscle gained by weight training is hypertrophy; embryonic growth is hyperplasia.
- Mis-timing prenatal events — implantation ends the blastocyst stage, not gastrulation.

#### Practice Prompts
1. A 12-year-old child with a pituitary adenoma secreting excess GH before epiphyseal closure develops what condition, and what would result if the same excess occurred after closure? *(Answer: gigantism; acromegaly.)*
2. Explain why surgical removal of the thyroid gland in a tadpole prevents metamorphosis but does not kill the animal, and predict the outcome if thyroxine were then administered. *(Answer: thyroxine is the obligate trigger; exogenous thyroxine restores metamorphosis — confirms causal role.)*
