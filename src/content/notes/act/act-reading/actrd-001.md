# Key Ideas and Details: Central Claims and Arguments — ACT Reading Notes

ACT Reading central claim questions evaluate understanding of main ideas, paragraph functions, and sequence of events across four passages. In the official ACT examination, this topic carries a weight rating of 5 out of 5 and appears consistently across testing administrations. Candidates preparing for high percentiles must master both theoretical fundamentals and rapid practical problem-solving strategies.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Weight & Frequency**: Rated 5/5 in priority; this topic appears reliably in every sitting.
- **Timing Goal**: Aim for disciplined pacing per item to leave sufficient buffer for complex multi-step problems at the end of the section.
- **Core Principle**: Locate stated facts and distinguish primary thesis claims from minor illustrative anecdotes.
- **Common pitfall**: The four passages always appear in fixed order: Literary Narrative, Social Science, Humanities, and Natural Science.
- **Systematic Strategy**: Always eliminate obviously incorrect distractors before attempting detailed calculations or granular textual analysis.
- **Answer Verification**: Cross-check final values or textual line references against the explicit wording of the prompt to avoid answering the wrong question.

#### Examiner traps

- Misreading key constraint words in the prompt (such as *except*, *not*, *least*, *integer*, *constant*).
- Falling for attractive partial answers that represent an intermediate calculation step rather than the final required quantity.
- Basing textual answers on outside background knowledge rather than direct evidence supplied within the passage.
- Spending excessive time struggling on a single difficult problem instead of flagging it, making an educated guess, and maintaining forward momentum.

### 🔴 Deep Dive — Mastery & Edge Cases (3mo–2yr)

#### Advanced variations and high-difficulty edge cases

Claims and anecdotes compete for the same paragraph space, and telling them apart is the skill:

- **Claim Distinction**: Separate the topic from the writer's position, summarize that position in one sentence, and reject choices that merely restate the subject alone.
- **Evidence Fit**: Pair each answer with the exact textual support it cites, then ask whether that support directly establishes the claim or only sounds related.
- **Argument Pattern**: Label claims as definitions, causes, contrasts, predictions, or recommendations, then track how each layer strengthens or limits the writer's conclusion in the passage.

#### Systematic drill schedule

Read with a pen, in this order, before answering any claim question:

- **Thesis marking**: Mark the thesis sentence in each passage and box the anecdotes. Thesis-level and illustration-level claims get confused constantly; the mark settles it.
- **Line-of-proof check**: For each answer choice, find the single line that supports it. A choice you cannot point to is eliminated regardless of how reasonable it sounds.
- **Three-line sketch**: Sketch the passage as claim, evidence, reasoning in three lines. Most central-idea questions are answered directly by that sketch.
