# Heart of Algebra — Linear Equations and Inequalities — SAT Math Notes

Heart of Algebra is the largest content domain of the SAT Math section. It covers linear equations in one and two variables, systems of linear equations, linear inequalities, absolute value equations and inequalities, and the graphical interpretation of linear functions. This domain typically accounts for a substantial share of Math items on the digital SAT.

> Verify the live SAT specifications and Heart of Algebra weighting on https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/ before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Linear equation in one variable:** ax + b = 0; solution x = -b/a (when a ≠ 0).
- **Linear equation in two variables:** y = mx + b; graph is a straight line.
- **Slope:** m = (y₂ - y₁) / (x₂ - x₁) between two points.
- **System of linear equations:** two or more equations in the same variables; solved by substitution or elimination.
- **Linear inequality:** ax + b > 0 (or ≥, <, ≤); solved by isolating the variable, remembering to flip the inequality sign when multiplying/dividing by a negative.
- **Absolute value equation:** |ax + b| = c has two solutions (ax + b = c and ax + b = -c).
- **Graphical interpretation:** the solution to f(x) = g(x) is the intersection of two graphs.

#### Examiner traps

- Forgetting to flip the inequality sign when multiplying or dividing by a negative.
- Treating two parallel lines as having a solution (they do not).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Solving linear equations

A linear equation in one variable is solved by isolating x:

1. Distribute any parentheses.
2. Combine like terms on each side.
3. Add or subtract to move variables to one side.
4. Multiply or divide to isolate x.

A linear equation in two variables has infinitely many solutions, but the relationship between x and y is fixed.

#### Solving systems of linear equations

Two methods:

1. **Substitution:** solve one equation for one variable, substitute into the other, solve, and substitute back.
2. **Elimination:** multiply the equations so that one variable cancels when subtracted, then solve.

A system has no solution if the lines are parallel. A system has infinitely many solutions if the lines are coincident.

#### Linear inequalities

Solve as you would a linear equation, but remember to flip the inequality sign when multiplying or dividing by a negative.

For an inequality involving absolute value:
- |ax + b| < c becomes -c < ax + b < c.
- |ax + b| > c becomes ax + b > c or ax + b < -c.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where Heart of Algebra appears across the SAT Math section

Heart of Algebra typically accounts for the largest share of SAT Math items. Strong performance here is the single highest-leverage preparation for the SAT Math section.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "The inequality sign never flips." It does when you multiply or divide by a negative.
- "Parallel lines have no solution." Correct — parallel lines have no intersection, so the system has no solution.
- "Slope is the y-intercept." No — slope is rise over run; y-intercept is the value of y when x = 0.

#### Exam technique

- For linear equations, isolate x by inverse operations.
- For systems, use substitution when one equation is easy to solve for one variable; use elimination when the coefficients are similar.
- For inequalities, flip the sign when multiplying/dividing by a negative.
