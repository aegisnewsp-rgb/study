# Gastrointestinal Physiology

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The GI tract performs **four coordinated functions**: motility, secretion, digestion, and absorption, regulated by the **enteric nervous system (ENS)**, autonomic nerves, and gut hormones. **Slow waves (BER)** generated by interstitial cells of Cajal set the pacemaker rhythm — about **3/min in stomach, 12/min in duodenum, 8–10/min in ileum** — with spike-burst action potentials appearing only when threshold is reached.

**Phases of gastric secretion**: cephalic (vagal → ACh + gastrin), gastric (G-cells release gastrin), intestinal (**inhibitory** via secretin, GIP, enterogastrone). **Parietal cell acid** is driven by **H⁺/K⁺-ATPase**, stimulated by ACh (M3), gastrin (CCK-B), histamine (H2). **Pancreatic juice** is alkaline (HCO₃⁻ via CFTR) plus enzymes.

| Nutrient | Key enzyme | Absorption site |
|---|---|---|
| Carbohydrate | Amylase + brush-border disaccharidases | Jejunum (SGLT1/GLUT5) |
| Protein | Pepsin, trypsin, chymotrypsin | Jejunum/ileum (PEPT1) |
| Fat | Pancreatic lipase + **colipase** | Jejunum → lacteals (chylomicrons) |
| Vitamin B12 | Requires **intrinsic factor** | **Terminal ileum** |
| Iron (Fe²⁺) | DMT1 transporter | **Duodenum** |

Daily GI fluid turnover ≈ **9 L**, but stool loses only ≈ 0.1 L.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Motility and the BER
Interstitial cells of Cajal generate **slow waves** that depolarise circular smooth muscle without contraction. Only when a critical threshold is reached (by stretch or ACh) do **Ca²⁺-driven spike bursts** trigger peristalsis or segmentation. The **migrating motor complex (MMC)** sweeps residual content every 90–120 min between meals, driven by motilin. **Mass movements** in the colon produce haustral shuttling and the **gastrocolic reflex** after meals.

#### Secretion and the Cephalic–Gastric–Intestinal Triad
**Cephalic phase**: taste, smell, chewing → vagal efferents → ACh on parietal and G-cells, releasing gastrin. **Gastric phase**: peptides and distension → gastrin from G-cells. **Intestinal phase**: chyme entering the duodenum activates S-cells (secretin), I-cells (CCK) and K-cells (GIP), which **inhibit** gastric acid and slow emptying.

#### Pancreatic and Biliary Contributions
**Secretin** stimulates ductal HCO₃⁻ secretion via **CFTR**, neutralising gastric acid. **CCK** stimulates acinar enzyme release (trypsinogen, chymotrypsinogen, procarboxypeptidase, proelastase, plus lipase, amylase, phospholipase). **Enterokinase** on the duodenal brush border converts trypsinogen → trypsin, which then activates the rest. **Bile salts** emulsify lipids and form mixed **micelles (2–6 nm)**; ~95% are recycled 5–7 times/day via the **enterohepatic circulation**.

#### Absorption Mechanisms
- **Glucose/galactose**: SGLT1 (Na⁺-coupled, secondary active).
- **Fructose**: GLUT5 (facilitated diffusion — slower, not Na⁺-coupled).
- **Amino acids/di-/tripeptides**: PEPT1 for peptides; Na⁺-coupled transporters for AAs.
- **Iron**: Fe³⁺ reduced to Fe²⁺ by Dcytb, absorbed via **DMT1**; exported by ferroportin, oxidised by hephaestin, carried on transferrin.
- **B12-IF complex**: endocytosed at the **terminal ileum** via cubilin receptors.

#### MCQ Traps to Avoid
- Pancreatic lipase requires **colipase** to bind lipid droplets amid bile salts.
- The intestinal phase of gastric secretion is **inhibitory**, not stimulatory.
- B12 is absorbed in the **terminal ileum**, not the duodenum.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Defecation Reflex and Sphincter Control
Rectal distension sends afferents via pelvic nerves → **rectoanal inhibitory reflex** (internal sphincter relaxation via ENS) → voluntary relaxation of the external sphincter through cortical control. Loss of this reflex underlies Hirschsprung disease, where aganglionic segments fail to relax.

#### Capillary Fluid Exchange in the Gut
Intestinal capillaries follow **Starling's equation**:  
**Jv = Lp · S · ([Pc − Pi] − σ[πc − πi])**, where Lp is hydraulic conductivity, S is surface area, Pc/Pi are hydrostatic pressures, πc/πi are oncotic pressures, and σ is the reflection coefficient (0–1). Absorption of 8.9 L/day depends on this balance; hypoproteinaemia shifts the gradient and produces intestinal oedema.

#### Acid–Base and Osmotic Calculations
Gastric H⁺ secretion obeys **Henderson–Hasselbalch**: pH = 6.1 + log₁₀([HCO₃⁻]/[H₂CO₃]). The **osmotic load** of a meal is **Osm = m × c**, where m is osmoles and c is particles per mole — hyperosmolar chyme drives water secretion until equilibrated.

#### Clinical Correlations
- **Pernicious anaemia**: autoantibodies destroy parietal cells → no intrinsic factor → B12 malabsorption → megaloblastic anaemia and subacute combined degeneration.
- **Cholera**: CFTR-mediated Cl⁻ secretion → Na⁺ and water efflux → secretory diarrhoea.
- **Crohn ileitis / ileal resection**: B12 deficiency + bile-salt loss → steatorrhoea and pigment gallstones (cholesterol stones from disrupted enterohepatic flow).
- **Lactose intolerance**: brush-border lactase deficiency → osmotic diarrhoea and gas from bacterial fermentation.

#### Common Mistakes
- Confusing **pepsinogen activation** (by HCl + pepsin autocatalysis) with **trypsinogen activation** (by enterokinase).
- Believing the **colon** absorbs most water — actually the **jejunum absorbs ≈ 6.5 L** of the 8.9 L daily load; the colon salvages only ≈ 1–2 L.
- Attributing vitamin B12 absorption to the duodenum; it requires the **terminal ileum** and intrinsic factor.
- Assuming **GIP stimulates** gastric acid — GIP is an **incretin** that **inhibits** acid secretion.

#### Practice Prompts
1. A patient with terminal ileum resection develops megaloblastic anaemia and cholesterol gallstones. Trace the physiological mechanism from resection to both findings.
2. Explain how loss of colipase produces steatorrhoea despite intact pancreatic lipase secretion.
