# Unit 7 — Long Lives — Kuwait Secondary Certificate English Notes

Unit 7 of the Kuwait MOE Grade 12 "Over To You" textbook (second term, 25 January to 5 February 2026) is "Long Lives" — a thematic unit on ageing, longevity, and intergenerational experience. The listening input is a journalist talking about elderly people she has met. Reading input is an article on the importance of sleep. The unit's writing focus is argumentative composition (an article). The unit's grammar focus is direct and reported questions and statements. Vocabulary is collocations with make/do, phrasal verbs with make and do, and theme-related vocabulary. Speaking focus is on discussing and comparing using language function expressions. This unit is examined at terminal level as part of the Kuwait Secondary Certificate (Thanawiya Amma) English paper.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Theme vocabulary:** old age, longevity, ageing population, retirement, generational gap, life expectancy, geriatric care.
- **Reported speech pattern:** "Do you sleep well?" → She asked (me) whether/if I slept well. Tense shifts back one step (present → past).
- **Reported statements:** "I work as a journalist" → She said (that) she worked as a journalist.
- **Collocations with make:** make a decision, make progress, make an effort, make a mistake, make sense.
- **Collocations with do:** do homework, do research, do a favour, do the dishes, do well.
- **Phrasal verbs with "make":** make up (invent), make up for (compensate), make out (understand), make over (transfer ownership).
- **Phrasal verbs with "do":** do away with (abolish), do without (manage without), do up (renovate), do over (redo).
- **Argumentative article structure:** claim, two reasons with supporting detail, counter-argument + rebuttal, conclusion.

#### Examiner traps

- "I am" does not shift to "I were" in reported speech — "He said he was tired" (not "He said he were tired").
- Make and do are not interchangeable — "make a mistake" but "do homework", no swap.
- In an argumentative article, the conclusion must restate the thesis, not introduce a new argument.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Reported speech

When reporting what someone said, the reporting verb tense shifts back one step:

- Present simple → Past simple: "I work" → She said she worked.
- Present continuous → Past continuous: "I am working" → She said she was working.
- Present perfect → Past perfect: "I have finished" → She said she had finished.
- Will → Would: "I will go" → She said she would go.
- Past simple → Past perfect: "I went" → She said she had gone.

Modals shift too: can → could, may → might, must → had to. Time expressions shift: today → that day, yesterday → the day before, tomorrow → the next day, here → there, this → that.

For questions, use whether/if or the wh-word with statement word order: "Do you sleep well?" → She asked (me) whether/if I slept well. "Where do you live?" → She asked where I lived.

#### Collocations and phrasal verbs

Make and do collocate differently. Make tends to collocate with creations, decisions, efforts, money, mistakes, plans, progress. Do tends to collocate with tasks, work, duties, exercises, homework. Memorise the most common ones as single units — they cannot be inferred.

Phrasal verbs are multi-word verbs whose meaning is idiomatic. The particle changes the meaning: make up (fabricate), make up for (compensate for), make out (discern), make over (transfer). Make sure to learn the particle and the meaning as a unit, not as separate words.

#### Theme vocabulary

Longevity and ageing vocabulary includes: life expectancy, geriatric, age-related, retirement age, pension, nursing home, assisted living, dementia, Alzheimer's, chronic illness, palliative care, hospice, intergenerational, demographic shift. Use these in speaking and writing with appropriate register.

#### Argumentative article structure

A standard argumentative article has the following structure:

1. **Title** — concise, informative, often with a strong claim.
2. **Introduction** — hook the reader (a question, a striking statistic, a short anecdote), then state your thesis clearly.
3. **Body paragraph 1** — first reason supporting the thesis, with evidence or a developed example.
4. **Body paragraph 2** — second reason, with evidence.
5. **Counter-argument + rebuttal** — state the opposing view fairly, then refute it.
6. **Conclusion** — restate the thesis in different words, summarise the strongest reason, end with a forward-looking statement or call to action.

Use cohesive devices (firstly, secondly, furthermore, in addition, on the other hand, however, therefore, in conclusion) but do not overuse them — one per paragraph is enough.

#### Speaking: discussing and comparing

Use language function expressions to introduce comparison: as for, regarding, with respect to, compared to, in contrast, whereas, similarly, on the other hand, just as, likewise. Avoid choppy lists — connect ideas with these expressions to show range.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where Unit 7 appears across the terminal English paper

- **Reading comprehension items:** an interview or article on ageing, longevity, sleep, or retirement, with vocabulary items drawn from the unit.
- **Grammar items:** a reported-speech transformation exercise (direct → reported), possibly with wh-questions and yes/no questions.
- **Writing item:** an argumentative article on a related topic (e.g. "Should retirement age be raised?", "Is longevity always a blessing?").
- **Vocabulary items:** phrasal verbs with make/do, theme vocabulary in context.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Tenses shift back two steps in reported speech." No — they shift back one step.
- "All modals shift in reported speech." Must → had to, but "had better" stays "had better"; would, could, might, should, ought to do not shift.
- "Make and do are interchangeable." No — the collocations are fixed in standard English.

#### Exam technique

- For reported-speech transformations, write the reporting clause (She said...) before the reported clause — the reporting clause is the method mark.
- For argumentative articles, plan before writing — outline the four paragraphs and the evidence for each, then write.
- For vocabulary items, read the surrounding context — many make/do items have an unambiguous collocation partner in context.
