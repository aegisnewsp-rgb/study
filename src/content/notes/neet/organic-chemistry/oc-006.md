# Amines

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Amines are **organic derivatives of ammonia (NH₃)** in which one or more N–H hydrogens are replaced by alkyl or aryl groups. They are classified as **primary (1°, RNH₂), secondary (2°, R₂NH), and tertiary (3°, R₃N)** based on how many carbon groups sit on nitrogen — not by what is bonded to carbon. The nitrogen is **sp³-hybridised** with a lone pair, which is why amines act as **Lewis bases and nucleophiles**.

#### Must-know facts
- **Basic strength in water:** (CH₃)₂NH > CH₃NH₂ > (CH₃)₃N > NH₃ > C₆H₅NH₂.
- **Aniline Kb ≈ 4.0 × 10⁻¹⁰**, far weaker than alkylamines because the lone pair delocalises into the benzene ring.
- **Carbylamine test** (CHCl₃ + KOH, heat) → foul-smelling isocyanide (R–NC). **Specific to 1° amines only.**
- **Diazotisation:** ArNH₂ + NaNO₂ + 2 HCl at **0–5 °C** → ArN₂⁺Cl⁻. Above 5 °C, the diazonium salt decomposes to phenol + N₂.

#### High-yield pointers
- Hofmann bromamide gives a **1° amine with one fewer carbon** than the starting amide.
- Hinsberg test distinguishes 1°/2°/3° using benzenesulphonyl chloride.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

Amines are central to NEET's Organic Chemistry because they sit at the crossroads of **basicity, nucleophilicity, and named reactions** that examiners love to combine into single MCQs.

#### Structure and classification

The nitrogen in an amine is **sp³-hybridised** with three σ-bonds and one lone pair. Because lone-pair–bond-pair repulsion is greater than bond-pair–bond-pair repulsion, the **C–N–C bond angle is ≈ 108°**, slightly less than the regular tetrahedral 109.5°. The count of R-groups on N decides 1°/2°/3° — quaternary salts R₄N⁺X⁻ are a separate category and carry no N–H.

#### Basicity and the role of solvation

Amines accept a proton on the lone pair:

> Kb = [RNH₃⁺][OH⁻] / [RNH₂], and **pKa (RNH₃⁺) + pKb (RNH₂) = 14** at 25 °C.

In aqueous solution the order **(CH₃)₂NH > CH₃NH₂ > (CH₃)₃N > NH₃ > C₆H₅NH₂** reflects a balance of **+I induction, steric hindrance, and solvation of the conjugate acid**. Trimethylamine, despite having three electron-donating methyls, is *less* basic than dimethylamine because three bulky groups hinder protonation and the N⁺–H cannot hydrogen-bond with water as effectively.

| Factor | Effect on basicity of aniline |
|---|---|
| –NH₂, –OCH₃, –CH₃ on ring | Increases (donate electrons into ring) |
| –NO₂, –CN, –COOH, –X on ring | Decreases (withdraw electrons; –NO₂ at *ortho/para* is strongest) |

#### Key preparative reactions

- **Hofmann bromamide:** R–CO–NH₂ + Br₂ + 4 NaOH → **R–NH₂** + Na₂CO₃ + 2 NaBr + 2 H₂O (one-carbon degradation).
- **Gabriel synthesis:** phthalimide + KOH + R–X → N-alkyl phthalimide → hydrolysis gives pure 1° amine (no over-alkylation).
- **Reduction of nitro compounds, nitriles, amides, oximes** all give amines; choice of reagent (Sn/HCl, LiAlH₄, H₂/catalyst) depends on the substrate.

#### Behaviour with nitrous acid — the classic trap

> 1° aliphatic amine + HNO₂ → alcohol + N₂ (with rearrangement).
> 1° aromatic amine + HNO₂ at 0–5 °C → **diazonium salt**; 2° amine → **N-nitrosamine** (yellow oil); 3° amine → no useful product below 5 °C.

NEET regularly asks the **carbylamine** (only 1°) and **Hinsberg** (1° soluble in alkali, 2° insoluble, 3° no reaction) tests — memorise the distinctions, not the prose around them.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Mechanism spotlight — Hofmann bromamide

The amide is **N-brominated** by Br₂ in base to give R–CO–NHBr. A hydroxide deprotonates it to **R–CO–N⁻Br**, which undergoes **loss of Br⁻** to form a **nitrene-type acyl nitrene** (R–CO–N:). This rearranges by **1,2-shift of R from C to N**, producing an isocyanate **R–N=C=O**, which is hydrolysed by base to the carbamate and finally decarboxylated to **R–NH₂**. Because the migrating R retains its configuration (for chiral centres on the migrating group), Hofmann is a reliable **stereospecific** degradation.

#### Aromatic amine reactivity — resonance and coupling

Aniline's lone pair conjugates with the ring, raising electron density at *ortho* and *para*. This explains why **aniline undergoes electrophilic substitution faster than benzene**, but the –NH₂ must be **protected by acetylation** (to acetanilide) before nitration/sulphonation; otherwise the ring is oxidised and polysubstituted.

The **diazonium salt ArN₂⁺** is a versatile electrophile. Coupling with **phenols (weakly basic medium)** or **aromatic amines (slightly acidic)** produces **azo compounds** — the chromophore behind many dyes and pH indicators (methyl orange from sulphanilic acid + N,N-dimethylaniline).

#### Cross-links to adjacent topics

- Amines connect to **amides** (Hofmann start-point) and to **carboxylic acids** (Hofmann is the reverse of amide formation).
- **Basicity of amines vs. amides:** amides are far weaker bases because the lone pair is tied up in resonance with the C=O.
- **Zwitterions** appear when an amino acid's –NH₂ is protonated and –COOH is deprotonated; the pI is the average of the two relevant pKa values.

#### Common mistakes examiners exploit

- Counting **alkyl groups on C** (alcohol classification) instead of **on N** when naming 1°/2°/3° amines.
- Assuming **3° > 2° > 1° basicity in water** — true only in gas phase; aqueous order inverts for dimethyl vs trimethylamine.
- Writing the carbylamine product as **R–CN** (nitrile) instead of **R–NC** (isocyanide).
- Forgetting that **diazotisation must be done at 0–5 °C** — above this, the diazonium salt hydrolyses to phenol.

#### Practice prompts

1. Arrange in increasing order of basicity in aqueous solution: aniline, *p*-nitroaniline, *p*-toluidine, cyclohexylamine. Justify using +I, –I, and resonance arguments.
2. Starting from acetanilide, outline a synthesis of *p*-nitroaniline, specifying why acetylation precedes nitration and what reagent removes the acetyl group at the end.
