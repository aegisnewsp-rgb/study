# Pakistan Internal Security

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Pakistan Internal Security covers the **laws, institutions, and operational mechanisms** safeguarding the federation against militancy, sectarianism, separatism, cybercrime, and externally-sponsored threats. The framework pivots on the **Constitution (Arts. 245–250)**, the **Anti-Terrorism Act 1997**, the **National Action Plan (NAP) 2014**, and the **Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act (PECA) 2016**.

| Pillar | Lead Institution |
| --- | --- |
| Counter-terrorism coordination | **NACTA** |
| Domestic intelligence | **Intelligence Bureau (IB)** |
| Military intelligence | **ISI / MI** |
| Border/area security | **Frontier Corps, Rangers** |
| Provincial investigation | **CTD (under Home Dept)** |

- Apex CT coordinator is **NACTA**, not the ISI; NACTA plans, monitors, and evaluates.
- Post-2014 reforms followed the **APS Peshawar attack** (16 Dec 2014); **Operation Zarb-e-Azb** and **Radd-ul-Fasaad** reshaped CT architecture.
- Pakistan exited the **FATF grey list in October 2022** after CFT/AML reforms.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Constitutional and Legal Foundation

Articles **245–250** of the Constitution empower the federal government to deploy armed forces "in aid of civil power" — the doctrinal basis for military operations inside Pakistan. The **Anti-Terrorism Act (ATA) 1997** established ATCs (Anti-Terrorism Courts) and defined terror financing, while **PECA 2016** criminalised cyber-terrorism, electronic forgery, and hate speech. The **NACTA Act 2013** operationalised NACTA as the apex civilian CT authority under the Interior Ministry.

#### Threat Spectrum Tested in LAT

LAT questions focus on identifying the **agency mandated to counter each threat**, not operational tactics.

| Threat Category | Designated Counter | Statutory Hook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Militant outfits (TTP, ISIS-K) | CTD + NACTA | ATA 1997 + NAP 2014 |
| Sectarian violence (SSP, LeJ) | CTD + NACTA | NAP 2014 (point 5) |
| Ethno-nationalist insurgency (BLA) | FC Balochistan + Levies | Art. 245 deployment |
| Cyber offences | FIA Cybercrime Wing | PECA 2016 |
| Terror financing | SBP / FMU / NACTA | AML Act 2010, FATF standards |

#### Federal–Provincial Coordination

The **18th Amendment (2010)** devolved policing to provinces, so Home Departments now run provincial CTDs. Federal agencies (IB, ISI, NACTA) coordinate through **Article 147** inter-provincial mechanisms and joint intelligence committees. A frequent LAT trap is assuming top-down command still exists.

- Memorise **NACTA ≠ ISI**; NACTA plans, ISI gathers intel.
- **FC** is provincial paramilitary under Interior Ministry, not a regular Army unit.
- **FATF compliance** is an internal-security deliverable, not merely a finance issue.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Hybrid Threats and Reforms Trajectory

The **National Internal Security Policy (NISP) 2014–2018** first integrated cyber, financial, and ideological counters. After APS Peshawar, the **21st and 22nd Constitutional Amendments** (2015) created **military courts for terrorism cases** with sunset clauses. The 26th Amendment later reshaped judicial appointments — a likely LAT flashpoint. **Zarb-e-Azb (2014–2017)** dismantled terrorist infrastructure in North Waziristan; **Radd-ul-Fasaad (2017+)** extended counter-terror sweeps nationwide.

#### Border and Kashmir Dimension

- **Durand Line (Pak-Afghan):** managed by **FCKP**; TTP sanctuaries remained a chronic friction point with Kabul.
- **Pak-Iran border:** **FC Balochistan** with mandated anti-smuggling patrols.
- **Line of Control (AJK):** regular Army formations; Rangers cover Sindh–Punjab internal lines.
