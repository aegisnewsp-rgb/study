# Tenses and Voice

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your LAT English MCQs.

**Tenses** mark the time of a verb's action across three times (Present, Past, Future) and four aspects (Simple, Continuous, Perfect, Perfect Continuous), giving **12 tense forms**. **Voice** shows whether the subject performs the action (**Active Voice**) or receives it (**Passive Voice**). For LAT, four question types dominate: fill-in-the-blank tense, sentence correction, active↔passive conversion, and identifying passive constructions in comprehension passages.

- **12 tenses** = 3 times × 4 aspects (Simple, Continuous, Perfect, Perfect Continuous).
- **Active:** Subject + V1 (+ Object) → *"The advocate filed the petition."*
- **Passive:** Object + be + V3 (+ by Agent) → *"The petition was filed by the advocate."*
- **Stative verbs** (know, own, believe) reject continuous forms — a frequent LAT trap.
- **Passive requires a transitive verb** — intransitive verbs (happen, occur) cannot be passivised.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### The 12-Tense Framework

Every English verb sits on two axes: **TIME** (present, past, future) and **ASPECT** (simple, continuous, perfect, perfect continuous). Aspect determines whether the action is a habit, ongoing, completed, or ongoing-until-completed. Continuous uses **be + V-ing**; perfect uses **have + V3**; perfect continuous combines **have + been + V-ing**.

#### Active vs Passive Structure

Active voice keeps the doer as the subject. Passive voice promotes the old object to subject position, assisted by a form of *be* plus the past participle (V3). Only **transitive verbs** (those taking a direct object) can be passivised. The agent — the doer — is introduced by *by* and is optional when the doer is obvious, unknown, or irrelevant.

#### Active-to-Passive Conversion Rules

| Tense (Active) | Passive Form |
| --- | --- |
| Simple Present | is/am/are + V3 |
| Present Continuous | is/am/are + being + V3 |
| Simple Past | was/were + V3 |
| Present Perfect | has/have + been + V3 |
| Future (will) | will + be + V3 |
| Modal (can/should/must) | modal + be + V3 |

#### Exam Patterns and Common Traps

- **Sequence of tenses** in reported speech: present back-shifts to past, past to past perfect, unless reporting a permanent truth.
- **Stative verbs** (know, own, resemble, believe) never take *-ing*.
- **Imperative passive**: *Let + object + be + V3* — *"Let the witness be cross-examined."*
- **Tense consistency** inside one paragraph; LAT passages often test an unjustified shift from past to present.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases in Passive Transformation

Not every *be + V3* string is a true passive. The construction *"He was amused"* contains a past participle acting as an **adjective**, not a passive verb — no agent can be inserted, and no action was performed *on* him by an external doer. LAT examiners exploit this by pairing adjectives with true passives in sentence-correction items. Similarly, **linking verbs** (seem, become, remain) followed by V3 are adjectival, not passive.

#### Forms That Frequently Mislead Candidates

- **Infinitive passive:** *to be + V3* → *"The judge wants the evidence to be presented."*
- **Gerund passive:** *being + V3* → *"He objected to being interrogated."*
- **Causative passive:** *have + Object + V3* → *"She had the affidavit notarised."*
- **Future-perfect passive:** *will + have + been + V3* → *"The verdict will have been delivered by noon."*

#### Legal-English Connection

Pakistani legal drafting is overwhelmingly passive because the actor is often the system rather than a person: *"The accused was acquitted,"* *"Notice is hereby issued,"* *"The statute was amended in 2020."* Recognising this register speeds up LAT comprehension reading, where roughly **60–70% of judicial sentences** are passive. Mastering the transformation rules also lets you rephrase confusing active judges into clean passive legal forms.

#### Practice Prompts

1. Convert to passive, keeping the tense: *"The High Court has dismissed the appeal."* → *The appeal has been dismissed by the High Court.*
2. Spot the error: *"The accident was happened on the motorway."* → *Happen* is intransitive; no passive exists. Rewrite as *"The accident happened on the motorway."*
