# Cellular and Molecular Biology — MCAT Bio/Biochem

Cellular and molecular biology form the bulk of AAMC Foundational Concept 2 of the Bio/Biochem section. The MCAT tests eukaryotic and prokaryotic cell structure, membrane transport, the cytoskeleton, organelles, cell signalling, the cell cycle, mitosis and meiosis, and the molecular biology of the cell. These topics underpin almost every physiology and genetics passage on the exam.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### Core concepts

- **Cell structure:** eukaryotes have a membrane-bound nucleus and organelles; prokaryotes do not.
- **Plasma membrane:** phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins. The fluid mosaic model.
- **Membrane transport:** passive (diffusion, facilitated diffusion, osmosis — no ATP); active (primary and secondary — uses ATP or a gradient).
- **Organelles:** nucleus (DNA), mitochondria (ATP production), ribosomes (protein synthesis), ER (rough = protein synthesis; smooth = lipid synthesis), Golgi (modification, packaging), lysosomes (digestion).
- **Cell cycle:** G1, S, G2, M. Regulated by cyclins and CDKs.
- **Mitosis:** one division, two diploid daughter cells. Stages: prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase.
- **Meiosis:** two divisions, four haploid daughter cells. Generates genetic diversity via crossing over and independent assortment.

#### One-pass drill

1. Identify the topic: cell structure, transport, organelle, or division.
2. Recall the relevant rule or feature.
3. Apply it to the specific cell or scenario.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing facilitated diffusion with active transport. Facilitated diffusion is passive (no ATP); active transport uses ATP or a gradient.
- Forgetting that prokaryotes have ribosomes (70S, smaller than eukaryotic 80S).
- Treating mitosis and meiosis as the same. Mitosis = 2 diploid cells; meiosis = 4 haploid cells.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Eukaryotic cell structure

The MCAT tests the structure and function of:

- **Nucleus:** houses DNA; site of transcription and ribosome assembly.
- **Mitochondria:** site of oxidative phosphorylation; double membrane; own DNA (maternal inheritance).
- **Ribosomes:** site of protein synthesis. Free ribosomes make cytosolic proteins; bound ribosomes (on rough ER) make secreted proteins.
- **Endoplasmic reticulum:** rough ER (ribosomes, protein synthesis, folding); smooth ER (lipid synthesis, detoxification, calcium storage).
- **Golgi apparatus:** modifies, sorts, and packages proteins for secretion or use within the cell.
- **Lysosomes:** contain hydrolytic enzymes; digest cellular waste and foreign material.
- **Peroxisomes:** contain oxidative enzymes; break down fatty acids and detoxify harmful compounds.

#### Membrane transport

- **Passive transport:** no ATP required. Diffusion (small, nonpolar molecules); facilitated diffusion (ions and polar molecules via channel or carrier proteins); osmosis (water across a semipermeable membrane).
- **Active transport:** requires ATP or a gradient. Primary active transport uses ATP directly (Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase); secondary active transport uses a gradient (e.g., glucose-Na⁺ cotransport).
- **Bulk transport:** endocytosis (into the cell); exocytosis (out of the cell). Phagocytosis (large particles); pinocytosis (fluid and dissolved solutes).

#### Cell signalling

The MCAT tests:

- **Receptors:** G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs); receptor tyrosine kinases (RTKs); ligand-gated ion channels.
- **Second messengers:** cAMP, IP₃, DAG, Ca²⁺.
- **Signal amplification:** one ligand binding can activate many downstream effectors.

#### Cell cycle and division

**Cell cycle:** G1 (growth), S (DNA replication), G2 (growth and preparation for mitosis), M (mitosis). Regulated by cyclins and cyclin-dependent kinases (CDKs). Checkpoints ensure proper progression.

**Mitosis:** prophase (chromosomes condense), metaphase (chromosomes align at the equator), anaphase (sister chromatids separate), telophase (nuclei reform). Cytokinesis divides the cytoplasm. Result: two genetically identical diploid cells.

**Meiosis:** meiosis I separates homologous chromosomes; meiosis II separates sister chromatids. Result: four genetically distinct haploid cells. Crossing over (prophase I) and independent assortment (metaphase I) generate genetic diversity.

#### Practice routine

- Take 5 cell structure items. Identify the organelle and its function.
- Take 5 membrane transport items. Distinguish passive from active; identify the transported molecule.
- Take 5 cell signalling items. Identify the receptor type and the second messenger.
- Take 5 cell cycle items. Identify the phase and the events.
- Take 5 mitosis/meiosis items. Compare the two processes.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How this topic connects to the other Bio/Biochem topics

- **Genetics** depends on the cell cycle (DNA replication in S phase) and on mitosis/meiosis (chromosome segregation).
- **Metabolism** depends on mitochondria (oxidative phosphorylation) and on enzymes in the cytosol.
- **Physiology** depends on cell signalling (hormones, neurotransmitters) and on membrane transport.

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"Active transport always uses ATP."** Primary active transport uses ATP directly; secondary active transport uses a gradient established by primary active transport.
- **"Mitochondria are only in muscle cells."** All eukaryotic cells (except mature red blood cells) have mitochondria.
- **"Crossing over occurs in mitosis."** Crossing over occurs in prophase I of meiosis, not in mitosis.

#### Exam technique

- For cell structure items, identify the organelle by its features (double membrane, ribosomes, etc.).
- For transport items, identify the molecule (small nonpolar, polar, ion, water) and the direction.
- For cell signalling items, identify the receptor type (GPCR, RTK, ion channel) and the downstream pathway.
- For cell cycle items, identify the phase and the regulatory proteins.
- Use the platform's calculator.
