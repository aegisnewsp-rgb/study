# I. Cells and Molecules of Life — HKDSE Biology Notes

This topic covers Topic I of the Compulsory Part of HKDSE Biology. It addresses the molecules of life (water, carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, nucleic acids); cellular organisation (prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells, organelles); movement of substances across membranes (diffusion, osmosis, active transport, endocytosis, exocytosis); cell cycle and division (mitosis, meiosis); and cellular energetics (photosynthesis, respiration, enzymes). This is one of the four Compulsory Part topics and is tested on both Paper 1 Section A (MCQ) and Section B (structured/essay).

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Molecules of life**: water (solvent, hydrogen bonding); carbohydrates (mono-, di-, polysaccharides, glycosidic bonds); lipids (triglycerides, phospholipids, steroids); proteins (amino acids, peptide bonds, primary/secondary/tertiary/quaternary structure); nucleic acids (DNA, RNA, nucleotides, base pairing).
- **Cellular organisation**: prokaryotic (bacteria, archaea — no membrane-bound nucleus); eukaryotic (plant, animal, fungal cells — membrane-bound nucleus and organelles). Key organelles: nucleus, mitochondria, chloroplasts, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, ribosomes, lysosomes, vacuoles.
- **Movement across membranes**: passive (diffusion, facilitated diffusion, osmosis — down the concentration gradient); active (active transport, endocytosis, exocytosis — against the gradient, requires energy).
- **Cell cycle**: interphase (G1, S, G2), mitosis (prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase), cytokinesis. Mitosis produces two genetically identical daughter cells; meiosis produces four genetically distinct haploid cells.
- **Cellular energetics**: photosynthesis (light-dependent and light-independent reactions, in chloroplasts); cellular respiration (glycolysis, Krebs cycle, oxidative phosphorylation, in cytoplasm and mitochondria); enzymes (proteins that catalyse reactions, lowered activation energy, affected by temperature and pH).

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing photosynthesis and respiration — photosynthesis stores energy in glucose; respiration releases energy from glucose.
- Forgetting that DNA replication is semiconservative — each new DNA molecule contains one original strand and one new strand.
- Treating osmosis as just "diffusion of water" — osmosis is the net movement of water across a selectively permeable membrane.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Molecules of life

**Water**: polar molecule with a partial positive charge on hydrogen and a partial negative charge on oxygen. Hydrogen bonding gives water its high boiling point, high specific heat capacity, surface tension and solvent properties.

**Carbohydrates**: empirical formula (CH₂O)ₙ. Monosaccharides (glucose, fructose, galactose) are reducing sugars. Disaccharides (maltose, sucrose, lactose) are formed by glycosidic bonds. Polysaccharides (starch in plants, glycogen in animals, cellulose in plant cell walls) are storage or structural molecules.

**Lipids**: triglycerides (glycerol + three fatty acids), phospholipids (phosphate head + two fatty acid tails — the basis of cell membranes), steroids (cholesterol, steroid hormones).

**Proteins**: 20 amino acids join by peptide bonds to form polypeptides. Primary structure is the linear sequence; secondary structure is the α-helix or β-pleated sheet held by hydrogen bonds; tertiary structure is the 3D folding held by disulfide bridges, hydrophobic interactions, hydrogen bonds and ionic bonds; quaternary structure is the assembly of multiple polypeptide subunits (e.g. haemoglobin).

**Nucleic acids**: DNA (deoxyribose, A-T, G-C base pairing, double helix) and RNA (ribose, A-U, G-C, single-stranded). Nucleotides join by phosphodiester bonds.

#### Cellular organisation

**Prokaryotic cells**: no membrane-bound nucleus; DNA is circular and located in the nucleoid; ribosomes are 70S; cell wall contains peptidoglycan (in bacteria). Examples: bacteria, cyanobacteria.

**Eukaryotic cells**: membrane-bound nucleus containing linear DNA; membrane-bound organelles; ribosomes are 80S. Plant cells have cell walls (cellulose), large central vacuoles and chloroplasts; animal cells lack these but have centrioles.

**Organelles and their functions**:

- **Nucleus**: contains DNA, controls cell activities, site of transcription.
- **Mitochondria**: site of aerobic respiration, double membrane, own DNA.
- **Chloroplasts**: site of photosynthesis, double membrane, thylakoids, own DNA.
- **Endoplasmic reticulum**: rough (with ribosomes, site of protein synthesis), smooth (lipid synthesis).
- **Golgi apparatus**: modifies, sorts and packages proteins.
- **Ribosomes**: site of protein synthesis.
- **Lysosomes**: contain digestive enzymes, breakdown of waste.
- **Vacuoles**: storage (large central vacuole in plant cells maintains turgor).

#### Movement across membranes

**Diffusion**: net movement of particles down a concentration gradient. No energy required.

**Facilitated diffusion**: net movement through a channel or carrier protein. No energy required.

**Osmosis**: net movement of water across a selectively permeable membrane down a water potential gradient.

**Active transport**: movement of particles against a concentration gradient using ATP. Requires specific carrier proteins.

**Endocytosis**: engulfing of large particles by the cell membrane forming a vesicle. Includes phagocytosis ("cell eating") and pinocytosis ("cell drinking").

**Exocytosis**: release of materials from a cell by vesicle fusion with the plasma membrane.

#### Cell cycle and division

The cell cycle has three phases:

- **Interphase**: G1 (cell growth), S (DNA replication), G2 (preparation for mitosis).
- **Mitosis**: prophase (chromosomes condense, nuclear envelope breaks down), metaphase (chromosomes align at the equator), anaphase (sister chromatids separate), telophase (nuclear envelopes reform), followed by cytokinesis.
- **G0**: quiescent phase.

Meiosis has two divisions: meiosis I (homologous chromosomes separate, reductional division) and meiosis II (sister chromatids separate, equational division). Meiosis produces four genetically distinct haploid cells — the basis of gametogenesis.

#### Cellular energetics

**Photosynthesis**: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂.

- Light-dependent reactions (in thylakoid membranes): water is split, oxygen is released, ATP and NADPH are produced.
- Light-independent reactions / Calvin cycle (in stroma): CO₂ is fixed using ATP and NADPH to produce G3P and ultimately glucose.

**Cellular respiration**: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + ATP.

- Glycolysis (in cytoplasm): glucose → 2 pyruvate + 2 ATP + 2 NADH.
- Krebs cycle (in mitochondrial matrix): pyruvate → CO₂ + ATP + NADH + FADH₂.
- Oxidative phosphorylation (in inner mitochondrial membrane): NADH and FADH₂ → ATP via the electron transport chain and chemiosmosis.

**Enzymes**: biological catalysts, mostly proteins. Lower the activation energy. Affected by temperature (optimum around body temperature for human enzymes), pH, substrate concentration, enzyme concentration, and inhibitors (competitive or non-competitive).

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-topic links

- **II. Genetics and Evolution**: DNA structure and replication are the foundations of inheritance.
- **III. Organisms and Environment**: cellular respiration connects to energy flow in ecosystems; photosynthesis connects to producers.
- **IV. Health and Diseases**: immunology and disease defence depend on the cellular machinery of this topic.

#### Exam technique

- **For diagram questions**: label every structure; examiners reward specific labels over general descriptions.
- **For "compare" questions**: use a table with two columns to lay out the differences clearly (e.g. mitosis vs. meiosis, prokaryotic vs. eukaryotic cells).
- **For process questions**: state the inputs and outputs explicitly before describing the process (e.g. "Photosynthesis converts CO₂ and H₂O into glucose and O₂, using light energy...").
- **For enzyme questions**: state the mode of action (lowers activation energy, substrate-specific active site) and the factors affecting rate.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "All bacteria are harmful." Many bacteria are beneficial (gut flora, nitrogen fixation, decomposition).
- "ATP is energy." ATP is an energy carrier — it stores energy in its high-energy phosphate bonds and releases it when hydrolyzed.
- "Mitochondria are the only sites of ATP production." Glycolysis also produces ATP, in the cytoplasm.
