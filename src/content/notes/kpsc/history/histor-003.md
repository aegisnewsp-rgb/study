# Modern Indian History

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your KPSC KAS prelims.

Modern Indian History spans roughly 1600–1947, beginning with European trading companies (Portuguese, Dutch, French, British) and ending with the Indian Independence Act 1947. For KAS, the spine of the syllabus is: **Battle of Plassey (1757) → Buxar (1764) → Diwani (1765) → 1857 Revolt → Crown Rule (1858) → Gandhian Mass Movements → Partition and Independence (1947) → Integration of Princely States (1947–49)**.

Three high-yield pointers for the prelims:

- **Acts chronology**: Regulating Act 1773 → Pitt's India Act 1784 → Charter Acts (1813, 1833, 1853) → GOI Act 1858 → Morley-Minto 1909 → GOI Act 1919 → GOI Act 1935.
- **Movement chronology**: Non-Cooperation (1920–22), Civil Disobedience (1930–34), Quit India (1942).
- **Karnataka linkages**: Anglo-Mysore Wars, Kittur Rani Chennamma (1824), Belgaum Congress Session (1924), Karnataka Unification Movement.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months before KAS.

#### British East India Company and Conquest
The British East India Company (chartered 1600) established coastal factories at Surat, Madras and Bombay. Political supremacy began with the Battle of Plassey (1757) and consolidated at Buxar (1764), after which the **Diwani of Bengal (1765)** was granted to the Company by Mughal Emperor Shah Alam II. Dual Administration (1765–72) followed under Robert Clive, with Diwani to the Company and Nizamat to the Nawab.

#### Administrative Acts and Land Revenue
Parliamentary control emerged through the **Regulating Act 1773** (first Governor-General: Warren Hastings) and **Pitt's India Act 1784** (Board of Control). Three land settlement systems were introduced: **Permanent Settlement (1793, Bengal/Bihar/Orissa)** by Cornwallis, **Ryotwari (Madras, Bombay)** by Thomas Munro, and **Mahalwari (North-Western Provinces)**.

#### 1857 Revolt and Crown Rule
The Revolt of 1857 had military, political, religious and economic causes; centres included Meerut, Delhi, Lucknow, Kanpur and Jhansi. After suppression, the **Government of India Act 1858** abolished Company rule, transferring power to the Crown, and Queen Victoria's Proclamation was issued. The Governor-General then also held the title of Viceroy.

#### Reform Movements and Early Nationalism
**Brahmo Samaj (1828)** by Raja Ram Mohan Roy, **Prarthana Samaj (1867)** by Justice M.G. Ranade, and **Arya Samaj (1875)** by Swami Dayanand Saraswati attacked caste rigidity and idolatry. The **Indian National Congress (1885, Bombay)** was founded by A.O. Hume. The **Surat Split (1907)** divided Moderates (Gokhale, Naoroji) and Extremists (Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal, Lala Lajpat Rai).

#### Gandhian Era and Constitutional Reforms
Gandhi launched **Champaran (1917)** and **Kheda (1918)** Satyagrahas. The **Rowlatt Act (1919)** and Jallianwala Bagh led to the **Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–22)**. The **Salt March / Dandi March (1930)** inaugurated Civil Disobedience. The **Round Table Conferences (1930–32)** preceded the **GOI Act 1935**. The **Cripps Mission (1942)**, **Wavell Plan (1945)**, **Cabinet Mission (1946)** and **Mountbatten Plan (3 June 1947)** shaped the transfer of power.

#### Karnataka-Specific History
The **Anglo-Mysore Wars (1767–1799)** ended Tipu Sultan's rule. Early resistance included **Kittur Rani Chennamma (1824)** and **Sangolli Rayanna (1829)**. The **Karnataka Ekikarana Movement** was led by Alur Venkat Rao, N.S. Hardikar and others. The **Belgaum Congress Session (1924)** was presided over by Mahatma Gandhi.

> **Tip:** In KAS prelims, MCQs on Acts and Committees frequently confuse Cripps (1942) with Cabinet Mission (1946). Remember Cripps = during WWII; Cabinet Mission = post-war failure.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students with months ahead, including Karnataka mains depth.

#### Edge Cases and Conceptual Traps
The terms **Governor-General** and **Viceroy** are not synonymous. Warren Hastings, Cornwallis, Wellesley, Dalhousie and Canning held the Governor-General title; only after the GOI Act 1858 did Canning simultaneously hold the Viceroy title. Likewise, the **Doctrine of Lapse** is Dalhousie (1848–56), not Dalhousie's permanent settlement — that was Cornwallis. The **Communal Award (1932)** by Ramsay MacDonald reserved separate electorates for Depressed Classes; **Gandhi's fast** led to the **Poona Pact (1932)** with B.R. Ambedkar, replacing separate electorates with reserved seats.

#### Revolutionary Nationalism and INA
**India House (London)** under Shyamji Krishna Varma, **Anushilan Samiti** and **Yugantar** carried out armed revolutionary acts. The **Kakori Conspiracy (1925)** and **Lahore Conspiracy (1930)** ended with the executions of Bhagat Singh, Rajguru and Sukhdev. The **INA (Azad Hind Fauj)** was first raised by Captain Mohan Singh (1942) with Japanese support and reorganised by **Subhas Chandra Bose (1943)** at Singapore. The **RIN Mutiny (1946)** hastened British withdrawal.

#### Karnataka Mains Layer
KAS Mains and Interview expect granular Karnataka coverage beyond mainstream national events. **Kittur (1824)** was an early princely-state revolt by Rani Chennamma against the Doctrine of Lapse. **Bedara Kannappa** and **Sangolli Rayanna** represent folk-roots resistance. The **Karnataka Vidyapeeth (Dharwad, 1944)** and the **Kannada Sahitya Sammelana** supported linguistic identity. The **Alur Venkat Rao-led Karnataka Ekikarana** publication and the **Mysore Chalo movement** culminated in the States Reorganisation Act (1956).

#### Exam Strategy and Weightage
History carries 3% explicit weight in the KAS scheme, but Modern Indian History overlaps with Polity, Economy and Karnataka-specific sections, raising effective weight to 10–12% in Mains. Allocate 15–20 minutes per question in Mains, prioritising clarity of cause–event–outcome triplets.

| Mistake | Correction |
| --- | --- |
| Permanent Settlement → Dalhousie | Lord Cornwallis, 1793 |
| Surat Split → 1905 | Surat Split is 1907 |
| Quit India ≡ CDM | Quit India 1942; CDM 1930–34 |
| Cabinet Mission = Cripps | Cabinet Mission 1946; Cripps 1942 |
| INA = only Bose | Raised by Mohan Singh (1942); reorganised by Bose (1943) |

> **Strategy box:** For KAS Mains, write each movement in a 3-paragraph frame: (1) causes, (2) course/events, (3) outcome and significance. Adding one Karnataka link per answer consistently scores above average.
