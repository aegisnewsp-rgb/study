# Companies Act & Corporate Governance

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The **Companies Act 71 of 2008** (as amended) governs every profit and non-profit company registered in South Africa, while the **King IV Report (2016)** sets the parallel governance code. A company becomes a **separate juristic person** on CIPC registration under s13, shielded by the *Salomon* principle but open to veil-piercing only in fraud or sham situations.

- **Three company types (s8):** non-profit company (NPC); profit company — either **private (Pty) Ltd** or **public**; plus state-owned and personal-liability variants.
- **Directors' duties (s76):** act in good faith, for a proper purpose, in the **best interests of the company**, with reasonable care, skill and diligence.
- **Solvency & Liquidity Test (s4):** forward-looking 12-month liquidity **plus** balance-sheet solvency — both must pass.

> Exam tip: the duty is owed to the **company**, never directly to shareholders — this is the most-tested single distinction.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### The Companies Act 71 of 2008 — Scope and Structure

The Act, read with the 2011 Regulations, replaced the 1973 Companies Act and introduces a flexible, principles-based framework. Registration with the **Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC)** under s13 brings the company into existence as a juristic person. From that moment the **Memorandum of Incorporation (MOI)** under s15 functions as the company's constitution, binding the company, its directors and its members.

#### Company Forms Under Section 8

| Form | Share capital? | Distribution to members? | Typical use |
|------|----------------|--------------------------|-------------|
| Non-profit company (NPC) | No | Forbidden | Section 21-style bodies, clubs, charities |
| Private (Pty) Ltd | Yes | Allowed | Closely-held businesses |
| Public company | Yes | Allowed | Listed and large unlisted entities |
| State-owned / personal liability | Varies | Varies | Public-interest vehicles |

#### Directors' Duties (s76) and Liability (s77)

Directors carry a **fiduciary duty** (loyalty, good faith, no conflict) and a **duty of care, skill and diligence** (objective standard of a reasonable person in the role). Breach triggering loss triggers **joint and several liability** under s77. Removal sits with shareholders under s71 by ordinary resolution unless the MOI provides otherwise.

#### Solvency, Liquidity and Business Rescue

The s4 test is two-pronged: assets ≥ liabilities **and** debts payable in the ordinary course over the next 12 months. A failed test bars distributions, share buy-backs and certain acquisitions. **Chapter 6 business rescue** is a rehabilitation procedure, not liquidation: appointment of a practitioner automatically suspends most creditor claims and shifts management control.

> Watch the trap: business rescue *rescues*; liquidation *ends* the company — examiners test this distinction directly.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Veil-Piercing and Separate Legal Personality

South African courts follow *Salomon v A Salomon & Co Ltd* (1897 AC 22) but apply the **alter ego doctrine** sparingly — confirmed in *Cape Pacific Ltd v Lubner Controlling Investments (Pty) Ltd* 1995 4 SA 790 (A). The veil is pierced only where the company is a **sham, façade or instrument of fraud**, never merely because a single shareholder dominates. For close corporations (now largely historic), the constitution could require personal liability; for companies, statutory personal liability arises only where the Act expressly provides.

#### King IV — "Apply and Explain"

King IV is **not legislation**. Listed issuers apply its 17 principles on an **apply-and-explain** basis in their integrated reports; unlisted entities adopt it voluntarily. The four outcomes are: **ethical culture, performance and value creation, adequate and effective control, legitimacy**. The board bears collective responsibility for ethical leadership; the audit committee and social-and-ethics committee (or combined committee) are mandatory governance organs in public and state-owned companies.

#### Worked Application — The Solvency Test

A (Pty) Ltd declares a R500,000 dividend. Its balance sheet shows total assets R2,000,000 against liabilities R1,400,000 (solvency margin R600,000). The directors then project cash-flow for the next 12 months and conclude a R150,000 shortfall in month 7 because of a lost client. **Result:** the **liquidity leg fails** even though solvency passes — the dividend is unlawful and each director voting for it assumes personal liability under s77.

#### Common Pitfalls and Adjacent Links

- Confusing s76 fiduciary duties with common-law delictual duties.
- Assuming NPC means "no profit" — an NPC may earn profit, but cannot **distribute** it.
- Overlooking that mandatory provisions (directors' duties, solvency) cannot be contracted out of via the MOI.
- Linking topic: this material interfaces with **insolvency law** (Chapter 6 vs liquidation under the Insolvency Act 24 of 1936) and **employment law** (directors as employees for tax purposes).

#### Practice Prompts

1. A director proposes a dividend despite projected cash strain within 9 months. Identify the statutory breach and the personal exposure of voting directors.
2. Distinguish, with section references, when a court will pierce the corporate veil versus when separate personality will be upheld.
