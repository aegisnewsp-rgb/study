# Urinary Tract Infections & Bacterial Meningitis

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Urinary tract infection (UTI) is microbial invasion of the urinary system, while bacterial meningitis is bacterial infection of the meninges, subarachnoid space, and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF). Both are diagnosed by combining clinical syndrome with microbiology of normally sterile fluid (urine or CSF).

- **UTI lead organism:** Uropathogenic *Escherichia coli* (UPEC), followed by *Klebsiella*, *Proteus*, *Enterococcus*, and *Staphylococcus saprophyticus* (young women).
- **Meningitis organisms by age:** Neonates — Group B *Streptococcus*, *E. coli*, *Listeria*; 1 month–50 years — *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Neisseria meningitidis*; >50 years/immunocompromised — add *Listeria monocytogenes* and Gram-negative bacilli.
- **Pyelonephritis triad:** Fever, flank pain, costovertebral-angle tenderness — distinguish from lower UTI (dysuria, frequency, urgency, suprapubic pain).
- **CSF of acute bacterial meningitis:** ↑ opening pressure, neutrophilic pleocytosis, ↑ protein, ↓ glucose, positive Gram stain/culture.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Pathogenesis of UTI
Most UTIs begin by **ascending colonization**: periurethral flora colonizes the urethra, ascends to the bladder (cystitis), and can ascend the ureters to the kidney (pyelonephritis). UPEC expresses **P fimbriae** that bind Gal(α1-4)Gal receptors of the uroepithelium, plus siderophores, hemolysins, and capsule for immune evasion. Hematogenous seeding is rare and in most keys from *S. aureus* bacteremia.

#### Pathogenesis of Bacterial Meningitis
Colonization of the nasopharynx by *S. pneumoniae* or *N. meningitidis* is followed by bacteremia, traversal of the blood–brain barrier, and replication in the subarachnoid space where low antibody and complement allow unchecked growth. Direct spread occurs from sinusitis, otitis media, mastoiditis, skull fracture, neurosurgery, or CSF shunts.

#### Distinguishing Lower vs Upper UTI and Uncomplicated vs Complicated

| Feature | Lower UTI (cystitis) | Upper UTI (pyelonephritis) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Site | Bladder/urethra | Renal pelvis/parenchyma |
| Symptoms | Dysuria, frequency, urgency, suprapubic pain | Fever, chills, flank pain, nausea/vomiting, CVA tenderness |
| Urinalysis | Pyuria, ± bacteriuria, ± nitrites | Pyuria, bacteriuria, in many papers WBC casts |
| Typical cause | UPEC, *S. saprophyticus* | UPEC, *Proteus*, *Klebsiella* |

**Uncomplicated UTI** = healthy, non-pregnant adult with normal urinary tract. **Complicated UTI** = structural/functional abnormality, catheter, obstruction, pregnancy, immunocompromise, or male sex — requires culture and susceptibility testing.

#### CSF Profile in Meningitis

| Parameter | Bacterial | Viral | Tubercular/Fungal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening pressure | ↑↑ | Normal/↑ | ↑ |
| Cells | Neutrophilic pleocytosis (1000+) | Lymphocytic (10–500) | Lymphocytic + mixed |
| Protein | ↑↑ (>100 mg/dL) | Normal/↑ | ↑↑ |
| Glucose | ↓↓ (<40 mg/dL) | Normal | ↓ |
| Gram stain/culture | In many papers positive | Negative | AFB/India ink |

- Empiric antibiotics **must not wait** for LP results in suspected bacterial meningitis.
- Do LP only after excluding raised ICP, focal deficit, papilledema, severe immunocompromise, or local spinal infection.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Age-Based Empiric Therapy and Chemoprophylaxis

| Age/Risk | Likely pathogens | Empiric regimen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Neonate (0–1 mo) | GBS, *E. coli*, *Listeria* | Ampicillin + cefotaxime (or aminoglycoside) |
| 1 mo–50 yr | *S. pneumoniae*, *N. meningitidis* | Ceftriaxone + vancomycin |
| >50 yr / immunocompromised | Add *Listeria*, Gram-negatives | Ampicillin + ceftriaxone + vancomycin |

Dexamethasone is given **before or with** the first antibiotic dose in adult pneumococcal meningitis to reduce neurologic sequelae.

#### High-Yield Traps and Connections

- **Asymptomatic bacteriuria** (≥10⁵ CFU/mL without symptoms) is **not** treated except in pregnancy and pre-urological procedure.
- **Urease-positive** organisms (*Proteus*, *Klebsiella*, *Ureaplasma*, *S. saprophyticus*) hydrolyse urea → alkaline urine → **struvite (magnesium ammonium phosphate)** stones.
- **Catheter-associated UTI** is the commonest healthcare-associated infection; biofilm-formers like *Enterococcus*, *Candida*, and *Pseudomonas* dominate.
- **Meningococcal chemoprophylaxis** for close contacts: rifampicin, ciprofloxacin, or ceftriaxone single dose. Pneumococcal meningitis does **not** require routine prophylaxis.
- CSF **neutrophil predominance can persist** in early viral meningitis and in neonates with bacterial disease — interpret with glucose, protein, Gram stain, and clinical context.
- WBC casts in urine strongly suggest **pyelonephritis** (renal origin), not lower UTI.
- Pyuria alone is **not diagnostic** of UTI — it indicates inflammation, which can be from STI, vaginal contamination, or stones.

#### Practice Prompts

1. A 22-year-old sexually active woman has dysuria, urgency, and suprapubic pain without fever. Urinalysis shows pyuria and positive nitrites. What is the most likely organism and the appropriate empiric outpatient therapy?
2. A 65-year-old diabetic presents with fever, neck stiffness, and altered sensorium. Before LP, which antibiotic regimen covers *Listeria*, *S. pneumoniae*, and Gram-negative bacilli, and what contraindication must be ruled out before lumbar puncture?
