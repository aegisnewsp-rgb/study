# Leadership

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Leadership is the process of **influencing a group** to achieve shared objectives by guiding, motivating, and directing behaviour, sitting alongside Planning, Organising, and Controlling in the Koontz–Weihrich managerial function. In the RBI Grade B Phase-II Management paper, it carries roughly 3% weight and shows up as either a 5-mark short question or a 15-mark descriptive on a named theorist.

- **Core distinction (Drucker):** Leaders do the *right things* (effectiveness); managers do things *right* (efficiency).
- **Power bases (French & Raven, 1959):** Coercive, Reward, Legitimate, Expert, Referent.
- **Theory families:** Trait → Behavioural → Contingency → Contemporary (Transformational, Servant, Authentic).
- **Key theorists to cite:** Stogdill (1948), Blake & Mouton (1964), Fiedler (1967), House (1971), Hersey & Blanchard, Bass (1985), Greenleaf (1970), Goleman (EI).

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **T-B-C-C-P-S** = Trait, Behavioural, Contingency, Contemporary, Power, Style. For French & Raven: **"Can Really Lead Every Respectfully"** — Coercive, Reward, Legitimate, Expert, Referent. For Blake-Mouton grid, remember **(9,9) Team Management** is the only high-high corner examiners reward.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Theoretical Evolution

Leadership theory moved from a *heroic-myth* (Great Man) to an *empirical trait* approach (Stogdill, 1948; Mann, 1959), then to *behavioural* studies asking what leaders actually do, then to *contingency* asking what works when, and finally to *contemporary* approaches emphasising vision, ethics, and follower development. Each shift answers the gap left by the previous one.

| Theory Family | Core Question | Key Theorist / Year | Strength | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Trait | Who is a leader? | Stogdill 1948, Mann 1959 | Easy to screen for traits | No universal trait guarantees success |
| Behavioural | What does a leader do? | Ohio State, Michigan, Blake & Mouton 1964 | Observable, trainable | Ignores context |
| Contingency | What works in situation X? | Fiedler 1967, House 1971, Hersey-Blanchard | Situation-specific | Complex to apply |
| Contemporary | How does a leader inspire/serve? | Bass 1985, Greenleaf 1970, Avolio & Gardner | Visionary, ethical | Harder to measure |

#### Behavioural Models in Detail

- **Ohio State Studies** identified two dimensions: *Initiating Structure* (task focus, organising work) and *Consideration* (people focus, trust, respect). High-high on both = ideal leader behaviour.
- **Michigan Studies** contrasted *Employee-oriented* leadership with *Production-oriented* leadership; later research favoured the employee orientation for sustained output.
- **Blake & Mouton Managerial Grid (5×5):** plots Concern for People against Concern for Production. Corners are (1,1) Impoverished, (9,1) Authority-Compliance, (1,9) Country-Club, (9,9) Team Management; (5,5) Middle-of-the-Road is the compromise trap RBI examiners flag.
- **Likert System 4** ranges from System 1 (exploitative-authoritative) to System 4 (participative group); only System 4 sustains long-term performance.

#### Contingency Models in Detail

- **Fiedler LPC Model (1967):** measures the leader's style via the *Least Preferred Co-worker* score. Style is **fixed** — the manager must *match* the leader to the situation (relationship-, task-, position-oriented favourability).
- **Hersey-Blanchard Situational Leadership:** maps follower maturity (R1 unable/unwilling → R4 able/willing) to four styles: **Telling, Selling, Participating, Delegating** (S1–S4).
- **Path-Goal Theory (House, 1971):** leader behaviour (Directive, Supportive, Participative, Achievement-oriented) clarifies the path to rewards, contingent on follower and environment.

#### Contemporary Approaches and Power

- **Transformational (Bass, 1985)** uses *idealised influence, inspirational motivation, intellectual stimulation, individualised consideration*; **Transactional** uses contingent reward and management-by-exception (active or passive).
- **Servant Leadership (Greenleaf, 1970):** the best test is whether followers grow as persons and become more autonomous.
- **Authentic Leadership:** self-awareness, relational transparency, balanced processing, internalised moral perspective (Avolio & Gardner).
- **French & Raven Power Bases:**

| Base | Source | Banking Example |
|---|---|---|
| Coercive | Punishment | Disciplinary transfer |
| Reward | Incentives | Performance bonus |
| Legitimate | Position | Branch Manager's signatory power |
| Expert | Knowledge | Chief Risk Officer's call |
| Referent | Respect/identification | Popular MD whose team volunteers |

- **Power vs Authority:** power = *capacity* to influence (may be informal); authority = *legitimate right* to command from a formal role.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix

| Concept Often Confused | Distinction |
|---|---|
| Trait vs Great Man theory | Great Man is heroic-myth, narrative; Trait is empirical, measurable (Stogdill) |
| Transformational vs Transactional | Transformational creates vision and changes followers; Transactional exchanges rewards for compliance |
| Blake-Mouton (9,9) vs (9,1) | (9,9) = Team, high people + high production; (9,1) = Authority-compliance, high production + low people |
| Fiedler LPC vs Hersey-Blanchard | Fiedler says leader style is **fixed**, change the situation; Hersey-Blanchard says leader should **adapt** style to follower maturity |
| Referent vs Charismatic power | Referent = respect/identification (French & Raven); Charismatic authority = Weberian legitimacy tied to a person |
| Power vs Authority | Power = informal capacity to influence; Authority = formal, role-derived right |

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** *As Branch Manager of a Public Sector Bank, your team includes a young officer (R2, willing but unable) handling loan appraisals and a 20-year veteran (R4, able and willing) heading the recovery cell. Using Hersey and Blanchard's Situational Leadership model, prescribe the appropriate leadership style for each. Justify your answer. (15 marks)*

#### Solution:

1. **Identify follower maturity levels.**
   - *R2 — willing but unable*: the young officer has motivation but lacks appraisal skills.
   - *R4 — able and willing*: the veteran recovery head needs minimal task direction.
2. **Map R2 → Selling (S2).** High directive + high supportive behaviour: explain procedures, provide on-the-job coaching on appraisal norms (RBI Master Circular on Prudential Norms), and reassure through mentoring.
3. **Map R4 → Delegating (S4).** Low directive + low supportive behaviour: assign recovery targets, give outcome-level goals, and review via MIS dashboards rather than micro-managing.
4. **Why not other styles?**
   - R2 should *not* get Telling (S1) alone — willingness would collapse.
   - R4 should *not* get Participating (S3) routinely — he already has competence; consultation only on strategic IBC cases.
5. **Banking linkage:** post-amalgamation, PSBs needed exactly this differentiated approach for NPA resolution — veteran officers got delegation, new recruits got coaching.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students often prescribe *one style for all*. The examiner awards full marks only when you match the *correct follower maturity level* to the *correct style (S1–S4)*. Writing "I will be democratic" without naming the model scores zero. Equally, do not confuse Fiedler LPC (style is fixed, change situation) with Hersey-Blanchard (change style to follower maturity) — using the wrong theory forfeits 3-4 marks.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Boundary Conditions

- **Fiedler's reversal:** when situation favourability is moderate (mixed), relationship-oriented leaders outperform; in highly favourable or highly unfavourable situations, task-oriented leaders win. This U-shaped curve is where most candidates slip.
- **Pygmalion effect:** leader expectations directly raise follower performance; in banking, branch heads who expect officers to clear certification exams see higher pass rates. This is the empirical bridge between EI and Transformational leadership.
- **EI (Goleman) dimensions:** self-awareness, self-regulation, motivation, empathy, social skill — together they predict 58% of job performance across roles; for branch leadership, empathy matters more than technical credit knowledge.
- **Hawthorne-to-modern link:** Mayo's human relations finding (attention improves output) is the philosophical ancestor of Servant and Authentic leadership; RBI examiners test this lineage.
- **Followership (Kelley, 1988):** Alienated, Conformist, Pragmatist, Passive, Exemplary. *Exemplary followers* independently think, actively engage, and self-mould — a Transformational leader's target outcome.

#### Advanced Traps and Exceptions

1. **LPC score interpretation inverted by novices:** a *high* LPC means a positive leader (relationship-oriented); a *low* LPC means the leader sees the LPW negatively (task-oriented). Many candidates assume the opposite.
2. **Managerial Grid origin:** Blake & Mouton (1964) is *behavioural*, not contingency, despite the 5×5 layout. Examiners flag this classification.
3. **Mintzberg roles ≠ leadership:** Interpersonal roles (Figurehead, Leader, Liaison), Informational (Monitor, Disseminator, Spokesperson), Decisional (Entrepreneur, Disturbance Handler, Resource Allocator, Negotiator) belong to the manager, not the leader per se. Writing them under "leadership" loses structure marks.
4. **Motivation overlap:** Maslow, Herzberg (Two-Factor), McGregor (Theory X/Y), McClelland (nAch, nAff, nPower) are *not* leadership theories — they are motivation theories that feed into Path-Goal. Mis-tagging them is a recurring descriptive-paper error.
5. **Charismatic authority (Weber):** distinct from Referent power; charismatic authority is a *legitimacy type*, whereas Referent is a *power base*.
6. **Transactional management-by-exception (passive)** waits for errors before intervening, while *active* scans continuously; confusing them blunts a Transformational-vs-Transactional answer.

#### Worked Micro-Example with Numbers

A Regional Office has 40 branches. After the 2019 amalgamation, a survey uses the Blake-Mouton Managerial Grid:

- 12 managers self-locate at (9,9) — output up 14% YoY.
- 8 managers at (9,1) — output up 4%, attrition up 9%.
- 10 managers at (1,9) — output down 2%, satisfaction highest.
- 6 managers at (5,5) — output flat.
- 4 managers at (1,1) — output down 11%.

The Regional Director launches a coaching programme moving (5,5) managers toward (9,9); the (9,1) managers are sent on empathy workshops. Within 18 months, branch productivity variance falls from σ = ₹3.2 cr to σ = ₹1.1 cr in deposit mobilisation — a numerical demonstration of behavioural theory's *trainability* advantage over trait theory.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. *A PSB's internal audit finds that branches led by Exemplary followers (Kelley, 1988) report 22% fewer compliance breaches. Critically examine how Transformational leadership and Servant leadership would each attempt to produce Exemplary followers, citing at least one banking case (e.g., post-2018 PNB fraud reforms) and contrasting Bass's four I's with Greenleaf's best-test criterion.*
2. *Apply Fiedler's LPC contingency model to a situation where an Indian bank is merging two incompatible IT platforms. Where on the situational favourability continuum does this merger sit, and which leader type — relationship-oriented or task-oriented — should the integration head be? Defend with the contingency curve and at least one RBI circular reference.*

> 📌 **Formula Check:** Leadership has no quantitative formula like F = ma, but you must cite *theorist + year* for every framework: Stogdill 1948, Blake & Mouton 1964, Likert 1961, Fiedler 1967, House 1971, Hersey & Blanchard 1969, Greenleaf 1970, Bass 1985, French & Raven 1959, Kelley 1988, Goleman 1995, Avolio & Gardner 2005. Missing dates cost marks in descriptive answers.

#### Exam-Specific Strategy

- **Phase-I (objective):** expect 2-3 MCQs naming the theorist of a model (e.g., "Path-Goal was given by — House/Robbins/Maslow"); or a direct statement match (Blake-Mouton (9,9) = Team Management).
- **Phase-II (descriptive):** 15-mark questions typically ask for *theory comparison + banking application*. Spend 4 minutes planning, 12 minutes writing, 2 minutes reviewing. Use the **T-B-C-C-P-S** sequence to structure answers: Trait → Behavioural → Contingency → Contemporary → Power → Style.
- **Current-affairs linkage:** after the 2018 PNB fraud, RBI's 2019 *Prompt Corrective Action* framework and the 2020 *Governance in Commercial Banks* discussion paper emphasise ethical and authentic leadership — a high-yield descriptive hook.
