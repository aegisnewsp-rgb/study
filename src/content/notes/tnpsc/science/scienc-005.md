# Plant Life and Reproduction

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> High-yield summary of photosynthetic pathways, plant physiological transport, phytohormones, and reproductive anatomy for TNPSC Group 1.

**Plant Physiology & Botany — High-Yield Essentials**

1. **Photosynthetic Energetics:**
   - **Light Reactions (Photochemical Phase):** Occurs in thylakoid membranes of chloroplasts. Photolysis of water (	ext{H}_2	ext{O} 	o 4	ext{H}^+ + 4e^- + 	ext{O}_2$) occurs at Photosystem II (PS II, P680), releasing oxygen. Photophosphorylation generates ATP and NADPH.
   - **Dark Reactions (Biosynthetic Phase / Calvin Cycle):** Occurs in the stroma. Catalyzed by **RuBisCO** (Ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate carboxylase-oxygenase), the most abundant protein on Earth. Fixes CO2 into 3-phosphoglycerate (3-PGA).
2. **Photosynthetic Variations (C3 vs C4 vs CAM):**
   - **C3 Plants:** First stable product is 3-carbon 3-PGA (wheat, rice, oats, barley). Exhibit photorespiratory loss (C2 cycle) under high temperature and O2.
   - **C4 Plants:** First stable product is 4-carbon oxaloacetate (maize, sugarcane, sorghum). Possess **Kranz anatomy** (dimorphic chloroplasts in mesophyll and bundle sheath cells). RuBisCO is sequestered in bundle sheath cells; zero photorespiration occurs.
   - **CAM Plants:** Crassulacean Acid Metabolism (*Opuntia*, pineapple, agave). Stomata open at night (*scotoactive*) to minimize transpiration water loss in arid environments.
3. **Phytohormones (Plant Growth Regulators):**
   - **Auxins (IAA):** Apical dominance, cell elongation, root initiation, induces parthenocarpy.
   - **Gibberellins (GA3):** Bolting in rosette plants, breaks seed dormancy, promotes stem elongation.
   - **Cytokinins (Zeatin, Kinetin):** Promotes cell division, delays leaf senescence (Richmond-Lang effect).
   - **Ethylene (Gaseous hormone):** Fruit ripening, abscission, triple response in seedlings.
   - **Abscisic Acid (ABA):** Stress hormone; closes stomata during drought, induces seed dormancy.

⚡ **TNPSC Group 1 Exam Trap:** Aspirants often confuse the site of Kranz anatomy. Kranz anatomy is unique to **C4 plants** (such as sugarcane and maize), preventing photorespiration and providing high photosynthetic efficiency in tropical climates.

## 1. Vascular Transport Dynamics: Xylem vs Phloem

Plants translocate water, minerals, and photoassimilates across long distances through specialized complex permanent vascular tissues:

| Feature | Xylem Tissue | Phloem Tissue |
|---|---|---|
| **Conducting Elements** | Tracheids and vessels (tracheary elements) | Sieve tube elements and companion cells |
| **Living / Dead State** | Tracheids, vessels, and xylem fibres are dead at maturity; xylem parenchyma is living | Sieve tubes, companion cells, and phloem parenchyma are living; phloem fibres (bast) are dead |
| **Direction of Flow** | Unidirectional: roots to stems and leaves | Multidirectional (bidirectional): source (leaves) to sinks (roots, fruits, buds) |
| **Driving Mechanism** | Transpiration pull driven by cohesion-tension theory (Dixon & Joly, 1894) | Mass Flow / Pressure Flow Hypothesis (Ernst Münch, 1930) driven by turgor gradients |
| **Major Substances Carried** | Water, dissolved inorganic mineral ions, small amounts of organic nitrogen | Sucrose (non-reducing disaccharide), amino acids, hormones, systemic signals |

## 2. Sexual Reproduction in Angiosperms and Double Fertilization

Angiosperm reproduction involves an alternation of generations where the dominant diploid sporophyte produces flowers housing microsporangia and megasporangia:

### Double Fertilization and Triple Fusion
Discovered by S.G. Nawaschin (1898) in *Lilium* and *Fritillaria*, double fertilization is an evolutionary hallmark unique to flowering plants:
1. **Syngamy (True Fertilization):** One haploid male gamete (n) fuses with the haploid egg cell (n) inside the embryo sac to form the **diploid zygote (2n)**, which subsequently develops into the embryo.
2. **Triple Fusion:** The second haploid male gamete (n) fuses with the diploid secondary nucleus (2n, formed by the fusion of two polar nuclei) in the central cell to produce the **triploid Primary Endosperm Nucleus (PEN, 3n)**.
3. **Endosperm Development:** The triploid PEN divides mitotically to form nutrient-rich endosperm tissue that nourishes the developing embryo. In non-albuminous (exalbuminous) seeds like gram and pea, endosperm is completely consumed prior to seed maturation; in albuminous seeds like castor and maize, endosperm persists.

## 3. Photoperiodism and Florigen Hypothesis

Plants respond to the relative lengths of light and dark periods to initiate floral transition:
- **Short-Day Plants (SDPs / Long-Night Plants):** Flower when day length is less than a critical photoperiod (e.g., *Chrysanthemum*, rice, soybean). A brief flash of light during the dark period inhibits flowering.
- **Long-Day Plants (LDPs / Short-Night Plants):** Flower when day length exceeds the critical photoperiod (e.g., wheat, radish, spinach).
- **Day-Neutral Plants (DNPs):** Flowering is unaffected by photoperiod (e.g., tomato, sunflower, maize).
- **Phytochrome Pigment:** A photoreceptor chromoprotein existing in two interconvertible forms: Pr (absorbs red light at 660 nm, biologically inactive) and Pfr (absorbs far-red light at 730 nm, biologically active trigger for physiological responses).

## 4. TNPSC Group 1 Previous Year Questions (PYQs)

**Question 1 (TNPSC Group 1 Prelims):** The plant hormone that delays senescence in detached leaves (Richmond-Lang effect) is:  
*(A) Auxin (B) Gibberellin (C) Cytokinin (D) Abscisic acid*  
**Answer:** (C) Cytokinin (Explanation: Cytokinins mobilize nutrients and sustain protein synthesis, delaying chlorophyll degradation and senescence in excised plant foliage).

**Question 2 (TNPSC Group 1 Prelims):** Which of the following plants exhibits Kranz anatomy in its leaves?  
*(A) Rice (B) Wheat (C) Sugarcane (D) Mango*  
**Answer:** (C) Sugarcane (Explanation: Sugarcane is a classic C4 tropical monocot featuring Kranz anatomy, where bundle sheath cells contain large chloroplasts lacking grana, surrounded by mesophyll cells).

**Question 3 (TNPSC Group 1 Prelims):** In angiosperms, the ploidy level of the functional endosperm tissue is typically:  
*(A) Haploid (n) (B) Diploid (2n) (C) Triploid (3n) (D) Tetraploid (4n)*  
**Answer:** (C) Triploid (3n) (Explanation: Double fertilization produces a triploid (3n) primary endosperm nucleus through the fusion of one haploid sperm nucleus with two haploid polar nuclei).
