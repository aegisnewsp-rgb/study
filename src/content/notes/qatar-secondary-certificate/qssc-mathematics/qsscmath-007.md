# Geometry — Similarity, Circles and Vectors — Qatar Secondary Certificate Mathematics Notes

Plane and solid geometry, circle theorems, and vectors form the Geometry and Measurement strand of the MoEHE 2018 Mathematics Curriculum Standards. At Grade 12 advanced track, the standards publish triangle similarity, circle theorems (tangent-chord, cyclic quadrilateral, alternate segment), the circle equation, vectors in two and three dimensions, and the scalar and vector products (MoEHE pp. 53, 60). This strand carries around 14 percent of the Grade 12 advanced Mathematics paper — smaller than algebra-calculus, but it is the strand most candidates score unevenly on.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Similar triangles:** corresponding angles equal, corresponding sides in the same ratio.
- **Circle theorems:** angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference subtending the same arc; angles in the same segment are equal; angle in a semicircle is 90; opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180; tangent-chord angle equals the angle in the alternate segment.
- **Circle equation:** (x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2, centre (h, k), radius r.
- **Vectors:** position vector, magnitude |a| = sqrt(a_x^2 + a_y^2), unit vector a / |a|, dot product a * b = a_x b_x + a_y b_y, cross product (3D) defined by determinant.
- **Direction:** a vector from P(x_1, y_1) to Q(x_2, y_2) is (x_2 - x_1, y_2 - y_1).

#### Examiner traps

- Using the wrong circle theorem (centre vs circumference) — they look the same on a sketch.
- Mixing column and row vectors in 3D.
- Treating scalar and vector products as interchangeable.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Similar triangles and proportions

For triangle ABC similar to triangle DEF with ratio k: AB/DE = BC/EF = CA/FD = k. Areas scale by k^2; volumes by k^3.

The Midpoint Theorem: the line segment joining the midpoints of two sides of a triangle is parallel to the third side and half its length.

#### Circle theorems in proof questions

The most-tested theorems: (1) the angle subtended by an arc at the centre is twice the angle subtended at any point on the remaining part of the circle; (2) the angle in a semicircle is a right angle; (3) opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180 degrees; (4) the alternate segment theorem — the angle between a tangent and a chord through the point of contact equals the angle in the alternate segment.

To solve a geometry proof, label every angle on the diagram and chase the equal angles step by step.

#### The circle equation

Given centre (h, k) and radius r: (x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2. To find the circle through three points, set up three equations and solve for h, k, r^2. Use the general form x^2 + y^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0, with centre (-D/2, -E/2) and radius sqrt((D/2)^2 + (E/2)^2 - F).

Tangent line at point (x_1, y_1) on circle (x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2: (x_1 - h)(x - h) + (y_1 - k)(y - k) = r^2.

#### Vectors in 2D and 3D

Position vector: OP = (p_x, p_y, p_z). Magnitude: |OP| = sqrt(p_x^2 + p_y^2 + p_z^2). Unit vector: OP / |OP|.

Dot product (scalar): a . b = a_x b_x + a_y b_y + a_z b_z. The angle between a and b is given by cos theta = (a . b) / (|a| |b|). Perpendicular vectors have a . b = 0.

Cross product (3D vector): a x b = (a_y b_z - a_z b_y, a_z b_x - a_x b_z, a_x b_y - a_y b_x). Magnitude |a x b| = |a| |b| sin theta.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### Why geometry carries marks despite the smaller weighting

The MoEHE 2018 standards place Geometry and Measurement at ~14 percent of the Grade 12 advanced paper — the second-largest strand. Geometry is the strand where careless sketching and missing angle labels cost 2-3 marks per question. Vectors are the algebraic entry to 3D geometry and appear in physics-style applied questions.

#### Exam technique

- For circle theorem proof, mark equal angles with the same arc on the diagram before writing the proof — visual labels prevent angle-chasing errors.
- For 3D vector questions, draw a 3D sketch with axes labelled.
- For the tangent line, use the form (x_1 - h)(x - h) + (y_1 - k)(y - k) = r^2, not the differentiated gradient — fewer algebra slips.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "All angles in a triangle sum to 180, so all angles in a quadrilateral sum to 360." True for simple quadrilaterals but NOT for cyclic quadrilaterals when measured as inscribed angles — the inscribed-angle subtended-arc relationships apply.
- "a . b = a * b." It equals |a| |b| cos theta, where theta is the angle between them.
- "Cross product is commutative." No, a x b = -(b x a).
