# The Seventh Schedule of the Constitution Divides Legislative

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The **Seventh Schedule** of the Indian Constitution divides legislative power between the **Union** and the **States** through three lists contained in Articles 246 and 246A–246B. Article 254 settles conflicts on overlapping subjects, and Entry 97 of the Union List gives Parliament exclusive **residuary powers**.

- **List I – Union List:** 97 entries on subjects of national importance (Defence, Railways, Foreign Affairs, Banking, Atomic Energy). Only Parliament legislates.
- **List II – State List:** 66 entries on local matters (Police, Public Order, Agriculture, Land, Local Government). Only State Legislatures legislate.
- **List III – Concurrent List:** 47 entries (Criminal Law, Marriage, Education, Forests, GST). Both Parliament and State Legislatures can legislate.

| List | Article empowering Parliament | Article empowering State | Current entries |
|---|---|---|---|
| Union | 246(2) | — | 97 |
| State | — | 246(3) | 66 |
| Concurrent | 246(3) | 246(3) | 47 |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"USC-USA-USA"** — **U**nion has **S**upremacy on **C**oncurrent conflicts (Art. 254); **S**tates are **U**niversal on their list; **A**ll else (residueary) goes to Parliament under Entry 97.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Constitutional Basis
Article 246 distributes legislative competence. **Article 246(1)** divides subjects into the three Lists; **Article 246(2)** gives Parliament exclusive power over the **Union List**; **Article 246(3)** gives State Legislatures power over the **State** and **Concurrent** Lists, while also permitting Parliament to legislate on Concurrent subjects. **Article 246A** (101st Amendment, 2016) confers simultaneous power on Parliament and every State Legislature to make laws on **Goods and Services Tax**. **Article 246B** grants Parliament exclusive power to legislate on **inter-State GST** supplies.

#### Repugnancy Doctrine under Article 254
When a Central law and a State law on a Concurrent List subject clash, **Article 254(1)** says the Central law prevails. **Article 254(2)** carves one exception: if the State law has been **reserved for the President's assent** and received it, the State law prevails **in that State alone**. This protects legislative autonomy where the Centre actively approves a State deviation.

> 📌 **Formula Check:** Rule of repugnancy applies **only** to Concurrent subjects where both laws are validly made; territorial operation, not legislative competence, decides precedence.

#### Constitutional Comparison Matrix

| Provision | Scope | Who Decides | Key Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Art. 246(2) – Union List | List I | Parliament | Exclusive |
| Art. 246(3) – State List | List II | State Legislature | Exclusive to State |
| Art. 246(3) – Concurrent List | List III | Both | Concurrent |
| Art. 246A – GST | Intra-State GST | Parliament + States | Concurrent on GST |
| Art. 246B – Inter-State GST | Inter-State supplies | Parliament only | Exclusive to Centre |
| Art. 254 – Repugnancy | Clash on List III | Centre prevails | With President's assent carve-out |
| Entry 97, List I | Residuary | Parliament | Exclusive |

#### Key Amendments That Reshaped the Lists
1. **42nd Amendment, 1976** — transferred five State subjects (Education, Forests, Weights & Measures, Wild Life & Birds, Administration of Justice) to the Concurrent List.
2. **44th Amendment, 1978** — omitted the Concurrent List for **Union Territories** and shifted them to the State List.
3. **101st Amendment, 2016** — introduced Articles 246A and 246B to accommodate GST.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem
**Question:** A State Legislature passes a law on "Education" after obtaining the President's assent. Parliament has already enacted a Central law on the same subject. Which law prevails in that State?

#### Solution:
- "Education" sits in the **Concurrent List** (List III) after the 42nd Amendment, 1976.
- Under **Article 254(1)**, a Central law normally prevails over a State law on a Concurrent subject.
- However, **Article 254(2)** activates where the State law has been **reserved for and received the President's assent**. In that case the State law prevails **in that State**.
- Since the State law here received Presidential assent, **the State law prevails in that State**, while the Central law continues to operate in all other States.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students wrongly pick "Central law always wins." The correct trigger is the **President's assent**, not mere gubernatorial assent. Without that assent, the State law would be void to the extent of repugnancy.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Constitutional Boundaries

- **Doctrine of Territorial Nexus:** A State law has no extra-territorial operation; Parliament can still legislate for the whole of India even on a Concurrent subject.
- **Doctrine of Pith and Substance:** Courts decide the true nature of a law — if its substance falls in one List, incidental encroachment on a different List is valid.
- **Colourable Legislation:** A legislature cannot do indirectly what it cannot do directly; courts examine substance over form.
- **Post-GST Architecture:** Article 246A read with the **GST Council** (Article 279A) creates a unique cooperative-federal structure where both Centre and States legislate on a single tax base, with the Council recommending rates.

#### Advanced Practice Traps

1. **Residuary trap:** A student is asked who legislates on "E-commerce data protection before 2017." Answer: **Parliament under Entry 97, Union List**, because no List mentioned it then — illustrating that residuary powers are not State powers.
2. **Concurrent count trap:** TNPSC sometimes tests whether Concurrent List has 47 or 52 entries. Correct figure is **47**, because only **five** subjects (not ten) were shifted by the 42nd Amendment, 1976.
3. **GST territorial trap:** A question on "GST on supply of goods from Tamil Nadu to Kerala" — Parliament alone under **Article 246B**; intra-state GST for Tamil Nadu is **Article 246A** (Parliament + State Legislature together).
4. **Article 254 trap:** "Does Article 254 apply to Union List subjects?" — **No.** Repugnancy doctrine is confined to the **Concurrent List**.
5. **Pre-constitutional laws trap:** Article 372(1) continuance operates independently and is **not** tested through the Seventh Schedule allocation.

#### Connection to Adjacent TNPSC Topics
- **Article 370 abrogation (2019):** Applied the Union List competence to Jammu & Kashmir after reorganisation.
- **Inter-State Council (Article 263):** Coordinates disputes arising from concurrent or overlapping legislative action.
- **Anti-Defection Law (10th Schedule):** Operates despite being a Central law on a subject touching State List governance — upheld under **Pith and Substance** doctrine.
- **Cooperative Federalism post-GST:** Marked the first revenue subject placed on the Concurrent List architecture via Article 246A.

Expect 1–2 questions per paper, often as direct MCQs on list-entry matching or as assertion-reason items linking Article 254 with the President's assent carve-out. Memorise the current **97–66–47** count and the five transferred subjects of the 42nd Amendment.
