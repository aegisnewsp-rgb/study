# Cardiovascular System

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The **cardiovascular system** is a closed circulatory loop driven by a four-chambered muscular pump — the **heart** — that propels blood through arteries, capillaries, and veins to every tissue. Structural mastery covers the **chambers, valves, conducting system, coronary circulation, pericardium, and fetal shunts**.

- **CO = SV × HR**, where SV (mL/beat) = EDV − ESV; **EF (%) = (SV/EDV) × 100**; normal EF 55–70%.
- **MAP = Diastolic BP + ⅓(Systolic − Diastolic)**; cardiac cycle = 60/HR seconds.
- **Conducting order:** SA node → AV node (0.3 s delay) → Bundle of His → bundle branches → **Purkinje fibres**.
- **Fetal shunts:** *foramen ovale* (atrial), *ductus arteriosus* (PA → aortic arch), *ductus venosus* (umbilical vein → IVC).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Heart wall and chambers

The heart wall has three layers: **endocardium** (inner endothelium-lined), **myocardium** (cardiac muscle, thickest in the left ventricle), and **epicardium** (= visceral serous pericardium). The **interatrial septum** carries the fossa ovalis; the **interventricular septum** has a small **membranous** part (just below the aortic valve, the usual site of VSDs) and a larger **muscular** part. The **right atrium** receives the SVC, IVC, and coronary sinus; the **left atrium** receives four pulmonary veins.

#### Valves and their supports

Two atrioventricular valves — **tricuspid** (right) and **bicuspid/mitral** (left) — sit on a fibrous skeleton and are anchored by **chordae tendineae** to **papillary muscles**. The **aortic** and **pulmonary** valves are semilunar with three cusps each and have **no chordae**.

#### Conducting system

Specialised muscle fibres generate and conduct the impulse: **SA node** (pacemaker in the right atrium near the SVC opening, rate ~70–75/min) → **AV node** (at the septal cusp of the tricuspid valve; 0.3 s delay) → **Bundle of His** → right and left bundle branches → **Purkinje fibres** (subendocardial).

#### Coronary circulation and dominance

- **Left coronary artery** (LCA): divides into **LAD** (anterior 2/3 septum, anterior LV) and **circumflex** (left atrium, lateral LV).
- **Right coronary artery** (RCA): supplies right atrium, right ventricle, posterior 1/3 septum, and — in **right-dominant** hearts (~70–80%) — the **posterior interventricular artery**.
- LCA dominance is rare; balanced circulation exists in the remainder.

| Concept | Key point |
| --- | --- |
| Valve auscultation | Aortic — right 2nd ICS; Pulmonary — left 2nd ICS; Tricuspid — lower left sternal border; Mitral — apex 5th ICS MCL |
| Pericardial sinuses | **Transverse** sinus (between aorta/PA anteriorly and SVC/atria posteriorly — used to clamp great vessels in surgery); **Oblique** sinus (posterior to LA) |
| Nerve supply | Sympathetic T1–T5 (accelerator); vagus CN X (decelerator); referred cardiac pain to T1–T5 dermatomes (left arm) |

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Pericardium in detail

The **fibrous pericardium** blends with the great vessel adventitia and resists overdistension. Inside it, the **serous pericardium** has a parietal layer (lining the fibrous sac) and a visceral layer (= epicardium), separated by pericardial fluid (~15–35 mL). Inflammation produces pericarditis with a friction rub; tamponade compresses chambers and drops CO.

#### Fetal circulation and shunt closure

Oxygenated placental blood travels via the **umbilical vein → ductus venosus → IVC → right atrium**, then mostly crosses the **foramen ovale** to the left atrium (right-to-left shunt). The rest passes through the right ventricle and pulmonary artery; high pulmonary vascular resistance shunts it through the **ductus arteriosus** into the aortic arch. At birth, lung expansion raises PaO₂, closing the ductus functionally within 24–48 h (anatomically in 2–3 weeks via the ligamentum arteriosum); foramen ovale closes as the flap seals against the septum secundum (fossa ovalis).

#### Edge cases and traps

- **Left-sided valves heard on the right chest** and vice versa: auscultation sites follow blood-flow direction, not anatomical position.
- **Purkinje fibres are cardiac muscle**, not nerves — only SA and AV nodes are true nodal tissue.
- A **posterior MI** (PDA territory) usually spares the LAD and may cause inferior ECG changes; correlate with RCA occlusion.
