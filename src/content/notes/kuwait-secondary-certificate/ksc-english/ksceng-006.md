# Unit 12 — Geniuses — Kuwait Secondary Certificate English Notes

Unit 12 of the Kuwait MOE Grade 12 "Over To You" textbook (second term, 3 May to 14 May 2026) is "Geniuses" — the closing unit of the year, on exceptional talent, prodigies and creativity. The reading input is an article about talented children, plus a Focus On text about Maha Al-Ghunaim. The unit's grammar focus is relative clauses, verbs ending in "-ing", "as... as" comparison, and prepositions. Vocabulary covers nouns and adjectives, and suffixes. The writing focus is a recommendation letter. Speaking focus is expressing possibility, guessing, giving explanations, and giving recommendations and praise.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Defining relative clause:** "The student who studies hard will succeed." Essential to the meaning, no commas.
- **Non-defining relative clause:** "The teacher, who is very experienced, will help us." Adds extra information, commas required, "who" cannot be replaced by "that".
- **Verbs ending in -ing as nouns:** "Swimming is good exercise." / "I enjoy reading." -ing form is the gerund.
- **As... as comparisons:** "She is as tall as her mother." / "It is not as easy as it looks."
- **Suffixes (revisited):** -tion, -ment, -ity, -al, -ive, -ful, -less, -ous.
- **Letter of recommendation:** date, recipient, opening (state the recommendation), body (specific examples), closing (restate), sign-off.

#### Examiner traps

- Using "that" for a non-defining relative clause — "The teacher, that is experienced,..." is wrong; only "who" works in non-defining clauses.
- Confusing -ing form (present participle / gerund) with past participle — "interesting" (active, present) vs "interested" (passive state).
- Using "so... as" in affirmative comparisons — modern English prefers "as... as"; "so... as" survives only in negatives ("not so... as").

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Relative clauses

A relative clause modifies a noun (the antecedent) and is introduced by a relative pronoun: who/whom (people), which (things), that (people or things, defining only), whose (possession), where (place), when (time), why (reason).

**Defining relative clause** is essential to the meaning. No commas around it. "The book that I borrowed is overdue."

**Non-defining relative clause** adds extra information that is not essential. Commas around it. "My brother, who lives in Kuwait City, is visiting next week." Cannot use "that" in non-defining clauses.

In formal English, "whom" is the object form: "The author whom I admire most is..." In modern English, "who" is often used informally: "The author who I admire most..."

#### Verbs ending in -ing

The -ing form has three distinct uses:

1. **Present participle (verb):** "She is swimming." (continuous aspect)
2. **Gerund (noun):** "Swimming is good exercise." / "I enjoy swimming." (subject or object of a verb)
3. **Adjective:** "This is an interesting book." / "I found the book interesting." (modifies a noun)

Some verbs take gerunds (enjoy, avoid, mind, suggest, consider, finish, admit, deny, imagine): "I enjoy reading." Some take infinitives (want, decide, expect, hope, plan, agree, seem): "I want to read." Some take both with different meanings (stop, remember, forget, try, regret): "I stopped smoking" (gerund, quitting the habit) vs "I stopped to smoke" (infinitive, paused to do it).

#### As... as comparison

"As... as" expresses equality between two items:

- Affirmative: "She is as clever as her sister."
- Negative: "It is not as easy as I thought." (American English also allows "not so easy as".)

The first "as" is an adverb, the second is a conjunction. Use the bare adjective or adverb between them: "as tall as," "as quickly as," "as well as." Some adjectives have irregular comparative forms even in "as... as" constructions: "as good as," "as bad as," "as far as."

For "as much as" with uncountable nouns: "I have as much work as you." For "as many as" with countable nouns: "I have as many books as you."

#### Suffixes revisited

Suffixes change part of speech and register. The same root can take multiple suffixes with different effects:

- Verb: "to create"
- Noun: "creation"
- Adjective: "creative"
- Noun (person): "creator"
- Adjective (opposite): "uncreative"
- Adverb: "creatively"

Each form has a specific grammatical role. In a writing item requiring "the ___ of the project", only a noun fits; an adjective would be ungrammatical.

#### Recommendation letter

A letter of recommendation has the following structure:

1. **Date and recipient address** — formal letter layout.
2. **Salutation** — "Dear Sir/Madam" or "Dear Mr/Ms [Name]".
3. **Opening sentence** — state the recommendation explicitly: "I am writing to recommend..."
4. **Body paragraph 1** — the candidate's qualities, with specific examples.
5. **Body paragraph 2** — the candidate's achievements, with dates and specifics.
6. **Body paragraph 3** — comparison to peers, suitability for the role.
7. **Closing** — restate the recommendation, offer further contact.
8. **Sign-off** — "Yours sincerely/faithfully" (depending on the salutation).

Use formal register, third-person references to the candidate ("the applicant," "the student"), and avoid informal language. The letter should be confident but not effusive — over-praise undermines credibility.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where Unit 12 appears across the terminal English paper

- **Reading comprehension:** an article or interview on a genius, prodigy, or exceptional figure, with vocabulary items drawn from the unit.
- **Grammar items:** defining/non-defining relative clause transformations, gerund vs infinitive gap-fills.
- **Writing item:** a recommendation letter, possibly for a job, scholarship, or programme.
- **Vocabulary items:** noun/verb/adjective suffixes, -ing forms.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "All relative clauses can be introduced by 'that'." No — non-defining relative clauses must use "who" (people), "which" (things), or "whose" (possession); "that" is only for defining clauses.
- "I enjoy to read." No — enjoy takes the gerund: "I enjoy reading."
- "She is as taller as her sister." No — "as" takes the base form, not the comparative: "as tall as".

#### Exam technique

- For relative-clause transformations, decide whether the clause is defining or non-defining first — that determines whether "that" is allowed and whether commas are required.
- For -ing items, identify whether the gap needs a verb, noun, or adjective — that determines which -ing form fits.
- For recommendation letters, plan the three body paragraphs (qualities, achievements, comparison) before writing.
