# English reading and writing at Level 3 — NCEA UE Literacy Notes

Level 3 English offers more advanced reading and writing standards that contribute to UE literacy. The key Level 3 English standards include AS 91472 (Respond critically to specified aspect(s) of studied written text(s)), AS 91473 (Respond critically to specified aspect(s) of studied visual or oral text(s)), AS 91474 (Produce a selection of crafted and controlled writing), and AS 91475 (Produce a formal writing portfolio). These standards require sophisticated critical engagement and polished writing, and they earn credits at the highest NCEA level.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Reading and writing credits** available at Level 3 through English achievement standards.
- Common standards: AS 91472 (Critically respond to written texts), AS 91473 (Critically respond to visual/oral texts), AS 91474 (Crafted writing selection), AS 91475 (Formal writing portfolio).
- All count toward the UE literacy requirement.
- Level 3 English credits are higher-level and more demanding than Level 2; they are appropriate for students aiming for university arts and humanities courses.

#### Examiner traps

- Assuming Level 3 is just "harder Level 2" — Level 3 requires independent critical thinking, not just more sophisticated analysis.
- Skipping the research component of AS 91475 (formal writing portfolio requires research).
- Confusing "crafted and controlled" (AS 91474) with "creative" — the Level 3 standard demands deliberate language choices, not just storytelling.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### The Level 3 English standards contributing to UE literacy

The exact standards contributing to UE literacy are listed on the NZQA UE literacy spreadsheet. Common Level 3 English standards include:

- **AS 91472** Respond critically to specified aspect(s) of studied written text(s) — credits contribute to UE reading and/or writing.
- **AS 91473** Respond critically to specified aspect(s) of studied visual or oral text(s) — credits contribute to UE reading and/or writing.
- **AS 91474** Produce a selection of crafted and controlled writing — credits contribute to UE writing.
- **AS 91475** Produce and present a formal writing portfolio based on research — credits contribute to UE writing.

A student taking the full Level 3 English programme typically attempts 4-6 standards and earns 12-18 credits at Level 3, of which most contribute to UE literacy.

#### Critical response vs analysis

Level 3 English distinguishes "responding critically" from "analysing":

- **Analyse (Level 2):** identify features, explain effects, support with examples.
- **Respond critically (Level 3):** engage with the text, take a position, develop an argument, justify with evidence.

The critical response demands a clear thesis and a sustained argument, not just a feature-by-feature analysis.

#### Crafted and controlled writing (AS 91474)

This standard asks the student to produce a selection of writing that demonstrates:

- Control of language (sentence structure, vocabulary, punctuation).
- Craft (deliberate choices for effect).
- Coherence (the writing is unified and well-organised).

The portfolio typically includes multiple pieces in different genres.

#### Formal writing portfolio (AS 91475)

This standard is research-based. The student:

1. Identifies a research question.
2. Locates and evaluates sources.
3. Synthesises information.
4. Produces a coherent written report.

The portfolio is a substantial piece of academic writing, similar to a first-year university essay.

#### Preparation strategy

1. **Read critically** — engage with texts, form opinions, argue positions.
2. **Write often** — Level 3 writing standards require sophisticated control that develops through practice.
3. **Get feedback** — teachers, peers, and self-assessment against exemplars.
4. **Revise rigorously** — Excellence is about polishing and refinement.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Excellence in critical response

The biggest gap between Merit and Excellence on AS 91472/91473 is the integration of argument and evidence.

For example, on "Respond critically to the way the writer presents the theme of isolation":

- Merit: The student identifies instances of isolation in the text, explains their effects, and concludes that isolation is a major theme.
- Excellence: The student identifies instances of isolation, explains their effects, AND develops a sustained argument about how the writer uses isolation to comment on the human condition. The argument is the spine of the response; the textual evidence supports it.

The Excellence response does not just describe the text; it makes a claim about the text and supports it.

#### Excellence in writing

The biggest gap between Merit and Excellence on AS 91474/91475 is the deliberate control of language.

For example, in a crafted writing portfolio:

- Merit: A piece of writing that is competent, with some effective language choices.
- Excellence: A piece of writing where every sentence is deliberate, the vocabulary is precise, and the form is carefully chosen for the purpose.

The Excellence writer can articulate WHY each choice was made.

#### Research skills for AS 91475

Research skills are essential for AS 91475:

- **Source evaluation:** authority, accuracy, currency, bias.
- **Note-taking:** paraphrasing, summarising, quoting, citing.
- **Synthesis:** combining sources into a coherent argument.
- **Citation:** acknowledging sources, avoiding plagiarism.

These skills are directly transferable to university study.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Level 3 English is like Level 2 but harder." It is harder AND different — it requires independent critical thinking, not just analysis.
- "AS 91474 is creative writing." No — it is "crafted and controlled" writing, which demands deliberate language choices for specific effects.
- "AS 91475 is just a long essay." No — it is a portfolio based on research, requiring planning, drafting, and revision.
- "Level 3 standards are only useful for English majors." No — the skills are applicable to all university courses.
