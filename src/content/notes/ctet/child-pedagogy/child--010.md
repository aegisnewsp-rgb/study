# Assessment and Evaluation

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> The single line that breaks CTET aspirants most often: assessment and evaluation are NOT the same word.

Assessment is the evidence-gathering phase. Evaluation is the judgment made on that evidence. CTET Paper I and Paper II carry 2–3 MCQs on this pair, almost always framed to test whether you can separate the two. **Formative assessment** runs during teaching and modifies it; **summative assessment** runs at the end and certifies it.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook — "ACED":** **A**ssessment **C**ollects, **E**valuation **D**ecides. Picture a teacher first *collecting* answer sheets (Assessment), then *deciding* pass/fail (Evaluation).

| Term | One-line meaning |
|---|---|
| Assessment | Systematic collection of information about learner progress |
| Evaluation | Judgment + decision based on the information collected |
| Formative | Ongoing, diagnostic, modifies instruction |
| Summative | Terminal, certifies achievement |
| CCE | Continuous and Comprehensive Evaluation (RTE 2009, Section 29) |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Most CDP MCQs hinge on pairs: assessment vs evaluation, formative vs summative, norm-referenced vs criterion-referenced, and AfL vs AoL.

#### What each term actually does

**Assessment** answers *"what is the learner able to do right now?"* The teacher observes, asks questions, checks homework, runs quizzes, maintains anecdotal records, or reviews a portfolio. **Evaluation** then answers *"is this performance good enough against the standard?"* and produces a grade, a label, a promotion decision, or a remediation plan. CTET options frequently flip the verbs in these definitions to trap you.

#### The core typology

Formative assessment is built into the lesson — oral questioning, exit tickets, peer feedback, peer assessment, self-assessment, observation schedules, anecdotal records, rubrics. Its purpose is to **close the learning gap**, not to produce a mark. Summative assessment happens at the close of a unit, term, or year and produces a **score or grade** that gets reported. The CCE framework under **Section 29 of the RTE Act, 2009** extends evaluation to co-scholastic domains: life skills, attitudes, values, sports, art, music.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix

| Concept | What it does | Who drives it | Output | Typical CTET trap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Assessment for Learning (AfL) | Informs next teaching steps | Teacher + learner together | Descriptive feedback, next-step plan | Treating AfL as a graded test |
| Assessment of Learning (AoL) | Reports achievement | Teacher-driven | Marks, grades, certificates | Treating AoL as ongoing feedback |
| Norm-referenced test | Compares learner with peers | External examiner | Percentile, rank | Calling it "objective and unbiased" |
| Criterion-referenced test | Compares learner with fixed standard | Teacher or board | "Mastered / not mastered" | Confusing it with AoL |
| Portfolio | Folder of work over time | Learner-led | Growth evidence | Reducing it to a mark sheet |
| Diagnostic test | Locates specific learning gaps | Teacher | Remediation plan | Calling it "summative" |

#### Worked Exam-Level Problem

A CTET Paper-II question reads: *"Which of the following best describes Continuous and Comprehensive Evaluation (CCE)?"* Options: (A) A system of frequent unit tests only; (B) Evaluation of scholastic subjects through terminal exams; (C) Continuous assessment of both scholastic and co-scholastic areas covering cognitive, affective and psychomotor domains; (D) Ranking of learners on a national percentile scale.

The correct response is **(C)**. CCE under RTE 2009 / NCF 2005 evaluates scholastic and co-scholastic aspects together, spanning all three domains of Bloom's taxonomy — cognitive (knowledge, comprehension), affective (attitudes, values), psychomotor (skills). Option (A) ignores co-scholastic areas. Option (B) restricts evaluation to terminal exams, which is the older model CCE replaced. Option (D) describes norm-referenced testing, not CCE.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Options A and D read plausible because "continuous" sounds like "frequent tests" and "comprehensive" sounds like "covers the whole syllabus". CCE is broader: continuous means *spread across the year*, comprehensive means *across all domains of learning*, not just marks.

#### Feedback that actually changes learning

Feedback under NEP 2020 and NCF 2005 must be **specific, timely, descriptive, and actionable**. It targets the **task** ("recheck the second step of long division"), the **process** ("show your working"), or **self-regulation** ("set your own next goal") — Dweck's growth-mindset research shows feedback aimed at *ability* ("you are bad at maths") damages motivation. CTET MCQs test whether the candidate can spot ability-targeted feedback as the wrong option.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Where Standard tier sorts the vocabulary, Extended tier sorts the *judgments* CTET expects you to defend.

#### Domain mapping that examiners love

| Bloom's domain | Sample verb | Common CTET example subject |
|---|---|---|
| Cognitive | Recall, analyse, evaluate | Maths problem-solving steps |
| Affective | Receive, value, organise | Attitude towards group work |
| Psychomotor | Imitate, perform, adapt | Handwriting fluency, lab skills |

A learner who can recite every capital city but cannot locate them on a map has only the cognitive slice. CCE demands evidence from all three. Affective data comes through observation schedules, anecdotal records, peer narratives, and self-report inventories — instruments primary teachers often overlook.

#### Reliability, validity, and objectivity

A good test is **reliable** (produces the same result on repetition) and **valid** (measures what it claims to measure). A weekly spelling dictation can be reliable but invalid for measuring comprehension. **Objectivity** reduces teacher bias in scoring — rubric-based marking, scoring keys, and structured observation schedules raise objectivity. CTET rarely asks definitions here; it asks you to choose the more defensible evaluation plan, e.g. "use a rubric" beats "use teacher impression".

#### Edge cases and recent shifts

- **NEP 2020** prefers competency-based assessment over content memorisation, pushing criterion-referenced interpretations.
- **Portfolio and self-assessment** are valid even when a child cannot write fluently — audio records, drawings, and teacher dictation count.
- **No-detention policy** under RTE Section 16 (now relaxed for Classes 5 and 8 via the 2019 amendment) reshapes evaluation in primary years; promotion decisions need explicit academic evidence, not automatic pass.
- **Diagnostic vs. placement** — diagnostic locates gaps within an ongoing year; placement decides which class a child enters. CTET options often call diagnostic testing "placement testing".

#### Two advanced prompts to test yourself

1. A teacher gives remedial worksheets after every Friday test. Is this formative or summative? Defend using the "modifies instruction" test, not the "happens often" test.
2. A school switches to criterion-referenced reporting. Predict two parent-meeting reactions and write a one-line explanation the teacher can use. The criterion-referenced shift is a recurring NEP 2020-aligned theme and parents confuse it with relative grading.
