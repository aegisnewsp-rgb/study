# Electrostatics: Coulomb's Law, Field & Potential Theory, Gauss's Law, and Conductors

Electrostatics forms the foundational cornerstone of classical electromagnetism in JEE Advanced Physics, consistently contributing 10% to 15% of the total marks across Paper 1 and Paper 2. Mastery in this domain requires moving far beyond plug-and-chug formula substitution into multi-concept synthesis: integrating vector calculus, surface and line integrals, spherical cavity electrostatics, electrostatic pressure on conductors, method of images, and dipole torque in non-uniform electric fields.

## Complete 3-Tier Study Guide

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Revision (1h–1d sprint)

For rapid formula recall before mock tests or examination day, keep these core formulas and field profiles ready:

- **Coulomb's Law in Vector Form**:
  $$\vec{F}_{12} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{q_1 q_2}{|\vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_2|^3} (\vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_2)$$
  where $\varepsilon_0 \approx 8.854 \times 10^{-12} \text{ F/m}$ and $\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \approx 8.99 \times 10^9 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{C}^2$. In a medium of dielectric constant $K$, force scales by $\frac{1}{K}$.
- **Electric Field & Potential Relationships**:
  $$\vec{E} = -\nabla V = -\left(\frac{\partial V}{\partial x}\hat{i} + \frac{\partial V}{\partial y}\hat{j} + \frac{\partial V}{\partial z}\hat{k}\right), \quad \Delta V = -\int_{\vec{r}_A}^{\vec{r}_B} \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{r}$$
  Equipotential surfaces are always perpendicular to local electric field lines; field lines point down the steepest gradient of potential.
- **Electric Dipole (Moment $\vec{p} = q\vec{d}$ pointing from $-q$ to $+q$)**:
  - Axial field ($r \gg d$): $\vec{E}_{axial} = \frac{2k\vec{p}}{r^3}$ (parallel to $\vec{p}$).
  - Equatorial field ($r \gg d$): $\vec{E}_{eq} = -\frac{k\vec{p}}{r^3}$ (antiparallel to $\vec{p}$).
  - Potential at general point $(r, \theta)$: $V(r, \theta) = \frac{kp\cos\theta}{r^2}$. Note that on the **equatorial plane** ($\theta = 90^\circ$), $V = 0$ everywhere!
  - Field at general point $(r, \theta)$: $E = \frac{kp}{r^3}\sqrt{1 + 3\cos^2\theta}$, with angle $\alpha$ to radius vector given by $\tan\alpha = \frac{1}{2}\tan\theta$.
  - Dipole in uniform field: Torque $\vec{\tau} = \vec{p} \times \vec{E}$; Potential Energy $U = -\vec{p} \cdot \vec{E}$. Net force is zero.
  - Dipole in non-uniform field: Net force $\vec{F} = (\vec{p} \cdot \nabla)\vec{E} = p \frac{\partial E}{\partial x}\hat{i}$ (for 1D gradient).
- **Gauss's Law**:
  $$\Phi_E = \oint_S \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{A} = \frac{q_{enc}}{\varepsilon_0}$$
- **Electrostatic Energy Density & Conductor Pressure**:
  - Electrostatic energy density in electric field: $u = \frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_0 E^2$.
  - Electrostatic outward pressure on charged conducting surface: $P = \frac{\sigma^2}{2\varepsilon_0}$.

#### 2. Electrostatics of Conductors

Conductors possess mobile free electrons that rearrange instantaneously under electrostatic conditions, yielding crucial physical properties tested in JEE Advanced:
1. **Internal Field is Zero**: $\vec{E}_{inside} = 0$. If there were an internal field, free charges would accelerate, violating the static equilibrium condition.
2. **Net Charge Resides Exclusively on the Outer Surface**: By Gauss's law applied to any Gaussian surface immediately beneath the conductor surface, $q_{enc} = 0$.
3. **Equipotential Body**: Because $\vec{E} = 0$ everywhere inside, $\Delta V = -\int \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{r} = 0$. The entire volume and surface of a conductor are at the identical potential.
4. **Surface Electric Field**: The electric field immediately outside a charged conductor is strictly normal to the surface and has magnitude:
   $$\vec{E} = \frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0}\hat{n}$$
   Contrast this with an isolated infinite non-conducting sheet where $E = \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}$. In a conductor, charges on all other parts of the surface contribute an equal field $\frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}$, doubling the local contribution immediately outside and canceling it inside!
5. **Surface Curvature Effect**: Surface charge density is inversely proportional to local radius of curvature:
   $$\sigma \propto \frac{1}{R_c}$$
   Sharp tips and needle points carry extremely high charge densities, leading to dielectric breakdown of surrounding air (**Corona Discharge** / Action of Points).

#### 3. Cavities in Conductors & Electrostatic Shielding

- **Empty Cavity in Conductor**: If an uncharged cavity exists inside a conductor and no charge is placed inside, the electric field inside the cavity is identically **zero**, regardless of external fields or charges on the outer boundary. This is the principle of **electrostatic shielding** (Faraday cage).
- **Charge $q$ Placed Inside Cavity**:
  - By Gauss's law enclosing the cavity wall, a charge of $-q$ is induced on the inner cavity surface, distributed non-uniformly to ensure $\vec{E} = 0$ in the metallic bulk.
  - By charge conservation, $+q$ appears on the outer surface of the conductor. The distribution of this outer charge depends solely on the external geometry and external fields, completely decoupled from the position of $q$ inside the cavity!

#### 1. The Spherical Cavity Superposition Theorem

A classic JEE Advanced problem involves finding the electric field inside an off-centre spherical cavity carved out of a uniformly charged solid non-conducting sphere with charge density $\rho$:

```
              Uniformly charged sphere (radius R₁, centre O₁)
                   +-----------------------------------+
                   |         . . . . . . . . .         |
                   |       .                   .       |
                   |     .      Cavity (O₂)      .     |
                   |    .     +---------------+   .    |
                   |   .      |     . P       |    .   |
                   |  .   O₁  |  <---d--->    |     .  |
                   |   .   *--------->*       |    .   |
                   |    .     +---------------+   .    |
                   |     .                       .     |
                   |       .                   .       |
                   |         . . . . . . . . .         |
                   +-----------------------------------+
```

Let the sphere have centre $O_1$ and the cavity have centre $O_2$, with displacement vector $\vec{d} = \vec{r}_{O_2} - \vec{r}_{O_1}$.
For any point $P$ inside the cavity, let its position vector relative to $O_1$ be $\vec{r}_1$ and relative to $O_2$ be $\vec{r}_2$. By vector addition:
$$\vec{r}_1 = \vec{d} + \vec{r}_2 \implies \vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_2 = \vec{d}$$

Using the principle of linear superposition, the cavity is treated as a solid sphere of charge density $+\rho$ plus a smaller sphere of charge density $-\rho$:
$$\vec{E}_1 = \frac{\rho \vec{r}_1}{3\varepsilon_0}, \quad \vec{E}_2 = \frac{-\rho \vec{r}_2}{3\varepsilon_0}$$
$$\vec{E}_{net} = \vec{E}_1 + \vec{E}_2 = \frac{\rho}{3\varepsilon_0}(\vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_2) = \frac{\rho \vec{d}}{3\varepsilon_0}$$

**Profound Result**: The electric field inside the cavity is **completely uniform in magnitude and direction**, parallel to the vector $\vec{d}$ joining the centres, completely independent of the coordinates of point $P$!

#### 2. Electrostatic Self-Energy of Charge Configurations

Electrostatic self-energy is the total work done in assembling a charge distribution from infinitesimal charges brought from infinity:
1. **Thin Spherical Shell of Radius $R$ and Charge $Q$**:
   Assembling from charge $q$ to $Q$:
   $$U = \int_0^Q V(q) dq = \int_0^Q \frac{kq}{R} dq = \frac{kQ^2}{2R} = \frac{Q^2}{8\pi\varepsilon_0 R}$$
   Alternatively, using field energy integration over all space ($E = 0$ inside, $E = \frac{kQ}{r^2}$ outside):
   $$U = \int_R^\infty \frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_0 E^2 (4\pi r^2 dr) = \frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_0 \int_R^\infty \left(\frac{Q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r^2}\right)^2 4\pi r^2 dr = \frac{Q^2}{8\pi\varepsilon_0 R}$$
2. **Uniformly Charged Solid Sphere of Radius $R$ and Charge $Q$**:
   Assembling shell by shell of radius $r$ and thickness $dr$:
   $$q(r) = Q\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^3, \quad dq = 4\pi r^2 \rho dr = \frac{3Q r^2}{R^3} dr$$
   $$U = \int_0^R \frac{k q(r) dq}{r} = \int_0^R \frac{k}{r} \left(\frac{Qr^3}{R^3}\right)\left(\frac{3Qr^2}{R^3}\right) dr = \frac{3kQ^2}{R^6}\int_0^R r^4 dr = \frac{3kQ^2}{5R} = \frac{3Q^2}{20\pi\varepsilon_0 R}$$

#### 3. Method of Electrical Images

When a point charge $q$ is positioned at a distance $d$ from an infinite, grounded conducting plane ($V = 0$ at $x = 0$):
- Boundary conditions require $V = 0$ everywhere on the plane $x = 0$.
- By the Uniqueness Theorem of Poisson's equation, the complex induced surface charge on the conducting plane can be mathematically replaced by a single fictitious **image charge $-q$** located at $-d$ (symmetrically behind the plane).
- The attractive force between the point charge and the grounded plane is:
  $$F = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{q^2}{(2d)^2} = \frac{q^2}{16\pi\varepsilon_0 d^2}$$
- The induced surface charge density on the plane at radial distance $\rho$ from the foot of the perpendicular is:
  $$\sigma(\rho) = -\frac{q d}{2\pi (d^2 + \rho^2)^{3/2}}$$
- Integrating $\sigma$ over the entire infinite plane yields exactly $-q$, validating total charge induction.

## Worked JEE Advanced Exam Problems

### Problem 1 (JEE Advanced Multi-Concept: Cavity & Force)

**Problem:** A non-conducting solid sphere of radius $R$ has a uniform volume charge density $\rho$. A spherical cavity of radius $R/2$ is carved out of the sphere such that the surface of the cavity passes through the centre of the sphere and touches its outer surface. A point charge $q$ is placed at the centre of the cavity. Find the electrostatic force exerted on the point charge $q$ by the remaining portion of the sphere.

**Step-by-Step Solution:**

1. **Superposition Analysis**:
   - The original solid sphere without cavity had centre $O_1$ and radius $R$.
   - The cavity has centre $O_2$ and radius $R/2$. The distance between the centres is $d = |\vec{r}_{O_2} - \vec{r}_{O_1}| = R/2$.
   - The remaining carved sphere is equivalent to:
     $$\text{Carved Sphere} = [\text{Solid Sphere of density } +\rho] + [\text{Spherical Region of density } -\rho]$$
2. **Field at Centre of Cavity ($O_2$) Before Placing $q$**:
   - The charge $q$ is located at point $O_2$.
   - The electric field produced at $O_2$ by the carved sphere is:
     $$\vec{E}_{carved}(O_2) = \vec{E}_{solid}(O_2) + \vec{E}_{negative}(O_2)$$
   - Due to the negative sphere centred at $O_2$, the field at its own centre is zero by symmetry:
     $$\vec{E}_{negative}(O_2) = 0$$
   - The field at $O_2$ produced by the complete solid sphere (with centre $O_1$) at a distance $r = R/2$ from $O_1$ is:
     $$\vec{E}_{solid}(O_2) = \frac{\rho \vec{r}}{3\varepsilon_0} = \frac{\rho (R/2)}{3\varepsilon_0}\hat{u} = \frac{\rho R}{6\varepsilon_0}\hat{u}$$
     where $\hat{u}$ is the unit vector pointing from $O_1$ to $O_2$.
3. **Electrostatic Force Calculation**:
   - Therefore, the net electric field at $O_2$ due to the remaining body of the sphere is:
     $$\vec{E}_{net}(O_2) = \frac{\rho R}{6\varepsilon_0}\hat{u}$$
   - The force on the charge $q$ placed at $O_2$ is:
     $$\vec{F} = q \vec{E}_{net}(O_2) = \frac{q \rho R}{6\varepsilon_0}\hat{u}$$
   - In terms of total initial charge $Q = \frac{4}{3}\pi R^3 \rho \implies \rho = \frac{3Q}{4\pi R^3}$:
     $$F = q \left(\frac{3Q}{4\pi R^3}\right) \frac{R}{6\varepsilon_0} = \frac{q Q}{8\pi\varepsilon_0 R^2} = \frac{k q Q}{2 R^2}$$
   - *Direction*: Directed radially outward along the line joining the sphere centre to the cavity centre.

### Problem 2 (JEE Advanced: Concentric Conducting Spherical Shells)

**Problem:** Two concentric conducting thin spherical shells of radii $R$ and $2R$ carry charges $Q$ and $-2Q$ respectively. The inner shell is now connected to the ground through a switch $S$. Determine:
1. The charge $q'$ on the inner shell after grounding.
2. The total heat generated during the grounding process.

**Step-by-Step Solution:**

1. **Condition for Grounding the Inner Shell**:
   - Grounding connects the inner shell to the Earth at zero potential: $V_{inner} = 0$.
   - Let the new charge on the inner shell be $q'$. The outer shell is isolated, so its total charge remains $-2Q$.
   - Potential of the inner shell ($r = R$):
     $$V_{inner} = \frac{k q'}{R} + \frac{k(-2Q)}{2R} = 0$$
     $$\frac{k q'}{R} - \frac{k Q}{R} = 0 \implies q' = +Q$$
   - Initially the inner shell had charge $Q$; after grounding it STILL has charge $+Q$!
   - Therefore, net charge flowed to/from ground is $\Delta q = q' - Q = Q - Q = 0$.
   - **Heat Generated**: Since no charge transferred across the switch ($q' = Q$), the energy of the system did not change:
     $$H = 0$$

## Common JEE Advanced Traps & Pitfalls

- **Trap 1: Dipole Potential on Equatorial Plane**: Many students confuse field and potential. For an electric dipole, the equatorial plane is an equipotential surface with $V = 0$ everywhere, but the electric field is **non-zero** ($E = \frac{kp}{r^3}$, directed opposite to $\vec{p}$).
- **Trap 2: Gauss's Law & Field Non-Zero Inside Cavity**: Gauss's law states $\oint \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{A} = 0$ if $q_{enc} = 0$. This does **not** mean $\vec{E} = 0$ everywhere on the surface unless symmetry guarantees that $E$ is constant and parallel to $d\vec{A}$.
- **Trap 3: Grounded Conductor Means Zero Potential, NOT Zero Charge**: Grounding forces $V = 0$, but charge will be induced on the conductor if other charges or fields exist in its vicinity.
- **Trap 4: Conductor Surface Field vs. Sheet Field**: Never confuse $E = \frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0}$ (immediately outside a conductor) with $E = \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}$ (near an isolated thin non-conducting sheet).
