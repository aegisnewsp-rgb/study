# Part 2 — Social Monologue — IELTS Listening Notes

Part 2 of the IELTS Listening test is a monologue in an everyday social context. Typical contexts: a guided tour, a speech about local facilities, arrangements for an event, a radio broadcast about community services. The recording runs about 5-7 minutes and has 10 questions. Part 2 is the second section of the test, slightly more difficult than Part 1.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Format:** 10 questions. Test types: plan/map/diagram labelling, summary completion, sentence completion, multiple choice, matching.
- **Transcript style:** one speaker, often with a clear structure (introduction, then several items in order).
- **Speed:** standard speaker pace with natural pausing.
- **Order of answers:** questions follow the recording in order.
- **Predictable content:** places on a map, stages of a process, items in a list, opinions or preferences.
- **Common distractor:** the speaker may mention several options before settling on one. The answer is the final choice.

#### Examiner traps

- Missing the answer because it comes after a long pause — the answer follows the speaker's first explicit statement of it.
- Confusing similar items on a plan or map — read the labels carefully before listening.
- Word count violations on sentence completion ("NO MORE THAN THREE WORDS").

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Question types in Part 2

- **Plan/map/diagram labelling:** label locations on a map or items on a diagram. The recording usually walks through the layout in a logical order (e.g. clockwise).
- **Summary completion:** a paragraph summarising the monologue with gaps. Usually a word from the recording fills the gap.
- **Sentence completion:** a series of incomplete sentences. The recording completes each one in order.
- **Multiple choice:** choose one of three options. Common distractor: the speaker mentions all three but only one is the answer.
- **Matching:** match items to lists (e.g. speaker to opinion, item to feature).

#### Active listening strategy

1. **Predict:** before listening, read the questions and predict the type of information expected (location, item, opinion).
2. **Note keywords:** underline the keywords in the questions to focus your listening.
3. **Listen for transitions:** the speaker usually signals changes ("next," "moving on," "another thing").
4. **Verify:** check that the answer matches the question format (singular/plural, word count).

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where Part 2 appears across IELTS Listening

Part 2 is always the second section, accounting for 10 of the 40 questions. It is the section where plan/map labelling is most common — practice these specifically.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Map items are always labelled in order." Usually yes, but the speaker may jump around the map (e.g. by category rather than spatially).
- "Multiple choice has one obvious answer." Often there are three plausible options; the question wording tells you which one to choose.
- "I can finish early and check answers." Use any spare time to re-read questions and predict answers for upcoming items.

#### Exam technique

- For map items, label as the speaker labels them, in the order they are mentioned.
- For summary-completion items, fill in the gap as soon as you hear the answer — the next gap follows in order.
- For multiple choice, listen for the keyword in the question (e.g. "first," "main," "best") — the answer is the option that matches.
