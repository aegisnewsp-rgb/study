# Vocabulary

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Vocabulary in NDA GAT English measures your command of word meaning, usage, and formation. Roughly 20–25 questions per NDA paper (out of the English portion inside GAT) directly test synonyms, antonyms, idioms, phrasal verbs, one-word substitutions, and contextual meaning.

| Term | What it tests | Quick example |
|------|---------------|---------------|
| Synonym | Similar meaning, watch connotation | *famous* (neutral), *renowned* (positive), *notorious* (negative) |
| Antonym | Opposite meaning | *generous* → *stingy* |
| Idiom | Figurative fixed phrase | *bite the bullet* = face hardship bravely |
| Phrasal Verb | Verb + particle shifts meaning | *give up* (surrender), *put off* (postpone) |
| One-word Substitution | Single word for a phrase | *somnambulist* = one who walks in sleep |

- Root + affix pattern: **bene** (good) + **dict** (speak) + **-ion** → **benediction** (a blessing).
- Denotation = literal dictionary meaning; connotation = emotional colouring (positive/negative/neutral).

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"SIA-PO-C"** — **S**ynonyms, **I**dioms, **A**ntonyms, **P**hrasal verbs, **O**ne-word substitution, **C**ontext. These six question types cover ~95% of NDA vocabulary items. Master one mnemonic per type and you have the paper's vocabulary skeleton.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Word Formation Mechanism
English words stack in layers: **root** (core meaning, often Latin/Greek) + **prefix** (before) + **suffix** (after). Knowing roots lets you decode unfamiliar words on sight — a frequent NDA cloze-test strategy.

- **bene** (good) + **volent** (wishing) → *benevolent* (well-wishing, kind).
- **mal** (bad) + **diction* → *malediction* (a curse).
- Common prefixes: *anti-*, *mono-*, *sub-*, *trans-*, *inter-*; common suffixes: *-able*, *-tion*, *-ous*, *-ify*, *-ment*.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix
Near-synonyms and confusables are where NDA loses careless candidates. The table below separates look-alike pairs that appear almost every paper.

| Confusable pair | Correct sense | Memory cue |
|-----------------|---------------|------------|
| *affect* vs *effect* | *affect* = verb (to influence); *effect* = noun (result) | **A**ffect = **A**ction (verb) |
| *principal* vs *principle* | *principal* = head/chief/money sum; *principle* = rule/belief | *principal* ends in **-pal** (your **pal** is the boss) |
| *stationary* vs *stationery* | *stationary* = not moving; *stationery* = paper goods | *stationery* contains **-er** for **paper** |
| *allude* vs *elude* | *allude* = refer indirectly; *elude* = escape | *elude* starts with **e-** like **escape** |
| *complement* vs *compliment* | *complement* = completes; *compliment* = praise | *compliment* has **i** like **praise** |

> 📌 **Formula Check:** Word meaning = **denotation** (literal) + **connotation** (emotional charge) + **context** (surrounding sentence). All three must align before you mark an option correct.

#### Standard Question Types
1. **Direct synonym/antonym MCQ** — pick the closest match, watch polarity.
2. **Idiom/phrase meaning** — match figurative meaning, never literal.
3. **Phrasal verb selection** — verb + particle together carry the meaning.
4. **One-word substitution** — choose the most precise, narrow term.
5. **Cloze/contextual** — use contrast signals (*but, although, despite*) and collocations.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem
**Question:** Choose the word closest in meaning to **RENOWNED**:
(a) *Famous* (b) *Notorious* (c) *Renowned* (d) *Obscure*

#### Solution:
Step 1 — Identify connotation of the stem word. *Renowned* carries a **positive** connotation (respected, celebrated for good reasons).
Step 2 — Score each option:
- (a) *famous* = neutral; known widely, no value judgement.
- (b) *notorious* = negative; famous for something bad.
- (c) *renowned* = positive; identical to the stem — usually the "echo" trap and not the intended answer unless no closer fit exists.
- (d) *obscure* = antonym; unknown, hidden.
Step 3 — In NDA's standard style, the positive-connotation synonym that is *not* identical to the stem is the safest pick: **(a) Famous** is closest among the alternatives, since (b) flips polarity and (d) reverses it. If (c) were rephrased as *illustrious* or *celebrated*, it would win.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students jump on the **negative-sounding word** (*notorious*) thinking "famous = bad = notorious". Wrong. *Renowned* is **positively** loaded. Always read the connotation flag before matching.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Precision Traps
NDA vocabulary items increasingly test **shade**, not surface meaning. Below are advanced traps that decide top ranks.

| Trap type | What examiners exploit | How to defend |
|-----------|------------------------|---------------|
| Near-synonym polarity | *famous* / *renowned* / *notorious* / *illustrious* / *celebrated* — all "well-known" but with different value charges | Tag each word as + / − / neutral before matching |
| False-cognate roots | *sensible* (reasonable, EN) vs *sensible* (sensitive, ES/FR) | Stick to English usage in the stem, not borrowed sense |
| Idiom literalism | *kick the bucket*, *spill the beans*, *bite the dust* — read figuratively | Memorise 50 high-frequency idioms; never translate word-for-word |
| Phrasal-verb particle swap | *bring up* (raise/topic) ≠ *bring about* (cause) ≠ *bring off* (succeed) | Treat particle as inseparable; learn verb + particle as a chunk |
| Over-broad substitution | "one who hates humanity" → *misanthrope*, not *misogynist* (women) or *misandrist* (men) | Match the *precise* referent, not the root family |
| Context-flip words | *although*, *despite*, *however*, *contrary to* — they invert the following clause | Underline contrast markers before choosing a meaning |

#### Etymology and Foreign-Origin Cues
Roughly 60% of English vocabulary originates from Latin, Greek, or French. NDA cloze passages lean on this for advanced items. Recognising a root lets you decode a word you've never seen.

- **Auto-** (Greek: self) → *autonomous*, *autopsy*, *autocrat*.
- **Chrono-** (Greek: time) → *chronology*, *synchronise*, *chronicle*.
- **Magna / Magn-** (Latin: great) → *magnify*, *magnanimous*, *magnate*.
- **Voy- / Vi-** (Latin: way) → *voyage*, *deviate*, *obvious*.

#### Links to Adjacent NDA Topics
Strong vocabulary feeds three other GAT sub-areas: **Reading Comprehension** (decoding dense prose), **Grammar/Sentence Correction** (choosing the right register), and **General English usage** in the SSB interview. A vocabulary weakness bleeds into every English-marking item.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts
1. **Antonym with trap:** Write three sentences — one using *famous* neutrally, one using *renowned* positively, one using *notorious* negatively — then justify why *infamous* cannot replace *notorious* in all three.
2. **Phrasal-verb cluster drill:** For each base verb (*put, bring, take, call, give, look, run, set*), list at least four particle combinations with distinct meanings. NDA has tested 3+ items from the same verb family in a single paper.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Don't memorise a "synonym list" as flat pairs. NDA scores higher when you can rank three near-synonyms on a **positive–neutral–negative** axis. Lists without polarity are half-prepared.
