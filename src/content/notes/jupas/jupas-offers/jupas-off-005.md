# Interview and Aptitude Test Preparation — JUPAS Notes

This topic covers interview and aptitude test preparation for JUPAS programmes. Some programmes (especially in Medicine, Dentistry, Education, Architecture, Law, and certain professional degrees) require an interview, aptitude test or portfolio review after Main Round. Preparation: read the programme's admissions page for the interview format (panel interview, MMI, group task); review the programme's learning outcomes and recent curriculum; practice common questions on motivation, subject interest and career plans.

> Verify the live interview and aptitude test requirements on each university's admissions page before planning revision.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Programmes requiring interviews/aptitude tests**: Medicine, Dentistry, Education, Architecture, Law, certain professional degrees, some competitive programmes.
- **Interview formats**: panel interview (one or more interviewers), MMI (Multiple Mini Interview), group task, portfolio review.
- **Aptitude tests**: subject-specific tests, critical thinking tests, situational judgement tests.
- **Source**: published on each programme's admissions page.
- **Timing**: after Main Round offers are announced; the interview schedule is published by the institutions.

#### Examiner traps

- Missing the interview schedule — forfeits the offer.
- Under-preparing for the MMI format — candidates who haven't practised MMI may be at a disadvantage.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Common interview formats

**Panel interview**: one or more interviewers ask the candidate questions. Typical questions:

- Why do you want to study this programme?
- What are your career goals?
- What experiences have prepared you for this programme?
- How do you handle challenges or failure?
- What recent developments in the field interest you?

**Multiple Mini Interview (MMI)**: a series of short stations (typically 6-10), each with a different scenario or question. Candidates rotate through the stations. MMI tests:

- Communication skills.
- Critical thinking.
- Ethical reasoning.
- Teamwork.
- Cultural sensitivity.

**Group task**: candidates work together on a problem or scenario. The interviewers observe the interaction and assess the candidate's contributions.

**Portfolio review**: candidates present a portfolio of work (relevant for Architecture, Design, Visual Arts).

#### Common aptitude tests

- **Subject-specific tests**: test of subject knowledge (Mathematics, Physics, etc.). Used by some science programmes.
- **Critical thinking tests**: test of analytical and reasoning skills. Used by some Law and Business programmes.
- **Situational judgement tests (SJT)**: candidates respond to hypothetical workplace scenarios. Used by some Education programmes.
- **English language tests**: some programmes test English ability separately.

#### Preparation strategy

**Research the programme**:

- Read the programme's curriculum, learning outcomes, and recent publications.
- Identify the programme's distinctive features (e.g. problem-based learning, research opportunities, clinical placements).
- Identify the institution's research strengths.

**Practice common questions**:

- Motivation: why this programme, why this institution.
- Subject interest: what aspect of the subject fascinates you.
- Career plans: short-term and long-term goals.
- Personal qualities: strengths, weaknesses, experiences.
- Ethical scenarios: how would you handle a difficult situation.

**Mock interviews**:

- Practice with a teacher, friend, or family member.
- Record yourself and listen back for improvements.
- Time yourself; ensure you can give concise, structured responses.

**For MMI**: practice the MMI format specifically. Each station is short (typically 5-10 minutes); practice answering under time pressure.

**Portfolio review**: prepare a clean, well-organised portfolio with relevant work.

#### Common mistakes

- Generic answers — every interview answer should be tailored to the specific programme and institution.
- Memorised scripts — interviewers recognise memorised responses.
- Lack of subject knowledge — interview questions often probe subject understanding.
- Poor communication — unclear, rambling, or evasive answers.
- Negativity — complaining about previous experiences or institutions.

#### Common misconceptions

- "The interview is just a formality." No — the interview can be decisive for borderline candidates.
- "Subject knowledge isn't tested in interviews." No — many programmes test subject understanding in the interview.
- "All interviews are the same." No — formats vary widely by programme.
