# Direction Sense

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

- **Definition:** Direction Sense questions track a person's **facing direction**, **turns**, and **straight-line movements** on a 2D plane marked by cardinal (N, S, E, W) and inter-cardinal (NE, NW, SE, SW) points.
- **Cardinal–angular mapping:** N = 0°/360°, E = 90°, S = 180°, W = 270°; NE = 45°, SE = 135°, SW = 225°, NW = 315°.
- **Turn values:** Right turn = **+90° clockwise**, Left turn = **−90° anticlockwise**, U-turn = **180°**.
- **Net rotation formula:** New facing = (Initial facing + 90°R − 90°L + 180°·U) mod 360°.
- **Displacement shortcut:** Resolve each move into East (x) and North (y) components, sum, then apply Pythagoras: distance = √(x² + y²), tan θ = x/y for final bearing.
- **High-yield CLAT pointers:** (1) Always distinguish **facing** from **movement**. (2) For "starts facing North, walks 5 km, turns right…" the turn is about the *new* position with the *new* facing. (3) Shadow questions use the rule **15° per hour from 12 noon IST**.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Compass Model
Treat each direction as a unit vector. N = (0, +1), S = (0, −1), E = (+1, 0), W = (−1, 0). Inter-cardinal vectors are diagonal sums, e.g. NE = (+1/√2, +1/√2). Every movement of d km adds d times that unit vector to the person's coordinates.

#### Mechanics of a Turn
A turn *changes the facing*, and the *next* movement occurs in that new facing. If facing East and you "turn right," you now face South; the subsequent walk of 5 km reduces y by 5, not x. This is the single most-tested mechanic in CLAT Direction Sense.

#### Worked Relationship (turn-only)
Start facing **N**. Sequence: Right → Right → Left → U-turn → Right.
Net = +90 + 90 − 90 + 180 + 90 = **+360° ≡ 0°** → still facing **N**.
Generic rule: **Net clockwise rotation = Σ(right turns) − Σ(left turns), and final facing = (initial + net) mod 360°**.

#### Vector-Addition Method (movement-only)
Walker starts at origin, faces N.
1. Walk 4 km N → (0, +4)
2. Turn right (now E), walk 3 km E → (+3, +4)
3. Turn right (now S), walk 4 km S → (+3, 0)
4. Turn left (now E), walk 2 km E → (+5, 0)

Final position = (5, 0) i.e. **5 km East of start**, facing **East**. Distance = 5 km, bearing = 090°.

#### Typical CLAT Patterns
| Pattern | What is asked | Key tool |
|---|---|---|
| Final direction / distance | After a mixed turn-walk sequence | Vector addition + mod-360° |
| Minimum-turn routing | Turn from facing A to facing B using fewest 90° turns | min(|Δ|, 360°−|Δ|) ÷ 90° |
| Shadow / clock | Direction of shadow at given IST time | (t − 12)·15° west of North |
| Distance from start | Given final direction θ and total path D | East = D·sin θ, North = D·cos θ |

#### Common Traps
- Reading "turns left and walks" as a left-then-walk when actually both occur in one step — track facing before the turn.
- Confusing 45° turns with 90°; CLAT almost exclusively uses 90° turns plus cardinal movement, but **premise twist questions** insert 45°.
- Counting a "U-turn" as two rights (it is a single 180° rotation).

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Case 1 — Minimum Number of 90° Turns
Given current facing α and target facing β (both in degrees clockwise from N), required clockwise turn = (β − α + 360°) mod 360°. Divide by 90° to get the count. Example: α = N (0°), β = SW (225°). Required = 225°, so **three right turns** (or one left turn of 135° rounded up to two rights + one U-turn = 3 moves). Always check the anticlockwise alternative (360° − required) and pick the smaller.

#### Edge Case 2 — Distance from Start When Only Bearing Is Given
If a person walks a total path D and ends at bearing θ (clockwise from N) directly from start (rare in CLAT but appears in merged-assertion items), decompose as **East = D·sin θ**, **North = D·cos θ**. For θ = 30° NE-ish: East = D/2, North = D·(√3/2). This is Pythagoras in disguise and lets you back-solve distance.

#### Edge Case 3 — Shadow Mechanics
At 12 noon IST the sun is due South, so the **shortest shadow of a vertical pole points North**. As Earth rotates 15° per hour anticlockwise (viewed from above North Pole), the sun's apparent azimuth shifts Westward, and the shadow swings **Westward at 15°/hr**. At 3 PM, shadow points **NW (315°)**; at 9 AM, **NE (045°)**.

#### Connection to Adjacent Topics
Direction Sense shares the vector framework with **Syllogisms-with-Directions** (rare in CLAT) and **Coding–Decoding rotation ciphers**. The same mod-360° wrap-around appears in **Clock–Calendar problems**, making them excellent cross-revision material.

#### Classic Mistakes to Avoid
1. Treating "facing North, turns to his right" as movement — it is a *rotation*, not a step.
2. Forgetting that after a 180° turn, "left" and "right" of the person swap relative to the original frame.
3. Double-counting distance when a problem says "walks 5 km, returns 2 km" — net displacement ≠ path length.

#### Practice Prompts
1. A walks 6 km North from P, turns right and walks 8 km, turns right again and walks 6 km. How far is she from P and in which direction?
2. Facing West, Ramesh takes three consecutive right turns. What direction does he face now, and what is the minimum number of additional 90° turns needed to face South?
