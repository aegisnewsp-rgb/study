# Grammar Across Units — Kuwait Secondary Certificate English Notes

This note consolidates the six cross-unit grammar strands examined at terminal level in the Kuwait MOE Grade 12 Over To You syllabus: direct and reported speech (Unit 7), inverted sentences for emphasis (Unit 8), causative verbs (Unit 9), past perfect simple and continuous (Unit 10), passive voice with modal verbs (Unit 11), and relative clauses (Unit 12). Each strand appears as a discrete grammar item on the terminal English paper. The cross-unit grammar carries the most marks of any single sub-domain on the language-focused section of the terminal paper.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Direct → reported speech:** "I am happy" → She said she was happy. Tense shifts back one step; pronouns shift accordingly.
- **Inversion after negative adverbials:** "Never have I seen such beauty." Pattern: adverbial + auxiliary + subject + main verb.
- **Causative:** "I had my hair cut." Pattern: have/get + object + past participle.
- **Past perfect simple:** "I had finished the climb by the time the sun set." Used for an action completed before another past event.
- **Past perfect continuous:** "I had been climbing for six hours when the storm hit." Used for a duration up to another past event.
- **Passive + modal:** "The experiment should be repeated." Pattern: modal + be + past participle.
- **Defining relative clause:** "The book that I borrowed is overdue." No commas.
- **Non-defining relative clause:** "The teacher, who is very experienced, will help us." Commas required.

#### Examiner traps

- Reported speech time shifts: today → that day, yesterday → the day before, tomorrow → the next day, here → there, this → that.
- "I had my car stealing" — wrong; passive needs past participle: "I had my car stolen."
- Past perfect needs a past reference point — without one, it sounds awkward.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Direct and reported speech

When converting direct speech to reported speech, the reporting verb tense shifts back one step:

- Present simple → Past simple: "I work" → She said she worked.
- Present continuous → Past continuous: "I am working" → She said she was working.
- Present perfect → Past perfect: "I have finished" → She said she had finished.
- Will → Would: "I will go" → She said she would go.
- Past simple → Past perfect: "I went" → She said she had gone.

Modals shift too: can → could, may → might, must → had to. Time expressions shift: today → that day, yesterday → the day before, tomorrow → the next day, here → there, this → that, these → those, ago → before.

For questions, use whether/if or the wh-word with statement word order: "Do you sleep well?" → She asked (me) whether/if I slept well. "Where do you live?" → She asked where I lived.

#### Inverted sentences

Inversion swaps subject and auxiliary for emphasis after certain adverbials:

- Negative adverbials at the start: Never, rarely, seldom, hardly, scarcely, not only, no sooner, under no circumstances, on no account, nowhere, in no way.
- "Only after/when/if/because" at the start.
- "So... that" and "Such... that" for emphasis.

The pattern is: adverbial + auxiliary + subject + main verb. When there is no auxiliary in the normal sentence, add "did" (past) or "do/does" (present).

#### Causative verbs

Causative constructions mean "I arranged for someone else to do it." Two forms:

- "Have + object + past participle" (more common in British English)
- "Get + object + past participle" (more common in American English, more informal)

Tense usage:

- Present simple: "I have my hair cut every month."
- Present continuous: "I am having my hair cut right now."
- Past simple: "I had my hair cut yesterday."
- Future simple: "I will have my hair cut tomorrow."

The agent (the person doing the work) can be mentioned with "by": "I had my hair cut by Maria."

#### Past perfect simple vs continuous

- Past perfect simple focuses on completion: "I had read the book (before the exam)."
- Past perfect continuous focuses on duration: "I had been reading the book (for three hours when the phone rang)."

Combined: "I had been reading for an hour when I had finished the chapter." (continuous for duration, simple for completion).

#### Passive voice with modals

Structure: modal + be + past participle.

- "The experiment can be repeated."
- "The data should be analysed."
- "The samples must be collected carefully."
- "The findings will be published."

Perfect passive: modal + have + been + past participle. "The data should have been analysed by now."

#### Relative clauses

Defining relative clause: essential to the meaning, no commas. "The book that I borrowed is overdue."

Non-defining relative clause: adds extra information, commas required. "My brother, who lives in Kuwait City, is visiting next week." Cannot use "that" in non-defining clauses.

Relative pronouns:

- who/whom (people)
- which (things)
- that (people or things, defining only)
- whose (possession)
- where (place)
- when (time)
- why (reason)

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where the cross-unit grammar appears across the terminal English paper

- **Transformation items:** each of the six strands typically gets one transformation exercise (e.g. change a direct quote into reported speech).
- **Gap-fill items:** the grammar items also appear in multiple-choice or open-cloze contexts.
- **Error correction items:** the terminal paper usually has one error-correction exercise testing the cross-unit grammar.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Reported speech tenses always shift back two steps." No — they shift back one step.
- "All modals shift in reported speech." Must → had to, but "had better" stays "had better"; would, could, might, should, ought to do not shift.
- "Inversion is always optional." No — inversion is grammatically required after the listed adverbials.
- "Past perfect means before now." No — past perfect means before a reference point in the past.

#### Exam technique

- For reported-speech transformations, write the reporting clause (She said...) before the reported clause — the reporting clause is the method mark.
- For inversion transformations, identify the negative adverbial first, then build the inverted pattern.
- For passive-modal transformations, identify the modal first; the structure is modal + be + past participle.
- For relative-clause items, decide whether the clause is defining or non-defining first — that determines whether "that" is allowed and whether commas are required.
