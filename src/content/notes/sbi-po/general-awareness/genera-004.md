# Government Budgeting and Taxation

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The **Union Budget** is the Centre's annual financial statement laid before Parliament under Article 112, covering receipts, expenditure, and fiscal priorities for a fiscal year (1 April – 31 March). Receipts split into **Revenue Receipts** (tax + non-tax, recurring) and **Capital Receipts** (borrowings, recoveries, disinvestment). Expenditure mirrors this split: **Revenue Expenditure** (salaries, pensions, subsidies, interest) versus **Capital Expenditure** (asset creation, loans to states, investments).

The four deficits examiners love:

- **Revenue Deficit** = Revenue Expenditure − Revenue Receipts
- **Fiscal Deficit** = Total Expenditure − (Revenue Receipts + Non-debt Capital Receipts)
- **Primary Deficit** = Fiscal Deficit − Interest Payments
- **Effective Revenue Deficit** = Revenue Deficit − Grants for Capital Asset creation

**Direct taxes** (income tax, corporate tax) are levied on the assessee; **indirect taxes** (GST, customs, excise) are levied on consumption. A **progressive tax** takes a higher share from higher incomes; a **regressive tax** does the opposite.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Budget Structure

The Budget has two accounts. The **Revenue Account** records revenue receipts (tax + non-tax) and revenue expenditure (recurring, no asset creation). The **Capital Account** records capital receipts such as market borrowings, small savings, and disinvestment proceeds, along with capital expenditure that builds assets or repays liabilities. The capital account must balance through net borrowing because the gap is funded via non-debt and debt capital receipts.

#### Deficit Formulas and What Each One Tells You

| Deficit | Formula | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Revenue Deficit | RE − RR | Shortfall after meeting day-to-day spending from current income |
| Fiscal Deficit | TE − (RR + Non-debt CR) | Total borrowing the government must do this year |
| Primary Deficit | FD − Interest Payments | Borrowing needed beyond rolling over past debt |
| Effective Revenue Deficit | RD − Grants creating capital assets | Cleaner measure of revenue-side stress |

`RR` = revenue receipts, `RE` = revenue expenditure, `TE` = total expenditure, `CR` = capital receipts. All figures appear in Budget at a Glance as Actuals (final), Revised (updated current year), and Budget (next year forecast).

#### Direct versus Indirect Taxes

Direct taxes are paid by the assessee and can be progressive, like income tax slabs and corporation tax. Indirect taxes fall on goods and services — GST, customs duty, and excise — and tend to be regressive in incidence. Tax buoyancy (Δ tax revenue ÷ Δ tax base) gauges how responsive tax collections are to GDP growth. A buoyancy above 1 means tax revenue grows faster than its base.

- Income tax, corporate tax, securities transaction tax → **Direct**
- GST (CGST, SGST, IGST), customs duty, stamp duty → **Indirect**
- Dividend, interest receipts, profit from RBI → **Non-tax revenue**

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Why the Distinctions Matter

Capital expenditure is not automatically "good" and revenue expenditure is not automatically "bad". A subsidy for DAP fertiliser is revenue expenditure but may ease food inflation; a statue built on government land is capital but may yield no return. The FRBM framework targets a fiscal deficit of 3.5% of GDP by 2025-26 with a 0.5% annual glide path, but it tolerates overrun if capital outlay rises. Quality of spend — asset creation versus consumption — matters more than headline numbers.

#### Common Traps in Numerical Questions

1. Subtracting **interest payments twice** when the question lists them inside revenue expenditure and again as a deficit adjustment.
2. Treating **disinvestment proceeds** as revenue receipts; they are non-debt capital receipts and do reduce fiscal deficit.
3. Confusing **Revenue Deficit with Fiscal Deficit** when only revenue items are quoted.
4. Using **Revised Estimates** as if they were Actuals.
5. Counting **GST compensation cess** as part of divisible pool — it is earmarked.

#### Worked Micro-Example

Suppose Revenue Expenditure = ₹20 lakh crore, Revenue Receipts = ₹16 lakh crore, Non-debt Capital Receipts = ₹2 lakh crore, Interest Payments = ₹3 lakh crore.

- Revenue Deficit = 20 − 16 = **₹4 lakh cr**
- Fiscal Deficit = 20 − (16 + 2) = **₹2 lakh cr** (when only revenue expenditure is total expenditure here)
- Primary Deficit = 2 − 3 = **−₹1 lakh cr** (negative implies interest alone exceeds borrowing need, possible only with illustrative figures)

#### Practice Prompts

- Why is a low revenue deficit a stronger sign of fiscal health than a low fiscal deficit alone?
- If the government raises the GST rate from 18% to 20% and collections rise 15%, compute buoyancy and comment.
