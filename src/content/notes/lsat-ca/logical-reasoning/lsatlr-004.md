# Flaw Identification and Reasoning Errors — LSAT Logical Reasoning

Flaw items ask what is wrong with the argument's reasoning. The LSAT tests a defined list of common reasoning errors, including ad hominem, straw man, false dilemma, circular reasoning, hasty generalisation, slippery slope, post hoc, and equivocation. Recognising the flaw quickly is the skill; the answer choice usually names the flaw in standard LSAT language.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### Common LSAT flaws

- **Ad hominem:** attacking the person making the argument rather than the argument itself.
- **Straw man:** misrepresenting the opposing position to make it easier to attack.
- **False dilemma (false dichotomy):** presenting only two options when more exist.
- **Circular reasoning:** the conclusion is used as one of the premises.
- **Hasty generalisation:** drawing a broad conclusion from a small or unrepresentative sample.
- **Slippery slope:** claiming that one step will inevitably lead to a chain of negative events without evidence.
- **Post hoc (false cause):** assuming that because B follows A, A caused B.
- **Equivocation:** using a word in different senses within the same argument.
- **Appeal to authority:** claiming a claim is true because an authority endorses it (without relevant expertise).
- **Appeal to popular opinion:** claiming a claim is true because many people believe it.

#### One-pass drill

1. Identify the conclusion and the premises.
2. Ask: "what assumption links the premises to the conclusion?"
3. Ask: "is that assumption justified by the premises?"
4. The flaw is the unjustified step.

#### Examiner traps

- Naming a flaw that is not in the argument. The LSAT uses specific flaw language; learn the standard terms.
- Confusing hasty generalisation with slippery slope. Hasty generalisation is about the sample; slippery slope is about the chain of events.
- Confusing ad hominem with appeal to authority. Ad hominem attacks the person; appeal to authority appeals to the person.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Strategy for flaw items

1. **Identify the conclusion and the premises.**
2. **Find the assumption** linking them.
3. **Test the assumption:** is it justified? If not, what kind of error is it?
4. **Match to a named flaw.** The LSAT uses standard flaw names; pick the one that fits.

#### Detailed flaw descriptions

**Ad hominem:** "X says P, but X is biased/ignorant. Therefore P is false." The personal attack does not affect the truth of P.

**Straw man:** "X says P. P implies Q (which X does not say). Q is absurd. Therefore P is false." The misrepresentation of P makes the attack easier.

**False dilemma:** "Either we do A or we do B." But there are other options (C, D, ...). The argument ignores them.

**Circular reasoning:** "P is true because Q. Q is true because P." The conclusion is one of the premises.

**Hasty generalisation:** "Three X are Y. Therefore all X are Y." The sample is too small or unrepresentative.

**Slippery slope:** "If we do A, then B, then C, then disaster. Therefore we should not do A." The chain is not justified.

**Post hoc:** "B followed A. Therefore A caused B." Correlation is not causation; other causes are possible.

**Equivocation:** "All banks are financial institutions. Some financial institutions are on the river. Therefore some banks are on the river." "Banks" is used in two different senses.

#### Practice routine

- Take 10 flaw items. For each, name the flaw before looking at the choices.
- Time yourself: under 1.5 minutes per item.
- Keep a personal "flaw list" with examples. Re-read the list weekly.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How flaw items connect to the other LR skills

- **Assumption items** ask for the missing link; flaw items ask what is wrong with that link.
- **Strengthen/weaken items** often address a flaw by supporting or undermining the questionable link.
- **Parallel reasoning items** require recognising the same flaw in a different argument.

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"Any error is a flaw."** The LSAT tests specific, named flaws. Learn the names and the patterns.
- **"Ad hominem is any personal attack."** Ad hominem is a specific fallacy: dismissing the argument because of the person, not the merits.
- **"Post hoc proves causation."** Post hoc only shows sequence; causation requires ruling out alternatives.

#### Exam technique

- For flaw items, identify the conclusion, the premises, and the assumption.
- Test the assumption. If it is unjustified, name the flaw.
- Match the flaw to the standard LSAT name.
- Use the platform's flag-for-review function for items you cannot answer.
