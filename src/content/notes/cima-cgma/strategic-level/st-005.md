# Practical Experience and the CGMA Designation

Every other page on this site is about an examination. This one is about the part of the CGMA that is not an examination at all, and it is the part most candidates underestimate.

AICPA & CIMA states that passing the examinations is necessary but not sufficient: the CGMA designation also requires three years of verified relevant professional experience, assessed through CIMA's Practical Experience Requirements process. If you are planning your timeline, that three years runs alongside the study, not after it — and the evidence you will need has to start accumulating from your first day in a relevant role.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> The designation is not the exams. Three years of verified relevant experience runs alongside your study, and the evidence has to start accumulating now.

**The requirement.** AICPA & CIMA states that passing the examinations is necessary but not sufficient. The CGMA designation also requires three years of verified relevant professional experience, assessed through CIMA's Practical Experience Requirements process.

**Relevant and verified are two separate tests.**

- **Relevant** means the work sits inside the finance, accounting or equivalent scope the qualification describes, at a level above routine clerical processing. Time in a role is not automatically countable time.
- **Verified** means someone qualified has attested to it. Two years of solid reporting work with no record of who confirmed it is weaker evidence than eighteen months documented properly.

**Four things to capture every month.** What you did, at what level of responsibility, what the outcome was, and who could verify it.

**Outcome, not task.** "Reconciled the ledgers" is a task. "Identified and corrected an unreconciled suspense balance that persisted across two period ends" is evidence of judgement — and judgement is what the designation attests to.

**The timeline error.** If you assumed you would be a designated CGMA eighteen months after starting, the practical requirement makes that plan wrong by two years.

### What "verified relevant" means

Relevant means the work has to sit within the finance, accounting or equivalent scope the qualification describes, at a level above routine clerical processing. Time in a role is not automatically countable time.

Verified means someone qualified has attested to it. This is the practical distinction that catches candidates out: two years of solid financial reporting work with no record of who confirmed it is weaker evidence than eighteen months documented properly, because the record and the experience are separate requirements and CIMA assesses both.

Start the record now. Most people assume they can reconstruct evidence later and most cannot reconstruct it accurately, particularly for a role that has ended.

### Keep the evidence as you work

The habit that makes this easy is a monthly note, not an annual scramble. Four things are worth capturing each month: what you did, at what level of responsibility, what the outcome was, and who could verify it.

Record the outcome, not just the task. "Reconciled the ledgers" is a task; "identified and corrected an unreconciled suspense balance that had persisted across two period ends" is evidence of judgement, and judgement is what the designation is attesting to.

Note the organisational context as well as the action. Scale, sector and reporting line all tell the reviewer whether the work was genuinely at the required level.

Keep a contemporaneous record rather than a reconstruction. A verifier signing something they did not witness is not a strength, and experience offices do check.

### Where the three years fits with the examinations

The examinations can be taken in any order and on demand, so most candidates sit Objective Tests while working and take the Case Studies around promotion. Two consequences follow.

First, start the practical experience clock early even if you are not sure you will stay in the profession. If you change jobs, the hours can often be aggregated, but only if they were recorded properly at the time.

Second, do not assume the examinations come first in the labour market's thinking. Many employers and recruiters look at the designation status, which is why candidates who pass all nine Objective Tests but cannot evidence three years sometimes find that the qualification has not yet changed what they can apply for.

### 🔴 Deep — Mastery (3mo+)
> Where the marks are won, and the boundary cases that separate a competent script from a top one.

#### Relevant and verified, tested separately

The two requirements fail independently, and the pairing that surprises people is long experience with no verification. "Relevant" is a judgement about scope and level: the work must sit inside the finance, accounting or equivalent scope the qualification describes, above routine clerical processing. "Verified" is a record: someone qualified has attested to it. Two years of solid financial reporting work with no record of who confirmed it is weaker evidence than eighteen months documented properly, because the record and the experience are assessed as two separate things.

#### What makes a record assessable rather than merely present

| Weak — a task list | Strong — evidence of judgement |
|---|---|
| "Reconciled the ledgers" | "Identified and corrected an unreconciled suspense balance persisting across two period ends" |
| "Prepared the management accounts" | "Reworked the reporting timetable to close three days earlier" |
| "Supported the audit" | "Resolved the inventory variance between the stock system and the floor count" |

The right-hand column describes decisions and their consequences. The left describes what you were handed. Only the first tells a reviewer whether you were operating at the level the designation attests to.

#### Why contemporaneous beats reconstructed

A verifier signing something they did not witness is not a strength, and experience offices do check. Most people assume they can reconstruct evidence later and most cannot reconstruct it accurately, particularly for a role that has ended — the detail that mattered is usually the part that was never written down at the time. A monthly note makes the requirement almost free; an annual scramble makes it expensive and unreliable.

#### How the three years interacts with the examinations

The examinations can be taken in any order and on demand, so most candidates sit Objective Tests while working and take the Case Studies around promotion. Two consequences follow, and both are scheduling decisions rather than study decisions.

First, start the practical experience clock early even if you are not certain you will stay in the profession. If you change jobs the hours can often be aggregated, but only if they were recorded properly at the time.

Second, do not assume the examinations come first in the labour market's thinking. Many employers and recruiters look at designation status, which is why candidates who pass all nine Objective Tests but cannot evidence three years sometimes find the qualification has not yet changed what they can apply for.

#### The boundary case that catches careful candidates

Routine clerical time does not count, and volume does not convert it. Someone performing the same reconciling task across a larger ledger for longer hours has not thereby moved up a level — scope and level are what change the classification. The related error is waiting for a promotion before starting to record: work at a lower level still counts if it is relevant, and the verifiers are the managers you already have.

### Common mistakes

Believing the examinations are the whole qualification. AICPA & CIMA states three years of verified relevant professional experience is required for the designation, assessed through its Practical Experience Requirements process.

Starting the evidence record after the exams. Experience is assessed as it accrues and cannot be back-assembled reliably.

Recording tasks without outcomes. A task list shows what you were given; outcomes show what you decided, which is the assessable part.

Counting routine clerical time as relevant. Scope and level matter, not just hours at a desk.

Letting the three years silently extend the timeline. If you assumed you would be a designated CGMA eighteen months after starting, the practical requirement makes that plan wrong by two years.

Waiting for a promotion to start recording. The work at a lower level still counts if it is relevant, and the verifiers are your existing managers.
