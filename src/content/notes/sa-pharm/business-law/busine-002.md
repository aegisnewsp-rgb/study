# Contract Law & Pharmaceutical Agreements

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

A pharmaceutical contract is a legally binding agreement involving a pharmacist, pharmacy owner, supplier, or patient, and its validity is tested against both the common law of contract and South African health legislation (Pharmacy Act 53 of 1974, Medicines Act 101 of 1965, Consumer Protection Act 68 of 2008). The five common-law requirements are **offer and acceptance**, **consideration**, **capacity**, **legality**, and **consent** (or *consensus*); failure of any one renders the agreement void or voidable.

- **Ownership rule:** Only a registered pharmacist (natural person, pharmacist partnership, or pharmacist-majority company) can own a pharmacy under Section 22.
- **Locum vs employee:** A *locum tenens* provides temporary professional services, not employment under BCEA.
- **Scheduled substances:** Schedule 5+ supply contracts require a current Section 22A permit, otherwise the contract is unenforceable.
- **Illegality:** Agreements breaching the Pharmacy Act are *void ab initio*, not merely voidable.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Common-law elements applied to pharmacy contracts

Every SAPC scenario question reduces to whether one of the five contractual elements failed. **Capacity** is uniquely framed: only a person registered with the South African Pharmacy Council (SAPC) under Section 14 can perform pharmaceutical acts, so a contract for dispensing signed by an unregistered person is void for lack of capacity. **Legality** adds a statutory layer: a contract whose object contravenes the Pharmacy Act or Medicines Act is unenforceable. **Consent** in patient-care agreements must satisfy the National Health Act 61 of 2003 and the Patient Rights Charter — disclosure of risks, alternatives, and cost is mandatory before dispensing.

#### Categories of pharmaceutical agreements

| Agreement type | Parties | Key legal anchor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contract of employment | Responsible pharmacist + owner | BCEA 75 of 1997; Section 14 registration |
| Locum tenens agreement | Locum pharmacist + owner | Common-law mandate; not BCEA |
| Pharmacy ownership transfer | Seller + buyer (pharmacist) | Section 22 + Council resolution |
| Scheduled-substance supply | Wholesaler + pharmacy | Section 22A permit |
| Lease of pharmacy premises | Landlord + pharmacist | Common-law lease + zoning |
| Patient-care service | Pharmacist + patient | NHA + Consumer Protection Act |

#### Vitiating factors examiners exploit

1. **Illegality under Section 22** — lay ownership makes the entire agreement void *ab initio*.
2. **Misrepresentation** — supplier misstates Schedule status of a substance; contract is voidable.
3. **Failure of consent** — patient not informed of a generic substitution is a breach of CPA Section 29.
4. **Lack of Council resolution** — transfer of ownership without SAPC approval is invalid until ratified.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Worked scenario: ownership transfer dispute

A non-pharmacist businessman buys a community pharmacy through a shelf company, signs a purchase agreement with the seller for R2.4 million, and applies for a Section 22A permit to continue dispensing. SAPC refuses the permit and declares the ownership structure unlawful under Section 22(4). The contract is void *ab initio* for illegality — the buyer cannot claim specific performance and the seller must refund the deposit because the agreement's object (operating a pharmacy without pharmacist control) was prohibited at signing. The remedy is a *declaratory order* plus restitution, not damages for breach.

#### Common mistakes in exam answers

| Mistake | Correct position |
| --- | --- |
| Locum = employee | Locum is a *mandatum*; no BCEA coverage |
| Any juristic person can own | Only pharmacist-controlled entities qualify |
| Section 22A permit is optional | Mandatory for Schedule 5+ supply |
| Illegality = voidable | Contravening Pharmacy Act = void *ab initio* |
| Patient consent is implied | NHA requires informed, documented consent |
