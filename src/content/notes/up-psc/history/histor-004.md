# Delhi Sultanate

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The **Delhi Sultanate** (1206–1526 CE) was a 320-year rule over northern India by five Turko-Afghan dynasties headquartered at Delhi. **Qutb-ud-din Aibak**, a former slave-general of Muhammad Ghori, founded it in 1206 and earned the title *Sipahsalar* before dying in 1210 during a *chaugan* (polo) accident.

| Dynasty | Founder | Span |
|---|---|---|
| Mamluk (Slave) | Qutb-ud-din Aibak | 1206–1290 |
| Khilji | Jalal-ud-din Khilji | 1290–1320 |
| Tughlaq | Ghiyas-ud-din Tughlaq | 1320–1414 |
| Sayyid | Khizr Khan | 1414–1451 |
| Lodi | Bahlul Lodi | 1451–1526 |

High-yield facts: **Alauddin Khilji** ran the most aggressive market-control regime in medieval India. **Muhammad bin Tughlaq** launched five famous experiments, including the **Token Currency** of 1328. The Sultanate ended when **Babur** defeated Ibrahim Lodi at the **First Battle of Panipat (1526)**.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Administrative Structure

The Sultanate ran on the **Iqta system**, where land revenue rights were assigned to nobles (*muqtis*) in exchange for cavalry service. Central departments (*diwans*) included **Diwan-i-Wazarat** (Finance), **Diwan-i-Arz** (Military), **Diwan-i-Insha** (Royal correspondence) and **Diwan-i-Qaza** (Judiciary). **Khalisa** lands — directly administered by the sultan — funded the royal household and standing army, a category distinct from iqta assignments.

#### Alauddin Khilji's Reforms (1296–1316)

Alauddin broke the entrenched nobility, banned wine and gambling, and introduced two lasting reforms. **Market reforms** fixed grain, cloth, sugar, and horse prices through appointed officers — *Shahna-i-Mandi* (market superintendent) and *Diwan-i-Riyasat* — backed by a spy network of *munhiyans* (secret informers). **Military reforms** paid soldiers in cash from Khalisa revenues and ran the *Dagh-Chehri* brand-horse system to prevent fraudulent musters.

#### Muhammad bin Tughlaq's Five Experiments (1325–1351)

| Experiment | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Token Currency (1328, copper coins at par with silver tanka) | Failed — mass counterfeiting |
| Capital shift Delhi → Daulatabad (1327) | Failed — population suffering |
| Kharaj raised to 50% of produce | Failed — agrarian revolt in Doab |
| Qarachil Expedition into Himalayas (1333) | Failed — logistical collapse |
| Establishment of Diwan-i-Amir-i-Kohi (agriculture dept.) | Partially succeeded |

> **Trap:** UPPSC answer-sheets routinely cite only 3–4 experiments. Naming all five with their dates earns the differentiation mark.

#### Sources and Cultural Legacy

**Minhaj-us-Siraj** wrote *Tabaqat-i-Nasiri* (covering up to Slave dynasty). **Ziauddin Barani** authored *Tarikh-i-Firoz Shahi* (Khilji-early Tughlaq). **Amir Khusrau** composed *Khazain-ul-Futuh* and *Tughlaqnama*. Architecture: Qutb Minar's first storey (Aibak) was completed by Iltutmish; **Alai Darwaza** is the masterpiece of Khilji building.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Theory of Kingship and the Nobility Question

**Ghiyas-ud-din Balban** articulated the *Niyabat-e-Khudai* doctrine — the sultan as God's vicegerent on earth — and enforced it through *Khoon-o-Khas* ("blood and iron"). He also sidelined the **Amiran-i-Sada** (the Forty, a powerful Turko-Afghan noble clique) by promoting a merit-based *Tajviz* (merit) system. Alauddin Khilji inherited and radicalised this approach: he publicly humiliated nobles, confiscated Iqta holdings, and instituted the *Marsiya* spy-report chain.

#### Firoz Shah Tughlaq: The Welfare Sultan

Unlike Muhammad, **Firoz Shah Tughlaq** (r. 1351–1388) is remembered for state-led welfare. He revived **Jizya** (though exempted women, children, the blind, and Brahmins), opened **Diwan-i-Khairat** for charity, ran *futuwwa* (public kitchens), and dug new canals from the Yamuna, Sutlej, and Ghaggar. His **Diwan-i-Bandagan** managed the welfare and manumission of *Bandagan-i-Khas* (slaves), a category first institutionalised by Iltutmish. **Shams-i-Siraj Afif's** *Tarikh-i-Firoz Shahi* documents this period.

#### Decline and Adjacent Topics

The **Sayyid** dynasty was largely a Timurid vassal regime; **Khizr Khan** ruled under Timur's *manzur* (sanction). **Sikandar Lodi** (1489–1517) moved the capital to Agra and imposed conservative religious policies, while **Ibrahim Lodi's** 1526 defeat marks the start of the Mughal era — the Sultanate question in UPPSC always terminates at this date.

#### Practice Prompts

1. "Discuss Alauddin Khilji's agrarian and market regulations as instruments of political control." (15 marks — link prices, Khalisa expansion, *Dagh-Chehri*.)
2. "Critically evaluate Muhammad bin Tughlaq's projects in the light of contemporary sources." (15 marks — invoke Barani, Ibn Battuta's *Rihla*.)

> **Common mistake:** Treating Kabir and Ramananda as Mughal-era Bhakti saints. Both flourished in the late Sultanate (15th century), overlapping with Sikandar Lodi's reign.
