# Incorporation & Formation of Companies

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Incorporation under the **Companies Act, 2013** is the legal process by which a proposed company becomes a **body corporate** on registration with the Registrar of Companies (RoC) and is governed by Sections 3–14, Schedule I, and the Companies (Incorporation) Rules, 2014. A company is formed in three stages — **Promotion**, **Incorporation** (registration), and **Commencement of Business** — each triggering distinct legal consequences and filings.

| Entity type | Min. members | Min. directors |
|---|---|---|
| Private Company | 2 | 2 |
| Public Company | 7 | 3 |
| One Person Company (OPC) | 1 | 1 + 1 nominee |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"P-P-R"** — **P**romoter drafts MoA/AoA → files **P**ublic document (SPICe+) → RoC issues Certificate of Incorporation (**R**egistration). Think **Promoter → Paper → RoC** in that order.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Stages of Formation

The formation journey has three distinct legal stages. **Promotion** involves identifying a business opportunity, assembling promoters, and conducting preliminary actions like feasibility studies and arranging finance. **Incorporation** begins with name approval through **RUN (Reserve Unique Name)** on the MCA portal, followed by filing the integrated **SPICe+ form (INC-32)** along with **e-MOA (INC-33)** and **e-AOA (INC-34)**, payment of stamp duty and filing fees, and registration with the RoC. **Commencement of Business** under **Section 11** requires a public company to file a declaration in **INC-20A** within **180 days** of incorporation, confirming that every subscriber has paid the agreed share value.

#### Memorandum vs. Articles — The Core Comparison

| Aspect | Memorandum of Association (MoA) | Articles of Association (AoA) |
|---|---|---|
| Section | 4 | 5 |
| Nature | **External charter** — defines company-object scope | **Internal rules** — governs management |
| Defines | Name, registered office, objects, liability, share capital | Rights of members, board meetings, dividend, accounts |
| Binding on | Company ↔ outsiders | Company ↔ members (inter se) |
| Doctrine applicable | **Ultra vires** | **Indoor management** (Turquand's rule) |

> 📌 **Formula Check:** A company acts through its directors; acts **beyond MoA objects** are void (ultra vires) and cannot be ratified even by unanimous shareholders — a classic examiner trap.

#### Common Exam Pitfalls in Doctrine

- **Doctrine of Ultra Vires** — Once an act is ultra vires, it is void ab initio. The company cannot sue on contracts beyond its objects. Leading case: *Ashbury Railway Carriage Co. v. Riche*.
- **Doctrine of Indoor Management** — Outsiders are presumed that internal procedures were followed. **Six exceptions** apply: (1) forgery, (2) knowledge of irregularity, (3) void/illegal acts, (4) acts requiring *sui generis* procedure, (5) Walsh v. Trevor — where the outsider had reasonable suspicion, (6) negligence of the outsider.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** M/s GreenVolt Pvt. Ltd. was incorporated on 1st January 2025 with 3 subscribers. On 1st March 2025, the directors borrow ₹50 lakh from a bank without obtaining any board resolution. The bank, having checked the AoA, argues the loan is binding because the company's articles permit borrowings up to ₹1 crore. The subscribers claim the loan is ultra vires. Decide the case with reference to relevant doctrines.

#### Solution:

1. **Identify the issue** — Is borrowing beyond internal procedure (AoA) void or merely irregular?
2. **Apply Ultra Vires** — The MoA objects of GreenVolt likely include "raising finance" — borrowing is *intra vires* the company, so the loan is **not ultra vires** the company itself.
3. **Apply Indoor Management** — The bank's claim fails because the *outward* act (signing the loan deed) was done by the director — the outsider is entitled to assume internal formalities (board resolution) were complied with under *Royal British Bank v. Turquand* (1856).
4. **Conclusion** — The loan is **valid and binding** on the company. The remedy of the subscribers lies against the defaulting director for breach of duty, not against the bank.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students wrongly invoke *ultra vires* here because they confuse "ultra vires the directors" with "ultra vires the company." Ultra vires applies only to acts beyond the **MoA objects**, not the AoA's internal procedural limits.

#### Key Steps in SPICe+ Filing

1. Obtain **DSC** (Digital Signature Certificate) for proposed directors.
2. Apply for **DIN** through DIR-3 or via SPICe+ itself.
3. Reserve name via **RUN** or **SPICe+ (Part A)**.
4. File **SPICe+ (INC-32)** with e-MOA, e-AOA, DIR-2, and DIR-12 attachments.
5. Pay stamp duty and RoC fees; upon approval, RoC issues **Certificate of Incorporation** allotting **CIN, PAN, TAN, GSTIN, EPFO, ESIC** simultaneously.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Statutory Exceptions

| Situation | Governing provision | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Public co. fails to file INC-20A within 180 days | Section 11(2) | Directors liable for ₹50,000 fine; company cannot commence business |
| OPC paid-up capital > ₹50 lakh or turnover > ₹2 crore | Section 18(3) proviso | Mandatory conversion to Pvt Ltd/Public within 6 months |
| Company ceases to have registered office | Section 12(1) | Punishment: ₹1,000/day for defaulting officer |
| Director sits in >20 companies | Section 165 | Office in excess directorship becomes vacant |

#### Promoter Liability — Beyond the Basics

A **promoter** stands in a **fiduciary position** to the company he promotes (per *Erlanger v. New Sombrero Phosphate Co.*). He must disclose any secret profit, account for any benefit derived without disclosure, and is personally liable on **pre-incorporation contracts** unless the company adopts the contract through **novation** after incorporation (*Kelantan Coconut Estates Ltd. v. National Union of Plantation Workers*). The promoter's right of indemnity arises only after the company expressly adopts the contract.

#### Lifting the Corporate Veil

Although registration grants **separate legal personality** (Section 9), courts may pierce the veil in specific statutory situations: (a) fraud or evasion of law, (b) protection of revenue/tax, (c) determination of enemy character, (d) group companies (*DHN Food Distributors*), and (e) under Sections 7, 8, 239, 447, 458 of the Act.

#### Common Mistakes in Application Questions

1. Stating that minimum paid-up capital of ₹1 lakh (public) or ₹5 lakh (private) still exists — **the 2013 Act removed this requirement**.
2. Treating OPC as immune from conversion — OPC *must* convert on crossing thresholds.
3. Assuming Section 8 companies have relaxed subscriber minimums — Section 8 still requires 2 subscribers minimum for private and 7 for public structure.
4. Forgetting that DSC and DIN are pre-conditions to SPICe+ filing — many students skip this in procedure questions.

#### Two Advanced Practice Prompts

1. *Application:* A promoter signs a lease for office space before incorporation. After incorporation, the company refuses to take the lease, and the landlord sues. Discuss (a) liability of the promoter, (b) whether the company can ratify, and (c) the novation requirement.
2. *Case-based:* Distinguish between *Ashbury Railway Carriage Co.* (ultra vires — MoA) and *Royal British Bank v. Turquand* (indoor management — AoA), and explain how Walsh v. Trevor modifies the Turquand rule.
