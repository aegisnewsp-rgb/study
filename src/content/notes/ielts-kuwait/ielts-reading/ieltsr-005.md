# Matching Information and Headings — IELTS Reading Notes

Matching Information and Matching Headings are two related IELTS Academic Reading question types. Matching Information asks you to locate which paragraph contains a specific piece of information. Matching Headings asks you to select the correct heading for each paragraph from a list. Both test paragraph-level comprehension.

> Verify the live Reading format on https://ielts.org/ before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Matching Information:** match a list of statements to the paragraphs that contain them. Each paragraph may contain more than one statement.
- **Matching Headings:** match a list of headings to the paragraphs of the passage. Each heading is used once.
- **Format:** the headings are usually shorter and more abstract than the paragraphs.
- **Order of answers:** not strictly in order (headings are in random order relative to the passage).
- **Common distractor:** some headings may apply to more than one paragraph; the test asks for the best match.

#### Examiner traps

- Choosing a heading that is partially correct but misses the paragraph's main idea.
- Matching a statement to the wrong paragraph when similar information appears in two places.
- Skipping the first paragraph (often the introduction contains key information).

---

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Matching Information strategy

1. Read the list of statements — identify the keyword in each.
2. Skim each paragraph for the keyword.
3. Match the statement to the paragraph that contains it.
4. Some statements may be unused; some paragraphs may contain more than one statement.

#### Matching Headings strategy

1. Read the list of headings — identify the key concept in each.
2. Skim each paragraph for the main idea (first sentence is usually the topic sentence).
3. Match the heading that best summarises the paragraph's main idea.
4. Eliminate headings that are too specific (they describe a detail, not the main idea) or too broad (they describe the whole passage, not the paragraph).

#### Identifying main ideas

The first sentence of a paragraph usually states the topic. The last sentence may summarise or transition. The middle sentences provide supporting detail. The heading summarises the topic, not the detail.

---

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where matching appears across IELTS Reading

Matching Information and Matching Headings appear in IELTS Academic Reading, often together or separately. They test paragraph-level comprehension and are a strong band discriminator.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Headings describe the whole passage." No — each heading describes one paragraph.
- "Matching Information statements are unique to one paragraph." Usually, but some statements may apply to multiple paragraphs.
- "Skipping the introduction is fine." No — the introduction usually contains the passage's main claim, which may be tested.

#### Exam technique

- For Matching Headings, eliminate headings that describe a detail or a specific example before matching.
- For Matching Information, scan the passage for keywords from the statements.
- If a heading applies to more than one paragraph, choose the one where it fits best.
