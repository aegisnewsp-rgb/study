# Task 2 — Cohesion, Lexical Resource and Grammar — IELTS Writing Notes

Cohesion, Lexical Resource and Grammatical Range and Accuracy are three of the four official IELTS Writing band descriptors (the fourth is Task Response). Each is scored independently, and together they determine the candidate's Writing band.

> Verify the live Writing format on https://ielts.org/ before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Cohesion:** logical organisation of information, clear progression, appropriate use of cohesive devices.
- **Lexical resource:** range of vocabulary, accurate word choice, no repetition, appropriate register.
- **Grammatical range and accuracy:** variety of complex sentence structures, accurate grammar, few errors.
- **Marking:** each descriptor is scored 0-9, then averaged.
- **Band 7+ requirements:** clear progression, varied vocabulary, complex sentences with few errors.

#### Examiner traps

- Overusing cohesive devices ("however," "however," "however") — mechanical writing loses the band.
- Using the same words repeatedly — repetition caps the lexical resource band.
- Complex sentences with errors — accuracy counts more than range at band 7+.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Cohesion

Cohesive devices are not just conjunctions. They include:

- **Pronouns:** he, she, it, they, this, that.
- **Reference words:** the former, the latter, such, said, above, below.
- **Substitution:** do so, the same, similarly.
- **Ellipsis:** omitting words that are recoverable from context.

Use a variety of cohesive devices, not just conjunctions. Avoid mechanical repetition of the same device.

#### Lexical resource

Build a wide vocabulary:

- **Topic-specific vocabulary:** research papers, education, environment, technology, health.
- **Academic vocabulary:** the Academic Word List (AWL) by Coxhead covers the most common academic words.
- **Collocations: "make a decision" (not "do a decision"), "heavy rain" (not "big rain").**
- **Word families:** develop (verb), development (noun), developing (adjective), undeveloped (adjective).

Avoid repetition: instead of "important" three times, use "significant," "crucial," "vital."

#### Grammatical range

Mix simple and complex sentences:

- **Simple:** "Smoking is harmful."
- **Compound:** "Smoking is harmful, and it should be banned."
- **Complex:** "Although smoking is harmful, some people continue to do it because they find it relaxing."
- **Compound-complex:** "Although smoking is harmful, some people continue to do it, and the government has yet to ban it effectively."

Use a variety of tenses, modals, conditionals, passives, and relative clauses.

#### Accuracy

Accuracy counts more than range at band 7+. Common error categories:

- **Subject-verb agreement:** "He go" → "He goes."
- **Article usage:** "the," "a," "an" — common errors for non-native speakers.
- **Prepositions:** "interested on" → "interested in."
- **Verb tense:** past vs present vs future.
- **Word form:** "decision" (noun) vs "decide" (verb).

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where cohesion, lexical resource and grammar appear across IELTS Writing

These three descriptors apply to both Task 1 and Task 2, but with different weights. Task 2 rewards more complex grammar and a wider vocabulary.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Complex sentences always score higher." No — accuracy matters more than range at band 7+.
- "Cohesive devices are conjunctions." No — they include pronouns, reference words, and substitution.
- "Topic-specific vocabulary is enough." No — academic vocabulary (AWL) is also required.

#### Exam technique

- Use a variety of cohesive devices, not just conjunctions.
- Build topic-specific and academic vocabulary before the exam.
- Check for subject-verb agreement, articles, prepositions, and verb tense.
- Aim for accuracy over range — a 70% accurate complex sentence scores higher than a 30% accurate complex sentence.
