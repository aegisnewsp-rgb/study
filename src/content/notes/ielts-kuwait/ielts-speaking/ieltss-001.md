# Part 1 — Introduction and Interview — IELTS Speaking Notes

IELTS Speaking Part 1 is the introduction and interview. The examiner introduces themselves, checks identification, then asks familiar questions on everyday topics (home, family, work, studies, hobbies). Part 1 lasts about 4-5 minutes.

> Verify the live Speaking format on https://ielts.org/ before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Format:** introduction + familiar questions on everyday topics.
- **Time:** 4-5 minutes.
- **Number of questions:** in most keys 4-6 questions on 1-2 topics.
- **Answer length:** 2-3 sentences per answer (longer is better, but do not ramble).
- **Topics:** home, family, work, studies, hobbies, food, weather, sports, music, daily routine.
- **Common distractor:** the examiner can ask follow-up questions to extend the answer.

#### Examiner traps

- Giving one-word answers — Part 1 expects developed responses, not single sentences.
- Rambling without focus — the answer should be relevant and concise.
- Forgetting to develop the answer with reasons or examples.

---

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Answer structure

A good Part 1 answer has:

1. **Direct response:** answer the question.
2. **Development:** explain why or give an example.
3. **Detail:** add a specific detail.

Example:

- Q: "Do you like cooking?"
- A: "Yes, I do. I especially enjoy cooking Arabic dishes because they bring back memories of my grandmother's kitchen. I in most keys cook for my family on weekends."

#### Vocabulary for Part 1

Build topic-specific vocabulary:

- **Home:** apartment, villa, balcony, kitchen, living room, bedroom.
- **Family:** siblings, parents, relatives, extended family, close-knit.
- **Work/Studies:** profession, colleagues, workplace, schedule, deadlines.
- **Hobbies:** reading, painting, hiking, photography, listening to music.

#### Pronunciation

Practice word stress, sentence stress, and intonation. The Pronunciation band descriptor rewards:

- Clear word stress.
- Appropriate intonation.
- Effective use of chunking (pausing between ideas).
- Accurate individual sounds.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where Part 1 appears across IELTS Speaking

Part 1 is the first section, accounting for about a third of the Speaking band. Strong Part 1 performance is the baseline; weak Part 1 performance is rare but caps the band.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "One-word answers are fine." No — Part 1 expects developed responses.
- "Memorised answers score high." Examiners recognise memorised phrases; the band for lexical resource drops.
- "Speaking fast scores higher." No — clear pronunciation and appropriate pace score higher.

#### Exam technique

- Listen to the question carefully and answer directly.
- Develop the answer with a reason or example.
- Use a range of vocabulary (avoid repetition).
- Speak at a natural, clear pace.

#### The 3-step fluency extension protocol for Part 1

In Part 1 of the IELTS Speaking test, the examiner asks simple questions about familiar everyday topics (work, study, hometown, hobbies, weather). Answering with a single short sentence damages your Fluency and Coherence score (Band 5 behavior). Apply the *Answer + Reason + Detail/Example* framework:

1. **Direct Answer**: Provide a direct, natural response without repeating the examiner's question word-for-word.
2. **Give a Concrete Reason**: Explain *why* you feel that way or why the situation exists.
3. **Add an Anecdotal Detail or Frequency Qualifier**: Mention how in many papers you do it, a recent memory, or how your preference has changed over time.

*Example*:
- *Examiner*: Do you enjoy reading books in your free time?
- *Candidate*: Absolutely, I'm quite an avid reader (Direct Answer), particularly specifically with historical non-fiction, because I find learning about real past events deeply fascinating (Reason). In fact, I in most keys try to read a chapter or two every evening before going to sleep to unwind after studying (Detail).
