# Economics

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Economics in SSC CGL General Awareness covers how an economy produces, distributes, and consumes goods and services, with roughly 4-6 MCQs in Tier-I and 8-10 in Tier-II (Finance & Economics). The two branches tested are **Microeconomics** (individual units: demand, supply, elasticity) and **Macroeconomics** (aggregate: GDP, inflation, policy).

#### Must-Know Identities at a Glance

| Term | Definition | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| **GDP** | C + I + G + (X − M), the monetary value of all final goods and services produced within a country in a year | ₹ crore |
| **GNP** | GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA); income of residents, at home and abroad | ₹ crore |
| **Real GDP** | (Nominal GDP ÷ GDP Deflator) × 100; removes price-effect | ₹ crore |
| **Inflation Rate** | [(CPI current − CPI base) ÷ CPI base] × 100 | % (dimensionless) |
| **Money Multiplier** | 1 ÷ CRR (decimal) | dimensionless |
| **Elasticity of Demand** | (% Δ Quantity Demanded) ÷ (% Δ Price) | dimensionless |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"CDG + XM = GDP"** (Consumption, Domestic Investment, Government, Exports minus Imports). For GNP, just **add NFIA**: "GNP = GDP + NFIA — Indians earning abroad minus foreigners earning in India."

#### Highest-Yield One-Liners for SSC CGL

- **RBI** = central bank, controls **Monetary Policy**; **Government** controls **Fiscal Policy** (taxes + spending).
- **Repo Rate** = rate RBI charges banks (not the rate banks charge you).
- **CRR** = cash parked with RBI; **SLR** = gold + government securities banks hold themselves.
- **Planning Commission** was replaced by **NITI Aayog** in **January 2015**.
- **GST** = dual levy: **CGST + SGST** (intra-state), **IGST** (inter-state).
- **1991 reforms = LPG**: **L**iberalization, **P**rivatization, **G**lobalization.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix — Easily Confused Pairs

| Concept A | Concept B | Key Difference |
|---|---|---|
| GDP | GNP | GDP = produced **within** borders; GNP = produced **by residents** anywhere (adds NFIA) |
| Microeconomics | Macroeconomics | Micro = households, firms, markets; Macro = national income, policy, aggregates |
| CRR | SLR | CRR = **cash** with RBI (no interest); SLR = gold + G-secs kept by bank itself |
| Repo Rate | Reverse Repo Rate | Repo = RBI lends **to** banks; Reverse Repo = RBI borrows **from** banks |
| Fiscal Deficit | Revenue Deficit | Fiscal = Total Expenditure − Total Receipts (excl. borrowings); Revenue = Rev. Expenditure − Rev. Receipts only |
| Fiscal Deficit | Primary Deficit | Primary = Fiscal Deficit − Interest Payments |
| Budget Deficit | Fiscal Deficit | Budget Deficit includes **all** expenditure vs **all** receipts; Fiscal Deficit excludes borrowings |
| WPI | CPI | WPI = wholesale/producer prices; CPI = retail/consumer prices (CPI base year = 2012 = 100) |
| Current Account | Capital Account (BoP) | Current = trade in goods/services/income; Capital = cross-border investments + financial flows |
| Planning Commission | NITI Aayog | Top-down, fund-allocating body (1950–2014); advisory, replacing body since January 2015 |

#### Indian Economy: Institutional Pillars

1. **RBI** — sets **Bank Rate**, **Repo Rate**, **Reverse Repo Rate**, **MSF** (Marginal Standing Facility), **CRR**, **SLR**, and conducts open market operations.
2. **NITI Aayog** — replaced the Planning Commission; advisory role, no fund allocation; chairperson is the Prime Minister.
3. **Union Budget** — classifies receipts into **Revenue Receipts** (taxes, non-debt capital) and **Capital Receipts** (borrowings, recoveries of loans, disinvestment).
4. **Census of India** — decadal exercise; reports literacy rate, sex ratio, population density, growth rate.

#### Inflation — Types Students Must Recognise

- **Demand-pull** — too much money chasing too few goods.
- **Cost-push** — supply-side shock raises production costs (e.g., oil price spike).
- **Creeping** — mild, ≤3%; **Walking** — 3–10%; **Galloping** — >10%; **Hyperinflation** — >50% per month.
- **Stagflation** — stagnant growth combined with rising inflation and unemployment.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** If the Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR) is set at 4%, what is the value of the money multiplier? (Options: 10, 20, 25, 50)

#### Solution:

Money Multiplier = 1 ÷ CRR

CRR = 4% = 0.04 (decimal fraction)

Money Multiplier = 1 ÷ 0.04 = **25**

Dimension check: 1 (dimensionless) ÷ 0.04 (dimensionless) = dimensionless ratio. Higher CRR → lower multiplier (less money banks can create from reserves).

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students often plug 4 instead of 0.04 into the formula and answer 0.25, or choose 4 itself. Convert the percentage to a **decimal** before dividing. A second trap: confusing Money Multiplier with the **Currency-Deposit Ratio** or **Reserve-Deposit Ratio** — the formula 1/CRR assumes banks lend out all excess reserves (no leakage).

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Advanced Traps — Why Aspirants Lose Marks

| Trap | What Students Write | Correct Position |
|---|---|---|
| "Repo Rate is the rate banks charge customers" | Repo Rate | Repo Rate is RBI → bank short-term lending rate; customer lending rate is the **Bank Rate** / internal MCLR |
| "GDP only counts goods, not services" | GDP | GDP includes **all final goods AND services**; only intermediate goods are excluded to avoid double-counting |
| "Planning Commission still exists" | Planning Commission | Replaced by **NITI Aayog** in **January 2015**; question may use present tense to mislead |
| "Fiscal Deficit = Budget Deficit" | Same thing | Budget Deficit = Total Expenditure − Total Receipts (incl. borrowings); Fiscal Deficit excludes borrowings |
| "GST is collected only by the Centre" | Central tax | GST is **dual**: CGST + SGST on intra-state; IGST on inter-state transactions |
| "CPI base year is 2004-05" | Old CPI | Current CPI base year is **2012 = 100**; WPI base year also revised — verify in latest MoSPI release |
| "WPI includes services" | WPI | WPI tracks goods at wholesale level only; CPI includes services |

#### Edge Cases and Adjacent Connections

- **NDP vs NNP**: subtract depreciation from GDP/GNP. NNP at **factor cost** = NNP at market price − Net Indirect Taxes (NIT).
- **Demand-Side vs Supply-Side Fiscal Policy**: supply-side uses tax cuts to incentivise production; demand-side boosts government spending to raise aggregate demand.
- **Five Year Plans** ran from 1951 to 2017 (12th Plan was the last); NITI Aayog publishes a **3-year Action Agenda** and **7-year Strategy** instead.
- **Schemes cluster**: Jan Dhan Yojana (financial inclusion), MGNREGA (100 days of guaranteed wage employment in rural areas), MUDRA (micro-finance for non-corporate, non-farm sector), Digital India, UPI (real-time payment system) — questions often ask the **launch year** or **ministry**.
- **Open Market Operations (OMO)** are RBI's tool to inject or absorb liquidity permanently; variable tools like MSF are short-window.

#### Two Advanced Practice Prompts

1. If nominal GDP rises 10% and the GDP Deflator rises 6%, what is the percentage change in **Real GDP**? (Hint: Real GDP growth ≈ Nominal − Inflation = 10 − 6 = **4%**.)
2. A country records exports of ₹500 crore, imports of ₹400 crore, private consumption of ₹2,000 crore, gross investment of ₹700 crore, and government expenditure of ₹300 crore. Compute GDP. (Solution: C + I + G + (X − M) = 2000 + 700 + 300 + (500 − 400) = **₹3,100 crore**.)

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** The most expensive mistake on numerical questions is forgetting the **(X − M)** sign — students add imports instead of subtracting them, inflating GDP.
