# Elective 2 — Extension of Trade Theory, Economic Growth and Development — HKDSE Economics Notes

This topic covers Elective 2 of the HKDSE Economics Elective Part. Candidates choose either Elective 1 or Elective 2. The topic addresses extension of trade theory (the Leontief paradox, intra-industry trade, strategic trade policy); economic growth (sources of growth, the Solow growth model, endogenous growth theory); and economic development (measures of development, the role of institutions, foreign aid, sustainable development goals).

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Leontief paradox (1953)**: Wassily Leontief found that the US (a labour-abundant country according to the Heckscher-Ohlin model) exported more capital-intensive goods than labour-intensive goods. This contradicted the standard H-O prediction.
- **Explanations of the Leontief paradox**:
  - The US has high-skilled labour, which complements capital.
  - Different industries have different productivity differences.
  - Trade barriers distorted the data.
- **Intra-industry trade**: trade between countries of similar products (e.g. Germany exporting BMWs to the US while importing Fords). Most of global trade is intra-industry.
- **Krugman's new trade theory**: explains intra-industry trade through increasing returns to scale and product differentiation.
- **Strategic trade policy**: government intervention to shift oligopolistic profits from foreign to domestic firms (e.g. subsidies to strategic industries).
- **Economic growth**: sustained increase in real GDP per capita.
- **Sources of growth**:
  - **Capital accumulation**: investment in physical capital.
  - **Labour force growth**: increase in the labour force.
  - **Productivity growth**: improvements in efficiency.
- **Solow growth model**: Y = F(K, L). Steady state when investment = depreciation. Technological progress shifts the steady state.
- **Endogenous growth theory**: explains persistent growth through investment in human capital, R&D, and innovation.
- **Economic development**: improvement in the standard of living and quality of life.
- **Measures of development**: GDP per capita, HDI (Human Development Index), Gini coefficient, poverty headcount, life expectancy, literacy.
- **Institutions**: property rights, rule of law, contract enforcement, government effectiveness. Strong institutions promote development.
- **Foreign aid**: official development assistance (ODA) from developed to developing countries.
- **Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)**: 17 goals adopted by the UN in 2015 covering poverty, hunger, education, gender, water, energy, work, infrastructure, inequality, cities, consumption, climate, oceans, land, peace, partnerships.

#### Examiner traps

- Treating intra-industry trade as a small part of world trade — most trade is intra-industry, especially among developed countries.
- Confusing GDP growth with economic development — development is broader.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Extension of trade theory

**Heckscher-Ohlin theorem**: a country exports goods that intensively use its abundant factor. The Leontief paradox challenged this prediction for the US.

**Leontief paradox (1953)**: the US (perceived as capital-abundant) exported more labour-intensive goods and imported more capital-intensive goods. Subsequent research reconciled this with the H-O framework by noting:

- The US has highly skilled labour, which is a substitute for capital.
- Trade patterns depend on relative productivity differences (Ricardian) and factor endowments (H-O), both of which matter.

**Intra-industry trade**: most of global trade is in similar products. Germany and the US both produce cars, but trade in different models.

**New trade theory (Krugman, 1979)**: intra-industry trade arises from:

- **Increasing returns to scale** (production at lower cost per unit when output is larger).
- **Product differentiation** (consumers prefer variety).
- **Imperfect competition** (firms charge prices above MC).

**Strategic trade policy (Brander-SKberg, 1985)**: in oligopolistic markets with imperfect competition, government intervention (subsidies, tariffs, R&D support) can shift profits from foreign to domestic firms. Critics argue that strategic trade policy can lead to retaliation and may not always work.

#### Economic growth

**Solow growth model (1956)**: Y = A · F(K, L). Output Y depends on technology A, capital K, and labour L.

- In the steady state, investment = depreciation; the capital-labour ratio is constant.
- Convergence: economies with low capital per worker grow faster (catch-up effect), conditional on similar technology and institutions.
- Technological progress is exogenous in the Solow model; it shifts the steady state up over time.

**Endogenous growth theory (Romer, Lucas, 1986-1990)**: explains persistent growth through:

- **Human capital** (Lucas): education and skills raise the productivity of labour.
- **R&D and innovation** (Romer): investment in new ideas leads to new products and processes.
- **Spillovers of**: knowledge spillovers raise the returns to R&D.

Endogenous growth theory predicts that policies that promote education, R&D, and innovation can sustain long-run growth.

**Convergence**: developed and developing countries may converge (catch-up) or diverge depending on institutions, technology transfer, and openness to trade.

#### Economic development

**Measures of development**:

- **GDP per capita**: average income per person.
- **Human Development Index (HDI)**: combines GDP per capita, life expectancy, and education.
- **Gini coefficient**: measures income inequality (0 = perfect equality, 1 = perfect inequality).
- **Poverty headcount**: the percentage of people living below the poverty line.
- **Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI)**: measures poverty across health, education and living standards.

**Determinants of development**:

- **Geography**: climate, natural resources, disease burden.
- **Institutions**: property rights, rule of law, contract enforcement, government effectiveness.
- **Human capital**: education, health.
- **Trade openness**: integration with global markets.
- **Foreign direct investment**: technology transfer and capital.
- **Macroeconomic stability**: low inflation, sustainable debt.
- **Demographics**: population growth, fertility rate, dependency ratio.

**Foreign aid**:

- **Official Development Assistance (ODA)**: government aid designed to promote economic development and welfare.
- **Criticisms**: aid dependency, corruption, fungibility, Dutch disease.
- **Effectiveness**: depends on institutions, governance, and aid modality.

**Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)**: 17 goals adopted by the UN General Assembly in 2015 as part of the 2030 Agenda. Cover economic, social and environmental dimensions of development.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-topic links

- **J. International Trade and Finance**: comparative advantage and H-O.
- **F. Measurement of Economic Performance**: real GDP growth.
- **I. Macroeconomic Problems and Policies**: inflation, unemployment.

#### Exam technique

- **For Leontief paradox questions**: state the paradox; cite the reconciliation (skilled labour as substitute for capital).
- **For growth questions**: distinguish Solow (exogenous technology) from endogenous growth theory (R&D and human capital).
- **For development questions**: cite specific measures (HDI, MPI); discuss determinants.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Foreign aid always helps development." Effectiveness depends on governance, institutions, and aid modality.
- "Trade openness always leads to growth." Trade openness is associated with growth, but causality is debated.
- "GDP growth equals development." Development is multidimensional; GDP captures only part of it.
