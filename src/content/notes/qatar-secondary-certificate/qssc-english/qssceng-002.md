# Speaking — Communicating, Register and Audience — Qatar Secondary Certificate English Notes

The MoEHE English Curriculum Standards at Grade 12 publish five Speaking sub-strands (S1-S5): communicating personal information, communicating general information, developing speaking strategies, using appropriate register, and communicating to small or large audiences. The Grade 12 CEFR exit level is B2 Low-Mid, which requires sustained monologue on familiar topics, presenting arguments, and adapting delivery to the audience.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Register:** formal vs informal vs neutral — chosen by audience and context.
- **Discourse markers:** "well", "actually", "I think", "in my opinion", "to be honest", "on the one hand", "having said that".
- **Repair strategies:** self-correction, rephrasing, asking for clarification.
- **B2 fluency markers:** hesitation without long pauses, self-correction without losing flow, use of connectors, range of vocabulary.

#### Examiner traps

- Speaking only in short single-sentence answers — B2 expects developed responses with reasons and examples.
- Using the same register in a class discussion and a job interview.
- Overusing fillers ("um", "uh") instead of discourse markers.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### S1-S2: Communicating personal and general information

At B2 the candidate is expected to describe experiences, dreams, hopes and ambitions, and to give reasons and explanations for opinions. For example: "I started learning Arabic two years ago because I'm planning to study in Qatar. It's been challenging, but I've made good progress and I can now hold a basic conversation with my colleagues."

Sustained answers should run for 30-60 seconds on a familiar topic, with development (reasons, examples, opinions), not just a list of facts.

#### S3: Developing speaking strategies

Compensation strategies: when a word is forgotten, paraphrase. "The thing you use to ... clean the floor ... a mop." Repair: "I mean a vacuum cleaner." Re-phrasing a complex idea in simpler terms is a B2 strength.

Fluency strategies: read aloud daily; record and listen back; speak with peers in timed monologues on a topic; use a list of discourse markers as a checklist.

#### S4: Appropriate register

Formal: "I would like to suggest that the school consider extending the library hours." Informal: "Yeah, the library should be open later." Neutral: "I think the library should be open later."

Match register to audience: peers, teachers, employers, family. Note when the register needs to shift in a single conversation (a teacher asks a question informally, the student answers formally).

#### S5: Communicating to small or large audiences

Small group (4-6 people): turn-taking, eye contact, response to others' points. Large audience (a presentation): clear signposting, structured argument, controlled pace. The B2 speaker can adjust without changing the core message.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### Where this maps to the terminal exam

The MoEHE English Grade 12 exit expectations include oral presentation, class discussion, and structured interview as the three speaking contexts. The terminal paper assesses the receptive skills (reading, listening) and the productive written skills (writing), with speaking graded via the school-internal continuous assessment; B2 is the published target.

#### Exam technique

- For oral presentations, plan signposts (introduction, three main points with transitions, conclusion) before speaking.
- For discussion, paraphrase what the previous speaker said to show engagement.
- For formal contexts, avoid contractions in writing, use full forms ("I would", "we do not"), keep vocabulary precise.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Longer answers always score better." No — relevance, cohesion, and accuracy matter more than length. Padding costs marks.
- "Formal English means using big words." No — formal English is precise. Use simple words with exact meaning.
