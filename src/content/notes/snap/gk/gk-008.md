# Environment & Ecology

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

> Two facts decide most SNAP Environment MCQs: which Indian Act was passed in which year, and whether an ecological pyramid can stand upside down.

**Ecology** = study of organisms and their interactions with each other and the abiotic surroundings (coined by Ernst Haeckel, 1866). An **ecosystem** = biotic community + abiotic environment functioning as a unit. Energy flows one way; nutrients cycle.

**Memory Hook — "LEAP-10":** Lindeman's Energy transfer is About 10% across each trophic level — and the energy pyramid is ALWAYS upright. Lose the habit of inverting it; number and biomass pyramids can flip, energy cannot.

| Quick comparator | Don't confuse |
|---|---|
| Greenhouse effect vs Global warming | Natural trapping vs anthropogenic enhancement |
| Wildlife Sanctuary vs National Park | Grazing/private land allowed vs strict no-activity zone |
| Biomagnification vs Bioaccumulation | Up the food chain vs within one organism |
| In-situ vs Ex-situ conservation | Field-based (parks) vs off-site (zoos, seed banks) |

Concentrate revision energy on dates: Wildlife Protection Act **1972**, Forest Conservation Act **1980**, Environment Protection Act **1986**, Stockholm **1972**, Montreal Protocol **1987**, Kyoto Protocol **1997**, Paris Agreement **2015**.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Ecosystem structure and energy flow

Every ecosystem has two layers — **abiotic** (light, temperature, water, soil, minerals) and **biotic** (producers → consumers → decomposers). Sunlight is the primary energy input for most ecosystems; chemosynthetic bacteria form the base in deep-sea hydrothermal vents.

**Lindeman's 10% Law:** only about 10% of energy at one trophic level is passed to the next. Roughly 90% is lost as heat, in metabolism, or in undigested matter. This single rule explains why food chains rarely exceed 4–5 levels and why top predators are scarce.

| Pyramid type | Can be inverted? | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Energy | Never | Heat loss at each level (2nd law of thermodynamics) |
| Biomass | Sometimes | E.g. inverted in marine ecosystems where zooplankton biomass exceeds phytoplankton at certain times |
| Number | Often | One tree supporting thousands of insects |

#### Cycles and pollution

The **carbon cycle** moves CO₂ between atmosphere, oceans, plants, and fossil fuels. The **nitrogen cycle** runs through fixation (N₂ → NH₃ by *Rhizobium*, lightning), nitrification (NH₃ → NO₂⁻ → NO₃⁻ by *Nitrosomonas* and *Nitrobacter*), assimilation, and denitrification (NO₃⁻ → N₂ by *Pseudomonas*). The **hydrological cycle** is powered by solar energy driving evaporation, condensation, precipitation, and runoff.

**BOD (Biochemical Oxygen Demand)** measures biodegradable organic pollution in water; high BOD = low dissolved oxygen = fish kill risk. **COD (Chemical Oxygen Demand)** includes non-biodegradable chemical oxidation. Air quality is tracked via **PM2.5** (particles ≤ 2.5 µm, penetrate alveoli) and **PM10**.

#### Worked problem

A student reads: *"Which statement about ozone depletion is correct?"* The options are:
- (A) CFCs deplete ozone in the troposphere
- (B) Montreal Protocol regulates CO₂ emissions
- (C) CFC breakdown in the stratosphere releases chlorine radicals that catalytically destroy ozone
- (D) Ozone hole was first observed over the Arctic

Correct answer is **(C)**. CFCs are chemically stable in the troposphere and reach the stratosphere intact, where UV-C radiation cleaves the C–Cl bond, releasing Cl• radicals. Each Cl atom destroys roughly 100,000 O₃ molecules via the **ClO + O → Cl + O₂** cycle. Montreal Protocol regulates ozone-depleting substances such as CFCs and HCFCs — not CO₂. The Antarctic ozone hole was first reported in 1985 by Farman, Gardiner, and Shanklin over the **Antarctic**, not the Arctic.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** students routinely mark (B) because they remember "Montreal reduces emissions" without checking the substance being reduced. Always verify whether the protocol targets ozone-depleting substances or greenhouse gases — they are separate treaties.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

#### Edge cases and advanced traps

1. **Keystone species removal collapses the web.** A keystone species has a disproportionately large effect on ecosystem structure relative to its abundance — sea otters, wolves, and tigers all qualify. Removing it triggers trophic cascades.
2. **Carrying capacity K is not fixed.** Logistic growth dN/dt = rN(K−N)/K assumes constant K, but K shifts with resource availability, climate, and species interactions. Real populations oscillate around K.
3. **Biodiversity hotspots vs Biosphere Reserves.** Hotspots (15 in India) are biologically rich AND threatened (Myers' criteria: ≥ 1,500 endemic vascular plants, ≥ 70% habitat lost). Biosphere Reserves are UNESCO-designated management units with **core**, **buffer**, and **transition** zones. Conflating them is the single most common error.
4. **Cultural eutrophication vs natural.** Natural lake eutrophication takes centuries; nutrient runoff from fertilizers and sewage does it in years, producing algal blooms, hypoxia, and dead zones (e.g. Gulf of Mexico hypoxia).
5. **Greenhouse gases and Global Warming Potential (GWP).** CO₂ has GWP = 1 (reference), CH₄ ≈ 28–34 over 100 years, N₂O ≈ 265–298. Don't quote GWP unless the exam specifies the time horizon.

#### Advanced practice prompts

1. Why is the energy pyramid always upright while the number pyramid can be inverted even in the same ecosystem? Cite the thermodynamic principle.
2. The Kyoto Protocol's second commitment period (Doha Amendment, 2012) runs until 2020, after which only Paris Agreement NDCs apply. Why does Paris struggle with the "free-rider" problem that Kyoto could not solve through binding targets?

#### Common mistakes worth flagging

- Mixing up **Stockholm 1972** (human environment), **Rio 1992** (Earth Summit), and **Johannesburg 2002** (sustainable development) — dates get scrambled under pressure.
- Calling **CBD** (Convention on Biological Diversity) the same as **CITES** (trade in endangered species). CBD is about conservation, sustainable use, and benefit sharing; CITES is about regulating cross-border trade.
- Forgetting that **Silent Valley** (Kerala) was India's first biosphere reserve saved from a hydroelectric project — Appiko and Chipko are forest-based mass movements, not reserves.
