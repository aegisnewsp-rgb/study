# Time Distance

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Time, Speed and Distance (TSD)** is built on one master relation: **D = S × T**, where D is distance (km or m), S is speed and T is time, each in consistent units. Two conversions carry the entire module: **1 km/h = 5/18 m/s** and **1 m/s = 18/5 km/h**.

| Concept | Formula |
|---|---|
| Average speed over equal distances | 2·S₁·S₂ / (S₁ + S₂) |
| Average speed over equal times | (S₁ + S₂) / 2 |
| Relative speed (same / opposite) | \|S₁ − S₂\| / (S₁ + S₂) |
| Boat upstream / downstream | B − R / B + R |

#### High-yield SSC CGL pointers:
- Train crossing a **pole** uses only train length (L/S); crossing a **platform** uses (L + P)/S.
- Always pick one unit system — mixing m/s with km breaks the answer.
- Harmonic mean applies when **equal distances** are covered at two speeds.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Mechanics

Every TSD problem reduces to D = S × T once units are aligned. The trap is not the formula but the **unit consistency check**: a train length given in metres and a speed given in km/h must first be reconciled via the 5/18 factor, otherwise the time answer is off by 3.6×.

#### Average Speed — Which Mean to Use

The choice between arithmetic and harmonic mean is the single most-tested sub-topic. When a body covers **two equal distances** at speeds S₁ and S₂, the average speed is the **harmonic mean** — 2·S₁·S₂/(S₁ + S₂). When it covers **two equal time intervals** at those speeds, the average is the **arithmetic mean** — (S₁ + S₂)/2. SSC examiners routinely invert this to catch candidates who blindly average.

#### Relative Speed and Trains

For two objects moving along a line, **relative speed** is the speed of one as observed from the other: |S₁ − S₂| when moving in the same direction (closing/separation rate), and S₁ + S₂ when moving oppositely (approach rate). Train problems apply this directly: crossing a pole means the train's own length clears the pole (time = L/S), while crossing a platform adds the platform length (time = (L + P)/S).

#### Boat and Stream

Effective speeds are **B − R upstream** and **B + R downstream**, where B is boat speed in still water and R is stream speed. Solved together: B = (down + up)/2 and R = (down − up)/2. Stream speed is non-zero, so up-time ≠ down-time for equal distances — another frequent trap.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Circular Tracks

On a circular track of circumference C, two runners gain/lose a lap when their relative distance equals C. Time to meet (same direction) = C / |S₁ − S₂|; time to collide head-on = C / (S₁ + S₂). A "first to lap" question frames the extra distance the faster runner must accumulate, not a new formula.

#### Common Mistakes Examined

> Using **arithmetic mean** on equal-distance splits is the #1 error — it overstates average speed because slower segments dominate total time.

- **Unit slip:** km/h divided by metres yields nonsense; convert with 5/18 first.
- **Direction confusion in relative speed:** same direction ⇒ subtract, opposite ⇒ add.
- **Train ignoring platform length:** crossing a bridge or platform needs (L + P), not L.
- **Boat–stream symmetry:** assuming upstream time equals downstream time when R ≠ 0.

#### Worked Micro-Example

A man rows 12 km downstream in 1 hour and the same 12 km upstream in 2 hours. Find B and R.

Downstream = B + R = 12 km/h; Upstream = B − R = 6 km/h ⇒ **B = 9 km/h, R = 3 km/h**. Average downstream speed for the round trip: total distance 24 km / total time 3 h = **8 km/h**, confirming the harmonic form.

#### SSC CGL Strategy

TSD contributes **2–4 questions** across Tier-1 and Tier-2 — short, formula-driven items that should take under 90 seconds each. Direct D-S-T, relative speed, train crossing, and one boat/stream question dominate the pattern. Memorise the 5/18 conversion and the two average-speed means cold; the rest is substitution under time pressure.
