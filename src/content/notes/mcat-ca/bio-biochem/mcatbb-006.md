# Metabolism: Lipids, Amino Acids and Integrated Pathways — MCAT Bio/Biochem

Lipid, amino acid, and integrated metabolism complete AAMC Foundational Concept 3 of the Bio/Biochem section. The MCAT tests beta-oxidation, fatty acid synthesis, ketogenesis, amino acid catabolism, the urea cycle, gluconeogenesis, the pentose phosphate pathway, and metabolic regulation. These topics connect lipid and protein metabolism to the central pathways and explain how the body responds to fasting and feeding.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### Core concepts

- **Beta-oxidation:** fatty acids → acetyl-CoA. Occurs in the mitochondrial matrix. Yields 1 NADH, 1 FADH₂, and 1 acetyl-CoA per cycle.
- **Fatty acid synthesis:** acetyl-CoA → fatty acids. Occurs in the cytosol. Acetyl-CoA is shuttled out of the mitochondrion as citrate.
- **Ketogenesis:** excess acetyl-CoA (from beta-oxidation during fasting) → ketone bodies (acetoacetate, beta-hydroxybutyrate, acetone). Used by the brain during prolonged fasting.
- **Amino acid catabolism:** amino acids are deaminated (NH₃ removed); the carbon skeletons enter the Krebs cycle at various points.
- **Urea cycle:** NH₃ + CO₂ → urea. Occurs in the liver. Converts toxic ammonia to water-soluble urea for excretion.
- **Gluconeogenesis:** pyruvate → glucose. Occurs in the liver and kidneys. Bypasses the irreversible steps of glycolysis.
- **Pentose phosphate pathway (PPP):** glucose-6-phosphate → ribose-5-phosphate (for nucleotide synthesis) and NADPH (for biosynthesis and antioxidant defence).
- **Metabolic state:** fed (high insulin, anabolic); fasted (low insulin, high glucagon, catabolic).

#### One-pass drill

1. Identify the metabolic state (fed, fasted, prolonged fasting).
2. Identify the pathway and its purpose.
3. Recall the key enzymes and the regulatory signals.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing beta-oxidation with fatty acid synthesis. Beta-oxidation happens in the mitochondrion; synthesis happens in the cytosol.
- Treating the urea cycle as a way to make energy. It is a detoxification pathway.
- Forgetting that gluconeogenesis is not the reverse of glycolysis. Three irreversible steps of glycolysis are bypassed by different enzymes.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Lipid metabolism

**Beta-oxidation:** fatty acids are activated (acyl-CoA) in the cytosol, transported into the mitochondrion via the carnitine shuttle, and broken down into acetyl-CoA. Each cycle yields 1 NADH, 1 FADH₂, and 1 acetyl-CoA. Long-chain fatty acids yield more ATP than glucose per carbon but require more O₂.

**Fatty acid synthesis:** acetyl-CoA is shuttled out of the mitochondrion as citrate, then converted back to acetyl-CoA in the cytosol. Acetyl-CoA is carboxylated to malonyl-CoA by acetyl-CoA carboxylase (the key regulatory enzyme). Fatty acid synthase then elongates the chain.

**Ketogenesis:** during prolonged fasting or uncontrolled diabetes, oxaloacetate is depleted (used for gluconeogenesis), and acetyl-CoA accumulates. The excess acetyl-CoA is converted to ketone bodies in the liver. The brain adapts to use ketone bodies after several days of fasting.

#### Amino acid metabolism

**Amino acid catabolism:** the amino group is removed by transamination (aminotransferases) or oxidative deamination (glutamate dehydrogenase). The carbon skeletons enter the Krebs cycle at various points: pyruvate (alanine), oxaloacetate (aspartate), alpha-ketoglutarate (glutamate), succinyl-CoA (valine, isoleucine, methionine, threonine), fumarate (phenylalanine, tyrosine), acetyl-CoA (leucine, isoleucine, tryptophan, lysine).

**Urea cycle:** ammonia (NH₃) is converted to urea (NH₂-CO-NH₂) for excretion. The cycle occurs partly in the mitochondria and partly in the cytosol. Key enzymes: carbamoyl phosphate synthetase I (rate-limiting), ornithine transcarbamylase.

#### Integrated metabolism

**Gluconeogenesis:** synthesis of glucose from non-carbohydrate precursors (lactate, glycerol, glucogenic amino acids). Occurs in the liver and kidneys. Bypasses the three irreversible steps of glycolysis (pyruvate kinase, PFK-1, hexokinase) using different enzymes (PEPCK, fructose-1,6-bisphosphatase, glucose-6-phosphatase).

**Pentose phosphate pathway:** generates NADPH (for biosynthesis and the glutathione antioxidant system) and ribose-5-phosphate (for nucleotide synthesis). The oxidative phase generates NADPH; the non-oxidative phase interconverts sugars.

#### Practice routine

- Take 5 lipid items. Identify beta-oxidation, synthesis, or ketogenesis; trace the molecules.
- Take 5 amino acid items. Identify the entry point into the Krebs cycle; trace the urea cycle.
- Take 5 integrated items. Distinguish fed, fasted, and prolonged fasting metabolic states.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How this topic connects to the other Bio/Biochem topics

- **Glycolysis and Krebs cycle** provide the central framework that lipid and amino acid metabolism feeds into.
- **Cell signalling** (insulin, glucagon) regulates metabolic pathways.
- **Clinical medicine** relies on these pathways: diabetes, obesity, inborn errors of metabolism.

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"Beta-oxidation happens in the cytosol."** Beta-oxidation happens in the mitochondrial matrix. The fatty acid must be activated and transported via the carnitine shuttle.
- **"Gluconeogenesis is the reverse of glycolysis."** Three irreversible steps of glycolysis are bypassed by separate enzymes. Gluconeogenesis is not a simple reversal.
- **"Insulin and glucagon do the same thing."** Insulin is anabolic (fed state); glucagon is catabolic (fasted state). They have opposite effects on most pathways.

#### Exam technique

- For lipid items, identify the metabolic state first (fed, fasted, prolonged fasting).
- For amino acid items, identify the entry point into the Krebs cycle.
- For integrated items, identify the hormones (insulin, glucagon) and the metabolic state.
