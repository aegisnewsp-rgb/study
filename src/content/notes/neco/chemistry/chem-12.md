# Organic Chemistry: Alcohols, Aldehydes and Ketones

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Alcohols** are compounds with one or more **hydroxyl (–OH)** groups bonded to a saturated (sp³) carbon; their general formula is **R–OH**. **Aldehydes** carry the terminal formyl group **–CHO** (general formula **R–CHO**), while **ketones** carry an internal **carbonyl C=O** flanked by two carbon groups (general formula **R–CO–R′**). Both aldehydes and ketones share the **carbonyl functional group**, but their position on the chain dictates their chemistry: aldehydes are easily oxidised, ketones resist mild oxidation. The **Lucas test** (ZnCl₂/HCl) classifies alcohols: tertiary alcohols cloud within seconds, secondary within minutes, primary alcohols do not react at room temperature. **Tollen's reagent** (ammoniacal AgNO₃) and **Fehling's solution** give positive results with aldehydes only, yielding a silver mirror and red Cu₂O precipitate respectively. Always remember ethanol gives a **yellow CHI₃ precipitate** in the iodoform test.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Classification and Nomenclature

Alcohols are classed by counting the carbons attached to the C–OH carbon: **primary (1°)** has one carbon attached, **secondary (2°)** has two, **tertiary (3°)** has three. IUPAC names end in **–ol** (e.g. propan-2-ol); the position of –OH is given the lowest locant. Aldehydes end in **–al** (methanal, ethanal, propanal) — always carbon-1. Ketones end in **–one** with a locant, e.g. **propanone** (CH₃COCH₃) and **butan-2-one** (CH₃COCH₂CH₃).

#### Laboratory Preparation of Ethanol

Two industrial routes dominate NECO questions:

- **Fermentation:** C₆H₁₂O₆ →(yeast, 30–40 °C, anaerobic) 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂. Yeast enzymes (zymase) are denatured above ~40 °C, so temperature control is critical.
- **Hydration of ethene:** CH₂=CH₂ + H₂O →(H₂SO₄ conc., 300 °C, 60 atm) C₂H₅OH.

#### Preparation of Aldehydes and Ketones

Aldehydes form by **oxidation of primary alcohols** with acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ or PCC: RCH₂OH + [O] → RCHO + H₂O. Further oxidation gives carboxylic acid, so the aldehyde must be distilled off immediately. Ketones form by **oxidation of secondary alcohols**: R₂CHOH + [O] → R₂C=O + H₂O. Ketones are also obtained by **dry distillation of calcium carboxylates**: (RCOO)₂Ca → R₂CO + CaCO₃.

#### Characteristic Reactions

| Reaction | Reagent | Aldehyde | Ketone |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oxidation | K₂Cr₂O₇/H⁺ | → RCOOH | resistant |
| Tollen's | Ag(NH₃)₂⁺ | Silver mirror | No reaction |
| Fehling's | Cu²⁺/alkaline | Red Cu₂O | No reaction |
| Reduction | NaBH₄ or LiAlH₄ | → RCH₂OH | → R₂CHOH |
| 2,4-DNPH | Brady's reagent | Orange ppt | Orange ppt |
| HCN addition | HCN/base | → cyanohydrin | → cyanohydrin |

The **iodoform test** uses I₂/NaOH: CH₃CH₂OH + 3I₂ + 4NaOH → CHI₃↓(yellow) + HCOONa + 3NaI + 3H₂O. Positive for ethanol, ethanal, methyl ketones and the CH₃CH(OH)R secondary alcohol motif.

#### Esterification and Dehydration

Alcohols condense with carboxylic acids in the **Fischer esterification** (conc. H₂SO₄ catalyst): CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH ⇌ CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O. Concentrated H₂SO₄ at 170 °C dehydrates ethanol to ethene (C₂H₅OH → C₂H₄ + H₂O); at 140 °C it gives diethyl ether (2C₂H₅OH → C₂H₅OC₂H₅ + H₂O).

#### Exam Pointers

NECO SSCE Paper II objective questions on this topic test: identification by functional group, naming, distinguishing aldehydes from ketones via Tollen's/Fehling's, the iodoform test, and calculation of empirical/molecular formula from percentage composition. Theory (Paper III) often asks for balanced equations of oxidation and esterification.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Intermolecular Forces and Physical Properties

**Hydrogen bonding** dominates alcohol boiling points: ethanol (b.p. 78 °C) boils far higher than dimethyl ether (–24 °C) of identical molecular mass, because the O–H⋯O hydrogen-bond network in alcohols demands extra energy to break. As –OH content rises in **polyhydric alcohols** (ethane-1,2-diol, propane-1,2,3-triol), viscosity, boiling point and water solubility all increase sharply. Aldehydes and ketones lack O–H, so their boiling points fall between those of alkanes and alcohols of similar mass; they accept hydrogen bonds from water, explaining why the lower members (methanal, ethanal, propanone) are completely water-miscible.

#### Mechanism of Nucleophilic Addition

The carbonyl C=O is **polar** (Cδ⁺=Oδ⁻). Nucleophiles attack the electrophilic carbon: HCN adds across the C=O to give a **cyanohydrin** RCH(OH)CN. The same mechanism underlies reaction with **NaHSO₃** (bisulfite adduct), **Grignard reagents** (RMgX, then H₃O⁺ yields an alcohol), and **2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazine** (orange DNPH derivative used to identify carbonyls). Aldehydes react faster than ketones because two alkyl groups in ketones sterically hinder nucleophile approach and donate electron density to Cδ⁺.

#### The Iodoform Test in Detail

Only molecules containing the **CH₃CO–** group (or oxidisable to it, i.e. CH₃CH(OH)R) respond. All six hydrogens of three I₂ molecules are abstracted by NaOH, and the C–C bond cleaves to deposit **triiodomethane (CHI₃)** — a pale-yellow solid with a medicinal odour. This test cleanly distinguishes ethanol from methanol, and propan-2-ol from propan-1-ol.

#### Common Pitfalls

- Students confuse **propanal (CH₃CH₂CHO)** with **propanone (CH₃COCH₃)**: only propanal gives silver mirror and Fehling's red precipitate.
- Concentrated H₂SO₄ dehydrates ethanol to **ethene at 170 °C**, but produces **diethyl ether at 140 °C** — temperature is the deciding factor.
- Fermentation stops at ~14% ethanol because the yeast cells die in high alcohol concentration; the mixture is then fractionally distilled to obtain 96% spirit.
- "Strong oxidation" of an aldehyde gives the carboxylic acid with the **same number of carbon atoms** — never loses CO₂.

#### Worked Micro-Example

A compound X has molecular formula C₃H₆O. It gives a silver mirror with Tollen's reagent but no yellow precipitate with I₂/NaOH. Identify X. Since X is an aldehyde (silver mirror) with three carbons, it must be **propanal, CH₃CH₂CHO**. The iodoform test is negative because no CH₃CO– group is present in propanal — only in its isomer propanone would the test be positive.

#### Connection to Other Topics

Alcohol oxidation reactions link directly to **carboxylic acids**, while reduction with NaBH₄ reverses the oxidation pathway. **Ester hydrolysis** (covered later) is the reverse of Fischer esterification. **Haloalkanes** are obtained by reacting alcohols with PCl₅, SOCl₂ or conc. HCl/ZnCl₂ (Lucas), tying this topic into **halogenoalkanes and acyl chlorides**.
