# Pathology: Systemic Disease

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review

> Step 1 is "constructed according to an integrated content outline that organizes basic science material along two dimensions: system and process". Systemic disease pathology is the system half; the process half is how you get from a slide, a film or a laboratory result to the mechanism.

| System | Organise by | The morphology that settles it |
|---|---|---|
| Cardiovascular | Vessel, pump or blood | Plaque in the wall, thinned wall, dilated wall |
| Respiratory | Obstructive, restrictive, vascular or pleural | Lumen, alveolar wall, or pleura |
| Renal | Glomerular, tubular, interstitial or vascular | The structure that is abnormal on the slide |
| Gastrointestinal | Mucosal, mural or peritoneal | Depth of the lesion |
| Endocrine | The gland, the pituitary or ectopic tissue | Hyperplasia, adenoma or carcinoma |
| Haematological | Production, loss or destruction | Cell size, cell shape, cells on the film |

**One lesion, several causes.** A granuloma is non-caseating in sarcoidosis and caseating in tuberculosis; a crescent is rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis whatever the cause; a duodenal ulcer is peptic, while a similar gastric ulcer may be malignant.

**One cause, several morphologies.** Infarction is pale in the heart, red and haemorrhagic in the lung, and liquefied in the brain. Chronic inflammation is granulomatous in tuberculosis, suppurative in an abscess, fibrosing in the bowel.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study

Step 1 assesses "important concepts of the sciences basic to the practice of medicine, with special emphasis on principles and mechanisms underlying health, disease, and modes of therapy". Since May 2026 it runs in fourteen 30-minute blocks in one 8-hour session, with no more than 20 items per block, so breadth beats depth here. Work every question the same way: locate the system, name the pattern of injury, name the cells the slide shows, then name the disease — classification first, obstructive before restrictive, nephritic before nephrotic, glomerular before tubular.

#### Cardiovascular disease

Atherosclerosis runs fatty streak, fibrous plaque with a lipid core, then complicated plaque with calcification, ulceration and superimposed thrombosis. It begins in the abdominal aorta below the renal arteries, then coronaries and popliteal vessels, sparing the lung, and thrombosis on a ruptured plaque is the usual event in acute coronary syndromes. Chronic hypertension gives hyaline arteriolosclerosis; malignant hypertension gives onion-skin narrowing with fibrinoid necrosis and a flea-bitten kidney. Early infarct complications cluster: free wall rupture tamponade, papillary muscle rupture acute mitral regurgitation, septal rupture a new septal defect. Rheumatic heart disease gives Aschoff bodies and commissural fusion, infective endocarditis gives bulky friable vegetations, and an intimal tear with blood tracking in the media is an aortic dissection.

#### Respiratory disease

The smoker's emphysema is centriacinar and upper-lobe predominant; alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency gives panacinar emphysema in the lower lobes. Chronic bronchitis is mucous gland hyperplasia; asthma adds mucus plugs, eosinophils, Charcot-Leyden crystals and a thick basement membrane; bronchiectasis is irreversible bronchial dilatation with copious purulent sputum. The usual interstitial fibrosis is patchy and subpleural with fibroblastic foci and honeycomb change, unlike the uniform fibrosis of non-specific interstitial pneumonia and the non-caseating granulomas of sarcoidosis. Mucus plugs with eosinophils are asthma, honeycomb change with patchy fibrosis is usual interstitial pneumonia, and non-caseating granulomas along lymphatics are sarcoidosis.

#### Renal disease

Classify before you name. Glomerular disease is nephritic, with haematuria, red cell casts, hypertension and sometimes low complement, or nephrotic, with heavy proteinuria, hypoalbuminaemia and oedema. Minimal change disease has a normal glomerulus by light microscopy with foot process effacement; membranous nephropathy shows diffuse capillary wall thickening with granular deposits and follows solid tumours and hepatitis B and C; IgA nephropathy shows mesangial IgA within days of a throat infection, while post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis follows after a latent interval with subepithelial humps and low complement. Crescents define rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis and nodular glomerulosclerosis with retinopathy defines diabetic nephropathy. Acute tubular necrosis shows muddy brown casts; pyelonephritis puts neutrophils in the tubules, and its chronic form fills them with eosinophilic casts in a thyroidisation pattern.

| Urine or slide finding | Points to |
|---|---|
| Red cell casts | Glomerulonephritis |
| White cell casts | Pyelonephritis or interstitial nephritis |
| Fatty casts and oval fat bodies | Nephrotic syndrome |
| Muddy brown granular casts | Acute tubular necrosis |
| Subepithelial humps with low complement | Post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis |

#### Gastrointestinal and hepatobiliary disease

Depth of the lesion does the classifying: mucosal disease bleeds, mural disease obstructs, peritoneal disease causes pain and guarding. A peptic ulcer has four layers, necrotic fibrinoid debris over inflammatory exudate, granulation tissue and a fibrous scar; the malignant ulcer has heaped-up irregular margins and folds that stop at the edge.

Crohn disease is transmural with skip lesions, cobblestoning and non-caseating granulomas, while ulcerative colitis is mucosal, continuous from the rectum, with crypt abscesses and pseudopolyps, carrying the colorectal cancer risk and toxic megacolon. The adenoma-carcinoma sequence underlies colorectal cancer, disrupted by APC in familial adenomatous polyposis and by mismatch repair in Lynch syndrome. Diffuse gastric carcinoma with signet-ring cells causes linitis plastica; femoral hernias strangulate more often because the ring below the inguinal ligament is rigid; cirrhosis is regenerative nodules in fibrous septa with portal hypertension behind ascites and varices; acute pancreatitis adds fat necrosis with chalky saponification, and carcinoma in the head of the pancreas gives painless obstructive jaundice.

#### Endocrine disease

Decide first whether the lesion sits in the gland or the pituitary: a pituitary adenoma gives mass effect with bitemporal hemianopia and drives hyperprolactinaemia, acromegaly or Cushing disease. In the adrenal cortex an adenoma is circumscribed and suppresses ACTH, while an adrenocortical carcinoma is large, necrotic and invades capsule and veins. Thyroid morphology separates Hashimoto, with lymphoid germinal centres and Hürthle cells, from Graves, with tall crowded follicles and scalloped colloid, from painful subacute granulomatous thyroiditis, and from Riedel, with dense fibrosis making a hard woody thyroid. In a nodule, capsule or vessel invasion decides follicular carcinoma, since cytology cannot separate adenoma from carcinoma. A parathyroid adenoma involves one gland with a rim of normal tissue; hyperplasia involves all four.

| Endocrine presentation | Localisation | Morphology |
|---|---|---|
| Cushing syndrome with high ACTH | Pituitary | Corticotroph adenoma, bilateral adrenal hyperplasia |
| Cushing syndrome with low ACTH | Adrenal | Adenoma, atrophy of the other cortex |
| Hypertension with hypokalaemic metabolic alkalosis | Adrenal | Aldosterone-producing adenoma |

#### Haematological disease

Anaemia is classified by mechanism before morphology: lost, destroyed in the vessel, or not made. Microcytic anaemia with low ferritin and high iron-binding capacity is iron deficiency; microcytic anaemia with target cells and a normal or raised ferritin is thalassaemia; macrocytic anaemia with hypersegmented neutrophils is megaloblastic, and dorsal column signs separate vitamin B12 deficiency from folate deficiency; normocytic anaemia fits acute blood loss or early haemolysis. Haemolysis splits intrinsic from extrinsic by where the damage happens, and the signature is the same in both: raised LDH, low haptoglobin, unconjugated hyperbilirubinaemia and reticulocytosis, with haemoglobinuria only in intravascular haemolysis.

Acute leukaemia is dominated by blasts, with Auer rods in myeloid disease and a mediastinal mass in T-cell acute lymphoblastic leukaemia. Chronic myeloid leukaemia carries the Philadelphia chromosome with a low leukocyte alkaline phosphatase score, while chronic lymphocytic leukaemia has smudge cells and a high score. Multiple myeloma shows plasma cells, punched-out lytic lesions, rouleaux and Bence Jones protein; consumptive DIC falls in fibrinogen and rises in D-dimer, while thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura leaves both normal.

| Peripheral blood clue | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Hypersegmented neutrophils | Megaloblastic anaemia |
| Spherocytes without haemoglobinuria | Extravascular haemolysis |
| Schistocytes | Microangiopathic haemolysis |
| Bite cells and Heinz bodies | Oxidative haemolysis, G6PD deficiency |
| Smudge cells | Chronic lymphocytic leukaemia |

### 🔴 Extended — Mastery

#### Worked example: a nephritic syndrome stem

A young man has cola-coloured urine and facial swelling shortly after a sore throat. Urinalysis shows red cell casts, blood pressure is raised, and serum complement is low.

The pattern first: haematuria with red cell casts and hypertension means nephritic, so the nephrotic diseases are out. Then the timing, the discriminator the item turns on: the nephritis appeared within days of the pharyngitis, which points to IgA nephropathy, the synpharyngitic disease, rather than post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis, which follows after a longer latent interval and in which complement consumption dominates. Its mechanism differs too: post-streptococcal disease is an immune complex disease with granular IgG and C3 and subepithelial humps, while IgA nephropathy deposits in the mesangium.

#### Common mistakes

Treating nephrotic syndrome as a diagnosis rather than a pattern, and never using the slide to choose between minimal change disease, focal segmental glomerulosclerosis and membranous nephropathy.

Calling every granuloma tuberculosis, when the caseating versus non-caseating distinction and the clinical context separate tuberculosis, sarcoidosis and a foreign-body reaction.

Missing the depth of a bowel lesion, so Crohn disease and ulcerative colitis get swapped because the stem said inflammatory bowel disease and stopped there.
