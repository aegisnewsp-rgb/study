# The Legislature and Law Making

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The **legislature** is the arm of government that makes, amends, and repeals laws. In Nigeria, the federal legislature is the **National Assembly**, made up of two chambers: the **Senate** (upper house) and the **House of Representatives** (lower house). A proposal before it becomes law is called a **bill**; once the President has assented to it, it becomes an **Act**.

A bill must pass through **First Reading**, **Second Reading**, **Committee Stage**, **Report Stage**, and **Third Reading** in both chambers before it reaches the President. The President must assent within 30 days, or the National Assembly can override with a **two-thirds majority** of each chamber.

- A **unicameral** legislature has one chamber; a **bicameral** legislature has two.
- A **Government Bill** is sponsored by the executive; a **Private Member's Bill** is sponsored by an individual legislator.
- Without **presidential assent**, a bill is NOT yet law, no matter how many times parliament voted for it.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Meaning and Types of Legislature

The legislature performs five core functions: **law making**, **representation** of constituents, **oversight** of the executive, **approval of budgets**, and **amendment of the constitution**. Nigeria operates a **bicameral** legislature at the federal level, while some states (such as Ekiti and Lagos historically) use a **unicameral** house of assembly.

#### Stages of Law Making in Nigeria

| Stage | What Happens |
| --- | --- |
| First Reading | Bill is read out and titled; no debate occurs. |
| Second Reading | Principle of the bill is debated; voted on for acceptance. |
| Committee Stage | Line-by-line scrutiny; public hearings may be held. |
| Report Stage | Committee reports back; amendments proposed. |
| Third Reading | Final passage in the chamber; transmitted to the other house. |

#### Bill Versus Act

A **bill** is a proposed law still being considered. It becomes an **Act of Parliament** only after both chambers pass it and the President assents. If the President withholds assent, the bill returns with reasons within 30 days; a two-thirds vote in each chamber forces it into law.

#### Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Saying the President **makes** laws (he assents to them; he cannot single-handedly legislate in a democracy).
- Believing the Senate alone passes bills (the House of Representatives must also pass it).
- Confusing the **veto** with refusing to sign a bill into law — the President must state his reasons to the National Assembly.
- Skipping **committee work**, which is where most clause-by-clause changes happen.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Roles and Powers of the Legislature

Beyond law making, the legislature holds the executive accountable through **questions**, **motions**, **committee investigations**, and **impeachment** powers. The **Speaker** presides over the House of Representatives, while the **President of the Senate** presides over the upper chamber, both guided by **Standing Orders** that govern debate and conduct.

#### The Conference Committee

When the Senate and House of Representatives pass **different versions** of the same bill, a **Conference Committee** (made up of members from both chambers) reconciles the differences. Its report returns to both chambers for final approval before going to the President.

#### Worked Example: Tracing a Bill

Imagine a bill called the "Clean Water Act." Sponsor A introduces it in the House of Representatives:

1. **First Reading** — titled and listed for debate.
2. **Second Reading** — principle debated and approved.
3. **Committee Stage** — committee examines clauses, takes public input.
4. **Report Stage** — committee reports with proposed amendments.
5. **Third Reading** — House passes the final version.
6. The bill moves to the Senate, which repeats stages 1–5.
7. If the Senate amends the bill, a **Conference Committee** resolves differences.
8. The President assents within 30 days, and the bill is published in the **Gazette** as law.

#### Exam Strategy for NCEE

NCEE Civic Education dedicates roughly 3% of its paper to the legislature and law making. Questions often ask candidates to:

1. Identify the **functions** of the legislature from a list of options.
2. Arrange the **stages of bill passage** in correct order.
3. Distinguish between a **bill** and an **Act** in short-answer form.
4. Name the two chambers of the **National Assembly** correctly.

A common trap: confusing **presidential assent** with the President's power to write laws. Memorise the 30-day assent window and the two-thirds override rule, as these are the most-tested numerical facts.
