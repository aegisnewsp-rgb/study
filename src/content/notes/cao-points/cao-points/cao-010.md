# Restricted Entry Courses and Supplementary Forms

Some CAO courses do not allocate places on points alone. They are marked **restricted entry**, and the restriction means the course applies an additional selection procedure on top of the ordinary points competition — and that the course cannot be placed freely on your list. A second group of courses asks for a **supplementary form**: an additional document you submit directly to the institution, with its own deadline, entirely separate from the CAO application. Missing either is a silent way to lose a place you had the points for.

## Restricted entry

A restricted-entry course is one where the institution applies a defined additional criterion before places are allocated. What the criterion actually is differs by course and institution — it may involve an assessment, an interview, a written submission, a portfolio, a health declaration, or an ordering requirement applied to how the course may appear on your list.

The point that catches candidates out: **the CAO application and the restricted-entry process are two separate systems running on separate clocks.** Completing the CAO application correctly does not satisfy a restricted-entry requirement, and completing a restricted-entry form does not place you in the CAO points competition. You have to do both, each within its own deadline.

Because the restricted process runs alongside the CAO allocation, a candidate can satisfy the points threshold and still not receive an offer, because the restricted criterion was not met. That is not a failure of the CAO system; it is what "restricted" means.

### Where the restriction bites hardest: list ordering

The most consequential feature of restricted entry for most applicants is not the assessment — it is where the course sits on your list.

The Handbook's restricted-entry rules limit the position at which a restricted course may appear relative to other courses at the same institution. In practice this means a restricted course cannot be dropped far down your list beneath other courses at that institution, on the reasoning that a candidate who genuinely wants the restricted course cannot be seen to treat it as a fallback to that same institution's open courses.

The consequence for planning is direct: a candidate with an ambitious first preference who tucks a restricted medicine course at position nine beneath four other courses at the same institution has not built a legal list. Read the specific course's Handbook entry for how it is ordered and place it accordingly. Restricted-entry rules are set per admission cycle, so the arrangement must be read from the current Handbook, not from memory or from an older application.

## Supplementary forms

A supplementary form is a direct application to an institution, used alongside the CAO application. The CAO publishes which courses use one. It is the mechanism through which a course asks for information the points grid cannot carry — written work, a personal statement, evidence of relevant experience, or an assessment sitting run by the university itself.

The two obligations run in parallel and are both mandatory. Points through the CAO, and the supplementary submission to the institution, by their own deadlines.

Where a supplementary process produces a ranked order of eligible candidates, that ranking interacts with the CAO allocation in ways the candidate cannot control from the CAO side. Read the institution's own documentation for how its result feeds into offers, rather than assuming the CAO rules cover it.

## The failure modes

**Form not submitted.** The most common and the most avoidable. The CAO application is complete and correct, the deadline is met, and no offer arrives because a document sent to a university address was never sent.

**Form submitted late.** Supplementary and restricted processes run on the academic year's calendar, not the CAO's. Their deadlines can fall while you are waiting on exam results.

**Restriction misread.** The course is assumed to be an open course because it appears on the CAO system like any other, and the ordering rules are not applied to the list.

**Both assumed to be equivalent to points.** A candidate with a very strong aggregate can still be beaten on a restricted course by someone with fewer points and a stronger assessment result. Points are one filter among several there, not the only one.

## A working checklist

1. Open the current CAO Handbook and find the entry for each course on your shortlist.
2. Record, per course: the entry requirements, the restricted or non-restricted status, the ordering rule if restricted, and whether a supplementary form is required.
3. Note the deadline for every supplementary or restricted process and put each one in a calendar with a reminder ahead of it.
4. Read the institution's own page for the course — not just the CAO entry — to understand what the additional process actually assesses.
5. Place restricted courses on your list in a position permitted by the Handbook rules.
6. Re-check the list after the change-of-list deadline passes, because a reorder can invalidate the position of a restricted course.

> Restricted-entry arrangements, ordering rules and supplementary-form requirements are all set per admission cycle. Read the current CAO Handbook and the individual institution's admissions pages on cao.ie before you build a list — do not work from last year's arrangement.

## Self-check before you close the page

1. You have a CAO application that is complete and on time, and you are strong on points for a restricted-entry course. Why would you still receive no offer? *(Because the restricted-entry selection runs separately and you have not satisfied it — or have not submitted what it requires. The CAO points competition and the restricted process are two systems, and clearing one says nothing about the other.)*
2. A candidate places a restricted medicine course at position nine beneath four other courses at the same institution. What is the problem? *(The restricted-entry ordering rules limit where a restricted course sits relative to other courses at that institution. The list as built may not be valid, which can compromise the restricted course or the entries beneath it.)*
3. When does a supplementary form's deadline fall relative to the CAO's? *(On the academic year's calendar, not the CAO's — it can fall while you are waiting on exam results. Track it separately.)*
4. Can a supplementary form substitute for the CAO application? *(No. They run in parallel and both are required. One is the points application through the CAO; the other is a direct submission to the institution with its own criteria and deadline.)*
5. Why does a lower-points candidate get an offer ahead of you on a restricted course? *(Because the restricted criterion is assessed separately from points and can rank eligible candidates independently of the Common Points Scale. Points are one filter there, not the only one.)*
