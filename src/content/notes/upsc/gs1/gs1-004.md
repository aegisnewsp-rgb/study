# Geography India

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> One-sentence anchor for the UPSC desk: India is a subcontinent, and every Prelims MCQ on this paper secretly tests whether you can place a fact on its physiographic map.

India splits into **six physiographic divisions**: the Northern Mountains, the Northern Plains, the Peninsular Plateau, the Indian Desert, the Coastal Plains, and the Islands. Monsoon climate drives rainfall: the **South-West monsoon (June–September)** delivers ~75% of total precipitation, and the **North-East monsoon (October–December)** rescues Tamil Nadu's coast.

Five population formulas are non-negotiable (SI units shown):

| Indicator | Formula | Unit |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> The standard sweep covers mechanisms, regional contrasts, and the traps UPSC reuses year after year.

#### Physiographic Backbone

The **Northern Mountains** (Himalayas, Trans-Himalayas, Purvanchal) feed three perennial river systems: Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra. The **Northern Plains** are the alluvial stretch of these rivers — the Indo-Gangetic-Brahmaputra belt holds a disproportionate share of India's population and wheat-rice agriculture.

The **Peninsular Plateau** is older, mineral-rich, and geologically stable. Two flanks matter: the **Western Ghats** (continuous, 900–1600 m, orographic rainfall belt) and the **Eastern Ghats** (discontinuous, broken by river deltas, rain-shadow side). The **Indian Desert** (Thar) lies in the rain shadow of the Aravallis. **Coastal Plains** on both sides are narrow alluvial strips, and the **Islands** (Andaman-Nicobar volcanic arc, Lakshadweep coral atoll) close the physiographic frame.

#### Monsoon Mechanism

Differential heating between land and sea pulls the **Inter-Tropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ)** north in summer. The **subtropical westerly jet** retreats, replaced by an easterly cross-equatorial flow from the Mascarene High. Onshore winds strike the Western Ghats first — **Agumbe (Karnataka), Mahabaleshwar, Cherrapunji (Meghalaya)** receive extreme orographic rainfall because they sit windward to moisture-laden flow.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix

| Feature | Western Ghats | Eastern Ghats |
|---|---|---|
| Continuity | Continuous ridge | Discontinuous, broken by deltas |
| Average elevation | 900–1600 m | 600–900 m (locally lower) |
| Rainfall side | Windward — heavy | Leeward — moderate |
| Rivers' direction | Short, westward (e.g. Mandovi, Zuari) | Eastward (Godavari, Krishna) |

| River System | Origin Type | Flow Direction | Key Tributary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Indus | Himalayan (Tibet) | West → Arabian Sea | Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, Sutlej |
| Ganga | Himalayan (Gangotri) | East → Bay of Bengal | Yamuna, Son, Gandak, Kosi |
| Godavari | Peninsular (Nasik) | East → Bay of Bengal | Manjira, Penganga |
| Narmada | Peninsular (Amarkantak) | West → Arabian Sea (rift) | Tawa, Hiran |

#### Worked Resolution: Monsoon Onset Trap

**Question.** "The South-West monsoon normally reaches Kerala around which date and then advances towards which region?" Choose from:
(a) May 1, retreating south-eastward to Sri Lanka
(b) June 1, advancing north-westward across India
(c) July 15, withdrawing from the Bay of Bengal
(d) September 1, retreating south-westward

**Step 1.** Recall that the South-West monsoon is a **summer** phenomenon driven by ITCZ shift and land-sea heating. It cannot arrive in May (too early) nor withdraw in July (peak is August).

**Step 2.** Standard onset over Kerala: around **June 1**, then it branches — **Arabian Sea branch** moves up the west coast and into the Northern Plains; **Bay of Bengal branch** sweeps over the north-east and curves up the Brahmaputra valley.

**Step 3.** Match: option (b) fits both date and direction.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students confuse the *onset over Kerala* (~June 1) with *onset over north-west India* (early July). UPSC options often swap dates — keep the two events apart.

#### Soil and Vegetation Edge Cases

| Soil | Parent Material | Typical Crop | Regional Anomaly |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alluvial | River deposits | Wheat, rice, sugarcane | Indo-Gangetic Plain, Brahmaputra valley |
| Black (Regur) | Basalt (Deccan trap) | Cotton, soybean | Extends from Gujarat through Maharashtra, MP, Karnataka, AP, TN — not "Maharashtra-only" |
| Red | Granitic-gneissic weathering | Groundnut, millet, pulses | Eastern Deccan, Odisha, Chhattisgarh |
| Laterite | Heavy leaching, humid tropics | Tea, coffee, cashew, rubber | Western Ghats, NE hills — nutrient-poor, NOT fertile |
| Desert (Arid) | Aeolian sand | Bajra, guar, mustard | Thar — Western Rajasthan, parts of Haryana, Kutch |

> 📌 **Census Watch:** Use **Census 2011** as the published baseline for population density, literacy, and sex ratio. Census 2021 was deferred; projections cited without a base year invite mark loss.

#### Industrial Geography Connectors

- **Iron ore belt:** Odisha (Mayurbhanj, Keonjhar), Jharkhand (Singhbhum), Chhattisgarh (Bailadila). The **Durg-Bhilai** and **Rourkela-Jamshedpur** steel axis is built on this belt.
- **Coal belt:** Jharkhand–Odisha–Chhattisgarh–West Bengal — Damodar valley hosts **Jharia, Raniganj, Bokaro**.
- **Petroleum hubs:** **Mumbai High** (offshore), Assam (**Digboi, Naharkatiak**), Gujarat (**Cambay basin, Ankleshwar**).
- **Industrial corridors (policy-driven):** Delhi–Mumbai (DMIC), Bengaluru–Chennai, Vizag–Chennai; SEZs and NIMZs are the formal planning instruments behind them.

#### Practice Prompts

1. **Assertion-Reason drill.** "Laterite soil is ideal for paddy cultivation." Identify the precise point where this statement breaks — nutrient leaching, iron-aluminium oxide dominance, and the actual crop profile (tea, coffee, cashew). Frame a 150-word Mains-style response anchoring each claim to a state example (Kerala, Karnataka, Odisha).
2. **Map-based inference.** Plot the eastward-flowing Peninsular rivers (Godavari, Krishna, Cauvery, Mahanadi) and the westward-flowing exceptions (Narmada, Tapti, Mahi). Why does the western group flow against the Deccan slope? Answer: they occupy **rift valleys / tectonic troughs** (Narmada–Son lineament, Tapti trough), not consequent drainage.
