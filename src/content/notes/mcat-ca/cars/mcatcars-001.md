# Foundations of Comprehension — MCAT CARS Skill 1

Foundations of Comprehension is CARS Skill 1 in the AAMC CARS framework. It is the ability to read a CARS passage for basic meaning: identifying the main idea, the theme, the author's purpose, the tone, and the structure of the argument. This skill underlies every other CARS skill — Reasoning Within the Text, Reasoning Beyond the Text, and Quantitative Reasoning all depend on a solid grasp of what the passage is saying.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### What Skill 1 tests

- **Main idea:** the central claim the passage is making. Usually stated or implied in the first or last paragraph.
- **Theme:** the underlying topic or idea the passage explores. Often broader than the main idea.
- **Author's purpose:** why the author wrote the passage — to inform, to persuade, to critique, to entertain, to reflect.
- **Tone:** the author's attitude toward the subject — formal, informal, sarcastic, sympathetic, hopeful, frustrated.
- **Passage structure:** how the passage is organised — chronological, compare-contrast, problem-solution, cause-effect, definition-example.

#### One-pass drill

1. Read the passage in 3-4 minutes. Underline or mentally mark the main claim in each paragraph.
2. Identify the main idea in one sentence after the first read.
3. Identify the author's purpose: is the author arguing, explaining, describing, or reflecting?
4. Identify the tone: how does the author feel about the subject?

#### Examiner traps

- Picking a detail as the main idea. The main idea is the central claim, not a specific fact.
- Confusing the author's purpose with the reader's purpose. The author writes for a reason; the reader reads for a different reason.
- Mistaking the tone for the topic. Tone is how the author feels; topic is what the passage is about.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Identifying the main idea

The main idea is the central claim the passage is making. To find it:

- Read the first and last paragraphs first. They often state the main idea explicitly.
- Look for a sentence that the rest of the passage supports, illustrates, or argues for.
- If the main idea is implied, summarise each paragraph in one sentence, then look for the common thread.

The MCAT main-idea items ask "the primary purpose of the passage is to..." or "the main idea of the passage is best described as..." The right answer is the choice that captures the central claim.

#### Identifying the author's purpose

The author writes for a reason. The MCAT purpose items ask "the author's primary purpose in the passage is to..." Common purposes:

- **Argue:** the author takes a position and supports it with reasons.
- **Explain:** the author describes a process, a concept, or a phenomenon.
- **Critique:** the author evaluates a position, a work, or an idea.
- **Reflect:** the author explores a personal experience or a thought.
- **Persuade:** the author tries to convince the reader to take a position.

#### Identifying the tone

Tone is conveyed through word choice, sentence structure, and the author's attitude toward the subject. Common tones on CARS:

- Formal vs informal.
- Objective vs subjective.
- Skeptical vs accepting.
- Sympathetic vs critical.
- Hopeful vs despairing.

#### Identifying passage structure

CARS passages use common organisational patterns:

- **Chronological:** events in time order.
- **Compare-contrast:** two or more positions or phenomena set against each other.
- **Problem-solution:** a problem is stated, then a solution is proposed.
- **Cause-effect:** a phenomenon is explained by its causes or consequences.
- **Definition-example:** a concept is defined, then illustrated.

#### Practice routine

- Take one CARS passage. Read it in 4 minutes. Identify the main idea, purpose, tone, and structure.
- Answer the Skill 1 items. Compare your answers to the rationale.
- Time yourself: under 4 minutes per passage.
- Take 5 passages in a row. Repeat the routine.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How Skill 1 connects to the other CARS skills

- **Skill 2 (Reasoning Within the Text)** requires Skill 1 first — you must understand what the passage says before you can evaluate the argument.
- **Skill 3 (Reasoning Beyond the Text)** extends Skill 1 by asking how the passage applies to new situations.
- **Skill 4 (Quantitative Reasoning)** requires Skill 1 to understand the numerical claims in the passage.

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"The main idea is stated in the title."** Sometimes, but not always. Read the passage to confirm.
- **"The author's purpose is to inform."** Informing is a purpose, but CARS passages often argue, critique, or reflect. Read the verbs and the framing.
- **"Tone is the same as topic."** Tone is how the author feels about the topic. Topic is what the passage is about.

#### Exam technique

- Read the passage in 3-4 minutes. Mark the main claim in each paragraph.
- Identify the main idea, purpose, tone, and structure after the first read.
- For Skill 1 items, ask: "what does the passage say?" not "what do I think about the topic?"
- Eliminate choices that contradict the passage's main idea or tone.
- Use the platform's flag-for-review function for items you cannot answer.
