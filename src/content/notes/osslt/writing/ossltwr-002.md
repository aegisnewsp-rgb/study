# Series of Paragraphs Expressing an Opinion — OSSLT Writing

The Series-of-Paragraphs is the second long-writing task on the OSSLT. Students are given an information source on a debatable issue and asked to write two or three connected paragraphs that state a clear opinion and support it with reasons drawn from the source. The EQAO OSSLT rubric scores the response on idea development, organisation, language and conventions. This is the single highest-weighted writing item on the OSSLT.

> Verify the live task stem, source pairing, and rubric on https://www.eqao.com/the-assessments/osslt/ before planning around the figures below.

---

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### What the Series-of-Paragraphs task asks for

- A clear opinion stated in the first paragraph.
- Two or three reasons supporting the opinion.
- Each reason supported by evidence from the supplied information source.
- Two or three connected paragraphs that flow logically from opinion to reasons to wrap-up.
- Writing that is grammatically correct, spelled correctly, and punctuated correctly.

#### One-pass structure

1. Paragraph 1: state your opinion clearly. Briefly preview the reasons.
2. Paragraph 2: state the first reason and support it with a fact from the source.
3. Paragraph 3: state the second reason and support it with a fact from the source.
4. (Optional) Paragraph 4: state the third reason, or wrap up the argument.
5. Final sentence: restate the opinion in light of the reasons.

#### Examiner traps

- Burying the opinion. The opinion must be stated clearly in the first paragraph, not implied.
- Inventing reasons. Reasons must be supported by facts from the supplied information source.
- Writing a single paragraph. The task asks for *a series of paragraphs* — at least two, ideally three.
- Missing the wrap-up. The response should end with a sentence that ties the reasons back to the opinion.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Strategy for the Series-of-Paragraphs task

**Read the information source twice.** The first read is for sense — what is the issue, what are the main arguments. The second read is to mark the usable facts — the specific claims, examples, statistics, and quotes that could support an opinion.

**Choose the opinion before writing.** The opinion should be one that you can support with at least two reasons from the source. Do not pick an opinion that requires inventing evidence.

**State the opinion in the first sentence.** "I believe that..." or "In my view..." or "The strongest argument is..." The first paragraph should make the opinion unmistakable.

**Use one reason per body paragraph.** Each reason gets its own paragraph, supported by one fact from the source. Do not stack multiple reasons into one paragraph — the rubric scores organisation on paragraph separation.

**Use transitions between paragraphs.** "Another reason is..." "Furthermore..." "In addition..." "On the other hand..." Transitions show logical flow between paragraphs.

**Close with a sentence that ties the reasons back to the opinion.** The wrap-up sentence is short — one or two clauses — and restates the opinion in light of the reasons.

#### Practice routine for the Series-of-Paragraphs task

- Take one EQAO sample Series-of-Paragraphs task. Read the prompt and source. List the usable facts in the source.
- Pick an opinion you can support with at least two of the facts. State the opinion in one sentence.
- Write the first paragraph (opinion + preview of reasons). Time yourself: under 5 minutes.
- Write two body paragraphs (one reason per paragraph). Time yourself: under 10 minutes total.
- Write the closing sentence. Time yourself: under 2 minutes.
- Total target: under 20 minutes for the long-writing task.
- After writing, count the spelling, grammar and punctuation errors. Target: fewer than three per response.

#### How the rubric scores the Series-of-Paragraphs

The EQAO OSSLT rubric for the long-writing tasks scores four dimensions:

- **Idea development.** Does the response include a clear opinion, two or three reasons, and supporting evidence from the source?
- **Organisation.** Does the response use a series of distinct paragraphs, with the opinion first and the wrap-up last, and transitions between paragraphs?
- **Language and vocabulary.** Does the response use precise verbs and nouns, varied sentence structure, and an appropriate formal-essay tone?
- **Conventions.** Is the response free of spelling, grammar and punctuation errors?

A response that scores well on idea development but poorly on organisation (single paragraph, no transitions) can still score in the middle range.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How the Series-of-Paragraphs task connects to the other OSSLT items

- **Reading 1** (explicit information) is needed to locate the supporting facts in the source.
- **Reading 2** (inference) is needed to interpret the source's claims — what the source implies rather than what it says outright.
- **Reading 3** (making connections) is useful for the wrap-up sentence, which often connects the argument back to the reader's experience or to a broader context.

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"I should give both sides of the argument."** The Series-of-Paragraphs task asks for *your* opinion supported by reasons. A balanced view is not the goal; a clear, supported opinion is.
- **"I can use my own experience as evidence."** The reasons must be supported by the supplied information source. Personal experience alone does not count.
- **"Three paragraphs is too many."** The task asks for *a series of paragraphs*. Two or three paragraphs is the standard; one paragraph is too few.
- **"I should pick the opinion that is most popular."** Pick the opinion that you can support with the strongest evidence from the source, not the one you think the reader will agree with.

#### Exam technique

- Read the source twice. Mark the usable facts.
- Choose the opinion you can support with the most facts. State the opinion in the first sentence.
- Write one body paragraph per reason. Use transitions between paragraphs.
- Close with a sentence that ties the reasons back to the opinion.
- Re-read for spelling, grammar and punctuation. Use the platform's spell-check if available.
- Use the platform's word-count or length indicator to check you have written enough but not too much.
