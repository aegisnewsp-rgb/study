# Indian Economy & Banking

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The **Reserve Bank of India (RBI)** is the apex monetary authority of the country, established under the RBI Act, 1934 and nationalised on 1 January 1949. It issues all currency notes except the **₹1 note/coin** (issued by the Ministry of Finance), regulates scheduled commercial banks, and decides the **repo rate** through the six-member **Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)** under Section 45ZA of the RBI Act.

Two reserve ratios govern every scheduled bank in India: the **Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR)**, maintained as cash with the RBI, and the **Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR)**, maintained as gold or government securities. Both directly determine the **money multiplier**, i.e. how much new deposits a unit of fresh reserves can generate.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"CRR = Cash with RBI; SLR = Securities/Liquidity"** — CRR earns zero interest, SLR earns interest on government bonds. Pair it with **"MPC = 6 (3 RBI + 3 external)"** and you cover half the AILET GK paper on banking.

- **Fiscal Deficit** = Total Expenditure − Total Receipts excluding borrowings (₹, INR).
- **Primary Deficit** = Fiscal Deficit − Interest Payments (₹, INR).
- **Money Multiplier (m)** = 1 / CRR, where CRR is a unitless fraction (e.g. CRR = 0.04 → m = 25).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Structure of the Indian Economy

India follows a mixed economy model with three productive sectors: **primary (agriculture)**, **secondary (manufacturing)**, and **tertiary (services)**. Services account for roughly 53% of GVA at current prices. Economic planning was guided by the **Planning Commission** from 1950 until 1 January 2015, when it was replaced by **NITI Aayog**, a policy think-tank chaired by the Prime Minister that does **not** allocate funds.

Fiscal policy is framed annually through the **Union Budget** presented by the **Finance Minister**, while monetary policy is set by the **MPC**, which meets at least four times a year and decides the policy repo rate by majority; the RBI Governor chairs the committee.

#### Banking Architecture and Monetary Tools

| Institution / Tool | Function | Key Statutory Reference |
|---|---|---|
| **Reserve Bank of India** | Apex monetary authority; currency issue; bank regulation | RBI Act, 1934 |
| **Scheduled Commercial Bank** | Bank listed in the Second Schedule of the RBI Act | RBI Act, Section 2(e) |
| **NABARD** | Agriculture and rural development refinance | NABARD Act, 1981 |
| **SIDBI** | Refinance to MSMEs | SIDBI Act, 1989 |
| **NHB** | Housing finance regulator (since 2019) | NHB Act, 1987 |
| **CRR** | % of NDTL kept as cash with RBI; no interest | RBI Act, Section 42(1) |
| **SLR** | % of NDTL in gold or approved securities | Banking Regulation Act, 1949, Section 24 |

The **repo rate** is the rate at which banks borrow short-term funds from the RBI against government securities under the **Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF)**. The reverse repo rate is what the RBI pays banks for parking surplus funds.

#### Comparing CRR vs SLR

| Feature | CRR | SLR |
|---|---|---|
| Maintained as | Cash with RBI | Gold + government securities + cash |
| Interest earned | No | Yes (on securities portion) |
| Daily maintenance | Yes (average) | Yes |
| Legal basis | RBI Act, Section 42(1) | Banking Regulation Act, Section 24 |
| Effect | Reduces lendable funds | Reduces lendable funds |

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** If the current CRR is 4% and SLR is 18%, and the public holds currency equal to 20% of demand deposits (cdr = 0.20), what is the theoretical money multiplier? (Take rdr = CRR as a simplification.)

#### Solution:
1. Money multiplier formula: **m = (1 + cdr) / (cdr + rdr)**.
2. cdr = C/D = 0.20.
3. rdr ≈ CRR = 0.04.
4. Substitute: m = (1 + 0.20) / (0.20 + 0.04) = 1.20 / 0.24 = **5.0**.
5. Interpretation: every ₹100 increase in high-powered money (RBI reserves) can theoretically create ₹500 of broad deposits in the economy.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students often quote the shortcut "m = 1/CRR" and write 25. That formula ignores the currency-drain (cdr) and reserve-drain (rdr); the correct multiplier including cdr is **5.0**, not 25.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Deficit Arithmetic and Debt Sustainability

The fiscal arithmetic of the Union Government follows a nesting order:

| Measure | Formula | Policy Use |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue Deficit | Revenue Expenditure − Revenue Receipts | Short-term gap |
| Fiscal Deficit | Total Expenditure − Total Receipts excluding borrowings | Borrowings gap |
| Primary Deficit | Fiscal Deficit − Interest Payments | Borrowings for non-debt expenditure |
| Effective Revenue Deficit | Revenue Deficit − grants for creation of capital assets | Introduced in 2012-13 |

> 📌 **Formula Check:** All deficits are measured in **₹ (INR)**. The Government of India finances the fiscal deficit chiefly through dated government securities, small savings, and external borrowings.

#### Landmark Banking Reforms Timeline

1. **1969 & 1980:** Nationalisation of 14 and then 6 major commercial banks.
2. **1991:** Narasimham Committee I — capital adequacy, asset classification (IRAC norms), NPAs at 90+ days overdue.
3. **1998:** Narasimham Committee II — capital market exposure, asset reconstruction.
4. **2014:** **Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana** — zero-balance accounts with RuPay debit card and ₹2 lakh accident cover.
5. **2016:** Demonetisation of ₹500 and ₹1000 notes (8 November).
6. **2017:** **Goods and Services Tax (GST)** rollout on 1 July; **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC), 2016** notified.
7. **2019:** Mega PSB consolidation — 27 PSU banks merged into 12 (e.g. PNB + OBC + United Bank → PNB).
8. **2020:** RBI extends **LTCG** (Long Term Repo Operations) and TLTROs for COVID liquidity.

#### Frequently Confused Pairs

1. **Fiscal Deficit vs Primary Deficit** — Primary excludes interest on past debt; Fiscal includes it.
2. **M1 vs M3** — M1 = C + DD; M3 = M1 + time deposits with banks (broad money).
3. **CPI vs WPI** — CPI (Combined, base 2012=100) drives RBI inflation targeting; WPI tracks wholesale goods.
4. **NPA provisioning under IRAC** — Standard, Sub-standard, Doubtful (D1/D2/D3), Loss assets.
5. **Repo vs Reverse Repo** — Repo = banks borrow from RBI; Reverse Repo = banks lend to RBI.

> 💡 **High-Yield Revision Hook:** For an MCQ on the current repo rate, remember that the MPC decisions are published in the * Monetary Policy Report * every two months. On fiscal deficit, the headline figure appears in **Budget at a Glance** Table 1.4 of the Union Budget documents.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. If the RBI raises CRR by 50 basis points from 4.00% to 4.50%, by how much does the theoretical multiplier fall, holding cdr at 0.20? (Answer: from 5.00 to 4.80.)
2. State which deficit (Fiscal or Primary) will change if the government waives interest on a past loan, and explain the policy signal. (Primary falls; signal of fiscal consolidation without lowering aggregate borrowing.)
