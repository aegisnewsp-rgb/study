# Legal News

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Legal News is the segment of the CLAT Current Affairs section (≈5% weight inside the broader 25% GK block) that tests your awareness of Indian judicial, legislative, and constitutional developments from the 12-month window before the exam.

- **Three new criminal codes** — BNS, BNSS and BSA replaced the IPC, CrPC and Indian Evidence Act from **1 July 2024**. Memorise the section-to-section mapping (e.g. old IPC 302 → BNS 302; old 376 → BNS 63; old 420 → BNS 318).
- **Article 142** of the Constitution gives the Supreme Court power to pass orders "necessary for doing complete justice" — frequently cited in landmark rulings.
- **Ratio decidendi** is the binding legal principle; **obiter dicta** are observations that do not bind lower courts.

| Source to follow daily | Why it matters for CLAT |
| --- | --- |
| The Hindu / Indian Express | Authoritative reporting of Supreme Court and Parliament developments |
| LiveLaw / Bar & Bench | Day-of judgment coverage with citation and bench strength |
| Supreme Court Observer | Long-form analysis of constitutional bench rulings |

**High-yield tip:** Always cite the **bench strength, judge name, citation and date** — examiners award marks for precision, not political framing.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### What CLAT Tests Under "Legal News"

Legal News items in CLAT appear as comprehension passages, factual MCQs, or principle-application sets. They focus on **what the law is now** after a recent judgment or notification, not on the political controversy surrounding it. A typical question gives a two-paragraph news summary and asks which constitutional article, statutory provision, or judicial remedy is engaged.

#### Mapping Cases to Subject Heads

Candidates should categorise every reported item into one of ten heads: criminal, constitutional, commercial, environmental, family, labour, tax, cyber, IPR, or ADR. This habit makes revision faster because the same head keeps repeating its core statutes.

#### The Three New Criminal Codes

| Old statute | New code (effective 1 July 2024) | Example mapping |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Indian Penal Code, 1860 | Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS) | Murder: IPC 302 → BNS 302 |
| Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 | Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS) | FIR: CrPC 154 → BNSS 154 |
| Indian Evidence Act, 1872 | Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA) | Documentary evidence: IEA 61 → BSA 57 |

Questions in the 2025 and 2026 CLAT cycles have already tested whether students know which code governs a fact pattern presented in the passage.

#### Common Exam Traps

- Treating a **Law Commission Report** as enacted law.
- Confusing an **ordinance under Article 123** with a parliamentary Act.
- Writing only the political headline instead of the legal issue and relief granted.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Sources and Reading Cadence

Reliable coverage comes from primary outlets only. Read **The Hindu** or **Indian Express** daily for front-page law reporting, and pair it with **LiveLaw** for same-day judgment notes that include citation, bench composition, and counsel. Reserve **Bar & Bench** and **Supreme Court Observer** for weekly deep-dives on constitutional bench rulings. The **Consortium of NLUs syllabus** (consortiumofnlus.ac.in) explicitly anchors the Current Affairs section to the 12 months preceding the test.

#### Distinguishing Ratio from Obiter

A frequent error is treating an **obiter dictum** — a judge's passing observation — as the binding rule. Only the **ratio decidendi**, the reason for the decision on the material facts, binds lower courts under the doctrine of stare decisis. When summarising a judgment for revision, isolate the operative paragraph first.

#### Constitutional Bench Reference Points

1. **Article 32** — direct petition to the Supreme Court for fundamental rights.
2. **Article 226** — wider High Court jurisdiction covering fundamental and "other" rights.
3. **Article 142** — complete justice power used sparingly, e.g. *Supreme Court Bar Association v. Union of India* (1998) reaffirmed its limits.
4. **Article 370 abrogation** (2019) remains a heavily tested constitutional event.
5. **PIL jurisdiction** under Article 32 read with the *S.P. Gupta* (1981) and subsequent benches.

#### Practice Prompts

1. Draft a 100-word note on the 2024 batch of criminal code notifications, naming two section mappings and one transitional provision.
2. Take any recent Supreme Court judgment reported on LiveLaw and write the ratio in one sentence, the obiter in another, and the relief granted.

**Exam strategy:** Legal News items sit in the Current Affairs passage cluster — budget 90 seconds per question after the first read, and always read the *relief* paragraph of any reported judgment first.
