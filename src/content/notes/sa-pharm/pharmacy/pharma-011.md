# Infectious Disease and Antimicrobial Therapy

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Infectious disease and antimicrobial therapy** integrates the microbiology of a presenting infection with the pharmacological principles used to select, dose, and monitor an antimicrobial. For SAPC registration scenarios, the pharmacist must match the likely pathogen to the **spectrum of activity**, apply the correct **PK/PD target**, and adjust for renal or hepatic impairment. South African practice is anchored in the **South African Antimicrobial Stewardship Programme (SAASP)** and the national **One Health** AMR containment framework.

| Term | Definition | Unit / Note |
|---|---|---|
| MIC | Minimum inhibitory concentration | mg/L (= µg/mL) |
| MBC | Minimum bactericidal concentration | mg/L |
| Cmax/MIC | Concentration-dependent target | ≥8–10 (e.g. aminoglycosides) |
| %T>MIC | Time-dependent target | 40–100% of dosing interval |
| AUC₂₄ₕ/MIC | Exposure target | ≥125 (vancomycin, MRSA) |

- **Bacteriostatic** agents stop growth (e.g. tetracyclines, linezolid); **bactericidal** agents kill (e.g. β-lactams, aminoglycosides, fluoroquinolones).
- **Empirical** therapy is started before culture results; **directed (definitive)** therapy follows identification and sensitivity testing.
- Renal dose adjustment is mandatory for vancomycin, aminoglycosides, and most cephalosporins when **CrCl <50 mL/min**.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"Time B-Lock, Concentration Knock"** — **β-Lactams** need **%T>MIC** (time on lock), while aminoglycosides and fluoroquinolones need **Cmax/MIC** (knock the peak high).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Mechanisms and PK/PD Principles

Antimicrobial efficacy depends on three pharmacodynamic indices, each linked to a killing pattern:

- **Time-dependent killing (β-lactams, vancomycin):** efficacy tracks the duration plasma concentration remains above the MIC. Target **40–50% T>MIC** for penicillins, **≥50–70%** for cephalosporins, **≥80–100%** for carbapenems.
- **Concentration-dependent killing (aminoglycosides, fluoroquinolones):** efficacy tracks the peak. Target **Cmax/MIC ≥8–10**.
- **Exposure-dependent killing (vancomycin, linezolid, fluoroquinolones):** efficacy tracks total daily exposure. Target **AUC₂₄ₕ/MIC ≥125** for vancomycin in MRSA and **≥30–40** for fluoroquinolones against *S. pneumoniae*.

Individualised dosing is calculated from:

$$\text{Dose (mg)} = \frac{C_{target} \text{ (mg/L)} \times CL \text{ (L/h)} \times \tau \text{ (h)}}{F \text{ (0–1)}}$$

where *C*ₜₐᵣ𝓰ₑₜ is the desired plasma concentration, *CL* is patient clearance, *τ* is the dosing interval, and *F* is the fraction absorbed.

#### Comparison of Commonly Confused Concepts

| Concept | Bacteriostatic | Bactericidal |
|---|---|---|
| Killing action | Inhibits growth, host clears organism | Kills organism directly |
| Typical classes | Tetracyclines, macrolides, linezolid, chloramphenicol | β-Lactams, aminoglycosides, fluoroquinolones, daptomycin, metronidazole |
| PK/PD link | Often %T>MIC (linezolid) or AUC/MIC | Concentration- or time-dependent |
| Preferred setting | Mild infection, immunocompetent host | Endocarditis, meningitis, neutropenia, severe sepsis |

| Mechanism of Resistance | Example | Counter-strategy |
|---|---|---|
| Enzymatic inactivation | ESBL, carbapenemase (NDM, KPC) | Carbapenem-sparing, novel BLBLI |
| Target modification | MRSA PBP2a, vanA/VanB VRE | Vancomycin, linezolid, daptomycin |
| Efflux pump | MexAB in *P. aeruginosa* | Avoid fluoroquinolone monotherapy |
| Reduced permeability | OmpK36 mutation | Switch class |

#### Stewardship and South African Context

The **SAASP** framework underpins rational prescribing: **right drug, right dose, right route, right duration**. The pharmacist should **de-escalate** within 48–72 h once culture data return, perform an **IV-to-oral switch** when the patient is afebrile, haemodynamically stable, and tolerating oral intake, and document the indication and planned stop date at the point of prescribing.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** A 58-year-old man (80 kg, CrCl 35 mL/min) with culture-confirmed MRSA bacteraemia is on vancomycin 1 g IV 12-hourly. The hospital protocol targets AUC₂₄ₕ/MIC ≥400 mg·h/L for serious MRSA infection, and his measured AUC₂₄ₕ is 350 mg·h/L with an MIC of 1 mg/L. What is the correct pharmacist action?

#### Solution:
1. Calculate the **AUC₂₄ₕ/MIC ratio**: 350 ÷ 1 = **350 mg·h/L** — below the target of 400.
2. Recognise the patient also has **renal impairment** (CrCl 35 mL/min), requiring interval extension or dose reduction rather than dose escalation.
4. Stepwise: increase dose modestly to 1.25 g 12-hourly **OR** extend the interval to 24-hourly while monitoring trough (target 15–20 mg/L for severe MRSA) and repeat AUC.
5. The correct pharmacist action is to **recommend dose individualisation guided by AUC, with renal-adjusted interval and therapeutic drug monitoring**, NOT simply to double the dose.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students double the vancomycin dose based on the trough alone. With CrCl 35 mL/min this risks **nephrotoxicity and ototoxicity**. AUC-guided dosing with interval adjustment is the SAPC-accepted answer.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Boundary Conditions

- **Obesity (BMI ≥30):** aminoglycosides and vancomycin require **adjusted body weight (ABW)** dosing because the volume of distribution rises with adipose tissue. Loading doses for vancomycin in obese patients often reach 25–30 mg/kg total body weight.
- **Hepatic impairment:** flucloxacillin, macrolides (erythromycin, clarithromycin), and chloramphenicol accumulate; β-lactams are largely safe.
- **TB/HIV co-treatment:** rifampicin induces CYP3A4 and P-gp, reducing exposure to **protease inhibitors, azoles, and macrolides** — dose adjustments or alternative agents are mandatory.
- **Pregnancy:** avoid fluoroquinolones (cartilage toxicity) and tetracyclines (tooth discoloration); β-lactams and erythromycin (except estolate) are preferred.
- **Antimicrobial-associated adverse effects:** linezolid → serotonin syndrome with SSRIs; fluoroquinolones → QT prolongation with amiodarone; aminoglycosides → nephro/ototoxicity potentiated by loop diuretics.

#### Common Clinical Traps

1. Continuing **empirical carbapenem** beyond 48–72 h without de-escalation once ESBL-negative culture returns.
2. Using **gentamicin once daily** in pregnancy, endocarditis, or with CrCl <20 mL/min where multiple-daily dosing or avoidance is required.
3. Failing to **report notifiable medical conditions** (TB, measles, cholera, meningococcal meningitis) to the NICD via the NHLS pathway.
4. Overlooking **post-antibiotic effect (PAE)** when scheduling doses of aminoglycosides (prolonged suppression of regrowth justifies 24-hourly dosing).
5. Prescribing **cotrimoxazole prophylaxis** without checking CD4 count criteria in HIV (start when CD4 <200 cells/µL or WHO stage IV).
