# VII. Microorganisms and Humans (Elective) — HKDSE Biology Notes

This topic covers Elective Topic VII of the HKDSE Biology Elective Part. Candidates sit any two of the four electives on Paper 2. The topic addresses microbiology (bacteria, viruses, fungi, growth and culture); use of microorganisms (in food production, biotechnology, sewage treatment); microbial genetics (recombination, plasmids, gene transfer); and harmful effects of microorganisms (pathogenic mechanisms, antibiotic resistance).

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Microbiology**: bacteria (single-celled prokaryotes, peptidoglycan cell wall, 70S ribosomes); viruses (non-cellular particles, need host cells); fungi (eukaryotes, chitin cell wall); growth (binary fission, lag/log/stationary/decline curve); culture (aseptic technique, nutrient media, agar plates, broth culture).
- **Uses of microorganisms**: food production (yoghurt, cheese, bread, soy sauce, wine, beer); biotechnology (insulin production, monoclonal antibodies, recombinant vaccines); sewage treatment (breakdown of organic matter); bioremediation (clean-up of oil spills).
- **Microbial genetics**: genetic variation through mutation and gene transfer; horizontal gene transfer (transformation, transduction, conjugation); plasmids (small circular DNA, often carry antibiotic resistance genes); gene transfer to other bacteria (e.g. antibiotic resistance spread).
- **Harmful effects**: pathogenic bacteria (tuberculosis, cholera, anthrax); pathogenic viruses (influenza, HIV); pathogenic fungi (candidiasis); antibiotic resistance (a growing global health threat); nosocomial (hospital-acquired) infections.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing bacteria with viruses — bacteria are cells; viruses are not.
- Forgetting that antibiotics target bacteria, not viruses.
- Treating "transformation" as a generic term — in microbiology, transformation specifically means the uptake of foreign DNA by a bacterium.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Microbiology

**Bacteria**: 0.5-5 μm, single-celled prokaryotes. Cell wall contains peptidoglycan. DNA is circular, located in the nucleoid. Some have flagella for movement. Reproduction is by binary fission (DNA replication, cell elongation, septum formation, daughter cells separate). Generation time can be as short as 20 minutes under ideal conditions.

**Viruses**: 20-300 nm, non-cellular particles consisting of a nucleic acid core (DNA or RNA) surrounded by a protein capsid; some have an envelope (e.g. HIV, influenza). Viruses cannot reproduce on their own — they must infect a host cell. Bacteriophages infect bacteria; plant viruses infect plants; animal viruses infect animals.

**Fungi**: single-celled (yeasts) or multicellular (moulds, mushrooms) eukaryotes. Cell wall contains chitin. Yeasts reproduce by budding; moulds reproduce by spore formation.

**Growth curve**: lag phase (adaptation), log phase (exponential growth), stationary phase (nutrient depletion, waste accumulation), decline phase (death exceeds reproduction).

**Culture**:

- **Aseptic technique**: sterilise equipment and work area; flame loops; minimise exposure to air.
- **Nutrient media**: agar plates (solid), broth (liquid). Selective media inhibit growth of some species; differential media distinguish species.
- **Inoculation**: streaking, spreading, pipetting.
- **Incubation**: at appropriate temperature and atmosphere (aerobic, anaerobic, CO₂).

#### Uses of microorganisms

**Food production**:

- **Yoghurt**: lactic acid bacteria (Lactobacillus, Streptococcus) ferment lactose, producing lactic acid that coagulates milk proteins.
- **Cheese**: rennet (from calf stomach or microbial) coagulates milk; specific bacterial and fungal cultures develop flavour.
- **Bread**: yeast (Saccharomyces cerevisiae) ferments sugar, producing CO₂ that leavens the dough; alcohol evaporates during baking.
- **Soy sauce**: Aspergillus moulds and yeasts ferment soybeans and wheat; produces salt, amino acids, organic acids.
- **Wine**: yeast ferments grape juice, producing alcohol and CO₂.
- **Beer**: yeast ferments malted barley, producing alcohol and CO₂.

**Biotechnology**:

- **Insulin production**: human insulin gene inserted into E. coli plasmid; bacteria produce insulin, which is harvested and purified.
- **Monoclonal antibodies**: hybridoma cells (B cell fused with myeloma cell) produce specific antibodies; used in diagnosis and treatment.
- **Recombinant vaccines**: hepatitis B vaccine produced in yeast; HPV vaccine produced in yeast or insect cells.
- **Sewage treatment**: aerobic bacteria break down organic matter in activated sludge; anaerobic bacteria produce biogas (methane) in anaerobic digesters.

**Bioremediation**: bacteria and fungi degrade pollutants. Examples: oil-degrading bacteria in oil spill clean-up, mycorrhizal fungi in soil restoration.

#### Microbial genetics

**Mutation**: spontaneous or induced changes in DNA sequence. Mutations may be beneficial, neutral, or harmful.

**Horizontal gene transfer**:

- **Transformation**: uptake of free DNA from the environment by a bacterium.
- **Transduction**: transfer of bacterial DNA by a bacteriophage.
- **Conjugation**: direct transfer of DNA between bacteria via a pilus; often involves plasmid transfer.

**Plasmids**: small, circular, double-stranded DNA molecules separate from the chromosome. Plasmids often carry genes for antibiotic resistance, virulence factors, or metabolic functions. Plasmids are the basis of many genetic engineering tools (cloning vectors).

#### Harmful effects of microorganisms

**Pathogenic bacteria**:

- **Mycobacterium tuberculosis**: tuberculosis.
- **Vibrio cholerae**: cholera.
- **Bacillus anthracis**: anthrax.
- **Streptococcus pyogenes**: strep throat, scarlet fever.
- **Escherichia coli O157:H7**: food poisoning, haemolytic uremic syndrome.

**Pathogenic viruses**:

- **Influenza virus**: influenza.
- **Human immunodeficiency virus (HIV)**: AIDS.
- **Hepatitis B and C viruses**: hepatitis, liver cancer.
- **SARS-CoV-2**: COVID-19.

**Antibiotic resistance**:

- A growing global health threat. Bacteria evolve resistance through mutation and horizontal gene transfer.
- Misuse of antibiotics (overuse, incomplete courses, agricultural use) accelerates the development of resistance.
- Examples: MRSA (methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus), VRE (vancomycin-resistant Enterococcus), XDR-TB (extensively drug-resistant tuberculosis).

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-topic links

- **II. Genetics and Evolution**: microbial genetics is applied molecular genetics.
- **IV. Health and Diseases**: pathogenic microorganisms cause communicable diseases.
- **VIII. Biotechnology (Elective)**: recombinant DNA technology uses the same tools as microbial genetics.

#### Exam technique

- **For microbiology questions**: identify the type of microorganism (bacteria, virus, fungus), the method of reproduction, and the conditions for growth.
- **For biotechnology questions**: trace the process from gene to product; cite specific applications.
- **For antibiotic resistance questions**: explain the mechanism (mutation, horizontal gene transfer, selection pressure) and cite specific resistant strains.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Antibiotics kill viruses." No — antibiotics work against bacteria. Antivirals target viruses.
- "All bacteria are pathogens." Many bacteria are beneficial (gut flora, nitrogen fixation, biotechnology).
- "Vaccines cause autism." No — large-scale epidemiological studies have consistently shown no link between vaccines and autism.
