# Ledger Posting

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Ledger Posting** is the act of transferring each debit and credit entry from the **Journal** (or subsidiary books) into the matching **Ledger Account**, so every account can later be balanced and a **Trial Balance** prepared. Under the **Double Entry System**, every transaction touches two accounts with equal totals, keeping the equation `Assets = Liabilities + Capital` intact.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Ledger** | A book of accounts containing all ledger accounts in classified form |
| **J.F. (Journal Folio)** | Journal page number written in the ledger entry |
| **L.F. (Ledger Folio)** | Ledger page number written in the journal entry |
| **Balancing c/d** | "Carried down" balance that equalises both sides of an account |
| **Balancing b/d** | "Brought down" opening balance of the next period |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"To = Debit side, By = Credit side"** — read "To Cash A/c" as Cash is the source being credited, and "By Cash A/c" as Cash is the receiver being debited. Think **T**owards the account being given = debited, **B**y the account being received = credited.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Purpose and Place in the Accounting Cycle

Ledger Posting is the second stage of the accounting cycle, sitting between **Journalising** (Stage 1) and **Trial Balance preparation** (Stage 3). Once transactions are journalised with proper **Narration** and **Compound/Contra Entry** treatment, each amount is moved into the affected ledger account. The ledger then becomes the source from which the **Trading Account**, **Profit & Loss Account**, and **Balance Sheet** are eventually built.

#### Procedural Steps of Posting

1. Identify the **two accounts** affected (debit and credit).
2. Locate each account in the **Ledger** (open a new page if absent).
3. Write the **date** of the transaction in the date column.
4. On the **debit side**, write `To <Credit Account Name>` with the amount; on the **credit side**, write `By <Debit Account Name>`.
5. Enter the **Journal Folio (J.F.)** in the ledger page and the **Ledger Folio (L.F.)** in the journal entry.
6. At period-end, total both sides and insert **Balance c/d** on the smaller side to equalise.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix — Easy Confusions

| Confusion Point | What Students Think | What the Rule Actually Says |
|---|---|---|
| Cash received from debtor | Credit Cash A/c | **Debit Cash A/c, Credit Debtor** — cash comes in |
| Purchase of furniture by cheque | Debit Cash A/c, Credit Furniture A/c | **Debit Furniture A/c, Credit Bank A/c** — asset increases |
| Contra entry | Two separate journal entries | A **single compound entry** where bank and cash are swapped |
| Opening balance of debtor | Always on debit side | On debit side **only if debit balance**; credit balance means the customer is owed back |
| Balance c/d vs Balance b/d | Both mean closing | **c/d** = closing of current period, **b/d** = opening of next period |

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** The following ledger balances of M/s Kohli & Sons on 31-03-2024 are: Cash ₹8,000 (Dr.), Bank ₹22,000 (Dr.), Debtors ₹35,000 (Dr.), Creditors ₹18,000 (Cr.), Stock ₹25,000 (Dr.), Machinery ₹60,000 (Dr.), Capital ₹1,32,000 (Cr.). Prepare the **Trial Balance** and state whether it agrees.

#### Solution:

Step 1 — Group the debit balances:
- Cash ₹8,000 + Bank ₹22,000 + Debtors ₹35,000 + Stock ₹25,000 + Machinery ₹60,000 = **₹1,50,000**

Step 2 — Group the credit balances:
- Creditors ₹18,000 + Capital ₹1,32,000 = **₹1,50,000**

Step 3 — Apply the trial balance equation:
- `Σ Debit balances = Σ Credit balances = ₹1,50,000` ✓

| Heads of Accounts | L.F. | Debit (₹) | Credit (₹) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Cash A/c | 1 | 8,000 | — |
| Bank A/c | 2 | 22,000 | — |
| Debtors A/c | 3 | 35,000 | — |
| Stock A/c | 4 | 25,000 | — |
| Machinery A/c | 5 | 60,000 | — |
| Creditors A/c | 6 | — | 18,000 |
| Capital A/c | 7 | — | 1,32,000 |
| **Total** | | **1,50,000** | **1,50,000** |

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Many candidates place **Creditors** on the debit side or treat **Capital** as a debit balance because both figures are large. Remember: creditors represent amounts **owed by** the business (credit balance), and capital is the owner's claim (credit balance). Reverse the sides and your trial balance will never tally.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Balancing Mechanics with Opening Balances

For an account with a **debit opening balance**, the closing formula is:

> **Closing Balance = Total of Debit side + Opening Balance − Total of Credit side**

For an account with a **credit opening balance**:

> **Closing Balance = Total of Credit side + Opening Balance − Total of Debit side**

The balance is shown on the **smaller** side as `To Balance c/d` (if debit balance) or `By Balance c/d` (if credit balance), and the same figure reappears on the next line as `By Balance b/d` or `To Balance b/d` to open the next period.

#### Suspense Account — When the Trial Balance Disagrees

Even after careful posting, mismatches occur. The ICAI-prescribed treatment routes the **difference** into a temporary **Suspense Account** until the error is located.

| Cause of Mismatch | Where to Look | Correcting Entry |
|---|---|---|
| Wrong total on debit side | Re-add the journal/day book | Reverse and repost the correct side |
| Posting to wrong account | Compare J.F. with L.F. entries | Rectify via rectification statement or journal proper |
| Omission of one aspect of compound entry | Check compound entry splits | Post the missing aspect; difference goes to Suspense A/c |
| Excess credit posting | Match ledger with journal vouchers | Cancel excess and debit Suspense A/c |
| Opening balance entered on wrong side | Check all opening balances | Reverse opening balance; clear Suspense A/c |

> 📌 **Formula Check:** When trial balance totals differ, the **Suspense Account balance = Σ Debit − Σ Credit** (if debit-heavy) or vice versa. This temporary account must be **nil** before final accounts are prepared.

- Paper 1 (Financial Accounting) carries **3% weightage** from this chapter. Two question formats dominate: **MCQs (2 marks each)** on identifying the correct debit/credit side, and **short numericals (4–8 marks)** on balancing accounts with opening balances and suspense treatment.

- Time allocation: spend **6–8 minutes** per balancing problem; reserve the last 10 minutes to **cross-check folio numbers and totals** before submission.
- Use the **"T-account dry run"** method on scratch paper — draw each account, mark the posting direction with an arrow, then total — this prevents the most common direction-reversal error.

#### Common Mistakes Catalogue

1. Writing `To` and `By` interchanged, especially in compound entries involving cash and bank.
2. Treating **all personal accounts as debit balances** — creditors and bank overdrafts carry credit balances.
3. Forgetting to write the **narration in the ledger**, which the ICAI study material recommends for clarity.
4. Posting a **compound entry's** debit and credit to the same account twice, leaving one aspect unposted.
5. Calculating closing balance without including the **opening balance**, producing a Trial Balance off by exactly that figure.
