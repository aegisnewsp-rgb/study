# Short-Answer Questions — IELTS Reading Notes

Short-answer questions test the ability to locate specific information in the passage and write a short, accurate answer. The questions follow the order of the passage. The word limit is strict.

> Verify the live Reading format on https://ielts.org/ before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Format:** open-ended questions answered in 1-3 words or a number.
- **Word limit:** strict — "NO MORE THAN THREE WORDS" is common.
- **Order of answers:** questions follow the passage in order.
- **Source:** answers come from the passage.
- **Common distractor:** the question may ask for a paraphrase of the passage.

#### Examiner traps

- Writing more words than the limit.
- Paraphrasing the answer instead of using the passage's exact words.
- Missing the answer because it comes after a long sentence.

---

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Strategy

1. Read the question carefully — identify the keyword.
2. Scan the passage for the keyword.
3. Read the surrounding sentence carefully.
4. Write the exact words from the passage (within the word limit).

---

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where short-answer questions appear across IELTS Reading

Short-answer questions appear in IELTS Academic Reading, usually in one of the three passages with 3-5 questions. They are less common than multiple choice or matching.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Paraphrasing is fine." No — write the exact words from the passage (within the word limit).
- "Numbers can be written as words." Sometimes — follow the question's format.
- "Skim the passage." No — short-answer questions require careful reading.

#### Exam technique

- For each question, locate the keyword in the passage first.
- Read the surrounding sentence carefully to extract the answer.
- Write the exact words from the passage, within the word limit.

#### Tactical resolution framework for Sentence Completion and Short Answer

Sentence Completion and Short Answer questions assess literal information retrieval under tight word-limit constraints:

- **Locate Heading Anchors**: Look for non-paraphrasable anchor words in the question stem: capitalized proper nouns, dates, scientific terms, or numbers.
- **Extract Exact Text Form**: Never modify the grammatical form of words lifted from the passage. If the text says *inadvertently*, do not write *inadvertent*.
- **Verify Spelling**: IELTS penalizes incorrect spelling; copying words accurately from the printed reading text directly onto your answer sheet prevents preventable score losses.
- **Keep Word Limits in Focus**: If the limit is *ONE WORD ONLY*, write exactly one word. Hyphenated words (e.g., *state-of-the-art*) count as a single word in official IELTS scoring.

#### 15-minute triage drill

- [ ] Can you locate specific dates or capitalized names in under 20 seconds using diagonal zig-zag scanning?
- [ ] Have you verified that your completed sentence makes syntactic sense when read as a complete English sentence?
- [ ] Are all spelling variants aligned with standard British or American conventions?
