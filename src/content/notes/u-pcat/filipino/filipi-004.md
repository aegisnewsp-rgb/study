# Topic 4

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Filipino 4** centres on **panitikan at retorika** — the literary-genre system, the structural elements of prose and poetry, and the rhetorical devices used by Filipino writers. Three blocks dominate the UPCAT test:

1. **Mga Uri ng Panitikan** — three top-level divisions: **prosa** (maikling kwento, nobela, sanaysay, talumpayi), **patula** (tulang sukat, tulang di-sukat, balagtasan), at **dula** (komedya, melodrama, dulang pantelebisyon).
2. **Mga Tayutay** — figurative-language identifiers: **pagtulad** (simile, with *tulad/kay/parang*), **pagawang-tulad** (metaphor, no connector), **pagmamalabis** (hyperbole), **panghihiram** (metonymy, e.g. *Korona* for monarchy), **pahiwatig** (personification).
3. **Pananda vs. Palatandaan** — **pananda** = *paksa* at *simuno* (the topic/subject); **palatandaan** = *panag-uri, pandiwa, pang-abay* (the descriptors).

A high-yield pointer: always match the device to its exact marker word. A UPCAT item asking "Anong tayutay ang ginamit?" rewards the *most specific* label, not a vague "figure of speech."

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Mga Elemento ng Maikling Kwento
Every prose narrative in UPCAT items contains seven building blocks: **tagpuan** (setting in time and place), **tauhan** (characters, including *pangunahing tauhan*, *katalikatan*, *sagisag na tauhan*), **banghay** (plot sequence), **suliranin** (conflict — *tao vs. tao, tao vs. sarili, tao vs. lipunan, tao vs. kalikasan*), **kakintalan** (climax), **resolusyon** (resolution), at **tema** (central idea). A quick litmus test for theme: it must be expressible as a complete lesson sentence, not a single noun.

#### Mga Tayutay at Kanilang Mga Palatandaan

| Tayutay | Palatandaan sa Pangungusap |
|---|---|
| **Pagtulad** (simile) | *tulad, kay, parang, gaya, higit sa* |
| **Pagawang-tulad** (metaphor) | direct equation, no connector |
| **Pagmamalabis** (hyperbole) | exaggerated degree marker (*sobrang, totoo ng*) |
| **Pagtatangi** (irony) | meaning opposite to stated words |
| **Panghihiram** (metonymy) | associated object stands for whole (*Korte* for judiciary) |
| **Pahiwatig** (personification) | inanimate noun + human verb |
| **Antastrofi** (inversion) | non-standard word order for emphasis |
| **Paralelismo** | repeated grammatical structure across clauses |

#### Mga Antas ng Wika
Six registers appear in UPCAT items: **balbal** (coarse slang, *erpat* = father), **kolokyal** (casual speech, *di pwede*), **pormal** (academic), **teknikal** (field-specific), **lingguwistika** (linguistic metalanguage), at **panitikan** (literary). Items usually give a one-line utterance and ask you to classify its register.

#### Pananda at Palatandaan sa Gramatika
**Pananda** names what is talked about — *paksa* (topic phrase) and *simuno* (subject of the sentence). **Palatandaan** describes or limits the pananda — *panag-uri* (adjective), *pandiwa* (verb), *pang-abay* (adverb), plus the function markers *pang-angkop*, *pang-ukol*, *pangsiwang pangkayarian*. Exam trap: students call the *panag-uri* a "pananda" because it sits near the noun — but grammatically it is the palatandaan.

#### Mapanuring Pagbasa
Critical-reading items ask you to identify **layunin** (purpose), **pananaw** (point of view), **audience**, **tono** (formal/satirical/pasyon), and **konteksto** (historical/social). Always anchor each answer to a textual detail — never choose an option unsupported by the passage.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases sa Tayutay
Mixed figures of speech (halong tayutay) appear in nearly every UPCAT cycle. A single saknong may contain *pagtulad*, *pahiwatig*, *at paralelismo* simultaneously. Strategy: scan line by line, mark each marker word, then list every device in the answer — partial credit is rare, so omission of one device loses the point. Also watch for **irony vs. sarcasm** — UPCAT uses *pagtatangi* strictly for situational or verbal irony, never for humorous asides.

#### Connections to Adjacent Topics
Filipino 4 overlaps with **Filipino 3** (grammar mechanics) on pananda/palatandaan, and with **pagsusuri ng akda** (literary criticism) on theme vs. message. **Tema** is the implicit lesson; **mensaheng pampanitikan** is the explicit moral the author states — UPCAT items deliberately blur the two, so verify whether the passage *shows* the lesson or *declares* it.

#### Common Mistakes
1. Treating *panitikan* as "anything written." True *panitikan* has **artistiko at hindi karaniwang layunin** — a grocery list is not literature.
2. Confusing *resolusyon* (denouement of a story) with *wakas* (terminal scene of a *dula*).
3. Calling any comparison "pagtulad" when no marker word appears — that is *pagawang-tulad*.
4. Misreading *panghihiram* as *pagtutulad*; the test for metonymy is *causal/associative proximity*, not visual resemblance.

#### Practice Prompts
1. Sa saknong na "Ang aking puso'y isang ibong lumilipad sa kawalan," ilahad ang dalawang tayutay na ginamit at bigyang-katwiran ang bawat isa sa pamamagitan ng pagtukoy sa eksaktong salita o parirala bilang batayan.
2. Sa isang maikling kwento kung saan ang tagpuan ay probinsya ng Laguna noong 1942, ang pangunahing tauhan ay isang guro na lihim na nag-aakda ng mga polyeto, at ang kakintalan ay ang kanyang pagdakip — tukuyin ang suliranin ayon sa apat na uri (*tao vs. tao, tao vs. sarili, tao vs. lipunan, tao vs. kalikasan*) at ipaliwanag kung bakit iisa lamang ang pinakapangunahing uri.
