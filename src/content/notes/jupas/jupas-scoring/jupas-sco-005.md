# Expected Score, Median and Range — JUPAS Notes

This topic covers the expected score, median admit score, and score range published by JUPAS participating institutions. These are reference numbers from the previous cycle; actual cutoffs vary by cycle. Most programmes publish an expected-score range and a median admit score. Treat published scores as guidance, not guarantees.

> Verify the live expected-score data on https://www.jupas.edu.hk/ and each university's admissions page before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Expected-score range**: published by most programmes, indicating the typical score range of candidates admitted in the previous cycle.
- **Median admit score**: published by some programmes; the median score of admitted candidates in the previous cycle.
- **Source**: JUPAS consolidates the published data in the 2025 JUPAS Admissions Scores report.
- **Use**: guidance for candidates; not a guarantee of admission.
- **Caveat**: actual cutoffs vary by cycle based on the applicant pool and the number of places.

#### Examiner traps

- Treating the published expected-score range as a cutoff — it is a guide.
- Forgetting that expected-score data is from the previous cycle, not the current one.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### What the expected-score data means

Each JUPAS participating institution publishes, for each programme:

- **Expected-score range**: a range of scores (e.g. 28-32) typically associated with offers in the previous cycle. Candidates with scores in this range have a reasonable chance of admission.
- **Median admit score**: the median score of candidates who received offers. Candidates above the median have a strong chance; candidates below have a lower chance.
- **Lower-quartile and upper-quartile scores**: for some programmes, the 25th and 75th percentile scores are published.

#### How to use the expected-score data

1. **Identify the published data for each programme you apply to**: from the JUPAS report or the programme's admissions page.
2. **Compare your expected score**: based on your HKDSE results and the programme's scoring formula, calculate your admission score.
3. **Estimate your probability**: if your score is in the upper-quartile range, your probability of admission is high. If your score is below the lower-quartile, your probability is low.
4. **Use the data as guidance, not a guarantee**: actual cutoffs vary by cycle.

#### Limitations of the expected-score data

- **Historical data**: the data is from the previous cycle, not the current one. The applicant pool and the number of places change each cycle.
- **Score variability**: actual scores depend on the specific applicants in the cycle. A strong applicant pool raises the cutoff; a weak applicant pool lowers it.
- **Subject weighting changes**: the scoring formula may change between cycles. Always check the current formula.
- **Concurrent considerations**: interviews, aptitude tests, school principal's nominations can affect admission even for candidates below the expected-score range.

#### Exam technique

- **For "estimate your probability of admission" questions**: identify the expected-score range; compare your score; state the probability (high/medium/low).
- **For "explain the limitations of the data" questions**: cite historical data, variability, and concurrent considerations.

#### Common misconceptions

- "Expected-score data is a cutoff." No — it is guidance from the previous cycle.
- "A score above the upper-quartile guarantees admission." No — the cycle may be more competitive, or concurrent considerations may apply.
- "A score below the lower-quartile guarantees rejection." No — concurrent considerations may apply.
