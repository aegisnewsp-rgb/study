# Endocrine Physiology: Hypothalamic-Pituitary Axis

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

- **Anterior pituitary (adenohypophysis)** secretes six tropic hormones — GH, PRL, ACTH, TSH, FSH, LH — under hypothalamic control delivered through the **hypophyseal portal system** from the median eminence.
- **Posterior pituitary (neurohypophysis)** stores and releases **ADH (supraoptic nucleus)** and **oxytocin (paraventricular nucleus)**; synthesis occurs in hypothalamic cell bodies, not in the posterior lobe.
- **Prolactin** sits under tonic **dopaminergic inhibition**; stalk section raises PRL but drops every other anterior lobe hormone — a classic stalk-effect question.
- **TRH** stimulates both TSH and PRL, which is why primary hypothyroidism can present with galactorrhea.
- Free hormone fraction drives biological activity: Effective dose ≈ Free fraction × Total hormone (mol/L or IU/L).
- **Long-loop feedback** — cortisol, T3/T4, sex steroids inhibit hypothalamic CRH/TRH/GnRH and the matching pituitary tropic hormone; **short-loop** — pituitary hormones feed back on the hypothalamus.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Two-Lobe Architecture

The pituitary divides into **adenohypophysis** and **neurohypophysis**, but the regulatory logic differs sharply. The anterior lobe has no direct nerve supply from the hypothalamus; control is **humoral** via the hypophyseal portal system — a primary capillary plexus at the median eminence drains into hypophyseal portal veins, which feed a secondary plexus bathing anterior pituitary cells.

The posterior lobe is neural — axons of **supraoptic** and **paraventricular** neurons descend through the infundibulum and release **ADH** and **oxytocin** directly into systemic circulation.

#### Hypothalamic Releasing and Inhibiting Factors

| Factor | Target cell | Hormone controlled |
|---|---|---|
| GHRH | Somatotroph | GH ↑ |
| Somatostatin (GHIH) | Somatotroph | GH ↓ |
| TRH | Thyrotroph / Lactotroph | TSH ↑, PRL ↑ |
| CRH | Corticotroph | ACTH ↑ |
| GnRH (pulsatile) | Gonadotroph | FSH, LH ↑ |
| Dopamine (PIF) | Lactotroph | PRL ↓ |

#### Long-Loop and Short-Loop Feedback

**Long-loop:** peripheral gland hormones — cortisol, T3/T4, estradiol, testosterone — inhibit both hypothalamic releasing factor secretion and the corresponding pituitary tropic hormone.

**Short-loop:** pituitary tropic hormones (ACTH, TSH) retro-inhibit hypothalamic releasing factor secretion. Feedback gain (Δset-point error / Δperturbation, dimensionless) is the standard exam way of quantifying how tightly a loop clamps a variable.

#### Prolactin — The Unique Case

Dopamine tonically inhibits PRL; loss of dopaminergic tone (stalk section, dopamine antagonists like metoclopramide) elevates PRL while suppressing GH, TSH, ACTH, FSH, LH — the **stalk effect**. Treatment reverses with dopamine agonists **bromocriptine** or **cabergoline**.

#### Exam Patterns

INI CET typically frames this as clinical vignettes: diabetes insipidus vs SIADH (ADH axis), Sheehan syndrome (postpartum anterior pituitary infarction), prolactinoma (galactorrhea, amenorrhea), or primary hypothyroidism with hyperprolactinemia. One to two questions per paper, often MCQs on hypothalamic factor names or feedback diagrams.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Quantitative Pharmacology of the Axis

Peptide hormones here follow a sigmoid log-dose response:

> Response = R_min + (R_max − R_min) / (1 + (ED50 / [Hormone])^n)

where **ED50** is the half-maximal effective concentration (mol/L) and **n** is the Hill coefficient (dimensionless). ACTH–cortisol coupling carries a **~100× amplification factor** at the adrenal cortex: each mole of ACTH releases ~100 moles of cortisol — the basis of why tiny pituitary perturbations cause large clinical syndromes.

Peptide half-life is short: **t½ ≈ 0.693 × Vd / CL**, where Vd (L) and clearance CL (L/min) yield t½ in minutes. This is why GH must be sampled as **IGF-1** (somatomedin C) — a single random GH level is uninterpretable because secretion is pulsatile with stage III/IV sleep surges.

#### Adjacent Connections

- **Stress axis:** CRH → ACTH → cortisol integrates with locus coeruleus noradrenergic drive; chronic stress enlarges adrenal cortex.
- **Reproductive axis:** pulsatile GnRH (every 60–120 min) is mandatory for gonadotroph response; continuous GnRH downregulates receptors (the basis of GnRH-agonist therapy in prostate cancer and endometriosis).
- **GH–IGF-1 axis:** GHRH and ghrelin stimulate somatotrophs; somatostatin inhibits them; IGF-1 produced by the liver closes a long feedback loop and mediates most peripheral growth effects.

#### Common Traps in INI CET MCQs

- Stating that hypothalamic neurons directly innervate the **anterior** pituitary — wrong; only the posterior lobe receives neural innervation.
- Forgetting that **TRH** raises PRL — leads to missed diagnoses of hypothyroidism-related galactorrhea.
- Calling oxytocin the "milk-ejection inhibitor" — it is the **ejection trigger**; prolactin governs milk synthesis.
- Treating "tropic" and "target" as interchangeable — tropic hormones act on other endocrine glands.

#### Practice Prompts

1. A 28-year-old on metoclopramide develops galactorrhea. Predict FSH, LH, and TSH, and explain mechanistically how dopamine antagonism alters each axis.
2. A postpartum woman with hemorrhage fails to lactate and has amenorrhea. Map her likely hormonal deficits onto long-loop feedback failure for cortisol, T4, and estradiol.
