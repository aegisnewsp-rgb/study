# Logical Reasoning

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Logical Reasoning in the CUET UG General Test measures pattern analysis, inference, and valid deduction from given information, without any specialised subject knowledge. The section draws 8–12 questions from three streams: verbal reasoning (syllogisms, blood relations, coding-decoding), non-verbal reasoning (series, mirror/water images, embedded figures), and analytical reasoning (seating, directions, Venn diagrams).

- **Syllogism** — evaluate with Venn diagrams; "Some A are B" never reverses to "All".
- **Coding-Decoding** — use A=1…Z=26 position shifts, then apply uniformly.
- **Blood Relations** — convert chains into a family tree with M, F, B, S, W, H symbols.
- **Number Series** — test arithmetic, square/cube, or interleaved patterns on ALL terms.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Verbal | Syllogism, analogy | Venn diagram, semantic logic |
| Non-verbal | Mirror image, odd-one-out | Reflection rule (x,y)→(−x,y) |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Verbal Reasoning Mechanics

**Syllogism** uses four standard statements — All A are B, Some A are B, No A is B, Some A are not B — combined with conclusions tested via two-circle or three-circle Venn diagrams. Only "All" and "No" conclusions are reversible; "Some" never converts to "All", even when diagrams overlap.

**Coding-Decoding** applies letter-position arithmetic (A=1, B=2 … Z=26). A word like CAT coded as DBU uses +1 to every letter. Reverse coding subtracts, while substitution ciphers swap whole letters (e.g., ROAD → WTEH if R→W, O→T, A→E, D→H).

**Blood Relations** chains such as "A is the son of B's father's only daughter" must be drawn as a family tree first. Symbols M, F, B, S, W, H, D shorten chains: "B's father's daughter" = B's sister, so A is B's nephew.

#### Non-Verbal Pattern Rules

| Pattern Type | Rule Applied | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Number series | Arithmetic progression + interleaving | 2, 6, 3, 9, 4, 12 (two streams ×2, ×3) |
| Mirror image | (x,y) → (−x,y) | Letter "p" reflects as "q" |
| Water image | (x,y) → (x,−y) | Letter "M" reflects as "W" |
| Odd-one-out | Hidden common property | 3, 5, 7, 9 — 9 is composite |

Mirror images flip horizontally across a vertical axis; water images flip vertically across a horizontal axis. Internal lines inside a figure must also be reflected — examiners deliberately test that detail.

#### Analytical Reasoning

**Directions** problems require resetting the facing direction after every turn. If you walk 5 km North, turn right and walk 3 km, then turn right again, you face South — not East. Final position is tracked separately from the facing direction.

**Seating Arrangements** come in linear, circular, rectangular, and square layouts. The phrase "immediate left" assumes all members face the centre in circular layouts; the same phrase in a linear row assumes a fixed facing direction. Negatives such as "A is not to the right of B" prune candidates fastest.

- Draw the diagram BEFORE reading all constraints.
- Mark definite placements first, then conditional ones.
- Verify "to the left of" by physical rotation, not assumption.
- For Venn diagrams, draw circles proportional to overlap area.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Examiner Traps

**Statement–Conclusion and Statement–Assumption** questions test whether a conclusion *necessarily* follows. A conclusion is valid only if it is true in every possible interpretation; "probably" or "may" conclusions are usually rejected as assumptions, not logical conclusions.

**Cause–Effect** pairs require the cause to precede the effect in time and the effect to be a direct outcome. "It rained heavily, therefore traffic jams occurred" is weak; "Heavy rain caused flooding, therefore traffic jams occurred" is acceptable because the chain is direct.

**Paper Folding and Embedded Figures** rely on counting fold layers. After folding a square twice along diagonals, a hole punched near the corner appears at multiple symmetric points — count by halving the number of layers each fold adds.

| Trap | Wrong Move | Correct Approach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Syllogism reversal | Treating "Some A are B" as "All B are A" | Draw Venn diagram, check overlap |
| Circular seating | Assuming left/right symmetry | Fix a reference seat, then place others clockwise |
| Coding partial shift | Shifting only the coded letter | Apply rule to every letter or full word |
| Blood relation skip | Confusing nephew with cousin | Build generational tree before answering |
| Direction turns | Carrying old facing direction | Reset direction after every 90° turn |

#### Practice Prompts

1. In a circular table of 8 facing the centre, P sits second to the left of Q, R sits opposite P, and S is immediate right of R. Who sits between Q and S?
2. If MEKQ is coded as QKEM (reverse + opposite shift of +2), decode the word coded as HTVG using the same rule.

#### Exam Strategy for CUET UG

Logical Reasoning carries roughly 4% of the General Test paper, with 8–12 questions and no negative marking in most slots. Target ≤45 seconds per question by skipping pure trial-and-error sets and returning later. Practise 30 Venn-diagram syllogisms and 30 seating arrangements before the exam — these two subtypes alone account for nearly half the section historically. Use official NTA mock tests at nta.ac.in to calibrate timing, not third-party portals that drift from the live pattern.
