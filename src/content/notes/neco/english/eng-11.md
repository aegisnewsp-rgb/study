# Pronouns and Agreement

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

A **pronoun** replaces a noun (its **antecedent**) to stop repetition, and **pronoun-antecedent agreement** is the rule that the pronoun must match that antecedent in **number**, **person**, and **gender**. NECO SSCE English tests this topic across Paper II objective items and Paper III essay grading, so a single slip costs marks in both.

- **Singular antecedent → singular pronoun:** "Every boy carries *his* bag." (Not "their".)
- **Plural antecedent → plural pronoun:** "The girls raised *their* hands."
- **Indefinite pronouns are singular:** *each, every, anyone, everyone, somebody, nobody, either, neither* all take *his/her* (or *their* in modern usage).
- **Case matters:** *I/me/myself*; *he/him/himself*; *she/her/herself*; *they/them/themselves*; *who/whom/whose*; *which/that*.

#### High-yield pointers

| Pointer | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Match number strictly | Most NECO options test singular vs plural |
| Watch indefinite pronouns | Frequent in gap-fill and error-identification |
| Choose correct case | *Between you and me*, never *between you and I* |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Pronoun cases at a glance

English pronouns change form based on their grammatical job in the sentence. The **subjective case** performs the action (*I, you, he, she, it, we, they*). The **objective case** receives the action (*me, you, him, her, it, us, them*). The **possessive case** shows ownership, in two forms: possessive determiners (*my, your, his, her, our, their*) before a noun, and possessive pronouns (*mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs*) standing alone.

#### Antecedent agreement rules

A pronoun must agree with its antecedent in three ways. **Number**: singular antecedents demand singular pronouns; plural antecedents demand plural pronouns. **Person**: first-person (*I, we, my, our*), second-person (*you, your*), and third-person (*he, she, it, they*) should not shift mid-sentence without purpose. **Gender**: masculine antecedents take *he/him/his*, feminine take *she/her/her(s)*, and neuter or non-personal antecedents take *it/its*.

#### Indefinite pronoun behaviour

Indefinite pronouns — *each, every, everyone, everybody, anyone, anybody, someone, somebody, no one, nobody, either, neither, one* — are grammatically singular, so they pair with singular pronouns. Formal NECO answers in most keys require *his or her*; contemporary usage accepts singular *they/their*, but examiners can still mark against it in strict objective items.

#### Relative and reflexive pronouns

**Relative pronouns** introduce clauses and connect them to antecedents: *who* and *whom* refer to persons, *which* refers to things or animals, *that* works in restrictive clauses for both, and *whose* signals possession. **Reflexive pronouns** (*myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, yourselves, themselves*) are used when the subject and object refer to the same entity, or for emphasis.

#### Common NECO traps

| Trap | Wrong | Correct |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Subjective vs objective after preposition | *Between you and I* | *Between you and me* |
| Who vs whom in object position | *To who did you speak?* | *To whom did you speak?* |
| Collective noun as unit | *The team played their best* | *The team played its best* |
| Reflexive for emphasis as subject | *Myself will do it* | *I myself will do it* / *I will do it* |

- Keep the antecedent crystal clear — no pronoun should point to two possible nouns.
- Maintain consistent person throughout a passage unless a shift is deliberate.
- For collective nouns, decide whether the group acts as one unit (singular) or as individuals (plural).

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge cases examiners exploit

**Singular *they*** has become standard in everyday English, but NECO objective papers in many papers treat it as a distractor. When you see options like *"Everyone should bring his book"* vs *"Everyone should bring their book"*, pick the form the examiner's key prefers — in legacy keys this is in most keys *his or her*, but newer keys accept *their*. Read the stem for cue words.

**Collective nouns** shift number based on meaning. British style treats *team, committee, family, government* as singular unless members act separately; American usage favours plural when individuals are emphasised. NECO follows British conventions, so default to singular: *"The jury has reached its verdict."*

**Who vs whom** is tested through substitution. Replace the pronoun with *he/him* or *she/her*: if *him/her* fits, choose *whom*; if *he/she* fits, choose *who*. Example: *"To whom it can concern"* — substitute *to him*, which fits, so *whom* is correct.

#### Ambiguity and reference

A pronoun with no clear antecedent is grammatically faulty. Sentences like *"When James met his brother, he was surprised"* leave *he* ambiguous. Rewrite to specify the doer. NECO error-correction items frequently hide ambiguity tests behind short clauses.

#### Connection to essay writing (Paper III)

Pronoun consistency and agreement are scored under *grammatical accuracy* in NECO essays — in standard papers 4–6 marks of the total. Inconsistent shifts between *I*, *you*, and *one* within an essay weaken coherence. Maintain one dominant person (in most keys first-person for personal essays, third-person for argumentative pieces) and verify that every *their, his, her, its* agrees with its nearest logical antecedent.

#### Worked micro-example

Original: *"Each of the contestants must register their name before noon."*
Step 1: Identify antecedent — *Each* (singular indefinite pronoun).
Step 2: Apply rule — singular antecedent → singular pronoun.
Step 3: Rewrite formally — *"Each of the contestants must register his or her name before noon."*
Step 4: Modern rewrite (acceptable in many keys) — *"Each of the contestants must register their name before noon."*

#### Two practice prompts

1. Rewrite correcting errors: *"The committee announced that anybody who wishes to contest must brings their ID and themself to the hall."*
2. Fill the gap with the correct pronoun: *"Neither of the applicants remembered to attach _____ credentials."* (Options in standard papers include *his, their, one's, its*.)

| Strategy | Application |
| --- | --- |
| Substitution test for case | Replace pronoun with *he/him* to fix *who/whom* |
| Unit vs members test | Decide singular/plural for collective nouns |
| Antecedent proximity | Match pronoun to the nearest logical noun |
| Person-lock check | Scan essay paragraphs for unwanted person shifts |
