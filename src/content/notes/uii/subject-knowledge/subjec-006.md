# Geography (Soshum)

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Geography (Soshum) for UI Entrance tests pre-university physical and human geography, with emphasis on Indonesia's lithosphere, atmosphere, hydrosphere, biosphere, and anthroposphere. Expect 2–4 multiple-choice items worth roughly 3% of the total score, mixing concept recall with quick numeric calculations. The four formulas that recur are map scale, thermal gradient, geometric population projection, and population density.

- **Map scale**: Skala = jarak peta (cm) / jarak sebenarnya (km × 100.000); dimensionless ratio, e.g. 1 : 100.000.
- **Thermal lapse rate**: T = T₀ − (0,6 °C × h/100 m); temperature drops ≈ 0,6 °C every 100 m of altitude gain.
- **Geometric population growth**: Pₜ = P₀ × (1 + r)ᵗ; convert r% to decimal, e.g. r = 2% → multiplier 1,02.
- **Crude density**: D = P/A, in people per km²; dimension [L⁻²]; differs from agrarian density Da = Pa/Aa.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"SKaTa PD"** — **S**kala, **Ka**rta (peta), **Ta**han (gradien termik), **P**royeksi penduduk, **D**ensitas. These five keywords unlock the four formulas above plus map-reading essentials.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Physical Geography Concepts

Indonesia sits on the equator astride three active tectonic plates (Indo-Australian, Eurasian, Pacific), producing volcanic arcs, deep ocean trenches, and frequent seismicity. The Earth's structure layers — crust (kerak), mantle (mantel), core (inti) — govern plate dynamics that shape Indonesian landforms such as the Bukit Barisan and the Maluku Sea plate boundary.

Climate classification follows three schemes tested at UI:
1. **Köppen** — letter codes Af, Am, Aw for tropical rainforest, monsoon, savanna.
2. **Schmidt-Ferguson** — based on Q ratio of dry to wet months; type A (>0,333) up to type E (<0,063).
3. **Oldeman** — agricultural climate zones using consecutive wet/dry months, critical for rice planting calendars.

The hydrosphere revolves around the **hydrological cycle** (evaporation → condensation → precipitation → runoff → infiltration) and the **DAS (Daerah Aliran Sungai)** as the unit of water-resource management.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix

| Concept | Formula / Marker | Common Confusion |
|---|---|---|
| Crude density (D) | D = P / A (people/km²) | Confused with agrarian density Da |
| Agrarian density (Da) | Da = Pa / Aa (farmers / farmland km²) | Uses farmer count, not total population |
| Geometric growth (r%) | Pₜ = P₀(1+r)ᵗ | Mixing r=2% with multiplier 1,02 |
| Exponential growth | Pₜ = P₀·eʳᵗ | Rarely tested; UI uses geometric |
| Schmidt-Ferguson Q | Q = bulan kering / bulan basah | Reversed ratio direction |
| Oldeman type | consecutive dry months ≤ 5 | Confused with Schmidt categories |

#### Population and Settlement Theory

Demographic transition theory describes four stages — high birth/high death (stage 1) shifting to low birth/low death (stage 4). Indonesia currently sits in stage 3, where death rates fall faster than birth rates, driving the 1–2% annual growth that powers geometric projection questions. Central-place theory by Christaller explains the hexagonal market-area hierarchy: market principle (k=3), transport (k=4), administrative (k=7).

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** A village had 8.000 inhabitants in 2015 (P₀) and 9.246 in 2020. Using geometric growth, what is the annual growth rate r?

#### Solution:
- P₀ = 8.000 jiwa, Pₜ = 9.246 jiwa, t = 2020 − 2015 = 5 tahun.
- Rearrange Pₜ = P₀(1+r)ᵗ → (1+r) = (Pₜ/P₀)^(1/t).
- Compute ratio: 9.246 / 8.000 = 1,15575.
- Take the 5th root: 1,15575^(1/5) ≈ 1,0295.
- Therefore r ≈ 0,0295 → **2,95% per year**.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students often plug r = 2,95% directly into (1+r) as if it equals 2,95 instead of 1,0295, producing Pₜ = 8.000 × 2,95⁵ — a wildly inflated number. Always convert the percent into the decimal-plus-one form before exponentiating.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Advanced Traps

1. **Thermal gradient sign errors**: At 2.000 m with T₀ = 27 °C, correct T = 27 − (0,6 × 20) = 15 °C; forgetting the minus sign yields 39 °C.
2. **Map scale inversion**: A "1 : 50.000" map means 1 cm on the map = 50.000 cm (500 m) in reality; swapping numerator/denominator is the classic blunder.
3. **Astronomical location boundaries**: Indonesia spans **6°LU–11°LS** and **95°BT–141°BT** — three time zones (WIB, WITA, WIT), with the equator bisecting Sumatra and Kalimantan, not Java.
4. **Von Thünen vs Weber**: Von Thünen explains agricultural land-use rings around a market; Weber explains industrial location by transport-cost minimisation — examiners love the swap.
5. **Schmidt-Ferguson vs Oldeman wet-month threshold**: Schmidt counts months with rainfall < 60 mm as "dry"; Oldeman uses < 100 mm — substituting one for the other changes the climate letter.
6. **GIS vs peta konvensional**: SIG layers (layer, atribut, basis data) are digital and queryable; peta konvensional is static — questions asking about "analisis tumpang susun (overlay)" imply GIS.

#### Connections to Adjacent Topics

Climate links to **ekonomi pertanian** through Oldeman planting calendars; DAS links to **lingkungan hidup** through debit air and erosi; geostrategi Indonesia links to **ekonomi internasional** through jalur perdagangan Selat Malaka. Population dynamics overlaps with **sosiologi** via migrasi desa–kota, a frequent essay source at UI's Ujian Mandiri.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. Calculate the crude density of Provinsi X (luas 32.500 km², penduduk 3,9 juta jiwa) and the agrarian density if 1,2 juta work as farmers on 8.000 km² of farmland. Discuss why agrarian density exceeds crude density in Java.
2. A map sheet shows 1 : 25.000 scale. Convert a measured 4 cm distance into kilometres, then determine the temperature at the endpoint if the starting point (200 m) is 28 °C and the endpoint rises to 1.200 m.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** When a question mixes altitude with temperature, recompute T₀ from sea-level data before applying the lapse rate — students frequently multiply 0,6 by h/100 but use the wrong base temperature.
