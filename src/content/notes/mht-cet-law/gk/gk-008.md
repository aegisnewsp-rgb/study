# Geography of India

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

India extends between **8°4′N–37°6′N** latitude and **68°7′E–97°25′E** longitude, with a land frontier near 15,200 km and a coastline of about 7,516 km. The country is divided into six physiographic regions — Northern Mountains, Northern Plains, Peninsular Plateau, Indian Desert, Coastal Plains, and Islands — and runs on a single **Indian Standard Time (IST = UTC+5:30)** based on the 82°30′E longitude passing through Mirzapur, Uttar Pradesh.

- **Tropic of Cancer (23.5°N)** passes through 8 states: Gujarat → Rajasthan → Madhya Pradesh → Chhattisgarh → Jharkhand → West Bengal → Tripura → Mizoram.
- **Neighbours (land):** Pakistan, China, Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh, Myanmar, Afghanistan. **Maritime:** Sri Lanka (Palk Strait), Maldives (8° Channel), Indonesia (Six Degree Channel).
- **Longest river:** Ganga (~2,525 km). **Largest river system:** Indus (~3,180 km). **Highest peak wholly in India:** Nanda Devi (7,816 m). **Southernmost point:** Indira Point on Great Nicobar Island.
- **Soil–region map:** Alluvial (Indo-Gangetic plain), Black/Regur (Deccan trap of Maharashtra–Gujarat), Laterite (Western Ghats, NE hills), Red (Deccan eastern flank), Desert (Thar), Mountain/Forest (Himalayas).

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"GRMC JW TM"** for the 8 Tropic-of-Cancer states — **G**ujarat, **R**ajasthan, **M**P, **C**hhattisgarh, **J**harkhand, **W**est Bengal, **T**ripura, **M**izoram. Remember the last two are in the Northeast, not Manipur.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Physiographic Divisions

India's relief is conventionally split into six units by NCERT. The **Northern Mountains** (Himalayas + Trans-Himalayas) are young, fold-type, and seismically active; the **Northern Plains** are alluvial and formed by the Indus–Ganga–Brahmaputra river system; the **Peninsular Plateau** is the oldest landmass made of igneous and metamorphic rocks; the **Indian Desert** lies in western Rajasthan; the **Coastal Plains** flank the Arabian Sea and Bay of Bengal; the **Islands** include the coral **Lakshadweep** (Arabian Sea) and the volcanic **Andaman & Nicobar** (Bay of Bengal).

#### Drainage Systems

| Feature | Himalayan Rivers (e.g., Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra) | Peninsular Rivers (e.g., Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri) |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | Glacial/snowmelt in Himalayas | Peninsular plateau / Western Ghats |
| Flow regime | Perennial, youthful | Seasonal, mature |
| Course | Long, meandering, gorges | Short, graded valleys, deltas (east coast) |
| Catchment | Indo-Gangetic & Brahmaputra plains | Bay of Bengal mostly; Narmada–Tapi are rift-valley and flow west |

#### Climate and Monsoon

The four-season classification by the IMD drives most GK questions: **Cold Weather (Dec–Feb)**, **Hot Weather (Mar–May)**, **Advancing/ South-West Monsoon (Jun–Sep)**, and **Retreating/ North-East Monsoon (Oct–Nov)**. Tamil Nadu's winter rainfall comes from the **retreating NE monsoon**, not the SW — this is the most-tested climate distinction.

#### Major Passes and Strategic Geography

- **Khyber Pass** — Pakistan–Afghanistan border (historical).
- **Bolan Pass** — Balochistan, Pakistan (rail and road link).
- **Shipki La** — Himachal Pradesh–Tibet (on Sutlej).
- **Nathu La** — Sikkim–Tibet (reopened 2006 for trade).
- **Rohtang Pass** — Himachal Pradesh (Manali–Leh route).
- **Jozila / Zoji La** — Ladakh (Srinagar–Leh route).

> 📌 **Formula Check:** IST offset = Local solar time at 82°30′E − UTC. For every 15° east of Greenwich, clocks advance by 1 hour; 82.5° ÷ 15° = 5.5 hours ahead of GMT.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** Which of the following sequences correctly lists the Indian states through which the Tropic of Cancer passes from west to east?
(a) Gujarat – Rajasthan – Madhya Pradesh – Chhattisgarh – Jharkhand – West Bengal – Tripura – Manipur
(b) Gujarat – Rajasthan – Madhya Pradesh – Chhattisgarh – Jharkhand – West Bengal – Tripura – Mizoram
(c) Gujarat – Rajasthan – Chhattisgarh – Madhya Pradesh – Jharkhand – Tripura – West Bengal – Mizoram
(d) Gujarat – Rajasthan – Madhya Pradesh – Bihar – West Bengal – Assam – Mizoram – Tripura

**Solution:** The Tropic of Cancer enters India at Kutch (Gujarat), crosses Rajasthan, runs through Jhansi area of MP, passes Raipur in Chhattisgarh, cuts across Jharkhand near Ranchi, enters West Bengal, exits through Tripura, and finally passes through Mizoram. The correct west-to-east order is option **(b)**.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Option (a) substitutes Manipur for Mizoram — a classic confusion. Manipur lies entirely north of 23.5°N, so the Tropic never touches it. Similarly, Chhattisgarh and MP are often swapped (option c) because students forget Raipur's latitude.

#### Soil and Vegetation Quick Map

| Soil | Parent material / region | Key crops |
|---|---|---|
| Alluvial | Indo-Gangetic & Brahmaputra plains | Rice, wheat, sugarcane |
| Black (Regur) | Deccan trap basalt (MH, GJ) | Cotton, soybean |
| Red | Crystalline rocks of eastern Deccan | Millets, groundnut |
| Laterite | High-rainfall Western Ghats & NE | Tea, cashew, rubber |
| Desert | Western Rajasthan (Thar) | Bajra, guar |
| Mountain / Forest | Himalayan slopes | Apple, tea, spices |

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Constitutional & Administrative Geography

Post the **Jammu & Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019**, India has **28 States and 8 Union Territories**. Constitutional geography questions for MHC-CET (Law) often hinge on: (i) the state with the longest coastline (Gujarat, ~1,600 km, not Andhra as commonly assumed); (ii) the state with the shortest coastline (West Bengal); (iii) the Union Territory closest to the Equator (Lakshadweep); and (iv) the only UT sharing a land border with both Pakistan and Afghanistan via PoK (Ladakh in extension, J&K historically).

#### Border Disputes & Maritime Boundaries

The **Sir Creek** dispute (India–Pakistan, Kutch region) and the **Indus Waters Treaty (1960)** are testable under "Geography + Polity" crossover. Maritime zones follow UNCLOS: **Territorial Sea (12 nm)**, **Contiguous Zone (24 nm)**, **EEZ (200 nm)**. The **Palk Strait** separates India from Sri Lanka, the **8° Channel** separates Minicoy (Lakshadweep) from Maldives, and the **10° Channel** separates Andaman from Nicobar — these three channels are the most-asked waterway questions.

#### Edge Cases and Confusions

1. **Indira Point vs Kanyakumari:** Many students answer "Kanyakumari" as India's southernmost point. Kanyakumari is the southernmost point of the **mainland**; **Indira Point** on Great Nicobar Island is the **southernmost** of the Republic. After the 2004 tsunami, an Indian naval base marker there was submerged.
2. **Kangchenjunga vs Nanda Devi:** Kangchenjunga (8,586 m) is on the India–Nepal border, so it is **not** entirely within India. **Nanda Devi (7,816 m)** in Uttarakhand is the highest peak wholly within India. For "highest peak of India," answer Kangchenjunga; for "highest peak in India," answer Nanda Devi.
3. **Wettest place:** Mawsynram (Meghalaya), not Cherrapunji, holds the current record though both are in the Khasi Hills.
4. **Largest state by area (post-2019):** Rajasthan (~3.42 lakh km²). **Smallest state:** Goa. **Largest UT:** Ladakh (created 2019 by merging Leh + Kargil).
5. **Rivers' confluence:** Devprayag (Alaknanda + Bhagirathi → Ganga), Triveni Sangam (Ganga + Yamuna + mythical Saraswati at Prayagraj).

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Don't equate "longest river" with "largest river system." **Ganga is longest** (~2,525 km within India), while the **Indus system is the largest** (~3,180 km). The Indus itself mostly flows through Pakistan; in India, its tributaries Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, Sutlej are largely allotted under the Indus Waters Treaty.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. A map shows a river entering India from Tibet at Shipki La and exiting into Pakistan before joining the Indus near Mithankot. Identify the river and list the Indian states through which it flows. *(Expected: Sutlej — flows through Himachal Pradesh, Punjab.)*
2. A Constitutional question links Article 1 (territory) with geography. List the eight Union Territories formed after the 2019 reorganisation and identify which one was carved out of a former state and which was newly created without merging. *(Expected: Ladakh = carved out of J&K; Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu = merged into one UT; others are pre-existing reorganisations.)*
