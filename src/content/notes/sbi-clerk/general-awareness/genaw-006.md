# Indian Geography and Physical Features

## Indian Geography and Physical Features

India's diverse geography shapes its agriculture, economy, climate, and settlement patterns — all of which are relevant for banking exams given their impact on economic activity.

## India's Location and Boundaries

### Geographic Position
- Located in the **Northern Hemisphere and Eastern Hemisphere**
- Latitudinal extent: 8°4'N to 37°6'N (Kashmir to Kanyakumari)
- Longitudinal extent: 68°7'E to 97°25'E (Gujarat to Arunachal Pradesh)
- **Tropic of Cancer (23°30'N)** divides India into two halves

### Boundaries
- **North**: Himalayas (China, Nepal, Bhutan)
- **Northwest**: Pakistan, Afghanistan
- **East**: Bangladesh, Myanmar
- **South**: Indian Ocean (Arabian Sea on west, Bay of Bengal on east)
- **Coastline**: 7,516.6 km (mainland) + additional 1,197 km from islands
- **Island territories**: Andaman & Nicobar Islands (Bay of Bengal), Lakshadweep (Arabian Sea)

## Major Physical Divisions of India

### 1. The Himalayan Mountains

The Himalayas are the youngest and highest mountain range in the world.

**Three Parallels:**
1. **Greater Himalayas (Himadri)**: Highest; snow-capped peaks; includes Nanda Devi (7,816m), K2 (Pakistan-administered), Kangchenjunga (8,586m)
2. **Lesser Himalayas (Himachal)**: Middle ranges; 2,000-5,000m; includes Mussoorie, Shimla hills
3. **Sub-Himalayas/Siwaliks (Shiwaliks)**: Southernmost; 500-1,500m; flat valleys (Doons)

**Himalayan Divisions:**
- **Punjab Himalayas**: Between Indus and Sutlej
- **Kashmir Himalayas**: Between Indus and Sutlej
- **Kumaon Himalayas**: Between Sutlej and Kali
- **Nepal Himalayas**: Kali to Teesta
- **Assam Himalayas**: Teesta to Brahmaputra

**Significance**: Source of India's major rivers; spiritual significance; tourism; biodiversity

### 2. The Indo-Gangetic Plain

- Fertile alluvial plain formed by Indus, Ganga, and Brahmaputra river systems
- Extends from Punjab in west to Assam in east
- **Punjab Plain**: Bounded by Indus and Sutlej; heavily irrigated
- **Ganga Plain**: Largest; most densely populated; major agriculture
- **Brahmaputra Plain (Assam)**: Eastern part; prone to floods

### 3. The Peninsular Plateau

India's peninsular plateau is one of the oldest landmasses on Earth.

**Two Divisions:**
- **Central Highlands**: Malwa Plateau (bounded by Vindhyas and Aravallis), Bundelkhand, Baghelkhand, Chota Nagpur Plateau (Jharkhand, West Bengal, Odisha, Chhattisgarh)
- **Deccan Plateau**: South of the Narmada; triangular shape
  - Western Ghats (Sahyadris): Continuous mountain range along western edge; 1,500-2,500m; includes Mullayanagiri (Karnataka, 1,930m)
  - Eastern Ghats: Discontinuous; lower; 900-1,100m
  - Satpura Range: Central India; forms watershed between Narmada and Tapti

### 4. The Thar Desert

- **Location**: Western Rajasthan; part of Gujarat
- **Characteristics**: Sandy; arid; low rainfall (<150mm/year); extreme temperature variation
- **Great Indian Desert**: Also called Thar Desert

### 5. Coastal Plains

**Western Coastal Plain** (Arabian Sea side):
- Narrow; 50-100 km wide
- **Konkan Coast**: Mumbai to Goa
- **Malabar Coast**: Kerala
- Has estuaries, backwaters (Kerala), natural harbors

**Eastern Coastal Plain** (Bay of Bengal side):
- Broader than western; 100-150 km wide
- **Mahanadi Delta**: Largest delta in India
- **Krishna-Godavari Delta**: Combined delta of two rivers
- **Coromandel Coast**: Tamil Nadu coast
- Has deltas, lagoons, Chennai port

### 6. Islands

**Lakshadweep**:
- Coral islands off western coast (Arabian Sea)
- 36 islands; Kavaratti is capital
- Minicoy, Andrott, Agatti

**Andaman & Nicobar**:
- Volcanic and coral islands
- Southernmost point: Indira Point (Great Coco Island)
- Indira Point: India's southernmost point, on Great Nicobar Island - the mainland's southernmost point is Kanyakumari

## Major Rivers of India

### Himalayan Rivers (Perennial)
- **Indus**: Transboundary; originates in Tibet; flows through Ladakh; enters Pakistan; five tributaries (Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, Sutlej — remember: "J-C-R-B-S")
- **Ganga**: Origins in Gangotri (Bhagirathi) and Alaknanda (Devprayag); major tributaries: Yamuna, Ghaghara, Gandak, Kosi; empties into Bay of Bengal via Ganga Delta
- **Brahmaputra**: Flows through Assam; known as Yarlung Tsangpo in Tibet; major tributaries: Manas, Teesta, Subansiri; merges with Ganga to form Meghna in Bangladesh

### Peninsular Rivers

**East-Flowing (Bay of Bengal):**
- **Mahanadi**: Odisha; Hirakud Dam
- **Godavari**: Largest peninsular river; 1,465 km; Maharashtra to Bay of Bengal; tributaries: Penganga, Pranhita, Indravati, Sabari
- **Krishna**: Maharashtra-Karnataka-Andhra Pradesh; Almatti Dam
- **Cauvery**: Karnataka-Tamil Nadu; KRS Dam; sacred river of South India

**West-Flowing (Arabian Sea):**
- **Narmada**: Central India; runs through Rift Valley between Vindhyas and Satpuras; Sardar Sarovar Dam
- **Tapti**: Central India; runs parallel to Narmada; empties into Gulf of Khambhat

**River Basin Planning**: India has 20 major river basins; interlinking of rivers (National River Linking Project) to address regional water imbalances

## Climate of India

### Monsoon Climate
India's climate is dominated by the **Southwest Monsoon** (June-September).

**Monsoon Mechanism:**
- Differential heating of land and sea creates low pressure over Indian subcontinent
- Southeast trade winds cross equator and become southwest monsoon winds
- Rise over Western Ghats → heavy rainfall (Cherrapunji, Mawsynram — world's wettest places)
- Retreating monsoon (October-November) brings cyclones to eastern coast

### Seasons:
1. **Winter (Jan-Feb)**: Cold, dry; north India affected by western disturbances
2. **Hot Weather/Summer (March-May)**: Hot, dry; heat waves in north; pre-monsoon thunderstorms
3. **Southwest Monsoon (June-Sept)**: Most rainfall; 75% of annual rainfall
4. **Post-Monsoon/Northeast Monsoon (Oct-Dec)**: Retreating monsoon; cyclones on eastern coast

## Natural Vegetation

**Forest Types in India:**
- Tropical Evergreen (Assam, Western Ghats) — high rainfall areas
- Tropical Deciduous/Monsoon (Most of India) — shedding leaves in dry season
- Tropical Dry (Rajasthan, Deccan) — arid areas
- Montane (Himalayas) — altitude-based gradation
- Mangrove (Sundarbans, Odisha) — tidal forests

**Forest Cover**: ~24% of India's land area (target: 33%)

## Soil Types of India

| Soil Type | Location | Characteristics |
|-----------|---------|----------------|
| **Alluvial** | Indo-Gangetic plain, river valleys | Fertile; loamy; good for agriculture |
| **Black Cotton** | Deccan plateau (Maharashtra, MP, Gujarat) | Rich in iron/magnesium; swells when wet |
| **Red** | Peninsular India (South of Vindhyas) | Porous; less fertile; iron oxide content |
| **Laterite** | Western/Eastern Ghats, Odisha | Hard; poor fertility; used for building |
| **Forest/Mountain** | Himalayan region | Light; acidic; depth varies |
| **Desert** | Western Rajasthan | Sandy; low organic matter |
| **Saline/Alkaline** | Arid areas, coastal | White crust; unsuitable for agriculture |

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

#### Read the map as a mechanism

Indian geography questions rarely test a name on its own; they test whether you can say *why* a feature sits where it does. Every division in this note follows from three forces: relief, latitude, and the direction the summer monsoon arrives from. Learn the mechanism and the list reconstructs itself.

#### Orographic rainfall and the rain-shadow rule

Moist air off the Arabian Sea meets the Western Ghats and is forced upward. Rising air cools, condenses, and rains out on the windward slope. Having shed its moisture, the same air descends the leeward side as dry, descending air.

| Position | Air movement | Rainfall | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Windward | Forced up, cools, condenses | Heavy | Mahabaleshwar, Agumbe on the Ghats |
| Leeward | Descends, warms, dries | Low, deficit-prone | Vidarbha and the interior Deccan |
| Windward of the Himalaya | Blocked, orographically lifted | Very heavy | Mawsynram, Cherrapunji on the Meghalaya plateau |

The Deccan interior sits in the shadow of the Western Ghats, which is why Vidarbha and Marathwada face water stress while the coast receives 250 cm. Where a question pairs heavy rainfall with the leeward side, the pair is the contradiction it wants you to catch.

#### The two-peninsular-river rule

Peninsular rivers split by slope, and the slope is decided by the Narmada-Tapi line.

| Flow | Rivers | Controlling landform | Basin character |
|---|---|---|---|
| East-flowing | Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Cauvery | Western Ghats to gentle eastern slope | Long, mature, navigable, delta-building |
| West-flowing | Narmada, Tapi | Rift valley between the Vindhyas and Satpuras | Short, steep, incised, delta-less |

> **Examiner trap:** Narmada and Tapi are the only west-flowing peninsular rivers, and also the only two that build no delta. A question asserting that the Narmada forms a large delta contains its own contradiction.

#### Pairing rivers with their projects

Repeated questions join a river to a dam, and the joins are conventional.

- **Hirakud** on the Mahanadi, **Almatti** on the Krishna, **KRS** on the Cauvery, **Sardar Sarovar** on the Narmada.
- **Bhakra** on the Sutlej, **Nagarjuna Sagar** and **Srisailam** on the Krishna, **Hirakud** on the Mahanadi.
- **Tehri** on the Bhagirathi, **Chamera** on the Chenab.

> **Examiner trap:** The Indus and its five tributaries - Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, Sutlej - belong to the Himalayan system, not the peninsular one. The entire system drains into the Arabian Sea through Pakistan and none of it reaches Indian territory.

#### Soils as an agricultural argument

Soil questions are agricultural questions in disguise. The crop follows the soil, and the soil follows the climate.

| Soil | Where | Behaviour under water | Typical crop |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alluvial | Indo-Gangetic plain and river valleys | Retains moisture, deep deposit | Wheat, rice, sugarcane |
| Black cotton | Deccan (Maharashtra, MP, Gujarat) | Swells when wet, cracks when dry | Cotton, soybean, jowar |
| Red | South of the Vindhyas | Porous, leaches readily | Millets, groundnut, tobacco |
| Laterite | Western and Eastern Ghats, Odisha | Hard crust once exposed | Tea, cashew, with heavy manuring |
| Desert | Western Rajasthan | Sandy, low organic matter | Bajra, moth bean under irrigation |

Black cotton soil's moisture behaviour is the most asked property in that table: it swells through the monsoon and shrinks through the dry season, which is why it is partly self-ploughing and why it cracks visibly by April.

#### Monsoon onset and withdrawal

- **Onset** begins over Kerala around 1 June and advances north-west, covering the country by mid-July.
- **Withdrawal** starts from the north-west in early September and completes by the end of October.
- The **Arabian Sea branch** strikes the Western Ghats first; the **Bay of Bengal branch** curves up the Gangetic plain.

The Bay branch decides whether a Kharif crop succeeds in north India, because it tracks the plain rather than the coast. A late Bay branch pushes sowing past the July window. Monsoon questions usually turn on that timing rather than on total rainfall.

#### A worked reading

*Two villages sit 30 km apart on opposite sides of a low hill in coastal Karnataka. One records 280 cm of rain a year and dense evergreen forest; the other records 65 cm and scrub. Neither is on the coast.*

The wetter village is on the windward, western side facing the Arabian Sea. The drier one lies in the interior rain-shadow. Same latitude, same distance from the sea, entirely different rainfall because of the hill standing between them. Any question that hands you rainfall figures without geography is asking you to infer position.

> **Examiner trap:** Do not read *coastal* as *high rainfall* without checking which side of the Ghats you are on. The Konkan coast is wet; the Karnataka coast just south of it is markedly drier for exactly this reason.
