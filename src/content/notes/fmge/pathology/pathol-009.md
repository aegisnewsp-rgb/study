# Hematopathology

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your FMGE.

**Hematopathology** is the diagnostic subspecialty that evaluates **blood, bone marrow, lymph nodes, and spleen** using morphology, immunophenotyping, cytogenetics, and molecular studies. The RBC indices classify every anemia into a workup pathway:

- **MCV = (Hct% × 10) / RBC count** in fL → microcytic <80, normocytic 80–100, macrocytic >100.
- **Reticulocyte production index (RPI)** = corrected reticulocyte count ÷ maturation factor; **RPI >2 = adequate marrow response**, **<2 = hypoproliferative**.
- **Transferrin saturation = (Serum iron ÷ TIBC) × 100%**; <20% iron deficiency, >45% iron overload.

**High-yield FMGE pointers:** microcytic anemia → check **ferritin first** (low = iron deficiency, normal/raised → Hb electrophoresis for thalassemia). **CML = t(9;22) BCR-ABL1** Philadelphia chromosome. **APL = AML M3 with t(15;17) PML-RARA**, treated with ATRA. **Reed-Sternberg cells = CD15+, CD30+**. **DIC** shows prolonged PT/aPTT, low fibrinogen, raised D-dimer, schistocytes.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Anemia workup by MCV

The **MCV** is the single most useful branching point in any anemia vignette. Once MCV places the case into microcytic, normocytic, or macrocytic, further tests follow a deterministic path.

| MCV category | First test | Differentiates |
|---|---|---|
| Microcytic <80 fL | **Serum ferritin** | Iron deficiency (low) vs ACD (normal/high) vs thalassemia (normal) |
| Normocytic 80–100 fL | **Reticulocyte count + RPI** | Hypoproliferative (RPI <2) vs hyperproliferative (RPI >2) |
| Macrocytic >100 fL | **B12, folate, TSH, LFT** | Megaloblastic vs non-megaloblastic (alcohol, liver disease) |

For microcytic anemia, **serum ferritin is the most specific single test** because it reflects stored iron. Iron deficiency gives low ferritin, high TIBC, low transferrin saturation. Anemia of chronic disease gives normal/high ferritin, low TIBC, low/normal saturation. Thalassemia trait shows normal iron studies with target cells and basophilic stippling on smear, confirmed by **Hb electrophoresis/HPLC** (raised HbA2 >3.5% in β-thalassemia trait).

#### Megaloblastic anemia mechanism

B12 or folate deficiency impairs DNA synthesis, causing nuclear–cytoplasmic asynchrony. Smear shows **hypersegmented neutrophils (>5 lobes)**, macro-ovalocytes, and pancytopenia. Distinguishing B12 from folate deficiency needs **methylmalonic acid (raised only in B12)** and **homocysteine (raised in both)**. B12 deficiency alone causes **subacute combined degeneration** of the spinal cord.

#### Leukemia classification

The **FAB system** (M0–M7 myeloid; L1–L3 lymphoid) is morphology-based. The **WHO 2022 classification** integrates cytogenetics and molecular markers, making it the current standard for FMGE.

| Marker | Disease |
|---|---|
| t(15;17) PML-RARA | APL (AML M3) — ATRA + arsenic |
| t(8;21) RUNX1-RUNX1T1 | AML with maturation |
| inv(16) CBFB-MYH11 | AML with eosinophilia |
| t(9;22) BCR-ABL1 | CML (also 25% of adult ALL) |
| t(14;18) BCL2 | Follicular lymphoma |
| t(8;14) MYC | Burkitt lymphoma (endemic, sporadic, immunodeficiency) |

**Cytochemistry:** MPO and Sudan Black B mark myeloid lineage; PAS and TdT mark lymphoid (especially ALL); NSE marks monocytes and some T-ALL.

#### Coagulation and DIC

The screening panel — **PT, aPTT, TT, fibrinogen, D-dimer, platelet count** — must be interpreted together. **DIC** prolongs PT and aPTT, drops fibrinogen, raises D-dimer, drops platelets, and shows schistocytes from microangiopathic hemolysis. Common triggers are sepsis, obstetric complications (amniotic fluid embolism, abruptio placentae), and APL.

> **FMGE trap:** A raised D-dimer alone is **not** DIC — D-dimer rises in DVT, PE, infection, pregnancy, and post-surgery. Confirm with the full panel and clinical context.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Hemolytic anemias and Coombs interpretation

Hemolysis is suspected when **RPI >2, indirect bilirubin raised, LDH raised, haptoglobin low**, with smear showing spherocytes, schistocytes, or bite cells. The **direct antiglobulin test (DAT/Coombs)** separates immune from non-immune causes.

| DAT result | Mechanism | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Positive (IgG or C3d) | Antibody-mediated RBC destruction | Warm AIHA (IgG), cold agglutinin disease (C3d), drug-induced (methyldopa, penicillin, cephalosporins), transfusion reaction, HDN |
| Negative | Non-immune hemolysis | G6PD deficiency (bite cells, Heinz bodies), hereditary spherocytosis (osmotic fragility test, EMA binding), PNH (flow cytometry for CD55/CD59), mechanical (prosthetic valve, TTP/HUS, DIC) |

> **Exam trap:** Drug-induced immune hemolysis can produce a positive DAT. Always take a drug history before labelling a patient as primary AIHA — methyldopa induces a true autoimmune picture that resolves weeks after stopping the drug.

#### Flow cytometry and CD markers in lymphoma

Immunophenotyping distinguishes Hodgkin from non-Hodgkin lymphoma and subclassifies NHL. **Hodgkin lymphoma** is defined by **Reed-Sternberg cells** in an inflammatory background — immunophenotype **CD15+, CD30+, CD45−, PAX5 weak**. **NHL** is split into B-cell (CD19, CD20, CD79a, PAX5), T-cell (CD2, CD3, CD4/CD8, CD5, CD7), and NK-cell neoplasms. Specific markers add diagnostic weight: CD10 and BCL6 in follicular lymphoma, CD5 and CD23 in CLL/SLL, cyclin D1 in mantle cell lymphoma.

#### Adjacent topics and integration

- **Transfusion medicine:** cross-match, indirect Coombs, ABO/Rh compatibility — links hematopathology to clinical transfusion reactions.
- **Plasma cell dyscrasias:** multiple myeloma shows **M-band on SPEP**, **Bence-Jones proteinuria**, lytic bone lesions, and plasma cells >10% on marrow; MGUS is the precursor.
- **Myeloproliferative neoplasms:** polycythemia vera (JAK2 V617F ~95%), essential thrombocythemia, primary myelofibosis — distinguished from reactive elevations by JAK2, CALR, MPL mutation panels.

#### Common pitfalls

- Reporting microcytic anemia as thalassemia without first excluding iron deficiency by ferritin.
- Confusing folate with B12 deficiency and missing the neurological signs of B12 deficiency.
- Misclassifying CML blast crisis as acute leukemia without checking prior chronic-phase history and Philadelphia chromosome.
- Equating positive DAT with primary AIHA before excluding drugs.
