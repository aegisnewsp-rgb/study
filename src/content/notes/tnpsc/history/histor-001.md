# Topic 1

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The **Modern Period of Indian History (1757–1947 A.D.)** opens with the Battle of Plassey and closes with Independence, framed by three economic phases: Merchant Capitalism (1757–1813), Industrial Capitalism (1813–1858), and Finance Capitalism (1858 onward) under direct Crown rule after the 1857 Revolt. **Land settlements** you must recall by name: **Permanent Settlement (Cornwallis, 1793, Bengal)**, **Ryotwari (Thomas Munro, Madras)**, and **Mahalwari (Holt Mackenzie, NWP)**. Expansion tools: **Subsidiary Alliance (Wellesley)** and **Doctrine of Lapse (Dalhousie)**. **Reform anchors**: Brahmo Samaj (Ram Mohan Roy), Arya Samaj (Dayananda), Sati Abolition (1829). **Congress milestones**: 1885 founding, 1906 Swaraj demand, 1929 Purna Swaraj (Lahore), 1942 Quit India. **Communal**: Muslim League (1906), Partition of Bengal (1905/1911), Poona Pact (1932). TNPSC Group 1 typically tests one factual MCQ from this period in the History paper.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Phases of British Rule
The British hold on India is best understood as three overlapping economic phases. **Merchant Capitalism (1757–1813)** saw the East India Company function as a trading monopoly, plundering Bengal's treasury after Plassey and monopolising trade through the *dastak* and inland duty manipulations. The **Charter Act of 1813** ended the trade monopoly, ushering in **Industrial Capitalism (1813–1858)** when India was converted into a raw-material supplier and a market for British finished goods — railways, plantations and English education were tools of this phase. After the 1857 Revolt, the **Government of India Act 1858** transferred power to the Crown, beginning **Finance Capitalism** with capital export, railway guarantees, and managing-agency houses controlling Indian industry.

#### Land Revenue Systems
Three systems restructured agrarian relations. The **Permanent Settlement (1793)** by Lord Cornwallis fixed revenue permanently with the zamindar of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa, creating a landlord class that often evicted peasants. **Thomas Munro's Ryotwari** in Madras recognised the individual ryot as the owner and was based on soil classification. **Holt Mackenzie's Mahalwari** covered the North-Western Provinces, settling revenue at the village (mahal) level with the lambardar as the revenue payer.

#### Expansionist Doctrines
**Lord Wellesley's Subsidiary Alliance (1798 onward)** forced Indian states to maintain British troops at their own cost in exchange for protection, draining their treasuries. **Lord Dalhousie's Doctrine of Lapse (1848–56)** annexed states whose rulers died without natural heirs — applied to Satara, Nagpur, Jhansi, and Awadh (on grounds of maladministration), fuelling the 1857 Revolt.

#### Reform and National Awakening
**Raja Ram Mohan Roy** founded the Brahmo Samaj (1828) and campaigned against Sati, leading to Lord William Bentinck's **Sati Abolition Regulation, 1829**. **Dayananda Saraswati** founded the Arya Samaj (1875) with the *Back to the Vedas* call. **Dadabhai Naoroji's *Poverty and Un-British Rule in India* (1867)** articulated the **Drain Theory**, quantifying the wealth transfer to Britain.

#### Indian National Congress and Legislative Acts
A.O. Hume founded the **INC in 1885**. The **Moderates (Naoroji, Gokhale)** relied on prayers and petitions; the **Extremists (Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal, Lala Lajpat Rai)** advocated Swaraj. Key acts: **1909 Morley–Minto (separate electorates)**, **1919 Montagu–Chelmsford (Dyarchy at provinces)**, **1935 Act (provincial autonomy)**.

#### Exam Patterns
TNPSC Group 1 typically frames 1–2 MCQs asking: which Act introduced Dyarchy (1919), who started the Drain Theory (Dadabhai Naoroji), year of Poona Pact (1932), or which Viceroy introduced the Subsidiary Alliance (Wellesley). Link each name to a single landmark act for fast recall.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### The 1857 Revolt as a Turning Point
Multiple causes converged: the greased cartridges, the Doctrine of Lapse, the Annexation of Awadh, social reform interference, and the *izzat* of soldiers and dispossessed rulers. Leaders included **Rani Lakshmibai (Jhansi), Kunwar Singh (Bihar), Begum Hazrat Mahal (Lucknow), Tantia Tope, and Nana Saheb**. Centres ranged from Meerut and Delhi to Kanpur, Lucknow, Jhansi and Hyderabad. The Revolt is officially termed the **First War of Indian Independence** by nationalist historians, though colonial scholars like W.W. Hunter called it a sepoy mutiny. Outcome: dissolution of the East India Company and direct Crown rule via the 1858 Act, with the Queen's Proclamation promising non-annexation and equal treatment — a promise soon violated.

#### The Gandhian Mass Movements
**Non-Cooperation (1920, Nagpur session)** withdrew students from aided schools, boycotted courts and titles, and united the Khilafat cause; suspended after Chauri Chaura (1922). The **Salt March (Dandi, 12 March 1930)** launched **Civil Disobedience**, breaking the salt law and producing the **Gandhi–Irwin Pact (1931)**. **Quit India (8 August 1942)** demanded immediate British withdrawal; Gandhi gave the *"Do or Die"* call. Side currents: the **Chittagong Armoury Raid** (1930), **Indian National Army trials (Red Fort, 1945–46)** of Shah Nawaz, Prem Kumar Sahgal and Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon, and the **Royal Indian Navy mutiny (February 1946)**.

#### Communal Politics and Partition
**Partition of Bengal (1905)** by Curzon was reversed in 1911 as a concession to nationalist protest. The **All-India Muslim League formed at Dhaka in 1906** under Nawab Salimullah. **Muhammad Iqbal's Allahabad Address (1930)** articulated the Two-Nation idea; **Choudhry Rahmat Ali** coined *Pakistan* in 1933. The **Communal Award (1932)** retained separate electorates; the **Poona Pact (20 September 1932)** between Gandhi and Ambedkar replaced separate electorates with reserved seats for Depressed Classes. The **Cabinet Mission (1946)** proposed a united federal India, rejected by the League, paving the way for the **Mountbatten Plan (3 June 1947)** and the **Indian Independence Act (18 July 1947)**.

#### Common Mistakes
1. Attributing the **Permanent Settlement to Dalhousie** — it was Cornwallis, 1793.
2. Calling the 1935 Act the one that gave India full independence — it granted provincial autonomy and a *proposed* (never realised) All-India Federation.
3. Crediting the **Drain Theory** to Mahatma Gandhi — it is Dadabhai Naoroji.
4. Saying Dyarchy was introduced by the 1935 Act — Dyarchy was introduced at the provincial level by the 1919 Act.
