# Journal Entries & Ledgers

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Journal Entries & Ledgers** sit at Stage 1 and Stage 2 of the accounting cycle: the journal is the **book of original (prime) entry** that records every transaction date-wise using the **dual-aspect rule (Debit the receiver, Credit the giver)**, and the ledger is the **book of final entry** holding the classified set of accounts that the journal entries are later *posted* into.

The **Accounting Equation** — `Assets = Liabilities + Capital` (₹) — underpins every entry, and the cardinal check is **Total Debits = Total Credits** per entry. Apply the three Golden Rules to Personal, Real and Nominal accounts, balance each ledger at period-end, and the resulting debit/credit totals flow straight into the **Trial Balance**.

| Account Type | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Personal | The receiver | The giver |
| Real | What comes in | What goes out |
| Nominal | Expenses / losses | Incomes / gains |

Three highest-yield points: (1) every journal entry needs a clear **narration**, (2) **Compound entries** (one debit, multiple credits or vice-versa) must be combined into one entry, and (3) ledger balancing errors are the most common cause of a mismatched Trial Balance.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### The Journal — Book of Original Entry

A **source document** (invoice, receipt, debit/credit note, voucher) triggers a **journal entry** written in chronological order with date, debit account, credit account, and a concise **narration** explaining the transaction. The ICSI study material specifically penalises missing narrations, so treat the line *"Being cash received from Ram"* as non-optional.

The supporting arithmetic identity is the **Accounting Equation**:

> `Assets (₹) = Liabilities (₹) + Capital (₹)`

Because every transaction preserves this equation, every journal entry must satisfy **Total Debits = Total Credits** in ₹.

#### The Three Golden Rules

The rules govern which side of an entry each account gets:

- **Personal Account** (debtors, creditors, capital, drawings) — Debit the receiver, Credit the giver.
- **Real Account** (assets like Cash, Furniture, Stock, Building) — Debit what comes in, Credit what goes out.
- **Nominal Account** (expenses, losses, incomes, gains) — Debit all expenses/losses, Credit all incomes/gains.

A frequent Paper 1 question threads 4–6 transactions across all three account types, e.g., *goods purchased for cash* → Debit Purchases A/c, Credit Cash A/c (Nominal debit, Real credit).

#### The Ledger — Book of Final Entry

**Posting** moves each debit and credit from the journal into the matching ledger account on the correct side. Period-end **balancing** follows two formulas:

| Account Nature | Closing Balance (₹) |
|---|---|
| Asset / Expense | Opening Balance + Total Debits − Total Credits |
| Liability / Income / Capital | Opening Balance + Total Credits − Total Debits |

If debits exceed credits the account carries a **Debit Balance**; otherwise a **Credit Balance**.

#### Variations of Journal Entries

Beyond the simple two-account entry, paper setters test specialised forms: **Opening entries** at the start of a financial year, **Closing entries** transferring nominal balances to Trading/Profit & Loss, **Adjusting entries** for accruals and prepayments, **Rectifying entries** fixing errors spotted before trial-balance preparation, **Transfer entries** moving items between ledgers, and **Contra entries** recording dual cash-and-bank transactions. **Compound entries** combine one debit with several credits (or vice-versa) under a single narration, e.g., credit purchase of furniture ₹5,000 and goods ₹20,000 from Mohan → Debit Furniture A/c ₹5,000, Debit Purchases A/c ₹20,000, Credit Mohan ₹25,000.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Mechanic Traps

**1. Mixed-account transactions.** A single deal can trigger both Personal and Real accounts. Example: Sharma started business with cash ₹1,00,000 and furniture ₹30,000 — one **Compound Opening Entry** (Debit Cash ₹1,00,000, Debit Furniture ₹30,000, Credit Capital ₹1,30,000) is required, never two separate entries.

**2. Contra entries.** Cash deposited into bank (or withdrawn) is recorded on both the Cash and Bank columns of the **Cash Book**, treated as one contra entry because both legs affect Real accounts — Cash comes in (debit) and Cash goes out at the bank (credit), but the practical shortcut is a single dual-sided Cash Book entry.

**3. Rectifying entries without altering prior balances.** An error of original entry (e.g., ₹1,200 sales to Ram wrongly debited to Shyam) needs a **rectifying entry** that uses the Suspense A/c only if a Trial Balance imbalance already exists; otherwise the entry is a direct debit/credit swap between the two parties.

#### Worked Numeric — Ledger Balancing

Ledger of Rent A/c for Q1 (Opening ₹0):

| Date | Debit (₹) | Credit (₹) |
|---|---|---|
| 05-Jan | 10,000 | — |
| 20-Feb | 10,000 | — |
| 28-Mar | — | 2,000 (received back) |

Applying the **Asset/Expense balance formula**: `0 + 20,000 − 2,000 = ₹18,000` Debit Balance. This becomes a line item in the Trial Balance debit column.

#### Common Mistakes

> Examiner traps: reversed Dr/Cr for goods withdrawn by proprietor (Debit Drawings, Credit Purchases — *not* Credit Stock), missing narrations, splitting compound entries, and forgetting to balance the ledger before drafting the Trial Balance.

#### Two Practice Prompts

1. Journalise: *"On 1st April, X, Y and Z partners contributed cash ₹50,000, ₹30,000 and ₹20,000 respectively and brought machinery worth ₹15,000 by X. Pass the opening entry and post the machinery to the fixed-asset ledger."*
2. From the following Trial Balance mismatch of ₹500 (credit side short), trace whether the cause is an unposted credit sales entry of ₹500 or a wrongly-debited expense, and write the rectifying journal entry.
