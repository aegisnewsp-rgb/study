# News Report Long-Writing Task — OSSLT Writing

The News Report is the first of the two long-writing tasks on the OSSLT. Students are given a visual prompt (often a captioned photograph) and a brief information source, and asked to write a short news report. The EQAO Framework and OSSLT rubric score the response on idea development, organisation, language and conventions. This is one of two single highest-weighted writing items on the OSSLT.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### What the News Report task asks for

- A short news report based on the visual prompt and the supplied information source.
- An inverted-pyramid structure — most important information first, background next, supporting details last.
- An attention-getting lead that answers who, what, when, where, why (and sometimes how).
- A clear ending — a wrap-up sentence that closes the report without introducing new information.
- Writing that is grammatically correct, spelled correctly, and punctuated correctly.

#### The five Ws of the lead

The lead paragraph should answer the five Ws in plain language:

- **Who** is the report about? Name the person, group, or organisation.
- **What** happened? State the event in one or two short sentences.
- **When** did it happen? Give the day, date, and (if relevant) the time.
- **Where** did it happen? Name the place.
- **Why** did it happen (or why does it matter)? State the cause or the significance.

#### One-pass structure

1. Lead: one short paragraph answering the five Ws.
2. Supporting paragraph: a quote, a statistic, or a background detail from the source.
3. Supporting paragraph: another piece of supporting information.
4. Closing paragraph: a short wrap-up sentence.

#### Examiner traps

- Burying the lead. The five Ws go in the first paragraph, not later.
- Inventing facts. The OSSLT News Report must be based on the visual prompt and the supplied source, not on the student's imagination.
- Mixing fact with opinion. News Report is factual; opinion belongs in the Series-of-Paragraphs task.
- Forgetting the wrap-up. A News Report that ends abruptly scores lower on organisation.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Strategy for the News Report task

**Read the visual prompt first.** The visual prompt carries most of the five Ws — who is in the picture, what is happening, where it is. The supplied information source adds the when, the why, and supporting quotes.

**Read the supplied information source twice.** The first read is for sense. The second read is to mark the usable facts — names, dates, places, quotes, statistics. These become the body of the report.

**Plan the lead in one sentence.** Before writing, draft a single sentence that answers the five Ws. That sentence becomes the basis of the lead paragraph.

**Use one source quote per supporting paragraph.** A direct quote (with quotation marks and attribution) makes the report sound like a real news article. Place the quote at the start or end of the supporting paragraph for emphasis.

**Close with a forward-looking sentence, not new facts.** The wrap-up sentence may state what will happen next, what officials plan to do, or what the community reaction has been. Do not introduce a new fact that needs its own paragraph.

#### Practice routine for the News Report task

- Take one EQAO sample News Report task. Read the prompt and source. Plan the lead in one sentence.
- Write the lead paragraph. Time yourself: under 5 minutes.
- Write two supporting paragraphs, each with one fact from the source. Time yourself: under 10 minutes total.
- Write the closing sentence. Time yourself: under 2 minutes.
- Total target: under 20 minutes for the long-writing task.
- After writing, count the spelling, grammar and punctuation errors. Target: fewer than three per response.

#### How the rubric scores the News Report

The EQAO OSSLT rubric for the long-writing tasks scores four dimensions:

- **Idea development.** Does the report include the relevant facts from the source, the relevant details from the visual, and a clear focus?
- **Organisation.** Does the report use inverted-pyramid structure, with the lead first and the wrap-up last?
- **Language and vocabulary.** Does the report use precise verbs and nouns, varied sentence structure, and an appropriate news-report tone?
- **Conventions.** Is the report free of spelling, grammar and punctuation errors?

A response that scores well on idea development but poorly on conventions can still score overall in the middle range. Conventions errors are capped in their impact, but they do drop the score.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How the News Report task connects to the other OSSLT items

- **Reading 1** (explicit information) is the skill the News Report task depends on most. The student must locate the relevant facts in the source and the visual.
- **Reading 2** (inference) is needed to interpret the visual — what is happening in the picture, what mood it conveys.
- **Reading 3** (making connections) is less central to the News Report task but is useful for the wrap-up sentence.

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"I need to write a long report."** The OSSLT News Report is short — typically four or five short paragraphs. A long, padded report scores lower on organisation.
- **"I should describe the picture in detail."** Describe the picture only as much as is needed to convey the five Ws. Detailed picture description is not the goal.
- **"I can make up facts."** All facts in the News Report must come from the supplied visual or information source. Invented facts score zero on idea development.

#### Exam technique

- Read the visual prompt first. Mark the five Ws.
- Read the supplied information source twice. Mark the usable facts.
- Plan the lead in one sentence. Write the lead paragraph.
- Write two supporting paragraphs, each with one source fact.
- Write the closing sentence.
- Re-read for spelling, grammar and punctuation. Use the platform's spell-check if available.
- Use the platform's word-count or length indicator to check you have written enough but not too much.
