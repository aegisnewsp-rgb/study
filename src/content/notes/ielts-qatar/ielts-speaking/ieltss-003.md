# Part 3 — Two-Way Discussion — IELTS Speaking (Qatar)

Part 3 of the IELTS Speaking test is the **Two-Way Discussion**: an
examiner-led discussion of abstract issues linked to the Part 2 cue-card topic.
It runs for **4–5 minutes**, and the examiner asks **4–6 questions** of
increasing complexity.

Unlike Part 2 (which is a monologue), Part 3 is a **conversation**. The
examiner asks a question, the candidate responds, and the examiner probes
deeper with a follow-up. The discussion moves from **concrete** (the same
domain as Part 2) to **abstract** (broader questions about society, culture,
or trends).

This is the **strongest discriminator at band 7 and above**. Candidates at
band 6 can usually answer Part 1 and Part 2 competently, but Part 3 is where
candidates at band 6 start to sound rehearsed, give short answers, or fail
to justify their opinions. Candidates who reach band 7+ on Part 3 do three
things differently: (1) they **speculate** (use phrases like *"I imagine
that…"*, *"perhaps in the future…"*), (2) they **compare** (use phrases like
*"compared to a generation ago…"*, *"in contrast with…"*), and (3) they
**justify** (give reasons, examples, or evidence for every opinion).

In the Qatari cohort, Part 3 commonly anchors on broad themes linked to
education, technology, family, environment, or cultural change — the same
domains the cue-card in Part 1 / Part 2 tests in concrete form.

This note shows the LCA (Long-Read Cohort Appetite) version: a **Lite**
(5-minute) read, a **Standard** (15-minute) read, and the **Exam Essentials**
table a Qatari candidate can use to revise in the last hour before the test.

## Lite (4-Step Method)

1. **Listen carefully to the question; do not start before you have.** Part 3
   questions are sometimes long and complex (*"Do you think the way people
   use technology has changed more in the last 20 years than in the previous
   100, and why?"*). Candidates who start talking before they have parsed the
   question often go off-topic and lose marks.
2. **Give a direct answer first, then justify it.** Start with *"Yes, I think
   so, because…"* or *"No, not really, because…"*. The direct answer signals
   confidence; the justification demonstrates depth.
3. **Speculate, compare, justify.** Band 7+ candidates use speculation
   (*"I imagine that…"*), comparison (*"compared to a generation ago…"*),
   and justification (*"the main reason is…"*) throughout Part 3. Band 6
   candidates rarely do this.
4. **Develop the answer, do not stop at one sentence.** A 5–10 second answer
   in Part 3 is too short. Aim for 15–30 seconds per answer, with
   speculation, comparison, or justification built in.

**Insight: Part 3 is where the examiner discriminates between band 6 and band
7+.** Band 6 candidates answer the question and stop. Band 7+ candidates
answer the question, then speculate, compare, or justify. The single biggest
predictor of a band 7 score in Speaking is sustained, justified reasoning in
Part 3.

### 6 Examiner Traps (Part 3 — Two-Way Discussion)

- **The yes/no trap** — candidates answer *"Yes"* or *"No"* without
  justification. The examiner scores your **development**, not your opinion.
  Always give a reason.
- **The short answer trap** — candidates give 5-second answers. The examiner
  expects 15–30 second answers in Part 3.
- **The repetition trap** — candidates repeat the same phrase (*"because it
  is important"*) to fill time. Use a range of justification structures
  (*"the main reason is…"*, *"another factor is…"*, *"as a result…"*).
- **The memorized answer trap** — Part 3 is the part where memorized answers
  are most easily exposed, because the examiner probes follow-ups that a
  memorized answer cannot anticipate.
- **The off-topic trap** — candidates answer a question they were not asked.
  Listen carefully and stay on the question.
- **The no-example trap** — abstract answers (*"technology is important"*)
  score low. Use an example (*"for example, smartphones have changed how
  students at QU study"*).

## Standard (7-Step Method with Long-Form Guidance)

### Step 1 — Listen to the question carefully

Part 3 questions can be long and complex. Take 2–3 seconds after the
examiner finishes to make sure you have parsed the question correctly. If
unsure, you can ask the examiner to repeat the question (*"Could you repeat
the question, please?"* — the examiner will not mark you down for this).

### Step 2 — Give a direct answer first

Start with *"Yes, I think so"*, *"No, not really"*, *"It depends"*, or
another direct answer. This signals confidence and shows you understood the
question.

### Step 3 — Justify your answer

Immediately after the direct answer, give a reason. Use phrases like:
- *"the main reason is…"*
- *"I think this is because…"*
- *"one factor is…"*
- *"as a result of…"*

### Step 4 — Speculate (if appropriate)

For questions about the future or hypothetical situations, use speculation
phrases:
- *"I imagine that…"*
- *"perhaps in the future…"*
- *"it might be the case that…"*
- *"I suspect that…"*

### Step 5 — Compare (if appropriate)

For questions about change or comparison, use comparison phrases:
- *"compared to a generation ago…"*
- *"in contrast with…"*
- *"whereas before…"*
- *"unlike in the past…"*

### Step 6 — Develop with an example

For every abstract point, give a concrete example. The example can be from
your own experience, your family, your community, or a public figure /
event.

### Step 7 — Listen for the follow-up

After you answer, the examiner will ask a follow-up question. Listen to the
follow-up; do not start talking before you have parsed it. Repeat the cycle.

## Previous Year Patterns (IELTS 2018–2025)

IELTS Speaking Part 3 has used the two-way discussion format consistently
since 2018, with the same structure: 4–6 questions of increasing complexity,
4–5 minutes, abstract topics. Over 2018–2025:

- 2018–2020: Part 3 commonly anchored on **education**, **technology**, and
  **environment** themes (60%+ of tests).
- 2021–2023: Part 3 diversified to include **work**, **family**, and
  **government** themes.
- 2024–2025: Part 3 frequently anchors on **AI / technology / future of
  work** themes, reflecting global concern about AI's impact.

The pattern: IELTS Speaking Part 3 has shifted from generic "society"
questions to more specific, topical questions (e.g. *"How is AI changing the
way students learn?"*). Candidates who prepare topical examples (AI,
climate, education, work) outperform those who prepare only generic
"change is good" answers.

## Pro Tips (10 enumeration)

1. **Listen carefully before answering.** Take 2–3 seconds to parse the
   question.
2. **Give a direct answer first.** *Yes, No, It depends.*
3. **Justify your answer.** Always give a reason.
4. **Speculate, compare, justify.** These three moves separate band 6 from
   band 7+.
5. **Give a specific example.** Abstract answers score low.
6. **Develop the answer (15–30s).** Short answers score low.
7. **Use varied vocabulary.** Paraphrase the question; do not repeat the
   question wording.
8. **Use discourse markers.** *First, in addition, on the other hand.*
9. **Listen for follow-ups.** Pause to parse the follow-up question.
10. **Avoid memorized answers.** The examiner probes follow-ups you cannot
    anticipate.
