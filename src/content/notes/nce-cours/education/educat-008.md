# Guidance and Counseling

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Guidance and Counselling** is the structured support service that helps learners understand their abilities, interests, aptitudes and environment in order to make sound educational, vocational, personal and social adjustments. **Guidance** is broader, preventive and developmental, while **counselling** is narrower, remedial, therapeutic and confidential.

| Term | One-line meaning | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| Guidance | Help in making wise choices across life | Preventive, for everyone |
| Counselling | One-to-one help to resolve a specific problem | Remedial, confidential |
| Counsellor | Trained professional who offers the help | School-based |
| Vocational guidance | Help with career/occupation choice | Career planning |
| Personal-social guidance | Help with emotions, peer, family issues | Behaviour, social life |
| Referral | Sending a client to a specialist | Psychologist, psychiatrist |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** "**G**uidance = **G**eneral & **G**roup-friendly; **C**ounselling = **C**lose, **C**onfidential, **C**lient-specific." Pair it with the acronym **CAGE** for counsellor qualities — **C**ongruence, **A**cceptance (positive regard), **G**enuineness/E**m**pathy.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Definition and Scope
Guidance is a continuous, developmental process that assists individuals from childhood through adulthood to understand themselves and their environment, and to plan realistic life choices. Counselling is a focused, face-to-face helping relationship built on trust, in which a trained counsellor helps a client explore feelings, clarify problems and reach self-determined solutions.

#### Difference Between Guidance and Counselling

| Basis | Guidance | Counselling |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Preventive, developmental | Remedial, therapeutic |
| Focus | Broad — educational, vocational, personal-social | Narrow — a specific personal problem |
| Relationship | One-to-many or one-to-one | Strictly one-to-one |
| Time | Long-term, lifelong | Limited sessions |
| Approach | Informational, advisory | Non-directive, Rogerian |
| Outcome | Wise decision-making | Behavioural/emotional adjustment |
| Who needs it | Everyone | A person with a defined problem |

> 📌 **Formula Check:** Use the 5-stage counselling process as a checklist — *Relationship → Assessment → Goal setting → Intervention → Termination/follow-up*. Skip a stage and your answer loses marks.

#### Principles of Guidance and Qualities of the Counsellor
The principles are: recognition of **individual differences**, continuous process, cooperation among stakeholders, **confidentiality**, need-based service, accessibility to all, flexibility, and concern for **total personality development**. A school counsellor must show **empathy**, **genuineness (congruence)**, **unconditional positive regard**, active listening, non-judgmental warmth, patience and respect for confidentiality.

#### Stages of the Counselling Process
1. **Relationship building** — establish rapport and trust.
2. **Problem identification / assessment** — explore the client's situation.
3. **Goal setting** — agree on realistic, client-chosen goals.
4. **Intervention (counselling proper)** — apply listening, reflection, paraphrasing.
5. **Termination and follow-up** — close sessions and check progress.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem
**Question:** A JSS3 student in your attached school has suddenly withdrawn from friends, lost appetite, sleeps poorly and scores have dropped. (a) Identify the helping service most appropriate for the student. (b) List four qualities you will demonstrate. (c) Outline the five stages you will follow.

#### Solution:
(a) **Counselling** — because the problem is specific (emotional/behavioural), personal and confidential, not a general choice to be made.
(b) **Empathy** (feel with the learner), **genuineness/congruence** (be real, not fake), **unconditional positive regard** (accept without judging), **active listening** (hear content and feeling).
(c) *Relationship building* (create safety through warmth and confidentiality) → *Assessment* (gather facts, feelings, history) → *Goal setting* (agree with the learner on what to change) → *Intervention* (use reflection of feelings, open-ended questions, paraphrasing) → *Termination and follow-up* (review progress, plan relapse prevention).

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Writing "guidance and counselling" as one answer in (a) costs marks. The scenario is a defined personal problem — the correct answer is **counselling**, not guidance. Also, do not list "give advice" or "be directive" among counsellor qualities; the NCE/Rogerian model is non-directive.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Types of Guidance Services in Detail
Schools in Nigeria are expected to mount four overlapping guidance programmes: **educational guidance** (study habits, subject choice, exam preparation), **vocational guidance** (career awareness, aptitude testing, job-market realities), **personal-social guidance** (peer pressure, drug abuse, teenage pregnancy, HIV/AIDS awareness, bereavement) and **health/moral/civic guidance** (hygiene, values, citizenship). Personal-social guidance is the most under-listed and the most tested.

#### Theories That Inform Practice

| Theory | Proponent | School-counselling use |
|---|---|---|
| Psychosocial stages | Erikson | Understand identity vs. role confusion in adolescents |
| Cognitive development | Piaget | Match advice to learner's thinking stage |
| Hierarchy of needs | Maslow | Address basic safety before academic goals |
| Person-centred counselling | Carl Rogers | Empathy, congruence, UPR — core qualities |
| Career development | Donald Super | Life-span vocational stages, self-concept |

#### Problems Addressed in Nigerian Schools
Examination anxiety, poor study habits, career indecision, peer pressure, substance abuse, truancy, family conflict, teenage pregnancy, HIV/AIDS-related stigma, and bullying. The school counsellor works with the class teacher but refers severe mental-health cases (psychosis, severe depression, suicidal ideation) to a **psychiatrist**, not a counsellor.

| Limitation in Nigeria | Practical effect |
|---|---|
| Shortage of trained counsellors | One counsellor handles 1,000+ pupils |
| No dedicated counselling office | Sessions held in staff rooms |
| Cultural and religious resistance | Families see counselling as "Western" |
| Stigma attached to seeking help | Pupils avoid the counsellor |
| Inadequate funding | No inventories, no testing kits |
| Teacher–counsellor role conflict | Counsellor still takes classes |

> 📌 **Formula Check:** Memorise this 6-limitation set — examiner questions often ask for "five problems/challenges of guidance and counselling in Nigeria."

#### Advanced Practice Prompts
1. *Scenario:* A principal asks you to design a comprehensive guidance programme for a co-educational secondary school. Outline the programme's objectives, staff needs, types of guidance to cover, and how you would handle cultural resistance from parents.
2. *Essay defence:* "The class teacher is the real counsellor in any Nigerian school." Argue **for** or **against**, drawing on the role conflict between teaching and counselling and on Rogers' person-centred principles.
