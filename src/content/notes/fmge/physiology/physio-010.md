# Special Senses and Neurophysiology

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your FMGE paper.

**Special senses** are mediated by dedicated receptor organs — eye, ear, tongue, olfactory epithelium, vestibular apparatus — whose afferents travel through restricted cranial nerves (CN I, II, VII, VIII, IX, X) to specific thalamic or cortical targets. **Neurophysiology** underpins every step: the resting membrane potential (~ −70 mV) generated by K⁺ leak and Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase, the threshold (~ −55 mV) at which voltage-gated Na⁺ channels open, and the all-or-none action potential propagated by saltatory conduction between nodes of Ranvier.

> High-yield FMGE pointers
- **Phototransduction is hyperpolarizing**, not depolarizing — light closes cGMP-gated Na⁺/Ca²⁺ channels on rods/cones.
- **Olfaction bypasses the thalamus** — mitral cells project directly to piriform cortex; olfactory neurons regenerate through the cribriform plate.
- **Endocochlear potential is +80 mV**, the largest in the body, generated by the stria vascularis.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Ionic basis of excitability
The Goldman–Hodgkin–Katz (GHK) equation describes the resting membrane potential as a weighted permeability ratio across K⁺, Na⁺, and Cl⁻:

| Quantity | Typical value | Determined by |
|---|---|---|
| RMP (neuron) | −70 mV | K⁺ leak >> Na⁺ leak |
| Threshold | −55 mV | Voltage-gated Na⁺ channel opening |
| Na⁺ equilibrium (E_Na) | +60 mV | Nernst equation for Na⁺ |
| K⁺ equilibrium (E_K) | −90 mV | Nernst equation for K⁺ |

When depolarization reaches threshold, **phase 0** Na⁺ influx fires the action potential; **phase 3** delayed K⁺ efflux repolarizes the membrane; **phase 4** Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase restores gradients. During the **absolute refractory period** no stimulus — however strong — can elicit a new spike, because Na⁺ channels remain inactivated.

#### Visual and auditory transduction
In rods, photon absorption converts 11-*cis*-retinal to all-trans-retinal, activates rhodopsin → transducin → phosphodiesterase 6 → cGMP hydrolysis → channel closure → **hyperpolarization**. Vitamin A deficiency impairs this cycle, producing **nyctalopia** (night blindness).

In the cochlea, the **organ of Corti** sits on the basilar membrane. Outer hair cells amplify the travelling wave via prestin-driven electromotility; inner hair cells are the true transducers, synapsing on ~95 % of Type I afferents. The **endocochlear potential (+80 mV)** plus the hair-cell resting potential (~ −45 mV) creates a 125 mV driving force across stereocilia — essential for hearing sensitivity.

#### Chemical and vestibular senses
Taste uses five basic modalities: sweet, umami, and bitter via GPCRs (T1R/T2R → PLCβ2 → IP₃ → TRP channels); salty via ENaC; sour via H⁺-gated K⁺ channel closure. **Olfactory** afferents are unique — they regenerate from basal cells and project directly to piriform cortex without a thalamic relay.

The **vestibular apparatus** has three semicircular canals (angular acceleration, detected by cristae) and two otolith organs — utricle and saccule (linear acceleration and gravity, detected by maculae with CaCO₃ otoconia). Afferents in CN VIII drive the **vestibulo-ocular reflex (VOR)**, which stabilizes gaze during head movement.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Sensory pathways and clinical correlations

| Pathway | Cranial nerve | Relay | Cortical target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vision | CN II | Lateral geniculate nucleus | V1 (calcarine) |
| Hearing | CN VIII | Cochlear nuclei → inferior colliculus → MGN | Heschl's gyrus |
| Olfaction | CN I | **None** (direct) | Piriform cortex |
| Taste | CN VII, IX, X | Nucleus solitarius → VPM | Gustatory cortex |
| Vestibular | CN VIII | Vestibular nuclei + cerebellum | — |

> High-yield traps
- **Bell's palsy** (CN VII lower-motor-neuron lesion) abolishes taste on the anterior two-thirds of the tongue and abolishes the **stapedius reflex**, producing hyperacusis.
- **Ramsay Hunt syndrome** reactivates varicella-zoster in the geniculate ganglion — vesicles on the pinna with facial palsy.
- **Ménière's disease** features endolymphatic hydrops with the tetrad of fluctuating hearing loss, tinnitus, vertigo, and aural fullness.
- **BPPV** (posterior-canal otoconia) is diagnosed by the **Dix-Hallpike** maneuver and treated by the **Epley** repositioning.

#### Edge cases and exam favorites
- **Weber and Rinne tests** distinguish conductive (Rinne negative, Weber ipsilateral) from sensorineural (Rinne positive, Weber contralateral) loss.
- **Conduction velocity** in myelinated fibers scales roughly as **v ≈ 5–6 × d** (m/s), where d is axon diameter in µm — explains why the 20 µm vestibular fibers conduct faster than 1 µm pain fibers.
- **Saltatory conduction** at nodes of Ranvier cuts metabolic cost and increases velocity; demyelination (e.g., multiple sclerosis, Guillain-Barré) slows conduction and produces conduction block.
- **Horner syndrome** (sympathetic chain disruption C8–T2): ptosis, miosis, anhidrosis, apparent enophthalmos.
- **Pupillary light reflex** afferent limb is CN II; efferent parasympathetic limb travels via the **Edinger–Westphal** nucleus → ciliary ganglion → CN III → sphincter pupillae. Afferent defect gives a **Marcus Gunn pupil** (relative afferent pupillary defect).
- **Dark adaptation** depends on the retinoid cycle; cone adaptation completes in ~5–7 minutes, rod adaptation in 30–45 minutes.

#### Practice prompts
1. A 62-year-old man has progressive high-frequency hearing loss with preserved word discrimination. Which structure is most likely degenerating, and what is the diagnosis?
2. Light striking a rod causes hyperpolarization, not depolarization. Trace the cascade from 11-*cis*-retinal to channel closure, and explain why this still increases glutamate release at the bipolar cell synapse.
