# Equilibrium, Torque and Circuits — MCAT Chem/Phys

Equilibrium, torque, fluid statics and dynamics, and DC circuits form part of AAMC Foundational Concept 4 of the Chem/Phys section. The MCAT tests these concepts in the context of human biomechanics, blood flow, and laboratory instrumentation. Items pair a passage describing a physical setup (a lever, a fluid column, an electrical circuit) with a multiple-choice item that requires applying the relevant formula.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### Core formulas

- **Static equilibrium:** ΣF = 0 and Στ = 0. The sum of forces and torques on a body at rest is zero.
- **Torque:** τ = r × F × sin(θ). Torque in N·m; r in m; F in N; θ the angle between r and F.
- **Pressure:** P = F / A. Pressure in pascals (Pa = N/m²); force in N; area in m².
- **Pascal's law:** P = ρ × g × h for a fluid column of height h.
- **Continuity equation:** A₁ × v₁ = A₂ × v₂ (incompressible fluid flow).
- **Bernoulli's equation:** P + (1/2) × ρ × v² + ρ × g × h = constant.
- **Ohm's law:** V = I × R. Voltage in volts; current in amperes; resistance in ohms.
- **Kirchhoff's laws:** ΣI = 0 at a junction; ΣV = 0 around a loop.

#### One-pass drill

1. Identify the topic: equilibrium, torque, fluid, or circuit.
2. List the known quantities from the passage.
3. Pick the formula that connects knowns to the unknown.
4. Solve algebraically; plug in numbers last.
5. Check the units of the answer.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing static and dynamic equilibrium. Static means no motion; dynamic means no net force but possible motion (e.g., uniform circular motion).
- Forgetting to use SI units. The MCAT uses metres, kilograms, seconds, amperes, volts, ohms.
- Treating series and parallel circuits the same. Series: same current, voltages add. Parallel: same voltage, currents add.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Static equilibrium in biomechanics

The human body is in static equilibrium when standing still or holding a posture. The MCAT frames this as a lever problem:

- Identify the pivot (the joint).
- Identify the forces: weight of the limb, weight of any load, muscle force.
- Apply Στ = 0 about the pivot to find the muscle force.
- The mechanical advantage is the ratio of load arm to effort arm.

#### Fluid statics and dynamics

**Fluid statics** (Pascal's law): pressure in a fluid depends on depth, density, and gravity. The MCAT uses this for blood-pressure measurements, atmospheric-pressure contexts, and the manometer.

**Continuity:** in an incompressible fluid, the product of cross-sectional area and velocity is constant. Narrowing a tube speeds the flow.

**Bernoulli:** the sum of pressure, kinetic energy per volume, and potential energy per volume is constant along a streamline. The MCAT frames this as the Venturi effect and as the basis for measuring flow rates.

#### DC circuits

**Series circuits:** same current flows through every component; voltages across components add; total resistance is the sum of individual resistances.

**Parallel circuits:** same voltage across every branch; currents in branches add; total resistance is given by 1/R_total = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + ...

**RC circuits:** the time constant τ = R × C governs the charging and discharging of a capacitor. The MCAT asks how long it takes for the voltage to reach a fraction of its maximum (e.g., one time constant ≈ 63% of maximum).

#### Practice routine

- Take 5 static-equilibrium items. Draw a free-body diagram for each.
- Take 5 fluid items. Identify the fluid context and the relevant formula.
- Take 10 circuit items. Mix series and parallel configurations.
- Take 3 RC-circuit items. Compute the time constant and the voltage at a given time.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How this topic connects to the other Chem/Phys topics

- **Translational motion** provides the basis for the equilibrium concept (net force zero).
- **Acid-base and electrochemistry** use the same circuit concepts at the molecular level (galvanic cells, electrochemical gradients).
- **Spectrophotometry** uses the Beer-Lambert law, which is a fluid-optics application.

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"Static equilibrium means no forces."** Static equilibrium means no *net* force. Individual forces can be present, but they cancel.
- **"Pressure depends on the volume of fluid."** Pressure in a fluid depends on the depth, density, and gravity — not on the total volume.
- **"Voltage is the same throughout a parallel circuit."** Yes, but current is not. Voltage is the same; current divides among branches.

#### Exam technique

- Draw a free-body diagram or a circuit diagram for every problem.
- Identify the type of problem (equilibrium, fluid, circuit) before picking the formula.
- Solve algebraically before plugging in numbers.
- For RC circuits, use the exponential charging/discharging formulas: V(t) = V_max × (1 - e^(-t/τ)) for charging; V(t) = V_0 × e^(-t/τ) for discharging.
- Use the platform's calculator.
