# Charyapada and Early Bengali Literature

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Charyapada (চর্যাপদ) is the oldest surviving anthology of **Bangiya literature**, written between the 10th and 12th centuries CE by Buddhist **Sahajiya Siddhacharyyas** who used tantric-cum-lyric imagery to encode spiritual experience. The anthology was rediscovered in 1907 by **Haraprasad Shastri** from the Royal Library in Kathmandu, Nepal, and published in 1916. Only **23 padas** survive from an original corpus estimated between 51 and 64 songs.

- **Author school:** Buddhist Sahajiya siddhas — Luipada, Kanhapada, Sarahapada, Bhusukupada, Dombipada, Kukkuripada, Kanha-Shabara, Bhadrapada.
- **Language stage:** Old Bengali / Radhi dialect with Apabhramsha and sandhyabhasha features.
- **Genre:** Charyagiti — mystical songs sung by caryacharyas during tantric ritual.
- **Discovery year:** 1907 (manuscript); 1916 (Shastri's publication *Hajar Bacharer Purano Bangala Bhasay Bauddhagan O Doha*).
- **Opening line:** *"Kaha Sri Kanha kaha Sri Kanha kaha Sri Kanha kaha re…"* — attributed to Luipada.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"LKSB-D-KKB"** — the eight most-tested composers: **L**uipada, **K**anhapada, **S**arahapada, **B**husukupada, **D**ombipada, **K**ukkuripada, **K**anha-**S**habara, **B**hadrapada. Pair with **"1907 Nepal, 1916 Shastri"** for the discovery date lock-in.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Historical Position in Bangla Literature

Charyapada sits at the absolute origin of the Bangla literary tradition. It precedes Krittibas Ojha's Ramayana (15th century), the Mangalkavyas, and the Vaishnav padavali tradition by roughly three to five centuries. For DU Admission (Bangladesh) A-Unit Bangla, this means Charyapada is the **chapter-one text** of Bangla literature — examiners test whether you can place it chronologically, identify its religious school, and recall discovery facts.

#### Discovery and Publication Timeline

Haraprasad Shastri (1858–1931), a scholar of the Asiatic Society of Bengal, conducted his second Nepal expedition during 1898–1899 and located a palm-leaf manuscript at the **Durbar Library, Kathmandu**. He brought the manuscript back and published it in the *Journal of the Asiatic Society of Bengal* in **1916** as part of the volume *Hajar Bacharer Purano Bangala Bhasay Bauddhagan O Doha*. The 23 surviving padas are written on **8 palm leaves**, preserved in the National Archive, Kathmandu.

#### Religious and Philosophical Context

The composers were practitioners of **Sahajiya Buddhism** — a tantric school that valued *sahaja* (innate) experience over scriptural orthodoxy. Their padas use **cryptic erotic symbolism** to describe *mahamudra* and *sahaja yoga*: the female body represents *prajña* (wisdom), the male consort represents *upāya* (method), and their union signifies awakening. Readers must not take the surface imagery literally.

#### Linguistic Significance

Charyapada is the **earliest documented evidence** of Old Bengali grammar, vocabulary, and prosody. The Radhi dialect of West Bengal-Bihar region dominates, with Apabhramsha and Sanskrit overlays. Muhammad Shahidullah's 1944 critical edition established the standard text used in Bangladeshi universities today.

#### Key Facts Checklist

1. Original corpus: 51–64 padas; **23 survive**.
2. Discovery: 1907 manuscript, 1916 publication.
3. Place: Royal Library / Durbar Library, Kathmandu, Nepal.
4. Discoverer: Haraprasad Shastri (Bangla: হরপ্রসাদ শাস্ত্রী).
5. Publication name: *Hajar Bacharer Purano Bangala Bhasay Bauddhagan O Doha*.
6. Later editors: Suniti Kumar Chatterji (1933), Muhammad Shahidullah (1944).
7. Religious school: Buddhist Sahajiya, tantric, mahamudra tradition.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** "Charyapada kobita ke kobe, ke, ar kothay oihuj korechilen?" — Who discovered Charyapada, when, and where?

#### Solution:
- **Discoverer:** Haraprasad Shastri (হরপ্রসাদ শাস্ত্রী), scholar of the Asiatic Society of Bengal.
- **Time of manuscript recovery:** 1907, during his second Nepal expedition (1898–1899).
- **Place:** Durbar Library (Royal Library), Kathmandu, Nepal.
- **Publication year:** 1916, in the volume *Hajar Bacharer Purano Bangala Bhasay Bauddhagan O Doha*.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students frequently write **1909** as the discovery year (confusing it with Shastri's first Nepal trip) or attribute the find to Suniti Kumar Chatterji or Muhammad Shahidullah — they produced later **editions**, not the discovery. The correct lock-in: **Shastri discovered, Shahidullah edited for Bangladesh**.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Advanced Traps

1. The 24th pada known as **Dohakosa of Kanhapada** is sometimes numbered as a Charyapada but was found separately from the Nepal manuscript; treat it as a borderline case.
2. **Bhanubhakta's Caryapada** (Nepal) is a **Nepali** text and must never be confused with Bengali Charyapada — examiners exploit this transliteration overlap.
3. The erotic surface of padas (e.g., Luipada Pada 1) is **symbolic of mahamudra**, not sensual poetry — quoting lines literally loses marks on religious-context questions.
4. Kanhapada of Charyapada (12th-century Buddhist siddha) ≠ Chandidas Kanha (Vaishnav Sahajiya, 14th–15th century) — different authors, different traditions.
5. The 23 padas contain **no explicit Vaishnav or Nath sampradaya content**; attributing them to those schools costs marks on the religious-school MCQ.

#### Connections to Adjacent Topics

Charyapada feeds directly into three later streams: (a) **Baul songs** of Bengal, which inherit the sahaja-mystic vocabulary; (b) **Vaishnava Sahajiya padavali** of Chandidas, which translates tantric symbolism into Radha-Krishna idiom; (c) the **Buddhist song tradition** (siddhacharyya padavali) preserved in Tibetan and Nepalese manuscripts. For DU A-Unit Bangla, linking Charyapada to these descendants earns higher-order marks.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. Compare the discovery history of Charyapada with that of the **Kirti-Bamsa** or **Dobhashi** texts — what does the Nepal-manuscript route reveal about medieval Bangla literary geography?
2. Analyse how Muhammad Shahidullah's 1944 edition differed from Haraprasad Shastri's 1916 edition in terms of textual reconstruction and linguistic annotation.

> 💡 **High-Yield Revision Hook:** For any DU A-Unit question on "the origin of Bangla literature," answer in this order — **Charyapada → 10th–12th c. → Buddhist Sahajiya → 23 padas → 1907 Nepal → Haraprasad Shastri → 1916 publication**. This chain alone secures full marks.
