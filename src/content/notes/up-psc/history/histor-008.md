# World History and Post-Independence India

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The UPPSC PCS General Studies syllabus splits this cluster into **World History (18th–20th century)** and **Post-1947 India**, both weighted near 3% in Prelims and recurring in GS Mains. World History centres on five pivot points: the **French Revolution (1789)**, the **Industrial Revolution (Britain, c. 1760–1840)**, **World War I (1914–18)** and the **Treaty of Versailles (1919)**, **World War II (1939–45)** and the **UN Charter (1945)**, and the **Cold War** with the **Non-Aligned Movement (NAM, Belgrade 1961)**. Post-Independence India runs from the **Partition (1947)** and **Republic Day (26 Jan 1950)** through the **Five Year Plans (1951 onward)**, the **1962 Sino-Indian War**, the **1965 and 1971 Indo-Pak Wars**, **Bank Nationalisation (1969)**, the **Emergency (25 Jun 1975 – Mar 1977)**, **Mandal Commission (1990)**, and **Liberalisation (1991)**.

| Pillar | Key date / fact | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| French Revolution | 1789, Bastille stormed 14 Jul | Birth of modern democracy & nationalism |
| Industrial Revolution | Britain, c. 1760–1840 | Steam power, factory system, colonialism |
| World War I → Versailles | 1914–18 / 1919 | Treaty of Versailles humiliated Germany → WW2 cause |
| World War II → UN | 1939–45 / 1945 | UN Charter signed in San Francisco |
| NAM | Belgrade, 1961 | India-led; Bandung 1955 was different (Asian-African Conference) |
| Republic Day | 26 Jan 1950 | Constitution enacted (adopted 26 Nov 1949) |
| 1991 Liberalisation | Union Budget 1991 | LPG reforms under P.V. Narasimha Rao & Manmohan Singh |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"FIRE-CRANE-LPG-E"** — **F**rench Revolution → **I**ndustrial Revolution → **R**epublic (1950) → **E**mergency (1975) → **C**old War/NAM → **R**eforms (1991, **LPG**) → **A**greements (Panchsheel 1954) → **N**uclear (1974 Pokhran-I) → **G**reen/White Revolutions → **E**conomy (Five Year Plans 1st=1951, 12th=2012–17). Order by year, not by chapter, to beat statement-reordering MCQs.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### World History backbone
The **French Revolution of 1789** ended the *Ancien Régime*, transferred sovereignty to the people via the *Declaration of the Rights of Man*, and seeded the Napoleonic Wars that redrew Europe. Britain's **Industrial Revolution** (steam engine 1769, spinning jenny 1764, railways 1825) created imperial demand for raw materials and markets, driving 19th-century **Imperialism** in Asia and Africa.

**World War I** broke out after the assassination of **Archduke Franz Ferdinand** (28 Jun 1914) because of the rigid European alliance system (Triple Entente vs. Triple Alliance). The war killed ~20 million and ended with the **Treaty of Versailles (1919)**, whose **War Guilt Clause (Article 231)** and reparations fuelled German revanchism. The **League of Nations** (1920) failed because the US never joined and it lacked an army.

**World War II** (1939–45) stemmed from Versailles humiliation, the 1929 Great Depression, and the rise of fascism (Hitler, Mussolini, Tojo). The **UN Charter** was signed on 26 Jun 1945 in San Francisco and came into force on 24 Oct 1945, replacing the League. The post-war **Cold War** (1947–91) was a bipolar US-USSR standoff; **NAM**, founded at **Belgrade in 1961** by Nehru, Nasser, Tito, Sukarno and Nkrumah, gave the Third World a non-bloc platform.

#### Post-Independence India
**Partition (15 Aug 1947)** displaced ~15 million people. Sardar Patel integrated **565 princely states** via the **Instrument of Accession** before the **Republic Day** Constitution came into force on **26 Jan 1950**, drafted by the **Constituent Assembly** chaired by **B.R. Ambedkar** (Drafting Committee chairman). Salient features: federal with unitary bias, parliamentary system, fundamental rights (Part III), directive principles (Part IV), and an independent judiciary.

The **Planning Commission** (1950) launched the **First Five Year Plan (1951–56)** modelled on the Harrod-Domar model; the **12th Plan (2012–17)** was the last before the **NITI Aayog** replaced it in 2015. Foreign policy rested on **Panchsheel (1954)** with China and Non-Alignment. Wars reshaped territory: **1947–48 (J&K)**, **1962 (China — defeat)**, **1965 (Pakistan, Tashkent Accord)**, **1971 (Bangladesh liberation — Shimla Agreement 1972)**, and **Kargil 1999**.

#### Comparison matrix: easily confused pairs

| Confused pair | Correct distinction |
|---|---|
| Green Revolution vs. White Revolution | Green = wheat/rice (M.S. Swaminathan, 1960s–70s); White = **Operation Flood** (dairy, Verghese Kurien, 1970) |
| Bandung 1955 vs. NAM 1961 | Bandung = Asian-African Conference; NAM = Belgrade 1961 (Jawaharlal Nehru was a co-founder) |
| 1962 vs. 1965 vs. 1971 wars | 1962 = China (lost, NEFA/Aksai Chin); 1965 = Pakistan (Rann of Kutch, Kashmir); 1971 = Pakistan → Bangladesh liberation |
| Constitution adopted vs. enacted | Adopted **26 Nov 1949**; enacted (came into force) **26 Jan 1950** |
| Panchsheel vs. Five Year Plans | Panchsheel = Five Principles of Peaceful Coexistence (1954); Plans = economic planning instrument |
| 1991 reforms year | Announced in **1991 Union Budget** by Finance Minister **Manmohan Singh** under PM **P.V. Narasimha Rao** |

#### Key milestones in chronological order
1. 1947 — Partition; Instrument of Accession for princely states.
2. 1950 — Republic; Planning Commission; Article 370 special status for J&K.
3. 1951–56 — First Five Year Plan; 1969 — Bank nationalisation; 1971 — Abolition of privy purse.
4. 1974 — Pokhran-I (Smiling Buddha); 1975–77 — Emergency; 1990 — Mandal Commission implemented.
5. 1991 — LPG reforms; 1999 — Kargil; 2005 — RTI & MGNREGA; 2017 — GST.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem
**Question:** Consider the following statements:
1. The Non-Aligned Movement was founded at the Bandung Conference in 1955.
2. The Indian Constitution came into force on 26 November 1949.
3. The Treaty of Versailles contained a War Guilt Clause (Article 231) that humiliated Germany.
Which of the above are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 3 only
(c) 2 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

**Solution:** Statement 1 is wrong because NAM was founded at **Belgrade in 1961**, while Bandung 1955 was the Asian-African Conference. Statement 2 is wrong because the Constitution was *adopted* on **26 Nov 1949** but *enacted* (came into force) on **26 Jan 1950**. Statement 3 is correct — Article 231 assigned war guilt to Germany and justified reparations, a structural cause of WW2.

#### Correct answer: (b) 3 only.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Candidates see "1949" and "1955" repeatedly and mark them as correct out of familiarity. UPPSC specifically tests the **adopted-vs-enacted** split and **Bandung-vs-Belgrade** confusion. Always re-check the verb ("founded" vs "attended") and the year of legal commencement.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge cases, exceptions & traps

| Trap / Exception | What students wrongly believe | The precise fact |
|---|---|---|
| Mandal implementation date | Mandal was *implemented* in 1990 by V.P. Singh | Mandal Commission **report** submitted **1980**; implemented via **Mandal notification 1990** (13 Aug) |
| LPG trigger | LPG was triggered by 1991 Gulf War oil shock only | Triggered by **BoP crisis 1991** plus **Soviet collapse** and **Gulf War**; announced on **24 Jul 1991** Budget |
| Planning Commission vs. NITI Aayog | NITI Aayog = Planning Commission renamed | NITI Aayog was **set up on 1 Jan 2015** by executive resolution; it **replaced** (not renamed) the Commission; **no Five Year Plan** after the 12th |
| 1971 war outcome | "India captured East Pakistan permanently" | Resulted in **Bangladesh as a sovereign state**; **Shimla Agreement 1972** returned captured PoWs/territory |
| Emergency scope | "All fundamental rights suspended" | Only **Article 20** and **Article 21** were *non-suspendable*; **42nd Amendment, 1976** added words "Socialist", "Secular", "Integrity" to the Preamble |
| UP reorganisation | "Uttarakhand formed in 2001" | **UP Reorganisation Act, 2000** carved out **Uttaranchal (renamed Uttarakhand in 2007)**; state capital Dehradun, HC at Nainital |

#### Adjacent-topic links
- **World Wars → UN structure**: General Assembly + Security Council (P5 veto) + ICJ + Secretariat + Economic & Social Council.
- **Cold War → India's nuclear policy**: **Pokhran-I (1974)** was "peaceful nuclear explosion"; **Pokhran-II (1998)** under Vajpayee declared India a nuclear state; **CTBT not signed**, India follows **No First Use** doctrine (revised 2003).
- **Five Year Plans → sectoral priorities**: 1st Plan = agriculture; 2nd/3rd = heavy industry; 6th = poverty removal; 8th = liberalisation begins inside plan; 12th = "Faster, More Inclusive and Sustainable Growth".
- **Green/White Revolutions → food security**: Green Revolution used HYV seeds, fertilisers, irrigation (Punjab, Haryana); White Revolution (Operation Flood I, II, III) made India the **world's largest milk producer**.

#### UP-specific deep dive
**Uttar Pradesh** has the **largest Lok Sabha representation (80 seats)** and **largest state assembly (403 seats since 2000)**. Post-independence milestones: **State Reorganisation Act 1956** added the Hills area; **2000 Uttarakhand bifurcation**; **Lucknow Plan** (capital); movements such as the **Peasant Movement (Saharanpur, 1947–48)**, **Bhoodan (Vinoba Bhave)** and **Chipko (1973, Chamoli)** that shaped forest policy. Economic profile: agriculture dominant, **Bundelkhand** drought-prone, **Purvanchal** high poverty — relevant to **MGNREGA, PM-KISAN, and State Finance Commission** questions.

#### Advanced practice prompts
1. *Analytical:* "The Treaty of Versailles sowed the seeds of World War II." Discuss with reference to Article 231, reparations, and the failure of the League of Nations. (10 marks)
2. *Statement-based:* Distinguish between the objectives of the **First** and **Eighth** Five Year Plans. Why is the **12th Plan (2012–17)** historically unique in India's planning history? (15 marks)

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Long-answer questions pair a *cause* with a *consequence* (e.g., Versailles → WW2; LPG → IT boom). Half-marks are lost when students list causes without chronological linkage, or confuse "Privy Purse abolition 1971" with "Princely States integration 1947–48". Always state the **year, the act/policy, and the principal actor**.
