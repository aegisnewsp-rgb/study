# Pakistan's Society and Demographics

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Pakistan is the world's **5th most populous country** with a young population (median age ~22 years) and a high **demographic dividend** potential. The 2017 Census recorded ~207.77 million people; PBS projections push the figure past 240 million by 2024–25, growing at ~1.9–2.0% per year.

#### Core demographic rates

| Indicator | Formula | Approximate Pakistan value |
|---|---|---|
| Crude Birth Rate (CBR) | Live births ÷ mid-year population × 1000 | ~28–30 per 1000 |
| Crude Death Rate (CDR) | Deaths ÷ mid-year population × 1000 | ~7 per 1000 |
| Natural Growth Rate | CBR − CDR | ~21–22 per 1000 (~1.9%) |
| Total Fertility Rate (TFR) | Sum of ASFR across women aged 15–49 | ~3.3–3.5 children/woman |
| Infant Mortality Rate (IMR) | Infant deaths (<1 yr) ÷ live births × 1000 | ~50–55 per 1000 |

Punjabi is the most spoken first language; Urdu is the **national** but not the mother tongue of the majority. Muslims form ~96%; Hindus, Christians, Ahmadis, Sikhs, Parsis, Buddhists, Bahais and Kalash are minorities.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Population structure and census data

The **Pakistan Bureau of Statistics (PBS)** is the official compiler, conducting the decennial **Population & Housing Census** (last: 2017) and the continuous **Pakistan Demographic Survey (PDS)**. The 2017 Census put the national headcount at 207.77 million, making Pakistan rank **5th globally by population** but only 33rd–36th by area (≈796,095 km²).

Pakistan sits in the **mid-stage of demographic transition**: fertility is declining from earlier highs but TFR still averages 3.3–3.5, while mortality has fallen faster, producing a youthful **population pyramid** with a wide base. Median age stays around 22 years, which is why economists spotlight the **demographic dividend** — a large working-age cohort can accelerate growth if jobs and education keep pace.

#### Ethnic and linguistic composition

- **Major ethnic groups:** Punjabis, Pashtuns, Sindhis, Baloch, Muhajirs (Urdu-speaking post-1947 migrants mainly in Sindh/Karachi), Kashmiris, Seraikis, Hazara, Kalash, Burusho, Brahui.
- **Provincial share of population (Census 2017):** Punjab ~53%, Sindh ~24%, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa ~14.7%, Balochistan ~6%.
- **Languages:** Punjabi (most spoken mother tongue), Pashto, Sindhi, Saraiki (distinct from Punjabi), Balochi, Urdu (national), Brahui, Hindko, Kashmiri, Shina, Balti.

#### Religious, urban and literacy profile

Religiously, Muslims are ~96%, with Hindus the largest minority (~1.6–2%), followed by Christians, Ahmadis, Sikhs, Parsis, Buddhists, Bahais and the Kalash community in Chitral.

Urbanisation stands at ~37–38%; **largest cities** include Karachi, Lahore, Faisalabad, Rawalpindi, Multan, Peshawar, Quetta and Hyderabad. Literacy (10+ years) is roughly 60–63%, but a significant **gender gap** persists — male literacy is noticeably higher than female literacy, especially in rural Balochistan and parts of KPK.

#### Common exam traps

> **Trap:** Treating Muhajirs as a province-specific ethnic group. They are **post-1947 migrants** of mixed origin, mainly settled in urban Sindh (especially Karachi). Another classic trap: assuming Lahore is the capital of Sindh — Karachi belongs to Sindh.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Interpreting demographic indicators

Each rate answers a different exam question:

- **CBR** and **CDR** use the **total mid-year population** as denominator, giving crude (not age-adjusted) rates.
- **TFR** measures **children per woman** over a lifetime, computed by summing **Age-Specific Fertility Rates** for women 15–49. CBR alone cannot tell you completed family size.
- **IMR** uses **live births** (not total population) as denominator — crucial in child-survival policies.
- **Dependency ratio** = (population aged 0–14 + 65+) ÷ working-age (15–64). A high youth dependency explains Pakistan's education-sector pressure.

#### Worked numeric example

Suppose a district has a mid-year population of 1,000,000, 30,000 live births in a year and 7,000 deaths.

- CBR = (30,000 ÷ 1,000,000) × 1000 = **30 per 1000**
- CDR = (7,000 ÷ 1,000,000) × 1000 = **7 per 1000**
- Natural Growth Rate = 30 − 7 = **23 per 1000 (≈2.3% annually)**

#### Adjacent-topic links

Pakistan's demographics feed directly into **economy** (job creation, remittance corridor with Gulf states), **education** (low female literacy → lower TFR responsiveness), and **politics** (provincial seat allocation, NFC Award distribution, National Finance Commission awards). Demographic data also underpins UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDG 3 health, SDG 4 education, SDG 5 gender).

#### Common mistakes to avoid

- Confusing **CBR** (births per 1000 population) with **TFR** (children per woman).
- Mixing **Islamabad Capital Territory** with Rawalpindi district — they are administratively separate, even if geographically adjacent.
- Quoting **pre-2017 census** numbers (136 m in 1998) as current.
- Treating Saraiki and Punjabi as one language.
- Using **population rank (5th)** interchangeably with **area rank (33rd–36th)**.

#### Self-test prompts

1. Compute the Natural Growth Rate if CBR = 29 and CDR = 7 per 1000, and state how long (years) Pakistan would take to double its population at that rate using the Rule of 70.
2. List the four largest provinces by population share (Census 2017) and match each with its provincial capital and the dominant language spoken there.
