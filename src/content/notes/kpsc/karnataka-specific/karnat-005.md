# Karnataka Medieval History: Kalachakra, Jain/Buddhist Centres, Hoysala, and Vijayanagara

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your KPSC KAS Prelims.

Two empires dominate the 3% Karnataka-specific slice: the **Hoysalas** (c. 1026–1343 CE) and the **Vijayanagara** (1336–1646 CE). Between them sit the **Jain-Buddhist centres** at Shravanabelagola, Chandragiri, Kambadahalli and Kanheri caves, and the **Kalachakra Tantra** — the Buddhist tantric cycle linked to Mahayana/Vajrayana rock-cut traditions, distinct from the Kalachuri (Kalyani) political dynasty.

| Empire / Centre | Capital | Peak Ruler | Iconic Monument |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hoysala | Sosevur → Halebidu → Belur | Vishnuvardhana (1117 CE), Veera Ballala II | Chennakeshava (Belur), Hoysaleswara (Halebidu) |
| Vijayanagara | Hampi, Penukonda, Chandragiri | Krishna Deva Raya (1509–1529) | Vittala Stone Chariot, Hazara Rama |
| Jain Centre | Shravanabelagola, Karkala | Chavundaraya (983 CE) | 57-ft Gommateshwara monolith |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** "**S**ala **S**lays **T**iger, **B**oar **B**ecomes **E**mblem" — **S**ala slaying the tiger = **Hoysala** crest; **B**oar (Varaha) = Vijayanagara crest. Two animals, two empires.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Hoysala Architecture: Vesara Hybrid on Soapstone

The Hoysalas built in soapstone (chloritic schist), a soft stone that allowed **lathe-turned pillars** and dense wall reliefs impossible in granite. Their temples rest on stellate (star-shaped) plans — a star-shaped base, multiple shrines around a central shrine, and a Vesara shikhara (curvilinear tower) on a Dravida base. Three temples recur in KPSC questions:

- **Chennakeshava, Belur** — commissioned 1117 CE by Vishnuvardhana after his conversion from Jainism to Vaishnavism under Sri Ramanuja's influence; 240+ horizontal friezes of the Ramayana, Mahabharata and Bhagavata.
- **Hoysaleswara, Halebidu** — twin shrines to Shiva, 1121 CE under Narasimha I; the outer walls carry the most extensive sculptural programme in Karnataka.
- **Keshava, Somanathapura** — 1268 CE, built by Soma Dandanayaka under Narasimha III; the most complete stellate-plan temple surviving intact, now a UNESCO site.

> 📌 **Formula Check:** "Vesara" = a synthesis (Dravida base + Nagara shikhara). Hoysala architects added a third feature unique to them — **soapstone + lathe-turned polish**. Do not write "Nagara style" in the answer sheet; it costs the mark.

#### Vijayanagara: Foundation, Dynasties, and Administration

Harihara I and Bukka I (Sangama brothers) founded the empire in **1336 CE** at Hampi on the Tungabhadra. Vidyaranya, pontiff of the Sringeri Math, gave political and religious legitimacy — he did not "found" the empire. Four dynasties followed:

1. **Sangama (1336–1485 CE)** — Deva Raya I, II; Kampili campaign losses; recovery under Mallikarjuna.
2. **Saluva (1485–1505 CE)** — Saluva Narasimha; restored order after Sangama infighting.
3. **Tuluva (1505–1570 CE)** — peak under **Krishna Deva Raya** (1509–1529); the **Ashtadiggajas** (eight court poets: Allasani Peddana, Tenali Ramakrishna, Nandi Timmana, Madayyagari Mallana and four others) flourished here.
4. **Araveedu (1570–1646 CE)** — Rama Raya (regent from 1542) led the army to **Rakshasa-Tangadi (Talikota) 1565** against the Deccan Sultanate coalition; Hampi was sacked; the dynasty retreated to Penukonda, Chandragiri, and Vellore.

#### Worked Practice Item

**Question:** Match the monuments to the dynasties — (a) Vittala Stone Chariot, (b) Chennakeshava, Belur, (c) Gommateshwara 57-ft monolith, (d) Hazara Rama temple, (e) Keshava Somanathapura. Dynasties: Hoysala, Vijayanagara, Ganga (Jain patronage), Hoysala (under Narasimha III), Vijayanagara.

#### Solution:
- (a) Vittala Stone Chariot → **Vijayanagara** (Tuluva phase, mid-16th century, dedicated to Vittala/Vishnu as Sthalasayana Vishnu).
- (b) Chennakeshava, Belur → **Hoysala**, Vishnuvardhana, 1117 CE.
- (c) Gommateshwara 57-ft monolith → **Ganga** patronage, Chavundaraya, 983 CE.
- (d) Hazara Rama → **Vijayanagara**, royal worship shrine inside the Royal Enclosure at Hampi.
- (e) Keshava, Somanathapura → **Hoysala**, Soma Dandanayaka, 1268 CE.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** The Gommateshwara at Shravanabelagola is *Jain* and *Ganga-era*, not Hoysala. A second statue at **Karkala** (1432 CE, 42 ft) was raised under a different patron (Veera Pandya Bhairava) — both get asked, and the years and heights must match.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

#### Religious Layering: Why Jainism Survived Vijayanagara

Vijayanagara rulers were formally Srivaishnava (under Ramanuja's Vishishtadvaita tradition) but patronised Madhva Vaishnavism (after the 14th century), Veerashaiva Lingayats, and Smarta Brahmins. Jain centres at **Shravanabelagola**, **Mudabidri** (Saavira Kambada Basadi — "Thousand Pillars"), and **Karkala** received grants across Vijayanagara rule because (i) wealthy Jain merchant communities (especially the Baligas and Setthis of coastal Karnataka) financed temple repairs; (ii) no Vijayanagara ruler persecuted Jain maths systematically, unlike some Mughal-era Deccan courts; (iii) the Chandragiri hills near Shravanabelagola continued as a monastic university through the 17th century.

The Buddhist footprint is thinner in Karnataka than in coastal Maharashtra, but the **Kalachakra** link survives in three archaeological strands: rock-cut caves at **Badami** (6th-century Mahayana cells), **Kanheri** (Mahakali caves near Mumbai but patronised by Karnataka trade guilds), and Basti monastic records at **Sravasti-style viharas** at Chandravalli. The tantric calendar's 12-year cycle and the Vajrayana Kalachakra empowerment rituals reached the Deccan via trade with eastern India and the Pala-Sena cultural zone — not as a Karnataka political force.

#### Vijayanagara Administration: The Nayaka System

The empire's military backbone was the **Amara-Nayaka** (or Poligar) system. Land grants carried fixed military obligations: a Nayaka holding 100 *amaranayaka* villages owed the empire a stipulated cavalry contingent, payable from the assigned *Siddhaya* land assessment. The Nayaka could retain the surplus after fulfilling the obligation — a proto-mansabdari arrangement later copied by the Mughals. The **Rajya-pradhani** (chief minister) and the **Sutradhari** (imperial priest/scholar) flanked the king; below them sat the **Mahanadu** (royal council of ministers and military chiefs). Krishna Deva Raya's wars demonstrate this — his 1513 Kalinga campaign against the Gajapati used Nayaka contingents from the Tamil and Telugu marches, and his **1510 treaty with the Portuguese (Albuquerque at Goa)** protected the Calicut spice route.

#### Extended Practice Prompts

1. **Hoysala–Vijayanagara continuity:** Trace how the Nayaka class that administered Hoysala feudatories (the *Mahamandaleshwaras* under Veera Ballala II) became the Amara-Nayakas of Vijayanagara. Identify two specific families (e.g., the **Nayaks of Keladi**, the chiefs of **Barkur**) that crossed the 1343 political break.
2. **The 1565 defeat re-examined:** Why did the Deccan Sultanate coalition win at Rakshasa-Tangadi despite Rama Raya's numerical superiority? Discuss (a) defection of the Golconda commander, (b) the Telangana-Tungabhadra geography, (c) Vijayanagara's internal succession crisis after Achyuta Deva Raya's death.

> ⚠️ **Extended Trap:** Students write "Vijayanagara ended in 1565." The Araveedu dynasty continued at Penukonda (1570), then Chandragiri, then **Vellore until 1646**. If the question says "last Vijayanagara ruler," the safest verified answer is the **Raya of Chandragiri** displaced by the Bijapur Sultanate in 1646 — not Rama Raya (died 1565).
