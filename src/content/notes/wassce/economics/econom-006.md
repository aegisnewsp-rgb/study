# Cost Theory

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Cost theory** explains how a firm's expenses change as output (Q) expands in the **short run** (where some inputs are fixed) and the **long run** (where all inputs vary). Seven measures matter:

- **TFC** — Total Fixed Cost: rent, loan interest, salaried staff (constant as Q changes).
- **TVC** — Total Variable Cost: raw materials, hourly wages (rises with Q).
- **TC** = TFC + TVC.
- **AFC** = TFC / Q, **AVC** = TVC / Q, **AC** = TC / Q.
- **MC** = ΔTC / ΔQ (cost of the next unit).

Key shape facts: AFC slides downward forever; AVC and AC are U-shaped; the **MC curve cuts AVC and AC at their lowest points**. Shut down if P < AVC; **break-even when P = AC**, at Q = TFC / (P − AVC).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Short-run vs long-run

In the **short run**, at least one factor (typically capital such as plant or machinery) cannot be altered, so output is raised by adding more variable inputs (labour, raw materials) to the fixed stock. In the **long run**, every input is variable, the firm can resize the plant, and there are **no fixed costs** (TFC = 0, so TC = TVC).

#### Deriving the curves

Start from a **production function** Q = f(L, K̄). The **total cost schedule** is built by multiplying each input's quantity by its price and summing. Dividing TC, TFC and TVC by Q yields AC, AFC and AVC. The **MC** of the *n*th unit equals the extra cost of producing it, ΔTC / ΔQ, and is read off the slope of the TC curve.

#### Why the curves have their shapes

- **AFC always falls** because a constant TFC is divided by a larger Q.
- **AVC and AC first fall, then rise** because of the **law of diminishing marginal returns**: as more variable inputs meet a fixed input, each extra input adds less output, so its cost per unit first drops then climbs.
- **AC sits above AVC**, with the vertical gap equal to AFC; as Q rises, AC and AVC converge.
- **MC passes through the minimum points of AVC and AC** — a geometric result of MC being below an average when pulling it down and above it when pulling it up.

#### Worked schedule

| Q | TFC | TVC | TC | AFC | AVC | AC | MC |
|---|-----|-----|----|-----|-----|-----|-----|
| 0 | 100 | 0 | 100 | — | — | — | — |
| 1 | 100 | 60 | 160 | 100 | 60 | 160 | 60 |
| 2 | 100 | 100 | 200 | 50 | 50 | 100 | 40 |
| 3 | 100 | 150 | 250 | 33.3 | 50 | 83.3 | 50 |
| 4 | 100 | 220 | 320 | 25 | 55 | 80 | 70 |
| 5 | 100 | 320 | 420 | 20 | 64 | 84 | 100 |

Notice MC is lowest at Q = 2 (where AVC and AC bottom out) and AFC halves each time Q doubles.

#### Break-even and shut-down

Break-even output is where **Total Revenue = TC**, giving **Q = TFC / (P − AVC)**. A firm **shuts down** whenever price cannot cover AVC, because continuing produces a loss larger than TFC. These rules tie directly into supply analysis under perfect competition.

#### Common traps

Treating MC as the change in AC, drawing U-shapes by habit rather than from diminishing returns, and forgetting that in the long run TFC = 0 are mistakes the WASSCE examiner routinely exploits.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Long-run average cost and returns to scale

The **LRAC** is the *envelope* of all possible SRAC curves — each SRAC corresponds to a different plant size, and LRAC traces the cheapest SRAC for every output level. Its shape reveals **returns to scale**: the downward-sloping portion reflects **economies of scale** (bulk buying, specialisation, indivisibilities, lower long-run average costs as the firm grows); the flat section shows **constant returns**; the rising portion signals **diseconomies of scale** (managerial bottlenecks, coordination failures, rising input prices from excess demand). This long-run view must not be confused with the short-run **diminishing returns** caused by a fixed factor.

#### Edge cases and finer distinctions

- When Q is measured in discrete units, MC is read between rows; with continuous output, MC = dTC/dQ.
- AFC is mathematically asymptotic to the Q-axis — it never reaches zero but gets arbitrarily close.
- AC can equal AVC only in the long run when AFC = 0; in the short run AC > AVC always.
- Marginal cost is independent of fixed cost: doubling TFC leaves MC unchanged because MC depends on ΔTC from variable inputs.
- If AVC is constant (a rare linear TVC), MC equals AVC at every output.

#### Connections

Cost curves sit between the **production function** and the **revenue-side decisions** (supply under perfect competition, shutdown, entry/exit). They also feed into **revenue curves** (TR, AR, MR) that WASSCE often asks you to draw on the same axes.

#### Practice prompts

1. From a cost schedule where TFC = GH₵200, TVC at Q = 5 is GH₵300, and at Q = 6 is GH₵420, calculate MC, AVC and AC at Q = 6 and state whether producing the 6th unit raises or lowers AC.
2. With TFC = GH₵500 and price GH₵25 while AVC at the candidate output is GH₵15, compute the break-even output and explain whether the firm should continue if it can only sell 30 units at that price.
