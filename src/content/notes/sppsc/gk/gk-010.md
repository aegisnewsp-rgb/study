# Islamic Studies & Pakistan Movement

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your SPSC exam.

**Islamic Studies & Pakistan Movement** is the cluster of religious, ideological and political events that produced Pakistan as a homeland for Indian Muslims. It traces reform movements, Muslim political awakening and constitutional Islamisation.

- **Two-Nation Theory**: Hindus and Muslims are distinct nations — separate homeland needed.
- **Allama Iqbal's Allahabad Address (December 1930)** — first articulated a Muslim state idea.
- **Lahore Resolution (23 March 1940)** at Minto Park — formal demand for independent Muslim-majority states.
- **Objectives Resolution (12 March 1949)** — declared sovereignty belongs to Allah; basis of Islamic statehood.
- **1973 Constitution, Article 2** — declared **Islam the State Religion of Pakistan**.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Foundations: Reform and Awakening
The intellectual groundwork for Muslim political identity began with **Shah Waliullah** (18th-century revivalist) and **Syed Ahmad Shaheed** (Balakot movement, 1831). The **Aligarh Movement** led by **Sir Syed Ahmad Khan** (founder of MAO College, 1875) promoted modern education among Muslims without political confrontation. The Indian National Congress (1885) was seen as a Hindu-dominated body, prompting Muslim political organisation.

#### Two-Nation Theory and Muslim League
The **Two-Nation Theory** argues Hindus and Muslims are distinct nations rooted in religion, culture, history and civilisation, hence requiring separate homelands. Its articulation evolved through **Sir Syed Ahmad Khan, Allama Iqbal** and **Choudhry Rahmat Ali** (who coined "Pakistan" in 1933). The **All-India Muslim League**, founded on **30 December 1906 at Dhaka** under Nawab Salimullah, became the political vehicle of Muslim demands.

#### Key Phases (1920–1947)
- **Khilafat Movement (1919–24)**: pan-Islamic solidarity led by Maulana Muhammad Ali and Shaukat Ali.
- **Allama Iqbal's Allahabad Address (1930)**: prescribed a consolidated Muslim state within India.
- **Lahore Resolution (23 March 1940)**: the formal demand for independent Muslim-majority states.
- **Cabinet Mission (1946)**: failed to preserve a united India; Muslim League rejected the plan.
- **3rd June 1947 Plan (Mountbatten Plan)**: accepted partition; **Radcliffe Award** drew the boundary, and Pakistan emerged on **14 August 1947**.

#### Sindh's Distinct Contribution
SPSC frequently tests Sindh's role: **Sindh Madressatul Islam (founded 1885 by Khan Bahadur Ghulam Mustafa Bhurgri)** nurtured a nationalist Muslim intelligentsia. **G.M. Syed** championed Sindhi Muslim identity and later Sindhu Desh, while remaining a founding voice of the Pakistan Movement.

> **Exam trap**: The 1905 **Partition of Bengal** (Lord Curzon, reversed 1911) is *not* the Partition of India — don't confuse them.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Constitutional Islamisation Sequence
The Objectives Resolution (1949) under Liaquat Ali Khan established that sovereignty belongs to Allah and that Muslims shall be enabled to order their lives according to Islam. It was made substantive part of the Constitution through **Article 227 (1973)** as the Council of Islamic Ideology's guiding principle. **Article 2 of the 1973 Constitution** specifically declared **Islam as the State Religion** — students must distinguish this 1973 declaration from the 1949 Objectives Resolution, which only laid the philosophical foundation.

#### Common Mistakes
- Attributing the Two-Nation Theory solely to **Jinnah** — it was crystallised over decades by multiple scholars.
- Misplacing the **Khilafat Movement (1919–24)** after 1930 events.
- Confusing **Separate Electorates (1909 Morley-Minto Reforms)** with the Communal Award 1932.
- Forgetting **Sir Muhammad Iqbal** also framed the Muslim-majority provinces' economic restructuring argument.

#### Worked Micro-Example (Chain of Causation)
Iqbal's idea (1930) → Rahmat Ali's coinage (1933) → Muslim League's adoption (1940 Lahore Resolution) → Cabinet Mission failure (1946) → 3rd June Plan acceptance → Independence (14 Aug 1947) → Objectives Resolution (1949) → 1973 Constitution (Article 2).

#### Practice Prompts
1. Explain how Allama Iqbal's 1930 address differs from the 1940 Lahore Resolution in legal-political status.
2. Trace Sindh's contribution to the Pakistan Movement, naming at least two figures and one institution.

> **Scoring tip**: SPSC CCE typically awards one MCQ each on dates (1906, 1930, 1940, 1947, 1949) and one on constitutional articles — memorise the table in Lite tier.
