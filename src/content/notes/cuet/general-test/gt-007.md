# Polity

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Polity for CUET UG General Test covers the structure and working of the Indian Constitution — its parts, articles, schedules, amendments, institutions, and the rights and duties of citizens. The CUET General Test carries 60 questions to attempt in 60 minutes, and Indian Polity contributes roughly 6–10% of the attempted set, usually 4–6 direct fact-based MCQs.

| Term | One-line meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Preamble** | Opening statement of the Constitution; lists Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic, Justice, Liberty, Equality, Fraternity (Socialist, Secular, Integrity added by 42nd Amendment, 1976). |
| **Fundamental Rights** | Part III, Articles 12–35 — six justiciable rights enforceable by courts. |
| **DPSP** | Part IV, Articles 36–51 — non-justiciable, but guide the State in governance. |
| **Fundamental Duties** | Part IVA, Article 51A — 11 duties added by the 42nd Amendment, 1976. |
| **Amendment** | Procedure under Article 368 — three methods of ratification depending on subject. |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"SOSE-DR JELF"** — the Preamble keywords in order (Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic · Justice, Liberty, Equality, Fraternity). For the six Fundamental Rights, recall **"E-FREE-RC"**: Equality, Freedom, Religion (against exploitation = Free), Cultural/Educational, Remedies (Constitutional).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Constitutional Framework
The Constitution of India, adopted on 26 November 1949 and enforced on 26 January 1950, has **395 Articles** in 22 Parts and **12 Schedules**. The Preamble was inspired by the US (object clause), Ireland (DPSP), and France (liberty, equality, fraternity).

Article 32 (Right to Constitutional Remedies) was called the "heart and soul" of the Constitution by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar because it allows citizens to directly approach the Supreme Court for enforcement of Fundamental Rights through writs — habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, certiorari, and quo warranto.

#### Fundamental Rights vs DPSP vs Duties
The trio is the most repeated confusion zone in CUET MCQs. The matrix below resolves it.

| Feature | Fundamental Rights (Part III) | DPSP (Part IV) | Fundamental Duties (Part IVA) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Articles | 12–35 | 36–51 | 51A |
| Nature | Justiciable | Non-justiciable | Non-justiciable |
| Applies to | State + Citizens | State | Citizens only |
| Source | USA | Ireland | USSR (borrowed post-1976) |
| Count | 6 rights (originally 7; Right to Property removed by 44th Amendment 1978) | ~16 principles | 11 duties |
| Enforced by | Courts | Public opinion, judiciary via Article 21 spillover | Moral obligation |

Post-**Minerva Mills (1980)**, neither FRs nor DPSP are absolute — they must be balanced without destroying the basic structure.

#### Parliament and Executive
**Lok Sabha** has up to 543 elected members (delimitation-constituency based) plus 2 Anglo-Indian nominees (removed by the 104th Amendment 2020). Maximum strength is 552. **Rajya Sabha** has 238 members — 238 elected by State Legislative Assemblies using proportional representation with single transferable vote, plus 12 nominated by the President for arts/literature/science/social service. Its members serve 6-year terms with one-third retiring every 2 years; it is **not** a permanent house like the US Senate.

The **President** (Article 52) is the nominal head elected by an electoral college. Real executive power lies with the Prime Minister and Council of Ministers, who aid and advise the President. The 42nd Amendment (1976) made the advice **binding** (Article 74).

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem
**Question:** Which Article is known as the "heart and soul of the Constitution" because it guarantees the right to move the Supreme Court for enforcement of Fundamental Rights?
(a) Article 14   (b) Article 19   (c) Article 32   (d) Article 226

**Solution:** Article 32 is titled "Right to Constitutional Remedies" and lets a citizen directly approach the Supreme Court when a Fundamental Right is violated. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar called it the "heart and soul of the Constitution" because without a remedy, a right is meaningless. Article 226 gives High Courts similar writ power but is not the symbolic core. **Correct answer: (c).**

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students pick (d) Article 226 because both Articles 32 and 226 grant writ jurisdiction. The distinction is that Article 32 is restricted to Fundamental Rights, while Article 226 covers Fundamental Rights "and for any other purpose." Only Article 32 carries the "heart and soul" tag.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Constitutional Bodies and Their Anchors
Many CUET MCQs test which Article establishes which body. Internalise these pairings.

| Body | Article | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Election Commission of India | 324 | Conducts Lok Sabha, Rajya Sabha, Vidhan Sabha, President/Vice-President polls |
| CAG (Comptroller & Auditor General) | 148 | Audits Union and State accounts, reports to Parliament |
| UPSC (Union Public Service Commission) | 315 | Conducts central civil services examinations and recruitment |
| Finance Commission | 280 | Recommends tax devolution between Centre and States every 5 years |
| Attorney General | 76 | Chief legal advisor to the Government of India |
| NITI Aayog | — (Executive resolution 2015) | Replaced Planning Commission; advisory policy body |

#### Schedules of the Constitution
1. States and UTs with territories
2. Salaries, allowances, privileges of President, Governors, Speaker, Judges, CAG
3. Forms of Oaths and Affirmations
4. Allocation of seats in Rajya Sabha to States/UTs
5. Administration and control of Scheduled Areas
6. Administration of Scheduled Tribes
7. **Three Lists** — Union, State, Concurrent
8. **Languages** — originally 14; 21 by 21st Amendment (1967, Sindhi); 22 by 92nd Amendment (2003, Bodo, Dogri, Maithili, Santhali)
9. Protection of land reforms and agrarian legislation
10. Anti-defection law provisions (52nd Amendment)
11. Panchayats (73rd Amendment)
12. Municipalities (74th Amendment)

#### Critical Amendment Acts
| Amendment | Year | Landmark Change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1st | 1951 | Added 9th Schedule; reasonable restrictions on speech |
| 7th | 1956 | Reorganisation of states on linguistic basis |
| 42nd | 1976 | "Mini-Constitution"; added Socialist, Secular, Integrity; bound President's advice |
| 44th | 1978 | Restored many pre-42nd limits; removed Right to Property |
| 73rd | 1992 | Gave Panchayati Raj constitutional status (Part IX, 11th Schedule) |
| 74th | 1992 | Constitutional status to municipalities (Part IXA, 12th Schedule) |
| 86th | 2002 | Right to Education as Article 21A |
| 101st | 2017 | Goods and Services Tax (GST) |
| 102nd | 2018 | Constitutional status to NCBC (National Commission for Backward Classes) |
| 103rd | 2019 | 10% EWS reservation in education and government jobs |
| 104th | 2020 | Removed Anglo-Indian nomination in Lok Sabha and State Assemblies |
| 106th | 2023 | 33% reservation for women in Lok Sabha and State Assemblies |

#### Advanced Traps and Edge Cases
1. **Money Bill vs Ordinary Bill:** Money Bill (Article 110) originates only in Lok Sabha; Rajya Sabha can only recommend changes within 14 days. Speaker's certification is final.
2. **Article 356 (President's Rule):** Proclaimed in a State on breakdown of constitutional machinery. It must be approved by both Houses of Parliament within 2 months, and thereafter every 6 months; maximum duration is 3 years (post-44th Amendment, with conditions).
3. **Judicial Review vs Judicial Activism:** Review is the power to strike down unconstitutional laws; activism refers to creative interpretation, often through PILs.
4. **Single Citizenship:** India follows single citizenship (unlike the US dual system) — all citizens are citizens of India, not of any state.
5. **Three-tier Government:** Central, State, and Local (Panchayats and Municipalities). The 73rd and 74th Amendments converted the third tier from a statutory to a constitutional body.

#### Practice Prompts
1. The 104th Constitutional Amendment, 2020, removed the provision for nomination of how many Anglo-Indian members from the Lok Sabha, and from which other constitutional body? Answer: 2 from Lok Sabha; 1 from each State Legislative Assembly.
2. Which constitutional amendment inserted Article 21A guaranteeing the Right to Education for children aged 6–14 years, and in which year? Answer: 86th Amendment, 2002.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students often write that the 8th Schedule contains "22 languages including Hindi" — the count is correct, but the schedule does **not** declare Hindi the national language; it merely lists official languages of the Union. Avoid calling Hindi "national" in any CUET answer.
