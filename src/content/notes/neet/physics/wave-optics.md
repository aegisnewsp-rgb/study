# Wave Optics

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Wave optics** treats light as a transverse electromagnetic wave and explains interference, diffraction, and polarisation. The chapter contributes **3–4 NEET Physics questions (≈4% weight)** — almost all are formula-driven MCQs.

#### Must-know formulas

| Phenomenon | Formula | Variables |
|---|---|---|
| Young's fringe width | β = λD/d | λ = wavelength, D = slit-to-screen distance, d = slit separation |
| Single-slit central max width | β₀ = 2λD/a | a = slit width |
| Malus' law | I = I₀ cos²θ | θ = angle between polarisation axis and transmission axis |
| Brewster's law | tan i_B = μ | μ = refractive index of denser medium relative to rarer |
| Microscope RP | RP = 2n sinθ / λ | NA = n sinθ |
| Telescope RP | RP = D / 1.22λ | D = objective diameter |

> **Exam trap:** Single-slit width is **2λD/a**, not λD/a. Missing the factor of 2 is the most common NEET error here.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Huygens' construction and wavefronts
A **wavefront** is the locus of points oscillating in phase. **Huygens' principle** states that every point on a wavefront is a source of secondary wavelets; the new wavefront is the envelope of these wavelets travelling at the speed of light. This construction derives the laws of reflection and refraction, and sets up the explanation for diffraction.

#### Interference — Young's double slit
Light from a single source falls on two narrow, close slits separated by distance **d**. Because both beams originate from one source, they are **coherent** (constant phase difference), producing a stable fringe pattern on a screen at distance **D**.

- Constructive (bright): Δx = nλ, n = 0, ±1, ±2 …
- Destructive (dark): Δx = (2n − 1)λ/2, n = ±1, ±2 …
- **Fringe width: β = λD/d**

All bright fringes have equal intensity because the two slits are illuminated equally — a point students confuse with single-slit diffraction.

#### Diffraction at a single slit
When monochromatic light passes through a slit of width **a**, it spreads into a pattern with a bright central maximum flanked by dimmer secondary maxima. The **central maximum width is β₀ = 2λD/a**, twice as wide as any secondary band, and carries most of the incident energy.

#### Polarisation
A polaroid transmits only the electric-field component along its transmission axis. **Malus' law, I = I₀ cos²θ**, gives the transmitted intensity, where θ is measured from the polarisation direction to the axis (not from the normal). **Brewster's angle i_B** satisfies tan i_B = μ: at this incidence, the reflected ray is completely plane-polarised and lies perpendicular to the refracted ray.

> **NEET pattern:** 60% of questions test formula substitution in fringe width, Malus' law, or resolving power — keep these expressions on a single flashcard.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Resolving power and the diffraction limit
Resolving power is the ability to distinguish two close point objects as separate. For a circular aperture of diameter **D**, the Rayleigh criterion gives the **limit of resolution θ_min = 1.22λ/D**.

| Instrument | Resolving Power | Best increased by |
|---|---|---|
| Microscope | 2n sinθ / λ = 2(NA)/λ | Increasing refractive index n (oil immersion) or decreasing λ |
| Telescope | D / 1.22λ | Larger objective diameter D |

Notice the inverse wavelength dependence: smaller λ (blue/UV light, or electron beams) gives sharper resolution. This is why **electron microscopes outperform optical ones** and why astronomical telescopes use large mirrors.

#### Why diffraction makes sound "bend" but light "go straight"
Sound wavelengths are ~1 m, comparable to everyday obstacles — strong diffraction. Light wavelengths are ~5×10⁻⁷ m, orders of magnitude smaller than doors or windows, so diffraction is usually negligible and ray optics works.

#### Polarisation as transverse-wave evidence
Only transverse waves can be polarised. Longitudinal waves such as sound cannot show Malus' law or Brewster's angle — both phenomena therefore directly prove the **transverse nature of light**.

#### Common mistakes to avoid
- Writing single-slit width as λD/a (should be **2λD/a**).
- Mixing microscope RP (2NA/λ) with telescope RP (D/1.22λ).
- Measuring Malus' law θ from the normal instead of from the transmission axis.
- Forgetting that Young's two slits must come from **one source** to be coherent.

#### Practice prompts
1. In Young's experiment with λ = 600 nm, d = 0.2 mm, D = 1.5 m, find β. (Answer: 4.5 mm)
2. Unpolarised light of intensity I₀ passes through two polaroids with axes at 30°. What is the transmitted intensity? (Answer: I₀/2 × cos²30° = 3I₀/8)
