# Sense Organs and Effectors

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your NECO SSCE Biology exam.

- **Stimulus** is a change in the environment detected by a **receptor**.
- A **sense organ** contains receptors that convert stimuli into nerve impulses.
- The eye, ear, nose, tongue and skin detect light, sound, chemicals, touch, pressure, pain and temperature.
- The **reflex arc** is: **receptor → sensory neurone → CNS → motor neurone → effector**.
- A **sensory neurone** carries impulses from a receptor to the central nervous system.
- A **motor neurone** carries impulses from the CNS to a **muscle or gland**.
- Effectors respond by **muscle contraction** or **glandular secretion**.
- Reflex actions are rapid, automatic and protective; many are coordinated in the spinal cord.
- The retina forms a **real, inverted and diminished** image.
- Rods support dim-light vision, while cones support colour vision in bright light.
- The cochlea detects sound; the semicircular canals help maintain balance.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days or months before the examination.

#### Receptors, neurones and effectors

A **receptor** detects a specific stimulus and converts it into electrical nerve impulses. Receptors can be grouped by the energy they detect: **photoreceptors** respond to light, **mechanoreceptors** to pressure or vibration, **chemoreceptors** to dissolved chemicals, **thermoreceptors** to temperature changes, and **nociceptors** to tissue damage that produces pain.

Sensory neurones carry impulses from receptors towards the brain or spinal cord, which together form the **central nervous system (CNS)**. Motor neurones carry impulses away from the CNS. An **effector** is a muscle or gland that produces the response. Muscles contract, while glands release secretions such as saliva or sweat.

| Structure | Main role |
| --- | --- |
| Receptor | Detects a stimulus and initiates an impulse |
| Sensory neurone | Conducts impulses from receptor to CNS |
| CNS | Interprets sensory information and coordinates responses |
| Motor neurone | Conducts impulses from CNS to an effector |
| Effector | Produces contraction or secretion |

#### The reflex arc

A typical reflex arc follows this order:

1. **Receptor** detects a dangerous stimulus, such as excessive heat.
2. A **sensory neurone** carries the impulse to the spinal cord.
3. An **interneurone** links the sensory pathway to a motor neurone within the spinal cord.
4. A **motor neurone** carries the impulse to a muscle.
5. The **muscle** contracts and withdraws the body part from danger.

> **Scoring tip:** Always write the reflex pathway in the direction of the nerve impulse. The effector comes after the motor neurone.

Reflex actions are in most keys involuntary, rapid and protective. They can occur before conscious awareness because the spinal cord can coordinate the response. Voluntary actions, by contrast, are initiated consciously and involve the brain.

#### Eye and ear functions

In the eye, the cornea and lens refract light onto the retina. The iris adjusts the pupil to regulate light entering the eye. The lens changes shape during **accommodation** so that near or distant objects can be focused on the retina.

| Eye structure | Function |
| --- | --- |
| Cornea and lens | Refract light |
| Iris | Controls pupil size |
| Retina | Contains rods and cones |
| Optic nerve | Carries impulses to the brain |

The ear has two major functions. The outer ear collects sound, the tympanic membrane vibrates, and the middle-ear ossicles amplify the vibration. The cochlea converts sound vibrations into nerve impulses, while the semicircular canals detect head movement and assist balance.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Deeper coverage for mastery, diagram accuracy and examination practice.

#### Receptor specialisation

Receptor cells respond most effectively to an appropriate stimulus, described as their **adequate stimulus**. Photoreceptors contain light-sensitive pigments; mechanoreceptors respond to mechanical distortion; chemoreceptors detect chemical concentrations; thermoreceptors monitor temperature; and nociceptors signal potential or actual tissue injury.

Different receptors occupy distinct locations. Rods and cones occur in the retina. Taste receptors are organised in taste buds, mainly on the tongue. Olfactory receptors lie in the olfactory epithelium of the nasal lining. Skin receptors include **Meissner’s corpuscles** for light touch, **Pacinian corpuscles** for deep pressure, temperature-sensitive end bulbs, and free nerve endings associated with pain.

#### Neural control and common errors

Sensory information entering the CNS can produce either a voluntary response or a reflex response. A spinal reflex uses a shorter neural pathway, which shortens reaction time. However, sensory information is still transmitted to the brain, where the event can become consciously recognised.

| Common mistake | Correct interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| The receptor carries impulses to the effector | The receptor detects the stimulus |
| All reflexes reach the brain first | Many reflexes are coordinated in the spinal cord |
| The tongue detects smell | The nose contains olfactory receptors |
| The ear only detects sound | Its semicircular canals also detect movement for balance |
| Rods detect colour | Cones provide colour vision in bright light |

A classic example is touching a sharp object. Pain receptors generate impulses that travel through a sensory neurone to the spinal cord. A motor neurone then activates an effector muscle, causing withdrawal. The biceps muscle contracts, while the triceps relaxes, producing coordinated movement.

> **Diagram trap:** Draw the retina at the back of the eye and remember that its image is real, inverted and diminished. In an ear diagram, label the malleus, incus and stapes, and do not assign balance to the cochlea.

#### Examination strategy

For NECO SSCE Biology, the 3% nervous-coordination allocation commonly includes labelled diagrams of the eye or ear, functions of parts, reflex-arc sequences, and distinctions between receptors and effectors. Practice drawing arrows that show impulse direction, not merely anatomical order.

#### Practice prompts:

1. Trace the pathway from a receptor in the finger to the muscle that withdraws the hand from a hot object.
2. Explain how the iris, lens, rods and cones contribute to vision in bright and dim conditions.
