# Ratio, Proportion and Rates of Change — GCSE Mathematics (AQA 8300) Notes

The Ratio, Proportion and Rates of Change strand carries the second-largest mark weighting at higher tier and tests applied problem-solving more than any other strand. AQA assesses it across Paper 2 and Paper 3 (calculator) and as embedded contexts on Paper 1 (non-calculator). It rewards fluency with units, confidence with fractions and decimals, and the habit of converting to a common form before calculating.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Three conversion habits that prevent most mark losses

1. **Convert all quantities to the same unit before calculating.** A speed of 5 km in 30 minutes needs minutes converted to hours (or km to m) before dividing.
2. **Convert percentages to decimals before multiplying.** 15% of £80 = 0.15 × 80 = 12, not 15 × 80.
3. **Express ratios in the form 1 : n or n : 1 before scaling.** 6 : 9 simplifies to 2 : 3, then doubling gives 4 : 6, not 12 : 18.

#### Formulae to remember (higher tier)

- Speed = distance / time (with consistent units)
- Density = mass / volume
- Pressure = force / area
- Compound growth: final amount = initial × (1 + r)ⁿ for percentage growth, or × (1 − r)ⁿ for decay.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Ratio

**Ratio** compares two or more quantities. The order matters: 3 : 4 means "for every 3 of the first, there are 4 of the second". To find how to share £35 in the ratio 3 : 4, total parts = 7, so each part = £5, giving £15 : £20.

**Simplifying.** Divide every term by the highest common factor. 18 : 24 simplifies to 3 : 4. Three-part ratios (e.g., 2 : 3 : 5) follow the same rule: total parts = 10, each part = total / 10.

**Ratio in context.** "On a map the ratio of the map distance to the real distance is 1 : 25,000". A 4 cm line on the map represents 4 × 25,000 = 100,000 cm = 1 km in real life.

#### Proportion

Two quantities are in **direct proportion** if doubling one doubles the other: y = kx, where k is the constant of proportionality. To find k, divide one quantity by the other for any pair of values.

Two quantities are in **inverse proportion** if doubling one halves the other: y = k/x. The graph of y against x is a hyperbola.

#### Compound units

A **compound unit** combines two simple units, e.g., miles per hour, kilograms per cubic metre, pounds per square inch. To convert:

- **Speed:** divide the distance by the time. 240 km in 3 hours = 80 km/h.
- **Density:** divide mass by volume. 600 g of liquid filling a 250 cm³ container = 2.4 g/cm³.
- **Pressure:** divide force by area. 120 N on 0.5 m² = 240 Pa (N/m²).
- **Unit pricing:** divide cost by quantity. £4.50 for 1.5 kg = £3.00/kg.

Always check units are consistent before dividing: do not divide grams by kilograms, or minutes by hours, without converting first.

#### Conversion graphs

A **conversion graph** plots one unit on the horizontal axis and the equivalent on the vertical axis. Reading from the graph avoids arithmetic but introduces reading error; use it for estimates and cross-check.

#### Direct and inverse proportion

**Direct proportion graphs** are straight lines through the origin (y = kx). The gradient k is the constant of proportionality. The reciprocal of k converts in the opposite direction.

**Inverse proportion graphs** are hyperbolas (y = k/x). The graph never touches either axis; it gets closer as one variable approaches 0 or infinity.

#### Exponential growth and decay (higher tier)

An **exponential** relationship has the form y = a × b^x, where b is the growth factor. A 5% annual increase gives b = 1.05, so after n years the multiplier is 1.05ⁿ. Compound interest, population growth, and radioactive decay are all exponential.

For decay with half-life T, the formula is y = a × (0.5)^(t/T). To find T from a decay curve, read the time taken for any value to halve.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where this strand is tested

- **Paper 2 and Paper 3 (calculator):** all ratio, proportion and rate-of-change questions.
- **Paper 1 (non-calculator):** simple ratio, percentage change, and unit conversions without a calculator.

#### Worked pattern — ratio problem with shared costs

Three friends split the cost of a meal in the ratio 2 : 3 : 4. The total is £81.

- Total parts = 2 + 3 + 4 = 9.
- Each part = £81 / 9 = £9.
- Shares: 2 × 9 = £18, 3 × 9 = £27, 4 × 9 = £36.
- Check: 18 + 27 + 36 = 81 ✓.

#### Worked pattern — compound percentage change

A house is valued at £250,000 and rises 4% per year for 3 years.

- Year 1: 250,000 × 1.04 = 260,000.
- Year 2: 260,000 × 1.04 = 270,400.
- Year 3: 270,400 × 1.04 = 281,216.
- Shortcut: 250,000 × 1.04³ = 281,216.

A 4% rise followed by a 4% fall does NOT return to the original. The fall multiplier is 0.96, the total is × 1.04 × 0.96 = × 0.9984, so the value is 0.16% lower than the start.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Percentage increase and decrease can be added." 10% increase + 10% decrease is not 0%, it is a 1% net decrease.
- "Ratio order does not matter." 3 : 4 means "3 of the first, 4 of the second". Reversing it changes the share.
- "Speed and velocity are the same." Speed is a scalar (magnitude only); velocity is a vector (magnitude and direction).
- "Inverse proportion graphs pass through the origin." They do not — both branches approach but never touch either axis.

#### Exam technique

- Write the units in every answer. The mark scheme penalises "3.2" when the question asked for £3.20 or 3.2 m/s.
- Convert compound units before calculating, not after. A 3-mark unit-conversion mistake at the start costs 3 marks at the end.
- For exponential questions, decide whether the multiplier is (1 + r) or (1 − r) before plugging in. A growth rate of 5% uses 1.05; a decay rate of 5% uses 0.95.
