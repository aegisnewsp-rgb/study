# Cell

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> The one fact that decides 80% of NEET Cell questions is this: mitochondria and chloroplasts carry 70S ribosomes, not 80S.

The **cell** is the smallest unit that qualifies as living — bounded by a plasma membrane, carrying DNA, and able to metabolise, grow, divide, and respond. **Cell theory** credits Schleiden (1838, plants) and Schwann (1839, animals); Virchow (1855) added *Omnis cellula e cellula*. **Prokaryotes** (bacteria, PPLO/Mycoplasma, cyanobacteria) have a nucleoid, 70S ribosomes, and no membrane-bound organelles. **Eukaryotes** have a true nucleus, 80S cytoplasmic ribosomes, and organelles.

| Feature | Prokaryote | Eukaryote |
|---|---|---|
| Nucleus | Nucleoid (no envelope) | True nucleus with envelope |
| Ribosome | 70S | 80S (70S in mitochondria/chloroplasts) |
| Organelles | Absent (mesosomes instead) | Present |
| Cell wall | Peptidoglycan | Cellulose (plant) or absent (animal) |

> 💡 **Memory Hook:** "**70S where endosymbiosis happened, 80S everywhere else in eukaryotes**" — mitochondria and chloroplasts keep bacterial-style ribosomes because they descended from engulfed bacteria.

**Plant vs animal cell — quick differentiator:** plant cells have a cellulose **cell wall**, a large central **vacuole**, **plastids**, and no centrioles (in angiosperms). Animal cells carry centrioles and lysosomes.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Organelle function tables and the Schleiden/Schwann/Virchow trio are where most of the NEET marks from this chapter come from — learn them as statements, not paragraphs.

#### Cell Theory — Who Said What

- **Schleiden (1838):** all plants are made of cells.
- **Schwann (1839):** all animals are made of cells.
- **Virchow (1855):** all cells arise from pre-existing cells (*Omnis cellula e cellula*).
- **Modern cell theory** adds: energy flux, biochemical similarity, and DNA as the hereditary material.

#### Plasma Membrane — Fluid Mosaic Model

Singer and Nicolson (1972) proposed the **fluid mosaic model**: a phospholipid bilayer with integral and peripheral proteins, cholesterol wedges (in animal cells), and surface **glycoproteins/glycolipids** for cell recognition. Selectively permeable. Carbohydrate chains project outward and act as antigens and receptors.

#### Endomembrane System

- **Rough ER:** ribosome-studded; synthesises and transports secretory and membrane proteins.
- **Smooth ER:** lipid synthesis, steroid manufacture, detoxification, Ca²⁺ storage.
- **Golgi apparatus:** receives vesicles from ER at the *cis* face, modifies cargo (glycosylation), sorts and ships from the *trans* face. Packages rER-derived hydrolytic enzymes into **primary lysosomes**.

#### Plastids (Plant-Specific)

| Type | Pigment | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Chloroplast | Chlorophyll a, b, carotenoids | Photosynthesis (thylakoids = light reactions; stroma = Calvin cycle) |
| Chromoplast | Carotene, xanthophyll | Yellow/orange/red colour in flowers and fruits |
| Leucoplast (amyloplast, elaioplast, aleuroplast) | Colourless | Starch, oil, protein storage |

Double-membrane, circular DNA, 70S ribosomes — same endosymbiotic signature as mitochondria.

#### Nucleus

**Nuclear envelope** is a double membrane interrupted by **pores**; perinuclear space lies between the two membranes. Inside, **chromatin** (DNA + histones) condenses into chromosomes at division. The **nucleolus** is the site of rRNA transcription and ribosome subunit assembly.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix

| Confusion zone | Correct answer |
|---|---|
| Who said *Omnis cellula e cellula*? | Rudolf Virchow, not Schleiden |
| PPLO/Mycoplasma — cell wall present? | No — wall-less; smallest living cells (0.1–0.3 μm) |
| Lysosomes in plant cells? | Absent in mature plant cells; hydrolytic work done by vacuole + spherosomes |
| Centrioles in angiosperm cells? | Absent; present in lower plants (algae, bryophytes, pteridophytes) |
| Where do 70S ribosomes sit in eukaryotes? | Mitochondria and chloroplasts (evidence for endosymbiosis) |

#### Worked Exam-Style Problem

#### Statement set (typical NEET assertion–reason):
- *Assertion:* Mitochondria contain 70S ribosomes.
- *Reason:* Mitochondria are believed to have originated from an engulfed aerobic bacterium.

Pick the correct option, justify it, and note the trap.

**Resolution:** Both statements are factually true. The 70S ribosome (in chloroplasts too) is a direct molecular fossil of bacterial ancestry, supporting the **endosymbiotic theory** (Lynn Margulis). The expected answer is "Assertion and Reason are both true, and the Reason correctly explains the Assertion."

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students who memorise "all eukaryotic ribosomes are 80S" mark the Assertion false and pick the wrong option. The ribosome inside mitochondria and chloroplasts is 70S — write this down before you sit the paper.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Two non-obvious numbers govern almost every microscope question: resolution of light microscope ≈ 0.2 μm, resolution of electron microscope ≈ 0.2 nm.

#### Microscopy and Magnification

**Magnification** is dimensionless: M = image size / object size, e.g. a 1.5 mm image of a 1 μm cell gives M = 1500×. **Resolution** (d) is the smallest distance two points can be distinguished: d = 0.61 λ / (n sin α), where λ is wavelength, n is refractive index, and α is the half-angle of the cone of light. Shorter wavelength → higher resolution. That is why electron beams (≈ 0.005 nm) beat visible light (≈ 550 nm) by a factor of about 1000.

#### Surface-Area-to-Volume Constraint

For a sphere of radius r, SA/V = 3/r (per micrometre). As a cell grows, volume scales with r³ while membrane surface scales with r². Above ~100 μm in diameter, diffusion and membrane transport cannot keep up — that sets the upper limit on most eukaryotic cell size.

#### Edge-Case Traps

1. **Chromatin vs chromosome:** chromatin is the dispersed interphase form (euchromatin = transcriptionally active, heterochromatin = condensed and silent); chromosome is the condensed mitotic/meiotic form. DNA amount per cell is constant for a given ploidy.
2. **Mycoplasma quirks:** smallest living cells (0.1–0.3 μm), wall-less, used in filter-sterilisation studies, cause atypical pneumonia, and were the workhorse for Beadle–Tatum's one-gene-one-enzyme experiments because they need cholesterol from the medium.
3. **Peroxisome vs lysosome:** both degrade substrates, but peroxisomes use **catalase** to split H₂O₂ and operate at physiological pH; lysosomal hydrolases need acidic pH (~5).
4. **Plasmodesmata:** cytoplasmic bridges through cell walls, lined by plasma membrane, with a desmotubule (ER-derived) running through the centre — enable symplastic transport in plants.
5. **Chloroplast compartments:** outer membrane (permeable), intermembrane space, inner membrane (selective), stroma (Calvin cycle enzymes), thylakoid lumen (proton gradient), thylakoid membrane (light reactions, ATP synthase, Photosystems I and II).

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. A 30 μm-diameter spherical cell has SA/V = 0.2 per μm. Predict what happens to nutrient flux if the cell grows to 300 μm without dividing, and identify the organelle that would become limiting.
2. PPLO are placed in the "prokaryote" list in NCERT yet they lack a cell wall. Justify their inclusion and explain why their ribosomes still read as 70S.
