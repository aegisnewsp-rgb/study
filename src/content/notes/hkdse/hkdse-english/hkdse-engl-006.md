# Assessment Objectives Across All Papers — HKDSE English Language Notes

The HKDSE English Language examination tests seven cross-cutting skill areas across its four papers plus the School-based Assessment. The seven skill areas — published in the HKDSE English Language Assessment Framework — cover reading comprehension, vocabulary in context, interpretation of tone and mood, evaluation of views and arguments, understanding of language features, and the integration of information from multiple sources. This topic compiles the seven skill areas into a single reference so that a student can audit their coverage across the whole examination.

> Verify the live assessment objectives on https://www.hkeaa.edu.hk/ before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **The seven skill areas** are: (1) understanding and interpreting purpose and meaning; (2) identifying the main theme and key details; (3) identifying the contextual meaning of words and phrases; (4) interpreting the tone and mood of a writer or speaker; (5) distinguishing and evaluating views, attitudes and arguments; (6) understanding the use of a range of language features; (7) interpreting, analysing, selecting and organising ideas and information from various sources.
- **Distribution across papers**: each of the seven skill areas is tested in at least two of the four papers, with some overlap.
- **SBA**: the SBA reading/viewing programme practises all seven skills at speaking level.

#### Examiner traps

- Treating the skill areas as separate — they overlap, and a strong answer demonstrates several at once.
- Practising only the skill areas tested in the easiest paper — the cross-paper design means each paper tests a different mix.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### The seven skill areas in detail

**1. Understanding and interpreting purpose and meaning.** What is the text trying to do? What is the writer's or speaker's intent? Identify the text type (article, letter, report, review, advertisement) and the function (inform, persuade, entertain, instruct).

**2. Identifying the main theme and key details.** What is the text about? Identify the central idea (theme) and the most important supporting details. The main theme is often stated explicitly; sometimes it is implied.

**3. Identifying the contextual meaning of words and phrases.** What does this word mean in this context? Use surrounding sentences and the broader text to disambiguate. This is a common Paper 1 and Paper 3 question type.

**4. Interpreting the tone and mood of a writer or speaker.** What attitude does the writer/speaker convey? Identify whether the tone is formal/informal, serious/humorous, optimistic/pessimistic, sympathetic/critical. The mood is the emotional atmosphere the text creates for the reader.

**5. Distinguishing and evaluating views, attitudes and arguments in fairly complex texts.** Identify the writer's or speaker's view; distinguish strong from weak evidence; recognise bias, exaggeration, understatement; recognise persuasive techniques (appeal to authority, appeal to emotion, bandwagon, false dichotomy).

**6. Understanding the use of a range of language features in fairly complex texts.** Identify literary devices (metaphor, simile, personification, alliteration), rhetorical devices (rhetorical question, repetition, parallelism), and language features (formal/informal register, jargon, dialect). Explain how each feature contributes to the text's meaning or effect.

**7. Interpreting, analysing, selecting and organising ideas and information from various sources.** In integrated tasks (Paper 3 Part B), select relevant details from a spoken source and a written source, combine them, and produce a short text. This skill is also tested in SBA.

#### Why this matters for revision

The cross-paper design means a candidate who drills only Paper 1 past papers misses the integration skill (skill 7), which is unique to Paper 3 Part B. A candidate who drills only Writing misses the tone-and-mood and language-features skills, which are tested in Paper 1 short-response items.

The efficient approach is to identify the skill area each practice question tests, then to revise the corresponding sub-skill. A weak run on "vocabulary in context" items across Paper 1 and Paper 3 points to a vocabulary gap; a weak run on "evaluating arguments" items points to a critical-reading gap.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-paper links

- **Reading (Paper 1)** + **Listening (Paper 3)**: same skill set tested through two channels. Practice both channels.
- **Writing (Paper 2)**: the audience awareness, register, argument and language-feature skills underpin Paper 2 Part B.
- **Speaking (Paper 4)**: tone and mood, argument and language features are tested in Part B.
- **SBA**: the SBA reading/viewing programme is the venue to practise all seven skills at speaking level.

#### Exam technique

- **For each practice paper item, label the skill area being tested.** Build a skill-by-skill profile across your practice papers.
- **For skill areas that are weak, drill the sub-skill**: e.g. for vocabulary-in-context, read extensively and practise guessing unknown words from context.
- **For Paper 3 Part B integration tasks, explicitly label each source** (Spoken / Written) and the contribution of each to the integrated text.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Each skill area is tested on one paper only." No — each skill area is tested across at least two papers.
- "Strong English at school means strong HKDSE performance." The seven skill areas are the explicit marking criteria; practice against them, not against general English fluency.
- "SBA is separate from the public exam." No — SBA contributes 15% of the subject mark and is integrated with the public exam through the seven skill areas.
