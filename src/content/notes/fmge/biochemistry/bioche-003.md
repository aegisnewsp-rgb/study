# Protein Chemistry

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your FMGE Biochemistry paper.

Proteins are biological macromolecules built from **L-α-amino acids** joined by **peptide (amide) bonds** between the α-carboxyl of one residue and the α-amino of the next, releasing one water molecule per bond. Twenty standard residues are encoded by the genetic code and are classified by R-group as nonpolar, polar uncharged, acidic, or basic. Organisational levels run primary → secondary (α-helix, β-pleated sheet) → tertiary → quaternary. **Denaturation** disrupts 2°/3°/4° structure but leaves peptide bonds and the primary sequence intact.

- **Peptide bonds in a linear chain = n − 1** (n = number of residues).
- **Protein (g) = Nitrogen (g) × 6.25** (proteins average 16% N by mass).
- **Biuret = peptide bonds (violet, 540 nm); ninhydrin = free α-amino groups (Ruhemann's purple, 570 nm).**
- Essential amino acids mnemonic: **HILL MVT PPT** (His, Ile, Leu, Lys, Met, Val, Thr, Trp, Phe).
- Normal serum: total protein **6–8 g/dL**, albumin **3.5–5.0 g/dL**, A:G ≈ **1.2:1** (reverses in cirrhosis).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students working through Biochemistry over weeks to months.

#### Amino Acid Backbone and Peptide Bond

Each standard amino acid shares the general formula **H₂N–CHR–COOH** in the unionised form; at physiological pH it exists as a **zwitterion** (–NH₃⁺ and –COO⁻ on the same carbon). The α-carboxyl of residue (n) condenses with the α-amino of residue (n+1), eliminating water (18.015 g/mol) and forming an **amide (peptide) bond** that is planar due to partial double-bond character from resonance.

For a single polypeptide chain: **peptide bonds = n − 1**; a protein with m chains has **n − m** peptide bonds. Residue mass = free amino acid mass − 18.015 g/mol.

#### Classification and Nutrition

R-groups define four chemical families: nonpolar/hydrophobic (Ala, Val, Leu, Ile, Met, Phe, Trp, Pro, Gly), polar uncharged (Ser, Thr, Cys, Tyr, Asn, Gln), acidic (Asp, Glu; negatively charged at pH 7), basic (Lys, Arg, His; positively charged). **Nine amino acids are essential** (His, Ile, Leu, Lys, Met, Phe, Thr, Trp, Val); tyrosine, cysteine, arginine, glutamine, glycine and proline are conditionally essential.

#### Levels of Structure

| Level | Stabilised by | Example / feature |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary | Covalent peptide bonds | Sequence of residues |
| Secondary | Backbone H-bonds | α-helix (3.6 residues/turn, i→i+4); β-pleated sheet (parallel / antiparallel) |
| Tertiary | Hydrophobic core, disulphide bridges, ionic, H-bonds, vdW | Myoglobin fold |
| Quaternary | Subunit interfaces | Haemoglobin tetramer (2α + 2β) |

#### Plasma Proteins and Colour Reactions

Albumin (~60% of total plasma protein) maintains **oncotic pressure**; α/β globulins transport lipids, hormones and metals; γ globulins are immunoglobulins; fibrinogen enables clotting. Total protein **6–8 g/dL**, albumin **3.5–5.0 g/dL**, A:G ≈ **1.2:1** (inverts in cirrhosis).

- **Biuret** — Cu²⁺ with peptide bonds → violet at **540 nm**; standard protein quantitation.
- **Ninhydrin** — free α-amino groups → Ruhemann's purple at **570 nm** (Pro gives yellow at 440 nm).
- **Xanthoproteic** — HNO₃ on Tyr/Trp/Phe → yellow; **Millon** — Tyr → red; **Hopkins–Cole** — Trp → violet; **Sakaguchi** — Arg → red; **Pauly** — His → orange (diazotised sulphanilic acid).

#### Quantitative Methods

| Method | Principle | Sensitivity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kjeldahl | Total N × 6.25 | Crude; reference method |
| Biuret | Cu²⁺–peptide bond complex, 540 nm | Low |
| Lowry | Cu²⁺ + Folin–Ciocalteu (Tyr/Trp) | Moderate |
| Bradford | Coomassie G-250 dye binding | High (~5× Lowry) |
| UV A₂₈₀ | Trp/Tyr absorbance (Beer–Lambert, ε·l·c) | Non-destructive |

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for in-depth revision and clinical linkage.

#### Denaturation vs Hydrolysis

Denaturation unfolds 2°/3°/4° structure by disrupting non-covalent interactions and disulphide bridges; the **primary sequence (peptide bonds) is preserved**, so the process can be reversible (ribonuclease refolding) or irreversible (egg-white coagulation, fried egg). Agents include heat, urea, guanidinium chloride, SDS, heavy metals (Hg²⁺, Pb²⁺) and extreme pH. **Hydrolysis** is different: it cleaves peptide bonds using acid, base or proteases (trypsin cleaves after Lys/Arg; chymotrypsin after aromatic residues).

#### Electrophoresis and the SDS Caveat

**SDS-PAGE** coats proteins with uniform negative charge, so mobility depends almost entirely on size (length of chain), giving the **denatured subunit mass**, not the native oligomeric mass. For native MW or activity assays, run **native PAGE** or use size-exclusion chromatography. Plasma protein electrophoresis separates albumin, α₁, α₂, β and γ bands — the **M-spike** in the γ region suggests monoclonal gammopathy (multiple myeloma).

#### Amino Acid Titration and pI

A neutral amino acid (e.g. Ala) titrates with two pKₐ values (α-COOH ≈ 2.0 and α-NH₃⁺ ≈ 9.7). **pI = (pKₐ₁ + pKₐ₂)/2** — at pI the zwitterion carries net zero charge and does not migrate in an electric field (used in isoelectric focusing). For acidic residues (Asp, Glu) and basic residues (Lys, Arg, His), additional R-group pKₐ values enter the average.

#### Conjugated Proteins and Clinical Correlates

Simple proteins yield only amino acids on hydrolysis; **conjugated proteins** carry a prosthetic group: glycoprotein (mucins, antibodies), lipoprotein (LDL/HDL), nucleoprotein (chromatin), metalloprotein (haemoglobin, ceruloplasmin), phosphoprotein (casein), chromoprotein (haem, retinal-binding opsin). Total protein rises in chronic inflammation and multiple myeloma, and falls in nephrotic syndrome, liver disease and protein-losing enteropathy; the A:G ratio inverts in cirrhosis because **albumin synthesis falls while globulins rise**.

#### Common Mistakes and Practice Prompts

- Forgetting that **SDS denatures** proteins, so SDS-PAGE MW ≠ native oligomeric MW.
- Quoting factor 6.25 for everything: milk ≈ **6.38**, wheat ≈ **5.7**, meat ≈ **6.25**.
- Stating ninhydrin measures peptide bonds — it reacts with **free α-amino groups** only.
- Reversing the A:G direction in cirrhosis (ratio falls **below 1**, not above).

#### Practice prompts:

1. A cirrhotic patient has total protein 6.8 g/dL, albumin 2.4 g/dL, globulin 4.4 g/dL. Calculate A:G and interpret.
2. Why does a Bradford assay underestimate a protein rich in lysine but rich in tryptophan?
