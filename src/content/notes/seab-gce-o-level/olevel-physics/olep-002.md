# Section II: Newtonian Mechanics — O-Level Pure Physics (6091) Notes

Section II (Newtonian Mechanics) is the largest single section in the O-Level Pure Physics 6091 syllabus by marks. It covers six topics: Kinematics, Dynamics, Mass/Weight/Density, Turning Effect of Forces, Pressure, and Energy/Work/Power. A Sec 4 student who is fluent in Section II will find that the highest-load structured questions on Paper 2 become accessible. This section is also the foundation for the mechanics parts of H2 Physics at A-Level.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### The seven facts every Sec 4 student needs

1. **Speed**: distance divided by time. Unit: m/s (or km/h).
2. **Acceleration**: rate of change of velocity. Unit: m/s².
3. **Newton's three laws**: an object stays at rest or moves with constant velocity unless acted on by a net force (1st); force = mass × acceleration (2nd); every action has an equal and opposite reaction (3rd).
4. **Mass**: the amount of matter in an object (kg). Weight: the gravitational force on an object (N). Weight = mass × gravitational field strength.
5. **Density**: mass per unit volume (kg/m³ or g/cm³).
6. **Moment of a force**: force × perpendicular distance from the pivot (Nm).
7. **Work, energy and power**: work done = force × distance (J); power = work done / time taken (W); energy = ability to do work (J).

#### Key equations

- v = u + at
- s = ut + ½ at²
- v² = u² + 2as
- F = ma
- W = mg (where g ≈ 10 m/s² on Earth)
- ρ = m / V
- Moment = F × d
- W = Fs (work done)
- P = W / t (power)

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Kinematics

Kinematics is the description of motion without reference to its causes. Three quantities define motion: displacement (s), velocity (v) and acceleration (a). At O-Level, motion is usually along a straight line.

**Uniform motion** (constant velocity): s = vt. The distance-time graph is a straight line through the origin (or with a positive intercept if motion started from a non-zero position).

**Uniformly accelerated motion** (constant acceleration): four equations relate s, u, v, a and t.

- v = u + at (final velocity = initial velocity + acceleration × time)
- s = ut + ½ at² (displacement = initial velocity × time + ½ × acceleration × time²)
- v² = u² + 2as (links v, u, a, s without t)
- s = ½ (u + v) t (links s, u, v, t without a)

The velocity-time graph for uniform acceleration is a straight line; the displacement is the area under the line. For motion with changing acceleration, the area under the velocity-time graph still gives the displacement.

**Free fall**: near the Earth's surface, all objects accelerate downward at g ≈ 10 m/s² (a common simplification at O-Level). Air resistance is ignored unless the question specifies it.

#### Dynamics

Newton's three laws:

1. **First law (inertia)**: an object stays at rest, or moves with constant velocity, unless acted on by a net external force.
2. **Second law**: the net force on an object is equal to the mass of the object times its acceleration. F = ma. The direction of the force is the direction of the acceleration.
3. **Third law**: when object A exerts a force on object B, object B exerts an equal and opposite force on object A. Action and reaction forces act on different objects, so they do not cancel.

The **net force** is the vector sum of all the forces acting on an object. An object in equilibrium (at rest or moving with constant velocity) has zero net force.

**Friction**: a force that opposes motion between two surfaces in contact. The frictional force depends on the normal force between the surfaces and on the roughness of the surfaces; for many O-Level problems, friction is given as a constant force in the question.

**Air resistance**: a special form of friction that opposes the motion of an object through air. It depends on the speed and shape of the object.

#### Mass, weight and density

**Mass** (m) is the amount of matter in an object, measured in kilograms (kg). It is a scalar quantity and does not change with location.

**Weight** (W) is the gravitational force on an object, measured in newtons (N). It is a vector quantity, directed toward the centre of the Earth (or other gravitating body). W = mg, where g is the gravitational field strength (≈ 10 m/s² near the Earth's surface).

**Density** (ρ) is the mass per unit volume of a substance, measured in kg/m³ (or g/cm³). ρ = m / V. Density is a property of the substance itself; different substances have different densities.

#### Turning effect of forces (moments)

The **moment of a force** about a pivot is the product of the force and the perpendicular distance from the pivot to the line of action of the force. Moment = F × d (in N m).

The **principle of moments**: for an object in equilibrium, the sum of the clockwise moments about any point equals the sum of the anticlockwise moments about the same point.

**Couple**: a pair of equal and opposite forces that produce a turning effect but no net force. The moment of a couple is constant about every point; it equals one of the forces times the perpendicular distance between the lines of action.

#### Pressure

**Pressure** (P) is the force per unit area. P = F / A. Units: pascals (Pa = N/m²).

In a fluid at rest, the pressure at a depth h is P = ρgh, where ρ is the density of the fluid and g is the gravitational field strength. Pressure acts equally in all directions at a point in a fluid.

**Atmospheric pressure**: the pressure exerted by the Earth's atmosphere. At sea level it is approximately 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa.

#### Energy, work and power

**Work** is done when a force moves through a distance. W = F × s, where F is the force and s is the distance moved in the direction of the force. Units: joules (J).

**Energy** is the ability to do work. Several forms of energy are important at O-Level: kinetic energy (½ mv²), gravitational potential energy (mgh), elastic potential energy (½ kx² in a spring), chemical energy (in fuel and food), electrical energy, thermal energy.

The **principle of conservation of energy**: energy cannot be created or destroyed, only converted from one form to another. The total energy in a closed system is constant.

**Power** (P) is the rate of doing work (or the rate of transferring energy). P = W / t = F × v (when the force and velocity are in the same direction). Units: watts (W = J/s).

**Efficiency** is the ratio of useful output energy (or power) to total input energy (or power). Efficiency = useful output / total input × 100%.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where Newtonian Mechanics appears across the papers

- **O-Level Pure Physics 6091 Paper 1**: short structured items on speed, acceleration, force, and energy.
- **O-Level Pure Physics 6091 Paper 2**: long structured items (5 to 10 marks) combining kinematics with forces, energy with power, and pressure with moments. Often the highest-mark structured items on the paper.
- **Across both papers**: drawing a clear free-body diagram (with all forces labelled and their directions shown) is the highest-leverage exam technique for dynamics questions.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Mass and weight are the same." Mass is the amount of matter (kg); weight is the gravitational force on an object (N). Weight depends on the local gravitational field; mass does not.
- "A constant force means constant velocity." A constant net force produces a constant acceleration, which means the velocity is changing (unless the force is zero).
- "Action and reaction forces cancel out." They do not — they act on different objects. The forces on a single object do not include both an action and its reaction.
- "Pressure increases with force." Pressure is force per unit area; the same force on a smaller area produces a higher pressure.
- "Work is the same as energy." Work is a transfer of energy; the energy transferred equals the work done.

#### Exam technique

- For kinematics questions, list u, v, a, s and t at the start of the solution. The list identifies which equation to use.
- For dynamics questions, draw a free-body diagram showing all forces and their directions. The diagram is the method mark.
- For moment questions, identify the pivot and the perpendicular distance. The distance is the most common error.
- For energy questions, state the form of energy at the start and at the end of the process. The energy balance is the method mark.
