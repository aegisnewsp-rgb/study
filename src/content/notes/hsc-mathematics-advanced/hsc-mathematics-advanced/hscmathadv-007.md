# Financial Mathematics — HSC Mathematics Advanced (NESA 2024) Notes

Financial Mathematics is one of the seven Areas of Study in the HSC Mathematics Advanced syllabus. It is a Year 12 topic, taught after Sequences and Series because most financial mathematics problems reduce to arithmetic and geometric series. Compound interest, depreciation, loans and annuities are core HSC content.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Simple interest.** A = P(1 + rn). P = principal, r = rate per period, n = number of periods.
- **Compound interest.** A = P(1 + r)ⁿ for n compounding periods. A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt) for n compounding periods per year for t years.
- **Effective annual rate.** The actual annual interest rate when compounding is more frequent than annual. EAR = (1 + r/n)ⁿ − 1.
- **Depreciation.** Reducing balance: A = P(1 − r)ⁿ. Straight line: A = P(1 − rn) up to the salvage value.
- **Annuity (future value).** FV = M × ((1 + r)ⁿ − 1)/r, where M is the periodic payment.
- **Annuity (present value).** PV = M × (1 − (1 + r)⁻ⁿ)/r.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing simple and compound interest — simple interest grows linearly, compound interest grows exponentially.
- Mixing up future value (FV) and present value (PV) formulas — they are related by PV = FV × (1 + r)⁻ⁿ but are different formulas.
- Using the wrong compounding frequency — when interest is compounded monthly, r is the monthly rate and n is the number of months.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Simple and compound interest

Simple interest: interest is calculated only on the original principal. A = P + Prn = P(1 + rn). Total interest = Prn.

Compound interest: interest is added to the principal each period and earns interest in subsequent periods. A = P(1 + r)ⁿ. Total interest = A − P = P((1 + r)ⁿ − 1).

Compound interest with sub-annual compounding: if interest is compounded n times per year at nominal annual rate r, then A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt) after t years.

#### Effective annual rate

The effective annual rate (EAR) is the actual annual interest rate when compounding is more frequent than annual. If nominal annual rate is r compounded n times per year, then EAR = (1 + r/n)ⁿ − 1.

For example, 6% compounded monthly: EAR = (1 + 0.06/12)¹² − 1 ≈ 6.168%. Two investments with the same nominal rate but different compounding frequencies have different effective rates.

#### Depreciation

Straight-line depreciation: the asset loses the same dollar value each period. A_n = P − nd, where d is the depreciation per period. The asset reaches its salvage value when A_n = S.

Reducing-balance depreciation: the asset loses a constant percentage of its current value each period. A_n = P(1 − r)ⁿ, where r is the depreciation rate per period. The asset approaches but never reaches zero.

#### Annuities

An ordinary annuity pays (or receives) a fixed amount M at the end of each period for n periods, with interest i per period.

Future value: FV = M × ((1 + i)ⁿ − 1)/i.

Present value: PV = M × (1 − (1 + i)⁻ⁿ)/i.

#### Loans

A loan of amount PV is repaid by n equal payments of M at the end of each period with interest i per period. The relationship is PV = M × (1 − (1 + i)⁻ⁿ)/i. So M = PV × i / (1 − (1 + i)⁻ⁿ).

To find the outstanding balance after k payments: outstanding = M × (1 − (1 + i)⁻⁽ⁿ⁻ᵏ⁾)/i.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Why Financial Mathematics tests more than arithmetic

- **Financial questions test the modelling pipeline.** Take a word problem, identify the formula, identify which variables are given, solve for the unknown. Many HSC students lose marks on the modelling step, not the arithmetic.
- **Compounding frequency matters.** An investment at "6% per year" may compound annually, semi-annually, quarterly or monthly. The effective rate differs in each case.
- **Loans and annuities are mirror images.** A loan is a present-value problem: PV of repayments equals loan amount. An annuity is a future-value problem: FV of deposits equals target amount.

#### Exam technique

- ALWAYS identify the formula before substituting. Identify: simple or compound? Future value or present value? Annuity (regular payments) or lump sum?
- For depreciation, identify straight-line vs reducing-balance. The formula is different.
- For annuity problems, identify the compounding frequency and convert the rate to per-period before applying the formula.
- For loan problems, identify the loan amount, the periodic payment, the rate per period, and the number of periods.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Compound interest is just simple interest with the rate doubled." No — compound interest grows exponentially; doubling the rate gives a different exponential.
- "Effective annual rate equals nominal rate." Only when compounding is annual. For sub-annual compounding, effective rate is higher.
- "Annuities always grow to a positive future value." Only when interest is positive. With negative interest (deflation), an annuity can shrink.
- "Outstanding loan balance after k payments = original balance − k × payment." No — outstanding balance is the present value of the remaining n − k payments, not the original minus payments.
