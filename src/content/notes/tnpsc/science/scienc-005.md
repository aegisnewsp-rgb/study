# Plant Life and Reproduction

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

> **Memory Hook anchor — read first:** Double fertilization in angiosperms produces one diploid zygote (2n) AND one triploid endosperm (3n) — both inside the same embryo sac, never let an examiner catch you calling gymnosperms "double" too.

A flower is the reproductive unit of angiosperms. Its four whorls are **calyx** (sepals), **corolla** (petals), **androecium** (stamens — anther + filament) and **gynoecium** (carpel — stigma, style, ovary with ovules). Sexual reproduction hinges on pollination, fertilization, seed and fruit formation, while asexual routes let plants clone themselves without gametes.

| Mode | Examples | Key feature |
|------|----------|-------------|
| Asexual (vegetative) | Potato tuber, ginger rhizome, onion bulb, strawberry runner, Agave bulbil | Clonal — offspring genetically identical to parent |
| Sexual (flower-based) | Wheat, Salvia, mango, pea | Gametes fuse; double fertilization is diagnostic of angiosperms |

> 💡 **Memory Hook:** *"Two sperms, two fates"* — one sperm + egg = zygote (2n); second sperm + two polar nuclei = endosperm (3n). Discovered by Nawaschin in 1898.

Life-span classes to recall cold: **annual** = one season (rice, wheat), **biennial** = two seasons (carrot, radish), **perennial** = many years (mango, banyan).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Floral architecture and gametophytes

The anther produces **pollen grains** (male gametophyte, microspore) inside microsporangia. The ovule, inside the ovary, holds the **embryo sac** (female gametophyte), which in most angiosperms follows the **Polygonum type**: 7 cells, 8 nuclei — three antipodals, two polar nuclei (often in a central cell), one egg and two synergids. The two male gametes inside the pollen tube are not made by meiosis directly; they arise from division of the **generative nucleus**.

| Structure | Diploid (2n) or haploid (n) | SI unit | Role |
|-----------|-----------------------------|---------|------|
| Pollen grain | n | cells per grain | Delivers two male gametes |
| Embryo sac (mature) | n | cells per sac | Houses egg + central cell |
| Zygote | 2n | chromosomes per cell | Future embryo |
| Endosperm | 3n | chromosome sets per cell | Nourishes embryo |

#### Pollination agencies

The pollen vector decides whether a flower is wind-, water-, animal- or gravity-borne.

| Agency | Pollen trait | Typical examples | Common trap |
|--------|--------------|------------------|-------------|
| Anemophily (wind) | Light, dry, non-sticky | Wheat, rice, maize | Maize is anemophilous, NOT entomophilous |
| Entomophily (insects) | Sticky, sculptured, scented | Salvia, Aristolochia, sunflower | Salvia's lever mechanism is insect-specific |
| Hydrophily (water) | Filamentous or floating | Vallisneria, Hydrilla | Vallisneria is NOT wind-pollinated |
| Ornithophily (birds) | Copious nectar, bright colour | Bombax, silk-cotton tree | Bird flowers are usually red, scentless |
| Chiropterophily (bats) | Dull colour, musty smell | Bauhinia, banana at night | Bats visit dull, nocturnal flowers |

#### Outbreeding devices versus selfing

Plants that favour cross-pollination deploy **dicliny** (unisexual flowers), **monoecy**, **dioecy**, **self-incompatibility**, **heterostyly** (pin and thrum in *Primula*), **protandry** (androecium matures first) and **protogyny** (gynoecium matures first). Watch the distinction between **geitonogamy** (pollen moves between flowers on the same plant — genetically self, even though physically cross) and **xenogamy** (pollen moves between different plants — the only true outcrossing mechanism).

#### After fertilization: the fate map

| Pre-fertilization organ | Post-fertilization product |
|-------------------------|----------------------------|
| Ovary | Fruit |
| Ovule | Seed |
| Integuments | Seed coat (testa + tegmen) |
| Zygote | Embryo |
| Primary endosperm nucleus (3n) | Endosperm |
| Synergids, antipodals | Degenerate |

Endosperm development is **nuclear** in cereals and castor (free-nuclear first, then cell walls) and **cellular** in *Petunia*, *Datura* and *Magnolia* (walls form every division).

#### Worked example — resolving a TNPSC-style MCQ

**Question.** In angiosperms the endosperm is formed by the fusion of:
(a) one sperm with the egg cell
(b) two sperms with two polar nuclei
(c) one sperm with two polar nuclei
(d) two sperms with one polar nucleus

**Resolution.** Double fertilization (Nawaschin, 1898) involves two sperm cells from a single pollen tube. Sperm A fuses with the egg → zygote (2n). Sperm B fuses with the two polar nuclei (each n) sitting inside the central cell of the embryo sac → primary endosperm nucleus (3n). One sperm, two polar nuclei. Answer: **(c)**.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Option (b) sounds plausible because "two sperms" is mentioned in double fertilization — but only ONE of those sperms touches the polar nuclei. The other sperm is busy fusing with the egg. Students who forget this end up circling (b).

**Question.** Identify the hypogeal germinator from this list — bean, castor, onion, groundnut.

**Resolution.** Hypogeal germination keeps the cotyledons underground; the **hypocotyl stays short** and the **epicotyl elongates**. Groundnut fits this pattern (also pea, maize, gram). Bean, castor and onion are epigeal — the hypocotyl elongates and pushes cotyledons above the soil. Answer: **groundnut**.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students routinely mark groundnut as epigeal because groundnuts grow upward — but the cotyledons themselves stay buried while the plumule pushes out. Always judge by where the **cotyledons end up**, not by where the shoot tip appears.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

#### Vegetative propagules — identifying the modified organ

Each vegetative structure is a modified shoot system, never a true root (with the exception of root tubers like sweet potato, which students should note separately).

| Propagule | Modified organ | Example | Diagnostic eye |
|-----------|----------------|---------|----------------|
| Runner / stolon | Stem | Strawberry, *Cynodon* | Creeps on soil surface |
| Rhizome | Underground stem | Ginger, turmeric | Horizontal, with nodes and scale leaves |
| Tuber | Stem (swollen tip) | Potato | "Eyes" are nodes, each with a bud |
| Bulb | Stem with fleshy leaves | Onion, garlic | Concentric fleshy scales |
| Corm | Short vertical stem | Colocasia, Gladiolus | Solid, scaly base |
| Offset | Short horizontal branch | *Pistia*, *Hydrilla* | Rosette form on water plants |
| Bulbil | Axillary bud | *Agave*, *Oxalis* | Detachable miniature plant |

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** A potato tuber is a stem, not a root. The "eyes" are nodes bearing buds and scale leaves; adventitious roots grow from the underside. Marking it as a modified root loses marks even when the rest of the answer is correct.

#### Edge cases and special reproductive phenomena

- **Apomixis** — asexual seed formation without fertilization. Used commercially to fix hybrid vigour; common in *Citrus*, *Mangifera* (some varieties) and several Asteraceae.
- **Polyembryony** — more than one embryo per seed. *Citrus* and mango show it; one embryo is zygotic, the others arise from nucellar cells (nucellar embryony).
- **Parthenocarpy** — fruit develops without fertilization; seedless. Banana and seedless papaya, grape and watermelon are textbook examples; treated with auxins (e.g., IAA, NAA) to induce commercially.
- **Cleistogamy** — flowers never open; forced self-pollination inside a closed bud (e.g., *Commelina*, *Oxalis*, *Viola*).

#### Numerical relations worth committing

- Daughter cell chromosome number after meiosis: n = 2n ÷ 2 (dimension: number of chromosomes per cell).
- Daughter cell DNA content after meiosis: c = 2c ÷ 2 (unit: picograms of DNA per cell).
- Endosperm ploidy in angiosperms: 3n = (n + n) polar nuclei + (n) sperm nucleus.
- Zygote ploidy: 2n = n (egg) + n (sperm).
- Pollen viability generally lasts 3–5 days, but cereal pollen can remain viable up to about 30 days — both ranges reported in NCERT Class XII Botany.

#### Common pitfalls examiners probe

1. Treating **double fertilization** as universal in seed plants — it is restricted to angiosperms; gymnosperms perform single fertilization only.
2. Confusing **monoecious** (both sexes on the same plant, e.g., maize, coconut) with **bisexual/hermaphrodite** (both sexes in the same flower).
3. Saying the **scutellum** of a maize grain stores food. It is the **endosperm** that stores starch and protein; the scutellum merely absorbs and transfers nutrients to the embryo.
4. Choosing Vallisneria for "anemophilous plant" — its male flowers detach and float on the water surface to reach female flowers (epihydrophily).
5. Forgetting that pollen tube entry is typically through the **micropyle** (porogamy), though chalazogamy (through chalaza) and mesogamy (through integuments) exist in a few species like *Casuarina* and *Cucurbita*.
