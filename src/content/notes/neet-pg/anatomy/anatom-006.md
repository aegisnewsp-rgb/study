# Cardiovascular System

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The cardiovascular system is a **closed circulatory system** built around a four-chambered muscular pump (the heart) and a branching network of arteries, capillaries, and veins. Blood flows in one direction through four valves: **tricuspid**, **mitral (bicuspid)**, **pulmonary**, and **aortic**.

The cardiac cycle is governed by an intrinsic conducting system: **SA node → AV node → Bundle of His → bundle branches → Purkinje fibres**, with a 0.1 s AV nodal delay allowing atrial emptying before ventricular systole.

- **Cardiac Output (CO)** = SV × HR; normal ≈ 5 L/min at rest.
- **Ejection Fraction** = (SV/EDV) × 100; normal 55–70 %.
- **Fetal shunts**: foramen ovale, ductus arteriosus, ductus venosus — all close functionally within hours to weeks of birth.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Heart Wall and Chambers

The heart wall has three layers: **endocardium** (inner, endothelium-lined), **myocardium** (middle, cardiac muscle; thickest in the left ventricle), and **epicardium** (outer, visceral serous pericardium). The **fibrous pericardium** is a tough outer sac anchoring the heart to the diaphragm and great vessels.

The right atrium receives the SVC, IVC, and coronary sinus; the left atrium receives four pulmonary veins. The **interventricular septum** has a thin membranous portion (just below the aortic valve) and a thick muscular portion — the membranous part is the common site of VSDs.

#### Valves and Conducting System

| Valve | Type | Auscultation Site | Closure Sound |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Aortic | Semilunar | Right 2nd ICS, sternal border | S2 (A2) |
| Pulmonary | Semilunar | Left 2nd ICS, sternal border | S2 (P2) |
| Mitral | Atrioventricular | Apex, 5th ICS midclavicular | S1 |
| Tricuspid | Atrioventricular | Lower left sternal border, 4th ICS | S1 |

AV valves (mitral, tricuspid) anchor via **chordae tendineae** to **papillary muscles**; semilunar valves have **no chordae**. The SA node fires at ~70–75 bpm; sympathetic nerves from T1–T5 accelerate it, vagus (CN X) slows it.

#### Coronary and Fetal Circulation

The **left coronary artery** divides into the LAD (anterior 2/3 septum, anterior LV) and circumflex (left atrium, lateral LV). The **right coronary artery** supplies the right atrium, RV, posterior 1/3 septum, SA node (in ~60 %) and AV node (in ~80 %). **Right dominance** (RCA giving the posterior interventricular artery) occurs in ~70–80 % of people.

- **Foramen ovale**: right atrium → left atrium, closes functionally at birth, anatomically by ~1 year.
- **Ductus arteriosus**: pulmonary trunk → aortic arch, closes within 24–48 h (prostaglandins keep it open).
- **Ductus venosus**: umbilical vein → IVC, bypassing the liver; closes within 1–2 weeks.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Pericardial Sinuses and Surgical Landmarks

The serous pericardium reflects around the great vessels forming two recesses. The **transverse pericardial sinus** lies between the aorta/pulmonary trunk anteriorly and the SVC/atria posteriorly — surgeons pass a clamp through it to occlude both arterial trunks during coronary surgery. The **oblique sinus** lies posterior to the left atrium, bounded by the pulmonary veins.

#### Applied Correlates and Common Traps

Cardiac referred pain travels via sympathetic afferents from T1–T5, explaining **left arm/jaw pain in MI**. Coarctation of the aorta presents with radio-femoral delay and rib notching from collateral intercostal enlargement. Pericarditis produces a friction rub best heard in the left lower sternal border with the patient leaning forward.

| Trap | Correction |
| --- | --- |
| Aortic valve heard on right | Anatomically left-sided; auscultation site is right 2nd ICS |
| Chordae only on AV valves | Semilunar valves have no chordae tendineae |
| LAD supplies SA node | RCA supplies SA node in ~60 % (right dominant) |
| Ductus arteriosus = aorta to pulmonary vein | Connects pulmonary trunk to aortic arch |

**Worked example**: HR = 72 bpm, EDV = 120 mL, ESV = 50 mL. SV = 120 − 50 = 70 mL. CO = 70 × 72 = 5040 mL/min (~5 L/min). EF = (70/120) × 100 = 58.3 % — within the normal 55–70 % range.

**Practice prompts**: (1) Trace a drop of blood from the SVC through fetal shunts to the umbilical artery. (2) Identify which coronary artery is most likely occluded given ST elevation in leads II, III, aVF (inferior MI = RCA in right-dominant patients).
