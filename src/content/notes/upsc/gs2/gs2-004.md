# Polity

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Polity** in UPSC CSE refers to the Indian Constitution, the political system it creates, and the governance structures that flow from it. The single highest-yield idea is the **Basic Structure Doctrine** (*Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala*, 1973): Parliament can amend any article, but cannot destroy the Constitution's basic structure.

- **Fundamental Rights (Articles 12–35)** are justiciable; **Directive Principles (Articles 36–51)** are non-justiciable, but **Fundamental Duties (Article 51A)** are obligatory on citizens.
- The **Seventh Schedule** distributes powers via the **Union, State, and Concurrent Lists**; residuary powers belong to Parliament under Article 248.
- A **Money Bill** (Article 117) is certified solely by the **Speaker**, can only originate in the Lok Sabha, and the Rajya Sabha can recommend changes within **14 days** but cannot reject or amend it.
- Federal features borrowed from **Government of India Act, 1935**, parliamentary form from the **UK**, judicial review from the **USA**, and fundamental rights from the **USA Bill of Rights**.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Constitutional Foundations

The Constitution's **Preamble** declares India a **Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic** committed to **Justice, Liberty, Equality, Fraternity** — amended by the **42nd Amendment, 1976** (adding Socialist, Secular, Integrity) and later stripped of "Integrity" by the 44th Amendment in 1979.

Citizenship is **single and uniform** (Articles 5–11); dual citizenship exists only for persons registered as **OCI** under the Citizenship Act, 1955. The **Eighth Schedule** now lists **22 languages** (92nd Amendment, 2003 added Bodo, Dogri, Maithili, Santhali).

#### Legislature and Executive

The **President** is the nominal head and an integral part of Parliament (Article 52). Under **Article 74**, the Council of Ministers' aid and advice is binding, save for a narrow discretionary window — chiefly when no clear majority emerges. The **Vice-President** is the Rajya Sabha's ex-officio Chairperson, with the **Chairman of Rajya Sabha** presiding in his absence.

| Feature | Lok Sabha | Rajya Sabha |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum strength | 552 (530 + 25 Anglo-Indian, now 543) | 250 (238 elected + 12 nominated) |
| Tenure | 5 years (dissolvable) | 6 years (permanent, 1/3 retire every 2 years) |
| Speaker | Elected from members | Chairman (Vice-President); Deputy Chairman elected |
| Exclusive powers | Money Bill, no-confidence motion | Equal on creation of All-India Services, Article 312 |

A **joint sitting** under Article 108 is summoned by the President and is presided over by the **Speaker** (not the Vice-President). The **Governor** (Articles 153–167) is appointed by the President and holds office during the President's pleasure (no fixed tenure), exercising discretion in specific areas such as withholding assent, reserving a bill, or recommending President's Rule.

> **Exam trap:** Money Bills cannot be referred to a Joint Sitting. Only ordinary bills on which the two Houses disagree can trigger Article 108.

#### Judiciary and Federalism

**Article 32** (Supreme Court) and **Article 226** (High Court) confer **writ jurisdiction** — *Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, Quo Warranto*. Article 226 is wider because the High Court can issue writs against private persons, not just the State.

Federalism operates through three lists plus residuary powers; Article 249 lets Parliament legislate on the State List in the **national interest** for a limited period. The **Finance Commission** (Article 280) is a **constitutional** body, whereas **NITI Aayog** (2015) **replaced** the non-statutory **Planning Commission** and has merely advisory standing.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Doctrinal Mastery and Constitutional Bodies

The **Anti-Defection Law (Tenth Schedule, 52nd Amendment 1985)** was tightened by the **91st Amendment, 2003**, requiring **two-thirds** of a party legislature group for a valid merger. Splitting below this floor disqualifies defectors from the date of defection. The **Election Commission (Article 324)**, **CAG (Article 148)**, and **UPSC (Article 315)** are constitutional bodies, all reporting to the President, while the **Lokpal** was established only by statute in **2014**, decades after the **ARC-I** recommendation.

#### Emergency, Exceptions, and Recent Changes

Three emergencies exist: **National Emergency (Article 352)** — needs written Cabinet advice and parliamentary approval within one month, affects only Centre–State relations and Fundamental Rights under Article 358–359; **President's Rule (Article 356)** — *failure of constitutional machinery*, not technically an emergency, but most consequential (*S.R. Bommai v. Union of India*, 1994, held it is subject to judicial review); and **Financial Emergency (Article 360)** — never proclaimed. Article 370 lapsed with effect from August 5, 2019 via the **J&K Reorganisation Act, 2019**, creating the UTs of **J&K** (with legislature) and **Ladakh**.

> **Common pitfall:** Treating Article 370 as still alive loses a year of marks. Always state it as **defunct since 2019**, with the constitutional challenge pending in the Supreme Court.

#### Worked Mini-Example

**Prompt A (Prelims 2018 repetition):** "Money Bill is certified by:" — Answer: *the Speaker of Lok Sabha*. **Prompt B (Mains-style):** "Discuss how the 73rd Amendment reshaped grassroots democracy." — Credit triggers: three-tier **Panchayati Raj**, **29 subjects in the 11th Schedule**, **5-year elections reserved for SC/ST/women**, and **State Finance Commissions** mirroring the central Finance Commission. Exam weight: Polity features in **GS Prelims** (~8–15 questions in Indian Polity), **GS Mains II** (~250 marks across the paper), and is recycled in **Essay Paper** and **Interview** dossiers on governance, federalism, and constitutional morality.
