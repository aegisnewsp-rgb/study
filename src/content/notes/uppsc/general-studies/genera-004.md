# Moderates vs Extremists

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Indian National Congress (INC)** was founded on **28 December 1885** by **A.O. Hume**, a retired British civil servant, with **W.C. Bonnerjee** as the first president. It began as a "safety valve" channel for moderate demands — civil rights, civil service reform, and representative government — but evolved into the principal vehicle of mass anti-colonial struggle.

- **Moderates (1885–1905)** — *Dadabhai Naoroji, Gopal Krishna Gokhale, Surendranath Banerjee*: relied on prayers, petitions, and constitutional methods. Naoroji authored **"Drain of Wealth"** (*Poverty and Un-British Rule in India*, 1876).
- **Extremists (1905–1919)** — *Lal-Bal-Pal* (Lala Lajpat Rai, Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal): demanded **Swaraj**, used Boycott–Swadeshi–National Education.
- **Partition of Bengal (1905)** by **Lord Curzon** triggered the **Swadeshi Movement**; reversed in 1911 by **Lord Hardinge**.

#### Moderates vs Extremists
| Aspect | Moderates | Extremists |
|---|---|---|
| Period | 1885–1905 | 1905–1919 |
| Goal | Self-government within British Empire | Complete Swaraj |
| Methods | Petitions, prayers, press | Boycott, swadeshi, national education |
| Leaders | Gokhale, Naoroji, S.N. Banerjee | Tilak, Lajpat Rai, B.C. Pal, Aurobindo Ghosh |
| Belief | British sense of justice | British as exploiters; passive resistance legitimate |

#### Gandhian Mass Movements
**Champaran Satyagraha (1917)** — first civil disobedience in India against the **tinkathia system** forcing indigo planters to grow indigo on 3/20 of their land. Gandhi's first satyagraha in India.

**Rowlatt Act (March 1919)** — permitted detention without trial; led to **Rowlatt Satyagraha** and the **Jallianwala Bagh massacre** (13 April 1919) at Amritsar, where **General Reginald Dyer** ordered firing on an unarmed gathering, killing hundreds.

**Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–22)** — non-cooperation with British institutions (courts, titles, schools), joined with the **Khilafat Movement**. Withdrawn after the **Chauri Chaura incident** (5 February 1922) when a police station was torched, killing 22 policemen.

**Civil Disobedience Movement (1930–34)** — began with the **Dandi March (12 March – 6 April 1930)** when Gandhi broke the salt law at **Dandi**. Key features: violation of salt laws, forest laws, foreign cloth.

#### Constitutional Acts
- **Government of India Act 1919** — introduced **dyarchy** in provinces.
- **Government of India Act 1935** — proposed **provincial autonomy** and a federal structure; abolished dyarchy in provinces, retained it at centre.
- **Indian Independence Act 1947** — passed by British Parliament on **18 July 1947**, effective **15 August 1947**, creating India and Pakistan as dominions.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Subhas Chandra Bose and the INA
Bose resigned from the Indian Civil Service, formed the **Forward Bloc** (1939) within Congress, and escaped from house arrest in Calcutta (January 1941) via the **Kabul route**, reaching **Berlin**. He formed the **Azad Hind Fauj (Indian National Army)** initially under **Captain Mohan Singh** (1942) from Indian POWs of Singapore. Later, in **Tokyo (4 July 1943)**, Bose reorganised the INA and proclaimed the **Provisional Government of Free India (21 October 1943)** with Axis recognition. Japanese-occupied **Andaman** was renamed **Shaheed** and **Nicobar** as **Swaraj**. The **Red Fort trials (1945–46)** of INA soldiers by the British sparked nationwide unrest, accelerating the British decision to quit India.

#### Key Wartime Negotiations
- **August Offer (1940)** by **Lord Linlithgow** — promise of dominion status post-war; rejected by Congress.
- **Cripps Mission (March 1942)** — offered dominion status after the war but rejected because it denied immediate self-government and a real constituent assembly election. Quit India was launched in August 1942.
- **Cabinet Mission (1946)** — **Pethick-Lawrence, Stafford Cripps, A.V. Alexander** — proposed a united federal India with weak centre; Congress accepted initially, League rejected.
- **Mountbatten Plan (3 June 1947)** — accepted partition; led to the **Radcliffe Line** demarcation by **Sir Cyril Radcliffe**.

#### Edge Cases & Common Traps
- **Annie Besant** — Irish theosophist, founded the **Home Rule League (1916)**; she was **not Indian**, students confuse her with Indian Home Rulers.
- **Chittagong Armoury Raid (1930)** — by **Surya Sen**, often missed.
- **First session of INC** — held in **Bombay (1885)** with **72 delegates**, not Calcutta.
- **Poona Pact (1932)** — between **Gandhi** and **Ambedkar**, replaced separate electorates for Depressed Classes with reserved seats in joint electorate.
- **Doctrine of Lapse** — Dalhousie's policy (1848–1856), predates INC; sometimes confused with later reforms.
- UPPSC trap: **Simon Commission (1928)** had **no Indian member**, leading to the slogan **"Simon go back"**.

2. Match the leader to movement: **(a)** Tilak — Home Rule; **(b)** Bhagat Singh — Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (1928); **(c)** Vinoba Bhave — Individual Satyagraha (1940).
