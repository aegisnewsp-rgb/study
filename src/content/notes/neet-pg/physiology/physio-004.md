# Gastrointestinal Physiology

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Gastrointestinal (GI) physiology covers the six coordinated functions of the alimentary tract — motility, secretion, digestion, absorption, circulation, and neuroendocrine regulation — that convert a mixed meal into absorbable nutrients while recycling ~8–10 L of endogenous secretions per 24 h.

- **Daily secretions (mL/24 h):** saliva ~1500, gastric ~2500, bile ~500, pancreatic ~1000, intestinal ~3000; ~90% is reabsorbed, mostly by the small intestine.
- **Basal vs maximal acid output:** BAO = 1–5 mEq/h; PAO (post-pentagastrin) = 20–25 mEq/h; PAO/MAO ratio is used clinically (e.g., Zollinger-Ellison).
- **GI hormone origins:** G cells → gastrin (antrum); S cells → secretin (duodenum); I cells → CCK (duodenum/jejunum); K cells → GIP; M cells → motilin; D cells → somatostatin.
- **Motility patterns:** peristalsis (propulsive), segmentation (mixing), MMC (fasting "housekeeper," 90–120 min cycle).
- **Fat route:** triglycerides → micelles → enterocyte re-esterification → chylomicrons → **lymphatics (lacteals)**, NOT portal blood.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"GAS-C-SIP-MoS-VIP"** for the GI hormone alphabet — **GA**strin (G), **S**ecretin (S), **CCK** (C), **S**omatostatin (S), **I** = GIP (K), **P** = motilin, **V**IP. Pair with **"SSS — Salt, Sugar, Small-peptides"** absorbed by the SGLT1, GLUT2, and PepT1 transporters.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Phases of Gastric Acid Secretion

Acid secretion by parietal (oxyntic) cells is divided into three overlapping phases, each contributing a different fraction of total HCl output:

| Phase | Trigger | Main mediator | Approx. % of HCl |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cephalic | Sight, smell, taste, chewing | Vagus → ACh + gastrin release | ~30% |
| Gastric | Distension + peptides in lumen | Local gastrin + histamine (ECL cells, H2) | ~60% |
| Intestinal | Chyme in duodenum | Secretin, CCK, GIP, enterogastric reflex | Inhibitory |

Parietal cell stimulation converges on the apical **H⁺/K⁺ ATPase** (proton pump): gastrin/CCK → CCK-B receptor (Gq, IP₃, Ca²⁺); ACh → M₃ (Gq, IP₃); histamine → H₂ (Gs, cAMP). Somatostatin and PGE₂ inhibit via Gi. The pump is **electroneutral (1 H⁺ out, 1 K⁺ in)** and depends on basolateral **Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase** to maintain the Na⁺ gradient that drives K⁺ recycling. Luminal pH < 3 triggers somatostatin release — a negative feedback loop that shuts down gastrin.

#### Pancreatic and Biliary Secretion

- **Acinar cells:** secrete digestive enzymes (trypsinogen, chymotrypsinogen, procarboxypeptidase, amylase, lipase, phospholipase A₂) — stimulated mainly by **CCK** (and ACh).
- **Ductal cells:** secrete alkaline fluid rich in HCO₃⁻ — stimulated mainly by **secretin** via cAMP; [HCO₃⁻] rises linearly with secretory rate (≈25 → 150 mEq/L).
- **Trypsin autoactivation:** enterokinase (brush border) converts trypsinogen → trypsin, which then activates other zymogens — a positive-feedback cascade.
- **Bile:** bile salts (conjugated cholic, chenodeoxycholic), phospholipids, cholesterol. Emulsify lipids into micelles allowing lipase–colipase action at the oil–water interface. **Enterohepatic circulation** recycles ~95% of bile salts (5–6 cycles per meal).

#### Macronutrient Digestion and Absorption

| Nutrient | Key enzymes (optimal pH) | Final products | Apical transporter |
|---|---|---|---|
| Carbohydrate | Salivary + pancreatic α-amylase (6.7–7.5); brush-border disaccharidases | Glucose, galactose, fructose | SGLT1 (Na⁺-coupled, glucose/galactose); GLUT5 (fructose) |
| Protein | Pepsin (1.5–3.5); trypsin, chymotrypsin, elastase; carboxypeptidases | Di-/tripeptides + free AAs | PepT1 (H⁺-coupled di/tripeptides); Na⁺-dependent AA transporters |
| Fat | Pancreatic lipase + colipase | 2-MAG + free FAs → micelles | Passive into enterocyte → chylomicrons → lymph |

Basolateral exit for sugars is via **GLUT2** (Na⁺-independent, high capacity); fats bypass the portal vein entirely because chylomicrons (~75–1200 nm) are too large for blood capillaries.

#### Motility and the Enteric Nervous System

The enteric nervous system comprises the **myenteric (Auerbach) plexus** (between longitudinal and circular muscle — controls motility) and the **submucosal (Meissner) plexus** (controls secretion and local blood flow). The **Law of the Intestine** states that peristalsis is produced by an ascending excitatory cholinergic contraction wave and a descending inhibitory nitrergic/VIP wave. The **MMC** cycles every 90–120 min during fasting, with motilin-driven phase III sweeping residual debris.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** A 45-year-old man with recurrent duodenal ulcers has fasting serum gastrin 850 pg/mL (normal <100) and a basal acid output of 18 mEq/h. Secretin (2 U/kg) is infused; gastrin rises paradoxically to >1200 pg/mL. Which cell type is the source of the excess gastrin, and what is the mechanism of the paradoxical rise?

#### Solution:
1. Markedly elevated fasting gastrin + high BAO + paradoxical rise after secretin → **gastrinoma (Zollinger-Ellison syndrome)**. The tumour arises from **G cells** (or non-β islet cells) in the pancreas/duodenum ("triangle").
2. Normal G cells express the secretin receptor coupled to Gi, so secretin *inhibits* gastrin. Gastrinomas aberrantly express secretin receptors coupled to **Gs → cAMP**, so secretin *stimulates* gastrin release — a >120 pg/mL rise is diagnostic.
3. Total acid output follows BAO ≈ 18 → PAO/MAO ratio will be **>0.6**, another diagnostic feature.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students often confuse intrinsic factor (IF) secretion: IF is made by **parietal cells**, NOT chief cells. The chief cell secretion to remember is **pepsinogen**.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### GI Hormone Receptor Pharmacology and Edge Cases

| Hormone | Cell of origin | Receptor on target | Second messenger | Net effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gastrin | G cells (antrum) | CCK-B/Gastrin-R (parietal, ECL) | Gq → IP₃/Ca²⁺ | ↑ HCl, ↑ pepsinogen, growth |
| CCK | I cells (duodenum/jejunum) | CCK-A (acini, gallbladder) | Gq → IP₃/Ca²⁺ | ↑ enzymes, GB contraction, satiety |
| Secretin | S cells (duodenum) | Secretin-R (ductal) | Gs → cAMP | ↑ HCO₃⁻, relaxes sphincter of Oddi |
| GIP | K cells (duodenum) | GIP-R (β cell) | Gs → cAMP | Incretin → ↑ insulin |
| Motilin | M cells (duodenum, jejunum) | Motilin-R (smooth muscle) | Gq → IP₃ | Phase III MMC |
| Somatostatin | D cells (pancreas, GI) | SST-R (parietal, G cell) | Gi | Inhibits acid + all GI hormones |
| VIP | Enteric neurons | VPAC₁/₂ | Gs → cAMP | Smooth-muscle relaxation, ↑ secretion |

#### High-Yield Edge Cases and Clinical Correlates

1. **Lactase non-persistence:** brush-border lactase declines after weaning in ~70% of adults; undigested lactose draws water osmotically and is fermented by colonic bacteria → bloating, flatulence, acidic stool (pH < 6).
2. **VIPoma (WDHA syndrome):** watery diarrhoea, hypokalaemia, achlorhydria — VIP-driven Na⁺/Cl⁻ secretion via CFTR.
3. **Cystic fibrosis:** defective CFTR reduces pancreatic HCO₃⁻ and fluid → thick secretions, ductal obstruction, malabsorption (low fecal elastase-1).
4. **Bile-salt deficiency** (e.g., ileal resection, cholestasis) → impaired micelle formation → steatorrhoea and fat-soluble vitamin (A, D, E, K) deficiency.
5. **Enteroglucagon (GLP-1):** released by L cells of the ileum/colon in response to unabsorbed nutrients; slows gastric emptying ("ileal brake") and is the basis of GLP-1 agonist drugs (e.g., semaglutide).
6. **Hepatic first-pass vs lymphatic route:** short-/medium-chain FAs enter the **portal vein**, whereas long-chain FAs packaged in chylomicrons enter **lymph** — clinically relevant in medium-chain triglyceride (MCT) diets for malabsorption.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. A patient with autoimmune gastritis has loss of parietal cells. Predict the haematological finding, the mechanism of any neurological symptom, and the appropriate replacement therapy.
2. Explain why a patient with chronic pancreatitis has steatorrhoea despite normal bile-salt secretion, and how this differs from the stool pattern in a patient with bile-duct obstruction.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Don't write that CCK stimulates pancreatic **bicarbonate** — CCK stimulates **enzymes**; **secretin** stimulates **HCO₃⁻**. This is among the most frequently repeated MCQ errors in NEET PG Physiology.
