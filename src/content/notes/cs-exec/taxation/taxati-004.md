# Income Tax — Company Taxation

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

A **company** under the Income-tax Act, 1961 is taxed as a separate assessee without any basic exemption limit. **Domestic companies** pay tax on global income; **foreign companies** pay tax only on India-source income. Three compulsory add-ons sit on top of base tax: **Surcharge** + **Health & Education Cess @ 4%** + applicable **MAT u/s 115JB**.

- **Domestic company base tax:** 25% (general) or **22%** under Sec 115BAA, or **15%** under Sec 115BAB (new manufacturing).
- **Foreign company base tax:** 40% (treaty rate may override).
- **MAT u/s 115JB:** Tax = (Book Profit − 0) × 15% (or **9%** if opted for 115BAA) + SC + 4% Cess.
- **DDT abolished w.e.f. 01-04-2020**; dividends now taxed in shareholder's hands.
- **Sec 115QA:** Buy-back taxed at 20% + 12% SC + 4% Cess in the hands of the company (not deductible).

| Regime | Base Rate | Surcharge | Cess | Effective Rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Domestic (general) | 25% / 30% | 7% / 12% | 4% | 25.17% – 34.94% |
| Sec 115BAA | 22% | 10% | 4% | **25.168%** |
| Sec 115BAB | 15% | 10% | 4% | **17.16%** |
| Foreign company | 40% | 2% / 5% | 4% | 41.60% – 43.68% |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"22 + 10 + 4 = 25.168"** (Sec 115BAA) and **"15 + 10 + 4 = 17.16"** (Sec 115BAB). Remember: **MAT = Book Profit × 15% or 9%**, never on total income.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Classification of Companies
A company is **Indian/domestic** if its **Place of Effective Management (POEM)** is in India during the relevant previous year; otherwise it is a **foreign company**. Companies are further split into companies in which the public are substantially interested, closely held companies, and One Person Companies (OPCs) — the classification drives which deductions are denied and whether Sec 79 applies.

#### Concessional Regimes (Sec 115BAA & 115BAB)
**Section 115BAA (inserted by Finance Act 2019, applicable from AY 2020-21)** lets a domestic company pay tax at **22% + 10% surcharge + 4% cess = 25.168%**, but it is a **one-time irrevocable** election. The company must forgo MAT u/s 115JB, DDT (now redundant), and additional depreciation u/s 32(1)(iia) plus deductions 80IA–80RRB (except 80JJA, 80JJAA). **Section 115BAB** grants the **15% + 10% surcharge + 4% cess = 17.16%** rate exclusively to **new manufacturing domestic companies** incorporated on/after 01-10-2019 that commence manufacturing by 31-03-2024 and do not claim specified incentives.

#### Minimum Alternate Tax (MAT) u/s 115JB
Every company (including foreign, where applicable) is liable to MAT when normal tax on total income is **less than 15% of Book Profit** (or **9%** if Sec 115BAA is opted). Book profit equals profit per Statement of P&L (Schedule III, Companies Act 2013) adjusted for amounts carried to reserves, deemed provisions, and the prescribed additions/deductions in Sec 115JB(2). MAT credit is **not available** to foreign companies, but Indian companies can carry it forward for **15 succeeding assessment years** under Sec 115JBA.

#### Comparison Matrix — Easily Confused Provisions

| Feature | Sec 115BAA (22%) | Sec 115BAB (15%) | Sec 115JB (MAT) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Applicable to | Existing domestic company | New manufacturing domestic company | All companies (incl. foreign for India income) |
| Base rate | 22% | 15% | 15% (or 9% if 115BAA opted) |
| Effective rate | 25.168% | 17.16% | 17.472% / 10.476% |
| MAT applicable? | No | No | Yes |
| Election | One-time, irrevocable | One-time, irrevocable | Not optional |
| Specific deductions 80IA–80RRB | Not allowed | Not allowed | Allowed |
| Eligible from AY | 2020-21 | 2020-21 | All years |

#### Dividend Taxation Post-DDT
**DDT u/s 115-O was abolished from 01-04-2020.** Dividends are now taxable in the hands of shareholders at their slab/ applicable rates. To prevent cascading, **Sec 80M** allows a domestic company receiving dividends from another domestic company to deduct the **lower of**: (a) tax rate of the distributing company, or (b) the gross dividend received. Sec 80M deduction cannot exceed the dividend distribution grossed-up by the distributing company's tax rate.

#### Sec 79 Set-off of Business Losses
A closely held company's business loss can be carried forward and set off only if **≥51% of voting power** continues to be held by the same beneficial owners on the last day of the previous year. The relaxation is extended to companies whose loss arose from eligible start-ups.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem
**Question:** X Ltd. (domestic, manufacturing, existing) has total income of ₹80 lakh and Book Profit u/s 115JB of ₹6 crore. The company has not opted for Sec 115BAA. Compute the tax liability under normal provisions and MAT, and state which is applicable.

#### Solution:
Normal tax = ₹80,00,000 × 25% = ₹20,00,000; Surcharge = Nil (income < ₹1 cr); Cess @ 4% = ₹20,00,000 × 4% = ₹80,000; **Normal tax liability = ₹20,80,000**.
MAT = ₹6,00,00,000 × 15% = ₹90,00,000 + Cess ₹3,60,000 = **₹93,60,000**.
Since normal tax (₹20,80,000) < 15% of Book Profit = ₹90,00,000, **MAT u/s 115JB applies**; tax payable = **₹93,60,000**.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students often apply MAT only on total income and ignore Book Profit. Remember — MAT is on **Book Profit**, not Total Income, and 115JB applies **even when there is a loss under normal computation**.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Deemed Dividend u/s 2(22)
Sec 2(22)(a)–(d) deems certain payments as dividends taxable in the shareholder's hands: (a) accumulation of profits by a company on liquidation, (b) distribution on share buy-back (now overlapped by Sec 115QA), (c) distribution of debentures, stock-in-trade, or property on a reduction of capital, and (d) any advance/loan to a **shareholder beneficially holding ≥20% voting power** (raised from earlier 10% threshold). The proviso to Sec 2(22)(e) exempts bona fide loans to business shareholders carrying on non-banking finance activity.

#### Sec 115QA — Buy-back of Shares
A listed company buying back its own shares pays tax at **20% + 12% surcharge + 4% cess = 23.296%** on the **distributed income** (consideration − issue price). Tax is paid as **TDS via the buyer (Sec 194BA)** and is **not deductible** for the company under Sec 115QA(2). The shareholder is **not** required to pay additional tax on the deemed dividend portion.

#### Sec 80M Mechanics — Worked Trick
Where the **gross dividend received** is ₹100 and the distributing company's tax rate is **25.168%**, Sec 80M deduction = ₹100 ÷ (1 + 25.168%) × 25.168% ≈ **₹20.12**. If the receiving company itself pays tax at a lower rate, deduction is capped at its own tax rate × gross dividend.

#### Foreign Company Surcharge Structure
Unlike domestic companies (7%/12% slabs), foreign companies attract a flat surcharge of **2%** on income exceeding ₹1 cr but ≤ ₹10 cr, and **5%** above ₹10 cr. Many DTAA treaties reduce the base rate (e.g., 10%/15%) — but once treaty rate is chosen, MAT exposure continues, and Sec 115JB remains applicable even for foreign companies computing on India income.

#### Advanced Traps and Exceptions

1. **MAT to foreign companies:** Applies only on India-source income; book profit is restricted to that portion.
2. **Sec 115BAB shareholding test:** Even a 1% change in beneficial ownership voids the 15% regime eligibility — verify before claiming.
3. **Sec 115BAA cannot be withdrawn** even if the company later undergoes amalgamation or change in management.
4. **AMT u/s 115JC:** Applicable to **non-corporate** assessees (LLPs, firms, individuals) — does **not** apply to companies. Students confuse AMT and MAT.
5. **TDS u/s 194BA on buy-back:** Threshold for deduction in P&L ≠ nil; the buy-back tax is borne by the company but computed on deemed dividend.
6. **One Person Company (OPC):** Still treated as a "company" and subject to MAT and Sec 79 restrictions.
8. **Section 80M deduction:** Available only when both paying and receiving companies are **domestic** and the paying company has paid DDT (now irrelevant) or tax on distributed profits.

> 📌 **Formula Check:** Tax = (Base Tax + Surcharge) × 1.04. Always gross up cess after computing surcharge on base tax; never charge cess on surcharge twice.

#### Two Advanced Practice Prompts
1. A new manufacturing domestic company incorporated on 15-11-2019 commenced production on 30-06-2023 and has total income ₹12 crore with Book Profit ₹20 crore. Compute tax under all three regimes and identify the lowest-cost option.
2. ABC Pvt. Ltd. pays a loan of ₹25 lakh to its shareholder Mr. X who holds 22% voting power. Accumulated profits are ₹60 lakh. Discuss whether Sec 2(22)(e) applies and the tax consequence in Mr. X's hands.
