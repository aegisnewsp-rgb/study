# Question Types — Multiple Choice, Matching, Labelling, Short Answer — IELTS Listening Notes

The four "selection" question families appear across all four parts of the IELTS Listening test. Multiple choice, matching, plan/map/diagram labelling, and short-answer questions all require the test-taker to select from options or write short answers. Mastery of these families is essential for the Listening test.

> Verify the live Listening format on https://ielts.org/ before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Multiple choice:** choose one of three options (A/B/C) or one of five options (A-E). Common in Parts 3 and 4.
- **Matching:** match items to lists. Common in Parts 2 and 3.
- **Plan/map/diagram labelling:** label locations on a map or items on a diagram. Common in Part 2.
- **Short-answer questions:** open-ended questions answered in 1-3 words.
- **Common distractor:** the recording may mention all options; the question asks for the correct one.
- **Word limits:** strict on short-answer questions.

#### Examiner traps

- Choosing the first plausible option instead of listening for the trigger phrase.
- Missing the answer because it comes after a long sentence.
- Word count violations on short-answer questions.

---

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Multiple choice

Each question has a stem (a partial sentence or question) and three or five options. Listen for the keyword in the question — it usually signals the answer.

For "Which of the following..." questions, the correct option is the one that fully satisfies the question stem. Partial matches are wrong.

#### Matching

Match items to lists. The recording describes each item in turn. Note the names or labels in advance.

#### Plan/map/diagram labelling

Label locations on a map or items on a diagram. The recording usually walks through the layout in a logical order (e.g. clockwise). Labels are short (often a single noun or noun phrase).

#### Short-answer questions

Answer open-ended questions in 1-3 words. The word limit is strict. Write what you hear, in the correct grammatical form.

---

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where the selection families appear across IELTS Listening

The four selection families appear across all four parts, with different frequency:

- Part 1: rarely (mostly completion items).
- Part 2: matching and labelling (monologue context).
- Part 3: multiple choice and matching (discussion context).
- Part 4: multiple choice (lecture context).

Master all four — they appear in different mixes in different tests.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "All multiple-choice options are mentioned." Usually all three are mentioned; the question asks for the correct one.
- "Matching is alphabetical." No — the recording matches items by topic, not alphabetically.
- "Short-answer questions accept paraphrasing." No — write what you hear, within the word limit.

#### Exam technique

- For multiple-choice items, listen for the keyword in the question — it usually signals the answer.
- For matching items, scan the list of options before the recording starts, so you know what to listen for.
- For short-answer items, write exactly what you hear — paraphrase is usually wrong.
