# Algebra and Functions — Junior Cycle Mathematics Notes

The Algebra and Functions strand spans algebraic expressions and formulae, linear equations in one and two variables, quadratic equations and their graphs, inequalities, simultaneous equations, arithmetic and geometric sequences and series, functions and their graphs (linear, quadratic, cubic, exponential), and at Higher level, introductory calculus concepts (rates of change, differentiation of simple polynomial functions). It is the workhorse strand of the SEC paper - almost every other question either depends on, or is built from, an algebraic manipulation in this strand. The weight rating of 4 out of 5 reflects the breadth rather than the difficulty; the highest-stakes marks on the Higher paper come from graph interpretation and from solving quadratic equations by both factorisation and the quadratic formula.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Weight & Frequency**: Rated 4/5 - appears on every paper, often with a sustained run of algebra in the second half of the paper and a graph-interpretation extended-response item near the end. Confirm the live mark allocation on the latest SEC specimen paper.
- **Timing Goal**: Linear equation items should average 45 to 75 seconds; quadratic items should sit in the 90-second to 2-minute band; graph-interpretation items should sit in the 3 to 5 minute band.
- **Core Principle**: Master the four moves that unlock 80% of the marks: (1) collect like terms, (2) factorise by common factor, (3) solve a linear equation in one variable by inverse operations, (4) solve a quadratic by factorising into two brackets or by the quadratic formula.
- **Quadratic formula**: *x = (-b ± √(b² - 4ac)) / 2a* for *ax² + bx + c = 0*. The discriminant *b² - 4ac* tells you the number of real roots: positive (two distinct), zero (one repeated), negative (no real roots).
- **Linear graphs**: *y = mx + c* where *m* is the slope (rise/run) and *c* is the y-intercept. Parallel lines have equal slopes; perpendicular lines have slopes whose product is -1.
- **Quadratic graphs**: *y = ax² + bx + c* is a parabola. It opens upward if *a > 0*, downward if *a < 0*. The axis of symmetry is *x = -b / (2a)*. The vertex is at (*-b/(2a)*, *f(-b/(2a))*).
- **Sequences**: Arithmetic sequences have a constant common difference (*d*); the *n*-th term is *a + (n-1)d*. Geometric sequences have a constant common ratio (*r*); the *n*-th term is *a × r^(n-1)*.
- **Answer Verification**: Substitute the solution back into the original equation. For graphs, check whether the answer makes sense in the context (e.g. time cannot be negative).

#### Examiner traps

- Forgetting to flip the inequality sign when multiplying or dividing by a negative number.
- Solving a quadratic by the formula but only writing one root (use ±).
- Confusing the y-intercept (*c*) with the x-intercept (the roots, where *y* = 0).
- Treating arithmetic and geometric sequences interchangeably - the term formulas are different.
- Dividing by a variable without checking it cannot be zero.
- Drawing a graph from a table of values but extending the line beyond the data range when the question asks for interpolation, not extrapolation.

#### 20-minute triage checklist

- [ ] Can you solve a linear equation in one variable with fractions, brackets, and variables on both sides without hesitation?
- [ ] Do you know when to use factorisation vs the quadratic formula, and can you execute both fluently?
- [ ] Can you identify whether a sequence is arithmetic or geometric from the first three terms?
- [ ] Have you practiced sketching linear and quadratic graphs from their equations without a table of values?

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Conceptual framework and solving methodology

Algebra questions reward a four-step discipline that the SEC mark schemes are written to reward. Apply this scaffold to every algebra item:

1. **Identify the unknown and the relationships**:
   What letter or symbol represents the unknown? What equations or conditions tie it to other quantities? State both before substituting - the equation line is a method mark.

2. **Choose the technique (factorise, formula, complete the square, graph)**:
   A quadratic with integer coefficients that factorises easily (e.g. *x² - 5x + 6* = (*x - 2*)(*x - 3*)) is faster by factorisation. A quadratic with awkward coefficients (*2x² - 7x - 3* = 0) is faster by the quadratic formula. A question about the maximum or minimum of a quadratic is fastest by completing the square to read off the vertex.

3. **Execute with sign discipline**:
   Track negative signs through every line. For simultaneous equations, write one equation above the other and subtract (or add) to eliminate a variable. For inequalities, reverse the sign when multiplying or dividing by a negative number. For graphs, plot at least five points and label the axis of symmetry and the y-intercept.

4. **Verify by substitution or by a sanity check**:
   Plug the solution back into the original equation. For a quadratic, both roots should satisfy the equation. For a graph interpretation, the y-intercept should match the value at *x* = 0 on the sketch. For a sequence question, the first term and common difference/ratio should reproduce the second term.

#### Detailed worked example

**Problem Context**:
A combined quadratic-and-graph question typical of the Higher level paper.

**Item Prompt**:
A ball is thrown vertically upwards. Its height *h* metres above the ground after *t* seconds is given by *h = 20t - 5t²*. **(a)** Find the height of the ball after 2 seconds. **(b)** Find the times at which the ball is at a height of 15 metres. **(c)** Sketch the graph of *h* against *t* for the first 4 seconds, and use it to find the maximum height of the ball.

**Step-by-Step Solution**:
- **Step 1 (Decode)**: (a) substitute *t* = 2. (b) solve a quadratic by setting *h* = 15 and rearranging. (c) sketch the parabola and identify the vertex.
- **Step 2 (Represent)**: For (a), direct substitution. For (b), bring 15 to one side: 20t - 5t² = 15 → 5t² - 20t + 15 = 0 → t² - 4t + 3 = 0. For (c), the parabola opens downward (coefficient of *t²* is negative), with zeros at *t* = 0 and *t* = 4.
- **Step 3 (Execute)**:
  - (a) *h* = 20(2) - 5(2)² = 40 - 20 = 20 metres.
  - (b) *t² - 4t + 3 = 0* → (*t - 1*)(*t - 3*) = 0 → *t* = 1 or *t* = 3 seconds.
  - (c) The graph is a downward parabola passing through (0, 0), (4, 0), with vertex at the axis of symmetry *t* = 2. Maximum height = *h*(2) = 20(2) - 5(2)² = 40 - 20 = 20 metres.
- **Step 4 (Verify)**: (a) At *t* = 2 the height is 20 m, the same as the maximum - makes sense since the vertex is at *t* = 2. (b) The ball reaches 15 m on the way up (*t* = 1) and on the way down (*t* = 3) - both before the landing at *t* = 4. (c) Vertex at *t* = 2, *h* = 20, matches the algebra. The ball lands at *t* = 4 since the second root of *h* = 0 is *t* = 4.
- **Conclusion**: Three marks from one algebra question - the substitution mark, the factorisation mark, and the graph-interpretation mark. The (b) question earns two marks (one per root).

### 🔴 Deep Dive — Mastery & Edge Cases (3mo–2yr)

#### Advanced variations and high-difficulty edge cases

At Distinction level, Algebra and Functions extends to calculus and to graph reasoning. Practice the four highest-leverage patterns:

- **Differentiation of simple polynomials**: *d/dx (axⁿ) = anx^(n-1)*. For *y = 20t - 5t²*, *dy/dt = 20 - 10t*. The rate of change at the maximum is zero; set 20 - 10t = 0 → t = 2, matching the vertex.
- **Sum of arithmetic and geometric series**: *Sₙ = n/2 × (2a + (n-1)d)* for arithmetic; *Sₙ = a(1 - rⁿ) / (1 - r)* for geometric with *r ≠ 1*. The trap is using the wrong series formula.
- **Simultaneous equations with non-linear second equation**: Substitution into a quadratic can produce a quadratic in the remaining variable; solve with the quadratic formula.
- **Functions and inverse functions**: A function *f* maps input *x* to output *f(x)*. The inverse *f⁻¹* swaps input and output. For *y = 2x + 3*, *f⁻¹(x) = (x - 3)/2*. The graph of an inverse is the reflection of the original across the line *y = x*.

#### Systematic drill schedule

A four-week Algebra and Functions pass before mocks:

- **Week 1**: Linear equations, inequalities, and rearranging formulae - 10 items per day, mixing one-variable, two-variable, and "make *x* the subject" forms. Force the inverse-operation line before each calculation.
- **Week 2**: Quadratics by factorisation, formula, and completing the square - 8 items per day. Track the discriminant on every quadratic to anticipate the number of real roots.
- **Week 3**: Sequences and series - 6 items per day, mixing arithmetic, geometric, and "find the *n*-th term" forms. Distinguish the term formula from the sum formula.
- **Week 4**: Graphs and functions, including Higher level differentiation - 5 extended-response items per sitting. Sketch by hand, label axes, identify key features (intercept, vertex, asymptote), and write a one-sentence interpretation.
