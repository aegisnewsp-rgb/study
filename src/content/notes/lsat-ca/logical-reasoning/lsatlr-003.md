# Strengthen and Weaken Items — LSAT Logical Reasoning

Strengthen and weaken items ask which answer choice, if true, would most strengthen or most weaken the author's argument. They are the most-tested Logical Reasoning question type on the LSAT. The skill is identifying the argument's conclusion and the assumption that links premises to conclusion, then evaluating which choice most directly affects that link.

---

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### What strengthen and weaken items test

- **Strengthen:** the choice that, if true, would make the conclusion more likely to follow from the premises.
- **Weaken:** the choice that, if true, would make the conclusion less likely to follow from the premises.

#### One-pass drill

1. Identify the conclusion and the premises.
2. Identify the assumption (the missing link).
3. For strengthen items, find the choice that supports the assumption or adds a new premise for the conclusion.
4. For weaken items, find the choice that undermines the assumption or adds a new premise against the conclusion.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing strengthen with weaken. Read the stem carefully.
- Picking a choice that is true in real life but irrelevant to the argument.
- Picking a choice that affects a different part of the argument than the one being tested.

---

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Strategy for strengthen items

1. **Identify the conclusion.** The strengthen item asks which choice helps the conclusion.
2. **Identify the assumption.** Strengthening the assumption is the most direct way to strengthen the argument.
3. **Evaluate each choice.** Does it support the conclusion? Support the assumption? Add a new premise?
4. **Pick the most direct support.** The right answer is the choice that, if true, makes the conclusion more likely.

#### Strategy for weaken items

1. **Identify the conclusion.** The weaken item asks which choice hurts the conclusion.
2. **Identify the assumption.** Weakening the assumption is the most direct way to weaken the argument.
3. **Evaluate each choice.** Does it undermine the conclusion? Undermine the assumption? Add a new premise against the conclusion?
4. **Pick the most direct undermining.** The right answer is the choice that, if true, makes the conclusion less likely.

#### Common patterns

- **Methodological strengthen:** the study, sample, or method is more reliable than the argument assumes.
- **Methodological weaken:** the study, sample, or method is less reliable than the argument assumes.
- **Counterexample strengthen:** the argument's generalisation is supported by a specific case.
- **Counterexample weaken:** the argument's generalisation is undermined by a specific case.

#### Practice routine

- Take 10 strengthen items and 10 weaken items.
- For each, identify the assumption before evaluating the choices.
- Time yourself: under 1.5 minutes per item.
- Re-take the missed items the next day.

---

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How strengthen and weaken items connect to the other LR skills

- **Assumption items** ask for the missing link; strengthen/weaken items test which choice most affects that link.
- **Flaw items** ask what is wrong with the argument; weaken items often add evidence of the flaw.
- **Inference items** ask what must be true; strengthen/weaken items add new (true) information to test the conclusion.

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"Strengthen means prove."** Strengthen means make more likely, not prove beyond doubt.
- **"Weaken means disprove."** Weaken means make less likely, not disprove entirely.
- **"Any related choice works."** The right choice must directly affect the argument's conclusion or assumption.

#### Exam technique

- For strengthen items, ask: "which choice most supports the conclusion?"
- For weaken items, ask: "which choice most undermines the conclusion?"
- Use the denial test: which choice, if false, would most weaken the strengthen candidate?
- Use the platform's flag-for-review function for items you cannot answer.
