# Thermal Properties of Matter

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Thermal Properties of Matter** links microscopic kinetic energy to macroscopic quantities like heat *Q* (J), temperature *T* (K), expansion, and radiation. JEE Main draws one question per paper from this chapter, most often on expansion coefficients, calorimetry, Newton's cooling, or Stefan's law.

| Quantity | Formula | Variables & SI units |
|---|---|---|
| Linear expansion | ΔL = α L₀ ΔT | α in K⁻¹, L₀ in m, ΔT in K |
| Volume expansion | ΔV = γ V₀ ΔT | γ = 3α in K⁻¹, V₀ in m³ |
| Sensible heat | Q = m c ΔT | c in J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹, m in kg |
| Latent heat | Q = m L | L in J kg⁻¹ (water: L_f = 3.34×10⁵, L_v = 2.26×10⁶) |
| Radiated power | P = σ e A T⁴ | σ = 5.67×10⁻⁸ W m⁻² K⁻⁴, e dimensionless |
| Wien's law | λ_m T = 2.93×10⁻³ m·K | λ_m in m |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"α β γ go 1-2-3"** — for isotropic solids, the coefficients scale as linear : area : volume = 1α : 2α : 3α. And for radiation, **"SEAT"**: P = **S**igma · e · **A**rea · **T**⁴.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Mechanism — Heat vs Temperature

Heat *Q* is energy in transit measured in joules; temperature *T* is a state variable tied to the average translational kinetic energy of molecules (½ m⟨v²⟩ = 3/2 k_B T per molecule). Three transport modes move heat: **conduction** (Fourier's law, needs a medium), **convection** (bulk fluid motion), and **radiation** (electromagnetic waves, no medium required, governed by Stefan–Boltzmann and Wien).

#### Thermal Expansion — Solids, Liquids, Gases

Isotropic solids expand along all axes with related coefficients: **β = 2α** (area) and **γ = 3α** (volume). Liquids have only a volume coefficient; for a liquid in a vessel, the *apparent* expansion γ_apparent = γ_real − γ_vessel. Gases at constant pressure expand by γ = 1/T (an ideal-gas result, not a constant).

Water is anomalous: its density peaks at **4 °C**, so it contracts from 0→4 °C and expands from 4→100 °C. This is why ice floats and lakes freeze from the surface.

#### Calorimetry Principle

For an isolated system, **heat lost = heat gained**:
Σ m_i c_i ΔT_i + Σ m_j L_j = 0, summed over all components and any phase changes.

Steps for a mixing problem:
1. List masses, initial temperatures, and specific heats.
2. Assume a final equilibrium temperature T_f.
3. Write Σ m c (T_f − T_i) = 0; if T_f crosses a phase boundary, add mL terms.
4. Solve algebraically and verify T_f lies between the extremes.

#### Newton's Law of Cooling — When It Works

dT/dt = −k (T − T_s), giving T(t) = T_s + (T₀ − T_s) e^(−kt). Valid only for small excess temperature (≲ 30 K) and dominant convection/radiation losses; evaporation or forced drafts break the model.

#### Comparison Matrix — Easily Confused Quantities

| Term | Definition | SI unit | Common confusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Heat *Q* | Energy transferred due to ΔT | J | Confused with temperature |
| Temperature *T* | State variable, average KE/particle | K | Used as °C in formula (only ΔT is safe) |
| Specific heat *c* | Heat per kg per K | J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹ | Called "specific heat capacity" — both mean J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹, not J kg⁻¹ °C⁻¹ as a different unit |
| Latent heat *L* | Heat per kg during phase change | J kg⁻¹ | Temperature does **not** change during phase change |
| Conductivity *K* | Heat flow rate per area per gradient | W m⁻¹ K⁻¹ | Not the same as thermal conductance K·A/L (W K⁻¹) |
| Emissivity *e* | Fraction of black-body power radiated | dimensionless | Equals absorptivity *a* (Kirchhoff's law) |

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** A copper rod of length 2.000 m is heated from 20 °C to 120 °C. Given α_copper = 1.7 × 10⁻⁵ K⁻¹, find (a) the increase in length and (b) the new length. If a circular hole of diameter 10.0 mm is drilled through the rod, what is the diameter at 120 °C? [JEE Main style NVQ]

#### Solution:
Given: L₀ = 2.000 m, T₁ = 20 °C, T₂ = 120 °C, α = 1.7 × 10⁻⁵ K⁻¹.

(a) ΔT = 100 K (a 1 °C interval equals a 1 K interval for differences).
ΔL = α L₀ ΔT = (1.7 × 10⁻⁵)(2.000)(100) = 3.4 × 10⁻³ m = **3.4 mm**.

(b) L_f = L₀ + ΔL = 2.000 + 0.0034 = **2.0034 m**.

For the hole: a cavity expands as if filled with the surrounding material, so Δd = α d₀ ΔT = (1.7 × 10⁻⁵)(10.0 × 10⁻³)(100) = 1.7 × 10⁻⁵ m = **1.7 × 10⁻² mm = 17 µm**. New diameter = 10.017 mm.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students often assume the hole shrinks on heating because "material expands inward". The opposite is true: every linear dimension of an isotropic solid — including the diameter of any hole — grows by the factor (1 + α ΔT). Another common error: writing ΔT = 100 °C in a formula that demands kelvin; only ΔT values are interchangeable between K and °C, never absolute temperatures like 120 °C used as 120 in an exponent or in Stefan–Boltzmann's T⁴.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Boundary Conditions

1. **Composite (series) slab.** Steady-state conduction through *n* layers in series gives H = A ΔT_total / Σ(L_i / K_i). Parallel layers add conductances. This composite-slab problem recurs in JEE Main as a 1-mark calculation.
2. **Stefan's law with surroundings.** Net power radiated by a body at temperature T in an environment at T_s is **P_net = σ e A (T⁴ − T_s⁴)**. Many students omit T_s⁴ when T_s is "just room temperature" — but T = 300 K and T_s = 293 K differ enough to matter in numerical value questions.
3. **Wien's law and the solar constant.** The Sun's surface temperature ≈ 5800 K gives λ_m ≈ 2.93×10⁻³ / 5800 ≈ 505 nm, the visible green peak — a memorable sanity check.
4. **Newton's law breakdown.** At excess temperatures above ≈ 30 K or with strong forced convection, the linear approximation fails; radiative loss scales as T⁴ and dominates at high T. The rate constant k in Newton's law is **not universal** — it embeds A, e, and c of the body.
5. **Anomalous water — quantitative.** ρ_water peaks at 4 °C at 1000 kg m⁻³; the relative change from 4 °C to 0 °C is about 0.013 %, small but critical for aquatic ecosystems in winter.
6. **Calorimetry with latent heat.** A 10 g ice cube at −10 °C reaching 30 °C water requires Q = m c_ice (10) + m L_f + m c_water (30) = 10·2.1·10³·10 + 10·3.34×10⁵ + 10·4.18×10³·30 ≈ 4.6 kJ. Phase boundaries must be crossed in order.

#### Connections to Adjacent Topics

- **Kinetic Theory:** Average KE per molecule = (3/2) k_B T links temperature to gas microphysics; PV = nRT follows.
- **Thermodynamics:** Specific heats obey Mayer's relation c_P − c_V = R for one mole of ideal gas; ratio γ = c_P/c_V governs adiabatic processes.
- **Waves and Sound:** Speed of sound in an ideal gas c_sound = √(γRT/M) uses the same γ.
- **Modern Physics:** Black-body radiation curve underpins the UV catastrophe and Planck's hypothesis.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. A composite wall has three layers of thicknesses 2 cm, 3 cm, 5 cm with conductivities 50, 200, 100 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹. Inner face is at 600 K, outer at 300 K. Find interface temperatures and total heat flux per m².
2. A black sphere of radius 5 cm cools from 400 K to 350 K in 5 minutes in a room at 300 K. Using Stefan's law (treat as black, e = 1), estimate its specific heat capacity, assuming Newton's cooling form for the temperature range. Comment on whether the assumption is valid.

> 💡 **High-Yield Revision Hook:** For radiation NVQs, always write **P_net = σ e A (T⁴ − T_s⁴)** and check whether surroundings are included. For calorimetry, list Q = mcΔT first, then add mL at every phase boundary.
