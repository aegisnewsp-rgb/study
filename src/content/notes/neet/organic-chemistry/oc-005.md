# Carboxylic Acids

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

A **carboxylic acid** carries the functional group **–COOH** (carboxyl), in which a hydroxyl is bonded to a carbonyl carbon; general formula **R–COOH**, where R = H, alkyl, or aryl.

- Acidic because the conjugate base **RCOO⁻** is resonance-stabilised over two equivalent C–O bonds (bond order ≈ 1.5).
- **pKa trend:** HCOOH (3.75) > CH₃COOH (4.76) ≈ C₆H₅COOH (4.20) >> CF₃COOH (0.23) — electron-withdrawing groups sharpen acidity, electron-donating groups blunt it.
- Named reactions to remember: **Hell–Volhard–Zelinsky** (α-bromination with Br₂/red P), **Kolbe electrolysis** (2 RCOO⁻ → R–R + 2 CO₂), **Rosenmund** (RCOCl → RCHO with H₂/Pd-BaSO₄), **soda-lime decarboxylation** (RCOONa + NaOH/CaO → R–H).
- **NEET trap:** NaHCO₃ releases CO₂ only with carboxylic acids (stronger than carbonic acid, pKa ≈ 6.35), not with phenols or alcohols.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Nomenclature and Structure

IUPAC names end in **-oic acid**: methanoic (HCOOH), ethanoic (CH₃COOH), propanoic, butanoic, up the homologous series. Common names — formic, acetic, propionic, butyric, valeric — must be memorised for assertion–reason items.

The carbonyl carbon is **sp² hybridised**, planar, with the –OH oxygen also able to participate in resonance. In the carboxylate anion, both C–O bonds are equivalent (≈ 132 pm), an exam favourite illustrated by a single-line resonance depiction.

#### Acid Strength and Substituent Effects

| Acid | pKa (25 °C) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Trifluoroacetic (CF₃COOH) | 0.23 | Strong –I of three F atoms |
| Formic (HCOOH) | 3.75 | No alkyl, no +I donation |
| Benzoic (C₆H₅COOH) | 4.20 | Phenyl is mildly electron-withdrawing |
| Acetic (CH₃COOH) | 4.76 | Methyl donates electrons by +I |

Order of acidity: **RCOOH > H₂CO₃ > C₆H₅OH > HOH > ROH**, because each conjugate base enjoys greater resonance or electronegativity stabilisation.

#### Preparation Methods

1. Oxidation of primary alcohols or aldehydes with **KMnO₄** or **K₂Cr₂O₇/H⁺**.
2. Hydrolysis of nitriles: R–CN → R–CONH₂ → R–COOH (H⁺/H₂O).
3. **Grignard carbonation:** RMgX + CO₂ → RCOOMgX → (H₃O⁺) RCOOH.
4. Ozonolysis of alkenes, then oxidative work-up.

#### Reactions at a Glance

- With NaOH → RCOONa; with NaHCO₃ → RCOONa + H₂O + CO₂↑ (diagnostic test).
- **Fischer esterification:** RCOOH + R'OH ⇌ RCOOR' + H₂O (acid catalyst, reversible).
- With PCl₅ / SOCl₂ → RCOCl; with NH₃ → RCONH₂; with (RCO)₂O → acid anhydride.
- **HVZ bromination** installs Br at the α-carbon; **Kolbe electrolysis** dimerises two acyl units into a higher alkane.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Physical Properties and Dimerisation

Even in vapour and non-polar solvents, two acid molecules associate head-to-tail through **two O–H···O hydrogen bonds**, forming a cyclic **dimer**. This raises boiling points roughly **20–25 °C per added CH₂** along CH₃(CH₂)ₙCOOH — a value worth memorising for assertion–reason stems. Water miscibility fades after C₄ because the hydrophobic alkyl chain outweighs H-bonding capacity.

#### Edge Cases and the Ortho Effect

*Ortho*-substituted benzoic acids (e.g., *o*-nitro, *o*-methyl) often break the simple inductive/resonance ranking: steric crowding twists –COOH out of the ring plane, **inhibiting resonance with the ring** and amplifying the substituent's inductive pull. Expect NEET to ask why *o*-toluic acid is anomalously stronger than its *p*-isomer despite –CH₃ being a +I donor.

#### Named-Reaction Micro-Example — Kolbe Electrolysis

Electrolyse sodium acetate in aqueous solution at inert electrodes. At the anode:
2 CH₃COO⁻ → CH₃–CH₃ + 2 CO₂ + 2 e⁻. Net result: **two acyl radicals couple, losing both carboxyl groups as CO₂**, producing ethane. This is the cleanest exam-style test of "which bonds break and which form."

#### Common Exam Traps

- Writing the decarboxylation product as R–CH₃; soda lime genuinely replaces –COOH with **–H**, giving R–H.
- Confusing Rosenmund (acid chloride → aldehyde) with LiAlH₄ reduction (acid → primary alcohol, never stops at aldehyde).
- Drawing Fischer esterification with arrows in the wrong direction; the acid-catalysed mechanism passes through a tetrahedral intermediate.

#### Practice Prompts

1. Arrange in increasing acid strength: **CH₃CH₂COOH, ClCH₂CH₂COOH, CH₃CHClCOOH, Cl₂CHCH₂COOH, Cl₃CCH₂COOH** — justify using inductive distance and number of –Cl.
2. Identify reagents for the chain: **CH₃CH₂OH → CH₃CHO → CH₃COOH → CH₃COCl → CH₃CONH₂**, naming each step's reagent and mechanism class.
