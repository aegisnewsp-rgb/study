# Measures, Shape and Space — Geometry of Circles — HKDSE Mathematics Notes

This topic covers the four HKDSE Compulsory Part learning units in the Measures, Shape and Space strand: basic properties of circles (chord-tangent angles, alternate segment theorem, cyclic quadrilateral), locus in the plane, equations of straight lines and circles, and more about trigonometry. These units appear on both Paper 1 and Paper 2; they require fluency in theorem application and coordinate geometry.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Circle theorems**: angle in a semicircle is 90°; angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference subtending the same arc; angles in the same segment are equal; the angle between a tangent and a chord through the point of tangency equals the angle in the alternate segment.
- **Cyclic quadrilateral**: opposite angles sum to 180°; exterior angle equals the interior opposite angle.
- **Locus**: the set of points satisfying a given geometric condition. The perpendicular bisector of segment AB is the locus of points equidistant from A and B. The bisector of angle ABC is the locus of points equidistant from BA and BC.
- **Straight-line equation**: y = mx + c with slope m and y-intercept c. Parallel lines have equal m; perpendicular lines satisfy m₁·m₂ = -1.
- **Circle equation**: (x - a)² + (y - b)² = r² has center (a, b) and radius r. The general form x² + y² + Dx + Ey + F = 0 can be converted to standard form by completing the square.
- **Trigonometry**: sine rule a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C; cosine rule a² = b² + c² - 2bc·cos A; area = 1/2 · ab · sin C; bearings and heights-and-distances problems.

#### Examiner traps

- Forgetting to use the alternate segment theorem correctly — the angle in the alternate segment must be on the opposite side of the chord from the tangent.
- Confusing the radius (perpendicular to the tangent at the point of contact) with the diameter (a chord through the centre).
- Mixing up the sine and cosine rules — use the cosine rule when two sides and the included angle (SAS) or three sides (SSS) are known; use the sine rule when two angles and a side (AAS) or two sides and a non-included angle (SSA) are known.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Circle theorems and their use

The most-tested circle theorems in the HKDSE are:

1. **Angles in the same segment are equal.** Two angles subtended by the same chord and standing on the same arc are equal.
2. **Angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference.** If O is the centre and A, B, C are points on the circle with C on the major arc, then ∠AOB = 2∠ACB.
3. **Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180°.** If ABCD is cyclic, then ∠A + ∠C = 180° and ∠B + ∠D = 180°.
4. **Exterior angle of a cyclic quadrilateral equals the interior opposite angle.** If ABCD is cyclic, the exterior angle at A equals ∠C.
5. **Tangent-chord angle equals the angle in the alternate segment.** If TA is tangent at A and AB is a chord, then ∠TAB equals the angle subtended by AB at any point on the circle on the opposite side of AB from T.

For each theorem, identify the chord, the tangent, the arc and the relevant angle before applying.

#### Locus in the plane

The HKDSE Compulsory Part tests the following loci:

- **Perpendicular bisector** of a segment: equidistant from the two endpoints.
- **Angle bisector**: equidistant from the two arms of the angle.
- **Circle**: equidistant from a fixed point.
- **Arc of a circle**: equidistant from a fixed point but restricted to a specified half-plane.

Locus problems are often combined: "find the locus of points that are 3 cm from point P and at least 2 cm from line L" → circle of radius 3 centred at P, intersected with the half-plane at distance ≥ 2 from L.

#### Equations of straight lines and circles

For a straight line:

- **Slope-intercept form**: y = mx + c.
- **Point-slope form**: y - y₁ = m(x - x₁).
- **General form**: Ax + By + C = 0 (slope = -A/B, intercept = -C/B).

For a circle with centre (a, b) and radius r:

- **Standard form**: (x - a)² + (y - b)² = r².
- **General form**: x² + y² + Dx + Ey + F = 0 with a = -D/2, b = -E/2, r² = (D/2)² + (E/2)² - F.

Intersection of a line and a circle: substitute y = mx + c into (x - a)² + (y - b)² = r², solve the resulting quadratic. The discriminant determines whether the line is a secant (Δ > 0), tangent (Δ = 0) or external (Δ < 0).

#### Trigonometry

The sine rule, cosine rule and the formula area = 1/2·ab·sin C solve most HKDSE triangle problems. For a height-and-distance problem:

1. Draw a diagram labelling the known angle (usually the angle of elevation or depression), the known side, and the unknown.
2. Choose sine rule, cosine rule or right-angled trigonometry based on the data given.
3. Compute the unknown, then check units and reasonableness.

For bearings, remember that bearings are measured clockwise from north and lie in [0°, 360°]. When a problem involves two bearings from different points, the angles between them relate to the geometry of the triangle formed.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-topic links

- **Locus + algebra**: locus problems reduce to Cartesian equations. The perpendicular bisector of (0, 0) and (6, 0) is x = 3. The angle bisector of the axes y = 0 and x = 0 in the first quadrant is y = x.
- **Circles + inequalities**: a region inside or outside a circle is described by an inequality. The set of points with (x - 1)² + (y + 2)² < 9 is the interior of a circle of radius 3 centred at (1, -2).
- **Trigonometry + coordinate geometry**: parametric form (x = r·cos θ, y = r·sin θ) describes points on a circle. Angles in the unit circle link trigonometry to coordinates.
- **Vectors (Extended Part Module 2)**: the scalar and vector products in 3D extend the trigonometry of the plane to space.

#### Exam technique

- For circle theorem problems, state the theorem you are using by name (or mark the equal angles with the same arc symbol). HKDSE examiners credit correct identification.
- For locus problems, show the construction (perpendicular bisector, angle bisector, circle) and state the locus verbally ("the locus is the perpendicular bisector of AB") before moving to the algebraic equation.
- For line-circle intersections, write the quadratic in standard form first, then apply the discriminant and the quadratic formula. Show all substitution work — partial marks depend on it.
- For height-and-distance, sketch the diagram carefully; a mislabelled angle loses 1-2 marks even if the subsequent work is correct.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "A tangent touches the circle at one point." True, but the tangent is also perpendicular to the radius at the point of tangency — this is the more useful property for HKDSE problems.
- "The angle between a tangent and a chord is 90°." It is not 90° unless the chord is the diameter. Use the alternate segment theorem.
- "Bearing N 30° E is the same as 30° clockwise from east." Bearing N 30° E is 30° east of north — i.e. 30° clockwise from north, not from east.
