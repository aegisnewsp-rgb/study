# Further Learning Unit — Inquiry, Investigation and Applications — HKDSE Mathematics Notes

This topic covers the HKDSE Compulsory Part Further Learning Unit, which comprises the two strands "Inquiry and Investigation" and "Further Applications". Unlike the other Compulsory Part units which are assessed directly through their own questions, the Further Learning Unit is assessed implicitly across Paper 1 Section B and Paper 2 Section B: open-ended problems, modelling questions, and items requiring mathematical reasoning, proof and communication. The strand is the published venue for the HKDSE's emphasis on mathematical literacy, problem-solving and communication.

> Verify the live Compulsory Part unit list and the assessment framework on https://www.hkeaa.edu.hk/ before planning revision.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Inquiry and investigation**: tackling open-ended problems; formulating a mathematical model from a real-world situation; communicating results; reasoning and proof at secondary level.
- **Further applications**: cross-strand problems that require integration of algebra, geometry and data handling; modelling with functions; interpreting mathematical results in context.
- **Assessment venue**: Paper 1 Section B (open-ended conventional questions) and Paper 2 Section B (multiple-choice items that require interpretation or application). The Further Learning Unit is not a separate paper — its content is integrated into the two Compulsory Part papers.

#### Examiner traps

- Treating the Further Learning Unit as "extra" — it is a required part of the curriculum and contributes marks indirectly through Paper 1 Section B.
- Skipping proof-style questions on Paper 1 — these test mathematical reasoning, a separately-weighted competency.
- Failing to communicate the solution — HKDSE marks for communication include labelled diagrams, clear notation, and explicit "therefore" statements.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Inquiry and investigation

The HKDSE Compulsory Part inquiry-and-investigation work falls into four categories:

1. **Modelling**: given a real-world situation, set up variables, write equations, solve, and interpret the result in context. Example: "a tank fills at 5 litres per minute and empties at 3 litres per minute; how long until the tank is full?" → equation 5t - 3t = capacity → t = capacity / 2.
2. **Reasoning and proof**: prove a statement by direct argument, by contradiction, or by counterexample. At the HKDSE Compulsory Part level, proofs are typically short — show two cases satisfy a property, or show a general expression simplifies to a known form.
3. **Open-ended problems**: a problem with multiple solution paths, multiple valid answers, or no single "right answer". These test the ability to choose and justify an approach.
4. **Communication**: present a solution clearly with diagrams, tables, and explanatory text. Marks for communication are common in HKDSE marking schemes.

#### Further applications

Cross-strand problems combine topics from at least two of the three strands (Number and Algebra, Measures Shape and Space, Data Handling). Typical examples:

- A 2D shape problem requiring both coordinate geometry (to find a vertex) and algebra (to write a model equation).
- A data-handling problem requiring measures of dispersion (to interpret the spread) and algebra (to compute a regression line).
- An optimisation problem requiring linear programming (graphical) and the geometry of the feasible region.

The key skill is identifying which tools to apply. Practice by working through past Paper 1 Section B items and labelling each step with the tool used.

#### Mathematical communication at secondary level

The HKDSE marking scheme rewards:

- Labelled diagrams (vertices named, axes labelled, distances marked).
- Clear notation (using the same letter for the same quantity; introducing variables explicitly).
- Logical flow ("from X, we deduce Y because Z").
- Final answers with units where applicable.

A well-communicated solution does not need to be long — but each step must be clear. A 5-mark question typically rewards 2-3 marks for method, 1 mark for correct simplification, and 1 mark for the final answer.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Why this strand matters beyond HKDSE

- **Mathematical reasoning** is the foundation for Extended Part Module 1 (Calculus and Statistics) and Module 2 (Algebra and Calculus). Proof and argument are central to calculus limits and to vectors.
- **Communication** is the skill that determines whether a student can apply mathematics to novel problems at university. HKDSE Paper 1 Section B items test this directly.
- **Modelling** is the bridge between school mathematics and applied mathematics, statistics, physics and engineering at university.

#### Exam technique

- For an open-ended problem, read the question carefully and identify the givens and the unknown before writing anything. Many candidates lose marks by jumping into a calculation that does not answer the question.
- For a proof, start from the side of the statement that is easier to work with, then derive the other side. State "W" or "to prove" at the top of the proof so the examiner knows where the argument is going.
- For a modelling problem, define all variables explicitly before writing any equations. "Let t be the time in minutes since the tank starts filling" is clearer than "let t = time".

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Open-ended means anything goes." It means the question has multiple solution paths, not that any answer is correct. Each path must be justified and the answer interpreted.
- "A proof is just a calculation." A proof establishes a general statement; a calculation finds a specific value. The difference is the level of generality.
- "Communication is not marked." It is — the HKDSE marking scheme explicitly rewards clear diagrams, labelled axes, and explicit "therefore" statements.
