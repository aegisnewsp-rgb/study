# Legal Maxims

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Legal maxims are short Latin (occasionally English) phrases that compress a rule of law into a memorable formula. CLAT tests them three ways: straight recall of meaning, identification of the correct maxim for a fact pattern, and inference of the legal consequence.

- **Mens Rea** — guilty mind; required alongside *Actus Reus* (guilty act) for most crimes.
- **Audi Alteram Partem** — hear the other side; the natural-justice hearing rule.
- **Ignorantia Juris Non Excusat** — ignorance of law is no excuse.
- **Caveat Emptor** — let the buyer beware; displaced in modern consumer law.
- **Res Ipsa Loquitur** — the thing speaks for itself; negligence is presumed from the very nature of the injury.

| Maxim | Core Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Volenti Non Fit Injuria | Consent to risk bars the claim |
| Nemo Judex In Causa Sua | No one may be judge in their own cause |
| Pacta Sunt Servanda | Agreements must be kept |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Classification by Function

Legal maxims fall into three functional buckets, each tested differently. *Substantive-law* maxims (Mens Rea, Pacta Sunt Servanda, Caveat Emptor) create or negate liability. *Procedural* maxims (Audi Alteram Partem, Nemo Judex In Causa Sua, Habeas Corpus) govern how disputes are decided. *Evidential* maxims (Res Ipsa Loquitur, Prima Facie) shift the burden of proof.

#### Contract-Law Maxims

- **Pacta Sunt Servanda** — once parties bind themselves, the contract binds (Section 10 Indian Contract Act anchors free consent).
- **Expressio Unius Est Exclusio Alterius** — naming one thing excludes another; used in statutory interpretation.
- **Noscitur a Sociis** — a word takes colour from its companion words.
- **Ejusdem Generis** — general words following specific words are restricted to the same class.

#### Criminal-Law Maxims

- **Actus Non Facit Reum Nisi Mens Sit Rea** — an act alone does not convict without guilty mind (Section 6 IPC).
- **Ignorantia Juris Non Excusat** — even honest ignorance of law is no defence.
- **Volenti Non Fit Injuria** — freely accepted risk extinguishes negligence liability.

| Maxim | Statutory Anchor | Common Trap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mens Rea | Section 6 IPC | Mistaken for Actus Reus |
| Caveat Emptor | Section 14 Sale of Goods Act, 1930 | Still absolute today |
| Res Ipsa Loquitur | Evidence law (British origin) | Treat as a rule of liability |
| Audi Alteram Partem | Article 14 / 21 Constitution | Confused with Nemo Judex |

#### Procedure and Evidence

*Res Ipsa Loquitur* lets the court infer negligence when an injury would not occur without it (e.g., a surgical swab left inside a patient). *Audi Alteram Partem* and *Nemo Judex In Causa Sua* together form the two pillars of natural justice embedded in Article 14.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Worked Application

**Fact pattern:** A, a labourer, agrees to climb a crumbling wall knowing it may collapse, and is injured. He sues B, the contractor.

- Wrong answer: *Caveat Emptor* — sale doctrine, irrelevant here.
- Wrong answer: *Audi Alteram Partem* — procedural, not a liability shield.
- Correct answer: **Volenti Non Fit Injuria**, because consent to known risk defeats negligence.
- Follow-up test: was the consent free? Compulsion or fraud under Section 14 Contract Act rebuts it.

#### High-Yield Pitfalls

1. Translating *Audi Alteram Partem* as "judge in one's own cause" — that is *Nemo Judex In Causa Sua*. The two maxims are distinct.
2. Treating *Caveat Emptor* as still absolute — the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 imposes strict liability for defects and unfair trade practices.
3. Reading *Ignorantia Juris Non Excusat* as covering mistaken private rights — it covers law only, not fact (*Ignorantia Facti Excusat*).
4. Confusing *Prima Facie* with *Res Ipsa Loquitur* — the first shifts an evidentiary burden; the second permits an inference of negligence from the event itself.

#### CLAT Strategy

Expect 4–6 questions per paper, drawn heavily from Mens Rea, Volenti, Ignorantia Juris, Res Ipsa, Audi Alteram Partem, and Caveat Emptor. Master 25–30 maxims with their English meaning and one Indian statutory anchor; answer time per item is under 90 seconds.

#### Practice Prompts

1. A pedestrian is hit by a lorry whose brakes failed. Identify the maxim permitting the court to infer the driver's negligence.
2. A seller knowingly hides a car's accident history. The buyer sues. Which maxim of contractual interpretation overrides *Caveat Emptor* here?
