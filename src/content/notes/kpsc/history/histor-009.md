# Contemporary India: Issues and Challenges

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Contemporary India: Issues and Challenges** is a KAS General Studies theme covering post-independence socio-economic, communal, regional, and developmental problems confronting the Indian state since 1947. The theme links historical nation-building with present-day policy stress points like communalism, regionalism, caste conflict, terrorism, Naxalism, environmental decay, and globalization's uneven fallout. For KAS, expect mains analytical questions (150–250 words) and factual prelims MCQs on definitions, Articles, and committee names.

- **Secularism (Art. 25–28)** — Indian model means equal respect for all religions, not Western separation of state and religion; Khilnani calls this *principled distance*.
- **LPG Reforms 1991** — Liberalization, Privatization, Globalization triggered growth but widened inequality, displaced labour, and stressed MSMEs and agriculture.
- **Poverty measurement** — Tendulkar Committee (2009–10) revised the poverty line using calorie-norm + consumption; replaced the older Lakdawala method.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Defining the Theme
Post-1947 India inherited thePartition's communal trauma, princely-state integration legacies, and a planned-economy framework. By the 1990s, three fault-lines sharpened: identity-based politics (communalism, regionalism, casteism), market-driven inequality (post-1991 LPG reforms), and resource stress (urbanization, climate, river disputes). KAS examiners frame this theme to test whether candidates can connect **historical causation** to **contemporary policy failure**.

#### Core Issues at a Glance
| Issue | Mechanism | Constitutional / Policy Anchor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Communalism | Religious identity politicised; riots (1947, 1984, 2002, 2013 Muzaffarnagar) | Article 25–28; Sachar Committee 2006 |
| Regionalism | Linguistic / ethnic assertion; sub-national movements | Article 3 (statehood); 6th Schedule (tribal areas) |
| Caste & Class | Hereditary hierarchy + market inequality intersect | Article 15(4), 16(4); Mandal Commission |
| Naxalism / Insurgency | Ideological + governance vacuum in tribal belts | CRPF, SAMADHAN doctrine; 5th Schedule |
| Gender Issues | Skewed sex ratio, violence, unpaid care work | POSH Act 2013; Dowry Prohibition Act 1961; BBBP |
| Environmental Stress | Air pollution, deforestation, river disputes | NCAP 2019; EPA 1986; CPCB |

#### Exam Patterns to Watch
- Mains: 10-mark questions ask "critically analyse" the communalism-secularism tension or "discuss" the social impact of LPG reforms.
- Prelims: match the following — Tendulkar Committee with poverty line; Sachar Committee with Muslim socio-economic status.
- **Common trap**: treating Indian secularism as identical to the US First Amendment model — it's *principled distance*, not separation.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Mechanism: How Challenges Interlock
Communalism and regionalism often fuse with caste politics to fragment the vote bank, while LPG reforms concentrate capital and trigger agrarian distress — this distress feeds Naxal recruitment in tribal belts (Red Corridor spans 90+ districts across Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Odisha). Urbanization compounds the cycle: rapid migration to Bengaluru, Mumbai, Delhi stretches civic infrastructure, inflates housing costs, and produces the very informal labour pool that globalization exploits cheaply.

#### Karnataka Linkages (KAS-Specific)
Karnataka features heavily in this theme. The **Cauvery dispute** with Tamil Nadu, the **Mahadayi / Kalasa-Banduri Nala** issue with Goa, **Tiger reserves (Bandipur, Nagarhole)** vs. development pressure, and the **Kodagu / DK district** linguistic-cultural assertions all surface in KAS Mains. Linkages to **Mullaperiyar** (Kerala) and **Krishna Water Tribunal** rulings are common sub-questions.

#### Common Mistakes Candidates Make
1. Citing poverty statistics without naming the **Tendulkar (2009–10)** or **Rangarajan** methodology — examiners deduct marks for vague figures.
2. Confusing **Article 25** (freedom of religion) with **Article 44** (Uniform Civil Code directive).
3. Treating regionalism as automatically separatist — ignore constructive statehood / autonomy demands (Telangana 2014 is a recent success case).
4. Ignoring Naxalism's **internal** dimensions because the discourse defaults to cross-border terrorism.
5. Writing only political answers and skipping environmental / gender dimensions — losing breadth marks.

#### Practice Prompts
1. "Communalism in India is more a political than a religious phenomenon." Discuss with reference to post-1992 developments.
2. Critically examine the social costs of LPG reforms on Karnataka's agrarian and informal-labour economy.
