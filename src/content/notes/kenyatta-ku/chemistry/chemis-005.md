# Alcohols: Classification, Preparation, and Reactions

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> The -OH carbon decides everything: how the molecule is named, how it boils, which tests it answers.

An **alcohol** carries a hydroxyl (-OH) bonded to a saturated sp³ carbon of an alkyl chain, giving the general formula **CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH** for monohydric members. Methanol (CH₃OH), ethanol (C₂H₅OH), and propan-1-ol (C₃H₇OH) sit at the head of the homologous series. Classification splits three ways: by -OH count (mono-, di-, tri-hydric), by the carbinol carbon (1°, 2°, 3°), and by ring type (aliphatic vs aromatic; phenols are *not* alcohols).

| Property | Detail |
|---|---|
| General formula | CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH (monohydric, saturated) |
| Functional group | Hydroxyl (-O-H) on sp³ C |
| Polarity source | Intermolecular H-bonding |
| Key test reagents | Lucas (ZnCl₂/HCl), K₂Cr₂O₇/H⁺, I₂/NaOH (iodoform) |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook — "1° slow, 2° medium, 3° zip":** *Tertiary alcohols react instantly with Lucas reagent, secondary take about five minutes, primary stay quiet at room temperature.* Memorise the order **3° > 2° > 1°** and you have answered half the classification questions on a KU paper.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Work through nomenclature first, then the physical-property anomalies, then the three named tests examiners reach for every year.

#### Nomenclature and Classification

Replace the terminal **-e** of the parent alkane with **-ol**; number the chain to give the -OH the lowest locant. Methanol, ethanol, propan-1-ol, propan-2-ol, ethane-1,2-diol, and propane-1,2,3-triol (IUPAC for glycerol) are the bench compounds. The carbinol carbon's substitution count fixes the 1°/2°/3° label; diols and triols add the dihydric/trihydric prefix.

#### Physical Properties — Why Boiling Points Misbehave

Alcohols boil far higher than alkanes or haloalkanes of comparable molar mass because each molecule hydrogen-bonds to its neighbours. The O–H···O bond energy sits around 20 kJ·mol⁻¹ per interaction, and lower members (methanol, ethanol, propan-1-ol) are fully miscible with water. As the alkyl chain grows past C₄, water solubility drops sharply while boiling point keeps climbing.

#### Preparation Routes

| Route | Reagent / condition | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Fermentation of sugars | C₆H₁₂O₆ —(zymase, 298–303 K)→ 2 C₂H₅OH + 2 CO₂ | Industrial ethanol |
| Hydration of ethene | CH₂=CH₂ + H₂O —(H₃PO₄, 573 K, 60–70 atm)→ C₂H₅OH | Petroleum-route ethanol |
| Hydrolysis of haloalkanes | R–X + aq. NaOH (Sₙ1/Sₙ2) | R–OH |
| Reduction of carbonyls | NaBH₄ or LiAlH₄ on R-CHO, R-CO-R′, R-COOH | 1° or 2° alcohol |
| Grignard addition | R′-MgX + R-CHO → 2° alcohol; + R-CO-R′ → 3° alcohol | Custom carbon skeleton |

> 📌 **Formula Check:** ΔH of fermentation ≈ −118 kJ·mol⁻¹; theoretical yield of ethanol reaches about 95 % of stoichiometric maximum when zymase remains active.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix — Tests That Decide 1°, 2°, and 3°

| Test | Reagent | 1° alcohol | 2° alcohol | 3° alcohol |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lucas | ZnCl₂ / conc. HCl | No reaction (RT) | Cloudiness in ≈ 5 min | Cloudiness immediately |
| Oxidation | K₂Cr₂O₇ / H⁺ | R-CHO → R-COOH | R-CO-R′ (ketone) | No change (no α-H on carbinol C) |
| Victor Meyer | P/I₂, then AgNO₃/NaOH | Red colour | White → violet | No colour |
| Iodoform | I₂ / NaOH, warm | Yellow ppt only if CH₃CH(OH)R | Yellow ppt if CH₃CH(OH)R | Negative |

#### Worked Problem

A Kenyan KCSE-A level question asks: *"Compound X has molecular formula C₃H₈O. With K₂Cr₂O₇/H⁺ it gives a ketone; with conc. H₂SO₄ at 443 K it loses water to form an alkene; with I₂/NaOH it forms a yellow precipitate. Identify X and write equations for the three reactions."*

Compound X must be **propan-2-ol** (CH₃-CH(OH)-CH₃), the only C₃H₈O isomer that oxidises to a ketone *and* gives a positive iodoform test.

1. **Oxidation to ketone:** (CH₃)₂CHOH + [O] → (CH₃)₂C=O + H₂O. Acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ (M = 294.19 g·mol⁻¹) supplies [O]; acetone forms because the carbinol carbon still holds one H.
2. **Dehydration to alkene:** (CH₃)₂CHOH —(conc. H₂SO₄, 443 K)→ CH₂=CH-CH₃ + H₂O. Temperature is in **kelvin**; 443 K equals 170 °C.
3. **Iodoform test:** CH₃-CH(OH)-CH₃ + 4 I₂ + 6 NaOH → CHI₃↓ + CH₃COONa + 5 NaI + 5 H₂O. The yellow CHI₃ precipitate confirms the CH₃-CH(OH)- group.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Writing "C₆H₁₂O₆ → C₂H₅OH + CO₂" loses the coefficient 2 on each product. The balanced form is C₆H₁₂O₆ → **2**C₂H₅OH + **2**CO₂. Kenyatta markers also deduct for naming glycerol "glycol" instead of propane-1,2,3-triol.

#### Chemical Reactions Summary

- **Acidity:** R-OH + Na → R-ONa + ½H₂. Order: CH₃OH > 1° > 2° > 3°; phenols sit above water.
- **Esterification (Fischer):** R-COOH + R′-OH ⇌ R-COOR′ + H₂O; catalysed by conc. H₂SO₄, reversible.
- **Halogenation:** R-OH + PCl₅ → R-Cl + POCl₃ + HCl; PBr₃ and SOCl₂ are milder alternatives.
- **Alkoxide formation:** R-OH + NaOH (limited) or R-ONa + R′-X (Williamson ether synthesis).

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Edge cases live where mechanism meets substitution. Push past memorised reagents and ask which bond breaks first.

#### Mechanism Boundaries

Dehydration of 1° alcohols follows **E2** because the carbinol carbon is unhindered; 3° alcohols prefer **E1** through a stable carbocation. The same logic predicts Lucas reactivity: 3° cations form fastest, so ZnCl₂-HCl clouds immediately. Attempting to oxidise a 3° alcohol with K₂Cr₂O₇ produces nothing because the carbinol carbon carries no α-hydrogen to lose; this is the single most-marked distinction in KU organic essays.

#### Phenols Are Not Alcohols

Although both carry -OH, a phenol's -OH sits on an sp² aromatic carbon. Consequences cascade: phenols are weak acids (pKₐ ≈ 10), give a violet colour with neutral FeCl₃, and fail the Lucas and iodoform tests. Naming Ar-OH as "alcohol" is an automatic deduction.

#### Kenyan Industrial and Environmental Contexts

Ethanol in Kenya arrives through two pipelines: sugarcane molasses fermentation in Western Kenya and the East African Breweries hydrating imported ethene. The Kenyan government taxes illicit brews (*chang'aa*) partly because their methanol content — produced when fermentation distils poorly — blinds consumers; this links the topic directly to public-health reporting. Bioethanol blending into petrol (E10) sits in the same syllabus week as catalytic hydration at 573 K.

#### Edge-Case Table

| Edge case | Behaviour | Common mistake |
|---|---|---|
| Allylic alcohol (e.g. CH₂=CH-CH₂-OH) | Oxidises to aldehyde *and* decarboxylates with excess K₂Cr₂O₇ | Treating it as a saturated 1° |
| Diol oxidation with HIO₄ | Cleaves 1,2-diols to two carbonyls | Confusing with periodic acid vs. periodate |
| Iodoform on ethanol | Yellow ppt of CHI₃ | Forgetting that the methyl carbinol must be present |
| Dehydration of a diol | Gives a diene or carbonyl (pinacol rearrangement for 1,2-diols) | Assuming simple alkene loss |
