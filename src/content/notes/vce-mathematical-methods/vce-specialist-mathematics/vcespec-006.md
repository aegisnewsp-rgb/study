# Mechanics and Kinematics — VCE Specialist Mathematics (VCAA 2023) Notes

Mechanics and Kinematics is a Year 12 topic in VCE Specialist Mathematics Units 3 and 4. The 2023 Study Design covers motion in one and two dimensions using vector and parametric methods, Newton's laws applied to particles on inclined planes and connected bodies, work, energy, power and momentum. Mechanics is the most applied strand of Specialist Mathematics.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Kinematics in 1D.** v(t) = s'(t), a(t) = v'(t). Constant acceleration: v = u + at, s = ut + (1/2)at², v² = u² + 2as.
- **Kinematics in 2D.** Position r(t) = x(t) i + y(t) j. Velocity v(t) = r'(t). Acceleration a(t) = r''(t). Projectile motion: x(t) = u cos θ × t, y(t) = u sin θ × t − (1/2) g t².
- **Newton's second law.** F = ma. F and a are vectors; m is a scalar.
- **Forces on inclined planes.** Component of weight parallel to slope: mg sin θ. Normal to slope: mg cos θ.
- **Work.** W = F · d = |F||d| cos θ.
- **Energy.** KE = (1/2) m v². PE = mgh. Conservation of energy: KE + PE = constant (no friction).
- **Momentum.** p = m v. Impulse J = F Δt = Δp.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing weight (a force) with mass (a property).
- Mixing up kinetic and potential energy in conservation problems.
- Treating F = ma as scalar when forces are at angles.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Kinematics

For motion in 1D: position s(t), velocity v(t) = s'(t), acceleration a(t) = v'(t). Constant acceleration formulas: v = u + at; s = ut + (1/2)at²; v² = u² + 2as; s = (u + v)t/2.

For motion in 2D using vectors: position r(t) = x(t) i + y(t) j. Velocity v(t) = r'(t). Acceleration a(t) = r''(t). Speed = |v(t)|.

Projectile motion (constant gravity, no air resistance): horizontal velocity is constant; vertical acceleration is −g. Trajectory is a parabola.

#### Newton's laws

First law: an object remains at rest or in uniform motion unless acted on by a net force. Second law: F = ma. Third law: for every action there is an equal and opposite reaction.

#### Inclined planes

For an object on a smooth incline of angle θ: weight mg acts vertically downward. Components: parallel to slope mg sin θ (down the slope), perpendicular to slope mg cos θ (into the slope, balanced by normal force).

#### Work and energy

Work W = F · d = |F||d| cos θ (when F is constant). Kinetic energy KE = (1/2) m v². Gravitational PE = mgh. Work-energy theorem: W_net = ΔKE. Conservation of mechanical energy: KE + PE = constant (no non-conservative forces).

#### Momentum and impulse

Momentum p = m v. Impulse J = F Δt = Δp. In collisions, momentum is conserved if no external forces. Elastic collisions conserve both momentum and KE.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Why Mechanics is the most applied Specialist strand

- **Mechanics questions test modelling.** A physics scenario must be translated into vectors and equations, then solved.
- **Newton's laws apply to connected bodies, pulleys, springs, and projectiles.** Each scenario has a standard approach.

#### Exam technique

- Always draw a diagram with forces labelled.
- Resolve forces into components along the direction of motion and perpendicular to it.
- Apply F = ma along the direction of motion.
- Use energy conservation where appropriate (smooth surfaces).
- Use momentum conservation for collision problems.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Mass and weight are the same." Mass is a property; weight = mg is a force.
- "Velocity and acceleration are the same." Velocity is a vector; acceleration is the rate of change of velocity.
- "All motion is at constant velocity." No — most realistic motion has variable acceleration.
