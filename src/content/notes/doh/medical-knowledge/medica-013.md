# Health Promotion and Patient Education

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Health promotion enables individuals and communities to gain control over the determinants of health, while patient education is the one-to-one clinical process of teaching self-management during consultations. In the DOH (UAE) scope, both align with the national Weqaya and Fit-for-Life agendas and must follow DOH Patient Education documentation standards.

- **Three levels of prevention:** primary (before onset, e.g. vaccination, smoking cessation), secondary (early detection, e.g. HbA1c screening, mammography), tertiary (limiting complications, e.g. diabetic foot care, post-stroke rehab).
- **Ottawa Charter (1986):** five action areas — build healthy public policy, create supportive environments, strengthen community action, develop personal skills, reorient health services.
- **Counselling tools:** motivational interviewing (OARS) and the transtheoretical stages of change.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Levels of Prevention Applied to UAE Practice

The levels-of-prevention framework is the single most tested concept on the DOH exam. Each level targets a different point along the disease timeline, and examiners often disguise screening as primary prevention to trap candidates.

| Level | Goal | DOH-context example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary | Prevent disease before onset | HPV and influenza vaccination, tobacco-cessation counselling, salt-reduction campaigns |
| Secondary | Detect disease early in at-risk groups | Annual HbA1c for adults ≥30, mammography every 2 years for women 40–69, newborn metabolic screening |
| Tertiary | Reduce complications and disability in established disease | Foot-care programme in diabetic neuropathy, cardiac rehab post-MI, asthma action plans |

#### Patient-Education Process and Documentation

Patient education follows a five-step clinical pathway: assess learning needs, set SMART objectives, plan content and method, deliver using teach-back, then evaluate adherence. The teach-back method — asking the patient to repeat instructions in their own words — is a DOH clinical-governance requirement, not optional bedside courtesy. Education must be documented in the medical record as a discrete entry covering topic, method, language used, and comprehension confirmed.

#### Behaviour-Change Counselling

Motivational interviewing uses **OARS** (Open questions, Affirmations, Reflections, Summaries) to resolve ambivalence. The **transtheoretical model** matches intervention to the patient's stage: precontemplation (raise awareness), contemplation (explore ambivalence), preparation (set goals), action (skills training), maintenance (relapse prevention). Matching intervention to stage is a common DOH MCQ stem.

- **Bloom's domains** to target: cognitive (knowledge), affective (attitudes), psychomotor (skills) — insulin injection is psychomotor, not cognitive.
- **Health literacy** adaptations: plain language, pictograms, avoid jargon, materials in Arabic, English, Hindi, Urdu, Tagalog, or Malayalam as appropriate.
- **Andragogy principles (Knowles):** adults learn best when content is relevant, problem-centred, and respects autonomy and prior experience.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Distinguishing Health Education from Health Promotion

A persistent MCQ trap is treating these terms as interchangeable. Health education is information transfer to an individual or group (the leaflet, the diabetic-diet talk). Health promotion is the broader socio-behavioural and environmental strategy — policy, community mobilisation, and reorienting health services — articulated in the Ottawa Charter. DOH exam stems that describe a school anti-tobacco policy, a workplace wellness programme, or a city-level cycling infrastructure test health promotion, not education.

#### UAE-Specific Priorities and DOH Standards

UAE public-health priorities under MOHAP and DOH include diabetes prevention (Tier-1 priority given the national prevalence approaching 12%), obesity, tobacco control under Federal Law 15 of 2009, mental-health stigma reduction, maternal and child health, and vaccine uptake. The **Weqaya** programme delivers cardiovascular-risk screening, while **Fit-for-Life** targets physical activity. Patient education delivered in DOH-licensed facilities must be recorded with date, topic, language, method, and the educator's signature — failure to document is treated as non-delivery in clinical-governance audits.

#### Common Pitfalls in DOH MCQs

1. Labelling diabetic foot screening as primary — it is secondary prevention (early detection of neuropathy).
2. Confusing insulin-injection teaching as a cognitive objective when the skill is psychomotor.
3. Omitting the teach-back step and assuming a leaflet equals education.
4. Ignoring cultural and linguistic adaptation for the UAE's multilingual patient base.

**Worked micro-example:** A 52-year-old Emirati man with newly diagnosed Type 2 diabetes (HbA1c 8.4%) attends your clinic. Primary-prevention advice covers weight loss and influenza vaccination. Secondary prevention includes annual retinopathy and foot screening. Tertiary prevention covers foot-care skills, SMBG technique, and sick-day rules. Education objectives should target psychomotor skills (SMBG, injection) and affective attitude (acceptance of lifestyle change), and be reinforced with Arabic-language materials plus teach-back documentation in the chart.
