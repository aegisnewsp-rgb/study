# Calculus (Differentiation and Integration) — H2 Mathematics (9758) Notes

Calculus is the largest single content strand in the H2 Mathematics 9758 syllabus by marks. It covers limits and continuity, the chain, product and quotient rules for differentiation, implicit differentiation, Maclaurin series, definite integrals, integration by parts and substitution, and differential equations with their applications. Calculus carries more marks on Paper 1 than any other H2 Math topic, and a JC2 student aiming at an A or B grade must secure this strand above all others.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### The seven facts every JC2 student needs

1. **Differentiation**: the process of finding the derivative f′(x), which gives the rate of change of f at x.
2. **Integration**: the reverse of differentiation; the process of finding the function whose derivative is given.
3. **The chain rule**: if y = f(g(x)), then dy/dx = f′(g(x)) × g′(x).
4. **The product rule**: if y = uv, then dy/dx = u′v + uv′.
5. **The quotient rule**: if y = u/v, then dy/dx = (u′v − uv′) / v².
6. **The definite integral** ∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx gives the signed area between the curve and the x-axis from x = a to x = b.
7. **Differential equation**: an equation involving a function and its derivatives; separable differential equations can be solved by separating the variables and integrating.

#### Key notations

- f′(x) or dy/dx — the derivative of f with respect to x
- f″(x) or d²y/dx² — the second derivative
- ∫ f(x) dx — the indefinite integral (family of antiderivatives)
- ∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx — the definite integral from a to b
- e — the base of natural logarithms (≈ 2.71828)
- ln x — the natural logarithm

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Limits and continuity

A function f is **continuous at x = a** if f(a) is defined, lim(x→a) f(x) exists, and lim(x→a) f(x) = f(a). Polynomial, exponential, logarithmic and trigonometric functions are continuous on their natural domains.

The **limit** of a function at a point is the value the function approaches as the input approaches that point. Limit rules: lim(c × f(x)) = c × lim f(x); lim(f(x) + g(x)) = lim f(x) + lim g(x); lim(f(x) × g(x)) = lim f(x) × lim g(x); lim(f(x) / g(x)) = lim f(x) / lim g(x) (provided the denominator limit is not 0).

**Indeterminate forms** of the form 0/0 or ∞/∞ require algebraic manipulation (factorisation, rationalisation, use of standard limits) before the limit can be evaluated. The standard limit sin(x) / x → 1 as x → 0 is a key tool.

#### Differentiation

**Basic derivatives**:

- d/dx (x^n) = n x^(n−1)
- d/dx (e^x) = e^x
- d/dx (ln x) = 1/x
- d/dx (sin x) = cos x
- d/dx (cos x) = −sin x

**Sum and difference**: d/dx (f ± g) = f′ ± g′.

**Product rule**: d/dx (uv) = u′v + uv′.

**Quotient rule**: d/dx (u/v) = (u′v − uv′) / v².

**Chain rule**: if y = f(g(x)), then dy/dx = f′(g(x)) × g′(x).

**Implicit differentiation**: differentiate both sides of an equation with respect to x, treating y as a function of x; solve for dy/dx.

**Higher-order derivatives**: the second derivative f″(x) is the derivative of f′(x); it gives the rate of change of the rate of change.

**Applications of differentiation**:

- **Tangents and normals**: the tangent at (a, f(a)) has slope f′(a); the normal has slope −1/f′(a) (when f′(a) ≠ 0).
- **Stationary points**: points where f′(x) = 0. Classify as local maximum, local minimum, or point of inflection using the second-derivative test or the first-derivative sign-change test.
- **Rates of change**: dA/dt gives the rate of change of A with respect to t; related-rates problems link two rates through a geometric or physical relationship.
- **Optimisation**: maximise or minimise a quantity subject to a constraint; use the constraint to express the quantity in one variable, then differentiate and set the derivative to 0.

#### Integration

**Basic integrals**:

- ∫ x^n dx = x^(n+1) / (n+1) + C (for n ≠ −1)
- ∫ e^x dx = e^x + C
- ∫ 1/x dx = ln |x| + C
- ∫ sin x dx = −cos x + C
- ∫ cos x dx = sin x + C

**Definite integrals**: ∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx = F(b) − F(a), where F is any antiderivative of f.

**Applications of definite integrals**:

- **Area under a curve**: the area between the curve y = f(x) and the x-axis from x = a to x = b is ∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx if f is non-negative on [a, b].
- **Area between two curves**: the area between y = f(x) and y = g(x) from x = a to x = b is ∫ₐᵇ |f(x) − g(x)| dx.
- **Volume of revolution**: the volume when the region between y = f(x) and the x-axis from x = a to x = b is rotated about the x-axis is V = π ∫ₐᵇ [f(x)]² dx.

**Integration techniques**:

- **Substitution**: u = g(x), du = g′(x) dx. Reduces ∫ f(g(x)) g′(x) dx to ∫ f(u) du.
- **Integration by parts**: ∫ u dv = uv − ∫ v du. Choose u and dv so the new integral is simpler.
- **Partial fractions**: express a rational function as a sum of simpler fractions, then integrate term by term.

#### Differential equations

A **differential equation** is an equation involving a function and its derivatives. A first-order differential equation involves only the first derivative.

**Separable first-order equations**: rearrange to put all x on one side and all y on the other, then integrate both sides.

Example: dy/dx = xy. Rearrange: dy/y = x dx. Integrate: ln |y| = x²/2 + C. Solve: y = A e^(x²/2).

**Linear first-order equations** (with constant coefficients): use the integrating factor e^(∫ P dx).

#### Maclaurin series (HL)

A Maclaurin series is a Taylor series centred at x = 0. For a function f that is infinitely differentiable at x = 0, the Maclaurin series is:

f(x) = f(0) + f′(0) x + f″(0) x²/2! + f‴(0) x³/3! + …

Standard Maclaurin series:

- e^x = 1 + x + x²/2! + x³/3! + … (for all x)
- ln(1 + x) = x − x²/2 + x³/3 − x⁴/4 + … (for −1 < x ≤ 1)
- sin x = x − x³/3! + x⁵/5! − … (for all x)
- cos x = 1 − x²/2! + x⁴/4! − … (for all x)

Maclaurin series are used to approximate functions and to evaluate limits of the form 0/0.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where Calculus appears across the H2 Mathematics papers

- **H2 Mathematics 9758 Paper 1**: calculus items are the dominant content. Differentiation (tangent / normal / stationary points / optimisation) appears on most papers; integration (area / volume / substitution / by parts) appears on every paper; differential equations appear on most papers.
- **H2 Mathematics 9758 Paper 3**: calculus items appear alongside the HL-extension content. The integration-of-rational-functions and differential-equations items are common Paper 3 load.
- **Across the papers**: drawing a clear sketch of the curve and labelling the area of interest is the highest-leverage exam technique for integration-applied items.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "The chain rule is the same as the product rule." The chain rule differentiates a composition; the product rule differentiates a product.
- "The area under a curve is the integral." The integral gives the SIGNED area; the area is the absolute value of the integral if the curve crosses the x-axis.
- "∫ f(x) dx = F(x)." The indefinite integral is a FAMILY of antiderivatives: F(x) + C.
- "dy/dx = 0 at a maximum." dy/dx = 0 at a stationary point, which could be a maximum, a minimum or a point of inflection. The second derivative or the sign-change test distinguishes.
- "Differential equations have a unique solution." A first-order differential equation has a family of solutions, one for each initial condition. The general solution includes a constant C; the particular solution fixes C.

#### Exam technique

- For differentiation questions, write the rule you are applying before substituting. The rule statement is the method mark.
- For integration questions, identify the technique before computing (substitution, by parts, partial fractions). The technique is the method mark.
- For definite-integral applications, draw a sketch of the region first. The sketch is the method mark.
- For differential-equation questions, separate the variables before integrating. The separation step is the method mark.
