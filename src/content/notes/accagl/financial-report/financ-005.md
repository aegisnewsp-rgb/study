# Group Financial Statements — Consolidation

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Group (consolidated) financial statements** combine a parent and its subsidiaries as if they were a single economic entity, under **IFRS 10**. **Control** has three elements: power over the investee, exposure to variable returns, and the ability to use that power to affect those returns.

The headline goodwill formula is:

> **Goodwill = Consideration transferred + FV of NCI at acquisition − FV of net identifiable assets acquired**

Two NCI methods exist: the **full (fair-value) method** measures NCI at its full fair value on day one, and the **proportionate method** measures NCI at its share of net assets. Mixing the two is a common marker of careless working.

#### Exam anchors you must memorise:
- Eliminate the parent's investment against subsidiary share capital + **pre-acquisition** reserves only.
- Remove unrealised profit (PURP) on any intra-group inventory still on hand: **Dr Cost of sales / Cr Inventory**.
- Use **equity method** (IAS 28) for **associates**; full consolidation is reserved for **subsidiaries**.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core definitions under IFRS 10

A **subsidiary** is an entity controlled by the parent. **Control** requires all three: (1) power over the investee, (2) exposure to variable returns, and (3) the ability to use power to affect those returns. **NCI** is the interest in a subsidiary not attributable to the parent; it appears as a separate component of equity and a separate line in profit or loss.

#### Acquisition method mechanics

The acquirer (parent) is identified, the acquisition date fixed, and the identifiable assets and liabilities of the subsidiary are restated to **fair value** at that date. Internally generated intangibles and contingent liabilities may be recognised if measurable. Goodwill then equals consideration plus NCI at fair value, minus the FV of net assets acquired.

#### Consolidation workflow

A clean set of workings usually follows seven layers: net assets at acquisition, goodwill (or bargain purchase taken to P&L), post-acquisition reserves split between parent and NCI, consolidated retained earnings, NCI column, consolidated statement of profit or loss, and consolidated statement of financial position.

#### Common elimination journal entries

| Adjustment | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Eliminate investment | Investment in sub | Share capital + Pre-acq reserves |
| Unrealised profit in inventory | Cost of sales | Inventory (PURP) |
| Intra-group sale of PPE profit | PPE / Cost of sales | PPE / Cost of sales |
| Intra-group dividend (in parent's books) | Income from subsidiary | Investment in subsidiary |

#### Worked goodwill illustration

Parent buys 80% of Sub for **CU 1,200**. Sub's net assets at acquisition have a carrying amount of **CU 900** but a fair value of **CU 1,000**. NCI is measured at fair value (5% premium), implying FV of NCI = **CU 240**.

- FV of net assets = 1,000 → Parent's 80% share = 800 → NCI share = 200.
- Full method: Goodwill = 1,200 + 240 − 1,000 = **CU 440**.
- Proportionate method: Goodwill = 1,200 + 200 − 1,000 = **CU 400** (cheaper goodwill, no goodwill attributed to NCI).

#### Typical ACCA/ICAP question patterns

FR/FA2-FAFA routinely serve a **15–25 mark** consolidation question with: an SFP and a P&L to prepare, a goodwill computation, fair-value adjustments, intra-group transactions to eliminate, and an NCI treatment decision. ICAP's **FR&AS** paper follows the same control-based logic but layers in Pakistani Companies (General Provisions) Regulations on disclosure.

> **Trap:** Examiners mix the full and proportionate NCI methods in one answer; the choice must be consistent throughout — goodwill, SFP equity, and P&L allocation.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge cases and method mixing

**Full vs proportionate NCI** changes more than the goodwill number. Under the **full method**, goodwill is grossed up for the entire subsidiary, and post-acquisition profit or loss is split between parent and NCI at their respective ownership percentages. Under the **proportionate method**, goodwill is recognised only on the parent's share; NCI carries no goodwill on its line.

A frequent marker of confusion is attributing NCI's share of post-acquisition **fair-value depreciation** correctly. Each fair-value uplift carries additional depreciation, and NCI's share of that incremental depreciation must reduce the NCI balance.

#### Step acquisitions, disposals, and changes in control

When a parent's stake crosses 50% after a staged purchase, **IFRS 3 Revised** treats the existing holding as if it were reacquired at fair value, recognising a gain or loss in P&L. The reverse — losing control but retaining significant influence — reclassifies the investment to an **associate** under IAS 28; only the retained share goes to the equity method, and the held-over interest is remeasured to fair value at the date control is lost.

> **Trap:** Don't simply "carry over" old goodwill from before the step acquisition; the existing stake must be revalued.

#### Associates, joint ventures, and the equity method

Associates (significant influence, usually **20–50%** ownership) and joint ventures under **IFRS 11** are **never** fully consolidated. They use the **equity method** (IAS 28): the investment is initially recognised at cost, then adjusted for the investor's share of post-acquisition profit or loss and other comprehensive income. Dividends received reduce the investment carrying amount.

Proportionate consolidation for joint ventures, once allowed under the old IAS 31, is no longer permitted under IFRS.

#### Common mistakes examiners target

- **Punishing part-marks:** the investment figure is eliminated only against share capital, ignoring **pre-acquisition reserves** (or vice versa).
- Forgetting to **gross up** for partly owned subsidiaries: bring in 100% of every line item, then split between parent and NCI in equity and P&L.
- Treating **intra-group dividends** as group dividend income (they aren't income at the group level).
- Applying goodwill impairment rules (IAS 36) incorrectly — goodwill is **not amortised**, but tested annually.

#### Practice prompts

1. **Goodwill & NCI:** A 75% subsidiary is acquired for CU 600 when net assets at FV are CU 500. Calculate goodwill under both the full and proportionate methods and show the SFP equity split.
2. **Intra-group inventory:** Parent sells goods costing CU 80 to Sub for CU 100; 40% remain in Sub's inventory at year-end. Calculate the PURP elimination and its effect on consolidated profit and closing inventory.
