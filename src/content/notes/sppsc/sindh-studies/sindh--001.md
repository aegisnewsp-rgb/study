# Topic 1

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Topic 1** for SPSC Sindh Studies covers the province of **Sindh**, located in southeast Pakistan and dominated by the **Indus River basin**. Key historical anchors: **Mehrgarh (~7000 BCE)** and the **Indus Valley Civilization** (Mohenjo-daro, Kot Diji) precede the **Arab conquest of 711 CE** under **Muhammad bin Qasim**. Local Muslim dynasties followed in sequence: **Samma (1351–1524) → Arghun (1524–1556) → Kalhora (1701–1783) → Talpur Mirs (1783–1843)**, ending with **Charles Napier's** annexation at the **Battle of Miani (1843)**.

- Sindhi is the official language; **Shah Abdul Latif Bhittai** (Shah Jo Risalo) anchors the Sufi literary tradition.
- The **Sukkur Barrage (1932)** and **Kotri Barrage (1955)** are distinct Indus irrigation structures.
- The **18th Amendment (2010)** devolved significant powers to Sindh under the 1973 Constitution.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Historical Timeline and Dynastic Sequence
Sindh's history begins at **Mehrgarh** (Baluchistan foothills, ~7000 BCE), one of South Asia's earliest agricultural sites, feeding into the mature **Indus Valley Civilization** at **Mohenjo-daro** (Larkana district) and **Kot Diji**. The Arab general **Muhammad bin Qasim** conquered Sindh in **711 CE** under the Umayyad Caliphate, introducing Islam initially through state structures before Sufi saints spread it culturally.

After a long period of local Hindu-Buddhist rule and Mongol pressure, Sindh's Muslim dynasties governed in sequence: the **Samma** (1351–1524) consolidated Sindhi identity from Thatta; the **Arghun** (1524–1556) briefly ruled before the **Mughals**; the **Kalhora** (1701–1783) patronised Sufi shrines; and the **Talpur Mirs** (1783–1843) centralised power from Hyderabad until **Charles Napier** defeated them at **Miani** in 1843.

#### Geography, Economy, and Administration
The **Indus River** bisects the province, feeding the **Sukkur Barrage (1932)** and **Kotri Barrage (1955)** that irrigate cotton, rice, wheat, and sugarcane. **Karachi** anchors finance and industry; the **Thar Coalfield** is Pakistan's largest energy reserve. SPSC recruits provincial civil servants (BPS-16+) under **Sindh SPSC Regulations**.

#### Typical Question Patterns
Expect MCQs on dynasty chronology, barrage construction dates, and identification of Sufi saints. **Assertion-reason** items often pair **1843** with Napier or test whether candidates confuse **711 CE** with later Turkic conquests.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Constitutional Position of Sindh
The **1973 Constitution** originally placed many subjects on the Federal Legislative List, including education and health. The **18th Amendment (2010)** reversed this, devolving those subjects to provinces and strengthening the **Council of Common Interests** as a dispute-resolution forum between provinces and the federation. Combined with the **Sindh Revenue Board** (2013) and provincial police reforms, Sindh now exercises substantially wider fiscal and administrative autonomy than under Musharraf-era governance.

#### Cultural and Religious Diffusion
Islam arrived in Sindh through two distinct channels. **Muhammad bin Qasim** established political authority via the Umayyad state (711 CE), while later Sufi missionaries — most prominently **Shah Abdul Latif Bhittai** (1689–1752) and **Sachal Sarmast** (1739–1829) — converted rural populations through vernacular poetry in **Sindhi, Saraiki, Urdu, Persian, and Arabic**. The annual **urs** at Bhit Shah remains a major cultural event.

#### Common Mistakes and Exam Traps

- Dating Muhammad bin Qasim's conquest to 711 CE is correct; placing it under the Abbasids or confusing it with Mahmud of Ghazni's later raids (1026) loses marks.
- Kalhora rule ends in **1783**, Talpur rule ends in **1843** — do not merge the two dynasties.
- The **Sukkur Barrage** (1932, Lloyd Barrage) is upstream; **Kotri Barrage** (1955) is downstream — distinct construction eras and irrigation command areas.
- SPSC is a **provincial** body, not federal; its jurisdiction is BPS-16 and above within Sindh government services.

#### Practice Prompts
1. Trace the chronology of Muslim dynasties in Sindh from 711 CE to 1843 CE, identifying each ruler's capital and one signature contribution.
2. Explain how the 18th Amendment altered the fiscal-administrative relationship between Sindh and the federal government.
