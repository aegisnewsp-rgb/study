# Exponents, Logarithms and Complex Numbers — Qatar Secondary Certificate Mathematics Notes

Exponents and logarithms extend the power notation from integers to reals and underpin the calculus of exponential and logarithmic functions. The MoEHE 2018 Mathematics Curriculum Standards publish the laws of exponents and logarithms, the natural exponential and Euler's number e, the change-of-base formula, complex numbers in rectangular and polar form, and De Moivre's theorem at Grade 12 advanced track (MoEHE pp. 51-52). These tools appear in growth-and-decay modelling, in the algebra of complex roots, and as the algebraic machinery of calculus.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Exponent laws:** a^m * a^n = a^(m+n); a^m / a^n = a^(m-n); (a^m)^n = a^(mn); a^0 = 1; a^(-n) = 1/a^n.
- **Logarithm laws:** log_a(xy) = log_a(x) + log_a(y); log_a(x/y) = log_a(x) - log_a(y); log_a(x^n) = n log_a(x).
- **Change of base:** log_a(x) = log_b(x) / log_b(a). In particular ln(x) = log(x) / log(e).
- **ln(e) = 1.** e is the unique base where d/dx e^x = e^x.
- **Complex number i:** i^2 = -1. Rectangular form z = a + bi; polar form z = r(cos theta + i sin theta).
- **De Moivre:** (cos theta + i sin theta)^n = cos(n theta) + i sin(n theta).

#### Examiner traps

- ln(x) is defined only for x > 0.
- log_a(x) for a between 0 and 1 is decreasing — easy to mis-sign the inequality.
- Modulus r is always non-negative; argument theta is in radians.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Exponent and logarithm laws in practice

Solve 2^x = 10. Take log base 2 of both sides: x = log_2(10) = log(10) / log(2) ~= 3.3219. Or take ln: x = ln(10) / ln(2).

Solve 5^(2x - 1) = 30. Take ln: (2x - 1) ln 5 = ln 30, so 2x - 1 = ln 30 / ln 5, so x = (1 + ln 30 / ln 5) / 2.

Compound interest formula A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt) is exponential in t. Continuous compounding A = Pe^(rt) uses e directly. Doubling time: t = ln 2 / r.

#### Natural exponential and logarithm

e^x and ln x are inverses. The derivative of e^x is e^x itself; the derivative of ln x is 1/x. The integral of e^x is e^x; the integral of 1/x is ln |x| + C.

The change-of-base formula is essential when working with non-natural bases in calculus. d/dx log_a(x) = 1 / (x ln a).

#### Complex numbers in rectangular form

Operations: (a + bi) + (c + di) = (a + c) + (b + d)i; (a + bi)(c + di) = (ac - bd) + (ad + bc)i. Division: multiply by the conjugate. The conjugate of a + bi is a - bi.

Modulus |z| = sqrt(a^2 + b^2). Argument arg(z) = arctan(b/a) with the correct quadrant.

#### Polar form and De Moivre

If z = r (cos theta + i sin theta) and w = s (cos phi + i sin phi), then zw = rs (cos(theta + phi) + i sin(theta + phi)). De Moivre's theorem for integer powers: z^n = r^n (cos n theta + i sin n theta).

Roots: the n-th roots of z are r^(1/n) (cos((theta + 2 pi k) / n) + i sin((theta + 2 pi k) / n)) for k = 0, 1, ..., n - 1.

For example, the cube roots of 8i = 8(cos(pi/2) + i sin(pi/2)) are 2(cos(pi/6 + 2k pi/3) + i sin(pi/6 + 2k pi/3)) for k = 0, 1, 2 — three distinct complex roots equally spaced on a circle of radius 2.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### Why this strand appears across the terminal paper

Exponential and logarithmic functions are part of the Functions and Graphs sub-topic of the MoEHE Algebra and Calculus strand (~69 percent of the Grade 12 advanced paper). Complex numbers are an extension of algebra; De Moivre's theorem appears in advanced track algebra and is the natural entry point to the geometry of roots and to the connection between exponential and trigonometric functions (Euler's formula e^(i theta) = cos theta + i sin theta) introduced at university.

#### Exam technique

- State the laws you are using by name on the line — method marks depend on it.
- For complex number division, multiply numerator and denominator by the conjugate of the denominator, then simplify.
- For De Moivre roots, draw the picture — the n roots are equally spaced on a circle of radius r^(1/n).

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "log(xy) = log x * log y." It is log x + log y.
- "i^2 = i, so i^3 = i." No: i^3 = i^2 * i = -i.
- "Every complex number has a real square root." Only non-negative reals do; complex numbers have square roots but they are complex.
