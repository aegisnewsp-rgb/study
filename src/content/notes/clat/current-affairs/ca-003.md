# Legal News

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Legal News for CLAT covers the **12-month reference window** of Indian judicial, legislative, and constitutional developments tested under the Current Affairs & General Knowledge section. The Consortium of NLUs expects familiarity with Supreme Court and High Court **landmark judgments**, parliamentary bills receiving Presidential assent, **Article 123 ordinances**, and amendments to the three new criminal codes — **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS)**, **Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS)**, and **Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA)** — which replaced the IPC, CrPC, and Indian Evidence Act from **1 July 2024**.

- **Read at least one national daily** (*The Hindu* / *Indian Express*) plus one legal outlet such as LiveLaw, Bar & Bench, or Supreme Court Observer.
- Map every news item to a **subject head** — criminal, constitutional, commercial, environmental, family, labour, tax, or cyber.
- Always cite **bench strength, judge names, citation, and date** when recalling a ruling.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### What the Examiner Tests

CLAT allocates roughly **25% of marks** to Current Affairs, of which Legal News carries an estimated **5%** standalone weightage. Items appear as passage-based comprehension, factual MCQs, or principle-application sets grounded in recent statutes and rulings. The trick is reading a news item for its **legal significance**, not its political slug.

#### Core Sources and Tracking Window

The reference window is strictly the **preceding 12 months** before the exam date. Anything older is out of scope. Candidates should track Ministry of Law and Justice notifications, Election Commission of India advisories, NALSA circulars, and reported judgments on the Supreme Court of India website.

#### Distinguishing Binding Law from Commentary

Every reported judgment contains a **ratio decidendi** (the binding principle) and **obiter dicta** (persuasive observations). CLAT questions frequently test whether you can isolate the ratio. A Law Commission Report or Standing Committee recommendation is **not enacted law** until Parliament passes it.

| Source category | What it yields | Binding status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Supreme Court judgment | Ratio decidendi on constitutional/statutory interpretation | Binding on all courts under Article 141 |
| Presidential assent to Bill | New Act of Parliament | Binding statute |
| Article 123 ordinance | Temporary law when Parliament is in recess | Valid for 6 weeks after reassembly |
| Law Commission Report | Reform recommendation | Non-binding, advisory only |
| PIL admission order | Interim relief possible | Not a final ruling on merits |

- Always cite the **corresponding BNS section**, not the old IPC section — BNS 302 replaces IPC 302, BNS 63 replaces IPC 376, BNS 318 replaces IPC 420.
- Note **Article 142** (complete justice) and **Article 32 / 226** (fundamental rights writs) when they appear in a ruling.
- Watch for **Article 370 abrogation follow-ups**, same-sex marriage and live-in relationship judgments, and PMLA, RTI, and ADR developments.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Subject Mapping

Legal News items often straddle categories. A ruling striking down Section 6A of the Citizenship Act touches **constitutional, immigration, and refugee law**. The June 2024 Electoral Bonds verdict simultaneously engaged **right to information, transparency, and fundamental rights** under Article 19(1)(a). Candidates who tag items by a single subject miss the cross-linkages CLAT passages exploit.

#### Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Quoting IPC sections after 1 July 2024** — the old criminal codes are repealed; use BNS/BNSS/BSA numbers.
- **Citing the wrong court** — a High Court division bench ruling is persuasive, not binding on other High Courts.
- **Treating an interim order as the final judgment** — interim stays expire or get modified.
- **Reporting the political headline** ("SC slams government") instead of the issue, the relief, and the provisions interpreted.
- **Ignoring the 12-month window** — citing the 2017 Right to Privacy ruling in a 2025 paper is wasted preparation.

#### Worked Micro-Example

Suppose the Supreme Court, in a five-judge Constitution Bench, holds on 15 November 2025 that the Electoral Bonds Scheme violates Article 19(1)(a). A correct CLAT-style answer cites the **bench strength (5J), date, citation (e.g., 2025 INSC 1142), ratio (compelled disclosure of political funding is proportionate), and the statutory provision struck down**. Mentioning only "SC cancels Electoral Bonds" loses marks because it omits the legal reasoning the exam tests.

| Legal term | Plain-English meaning | CLAT trap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ratio decidendi | Binding reasoning for the decision | Confusing it with obiter dicta |
| Obiter dicta | Judge's incidental observations | Quoting it as "the law declared" |
| Article 142 | Power to pass orders needed for complete justice | Treating it as a substantive right |
| Article 370 abrogation | Constitutional status of J&K reorganised into UTs | Confusing date of notification with date of effect |

#### Practice Prompts

1. Identify the ratio decidendi in a reported 2024–2025 PIL on **live-in relationships** and state whether the High Court's observations are binding nationwide.
2. A news item mentions the Law Commission recommending a new privacy law — explain why this report is **not yet an enacted statute** and what parliamentary steps remain.
