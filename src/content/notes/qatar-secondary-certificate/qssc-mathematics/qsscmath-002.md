# Functions, Graphs and Transformations — Qatar Secondary Certificate Mathematics Notes

Functions and their graphs are the language of calculus, the language of applied algebra, and the language of the MoEHE Mathematics Curriculum Standards. At Grade 12 advanced track the MoEHE 2018 standards publish quadratic, reciprocal, absolute-value, square-root, cube-root, exponential, logarithmic and trigonometric functions as core functions, with operations on functions and geometric transformations (translations, reflections, stretches) on their graphs (MoEHE pp. 50-53). Functions are a prerequisite for calculus: derivative and integral are defined as limits of function operations on the graph.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Function:** every input has exactly one output. Notation f: A -> B or y = f(x).
- **Domain:** the set of allowed inputs. **Range:** the set of actual outputs.
- **Composite:** (f o g)(x) = f(g(x)). Read right-to-left: g first, then f.
- **Inverse:** f^(-1) undoes f, so f(f^(-1)(x)) = x. Only one-to-one functions have inverses.
- **Transformations:** y = f(x - a) shifts the graph right by a; y = f(x) + k shifts up by k; y = -f(x) reflects in the x-axis; y = f(-x) reflects in the y-axis; y = a f(x) stretches vertically by factor a.
- **Standard graphs:** y = x^2 parabola, y = 1/x hyperbola, y = sqrt(x) half-parabola, y = e^x and y = ln x exponential and natural log.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing f(g(x)) with g(f(x)) — they are equal only when f and g commute.
- Shifting the wrong way — y = f(x - 3) shifts RIGHT by 3 because to evaluate at x = 3 you need the value of f at 0.
- Multiplying x by -1 reflects in the y-axis, multiplying y by -1 reflects in the x-axis.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Function families and their key features

**Quadratic** y = a(x - h)^2 + k: vertex (h, k), axis of symmetry x = h, opens up if a > 0, roots from the quadratic formula.

**Reciprocal** y = a/x + k: hyperbola with two branches, asymptotes at x = 0 (vertical) and y = k (horizontal).

**Absolute value** y = a|x - h| + k: V-shape, vertex at (h, k).

**Square root** y = a sqrt(x - h) + k: half-parabola opening right, domain x >= h.

**Exponential** y = a * b^x, b > 0, b not 1: passes through (0, a), increases if b > 1, decreases if 0 < b < 1, asymptote y = 0.

**Logarithmic** y = a * log_b(x): inverse of exponential, defined only for x > 0, passes through (1, 0).

**Trigonometric** y = sin x, y = cos x, y = tan x: period 2 pi for sin/cos, pi for tan; amplitude 1; sin and cos range [-1, 1], tan all reals.

#### Operations on functions

For functions f and g: (f + g)(x) = f(x) + g(x); (f - g)(x) = f(x) - g(x); (f * g)(x) = f(x) * g(x); (f/g)(x) = f(x) / g(x) where g(x) not 0. Composite (f o g)(x) = f(g(x)) — apply g first then f.

Domain restrictions: where g(x) takes a value outside f's domain; where the inner function is undefined; where the outer function is undefined. Always check domain after composing.

#### Transformations — the mapping

For y = a f(b(x - h)) + k:

- |a| > 1 stretches y vertically; 0 < |a| < 1 compresses.
- 0 < |b| < 1 stretches x horizontally; |b| > 1 compresses.
- h shifts right; -h shifts left.
- k shifts up; -k shifts down.
- Negative a reflects in x-axis; negative b reflects in y-axis.

Memorise the standard form. Sketch from a single labelled point.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### Why this strand matters at the MoEHE terminal

Functions appear in every algebra question at advanced track, in every calculus question at advanced track, and across the grade boundaries. The MoEHE 2018 standards put functions in the Algebra and Calculus strand (~69 percent of the Grade 12 advanced Mathematics paper). Confident sketching, transformation mapping, and composition are the prerequisites for differentiation, integration, and curve sketching.

#### Exam technique

- Label at least three points before sketching a transformation — one anchor, one to the left, one to the right.
- State the domain and range explicitly when the question asks for "the function" — a graph without domain loses the method mark.
- Show the composite step by step — write g(x), then f(g(x)). Skipping the intermediate step costs a mark on a 5-mark composite question.
- For inverse questions, swap x and y algebraically, then solve for y. Then check by composing f(f^(-1)(x)) = x.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "f + g and f * g have the same domain." No — (f + g) has domain intersection of f and g; (f * g) has the same; (f / g) excludes points where g is 0.
- "f^(-1) means 1/f." f^(-1) is the inverse function; 1/f is the reciprocal function. Different.
- "Vertical line test passes, so it is a function." Yes, that's the test for being a function. To test for being one-to-one (and thus invertible), use the horizontal line test.
