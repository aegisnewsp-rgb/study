# Geometry and Trigonometry — Kuwait Secondary Certificate Mathematics Notes

Geometry and Trigonometry is the second-largest strand at the Kuwait Secondary Certificate terminal, with the Kuwait MOE Mathematics curriculum allocating substantial marks to plane geometry, circle theorems, vectors in the plane, sine/cosine/tangent ratios, the sine and cosine rules, and the heights-and-distances applications. The strand integrates Euclidean with trigonometry at terminal level — most geometry questions on the Grade 12 paper require either a trigonometric identity or the sine/cosine rule to solve.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Angles in a triangle:** sum of interior angles = 180°. Exterior angle = sum of two non-adjacent interior angles.
- **Pythagoras:** a² + b² = c² in a right-angled triangle with hypotenuse c.
- **Sine, cosine, tangent:** sin θ = opposite/hypotenuse; cos θ = adjacent/hypotenuse; tan θ = opposite/adjacent.
- **Sine rule:** a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C = 2R (where R is the circumradius).
- **Cosine rule:** c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C (find a side); cos C = (a² + b² − c²) / (2ab) (find an angle).
- **Area of triangle:** ½ ab sin C.
- **Vector in the plane:** position vector, magnitude, direction; addition a + b, scalar multiplication ka; dot product a·b = |a||b|cos θ.
- **Circle theorems:** angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference; angle in a semicircle is 90°; angles in the same segment are equal.

#### Examiner traps

- Using the cosine rule when the sine rule is faster (or vice versa) — practice both before the exam so you can pick the right tool.
- Confusing "perpendicular bisector" with "angle bisector" — they meet at the circumcentre and in-centre respectively.
- Forgetting to convert degrees to radians (or vice versa) when the question switches units mid-problem.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Triangle geometry

A triangle's three sides and three angles are linked by the sine and cosine rules. The sine rule is the right tool for a problem where you know two angles and a side (AAS) or two sides and a non-included angle (SSA — the ambiguous case). The cosine rule is the right tool for SAS (two sides and the included angle) or SSS (three sides).

The area of a triangle can be written as ½ base × height, or more generally as ½ ab sin C when only two sides and the included angle are given.

#### Pythagoras and right-angled triangles

Pythagoras is the special case of the cosine rule where the included angle is 90° (cos 90° = 0). It applies to right-angled triangles only — for any other triangle, use the cosine rule instead.

In a right-angled triangle with hypotenuse c, the two shorter sides a and b satisfy a² + b² = c². The two standard 30-60-90 and 45-45-90 triangles have side ratios 1 : √3 : 2 and 1 : 1 : √2 respectively — these ratios appear often in terminal heights-and-distances items.

#### Trigonometric identities

The Pythagorean identity sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 is the foundation. From it follow:

- 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ
- 1 + cot²θ = cosec²θ
- sin(90° − θ) = cos θ
- cos(90° − θ) = sin θ
- tan(90° − θ) = cot θ

The addition formulas:

- sin(A ± B) = sin A cos B ± cos A sin B
- cos(A ± B) = cos A cos B ∓ sin A sin B
- tan(A ± B) = (tan A ± tan B) / (1 ∓ tan A tan B)

The double-angle formulas:

- sin 2A = 2 sin A cos A
- cos 2A = cos²A − sin²A = 2 cos²A − 1 = 1 − 2 sin²A

#### Vectors in the plane

A vector has magnitude and direction. Two vectors are equal iff they have the same magnitude and direction. The position vector of point P relative to origin O is OP. Vectors add tip-to-tail: a + b is the diagonal of the parallelogram with sides a and b. The scalar multiple ka is a vector in the direction of a (or opposite, if k < 0) with magnitude |k||a|.

The dot product a·b = a₁b₁ + a₂b₂ = |a||b| cos θ is the workhorse for angle-between-vectors problems. When a·b = 0, the vectors are perpendicular; when a·b = |a||b|, they are parallel and in the same direction.

#### Heights and distances

Heights-and-distances items ask for the height of a tower, the width of a river, the distance between two inaccessible points. They translate the geometry into a right-angled triangle (or two), then apply sin, cos or tan to the angle of elevation or depression. The standard angles are 30°, 45° and 60° — these should be automatic at terminal level.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where Geometry and Trigonometry appears across the terminal exam

- **Paper 1 (foundation):** Pythagoras, sin/cos/tan of standard angles, basic area and volume.
- **Paper 2 (terminal):** sine rule / cosine rule applied to non-right triangles, vectors in the plane with dot product, multi-step heights-and-distances problems, circle theorems applied to inscribed and circumscribed quadrilaterals.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "The sine rule works for any triangle." It works for all triangles, but only gives unique answers for AAS and SSA when SSA does not produce the ambiguous case.
- "cos θ can never be negative." cos θ is negative in the second and third quadrants (90° < θ < 270°). Most Kuwaiti terminal items stay in 0° to 180°, but circle-theorem items can go outside that range.
- "Vectors with the same magnitude are equal." No — direction matters. The vectors (3, 4) and (−3, 4) have the same magnitude (5) but different directions.
- "The angle bisector always passes through the centre of the circumscribed circle." No — the perpendicular bisector of every side passes through the circumcentre; the angle bisectors pass through the in-centre.

#### Exam technique

- For triangle problems, draw the triangle, label the sides and angles, then write the formula you are using (sine rule or cosine rule) — the formula statement is the method mark.
- For vectors problems, write the vector in component form (a, b) before doing any calculation — the component form is the method mark.
- For heights-and-distances problems, always draw a clear diagram with the angle of elevation or depression marked; a missing diagram is the most common reason for losing marks on a correct method.
