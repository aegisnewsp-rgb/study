# Reproduction — Leaving Certificate (Established) Biology Notes

Unit 2.5 is the unit where the whole course converges. Meiosis from unit 2.3 makes the gametes, the genetic code from unit 1.2 lets those gametes carry information, the endocrine system from unit 2.4 supplies every hormone, and the genetics from unit 1.4 explains why offspring are not identical to their parents. The specification also asks you to compare reproduction in plants and animals and to weigh fertility treatments and prenatal screening — so this is as much a biology-in-society unit as a mechanism unit.

The mechanical core is the **menstrual cycle**, because a question that asks you to model hormone levels across a month is the most reliable Higher-level item in the unit. Learn the shape of the two hormones and the shape of the two pituitary hormones, and the whole graph reconstructs itself. The other mechanical core is **pollination and seed dispersal in flowering plants**, which is where the specification asks you to compare structures and relate them to functions.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Male reproductive system and function**: **penis** (delivers sperm; contains the **foreskin** and is topped by the **glans**), **scrotum** (holds the **testes** at a temperature below core body temperature, which is required for sperm production), **testes** (produce sperm by meiosis and produce **testosterone**), **epididymis** (sperm mature and are stored here), **urethra** (carries sperm out of the body), **vas deferens** (a muscular tube carrying sperm from the epididymis, behind the bladder), **seminal vesicle** (contributes a fructose-rich fluid providing energy), **prostate** (adds an alkaline fluid which protects sperm from the acidity of the vagina).
- **Female reproductive system and function**: **vulva** (external opening, includes the **labia** and **clitoris**), **vagina** (passage for the penis, the menstrual flow and for birth; also where **lactobacillus** maintains an acidic pH that inhibits pathogens), **cervix** (lower neck of the uterus, whose mucus changes consistency around ovulation), **uterus** (site of implantation and where the foetus develops; lined by the **endometrium**), **fallopian tube** (site of fertilisation), **ovary** (produces ova and the hormones **oestrogen** and **progesterone**).
- **Gamete production** occurs by **meiosis**: sperm production in the testes is continuous from puberty; oocyte production is prenatal, and the cells arrest in metaphase II and are completed only if fertilisation occurs.
- **The four phases of the cycle**: **menstruation** → **follicular phase** → **ovulation** → **luteal phase**.
- **Hormone changes.** In the **follicular phase**, FSH from the pituitary stimulates a **Graafian follicle** to grow and secrete oestrogen; rising oestrogen stimulates more FSH and produces a surge of **LH**, which triggers **ovulation** at around day 14. In the **luteal phase**, the ruptured follicle becomes the **corpus luteum**, which secretes **progesterone** and oestrogen; progesterone maintains the endometrium. If pregnancy does not occur, the corpus luteum degenerates, progesterone and oestrogen fall sharply, the endometrium is no longer maintained, and it breaks down and is shed — that is menstruation. The fall in progesterone removes negative feedback on the pituitary, allowing FSH to rise again and the next follicular phase to begin.
- **Pregnancy**: **fertilisation** occurs in the fallopian tube → **zygote** → cleavage divisions produce a **morula** → a **blastocyst** forms, which **implants** in the endometrium → the **germ layers** (ectoderm, mesoderm, endoderm) form → **embryo** → **foetus** → **labour and birth**.
- **The placenta** forms from embryonic and maternal tissue and supplies oxygen and nutrients to the foetus, removes CO₂ and waste, and secretes **hCG**, which maintains the corpus luteum and hence progesterone until the placenta takes over.
- **Other reproductive hormones**: **FSH** (follicle growth and sperm production), **LH** (ovulation and testosterone production in males; Leydig cells), **testosterone** (male secondary sex characteristics and spermatogenesis), **oestrogen** (female secondary sex characteristics, builds the endometrium), **progesterone** (maintains the endometrium and the pregnancy), **oxytocin** (stimulates uterine contraction in labour and milk ejection), **prolactin** (stimulates milk production; produces **colostrum** for the newborn).
- **Screening**: **antenatal screening** monitors the health of the mother and developing foetus (ultrasound, blood tests); **genetic screening** detects chromosomal and genetic conditions; **postnatal screening** includes the **heel-prick test for PKU** (phenylketonuria), which must be caught early because diet changes prevent irreversible brain damage.
- **Contraception, four categories**: *natural* (abstinence, timing methods using the cyclical fertile period — note males are fertile continuously and females cyclically); *mechanical* (condom, diaphragm, intrauterine device); *surgical* (vasectomy in males, tubal ligation in females); *chemical* (the combined pill, containing oestrogen and progesterone, which suppresses FSH and LH release). Only condoms also reduce the risk of **STIs**; the others prevent pregnancy only.
- **Infertility treatments, based on the biology of fertility**: **stimulating ovulation** with FSH-type drugs where ovulation is not occurring; **artificial insemination** where sperm count or motility is low; **intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI)** where a single sperm is injected directly into the cytoplasm of an ovum; **in vitro fertilisation (IVF)** where eggs are fertilised outside the body and the resulting embryos are implanted back into the uterus.
- **Plant reproduction**: **flowers** produce **seeds** and **fruits**. **Sepals** protect the bud; **petals** attract pollinators; **stamen** = **anther** (produces pollen) + **filament** (supports the anther); **carpel** = **stigma** + **style** + **ovary**; **receptacle** bears the floral parts. Pollination transfers pollen from anther to stigma.
- **Insect-pollinated vs wind-pollinated plants.** Insect-pollinated: large, brightly coloured, scented petals, nectar guides, sticky/heavy pollen, a long style placing the stigma away from the anthers, and often a flower shape that lands pollinators in the right position. Wind-pollinated: small inconspicuous petals or none, no scent, no nectar, **large feathery stigmas** to catch pollen, **anthers dangling on long filaments** to release pollen into the wind, and very large quantities of **light-weight pollen**.
- **Seed structure and function**: **testa** (protective seed coat), **embryo** with **plumule** (shoot) and **radicle** (root), and **endosperm** (food store). Seeds also produce **growth regulators** that stimulate the growth of fruit tissues.
- **Dispersal** by **wind** (dandelion, sycamore — light seeds or wings), **water** (coconut), and **animal**, both **external** (hooked burrs, sticky seeds — *adhesive* or *epizoochory*) and **internal** (berries, fleshy fruits eaten and the seeds carried and deposited — *endozoochory*).

#### Examiner traps

- **Calling the follicular phase "the first half" and leaving it vague.** Name it, and state what happens in it (follicle growth, oestrogen rise, FSH stimulation).
- **Saying the corpus luteum forms from a new follicle.** It forms from the **ruptured** follicle after ovulation.
- **Getting the hormone that triggers ovulation wrong.** It is **LH**, not FSH and not progesterone.
- **Saying progesterone causes menstruation.** Progesterone *maintains* the endometrium; its withdrawal is what causes menstruation.
- **Claiming all contraception prevents STIs.** Only barrier methods do.
- **Confusing IVF and ICSI.** IVF fertilises eggs in vitro; ICSI injects a sperm directly into the egg.
- **Describing wind-pollinated flowers as having smaller pollen.** The opposite: enormous quantities of very light pollen.
- **Saying the endosperm is the embryo.** The embryo has plumule and radicle; the endosperm is its food supply.

#### 30-minute triage checklist

- [ ] Can you name and define function for every structure in both the male and female reproductive systems?
- [ ] Can you sketch the four-phase cycle with the oestrogen, progesterone, FSH and LH curves and mark the LH surge?
- [ ] Can you say which structure becomes the corpus luteum, and which hormone maintains the endometrium?
- [ ] Can you produce a table of insect- versus wind-pollination adaptations with a reason for each?
- [ ] Can you distinguish the four contraception categories and name the one that also prevents STIs?

### 🟡 Standard — Structured Study (1d–1mo)

#### Reading a hormone graph — the four moves

Every cycle question is answered the same way. Read the graph in four passes.

1. **Identify the phases from the axes and labels.** Day 1 is the start of menstruation; ovulation sits at about day 14; the cycle then runs to about day 28.
2. **Read each curve's shape.** Oestrogen: low at menstruation, rises gradually through the follicular phase, **peaks just before the LH surge**, falls, has a smaller second rise in the luteal phase, then falls. Progesterone: **low during the follicular phase**, rises sharply after ovulation as the corpus luteum forms, is high in the middle of the luteal phase, then falls before menstruation. FSH: rises in the follicular phase, dips, and shows a smaller surge. LH: stays low all cycle except a **large, sharp surge** at day 14.
3. **Link each event to its cause.** Rising FSH grows the follicle; the follicle secretes oestrogen; oestrogen feeds back positively on the pituitary until the **LH surge** triggers ovulation; the ruptured follicle becomes the **corpus luteum** and secretes progesterone; progesterone sustains the endometrium; **withdrawal** of progesterone triggers menstruation.
4. **Use it to explain real-world cases.** A woman whose progesterone level stays high will not menstruate — which is one reason the combined contraceptive pill works, because it keeps FSH and LH suppressed so no follicle matures and no ovulation occurs. A hormonal implant releases progesterone slowly, thinning the endometrium and preventing implantation.

*Worked question.* Explain why a woman taking the combined contraceptive pill does not ovulate.
*A.* The pill contains oestrogen and progesterone, which exert negative feedback on the pituitary and hypothalamus. Elevated progesterone inhibits FSH, so follicles do not grow and secrete less of their own oestrogen; elevated oestrogen and progesterone together inhibit the LH surge that triggers ovulation. With no ovulation there is no egg to be fertilised. The hormone levels are then withdrawn during the placebo week, the endometrium is no longer maintained, and withdrawal bleeding occurs — which is not a true menstruation, since no ovulation preceded it.

#### Fertilisation to birth

- **Fertilisation** — an ovulated secondary oocyte (arrested in metaphase II) meets a sperm, usually in the **fallopian tube**. The sperm's acrosome enzymes digest a path through the corona radiata and zona pellucida; the sperm and ovum nuclei fuse, triggering meiosis II to complete. Fertilisation restores the diploid number.
- **Cleavage** — the zygote divides mitotically without growth, producing smaller cells. It becomes a solid **morula**.
- **Blastocyst** — a fluid-filled cavity forms and the cells differentiate into an inner cell mass (which becomes the embryo) and outer cells (the trophoblast, which contributes to the placenta).
- **Implantation** — the blastocyst embeds in the endometrium. **hCG** is secreted and maintains the corpus luteum so progesterone continues and the endometrium is not shed.
- **Germ layers** — ectoderm (nervous system and outer covering), mesoderm (muscles, skeleton, circulatory system), endoderm (gut lining, liver, lungs). This is the first point at which the three primary germ layers appear.
- **Foetus and birth** — organ development proceeds, and **oxytocin** stimulates the uterus to contract. Positive feedback operates here: contraction triggers more oxytocin release, which strengthens contraction, until the baby is delivered.

#### Insect-pollinated versus wind-pollinated — with reasons

| Feature | Insect-pollinated | Wind-pollinated | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Petals | Large, coloured, scented | Small, dull or absent | Attracting an animal is efficient; attracting air is not needed |
| Nectar | Present | Absent | A reward for the animal; wind carries no reward |
| Pollen | Sticky, heavier, produced in modest amounts | Very light, large quantities | Must adhere to a visiting insect; must stay airborne |
| Anthers | Held firmly | Dangle on long filaments | To shed pollen into moving air |
| Stigma | Small, sticky, often on a short style | Large and feathery | To catch airborne pollen over a large area |

The exam rewards the reason column. "Feathery stigma" alone is half a mark; "a feathery stigma gives a large surface area so it traps more airborne pollen, since wind pollination is imprecise" is the full mark.

#### Seed dispersal

Each mechanism solves a different problem: getting the seed **away from the parent** (reducing competition with it and its pathogens) and getting it **somewhere it can germinate**. Wind and water move seeds large distances but land them anywhere; internal animal dispersal puts them in faeces, often near a nutrient source, and is highly successful for that reason. Seeds also produce growth regulators that stimulate the growth of the surrounding fruit tissues, so dispersal and fruit development are linked.

### 🔴 Deep Dive — Mastery & Edge Cases (3mo–2yr)

#### Worked Higher-level questions with answers

**Q1.** Compare artificial insemination, IVF and ICSI, saying what biological problem each addresses.
*A.* All three treat failure at a different step of the same sequence: sperm production, sperm reaching the egg, or sperm entering the egg. **Artificial insemination** places prepared sperm into the female reproductive tract at the appropriate point in the cycle, so it suits a problem with sperm count, motility, or with the timing of intercourse rather than with fertilisation itself. **IVF** removes eggs, matures them, and fertilises them in vitro with sperm, so it suits blocked or damaged fallopian tubes, or a need to use donated eggs, and it also allows embryos to be screened before implantation. **ICSI** injects a single sperm directly into an ovum's cytoplasm, bypassing the steps where many sperm must find and penetrate the egg, so it is used when there are very few or immotile sperm, or after repeated IVF failure. Each is justified by the stage of the biology it substitutes for.

**Q2.** Explain how the placenta supports the developing foetus and why its formation matters hormonally.
*A.* The placenta is formed from embryonic trophoblast tissue and maternal endometrial tissue, and its villi are bathed in maternal blood. Oxygen and nutrients diffuse across this interface into foetal blood, and CO₂ and metabolic wastes diffuse out. It also secretes **hCG**, which maintains the **corpus luteum** and therefore keeps **progesterone** high. This matters because progesterone maintains the endometrium and suppresses further ovulation; without it the uterine lining would break down and the pregnancy could not be sustained. After the first trimester the placenta takes over hormone production and the corpus luteum degenerates, which is why the hormone source changes during the pregnancy.

**Q3.** Evaluate the claim that screening every pregnancy for genetic conditions is always beneficial.
*A.* The evidence for it is that screening identifies conditions such as Down syndrome early, allowing parents to make an informed choice and enabling clinical intervention — for PKU, early dietary change prevents irreversible neurological damage, which is a clear health benefit. The limitations are that screening is not diagnosis: false positives cause unnecessary anxiety and further invasive testing such as amniocentesis, which itself carries a small miscarriage risk. Screening also raises ethical questions about selective termination and about the value placed on a life with a disability. A considered answer presents the health benefit, the risk of false positives and the ethical dimension, and reaches a position rather than a slogan.

**Q4.** A plant produces thousands of small, scentless, nectar-less flowers. Explain the pollination mechanism it must be using and predict two structural features of its pollen.
*A.* The combination points to **wind pollination**: abundant, small flowers with no investment in attracting animals, since attracting a pollinator with petals, scent and nectar costs resources that wind pollination does not need. Its pollen will be **very light** so it stays suspended and travels on air currents, and produced in **very large quantities** to compensate for the low probability that any individual grain lands on a receptive stigma. Associated features would be anthers held on long, dangling filaments outside the flower to release pollen into moving air, and a large feathery stigma with a high surface area and low threshold to trap grains. Contrast with a few large, scented, nectar-bearing flowers with sticky heavy pollen, which is the insect-pollinated pattern.

#### How this unit pays elsewhere

- **Unit 2.3** is the mechanical prerequisite — gametes exist only because meiosis makes haploid cells, and fertilisation restores the diploid number.
- **Unit 1.4** explains why offspring differ from their parents: independent assortment and recombination in meiosis, plus the random meeting of gametes at fertilisation.
- **Unit 2.4** supplies FSH, LH, oestrogen, progesterone, oxytocin, prolactin and ADH, and the feedback-loop framework that controls them.
- **Unit 1.2** supplies hCG and progesterone as steroid hormones derived from cholesterol.
- **Unit 3.3** applies it directly: IVF, ICSI, egg freezing, prenatal and genetic screening, and the ethical questions about each.
- **Biology in Practice Investigation** (40% of the grade) — investigating the effect of a growth regulator on fruit development, or pollen viability across a temperature range, produces exactly the primary-data-with-justified-conclusions evidence the descriptors reward.

#### Mistakes students actually make

1. **Forgetting that gamete production is by meiosis**, with the chromosome number halved. It is the link to unit 2.3 and it is examinable.
2. **Narrating the cycle without FSH and LH.** Four hormones, not two; the pituitary hormones are named explicitly in the specification.
3. **Saying oestrogen causes the endometrium to break down.** Progesterone maintains it; oestrogen builds it.
4. **Naming structures without their functions.** The specification asks you to *relate structure to function*, and the list of structures is long.
5. **Claiming the pill works by preventing implantation.** Its primary mechanism is suppressing FSH and LH, so no follicle matures and no ovulation occurs.
6. **Saying hormones are always the same in males and females.** They are present in both sexes; the levels and the feedback differ.
7. **Writing a list of pollination adaptations with no reasons attached.** The reasons are where the marks are.
8. **Omitting the practical consequences.** Disposable nappies, oxygenated storage of vegetables and the use of borage in planting are all real applications of the biology in this unit, and a "biology in society" question will often be about one of them.
