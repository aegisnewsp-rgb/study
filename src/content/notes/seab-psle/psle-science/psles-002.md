# Systems — PSLE Science Notes

Systems is the largest single section in the 2023 MOE Primary Science syllabus at Primary 6 by marks. The strand covers plant transport, the human digestive, circulatory, respiratory and excretory systems, and the interactions within and between systems. The PSLE Science paper assesses systems through a mix of multiple-choice items, structured questions and the open-ended question (OEQ), which is the highest-mark single item on Paper 2.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### The five systems a Primary 6 student must know

1. **Plant transport system**: xylem (water and minerals from roots to leaves), phloem (food from leaves to the rest of the plant).
2. **Human digestive system**: mouth, oesophagus, stomach, small intestine, large intestine, anus; the role of digestive juices.
3. **Human circulatory system**: heart, blood vessels (arteries, veins, capillaries), blood; the double circulation.
4. **Human respiratory system**: nose, trachea, bronchi, lungs, alveoli; gas exchange at the alveoli.
5. **Human excretory system**: kidneys, ureters, bladder, urethra; the skin also excretes sweat.

#### Key terms

- **Xylem**: vascular tissue that transports water and dissolved mineral salts from the roots upward.
- **Phloem**: vascular tissue that transports food (sugars) made in the leaves to other parts of the plant.
- **Transpiration**: loss of water vapour from the leaves through the stomata; the driving force for xylem transport.
- **Peristalsis**: wave-like muscular contractions that move food along the digestive tract.
- **Alveoli**: tiny air sacs in the lungs where gas exchange takes place.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Plant transport

The plant has two transport tissues, both inside the vascular bundles.

**Xylem** carries water and dissolved mineral salts from the roots upward to the leaves. Water enters the roots by osmosis, is pushed up by root pressure, and is pulled up by the transpiration stream. The driving force for the transpiration stream is evaporation of water from the leaves through the stomata. As water evaporates, more water is drawn up the xylem column to replace it (cohesion-tension theory, simplified for PSLE).

**Phloem** carries food (sugars) made in the leaves during photosynthesis to other parts of the plant — to the roots, stems, fruits, growing buds. The transport in phloem is in both directions (unlike xylem, which is mostly upward).

**Transpiration** is affected by light intensity, temperature, humidity and wind. A plant in bright light, high temperature, low humidity and high wind loses water faster. Faster transpiration means a faster transpiration stream.

#### Human digestive system

Food is broken down mechanically (chewing, churning) and chemically (digestive enzymes) as it passes through:

- **Mouth**: teeth chew food; saliva contains amylase that starts starch digestion.
- **Oesophagus**: tube connecting mouth to stomach; food moves by peristalsis.
- **Stomach**: muscular sac; gastric juice (hydrochloric acid + pepsin) digests protein.
- **Small intestine**: digestion is completed by pancreatic juice, bile and intestinal juice; digested food is absorbed into the blood through the villi.
- **Large intestine**: water is absorbed; undigested food forms faeces.
- **Anus**: faeces are excreted.

The digestive process converts large insoluble food molecules (starch, protein, fat) into small soluble molecules (sugars, amino acids, fatty acids and glycerol) that can be absorbed into the blood.

#### Human circulatory system

The heart is a double pump: the right side pumps deoxygenated blood to the lungs; the left side pumps oxygenated blood to the rest of the body.

- **Arteries** carry blood away from the heart; thick muscular walls; high pressure.
- **Veins** carry blood back to the heart; thinner walls; valves to prevent backflow; lower pressure.
- **Capillaries** are tiny vessels with walls one cell thick; the site of exchange of materials between blood and tissues.

**Double circulation**: blood goes through the heart twice in one complete circuit. Pulmonary circulation: right heart → lungs → left heart. Systemic circulation: left heart → body → right heart.

**Blood components**: red blood cells (carry oxygen), white blood cells (fight infection), platelets (clot blood), plasma (liquid that carries everything).

#### Human respiratory system

Air enters through the nose, passes through the trachea, splits into the two bronchi (one to each lung), then into smaller bronchioles, ending at the alveoli.

**Gas exchange at the alveoli**: oxygen diffuses from the alveolus into the blood in the surrounding capillaries; carbon dioxide diffuses from the blood into the alveolus. The alveoli are surrounded by a network of capillaries; the wall of an alveolus and the wall of a capillary together form a thin barrier through which gases diffuse easily.

**Breathing in** (inhalation): the diaphragm contracts and flattens; the rib cage moves up and out; the volume of the thorax increases; air pressure inside the lungs decreases; air flows in.

**Breathing out** (exhalation): the diaphragm relaxes and arches up; the rib cage moves down and in; the volume of the thorax decreases; air pressure inside the lungs increases; air flows out.

#### Human excretory system

The excretory system removes waste products from the body. The kidneys filter blood to remove urea, excess water, excess salts and other waste; the result is urine, which flows down the ureters to the bladder and out through the urethra.

The skin also excretes waste: sweat contains water, salts and a small amount of urea. Sweating helps regulate body temperature as well.

The lungs excrete carbon dioxide (a waste product of respiration) and water vapour.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where Systems appears across the paper

- **PSLE Science Paper 1**: multiple-choice and structured items testing the components of each system and the functions of each part.
- **PSLE Science Paper 2**: the open-ended question (OEQ) is most often a systems question — a passage describing an experiment, a clinical scenario or a real-world observation, followed by sub-questions that test interpretation across system boundaries.
- **Across both papers**: drawing clear labelled diagrams of each system is the highest-leverage exam technique. A correctly labelled diagram earns at least one method mark even if the explanation slips.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Xylem and phloem are the same thing." Xylem carries water and minerals (mostly upward); phloem carries food (sugars) in both directions.
- "The heart pumps blood around the body in one loop." The heart has two pumps; blood goes through the heart twice in one complete circuit.
- "Arteries always carry oxygenated blood." Most arteries do, but the pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood from the right heart to the lungs.
- "We breathe in oxygen and breathe out carbon dioxide." We breathe in air (which is mostly nitrogen, with about 21% oxygen), and we breathe out air with less oxygen and more carbon dioxide. The body uses the oxygen and produces the carbon dioxide.
- "The digestive system and the circulatory system are separate." The digestive system breaks down food into small soluble molecules; the circulatory system then transports those molecules to every cell in the body.

#### Exam technique

- For diagram questions, draw a clean labelled diagram before writing the explanation. The diagram is the method mark.
- For OEQs, read the whole passage first, then read the sub-questions, then go back to the passage for each sub-question. The PSLE Science OEQ tests interpretation of the passage, not recall of the syllabus.
- For "what would happen if X failed" questions, follow the molecule or the function step by step. A statement like "if the alveoli are damaged, less oxygen enters the blood" is the format that earns the method mark.
- For comparison questions (e.g. "compare the wall of an artery with the wall of a vein"), use the comparison table form. State the property, then state the difference explicitly.
