# Demand and Supply

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Demand** is the quantity of a good consumers are **willing and able** to buy at various prices during a given period, **ceteris paribus**. **Supply** is the quantity producers are willing and able to offer at various prices, ceteris paribus. The **Law of Demand** states an inverse price–quantity relationship; the **Law of Supply** states a direct relationship.

Market **equilibrium price (Pe)** and **equilibrium quantity (Qe)** occur where the demand curve intersects the supply curve. Above Pe → **surplus**; below Pe → **shortage**.

**Price Elasticity of Demand:** Ep = (%ΔQ) / (%ΔP) = (ΔQ/Q) ÷ (ΔP/P).

- Ep > 1 → elastic demand
- Ep = 1 → unit elastic
- Ep < 1 → inelastic demand

For CMA Foundation: memorise the **Total Expenditure method** (TE = P × Q) and the **arc formula** with average base.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Law of Demand and Its Reasons

The **Law of Demand** says that, holding other factors constant, quantity demanded falls when price rises and rises when price falls. Four reasons explain this inverse relationship: the **law of diminishing marginal utility** (each extra unit gives less satisfaction), the **income effect** (real purchasing power falls as price rises), the **substitution effect** (buyers switch to cheaper alternatives), and the **law of saturation of wants**.

#### Law of Supply

The **Law of Supply** states a direct relationship — producers offer more at higher prices to earn greater revenue. A change in *own price* causes **movement along** the supply curve; a change in *input costs, technology, taxes, or number of sellers* **shifts** the whole curve.

#### Shift vs Movement

| Change in | Curve action | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| Own price of the good | Movement along the curve | Up/down |
| Consumer income | Shift of demand curve | Right (normal good) / Left (inferior good) |
| Price of a substitute | Shift of demand curve | Right if substitute's price rises |
| Price of a complement | Shift of demand curve | Left if complement's price rises |
| Input cost / technology | Shift of supply curve | Right (cost ↓ or tech ↑) / Left (cost ↑) |

#### Market Equilibrium

**Equilibrium price (Pe)** clears the market: Qd = Qs. At any price above Pe, surplus pressure pushes price down; below Pe, shortage pulls price up.

#### Elasticity — Four Methods

- **Percentage method:** Ep = (ΔQ/Q) ÷ (ΔP/P)
- **Point method:** Ep = lower segment ÷ upper segment (of a linear demand curve)
- **Arc method:** Ep = [(Q₂−Q₁)/(Q₂+Q₁)] ÷ [(P₂−P₁)/(P₂+P₁)]
- **Total Expenditure method:** observe how TE = P × Q responds to a price change

> **Exam trap:** If TE *rises* when price *falls*, demand is **elastic (Ep > 1)** — many students reverse this rule.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Worked Example — Arc Elasticity

Suppose price of a commodity rises from ₹40 to ₹50, and quantity demanded falls from 100 units to 80 units.

Arc Ep = [(80−100)/(80+100)] ÷ [(50−40)/(50+40)]
= [−20/180] ÷ [10/90]
= −0.1111 ÷ 0.1111
= **|Ep| = 1** (unit elastic)

#### Cross and Income Elasticity

**Income elasticity (Ey):** positive for **normal goods**, negative for **inferior goods**. Ey > 1 indicates a luxury; 0 < Ey < 1 indicates a necessity.

**Cross elasticity (Ec):** Ec > 0 → **substitutes** (tea and coffee); Ec < 0 → **complements** (cars and petrol).

#### Common Mistakes in CMA Papers

- Forgetting the negative sign convention: price and quantity move in opposite directions, so Ep is reported as an **absolute value** in CMA numericals.
- Mixing **slope** with **elasticity** — on a straight-line demand curve, elasticity is high at the top (elastic), equals 1 at the midpoint, and is low at the bottom (inelastic), even though slope is constant.
- Using the **wrong base** in arc elasticity: average (Q₁+Q₂)/2 vs initial Q₁ gives different numerical answers; CMA specifies which to use.

#### Adjacent Connections

- **Consumer's equilibrium** (utility maximisation: MUx/Px = MUy/Py) extends the demand side using indifference-curve analysis.
- **Producer's equilibrium** (MC = MR) links supply theory to cost curves.
- **Government intervention:** price ceilings cause persistent shortages; price floors create surpluses — directly testable from a demand-supply diagram.

#### Practice Prompts

1. A fall in price from ₹20 to ₹18 raises quantity demanded from 50 to 60 units. Using the percentage method, compute Ep and state whether demand is elastic or inelastic.
2. Tea and coffee are substitutes. If the price of coffee rises by 10% and the quantity demanded of tea rises by 4%, calculate the cross elasticity Ec and interpret the relationship.

> **Scoring tip:** In CMA Foundation, two-markers ask for *definitions plus one reason*; four-markers require a labelled diagram (Pe, Qe, surplus/shortage) or a complete elasticity calculation with interpretation.
