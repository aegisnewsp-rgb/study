# Antonyms — Vocabulary Building for CLAT

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

An **antonym** is a word whose meaning is most nearly *opposite* to a given word in the same sense and register. CLAT tests antonyms inside the **English Language section**, where roughly **2–5 questions** appear per paper across passage-based, fill-in-the-blank, and word-substitution formats.

- **Three types to recognise:** **gradable** (hot ↔ cold, on a scale), **complementary/binary** (alive ↔ dead, no middle), and **relational** (teacher ↔ student, buy ↔ sell — they reverse a relationship).
- **Always read the sentence first.** The "correct" antonym is the one opposite in the **same sense** the passage uses, not in some other dictionary sense.
- **Spot the trap:** "most nearly the **same**" or "**NOT** an antonym" questions appear regularly — read the stem word by word.
- **Speed hack:** decode roots — *mal-, in-, un-, dis-, anti-* usually signal the negative side.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### How CLAT frames antonym questions

CLAT rarely asks "give an antonym of X" in isolation. The stem is usually embedded in a sentence or a short passage, and four options include one true antonym, two near-synonyms, and one distractor that is opposite in a *different* sense. Marks come from the **English Language** slot of the 150-mark paper; vocabulary contributes roughly **18–20%** of that section.

#### The three families of antonyms

| Type | How opposites work | CLAT example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Gradable** | Sit on a continuum; "more/less" applies | *big → small*, *amplify → diminish*, *optimist → pessimist* |
| **Complementary (binary)** | No middle ground; one implies the other | *alive → dead*, *present → absent*, *transparent → opaque* |
| **Relational** | Reverse a two-way relationship between roles | *borrow → lend*, *teacher → student*, *import → export* |

#### Choosing the right opposite in four steps

1. **Read the sentence** and lock the *exact sense* of the stem word (especially polysemous words like *bank*, *cast*, *cleave*).
2. **Strike out near-synonyms** — if an option means roughly the same thing, it cannot be the antonym.
3. **Test the remaining opposites** against the context: *slack* may need *taut* (relational), not *lazy* (graded).
4. **Watch the negative stem** — "which is **NOT** an antonym" flips the logic entirely.

- **Connotation trap:** *slim*, *slender*, *lean* are positive; *skinny*, *gaunt* are negative — only one of these pairs cleanly with *sturdy*.
- **Root-based decoding:** *benevolent* / *malevolent*, *verbose* / *laconic*, *ephemeral* / *perennial* — learn the root, harvest both ends.
- **Common CLAT-level pairs to drill:** *verbose ↔ laconic*, *ephemeral ↔ perennial*, *obstinate ↔ pliable*, *ostensible ↔ ulterior*, *sanguine ↔ despondent*.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge cases examiners exploit

The hardest antonym items involve **polysemy** — one stem word with two unrelated meanings, each having its own antonym. *Sanction* can mean "to approve" (antonym: *censure*) or "a penalty" (antonym: *reward*). CLAT picks the sense used in the sentence; missing that is the single most common error.

A second edge case is the **graded-pair reversal**. *Hot* has *cold, cool, warm, tepid, freezing* as possible opposites in different contexts. The answer key rewards the *best contextual* fit, not the textbook pair. Similarly, *optimist* → *pessimist*, not *sad*; *borrow* → *lend*, not *return*; *deflect* → *incur*, not *avoid*.

#### Worked micro-example

> Stem in sentence: *"The minister's **ostensible** reason for the resignation was ill health."*
> Options: (a) *apparent* (b) *ulterior* (c) *overt* (d) *stated*
> — *Apparent* and *stated* are synonyms of *ostensible*, so they go out.
> — *Overt* means "openly shown," which is close but not opposite.
> — *Ulterior* (hidden, concealed motive) flips the sense exactly. **Answer: (b).**

#### Common mistakes and how to avoid them

| Mistake | Why it loses marks | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Picking a word that is "different," not opposite | Different ≠ antonym | Ask: does this reverse the meaning? |
| Ignoring sentence context | Same word, different antonym per sense | Re-read the stem sentence first |
| Confusing antonym with synonym under time pressure | Speed misreads "opposite" as "similar" | Circle the stem verb: opposite or same? |
| Treating graded pairs as absolutes | *Warm* is not always the antonym of *cold* | Pick the opposite that fits the passage's intensity |
| Missing **NOT** in the stem | Logic flips silently | Underline *NOT*, *EXCEPT*, *FALSE* before solving |

#### Two practice prompts

1. *"Her **adamant** refusal left no room for negotiation."* — pick the antonym of *adamant* in this context from: *flexible, stubborn, firm, resolute, rigid*.
2. *"The treaty was deemed **mutual**, binding both parties equally."* — choose the antonym from: *shared, unilateral, joint, reciprocal, common*.

#### Building vocabulary for CLAT

- **Daily dose:** 5–10 word pairs (word + antonym + synonym + one usage sentence) from *Word Power Made Easy* or Manohar Pandey.
- **Weekly revision:** revisit last week's pairs using spaced repetition — most retention loss happens in days 1–3.
- **Passage exposure:** read *The Hindu* editorials; underline unfamiliar words and write their antonyms on the margin.
- **Mock-test audit:** after every mock, list every antonym you got wrong and classify the error type (polysemy, graded, relational, connotation).
