# Indian Geography & Environment

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

India sits between **8°4'N–37°6'N** latitude and **68°7'E–97°25'E** longitude, crossing the Tropic of Cancer at **23°5'N**. The country is divided into five physiographic units: the **Northern Mountains** (Greater, Lesser, Outer Himalayas), the **Northern Plains** (Indo-Gangetic), the **Peninsular Plateau** (oldest landmass, Deccan + Central Highlands), the **Indian Desert** (Thar), and the **Coastal Plains & Islands** (Andaman-Nicobar volcanic, Lakshadweep coral).

Climate is **tropical monsoon**, with the **South-West monsoon (June–September)** delivering ~75% of India's rainfall, driven by ITCZ shift, pressure gradient, and the Somali jet. The **North-East monsoon (Oct–Dec)** brings rain to Tamil Nadu. Forest cover is ~21.7% of geographic area; India hosts **4 biodiversity hotspots** (Himalaya, Indo-Burma, Sundaland, Western Ghats).

| Key Term | One-Line Fact |
|---|---|
| IST | Based on **82°30'E** (Mirzapur/Allahabad); UTC +5:30 |
| Highest peak (India) | **Kangchenjunga** (8,586 m) — K2 lies in PoK |
| Longest river | **Ganga** (2,525 km) |
| Largest lake | **Wular** (freshwater, J&K); Chilika is largest brackish |
| Largest delta | **Sundarbans** (Ganga–Brahmaputra) |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"K-W-M-H-R"** for NW-to-SE physiographic order — **K**ashmir Himalaya, **W**estern Ghats, **M**ountains (NE), **H**imalayas (Eastern), **R**ivers (deltaic SE). For monsoon dates: **"J-S for SW, O-D for NE"** (June–Sep / Oct–Dec).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Physiographic Divisions & Drainage

The **Himalayan drainage** (Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra) is **perennial, dendritic, and antecedent** — rivers older than the mountains they cut through. The **Peninsular drainage** (Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri, Narmada, Tapi) is **rain-fed, radial/trellis, and non-perennial**. Narmada and Tapi flow west through rift valleys and form **estuaries**, not deltas.

| Feature | Himalayan Rivers | Peninsular Rivers |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Glacial melt + rainfall | Rainfall only |
| Flow type | Perennial | Seasonal |
| Pattern | Dendritic, antecedent | Radial, trellis |
| Delta/Estuary | Sundarbans delta | Godavari/Krishna delta; Narmada/Tapi estuary |
| Example | Ganga, Indus | Godavari, Narmada |

#### Climate & Monsoon Mechanism

The **SW monsoon** originates from the **Mascarene High**, crosses the equator as the **Somali jet (Cross-Equatorial Flow)**, and is pulled by the **ITCZ shift to the Gangetic Plain**. Rainfall is **orographic** along the Western Ghats (Agumbe ~7,000 mm) and **cyclonic** in the Bay of Bengal (June–October cyclones). **Western Disturbances** are temperate cyclones from the Mediterranean that bring winter rain to NW India and snow to the Himalayas. **Jet streams** — the **subtropical westerly jet (winter)** and the **tropical easterly jet (summer)** — govern monsoon onset and withdrawal.

| Köppen Code | Climate Type | Region in India |
|---|---|---|
| **Aw** | Tropical savanna (dry winter) | Most of Deccan Plateau |
| **Am** | Tropical monsoon | Western coast, NE India |
| **BSh** | Hot semi-arid | Rajasthan, Kutch |
| **Cwb** | Subtropical highland | Himalayas, Nilgiris |
| **Dfb** | Humid continental | Higher Himalaya |

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** The Narmada river flows westward and forms an estuary rather than a delta. Which of the following is the correct reason?
(a) It flows through a rift valley with a steep gradient
(b) It receives heavy rainfall at its mouth
(c) It originates in a volcanic plateau
(d) It is a tributary of a larger peninsular river

**Solution:** Narmada rises at **Amarkantak (1,057 m)** in Madhya Pradesh and flows through the **Narmada–Tapi rift valley** — a tectonic fault created during the Cretaceous. Because the river descends sharply through the rift, it deposits coarse sediments before reaching the sea, preventing delta formation. It also lacks a major tributary near its mouth. Option (a) is correct.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students often pick (b), thinking heavy rainfall aids delta-building. In reality, delta formation requires a **gentle gradient and sediment-laden slow flow** — Narmada has the opposite.

#### Soil, Vegetation & Protected Areas

| Soil Type | Parent Material | Key Crops | Region |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alluvial | River deposits | Rice, wheat | Indo-Gangetic Plain |
| Black (Regur) | Basalt (Deccan trap) | Cotton, soybean | Maharashtra, Gujarat |
| Red | Crystalline igneous | Millets, groundnut | Eastern Deccan |
| Laterite | Leached tropical rock | Tea, cashew | Western Ghats, NE |
| Desert | Aeolian sand | Bajra, guar | Western Rajasthan |
| Mountain | Glacial/frost action | Apple, tea | Himalayas |

**Forest types** follow rainfall: Tropical Evergreen (>200 cm rainfall), Tropical Deciduous (100–200 cm, most widespread), Thorn (<50 cm, Rajasthan), Montane (altitude-zoned), and Mangrove (Sundarbans, Bhitarkanika). India has **75+ Tiger Reserves** under Project Tiger (1973), **18 Biosphere Reserves** (UNESCO MAB), and **80+ Ramsar Sites** as of 2025.

#### Key Environmental Laws

- **Wildlife Protection Act, 1972** — schedules I–VI; Schedule I has the highest protection
- **Forest Conservation Act, 1980** — restricts diversion of forest land for non-forest use
- **Environment Protection Act, 1986** — umbrella act post-Bhopal disaster
- **National Green Tribunal Act, 2010** — adjudicates environmental disputes
- **CAMPA** — Compensatory Afforestation Fund

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases & Frequently Confused Pairs

| Commonly Confused | Correct Distinction |
|---|---|
| Western Ghats vs Eastern Ghats | Western Ghats: **continuous, higher** (Anamudi 2,695 m), UNESCO site, **biodiversity hotspot**. Eastern Ghats: **discontinuous, lower**, broken by rivers |
| Retreating monsoon vs NE trade winds | Retreating SW monsoon = **Oct–Dec rainfall in Tamil Nadu**. NE trades = **dry winds over land**, pick up moisture only over Bay of Bengal |
| IST longitude confusion | IST = 82°30'E (Mirzapur), **not** Kolkata (88°30'E) or Mumbai (72°50'E) |
| K2 vs Kangchenjunga | K2 (Godwin-Austen, 8,611 m) lies in **PoK**. **Kangchenjunga (8,586 m)** is India's highest peak, on the Sikkim border |
| Coriolis & monsoon direction | Coriolis deflects winds **right in NH** — causes SW monsoon to strike Western Ghats first |
| Biosphere Reserve vs National Park | Biosphere Reserve has **core, buffer, transition zones** for research; National Park is stricter, no human activity allowed |

#### Advanced Traps in AILET-Style MCQs

1. Marking **Chilika (Odisha)** as the largest freshwater lake — it is the **largest brackish water lagoon**; **Wular (J&K)** is the largest freshwater.
2. Confusing **Loktak (Manipur)** — it has the only **floating lake (Keibul Lamjao)** and is a Ramsar site.
3. Assuming **mangroves** are limited to Sundarbans — significant mangroves also occur at **Bhitarkanika (Odisha)**, **Godavari delta (Andhra)**, **Gulf of Kutch (Gujarat)**.
4. Citing **Project Elephant** as covering all of India — it covers ~32 reserves across 14 states.
5. Believing the **Indian Desert (Thar)** is lifeless — it supports the **Great Indian Bustard Sanctuary (Nanaj, Solapur, was in news)** and **Desert National Park (Jaisalmer)**.

#### Exam Strategy for AILET

With **3% weight**, expect **1–3 MCQs** in the GK section. Prioritise factual recall: highest peaks, longest rivers, desert names, Köppen codes, biosphere reserves, and Ramsar sites added in the past year. Connect each fact to a current-affairs hook — e.g., **Pichavaram (Tamil Nadu)**, **Sundarbans**, **Vembanad-Kol** Ramsar additions, **cheetah reintroduction (Kuno, 2022)**. Allocate **30–45 seconds per question** in the GK section; skip and return rather than over-reason.

#### Two Advanced Practice Prompts

1. **Assertion–Reason:** "Assertion (A): The Eastern Ghats are not a biodiversity hotspot, but the Western Ghats are. Reason (R): The Western Ghats have continuous forest cover and higher orographic rainfall." Evaluate.
   - *Answer:* Both A and R are true, and R explains A. Western Ghats: continuous, 2,500 mm+ rainfall, **UNESCO World Heritage Site (2012)**, **one of the eight 'hottest hotspots'** (Norman Myers).
2. **Statement-based:** Which of the following is **not** correctly matched? (a) Sundarbans–Ganga–Brahmaputra delta, (b) Wular Lake–freshwater–J&K, (c) Loktak–floating lake–Assam, (d) Chilika–brackish–Odisha.
   - *Answer:* (c) is wrong — Loktak is in **Manipur**, not Assam.
