# Critical Thinking and Response to Texts — Qatar Secondary Certificate English Notes

The MoEHE English Curriculum Standards at Grade 12 (CEFR B2 Low-Mid) expect inference, evaluation of argument and evidence, comparison of perspectives, and the ability to produce a sustained written or spoken response that synthesises information from two or more sources. These are the higher-order reading and writing skills that move a candidate from B1 to B2.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Inference:** what the writer implies but does not state directly.
- **Evaluation:** is the argument logical? Is the evidence relevant and sufficient? Are there counter-arguments?
- **Perspective:** the writer's viewpoint, bias, or attitude.
- **Synthesis:** combining information from two or more sources to form a coherent view.
- **B2 critical thinking markers:** recognising the writer's stance, distinguishing fact from opinion, identifying underlying assumptions.

#### Examiner traps

- Treating inference as guesswork — inference must be supported by the text.
- Conflating the writer's view with the writer's description of others' views.
- Producing a summary without an argument — a B2 critical response argues, not just reports.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Inference in reading

The writer may not state the main idea directly. Look for: (1) the topic that dominates the passage; (2) the writer's repeated emphasis; (3) the words the writer chooses to describe the topic (positive vs negative language); (4) any explicit statement of the writer's view.

Example: a passage about social media that emphasises "anxiety", "isolation", "misinformation" — the writer's stance is critical. Even without saying "social media is harmful", the inference is clear.

#### Evaluation of argument and evidence

Logical structure: does the conclusion follow from the premises? Are there gaps in the argument? Are there alternative explanations?

Evidence: is it relevant? Sufficient? Recent? From a credible source? Consistent across sources?

Counter-arguments: what would someone who disagrees say? Is the counter-argument stronger or weaker than the original argument?

#### Comparing perspectives

Two articles on the same topic may present opposing views. Identify: (1) what each writer agrees on (the shared facts); (2) where they disagree (the interpretation or evaluation); (3) what each writer assumes but does not state.

A B2 response synthesises: where the two perspectives converge, where they diverge, and what the reader can conclude.

#### Synthesised response

A synthesised response (written or spoken) typically has: (1) introduction stating the topic and the two perspectives; (2) body paragraph 1 on perspective A with evidence; (3) body paragraph 2 on perspective B with evidence; (4) body paragraph 3 on the synthesis — what is the relationship between the two perspectives, and what is your informed view; (5) conclusion.

---

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### Where critical thinking appears at the terminal

The MoEHE Grade 12 English terminal tests critical reading through inference and evaluation questions, and tests critical writing through the essay component. B2 candidates are expected to engage with arguments, not just describe them.

#### Exam technique

- For inference questions, find the textual support — never answer without evidence.
- For comparative essays, plan the structure before writing — introduction, both perspectives, synthesis, conclusion.
- For evaluative tasks, identify the argument's structure (claim, evidence, reasoning) before evaluating.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Critical thinking = criticism." No — critical thinking evaluates strengths and weaknesses, not just weaknesses.
- "Synthesis = summary." Synthesis combines sources to form a new view; summary condenses one source.
