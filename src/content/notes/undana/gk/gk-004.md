# Verbal Reasoning Mechanics

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

This topic trains **penalaran verbal** and **penalaran numerik** — two skills that decide UNDANA General Knowledge scores far more than memorised facts. Verbal reasoning asks you to read a short Indonesian passage and pick the valid **kesimpulan** (conclusion), the hidden **asumsi** (assumption), or the weakest link in a **silogisme** chain. Numerical reasoning uses six workhorse formulas you must know cold:

- **Percentage:** P% = (bagian / keseluruhan) × 100%
- **Simple interest:** R = P × r × t / 100
- **Compound interest:** A = P × (1 + r/n)^(n·t)
- **Distance:** D = V × t (watch unit conversion km/h ↔ m/s)
- **Ratio scaling:** a : b = a·k : b·k

> Mnemonic: **PARC-S** — Percentage, Assumption, Ratio, Compound, Speed.

Skip "cannot be determined" options only when the passage leaves zero inference room.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Verbal Reasoning Mechanics

A UNDANA verbal item presents a 3–6 sentence passage, then asks one of four things: main idea, assumption, conclusion, or argument strength. The argument has three layers — **premis** (premise), **asumsi** (unstated belief needed for the conclusion to hold), and **kesimpulan** (stated conclusion). The trap is treating the assumption as the conclusion; the conclusion must already be stated, while the assumption is the bridge the author leaves unsaid.

A valid **silogisme** follows: Premis 1 (all A are B) + Premis 2 (C is A) → Conclusion (C is B). Any break in the chain — usually swapping "some" for "all" — invalidates the argument. Watch for **hasty generalization** (one case → all cases) and **false cause** (correlation claimed as causation); both appear in 1–2 items per UNDANA session.

#### Numerical Reasoning Patterns

| Operation | Formula | Typical trap |
|---|---|---|
| Percentage change | (baru − lama) / lama × 100% | Confusing % increase with percentage points |
| Speed conversion | 1 m/s = 3.6 km/h | Mixing units mid-calculation |
| Ratio share | Bagi = total × a/(a+b+c) | Forgetting the common factor |
| Simple vs compound | R = Prt/100 vs A = P(1+r/n)^(nt) | Using simple formula for >1 year |

#### Indonesian-Context Framing

Many passages reference **budaya Nusantara**, **sejarah singkat** Indonesia, or **Pancasila / NKRI** concepts. You do not need specialist history — you need reading-comprehension discipline. The civic framing is a wrapper; the reasoning task is the same as a generic verbal item.

> Time budget: 45–60 seconds per item. If two options both seem possible, pick the one the passage *states*, not the one you *infer* beyond the text.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases in Argument Evaluation

When an item offers a "cannot be determined" choice, the correct answer usually appears when the passage gives a **specific example** but the conclusion sweeps to **all members** of a category. Example: "Andi from Kupang scored above average, therefore all UNDANA applicants score above average." The data supports one case, not the population — select *cannot be determined* (or the hasty-generalization label).

**Strengthening vs weakening questions**: a strengthener adds a premise that closes an assumption gap; a weakener introduces a counterexample or alternative cause. UNDANA tests these through short Indonesian civic passages, so practice linking each option back to a specific sentence.

#### Numerical Edge Cases

Compound interest items occasionally compound monthly (n = 12) while the rate is quoted annually. Convert r to a decimal **before** substituting — r = 5% must enter the formula as 0.05, not 5. For speed problems, the formula D = V × t requires **consistent units**: convert km/h to m/s by dividing by 3.6, or convert m to km before multiplying.

#### Worked Micro-Example

A bank offers 5% annual interest, compounded quarterly for 3 years on Rp 10,000,000. Using A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt): A = 10,000,000 × (1 + 0.05/12)^(12·3) ≈ Rp 11,616,170. Using the simple formula would give only Rp 11,500,000 — a 116,170 IDR gap. Examiners exploit that gap.

#### Common Mistakes

- Choosing an assumption as the conclusion
- Forgetting the ratio common factor when redistributing totals
- Treating **correlation** as **causation** in civic passages
- Skipping the last 30 seconds to verify unit consistency

#### Practice Prompts

1. A passage claims "Because rainfall rose 10% in 2024, agricultural output must rise 10% in 2025." Identify the assumption and state whether the argument is valid.
2. Solve: a sum of Rp 8,000,000 grows at 6% simple interest for 2 years. Compare the result with 6% compounded annually for 2 years — which yields more, and by how much?
