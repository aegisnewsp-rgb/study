# School-based Assessment (SBA) — Reading/Viewing Programme — HKDSE English Language Notes

The School-based Assessment (SBA) of the HKDSE English Language examination contributes 15% of the subject mark and is compulsory for all school candidates. Private candidates are exempt — their result is based entirely on the public examination. The SBA is a reading/viewing programme in which S4-S6 students read or view 2 to 4 texts, including at least one print and one non-print text, and write personal reflections. Students either take part in a discussion with classmates or make an individual presentation. Two SBA marks are reported — one in S5 and one in S6. Of the two marks, one must be from a group interaction and one from an individual presentation. The SBA assesses oral performance; the reading/viewing/writing serves as the means and is not itself scored.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **SBA weight**: 15% of the subject mark.
- **Eligible candidates**: school candidates; private candidates are exempt.
- **Format**: a reading/viewing programme — students read/view 2 to 4 texts (at least one print and one non-print), write personal reflections, then either discuss in a group or make an individual presentation.
- **Frequency**: two assessments reported — one in S5 and one in S6.
- **Mark mix**: of the two marks, one must be based on a group interaction and one on an individual presentation.
- **Assessment focus**: oral performance; the written work is not scored but is the means for the oral task.

#### Examiner traps

- Submitting memorised presentations — the teacher-assessor recognises a rehearsed performance and the mark drops.
- Skipping the written reflections — they are not scored, but they support the oral task; weak reflections mean weak oral performance.
- Choosing texts that are too easy or too hard — the text must be substantive enough for discussion but accessible enough to engage with.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### The reading/viewing programme

Students select 2 to 4 texts over the three-year senior secondary course. The texts must include at least one print and one non-print text, and at least one fiction and one non-fiction text.

Print texts include: novels, short stories, poetry, magazine articles, newspaper opinion pieces, biography, travel writing.

Non-print texts include: films, documentaries, TED talks, podcasts, TV drama series, animation.

For each text, the student writes personal reflections — typically 200-500 words — covering:

- What the text is about (summary).
- What the student found interesting or challenging.
- How the text relates to the student's own experience or to other texts.

The reflections are not scored. They serve as the basis for the discussion or presentation.

#### The discussion (group interaction)

The discussion is a 8-12 minute conversation among 3-4 students (or with the teacher) about the texts read/viewed. The teacher-assessor observes and scores the student's oral performance.

What is scored:

- **Ability to express ideas** clearly and coherently.
- **Use of supporting evidence** from the texts.
- **Interaction with other students** — listening, responding, building on others' points.
- **Range of vocabulary** and language structures.
- **Pronunciation and fluency**.

#### The individual presentation

The individual presentation is a 3-5 minute talk by the student, followed by the teacher-assessor's questions. The student may refer to notes but should not read a script.

What is scored:

- **Clarity of argument** and structure.
- **Use of supporting evidence** from the texts.
- **Engagement with the text** — analysis, evaluation, personal response.
- **Range of vocabulary** and language structures.
- **Pronunciation, intonation, pace, gesture**.

#### Marking criteria

The HKEAA SBA Handbook provides detailed marking criteria and samples of performance at each level. The teacher-assessor scores the student's oral performance against these criteria. Marks are then moderated by HKEAA.

#### Retention requirements

Students must retain their SBA work (the texts read/viewed and the personal reflections) until the release of HKDSE results. HKEAA may inspect the work for authentication.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-paper links

- **Reading (Paper 1)**: the texts read in the SBA programme build the comprehension stamina needed for Paper 1.
- **Writing (Paper 2)**: the personal reflections in the SBA programme practise the writing skills needed for Paper 2.
- **Speaking (Paper 4)**: the SBA discussions and presentations are practice for the Speaking paper.

#### Exam technique

- **Choose texts you genuinely engage with**: a text you find interesting produces better reflections and better oral performance.
- **Keep a reading journal**: log each text, your reflections, and any discussion points. The journal supports the SBA assessment.
- **Practice the oral skills**: in class, participate actively in discussions. Outside class, record yourself giving presentations and listen back.
- **Read widely and diversely**: news, opinion, fiction, biography, science writing. The wider the range, the easier Paper 1 and the SBA become.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "The SBA marks are automatic." No — the teacher-assessor scores against published criteria. Marks can range from low to high.
- "Memorising a presentation guarantees a high SBA mark." No — the assessor scores the oral performance, which includes response to questions and interaction.
- "Private candidates have an advantage." No — private candidates are exempt from SBA, so their entire subject mark is the public exam. This puts more weight on Papers 1-4.
