# Earth and Space Science

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Earth and Space Science covers the planet as a system: its place in the **solar system**, its internal **layers (crust, mantle, core)**, the surrounding **atmosphere**, the circulating **hydrosphere**, and the long-term **climate** versus short-term **weather**. In the UPCAT, this block is roughly 3% of the UPG composite and is tested through conceptual multiple-choice items, not heavy computation.

- **Weather vs. climate** — weather changes hour-to-hour; climate averages 30+ years.
- **Seasons** come from Earth's 23.5° **axial tilt**, not from distance to the Sun.
- **Rotation** = spin on axis (≈24 h); **revolution** = orbit around the Sun (≈365.25 d).
- **Plate tectonics** drives earthquakes, volcanoes, mountain building, and seafloor spreading.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Earth's Internal Structure
The planet divides into four mechanical layers. The thin **crust** (5–70 km) floats on the **mantle**, which behaves as a slowly convecting solid. Below it sit the liquid **outer core** (source of the geomagnetic field via the geodynamo) and the solid **inner core**, where iron-nickel alloys endure pressures above 300 GPa. The rigid outer shell — crust plus uppermost mantle — is the **lithosphere**, broken into plates that move over the weaker **asthenosphere**.

#### Plate Tectonics and the Rock Cycle
Plate boundaries come in three flavours: **divergent** (seafloor spreading at mid-ocean ridges), **convergent** (subduction zones, volcanic arcs, Himalaya-type collisions), and **transform** (strike-slip faults like the San Andreas). These movements drive earthquakes and most volcanoes. Rocks cycle among **igneous** (cooled magma/lava), **sedimentary** (compacted clastic or chemical precipitates), and **metamorphic** (recrystallised under heat and pressure but not melted).

#### Atmosphere, Hydrosphere, and Weather
The atmosphere is layered from bottom up as **troposphere** (weather), **stratosphere** (ozone layer), **mesosphere**, **thermosphere**, and **exosphere**. The **water cycle** links ocean, atmosphere, and land through **evaporation, condensation, precipitation, and runoff**. **Weather** is the instantaneous state of temperature, humidity, pressure, and wind; **climate** is the multi-decadal statistical summary.

| Concept | Key point |
| --- | --- |
| Crust vs. mantle | Crust is rigid and thin; mantle convects on geological timescales. |
| Igneous vs. sedimentary | Igneous forms from cooled melt; sedimentary forms from compacted debris. |
| Troposphere vs. stratosphere | Weather occurs in the troposphere; ozone sits in the stratosphere. |

- Summer in the Northern Hemisphere arrives when the North Pole tilts toward the Sun, not when Earth is closest to the Sun (perihelion is in January).
- A **solar eclipse** happens only at new moon when the Moon crosses the ecliptic node; **lunar** eclipses happen at full moon under the same alignment.
- Tides are dominated by the Moon's gravitational pull; the Sun modulates them into spring tides and neap tides.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Earth-Sun-Moon Geometry
Earth rotates west-to-east on a tilted axis, producing the diurnal cycle. Its 365.25-day **revolution** and 23.5° tilt combine to produce the seasonal march of the equinoxes and solstices. **Precession** of the axis over ~26,000 years shifts the pole star, while **nutation** adds a shorter 18.6-year wobble linked to lunar nodal regression. Tidal locking keeps the Moon's rotation equal to its orbital period, so the same hemisphere faces Earth.

#### Climate, Greenhouse Effect, and Resources
The **greenhouse effect** is the absorption of outgoing longwave infrared by water vapour, CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, and O₃, followed by re-radiation that warms the lower atmosphere. Anthropogenic CO₂ emissions have intensified this effect since the Industrial Revolution. Renewable resources (solar, wind, hydro, geothermal) regenerate within human timescales; non-renewables (coal, oil, natural gas, uranium) do not.

#### Common Pitfalls and Exam Strategy
UPCAT Science items reward careful reading — distractors exploit exactly the confusions listed below.

| Mistake | Correction |
| --- | --- |
| Weather = climate | Weather is short-term; climate is the 30-year average. |
| Distance causes seasons | Axial tilt causes seasons; distance changes slightly affect northern-southern contrast. |
| Rotation = revolution | Rotation = ~24 h spin; revolution = ~365.25 d orbit. |
| Eclipses monthly | Eclipses require node alignment and happen only 2–5 times per year globally. |
| Crust = mantle | Crust is silica-rich and thin; mantle is silicate and ~2,900 km thick. |

- **Worked check:** A seismologist notes S-wave shadow zones above 103° from an earthquake epicentre. The cause is the liquid **outer core**, which blocks S-waves but lets P-waves through with reduced velocity — direct evidence for the core's state.
- **Worked check:** Manila experiences typhoons in the second half of the year because the **Intertropical Convergence Zone** migrates north, and the warm **Pacific** SSTs feed cyclogenesis.

#### Practice Prompts
1. Explain in three sentences why the Philippines experiences a wet season and a dry season, naming the wind system and the ITCZ.
2. A granite boulder is weathered, eroded, deposited in a river delta, buried, and recrystallised under pressure. Name each rock stage and the cycle transitions involved.
