# The Craft of Writing — HSC English Advanced (NESA 2024) Notes

The Craft of Writing is the Year 12 Module C of the HSC English Advanced syllabus. It is examined in HSC Paper 1 Section II. Students develop a portfolio of crafted writing in a range of forms for a range of audiences and purposes. The module is composition-focused and rewards a sustained writing habit across Year 12.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Module focus.** Composition in a range of forms (essay, speech, short story, narrative, reflection, feature article, blog post, script, poem). Craft means the deliberate shaping of language, structure and form for effect.
- **Outcomes.** Students compose texts in a range of forms for a range of audiences and purposes; refine their writing in response to feedback; reflect on their own writing process.
- **Assessment.** School-based tasks (portfolio of compositions). HSC Paper 1, Section II: composition under examination conditions.
- **No prescribed texts.** The module is about the student's own writing, not about analysing someone else's.

#### Examiner traps

- Treating composition as informal or low-stakes — composition in Section II is examined under timed conditions and carries a substantial mark share.
- Failing to plan before writing — the difference between a Band 5 and a Band 6 composition is often planning and drafting, not raw idea generation.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Composition forms

Imaginative: short story, scene, narrative, descriptive passage. Discursive: essay, reflection, opinion piece, column. Persuasive: speech, letter, open letter, manifesto, opinion editorial.

Each form has conventions. Short stories use scene-setting, dialogue, pacing and a turning point. Speeches use direct address, rhetorical questions, repetition and an emphatic close. Essays use a thesis, paragraph topic sentences and a conclusion.

#### The composition cycle

Plan: identify the form, audience and purpose. Outline the structure. Draft the composition in full. Edit for language features (diction, imagery, sentence rhythm). Edit for conventions (spelling, punctuation, grammar). Reflect on the process.

#### The reflection

The Craft of Writing requires reflection on the writing process. A strong reflection explains:
1. What the composer was trying to achieve (purpose, audience, form).
2. What choices the composer made (language features, structure).
3. How those choices shaped the composition.
4. What the composer would refine next time.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Why The Craft of Writing rewards a sustained writing habit

- **Composition improves with practice.** A student who writes one composition per fortnight across Year 12 will out-perform a student who writes one composition in Term 4.
- **The form matters.** Markers score form-specific conventions. A short story is judged by scene-setting, dialogue and pacing; a speech is judged by direct address and rhetoric.
- **The reflection is part of the grade.** A strong composition with no reflection scores lower than a strong composition with a strong reflection.

#### Year 12 planning pattern

- **Term 1.** Two compositions per fortnight in different forms. Peer review and self-assessment.
- **Term 2.** Composition + reflection tasks. Build a portfolio across forms.
- **Term 3.** Timed composition under examination conditions. Mark against the published HSC marking guidelines.
- **Term 4 (HSC).** Final revision. One composition per week in a different form.

#### HSC Paper 1 Section II strategy

In Section II, students are given several composition options (typically 3-5) and choose ONE. Read each option carefully. Identify the form and audience. Plan the structure before drafting. Allow 5 minutes for planning, 35 minutes for drafting, 5 minutes for editing.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "More adjectives = better writing." No — strong verbs and precise nouns do more work than clusters of adjectives.
- "Composition is about ideas." Composition is about the crafting of ideas through language. An excellent idea expressed in clumsy language scores lower than a modest idea expressed in precise language.
- "Longer = better." A focused 800-word composition beats a sprawling 1500-word composition. Stay within the recommended length.
