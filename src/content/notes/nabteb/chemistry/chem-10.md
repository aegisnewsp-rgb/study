# Alcohols and Carboxylic Acids

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Alcohols** are organic compounds with the hydroxyl group (–OH) bonded to a saturated carbon, general formula **R–OH**. They are classified as **primary (1°)**, **secondary (2°)**, or **tertiary (3°)** depending on how many carbon atoms are attached to the C–OH carbon.

**Carboxylic acids** contain the **carboxyl group (–COOH)**, general formula **R–COOH**. The –COOH group is a carbonyl (C=O) directly bonded to a hydroxyl (–OH), which gives these compounds their acidic character (pKa ≈ 4–5).

> High-yield tip: Carboxylic acids are roughly **10¹¹ times more acidic than alcohols** because the carboxylate anion (RCOO⁻) is resonance-stabilised across two oxygen atoms, while the alkoxide (RO⁻) is not.

- **Esterification**: RCOOH + R'OH ⇌ RCOOR' + H₂O (conc. H₂SO₄ catalyst)
- **Oxidation**: 1° alcohol → aldehyde → carboxylic acid; 2° alcohol → ketone; 3° resists oxidation
- **Test for –COOH**: effervescence with Na₂CO₃; CO₂ released turns limewater milky

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Functional Groups and Homologous Series

Both series belong to the **oxygen-containing organic family**. The first five straight-chain members are methanol/ethanoic, ethanol/propanoic, propanol/butanoic, butanol/pentanoic, and pentanol/hexanoic acid, each successive member differing by **–CH₂–** (molecular mass increase of 14).

| Property | Alcohols (R–OH) | Carboxylic Acids (R–COOH) |
|---|---|---|
| Functional group | Hydroxyl (–OH) on sp³ carbon | Carboxyl (–COOH) |
| General formula | CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH | CₙH₂ₙ₊₁COOH |
| Polarity | Polar (O–H dipole) | Polar (C=O + O–H) |
| Hydrogen bonding | Strong (O–H…O) | Strong, often as dimers |
| pKa range | 16–18 (very weak acids) | 4–5 (weak acids) |
| Boiling point | Higher than alkanes of similar mass | Highest among CₙH₂ₙ₊₁X isomers |

#### Classification and Oxidation

A **primary alcohol** (RCH₂OH) has one carbon on the C–OH; it oxidises first to an **aldehyde** (RCHO), then to a **carboxylic acid** (RCOOH) using acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ or KMnO₄. A **secondary alcohol** (R₂CHOH) oxidises only to a **ketone** (R₂C=O). A **tertiary alcohol** has no H on the carbinol carbon and **resists oxidation**, which is the standard chemical test for distinguishing the three classes.

#### Esterification (Fischer Reaction)

When a carboxylic acid and an alcohol are warmed with **concentrated H₂SO₄**, an **ester** and water form. The reaction is **reversible** — both the catalyst and removal of water drive the equilibrium toward the ester:

$$\text{CH}_3\text{COOH} + \text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} \xrightleftharpoons[\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4]{} \text{CH}_3\text{COOC}_2\text{H}_5 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$

#### Physical Properties and Hydrogen Bonding

Both families exhibit strong **intermolecular hydrogen bonding**, so their boiling points are much higher than alkanes of comparable molar mass. The first four members of each series are **miscible with water** because their –OH or –COOH groups hydrogen-bond to water; solubility drops sharply beyond C₅ as the non-polar hydrocarbon chain dominates.

#### Acidity and Salt Formation

Carboxylic acids react with **active metals** (Na, Mg), **alkalis** (NaOH → RCOONa + H₂O), and **carbonates** (Na₂CO₃ → 2RCOONa + H₂O + CO₂). The CO₂ test is the definitive qualitative test for the –COOH group.

> NABTEB pointer: roughly **8–12 marks per paper** come from naming, classification, balanced equations, and a short paragraph on fermentation or esterification.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Mechanism Outline: Acid-Catalysed Esterification

The carbonyl oxygen of the acid is **protonated** by H₂SO₄, increasing the electrophilicity of the carbonyl carbon. The alcohol oxygen then attacks as a **nucleophile**, forming a tetrahedral intermediate. A proton transfer expels water, and loss of a proton from the carbonyl oxygen yields the ester. Because every step is equilibrium-controlled, the reaction must be driven forward by **excess of one reactant** or by **removing water** (e.g., Dean–Stark trap).

#### Decarboxylation with Soda Lime

When the **sodium salt** of a carboxylic acid is heated with **soda lime (NaOH/CaO)**, the carboxyl group is expelled as Na₂CO₃ and a hydrogen from NaOH takes its place:

$$\text{RCOONa} + \text{NaOH} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{RH} + \text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$$

This reaction is the classical route for **descending a homologous series by one carbon** and often appears in NABTEB structured questions.

#### Reduction and Preparation Links

- **LiAlH₄** reduces RCOOH all the way to a **primary alcohol** (RCH₂OH), but NaBH₄ does not — a common MCQ trap.
- **Ethanol** is prepared industrially by **fermentation** of sugars using yeast (zymase enzyme) at 25–35 °C in anaerobic conditions:
  $$\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 \xrightarrow{\text{zymase}} 2\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} + 2\text{CO}_2$$
- **Ethanoic acid** is produced by oxidation of ethanol or by bacterial action ( Acetobacter) yielding **vinegar (~5% CH₃COOH)**.

#### Worked Example: Naming and Equation

**Question:** Name the compound CH₃CH₂CH₂OH and write a balanced equation for its complete oxidation to a carboxylic acid.

1. The –OH is on C1 of a three-carbon chain → **propan-1-ol** (a primary alcohol).
2. Under acidified K₂Cr₂O₇, it oxidises stepwise:
   - CH₃CH₂CH₂OH → CH₃CH₂CHO + H₂ (propanal)
   - CH₃CH₂CHO + ½O₂ → CH₃CH₂COOH (propanoic acid)
3. Overall: CH₃CH₂CH₂OH + 2[O] → CH₃CH₂COOH + H₂O

#### Common Mistakes and Exam Traps

- Writing the carboxyl formula as **–CO₂H** without brackets in equations — always clarify the structure with a drawn group.
- Assuming all **alcohols are soluble in water** — only those up to about C₄ are fully miscible.
- Forgetting that **esterification is reversible**; examiners deduct marks when the ⇌ sign and the H₂SO₄ catalyst are omitted.
- Confusing **dehydration** of ethanol (intramolecular, gives ethene + H₂O at ~170 °C) with **hydration** of ethene (addition of H₂O giving ethanol).

> Strategy: NABTEB rewards balanced equations with state symbols and reversible arrows. Memorise the **oxidation ladder** (alcohol → aldehyde → acid) and the **esterification equation template** — together they cover ~60% of marks on this topic.
