# Blood Relations

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Blood Relations** questions present a short family description (verbal statements or symbolic notation) and ask you to identify how two specific members are related. The two pillars are **gender fixing** and **generation counting**. Resolve every pronoun (*his/her*, *brother/sister*) before drawing the tree, then count generations vertically (parent = 1, grandparent = 2) and apply the **two-generation rule**: anyone on the same horizontal level as the central person is a sibling-class relation (sibling, cousin, spouse, in-law). The symbolic shorthand **A + B** means *A is the child of B*, **A − B** means *A is the husband/wife of B*, **A × B** means *A is the brother/sister of B*, and **A / B** means *A is the maternal uncle/aunt of B*. HAT-UG tests 1–3 such items using a single 4–6 statement family tree.

---

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Mechanism

A blood-relation puzzle is solved by converting every sentence into a **node-edge** structure on paper: each person becomes a node (♂ or ♀), and each sentence becomes an edge labelled *parent-of*, *spouse-of*, or *sibling-of*. Once the tree exists, the answer is read off by tracing paths and combining relationships along the path.

#### Symbolic Notation You Must Memorise

| Symbol | Reading | Example |
|---|---|---|
| A **+** B | A is the son/daughter of B | P + Q → P is Q's child |
| A **−** B | A is the husband/wife of B | P − Q → P is married to Q |
| A **×** B | A is the brother/sister of B | P × Q → P and Q are siblings |
| A **/** B | A is the maternal uncle/aunt of B | P / Q → P is mother's sibling of Q |

#### Gender-Fixing Procedure

Before relating anyone, scan the statements for explicit gender words: *mother, father, son, daughter, brother, sister, husband, wife*. If a person is only referenced as "A's brother" or "his sibling", use surrounding clues (e.g., "she has only one brother") to lock the gender. **Unfixed gender = wrong answer**, because "brother of my mother" (maternal uncle) and "sister of my mother" (maternal aunt) lead to different relations for downstream links.

#### Generation Rule

Vertical distance = generation gap. Parent sits one level above the central person, grandparent two levels up. Hence **grand + uncle/aunt ⇒ great-grand** adds one more level, and the words "great", "great-great" each add one extra generation. The horizontal level determines whether a relative is a *sibling-class* (brother, sister, cousin, brother-in-law, sister-in-law) or a *parent-class* (father, mother, uncle, aunt) or a *child-class* (son, daughter, nephew, niece).

#### Typical HAT-UG Pattern

You will usually see one paragraph naming 5–7 people with 4–6 relationship statements, followed by a question such as "How is X related to Y?" with four options. Read the full paragraph twice, draw the tree, then answer — do not rely on mental tracing beyond two generations.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases That Decide Marks

**In-law traps.** A *brother-in-law* can arise from (a) your spouse's brother, (b) your sibling's husband, or (c) your sister's husband — three structurally different paths. The tree must show a marriage node linking two families before the term is legal. HAT-UG option lists often include *cousin*, *uncle*, and *brother-in-law* together to test this.

**Maternal vs paternal.** "Uncle" alone is ambiguous; the lineage matters. An uncle reached through your father is your *paternal uncle*, reached through your mother is your *maternal uncle*. Cousins are the children of either kind of uncle/aunt and sit on your generation — they are sibling-class, not parent-class.

**Generation off-by-one.** "Great-grandfather" is three levels above you (father → grandfather → great-grandfather). "Grand-niece" is three levels below (you → your sibling → your niece → grand-niece). Mis-counting by even one level flips the answer between uncle/nephew or grandfather/grandson.

#### Mechanism Recap (Why the Tree Works)

Each statement fixes either a *vertical* relation (parent/child, generational step) or a *horizontal* relation (sibling/spouse, same level). Combining them: vertical + vertical + horizontal ⇒ compound label such as "maternal uncle's daughter" = cousin. The tree is the only representation that composes these without losing gender.

#### Common Mistakes

- Treating a spouse's sibling as a "cousin" because both are one step away on paper — they are an **in-law**, not blood.
- Reading **A − B** as "A is B's sibling" — the minus sign means **marriage**, not sibling.
- Stopping at "uncle" without checking which side of the family produces the uncle.

#### Practice Prompts

1. "A is the son of B. B is the sister of C. C is the mother of D." — *How is A related to D?*
2. "Pointing notation: P + Q, Q − R, R × S, S / T." — *Identify T's relation to P step by step using the table above.*
