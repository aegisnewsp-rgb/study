# Prepositions and Articles

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Prepositions** are short function words that link a noun or pronoun to another word, expressing relations of **time** (*at, on, in, since, for, by, until*), **place** (*at, on, in, above, between*), **direction** (*to, from, into, across*), and **manner/instrument** (*with, by, in*). **Articles** are a sub-class of determiners: the **indefinite articles** *a* and *an* introduce a non-specific singular countable noun; the **definite article** *the* marks a noun the listener can identify (unique, previously mentioned, or context-specific). A **zero article** applies to plural/uncountable nouns used generally (*Honesty is rare*).

High-yield pointers:
- *a* vs *an* depends on the **sound**, not the letter (*an hour, a university*).
- *since* + point in time; *for* + duration.
- *at* for points, *on* for surfaces/days, *in* for enclosed spaces/months/years.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Prepositions of Time
| Preposition | Usage | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **at** | precise clock time, festivals, night | *at 6 p.m., at night* |
| **on** | days, dates, specific mornings/afternoons | *on Monday, on 14 August* |
| **in** | months, years, seasons, centuries, morning/evening (longer spans) | *in 2025, in winter* |
| **since** | point in time (with perfect tenses) | *since 2019* |
| **for** | duration | *for three hours* |
| **by** | no later than a deadline | *by Friday* |
| **until / till** | continuous up to a point | *until 5 p.m.* |
| **during** | throughout a known period | *during the lecture* |

#### Prepositions of Place and Direction
*In* marks enclosed space (*in the box*); *at* marks a specific point (*at the door, at the bus stop*); *on* marks a surface (*on the wall*). Direction prepositions — *to, toward, from, into, onto, across, along, through* — show movement. Use **between** for two items and **among** for three or more.

#### Articles: Decision Rules
- **a** before consonant sounds; **an** before vowel sounds.
- **the** before: unique referents (*the sun, the President*), superlatives (*the tallest*), ordinals (*the first*), and nouns already introduced or made specific by context (*the book I bought*).
- **Zero article** before: proper names (*Karachi*), languages and subjects when used generally (*He studies Biology*), sports (*plays cricket*), meals (*have lunch*), and abstract uncountable nouns in general statements (*Knowledge is power*).
- With uncountable nouns, add *the* only when referring to a specific instance (*the water in this glass*).

#### Collocations
Many adjectives, verbs, and nouns demand fixed prepositions: *interested **in**, good **at**, dependent **on**, famous **for**, reason **for**, arrive **at**, believe **in**, consist **of**, differ **from**.* HAT-UG tests these as fill-in-the-blank items.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Sound-Based Article Rule and Its Traps
The *a/an* choice is **phonetic**, not orthographic. Common traps involve silent *h* (*an hour, an honest man*), *u* pronounced as /juː/ (*a university, a European*), consonant digraphs (*a one-rupee note*), and abbreviations (*an MBA, an FIR*). Exam questions often pair two options in the same blank to catch spell-based guesses.

#### Edge Cases in Preposition Choice
- *In the picture* vs *on the picture*: **in** = depicted within; **on** = printed/superimposed on its surface.
- *By car / by bus* but *on foot* and *on a bicycle* — instrument prepositions are idiomatic, not logical.
- *Arrive at* (small place/event), *arrive in* (city/country), *arrive at the airport* but *arrive at the station* — collocations override general rules.
- *Since* takes a **point** (since Monday, since 2010) and pairs with perfect tenses; *for* takes a **period** (for five years) and can pair with any tense.
- *Between* strictly with two; *among* with three or more — HAT-UG includes sentences like *“The prize was shared ___ the three brothers.”*

#### Worked Example
> Choose the correct option: *She has been working here ___ 2019, and she is known ___ her honesty ___ the staff.*
>
> **Solution:** *since 2019* (point in time + present perfect); *for* (reason/cause → *known for*); *among* (more than two people → *among the staff*). Final: *She has been working here **since** 2019, and she is known **for** her honesty **among** the staff.*

#### Common Mistakes
1. Writing *an university* (sound-based, not spelling).
2. *He is best player* — missing *the* before superlative.
3. *During Monday* — *during* needs a period, not a day.
4. *Interested of music* — wrong fixed preposition after adjective.
5. *Between the students* (more than two) — should be *among*.

#### Practice Prompts
1. Fill the blanks: *There is ___ apple on ___ table. Let’s meet ___ 5 p.m. ___ Monday.*
2. Correct the errors: *She is good in maths and she reached to the station since morning.*
