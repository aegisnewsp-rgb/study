# Sentence Correction

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Sentence Correction in CUET UG English presents a single sentence with one underlined portion and four replacement options; only one option removes every grammatical, syntactic, or idiomatic error. The section rewards rule-based reading, not intuition.

- **Core rule set to memorise:** Subject-Verb Agreement, Tense Consistency, Parallelism, Modifier Placement, Pronoun-Antecedent Agreement, Conditional forms, Idiomatic Prepositions.
- **High-yield formula:** Coordinating conjunctions (*and*, *but*, *or*, *nor*, *yet*, *so*) link elements of identical grammatical form. Mismatched forms = parallelism error.
- **Quick trick:** Read each replacement silently and ask — (1) Does the verb agree with the subject? (2) Are tenses consistent? (3) Does any modifier sit next to the noun it modifies?

| Error Type | One-line Trigger |
|---|---|
| S-V Agreement | Singular subject + plural verb, or vice versa |
| Tense | Time-shift without logical reason |
| Parallelism | *and/or* joining gerund + infinitive |
| Modifier | -ing phrase far from its noun |
| Preposition | Verb/adjective with wrong fixed preposition |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### How the Question Works

Each item shows a sentence with a segment lettered A–D. You must pick the option that, when substituted, makes the sentence grammatically correct, logically coherent, and stylistically tight. Three distractors usually fix one surface flaw while introducing another, so quick rule-checking beats gut-feel.

#### The Five Rules That Cover ~70% of Items

#### Subject-Verb Agreement
The verb agrees with its **true subject**, not the nearest noun. Phrases like *along with*, *as well as*, *together with*, *in addition to*, and *accompanied by* do **not** change number. Collective nouns (*team*, *jury*) take singular verbs in British usage and singular verbs in Indian exam contexts when the group acts as one unit.

#### Tense Consistency
A sentence should stay in one tense unless a time-shift is logical. Watch for past-perfect cues (*by 2015, she had finished*) that demand *had + past participle* in the dependent clause.

#### Parallelism
Elements joined by *and*, *or*, *but*, *not only…but also*, *either…or* must share form: noun-noun, gerund-gerund, infinitive-infinitive. Mixed forms are the most-tested parallelism trap.

#### Modifier Placement
A participial phrase (*Walking down the street, the trees…*) must modify the grammatical subject. If it modifies something else, the sentence is illogical.

#### Pronoun-Antecedent Agreement
A pronoun must match its antecedent in number, gender, and person. Ambiguous *they/it/this* references are flagged.

#### Conditionals Cheat-Sheet

| Type | If-clause | Main clause | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Simple present | Simple present | Universal truth |
| 1 | Simple present | will + base verb | Real/probable future |
| 2 | Simple past | would + base verb | Unreal present |
| 3 | Past perfect | would have + V3 | Unreal past |

#### Typical Exam Patterns
- **Spot-the-error style:** underline the segment containing the flaw.
- **Replace-the-underlined-part:** four options, one correct.
- **No-change option:** sometimes answer (D) keeps the original.

> Exam tip: Eliminate options that introduce a new error before declaring the surviving one correct. Two wrong reasons disqualify an option even if it fixes the visible error.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases Worth Memorising

#### Voice and Emphasis
Active voice (*The committee released the report*) is preferred when the doer matters. Passive (*The report was released*) is correct only when the doer is unknown, obvious, or deliberately de-emphasised. CUET items often test unneeded passives that obscure the agent.

#### Direct vs Indirect Speech
Tense back-shifting applies only when the reporting verb is past. Present-tense reporting verbs (*He says that…*) keep the reported tense unchanged. Questions in reported speech require statement word order, not inverted order.

#### Idiomatic Prepositions
Verbs and adjectives carry fixed prepositions that must be memorised in pairs: *interested in*, *fond of*, *capable of*, *absorbed in*, *convinced of*, *anxious about*, *keen on*, *indifferent to*. Logical-sounding alternatives such as *interested for* or *capable to* are common distractors.

#### Redundancy and Wordiness
Watch for double negatives, tautologies (*free gift*, *past history*), and pleonasms (*each and every*, *basic fundamentals*). Stylistic items prefer concise phrasing without losing meaning.

#### Connections to Adjacent Topics
Sentence Correction overlaps with **Reading Comprehension** (inference phrasing), **Verbal Ability** (idioms, phrasal verbs), and **Rearrangement** (logical connectors). Mastering parallel structure here directly improves jumbled-sentence accuracy.

#### Common Examiner Traps

- Choosing the option that *sounds* formal but breaks agreement.
- Fixing a tense error while introducing a parallelism error.
- Treating *data*, *media*, *criteria* as singular in CUET (exams here prefer these plurals as nouns referring to items).
- Reading only the underlined segment instead of the whole sentence.

#### Practice Prompts

1. **Rewrite correctly:** *Neither the principal nor the teachers was present at the meeting.* — Fix S-V agreement and explain the rule in one sentence.
2. **Identify the error class:** *Having finished the work, the television was switched off.* — Name the error, justify the placement rule, and supply the corrected sentence.

> Strategy note: Allocate 60–90 seconds per item. With 40 English questions across roughly 45 minutes, Sentence Correction items should take no more than 45 seconds each after the first pass; mark and revisit only those where two options survive rule-checks.
