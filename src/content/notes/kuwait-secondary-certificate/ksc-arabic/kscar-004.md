# Spelling (Imla') and Writing Mechanics — Kuwait Secondary Certificate Arabic Notes

Spelling (Imla' / الإملاء) and writing mechanics is the fourth terminal-level domain on the Kuwait MOE Arabic curriculum. At terminal level, the curriculum covers hamza rules, taa marbuta, alif maqsura, alif layyina, sukun and shadda, plus paragraph punctuation and orthographic conventions used in formal Arabic writing.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Hamza (همزة):** the glottal stop. Written on alif (أ), waw (ؤ), ya (ئ), or alif with hamza above/below (إ / أ) depending on the diacritic and position. Rules: at the start of a word, hamza sits on alif (أ); after a ya-sound (i / ii), on ya (ئ); after a waw-sound (u / uu), on waw (ؤ); after an alif-sound (aa), on alif alone (أ).
- **Taa marbuta (ة):** the "bound taa" at the end of a noun; written in pause but becomes taa (ت) in idafa and when the word is the first part of a construct.
- **Alif maqsura (ى):** an alif without hamza at the end of a word, pronounced "aa"; often confused with ya (ي).
- **Alif layyina (ا):** a regular alif at the end of a verb of the form fa'ala (e.g. دعا, رمى — but the latter is alif maqsura).
- **Sukun (ْ):** absence of a vowel.
- **Shadda (ّ):** doubling of a consonant.
- **Punctuation:** Arabic uses ، (comma) ; (semicolon) : (colon) . (full stop) ? (question mark) ! (exclamation).

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing alif maqsura (ى) with ya (ي) — alif maqsura is pronounced "aa"; ya is "ee".
- Writing taa marbuta where taa maftuha is required (e.g. "بنت" is open taa, "بنات" is open taa + plural suffix).
- Wrong hamza carrier after a ya or waw sound.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Hamza rules in detail

The hamza has three positions: at the start of a word, in the middle, and at the end.

**At the start:** hamza always sits on alif (أ). Examples: "أحمد" (Ahmad), "إبراهيم" (Ibrahim).

**In the middle:** hamza carrier depends on the vowel that precedes it and the diacritic of the hamza itself:

| Preceding vowel | Hamza diacritic | Carrier |
|-----------------|-----------------|---------|
| fatha (a) | fatha | alif (أ) |
| fatha | damma | waw (ؤ) |
| fatha | kasra | ya (ئ) |
| damma (u) | any | waw (ؤ) |
| kasra (i) | any | ya (ئ) |
| sukun | any | alif (أ) |

Examples: "سأل" (sa'ala, hamza on alif because of preceding fatha + hamza fatha); "مؤمن" (mu'min, hamza on waw because of preceding damma); "بئر" (bi'r, hamza on ya because of preceding kasra).

**At the end:** hamza carrier depends on the preceding vowel. Final-hamza rules:

- Preceding fatha → alif (أ): "بدأ"
- Preceding damma → waw (ؤ): "يؤوب"
- Preceding kasra → ya (ئ): "قارئ"

If the preceding letter is a long vowel (aa, uu, ii), the hamza is written on the long-vowel letter.

#### Taa marbuta vs taa maftuha

- Taa marbuta (ة) ends a noun; pronounced "a" in pause, "at" in construct.
- Taa maftuha (ت) ends a verb or broken plural; pronounced "t" always.

Examples: "مدرسة" (school, taa marbuta) → "مدارس" (schools, taa maftuha + plural). "بنات" (girls, taa maftuha + plural suffix).

When a taa-marbuta noun becomes the second part of an idafa, the taa becomes taa maftuha: "مدرسة" → "مديرة المدرسة" (the school's principal — but in construct, the taa is not pronounced "a").

#### Alif maqsura vs ya

- Alif maqsura (ى): pronounced "aa", no dots.
- Ya (ي): pronounced "ee" or consonant, has two dots underneath.

Common words: "هدى" (huda, guidance), "موسى" (Musa), "عيسى" (Isa), "متى" (mata, when — ends in alif maqsura).

When in doubt, check the dictionary. Terminal items often test the distinction.

#### Sukun and shadda

- Sukun (ْ): no vowel on a consonant. Common in conjunction (ال) where the lam carries sukun.
- Shadda (ّ): doubled consonant, written as the consonant with a small "w" above. Common in idafa, definite articles, and verb conjugations.

#### Paragraph punctuation

Arabic uses Western-style punctuation but with Arabic-comma (،) instead of comma (,) in formal writing. Quotation marks are » « for nested quotes and " " for outer quotes (in many conventions). The paragraph break is signalled by a full indentation on the new line.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where Spelling (Imla') and Writing Mechanics appears across the terminal Arabic exam

- **Dictation items:** the terminal exam usually includes a dictation passage testing hamza, taa marbuta, alif maqsura, and shadda.
- **Spot-the-error items:** identify the misspelled word in a sentence.
- **Composition items:** the writing section tests correct spelling and punctuation throughout.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Alif maqsura is interchangeable with ya." No — they differ in pronunciation and in some grammatical functions.
- "Hamza on alif is always wrong in the middle of a word." No — when preceded by fatha + hamza-fatha, the middle hamza sits on alif: "سأل."
- "Taa marbuta is always pronounced." It is pronounced "a" in pause but becomes "at" in construct.

#### Exam technique

- For hamza items, identify the preceding vowel first, then apply the carrier rule.
- For taa items, decide whether the word is a noun (taa marbuta likely) or a verb (taa maftuha likely).
- For alif maqsura items, check the pronunciation: "aa" = alif maqsura, "ee" = ya.
