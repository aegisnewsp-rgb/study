# Modern Indian History and Freedom Struggle

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> One fact, one chain, one recall hook — what every CTET Paper-II attempt needs on this 3%-weight slot.

The freedom struggle is the 1757–1947 arc from Plassey to Partition, with Gandhian mass movements (1920–1942) doing the heaviest lifting in the question paper.

- **Anchors you must hold:** 1857 Revolt → INC 1885 → Partition of Bengal 1905 → Jallianwala Bagh 1919 → Non-Cooperation 1920–22 → Civil Disobedience 1930 (Salt March, 12 March, Dandi) → Quit India 8 August 1942 → 15 August 1947.
- **Acts in exam-favourite order:** Regulating 1773, Pitt's India 1784, GOI 1858 (Crown rule begins), Morley-Minto 1909, Montagu-Chelmsford 1919, GOI 1935, Indian Independence Act 18 July 1947.
- **Founder trap:** A.O. Hume founded INC (1885); W.C. Bonnerjee only presided over the first session.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"P**artition of **B**engal 1905 lit the **S**wadeshi **B**oycott flame" → use the initials **PBSB** to recall the post-1905 Extremist surge under **L**al-Bal-Pal (**L**ajpat Rai, **B**ipin Chandra Pal, **T**ilak). Linking the event (B) to the trio (L-B-P-T) stops the Moderates/Extremists date swap that trips most CTET candidates.

| Slot | Quick recall |
|---|---|
| First INC President | W.C. Bonnerjee (1885, Bombay) |
| 1857 trigger | Greased cartridges + Mangal Pandey, Meerut |
| Quit India resolution | 8 Aug 1942, Bombay session |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Build the structure, not just the dates — CTET assertion-reason items reward cause-effect clarity, not rote.

#### Phase-by-Phase Structure of the National Movement

The Congress did not behave the same way across decades; CTET frames options around which phase used which method.

| Phase | Years | Method | Leaders | Signature event |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Moderate | 1885–1905 | Prayers, petitions | Dadabhai Naoroji, Gopal Krishna Gokhale | Economic critiques, *Drain Theory* |
| Extremist | 1905–1919 | Boycott, Swadeshi, national education | Tilak, Pal, Lajpat Rai, Aurobindo | Partition of Bengal (1905) → annulment (1911) |
| Gandhian | 1919–1947 | Satyagraha, mass mobilisation | Gandhi, Nehru, Patel, Maulana Azad | Champaran, Dandi, Quit India |
| Revolutionary | Always parallel | Armed action, HSRA, INA | Bhagat Singh, Azad, Bose | Lahore Conspiracy (1929), INA (1943) |

#### Concept Comparison: Movements vs Acts vs Years

CTET loves to swap dates or shuffle leaders across rows. Hold this matrix.

| Item | Year | Pair it with | Do NOT confuse with |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rowlatt Act → Jallianwala Bagh | 1919 | General Dyer, Amritsar | Chauri Chaura (1922) |
| Non-Cooperation Movement | 1920–22 | Khilafat merger | Civil Disobedience (1930) |

| Indian Independence Act | 18 Jul 1947 | Enacted by UK Parliament | Actual Independence, 15 Aug 1947 |

#### Worked CTET-Style Resolution

A typical CTET-II item reads: *"Quit India Movement was launched in 1940 by the Indian National Congress demanding immediate British withdrawal."* Two assertions, one cause-effect — and both halves are wrong.

1. **Resolve the year:** Quit India was launched on **8 August 1942** at the Bombay session of the INC. The 1940 offer was the **August Offer** (Viceroy Linlithgow), not Quit India.
2. **Resolve the demand:** Quit India's stated demand was *Purna Swaraj*-style immediate British withdrawal — accurate in spirit. But the *1940* date breaks the cause-effect, so the whole item must be marked false.
3. **Map the alternative pair:** 1942 → Cripps Mission (March 1942) preceded Quit India; both failed because Cripps refused to guarantee control of Defence.
4. **Pick the correct match:** "8 August 1942 + Bombay + immediate withdrawal" → **True**. "1940 + Quit India" → **False**.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Candidates see "Quit India" and write 1942, then auto-mark the item true. CTET slips in the wrong *year* (1940, 1939, 1944) while keeping the slogan correct. Train yourself to verify **both** the year *and* the demand before deciding — single-clause checks cost marks.

#### Socio-Religious Reform: Why It Sits in This Note

Reform movements predate Congress and supply answer options for "causes" sub-questions.

- **Brahmo Samaj (1828, Ram Mohan Roy)** — monotheism, opposed Sati; led to **Sati Abolition 1829** under Bentinck.
- **Arya Samaj (1875, Dayanand Saraswati)** — *Back to the Vedas*, Shuddhi, gave Gandhi the term *Satyagraha* influence.
- **Prarthana Samaj (1867, M.G. Ranade)** — Maharashtra counterpart to Brahmo.
- **Widow Remarriage Act 1856**, **Age of Consent 1891**, **Sarda Act 1929** — pen the chronology; CTET links them to Tilak's opposition (Age of Consent) versus Gandhi's support (Sarda).

> 📌 **Formula Check:** Reform movements + 1857 Revolt = the **ideological foundation** of Indian nationalism. Without the first, the second becomes a military mutiny rather than a "first war of independence" narrative — the *framing* itself is a CTET assertion-reason favourite.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Edge cases, link-points, and the two prompts that decide between 95 and 110 in CTET-II.

#### Edge-Case Traps Candidates Lose Marks On

| Trap | What students write | What the paper expects |
|---|---|---|
| 1857 = "planned war of independence" | Accepted without question | *Contested* — S.N. Sen and others call it a sepoy mutiny spread regionally; V.D. Savarkar popularised the "First War" label. Pick "V.D. Savarkar used the phrase" in assertion-reason. |
| Partition of Bengal motives | Religious partition | **Administrative** partition by Lord Curzon, reversed in **1911** (annulment at Delhi Durbar), not "cancelled by Swadeshi alone". |
| Bhagat Singh's party | Congress | **Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA)**; executed 23 March 1931 with Rajguru and Sukhdev. |
| Mountbatten's role | Granted freedom | Last Viceroy, announced **3 June 1947** plan, Radcliffe Line drawn in haste, Gandhi opposed partition. |
| Integration of princely states | Nehru-led | **Sardar Patel + V.P. Menon**; Hyderabad (Operation Polo, 1948), Junagadh plebiscite are the case studies. |
| Two-Nation Theory | Hindu-Muslim animosity in general | Specifically **Jinnah's** constitutional argument that Muslims are a separate nation demanding a separate state — formalised in the **Lahore Resolution, March 1940**. |
| Indian Independence Act date | 15 August 1947 | **Act passed 18 July 1947**; independence *commenced* 15 August. CTET asks exactly this difference. |

#### Link-Points to Adjacent CTET Topics

- **Indian Constitution** (separate topic, same Social Studies paper) draws directly from the 1935 GOI Act's federal skeleton and the Cabinet Mission's Constituent Assembly scheme.
- **Fundamental Rights** trace to the freedoms the freedom movement demanded — civil liberties (Rowlatt opposition) → Article 19/22.
- **Federalism** as a Polity concept gets its Indian grammar from the **Princely States integration** and the 1935 Act's provincial autonomy clauses.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. *Assertion:* "The Doctrine of Lapse was a subsidy instrument of the British." *Reason:* "It was used by Lord Wellesley to extract financial control over Indian states." Evaluate both. (Expected: Assertion false — Doctrine of Lapse is **Dalhousie**, annexation by lapse of natural heir; Reason false — subsidiary alliance is Wellesley's, lapse is Dalhousie's. Item false.)
2. *Match the following:* (a) Cabinet Mission, (b) Cripps Mission, (c) Mountbatten Plan, (d) August Offer — with (i) Rejected Pakistan but proposed a United India with provincial grouping (1946), (ii) Offered Dominion status after the war (1942), (iii) Announced partition and a boundary commission (1947), (iv) Viceroy's promise of expansion of Viceroy's Executive Council (1940). Answers: a-i, b-ii, c-iii, d-iv.

#### Exam Strategy for This 3% Slot

CTET-II Social Studies sets 1–2 direct questions and 1–2 assertion-reason items from this block. The yield-per-hour is highest on **Acts-and-years + Movement-and-leader pairs**; practise these as flashcards. Skip radical historiography debates — CTET follows NCERT Class 8 and Class 12 framing. Spend the saved minutes on Polity and Geography, which carry more weight in the same paper.

> 📌 **Boundary Condition:** If a question offers both 15 August 1947 and 18 July 1947, the discriminating answer is **18 July 1947 for the Act, 15 August 1947 for the event** — CTET papers routinely split this to test literal reading.
