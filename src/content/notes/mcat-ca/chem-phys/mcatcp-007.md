# Acid-Base Chemistry and Electrochemistry — MCAT Chem/Phys

Acid-base chemistry and electrochemistry form a heavily tested cluster of AAMC Foundational Concept 5 in the Chem/Phys section. The MCAT tests Brønsted-Lowry acids and bases, pKa, buffers, titration curves, galvanic and electrolytic cells, standard reduction potentials, the Nernst equation, and electrochemical gradients across membranes. These topics bridge to biochemistry (protein charge, enzyme pH optima) and physiology (nerve signalling, kidney acid-base balance).

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### Core formulas

- **pH and pOH:** pH = -log[H⁺]; pOH = -log[OH⁻]; pH + pOH = 14.
- **Ka and Kb:** Ka × Kb = Kw = 10⁻¹⁴ for a conjugate acid-base pair at 25°C.
- **pKa:** pKa = -log(Ka). Lower pKa = stronger acid.
- **Henderson-Hasselbalch:** pH = pKa + log([A⁻]/[HA]).
- **Standard cell potential:** E°_cell = E°_cathode - E°_anode.
- **Nernst equation:** E = E° - (RT / nF) × ln(Q). At 25°C, E = E° - (0.0592 / n) × log(Q).
- **Gibbs free energy from cell potential:** ΔG° = -n × F × E°_cell.

#### One-pass drill

1. Identify the topic: acid-base, buffer, titration, or electrochemistry.
2. List the known quantities.
3. Pick the formula; solve algebraically.
4. For electrochemistry, identify the cathode (reduction) and anode (oxidation) first.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing strong and weak acids. Strong acids fully dissociate; weak acids do not.
- Mixing up Ka and pKa. Ka is the equilibrium constant; pKa = -log(Ka).
- Forgetting that the cathode is where reduction happens, the anode is where oxidation happens.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Acid-base chemistry

The MCAT tests:

- **Brønsted-Lowry acids and bases:** acids donate protons; bases accept protons. The conjugate base of HA is A⁻; the conjugate acid of B is BH⁺.
- **Strong versus weak acids:** strong acids (HCl, HBr, HI, HNO₃, HClO₄, H₂SO₄) fully dissociate; weak acids have Ka < 1.
- **Buffers:** a weak acid and its conjugate base resist changes in pH. The buffer region is centred at pH = pKa. Henderson-Hasselbalch gives the pH of a buffer.
- **Titration curves:** plot pH against volume of titrant. Strong acid-strong base titration has pH 7 at the equivalence point; weak acid-strong base has pH > 7.

#### Electrochemistry

**Galvanic (voltaic) cells:** spontaneous redox reaction; ΔG < 0; the cell does work. The MCAT tests galvanic cells in the context of biological redox (NAD⁺/NADH, FAD/FADH₂).

**Electrolytic cells:** non-spontaneous redox reaction; ΔG > 0; an external power source drives the reaction. The MCAT tests electrolytic cells in the context of nerve conduction and electrochemical gradients.

**Standard reduction potentials:** tabulated in the AAMC reference. The MCAT provides a table of standard reduction potentials on test day. Use the table to compute E°_cell = E°_cathode - E°_anode.

**Nernst equation:** relates cell potential to concentrations. The MCAT uses the Nernst equation for cell potentials under non-standard conditions, including the resting membrane potential.

#### Practice routine

- Take 5 acid-base items. Compute pH from [H⁺]; compute pOH from pH.
- Take 5 buffer items. Use Henderson-Hasselbalch to compute pH.
- Take 5 titration items. Identify the equivalence point and the buffer region.
- Take 5 electrochemistry items. Compute E°_cell from a table of standard reduction potentials.
- Take 5 Nernst items. Compute E at non-standard concentrations.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How this topic connects to the other Chem/Phys topics

- **Kinetics and thermodynamics** connect through ΔG° = -RT × ln(K_eq) and ΔG° = -n × F × E°_cell.
- **Phases and solutions** connect through solubility of ionic compounds and through ionic strength effects on equilibria.
- **Biochemistry** uses pKa to explain enzyme pH optima, protein charge, and buffer systems (bicarbonate, phosphate, protein).

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"Strong acids have high pKa."** No — strong acids have low pKa (or negative pKa).
- **"The cathode is always the positive electrode."** In galvanic cells, the cathode is positive. In electrolytic cells, the cathode is negative (connected to the negative terminal of the power source).
- **"E°_cell = E°_cathode + E°_anode."** No — E°_cell = E°_cathode - E°_anode. Use the table as published; do not flip the sign of the anode reaction.

#### Exam technique

- For acid-base items, identify the acid or base strength first; then pick the formula.
- For buffer items, use Henderson-Hasselbalch. The buffer works best within ±1 pH unit of pKa.
- For electrochemistry items, identify the cathode and anode by location of reduction and oxidation. Use the AAMC-provided table for standard reduction potentials.
- For Nernst items, use R = 8.314 J/(mol·K), F = 96,485 C/mol, T in K.
- Use the platform's calculator.
