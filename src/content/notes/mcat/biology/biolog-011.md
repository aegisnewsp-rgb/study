# Inheritance — MCAT Pakistan

Inheritance is the highest-weight unit in the PMDC Biology syllabus: under the MDCAT pattern of 81 Biology MCQs (≈45 % of the 180-question paper; PMDC 2026 Public Notice), the genetics block — Mendelian ratios, molecular genetics, sex-linked inheritance, and pedigree reading — produces the densest single-topic return per hour of study. ### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

**What to lock in within the first hour.**
- **Mendel's laws.** Law of Segregation: each gamete receives one allele per gene. Law of Independent Assortment: alleles of different genes assort independently during gamete formation — *except* for genes on the same chromosome (linkage) or *X*-linked genes.
- **Cross vocabulary.** Monohybrid = one trait (F₂ ratio 3 : 1, genotypic 1 : 2 : 1). Dihybrid = two traits (F₂ ratio 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 when unlinked). Test cross = cross with homozygous recessive to expose hidden recessives.
- **Sex linkage.** *X*-linked recessive traits (haemophilia, red–green colour blindness, Duchenne muscular dystrophy) appear far more often in males (XᵃY) than females. A carrier mother (XᴬXᵃ) passes the allele to 50 % of sons.
- **Molecular genetics triplet.** Replication (semi-conservative, Meselson–Stahl), transcription (DNA → mRNA, 5′→3′), translation (mRNA codon → tRNA anticodon → amino acid on a ribosome).
- **Mutation types.** Point (silent, missense, nonsense), frameshift (insertion/deletion not in multiples of three), chromosomal (deletion, duplication, inversion, translocation). Sickle-cell anaemia is the textbook missense example.

**One-line recap before walking in.** Mendel's ratios → molecular mechanism → mutation as the source of variation → pedigree reading.

### 🟡 Standard — Exam Prep (3d-3w)

#### Mendelian inheritance and crosses
Every Mendelian cross question reduces to (1) write the parental genotypes, (2) build the gamete set, (3) combine in a Punnett square, (4) read off the ratio. For an F₁ monohybrid cross Aa × Aa, expect 1 AA : 2 Aa : 1 aa (3 : 1 phenotypic). Codominance (AB blood groups) and incomplete dominance (snapdragon pink from red × white) are common traps — neither obeys the simple 3 : 1.

A **test cross** (Aa × aa) is the cleanest pedigree probe: a 1 : 1 phenotypic ratio proves the parent was heterozygous; a uniform phenotype proves it was homozygous dominant. Use this whenever the question gives you an F₁ phenotype with unknown genotype.

**Worked example.** Two tall pea plants are crossed and produce 3 tall : 1 dwarf offspring. What is the probability that the next offspring is dwarf? Treat the parents as Tt × Tt → 1/4 dwarf; the previous ratio is irrelevant once the parental genotypes are fixed. Answer: **0.25**.

#### Dihybrid, linkage, and epistasis
For two unlinked genes (AaBb × AaBb) the F₂ ratio is 9 : 3 : 3 : 1. If the genes are linked on the same chromosome, expect parental-type gametes (AB, ab) to dominate over recombinants (Ab, aB); the recombination frequency in a test cross equals the map distance in cM (1 % recombination = 1 cM). **Epistasis** (one gene masking another) flips the ratio: recessive epistasis (e.g., *aa* masks B/b) gives 9 : 3 : 4; dominant epistasis gives 12 : 3 : 1. Memorise these two — they appear in nearly every PMDC paper.

#### Molecular genetics
**DNA replication** is semi-conservative. Helicase unwinds the double helix; DNA polymerase synthesises the new strand 5′→3′; the leading strand is continuous, the lagging strand is Okazaki fragments joined by DNA ligase. Prokaryotes have one origin of replication; eukaryotes have many.

**Transcription** produces a primary mRNA from the template (antisense) strand. RNA polymerase adds ribonucleotides 5′→3′; in eukaryotes the pre-mRNA is capped (5′ cap), polyadenylated (3′ poly-A tail), and introns are spliced out. Translation reads mRNA codons (5′→3′) on the ribosome's A, P, and E sites; tRNAs deliver amino acids specified by their anticodon (3′→5′ pairing with the codon).

**Worked example.** A template strand reads 3′-TAC GGA CAT-5′. The mRNA is 5′-AUG CCU GUA-3′, which translates to Met–Pro–Val. A single-base deletion in the second codon shifts the frame from that point onward — the *third* amino acid changes even though only one nucleotide is removed.

#### Sex-linked and sex-influenced inheritance
*X*-linked recessive: skip a generation through carrier females. Pattern: affected males → carrier daughters → affected grandsons. *Y*-linked ( holandric): father → all sons only. Sex-influenced traits (pattern baldness) behave as autosomal in one sex and dominant/recessive in the other — read the question stem carefully.

#### Mutations and chromosomal disorders
A **missense** mutation swaps one amino acid (sickle-cell: GAG → GTG, Glu → Val at position 6 of β-globin). A **nonsense** mutation introduces a premature stop codon (thalassaemias). Aneuploidies from non-disjunction: Turner (45, X0), Klinefelter (47, XXY), Down syndrome (trisomy 21). Polyploidy is tolerated in plants, lethal in humans.

#### Population genetics
Hardy–Weinberg: $p^2 + 2pq + q^2 = 1$, where $p$ = dominant allele frequency, $q$ = recessive. Solve for carrier frequency (2pq) once an allele frequency is given. Conditions: no mutation, no selection, no migration, large population, random mating — say so in any short-answer follow-up.

#### Worked practice set
1. A woman with normal vision (carrier XᴴXʰ) marries a colour-blind man (XʰY). What fraction of daughters will be colour blind? — **50 %** (XʰXʰ from carrier mother × affected father).
2. A monohybrid cross of Tt × tt yields 50 % tall : 50 % dwarf. What cross would prove the tall parent was heterozygous? — It already is; the 1 : 1 ratio is the proof.
3. mRNA codon 5′-UAA-3′. What does it code for? — **Stop** (ochre). Do not pick an amino acid.

### 🔴 Deep — Mastery (1mo+)

#### Pedigree analysis and probability stacking
For a pedigree, apply the rules in this order: (1) determine dominance/recessiveness from the pattern (skip generations = recessive, every generation = dominant); (2) test sex-linkage (X-linked recessive skips through carrier mothers); (3) write every genotype that is consistent; (4) compute the requested probability using product and sum rules, multiplying for independent events and adding for mutually exclusive ones. Watch for consanguineous matings — they raise the chance of autosomal recessive disease by exposing rare alleles.

#### Gene regulation and the lac operon
In the *E. coli* **lac operon**, the repressor (product of the *lacI* gene) binds the operator and blocks transcription when lactose is absent. Lactose (or allolactose) inactivates the repressor; cAMP–CAP, made when glucose is low, then binds the promoter and *switches on* transcription. The operon is therefore *inducible* and subject to catabolite repression. Translate this directly: "low glucose + lactose present" = full transcription; "high glucose + lactose present" = basal transcription only.

#### Modern extensions
PCR amplifies a DNA segment using a heat-stable Taq polymerase, primers flanking the region of interest, and 30+ thermal cycles of denature → anneal → extend. Gel electrophoresis separates fragments by size; smaller fragments migrate farther in an agarose gel. CRISPR-Cas9 is the dominant site-specific nuclease for genome editing — the guide RNA directs Cas9 to a matching 20-nt target adjacent to an NGG PAM. Use these three technologies together when a question links a disease gene to a treatment pipeline.

#### Connections to other units
Pair this chapter with **Cell Structure** (mitosis vs meiosis: M I halves the chromosome number, M II separates sister chromatids; non-disjunction in either causes aneuploidy), with **Biotechnology** (recombinant DNA, vectors, GMOs), and with **Evolution** (Hardy–Weinberg deviations detect selection; sickle-cell heterozygote advantage against malaria is a standard example).

## Common traps

1. **Reading "Tt × Tt" and writing 3 : 1 without checking for codominance.** Blood-group problems (IᴬIᴮ × IᴬIᴮ → 1 IᴬIᴬ : 2 IᴬIᴮ : 1 IᴬIᴮ) look like 3 : 1 but the phenotype split is 1 : 2 : 1. If both phenotypes appear in the parents, check the inheritance pattern before defaulting.
2. **Assuming independent assortment always.** Two genes on the same chromosome do not assort independently; you will see a deformed 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 with too many parental types. Recombination frequency < 50 % = linkage.
3. **Confusing the template strand with the mRNA.** mRNA is built *antiparallel* and *complementary* to the template; it has the same sequence as the coding strand (except U replaces T). A question asking "what is the mRNA?" requires reading the template 3′→5′ and writing the mRNA 5′→3′.
4. **Treating sex-linked dominant and recessive as the same.** X-linked dominant affects *both* sexes equally and is passed father to *all* daughters (none to sons). X-linked recessive skips generations and is overwhelmingly male. Read the pedigree before assigning a probability.
5. **Forgetting that Hardy–Weinberg allele frequency is the *allele*, not the phenotype frequency.** If 1 % of a population shows an autosomal recessive disease, q² = 0.01, q = 0.1, so the carrier frequency is 2pq ≈ 2 × 0.9 × 0.1 = **0.18**, not 0.01.
