# One Word Substitution

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**One Word Substitution** replaces a descriptive phrase with a single precise word (e.g., "a person who knows many languages" = **polyglot**). MDCAT tests this under English Verbal, usually as MCQs where a phrase is given and the closest single-word replacement must be selected.

Must-know **affixes** for decoding:
- **Number prefixes:** *mono-, bi-, tri-* (monolingual, biennial, triennial).
- **Negative prefixes:** *in-, un-, im-, dis-, mis-* (inaudible, illegible, misanthrope).
- **Quality suffixes:** *-phile* (lover), *-phobe* (fearer/hater), *-cide* (killer), *-crat* (ruler), *-logy* (study), *-mania* (obsession), *-ist* (doer).
- **Adjective suffixes:** *-able/-ible, -ous, -al* (invincible, audacious, ephemeral).

High-yield classics: **philatelist** (stamp collector), **bibliophile** (book lover), **philanthropist** (lover of mankind), **misanthrope** (hater of mankind), **misogynist** (hater of women), **nepotism** (favoring relatives), **egocentric** (self-centered), **altruist** (selfless person), **omniscient** (all-knowing), **invincible** (cannot be conquered), **ephemeral** (short-lived), **interminable** (endless).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Function First, Word Second
Before choosing a substitute, identify what the phrase describes: a **person/role**, **place**, **thing**, **action**, or **quality**. This determines whether you need an agent noun (ending in *-ist, -er, -ant*), an abstract noun (ending in *-tion, -ity, -ism*), or an adjective (ending in *-ous, -able, -ive*). MDCAT stems often telegraph the part of speech through the determiner "a/an" (singular countable noun) or "the act of" (verbal noun).

#### Affix Decoding Strategy
Break the target word into **root + prefix + suffix**. Recognizing affixes lets you eliminate distractors even when the exact word is unfamiliar. For example, *bi-* + *annual* = biennial (every two years), not biannual (twice a year). Confusing these is a tested trap. Suffixes like *-cide* always denote killing (*homicide, suicide, genocide*), while *-cracy* always denotes rule (*democracy, theocracy*).

#### High-Frequency MDCAT Substitutions

| Phrase | One Word |
|---|---|
| A person who is afraid of heights | acrophobic |
| One who can speak many languages | polyglot |
| Lover of books | bibliophile |
| A place where bees are kept | apiary |
| A speech delivered without preparation | extempore |
| Rule by a single person | autocracy |
| A hater of mankind | misanthrope |
| A handwriting that cannot be read | illegible |
| A disease that spreads widely | pandemic |
| A person who is all-powerful | omnipotent |

#### Standard Question Patterns
1. *Choose the word that best substitutes the underlined group of words.* — Tests register and part of speech.
2. *A person who knows many languages is called…* — Tests precise vocabulary.
3. *The word X means…* — Tests meaning recall, not substitution proper, but uses the same word bank.

#### Common Traps
- **Near-synonym confusion:** *illiterate* (cannot read/write) vs *ignorant* (lacks knowledge); *invincible* (cannot be conquered) vs *invulnerable* (cannot be wounded).
- **Wrong part of speech:** selecting *gregarious* (adjective) when the stem demands a noun like *sociable person*.
- **Prefix misreads:** *unaware* (not knowing) vs *unaware of* (inattentive context); *disinterested* (impartial) vs *uninterested* (bored).

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Etymology as a Problem-Solving Tool
MDCAT rewards students who read roots, not just lists. Greek and Latin roots behave predictably:
- *-graphy* (writing): biography, calligraphy.
- *-logy* (study of): biology, anthropology.
- *-phagy* (eating): anthropophagy (cannibalism).
- *-philia* (love) vs *-phobia* (fear): hydrophilia vs hydrophobia.
- *-mania* (obsession): kleptomania, megalomania.

When the stem hints at a *lover/hater*, the suffix is decisive. A "lover of mankind" can only be **philanthropist** (*phil-* = love, *anthrop-* = man, *-ist* = one who); a "hater of women" is **misogynist** (*mis-* = hate, *gyn-* = woman). Choosing *misanthrope* here is the classic distractor.

#### Edge Cases and Register
- *Ephemeral* (adjective) vs *ephemerality* (noun) — a stem asking for the quality needs the noun form.
- *Omniscient* (all-knowing) vs *omnipotent* (all-powerful) vs *omnipresent* (everywhere) — common MCQ trio; the stem will specify the exact domain (knowledge, power, presence).
- *Altruist* (noun, selfless person) vs *altruism* (noun, the practice) vs *altruistic* (adjective) — match the part of speech the sentence demands.
- *Incredible* (extraordinary in modern usage) vs *incredulous* (disbelieving) — register trap.

#### Practice Prompts
1. *A person who neither eats nor drinks* — Test the prefix *a-/an-* (without) + Greek *-phagia* (eating) + *-ist*. Answer: **abstainer** (commonly tested) or, by strict etymology, **anophagi** is plural; the correct formal noun for a fasting abstainer is **abstinent**. Discuss why context forces a single answer.
2. *Something that cannot be revoked or called back* — Combine *ir-* (not) + Latin *vocare* (to call). Answer: **irrevocable**. Distractors: *irreversible* (cannot be undone physically), *irreparable* (cannot be repaired).

#### Study Strategy
In MDCAT, this sub-topic typically contributes 2–4 MCQs in the English section. Build a flashcard deck grouped by suffix (*-ist, -phile, -cide, -crat*) and review 10 cards daily; the affix pattern recognition will cover 70% of unfamiliar stems on exam day.
