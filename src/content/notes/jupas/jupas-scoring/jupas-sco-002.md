# "Best 5" and "Best 6" Programme Scoring Formulae — JUPAS Notes

This topic covers the common JUPAS programme scoring formulae. JUPAS participating-institution programmes adopt different formulae for ranking applicants. The common patterns are: (i) Any Best 5 subjects; (ii) Chinese + English + Any Best 3; (iii) Any Best 5 with a bonus for the 6th subject; and (iv) weighted formulae (with English, M1/M2 or other electives weighted above 1). Candidates must check the live scoring formula on the admissions page for each programme they apply to.

> Verify the live programme-specific scoring formulae on each university's admissions page and on https://www.jupas.edu.hk/ before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Formula (i) — Any Best 5**: the 5 subjects with the highest JUPAS scores are summed.
- **Formula (ii) — Chinese + English + Any Best 3**: Chinese and English are required; the 3 remaining subjects are the highest-scoring of the rest.
- **Formula (iii) — Any Best 5 with bonus for 6th subject**: the 5 highest-scoring subjects are summed; the 6th subject may receive a bonus (e.g. +5% or +10% of the 5-subject score).
- **Formula (iv) — Weighted formulae**: subjects are multiplied by weights. Examples: English × 1.5, M1/M2 × 2 in some programmes.
- **Where to find the formula**: published on each programme's admissions page (e.g. https://admissions.hku.hk/, https://join.hkust.edu.hk/, https://www.cityu.edu.hk/admo/, https://www.polyu.edu.hk/study/ug/admissions/jupas/).
- **Source**: 2025 JUPAS Admissions Scores report consolidates the published formulae.

#### Examiner traps

- Assuming all programmes use the same formula — they don't.
- Forgetting to check the weighted formulae — the same HKDSE result produces different admission scores against different programmes.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Common JUPAS programme scoring formulae

**Formula (i) — Any Best 5**:

Score = sum of the 5 highest-scoring Category A subjects (using the JUPAS conversion).

Used by many programmes across the UGC-funded institutions, especially those that do not have subject-specific requirements.

**Formula (ii) — Chinese + English + Any Best 3**:

Score = Chinese score + English score + sum of the 3 highest-scoring of the remaining Category A subjects.

Used by many programmes that emphasise English and Chinese (e.g. humanities, social sciences, business).

**Formula (iii) — Any Best 5 + bonus for 6th**:

Score = sum of the 5 highest-scoring Category A subjects + bonus for the 6th subject.

The bonus may be:
- A fixed percentage of the 5-subject score (e.g. +5%, +10%).
- A fixed-point bonus (e.g. +1, +2 points).

This formula recognises that candidates with strong 6th subjects are stronger than candidates with only 5 strong subjects.

**Formula (iv) — Weighted formulae**:

Score = weighted sum of selected subjects.

Examples:
- English × 1.5 + Math + Best 2 from Biology/Chemistry/Physics/M1/M2 + Best 1 other subject (some HKU programmes).
- English × 1.5 + Math + Best 2 from Physics/M1/M2 + Best 2 other subjects (HKUST science programmes; the highest score of at most TWO weighted electives is taken).
- Weighted by specific subject (e.g. Physics × 2, Math × 2).

#### How to calculate your score

1. Identify the formula for each programme you have applied to.
2. Convert each HKDSE Category A grade to the JUPAS score using the conversion table.
3. Identify the subjects to be summed (based on the formula).
4. Apply the weights (if any).
5. Sum the weighted scores.
6. Apply the bonus (if any).
7. Compare your score to the programme's expected-score range.

#### Exam technique

- **For "calculate your score" questions**: apply the formula step by step.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "All programmes use Any Best 5." No — many programmes use Chinese+English+Best 3 or weighted formulae.
- "A higher HKDSE grade always gives a higher score." Yes, for the same subject in the same formula. But across formulae, the same HKDSE grade may carry different weights.
- "The JUPAS score is the final score." No — the JUPAS score is used for ranking; the actual cutoff varies by cycle.
