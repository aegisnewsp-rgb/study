# Structures and Processes of Life — Leaving Certificate (Established) Biology Notes

Structures and Processes of Life is the largest strand of the NCCA Leaving Certificate Biology specification by mark weight, and it bundles three enormous sub-topics that together dominate the SEC paper: genetics and evolution (DNA structure and replication, gene expression, inheritance patterns, genetic engineering and biotechnology), energy and metabolism (photosynthesis, respiration, the biochemistry of ATP), and the biology of whole organisms (plant biology including structure, transport and reproduction; animal physiology including digestion, circulation, immunity, the nervous system and the endocrine system). The strand carries a weight rating of 5 out of 5 in the topic catalogue — the highest in the Biology specification — because every other strand in the syllabus depends on the cellular machinery introduced here.

At Higher level, Structures and Processes of Life extends into more detailed molecular genetics (transcription, translation, the operon model), more detailed inheritance (sex-linked inheritance, codominance, multiple alleles, polygenic inheritance), more demanding plant biology (the details of xylem and phloem transport, the role of auxins in phototropism), and more detailed animal physiology (the details of the cardiac cycle, the sliding-filament model of muscle contraction, the structure of the synapse). At Ordinary level the same content is examined but the mark scheme is more forgiving on the molecular detail and the kinetic vocabulary. A candidate who has memorised the key pathways (photosynthesis, respiration, protein synthesis, digestion) and who can sketch the structures (the heart, the nephron, the synapse, the chloroplast) will pick up marks across the largest single block of the paper.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Strand weight & spread**: Weight 5/5 in the topic catalogue — the highest in the Biology specification. Marks are spread across genetics, photosynthesis, respiration, plant biology and animal physiology. Under-preparing any one sub-topic depresses the largest block of the paper.
- **Central dogma of molecular biology**: DNA → RNA → Protein. DNA is transcribed into mRNA; mRNA is translated into protein at the ribosome. The flow of genetic information is one-way.
- **Photosynthesis core equation**: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ (in the light, in the chloroplast). The light-dependent reactions occur on the thylakoid membrane; the light-independent reactions (Calvin cycle) occur in the stroma. Confirm the live equation form on curriculumonline.ie.
- **Respiration core equation**: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + ATP (in the cytoplasm and the mitochondrion). Aerobic respiration has three stages: glycolysis (cytoplasm), the Krebs cycle (mitochondrial matrix), the electron transport chain (inner mitochondrial membrane). Anaerobic respiration in yeast produces ethanol and CO₂; in animals it produces lactate.
- **Mendelian inheritance**: dominant and recessive alleles; homozygous and heterozygous genotypes; phenotype vs genotype. Punnett squares predict offspring ratios. A monohybrid cross of two heterozygotes gives a 3:1 phenotype ratio and a 1:2:1 genotype ratio.
- **Plant biology essentials**: xylem transports water and minerals from roots to leaves; phloem transports sugars from sources to sinks. Stomata (in the epidermis) control gas exchange; guard cells open and close the stomatal pore. Roots absorb water by osmosis and minerals by active transport.
- **Animal physiology essentials**: digestion breaks down large food molecules into small absorbable units; the circulatory system transports nutrients, gases and wastes; the immune system defends against pathogens; the nervous system coordinates rapid responses; the endocrine system coordinates slower, longer-lasting responses.
- **First move on a genetics question**: identify the inheritance pattern (dominant/recessive, sex-linked, codominant) before predicting offspring ratios. Naming the pattern is the lead-in mark.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing genotype with phenotype. Genotype is the alleles present; phenotype is the observable characteristic. The distinction is a mark.
- Forgetting that mRNA uses uracil (U) where DNA uses thymine (T). A candidate who writes "A-T" in a translation question forfeits the molecular-biology mark.
- Treating anaerobic respiration as "no respiration". Anaerobic respiration *does* produce ATP, but far less than aerobic respiration. The mark scheme reads for the comparison.
- Confusing xylem and phloem. Xylem carries water and minerals upward; phloem carries sugars in both directions. The direction and content are both marks.
- Skipping the units when describing a physiological measurement (heart rate in bpm, breathing rate in breaths per minute). Units are a discrete mark on most SEC questions.

#### 30-minute triage checklist

- [ ] Can you write the equations for photosynthesis and respiration from memory?
- [ ] Can you draw and label a mitochondrion and a chloroplast in under five minutes each?
- [ ] Can you complete a Punnett square for a monohybrid and a dihybrid cross?
- [ ] Can you name the four chambers of the heart and the direction of blood flow through each?

### 🟡 Standard — Structured Study (1d–1mo)

#### Genetics and evolution

The genetics sub-topic at Higher level extends well beyond Mendel's original pea experiments. The mark scheme reaches for:

- **DNA structure**: the double helix, antiparallel strands, base-pairing rules (A-T, G-C), the sugar-phosphate backbone. The structure-function link is what the mark scheme rewards.
- **DNA replication**: semiconservative — each new double helix contains one original strand and one new strand. The enzyme helicase unwinds the double helix; DNA polymerase synthesises the new strand. The Meselson-Stahl experiment established that replication is semiconservative.
- **Transcription**: DNA is transcribed into mRNA in the nucleus. The enzyme RNA polymerase synthesises the mRNA strand using the template DNA strand; the mRNA then leaves the nucleus for the ribosome.
- **Translation**: mRNA is translated into protein at the ribosome. tRNA molecules carry specific amino acids; the anticodon on the tRNA pairs with the codon on the mRNA. The ribosome moves along the mRNA three bases at a time.
- **Inheritance patterns**: dominant/recessive (Mendelian), sex-linked (X-linked, e.g. haemophilia, colour blindness), codominance (both alleles expressed, e.g. ABO blood groups), multiple alleles (more than two alleles in the population, e.g. ABO), polygenic inheritance (many genes contribute, e.g. height, skin colour).
- **Genetic engineering**: restriction enzymes cut DNA at specific sequences; ligase joins DNA fragments; plasmids are used as vectors to introduce foreign DNA into bacteria; the gene of interest is inserted into the plasmid and the recombinant plasmid is taken up by the bacterium.
- **Evolution**: natural selection acts on variation within a population; the fittest individuals are most likely to survive and reproduce; the favourable alleles become more common in the population over generations. The mark scheme rewards candidates who can name Darwin and Wallace as co-discoverers of natural selection.

A candidate who has rehearsed each of these sub-topics with a worked example will pick up the genetics marks on any SEC paper.

#### Photosynthesis and respiration

The two energy pathways are the most-tested sub-topics in the strand. The mark scheme reads for:

- **Photosynthesis — light-dependent reactions**: occur on the thylakoid membrane. Light energy is absorbed by chlorophyll; water is split (photolysis) to release O₂, H⁺ and electrons; the electrons pass along an electron transport chain, releasing energy to synthesise ATP (photophosphorylation); NADP⁺ is reduced to NADPH.
- **Photosynthesis — light-independent reactions (Calvin cycle)**: occur in the stroma. CO₂ is fixed by RuBisCO to form an unstable 6-carbon compound, which splits into two 3-carbon molecules (3-phosphoglycerate); these are reduced to glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate (G3P) using ATP and NADPH from the light-dependent reactions; some G3P leaves the cycle to form glucose, the rest is recycled to regenerate RuBP.
- **Aerobic respiration — glycolysis**: occurs in the cytoplasm. Glucose is split into two pyruvate molecules; a small amount of ATP and NADH is produced. No oxygen is required.
- **Aerobic respiration — Krebs cycle**: occurs in the mitochondrial matrix. Pyruvate is broken down to CO₂, with the production of NADH, FADH₂ and a small amount of ATP.
- **Aerobic respiration — electron transport chain**: occurs on the inner mitochondrial membrane. NADH and FADH₂ donate electrons; the electrons pass along a chain of carriers; the energy released is used to pump H⁺ across the membrane, creating a gradient; the H⁺ flows back through ATP synthase, generating ATP (oxidative phosphorylation). Oxygen is the final electron acceptor, forming water.
- **Anaerobic respiration**: in yeast, pyruvate is converted to ethanol and CO₂. In animal muscle, pyruvate is converted to lactate. Both pathways regenerate NAD⁺ so that glycolysis can continue.

The mark scheme rewards candidates who can write the *equation* for each pathway, locate it in the *correct cellular compartment*, name the *inputs* and *outputs*, and explain *why* each step matters.

#### Plant biology

The plant biology sub-topic covers structure, transport and reproduction.

- **Plant structure**: roots (absorption, anchorage), stems (support, transport), leaves (photosynthesis, gas exchange), flowers (reproduction). Each organ has tissues specialised for its function.
- **Xylem transport**: water and minerals move upward from roots to leaves. The transpiration stream is driven by evaporation from the leaves; cohesion-tension pulls the water column upward. Xylem vessels are dead, hollow tubes reinforced with lignin.
- **Phloem transport**: sugars move from sources (leaves) to sinks (roots, fruits, growing tips). The pressure-flow hypothesis proposes that active loading of sugars at the source creates a pressure that pushes the phloem sap toward the sink. Phloem tissue consists of sieve tube elements (alive, lack a nucleus) and companion cells.
- **Plant reproduction**: flowers are the reproductive organs. Pollination transfers pollen from anther to stigma; fertilisation occurs when the pollen tube delivers sperm cells to the ovule; the ovule develops into the seed; the ovary develops into the fruit.

A candidate who has memorised the structure of a flower and can describe the *events* of pollination, fertilisation and seed development will pick up the plant-biology marks.

#### Animal physiology

The animal physiology sub-topic covers digestion, circulation, immunity, the nervous system and the endocrine system.

- **Digestion**: mechanical digestion (chewing, churning) increases the surface area of food; chemical digestion (enzymes) breaks down large molecules into small absorbable units. Carbohydrates are digested to monosaccharides, proteins to amino acids, lipids to glycerol and fatty acids. Absorption occurs primarily in the small intestine; the surface area is increased by villi and microvilli.
- **Circulation**: the heart is a double pump — the right side pumps deoxygenated blood to the lungs; the left side pumps oxygenated blood to the body. Blood flows through arteries (away from the heart), capillaries (gas and nutrient exchange), veins (toward the heart). The cardiac cycle consists of atrial systole, ventricular systole and diastole.
- **Immunity**: non-specific defences (skin, mucous membranes, phagocytes) act against any pathogen; specific defences (lymphocytes — B cells and T cells) act against specific antigens. Antibodies are produced by plasma cells (differentiated from B cells); T cells include helper T cells (coordinate the immune response) and killer T cells (destroy infected cells).
- **Nervous system**: sensory neurons carry impulses from receptors to the CNS; motor neurons carry impulses from the CNS to effectors; interneurons connect sensory and motor neurons in the CNS. The synapse is the junction between two neurons; neurotransmitters carry the signal across the synaptic cleft.
- **Endocrine system**: glands (e.g. pituitary, thyroid, adrenal, pancreas) secrete hormones into the bloodstream; hormones travel to target cells with specific receptors; the response is slower but longer-lasting than nervous responses.

A candidate who has rehearsed each sub-system with a worked example (e.g. the digestion of a sandwich, the cardiac cycle, the reflex arc) will pick up the animal-physiology marks.

### 🔴 Deep Dive — Mastery & Edge Cases (3mo–2yr)

#### Inheritance patterns in detail

At Higher level, the inheritance sub-topic tests each pattern with a worked Punnett square. The mark scheme rewards candidates who can:

- **Name the inheritance pattern** from a pedigree or a verbal description.
- **Deduce parental genotypes** from offspring phenotypes.
- **Predict offspring ratios** using a Punnett square.
- **Discuss probability** — stating the ratio *and* the probability for any given phenotype.

The most-tested patterns are:

- **Autosomal recessive**: skips generations; two unaffected parents can have an affected child if both are carriers (e.g. cystic fibrosis).
- **Autosomal dominant**: appears in every generation; an affected parent has a 50% chance of passing the allele to each child (e.g. Huntington's disease).
- **X-linked recessive**: more common in males (who have only one X chromosome); an affected father cannot pass the trait to his son (the Y is passed instead); a carrier mother has a 50% chance of passing the allele to each son (e.g. haemophilia, colour blindness).
- **Codominance**: both alleles are fully expressed in the heterozygote (e.g. ABO blood groups, where the A and B alleles are codominant and the O allele is recessive).
- **Multiple alleles**: more than two alleles in the population, but each individual still has two (e.g. ABO has three alleles — I^A, I^B and i).
- **Polygenic inheritance**: many genes contribute to a single phenotype; the result is a continuous distribution (e.g. height, skin colour, intelligence).

A candidate who can recognise the pattern from the pedigree and write a Punnett square for it picks up the full inheritance mark on any SEC question.

#### The cardiac cycle and ECG

At Higher level, the cardiac cycle is tested with the timing of the ECG trace. The mark scheme reads for:

- **P wave**: atrial depolarisation (atrial systole follows).
- **QRS complex**: ventricular depolarisation (ventricular systole follows).
- **T wave**: ventricular repolarisation (ventricular diastole follows).

A candidate who can label an ECG trace and explain what each wave represents picks up marks that a candidate who only knows the chambers of the heart forfeits.

#### The synapse and neurotransmitters

At Higher level, the synapse is tested in detail. The mark scheme reads for:

- **Synaptic vesicles**: contain neurotransmitter, release contents into the synaptic cleft when the action potential reaches the axon terminal.
- **Neurotransmitter**: diffuses across the synaptic cleft and binds to receptors on the postsynaptic membrane.
- **Postsynaptic response**: opens ion channels, generating a new action potential in the postsynaptic neuron (excitatory) or hyperpolarising the membrane (inhibitory).
- **Neurotransmitter removal**: broken down by enzymes in the cleft, or reabsorbed by the presynaptic neuron. Many drugs act by mimicking or blocking neurotransmitters.

A candidate who can describe the steps in order and name an example neurotransmitter (e.g. acetylcholine, dopamine, serotonin) picks up the full synapse mark.

#### Common mistakes that depress the grade

- **The "dominant = common" trap** — confusing dominant alleles with common alleles in the population. The mark scheme reads for the *inheritance pattern*, not for population frequency.
- **The "photosynthesis produces oxygen" trap** — forgetting that the O₂ released by photosynthesis comes from the *splitting of water*, not from CO₂. The source of the oxygen is a mark.
- **The "ATP is energy" trap** — saying "ATP is energy" without explaining that ATP is the *molecule that carries energy*. The mark scheme reads for the structure-function link.
- **The "veins carry oxygenated blood" trap** — forgetting that the pulmonary vein carries oxygenated blood while most other veins carry deoxygenated blood. The exception is a mark.
- **The "all nerves are the same" trap** — confusing sensory, motor and interneurons. The mark scheme reads for the *direction* of the nerve impulse.
