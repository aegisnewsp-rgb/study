# Legal News

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Legal News is the Current Affairs sub-area testing awareness of Indian judicial pronouncements, statutes, ordinances, and constitutional developments reported in the 12 months before CLAT. It carries roughly **5% weightage** inside the larger 25% Current Affairs & GK block.

#### Must-know anchors:

- **Three new criminal codes**: Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA) — replaced IPC, CrPC and Indian Evidence Act from **1 July 2024**.
- **Article 142** = complete justice power of the Supreme Court; **Article 32** and **Article 226** = writ jurisdictions (SC and HC respectively).
- **Ratio decidendi** = binding reason; **obiter dicta** = persuasive observation, not binding.

#### High-yield pointers:

- Track Supreme Court and High Court **landmark judgments** of the preceding year with bench strength and citation.
- Distinguish **ordinances** (Article 123) from **enacted Acts** — ordinances lapse if not ratified within 6 weeks of Parliament reassembly.
- Cite the **correct BNS section** (e.g. Section 302 BNS for murder; Section 63 BNS for sexual offences) — the IPC numbering no longer applies post-July 2024.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Scope of Legal News in CLAT

CLAT's Current Affairs section tests factual recall and comprehension of legal developments reported in mainstream dailies (The Hindu, Indian Express) and law-specific outlets (LiveLaw, Bar & Bench, Supreme Court Observer) during the **12-month reference window** preceding the exam. The Consortium of NLUs frames this under "Contemporary legal issues" within General Knowledge, and questions appear as factual MCQs, passage-based comprehension, or principle-application sets.

#### Reading a Reported Judgment

A news report must be parsed for **four mandatory elements**: court and bench strength, judge name(s), date of judgment, and the **legal issue** plus relief. The binding portion is the **ratio decidendi**; everything else is **obiter dicta**. Misclassifying obiter as the law declared is the most common trap in assertion-reason questions.

#### Mapping Statutes to the New Criminal Codes

| Old provision (pre-1 July 2024) | New provision (post-1 July 2024) | Subject |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IPC Section 302 | BNS Section 302 | Punishment for murder |
| IPC Section 376 | BNS Section 63 | Sexual offences |
| CrPC Section 154 | BNSS Section 154 | FIR registration |
| Indian Evidence Act Section 27 | BSA Section 27 | Confession admissibility |

#### Sources to Track Daily

- Supreme Court of India website (main.sci.gov.in) for daily orders and judgments.
- LiveLaw and Bar & Bench for curated case commentary with citations.
- Ministry of Law and Justice notifications for ordinances and amendments.
- NALSA and Election Commission of India press releases for institutional news.

**Trap:** A **Law Commission Report** or **Standing Committee recommendation** is not law until Parliament passes it. Quoting a report as enacted legislation loses marks.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Case-to-Subject Mapping Strategy

CLAT rewards candidates who can **categorise** a reported development. Build a mental grid: criminal (BNS/BNSS/BSA), constitutional (writs, basic structure, Article 370 abrogation aftermath), commercial (IBC, arbitration, competition), environmental (EPA, NGT orders), family (live-in relationships, same-sex marriage rulings), labour (Code on Wages, industrial disputes), tax (GST Council, direct tax amendments), cyber (IT Act 2000, DPDP Act 2023). This grid prevents the failure mode of writing a "politically-slugged" answer like "SC slams government" instead of the actual **legal issue, statutory provision interpreted, and relief granted**.

#### Common Mistakes Examiners Exploit

1. Citing IPC sections (302, 376, 420) instead of the equivalent BNS sections after 1 July 2024.
2. Treating **interim orders** as final judgments and inflating their importance.
3. Confusing PILs **admitted** with PILs **disposed of**.
4. Reporting a High Court division bench ruling as a Supreme Court judgment.
5. Ignoring the 12-month reference window and quoting older landmark cases as "current".

#### Worked Example (Application Type)

**Prompt:** "The Supreme Court invoked its power under Article 142 to dissolve a marriage held to be a dead relationship, observing that the right to personal liberty under Article 21 encompasses the dignity of choice."

**What to extract:** Court = SC; Power = Article 142 (complete justice); Sub-issue = Article 21 dignity; Effect = marriage dissolved (binding relief, not obiter). Note the bench strength and citation if quoted.

#### Practice Prompts

1. List three judgments from the past year where Article 21 was the central provision, with bench strength.
2. Identify the BNSS section that corresponds to anticipatory bail (old CrPC Section 438) and state one procedural change.
