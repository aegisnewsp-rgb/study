# Studies of Diverse Cultures Passages — MCAT CARS

Studies of Diverse Cultures is the one CARS passage type that AAMC deliberately leaves unfiled. In the AAMC's own passage-type list it appears under both Humanities and Social Sciences, with the note that "depending on the focus of the text a Studies of Diverse Cultures passage could be classified as belonging to either the Humanities or Social Sciences." A passage about how a ritual structures belonging within a community reads as humanities; a passage about how that same ritual shifts migration and public-health outcomes reads as social sciences. The label describes the subject matter, not a separate skill.

This matters because it is the passage type most likely to feel unfamiliar to a candidate. When a passage describes a custom, a language structure, a diaspora experience, or a small community the reader has no context for, the instinct is to disengage. The AAMC position is blunt about the fix: "no additional coursework or specific knowledge is required to do well on the Critical Analysis and Reasoning Skills section," and "everything you need to know to answer test questions is in the passages and the questions themselves." The passage explains its own terms.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### How AAMC files this passage type

- Appears in the **Humanities** list *and* the **Social Sciences** list.
- Which bucket it lands in depends on whether the passage is about ideas (humanities) or about patterns and outcomes (social sciences).
- It is not a fourth CARS skill and not a separate passage category — it is a content tag.
- All three CARS skills still apply, exactly as they do to any other passage.

#### What these passages look like

- A practice, belief, or social norm described from the inside, with the author's own framing.
- Often an interpretation or application of a theory to a specific population group or country.
- Frequently conversational in tone — the author explains, argues, or evaluates rather than asserts flatly.
- Self-contained: specialist terms are glossed in the passage before they are used in the items.

#### One-pass drill

1. Read the passage in 4 minutes. Write down the central claim in one sentence.
2. Name the community or tradition the passage is about, and the author's stance toward it.
3. Decide which side of the line it falls on: relationships between ideas (humanities) or patterns and outcomes (social sciences).
4. Answer the items from the passage alone. If you needed outside knowledge to answer, you have misread the question.

#### Examiner traps

- **Assuming the passage is testing cultural knowledge.** It is not. The items test comprehension and reasoning.
- **Reading your own priors into the author's position.** These passages often open by paraphrasing a position they then complicate. Track who is speaking.
- **Skipping unfamiliar terms instead of reading their gloss.** The author defines what matters. That definition is the answer to the item.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Reading for the argument, not the anthropology

The error on these passages is treating the passage as a source of facts to memorise. It is not. It is a piece of argumentative writing about a culture, and the items behave like any other CARS items.

- Identify the **author's claim**, not the community's practice in reality.
- Identify the **evidence** the author offers for that claim.
- Identify what the author **assumes** and what they leave out — omission is a standard Reasoning Within the Text target.
- For Reasoning Beyond the Text items, ask what would follow *from the author's argument*, not from what you know about the culture.

#### Distinguishing the two readings

When a Studies of Diverse Cultures passage could reasonably file either way, the distinction is about what the passage is doing with the material:

- **Humanities reading** — the passage examines how ideas relate: how art reflects social change, how a philosophy of ethics adapts to new technology, how a narrative voice frames belonging. The claim is about meaning.
- **Social sciences reading** — the passage examines how patterns work: how an anthropological or archaeological reconstruction infers past behaviour from artifacts, how a demographic trend is explained, how a population-health policy is evaluated. The claim is about cause and effect.

The items follow the passage's framing. A passage that opens with an observation and moves to an explanation is asking you to reason like a social scientist, whatever the cultural content.

#### Handling a passage that unsettles you

This is a real problem, not a hypothetical one, and the AAMC design accounts for it: the section measures analysis and reasoning skills, not cultural recall. Three techniques keep it workable.

1. **Write the gloss, do not skip it.** Every passage here defines its own terms. Copy the author's definition down once and the unfamiliarity largely dissolves.
2. **Track attribution.** These passages frequently present an outside scholar's interpretation of a community and then complicate it. Knowing *who currently holds the claim* resolves many items.
3. **Re-read for structure on the second pass.** Once the vocabulary is settled, read for the argument skeleton: claim, evidence, caveat, implication.

#### Practice routine

- Take one Studies of Diverse Cultures passage. Read it in 4 minutes.
- Write the central claim and the author's stance in two lines.
- Answer the items. Time yourself: under 8 minutes per passage.
- Take 3 passages in a row, rotating between the two readings. Repeat the routine weekly.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How these passages connect to the other CARS content

- **Humanities Passages** — same conversational, opinionated style; Studies of Diverse Cultures passages often simply carry a humanities argument about a cultural practice.
- **Social Sciences Passages** — same factual, scientific tone; the passage may be describing how a scholar reconstructs a pattern from artifacts or how a population-level outcome is explained.
- **All three CARS skills** apply, with Reasoning Within the Text and Reasoning Beyond the Text doing most of the work because these passages lean on interpretation and application.

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"I need to know the culture to answer the items."** No — AAMC states that everything needed is in the passage and questions. The test is reasoning, not recall.
- **"The author must be right about the culture."** The items test the author's argument. A passage can describe an interpretation in order to challenge it.
- **"This passage is humanities because it is about culture."** The bucket depends on whether the passage is reasoning about relationships between ideas or about patterns and outcomes.
- **"I should flag every item I cannot answer."** Flagging is for time pressure, not for discomfort. An unfamiliar passage is still a tractable passage.

#### Exam technique

- Read the passage in 4 minutes. Get the central claim and the speaker's stance down first.
- For argument items, mark claim, evidence, and assumption.
- For application items, extend the author's reasoning, not the underlying facts.
- For tone items, note whether the author is neutral, sympathetic, critical, or complicating — these passages rarely stay neutral.
- Use the platform's flag-for-review function for items you run out of time on, not for items that merely unsettle you.

#### Specification reference

Studies of Diverse Cultures is an AAMC CARS passage type that can be classified as either Humanities or Social Sciences depending on the focus of the text. AAMC lists it in both the humanities discipline set (architecture, art, dance, ethics, literature, music, philosophy, popular culture, religion, theater) and the social sciences discipline set (anthropology, archaeology, economics, education, geography, history, linguistics, political science, population health, psychology, sociology).
