# Alcohols Phenol Ether

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Alcohols** are compounds where a hydroxyl (–OH) group is attached to an sp³ carbon of an alkyl chain (R–OH). **Phenols** carry the –OH directly on a benzene ring (Ar–OH), while **ethers** have an oxygen bridging two alkyl or aryl groups (R–O–R′). These three families share the C–O bond but differ sharply in bonding environment, which drives their reactivity.

- **Acidity order**: phenol (pKa ≈ 10) > water > 1° alcohol > 2° alcohol > 3° alcohol. Phenoxide is resonance-stabilised, alcoholic alkoxide is not.
- **Lucas test**: ZnCl₂/HCl turns turbid immediately with 3° alcohols, in 5–10 min with 2°, and not at all with 1° alcohols at room temperature.
- **Williamson synthesis** is the go-to route for unsymmetrical ethers: R–X + R′O⁻Na⁺ → R–O–R′ + NaX (always use a 1° alkyl halide to avoid E2 elimination).

| Compound | General formula | Functional group |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alcohol | R–OH | –OH on sp³ carbon |
| Phenol | Ar–OH | –OH on aromatic ring |
| Ether | R–O–R′ | C–O–C linkage |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Classification and Nomenclature

Alcohols are classified as **1°, 2°, or 3°** based on how many carbons are attached to the carbon bearing the –OH. Phenols are usually named after the parent ring (phenol, *o*-cresol, resorcinol), while ethers are named as **alkoxyalkanes** — for example, CH₃OCH₂CH₃ is methoxyethane, not ethyl methyl ether. IUPAC rule for alcohols: replace the terminal **-e** of the parent alkane with **-ol** (ethanol, propan-2-ol).

#### Acidity and the Role of Resonance

The defining conceptual point of this chapter is the acidity gap between alcohols and phenols. When phenol loses H⁺, the resulting **phenoxide ion** delocalises the negative charge over the ortho and para positions of the ring, giving extra stability. An alkoxide (RO⁻) has no such resonance, so ethanol has pKa ≈ 16, nearly a million times weaker than phenol. This is why phenol dissolves in NaOH but alcohols do not.

#### Reactions of Alcohols

- **Dehydration** with concentrated H₂SO₄ at 413 K gives an ether; at 443 K it gives an alkene (Zaitsev product is the more substituted alkene).
- **Oxidation**: 1° alcohol → aldehyde → carboxylic acid; 2° alcohol → ketone; 3° alcohol resists oxidation under mild conditions.
- **Esterification** with a carboxylic acid in the presence of conc. H₂SO₄ is reversible; the ester mass equals the sum of acid and alcohol masses minus 18 (for H₂O lost).

#### Reactions of Phenols

Phenols undergo electrophilic aromatic substitution far more readily than benzene because the –OH group is an activator. With bromine water, phenol instantly gives a white precipitate of **2,4,6-tribromophenol** — a classic NEET spotter. A neutral FeCl₃ solution turns violet with phenol due to the [Fe(OAr)₆]³⁻ complex.

| Test | Reagent | Observation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lucas | ZnCl₂/HCl | Turbidity: 3° instant, 2° slow, 1° none |
| FeCl₃ (neutral) | FeCl₃ aq. | Violet/blue colour with phenols |
| Bromine water | Br₂/H₂O | White ppt of 2,4,6-tribromophenol |
| 2,4-DNP | 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazine | Orange ppt for aldehydes/ketones |

#### Preparation of Ethers

- **Williamson synthesis** (SN2) is reliable for unsymmetrical ethers and phenoxide alkylation.
- **Acid-catalysed dehydration** of alcohols at 413 K produces symmetrical ethers; at 443 K the alkene dominates.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Kolbe's vs Reimer-Tiemann — The Classic Confusion

Both reactions start from sodium phenoxide and use NaOH, but the electrophile differs and so does the product. In **Kolbe's reaction**, CO₂ at 400 K and 4–7 atm pressure introduces a –COOH group at the ortho position, giving **salicylic acid**. In **Reimer-Tiemann**, CHCl₃ with NaOH generates the dichlorocarbene intermediate (:CCl₂), which attacks the ring to install a –CHO group, producing **salicylaldehyde**. NEET has historically asked students to identify which reagent pair gives which product.

#### Boiling Point and Hydrogen Bonding

Among isomers and near-isomers of comparable molar mass, the order of boiling points is **alcohol > phenol > ether**. Alcohols and phenols form strong intermolecular O–H···O hydrogen bonds, while ethers only act as H-bond acceptors (no O–H donor). This is why diethyl ether (molar mass 74 g/mol) boils at 308 K, well below 1-butanol (molar mass 74 g/mol) at 390 K.

#### Ether Cleavage — A Frequently Missed Trap

Although ethers are often called "inert solvents," they are cleaved by excess concentrated **HI or HBr**. The mechanism proceeds via protonation of oxygen, then SN1 or SN2 attack by halide. With a tertiary alkyl group, the reaction follows SN1 and produces a tertiary alkyl halide plus an alcohol — a fact often missed by students who assume ethers are chemically unreactive.

#### Worked Micro-Example

Predict the products when ethanol is treated with concentrated H₂SO₄ at (a) 413 K and (b) 443 K.

- At 413 K: two ethanol molecules condense to form **diethyl ether (C₂H₅–O–C₂H₅)** with loss of water.
- At 443 K: intramolecular dehydration gives **ethene (CH₂=CH₂)** as the Zaitsev product (only one possible alkene here).

#### Practice Prompts

1. Arrange phenol, ethanol, and *tert*-butanol in increasing order of acidity and justify using resonance.
2. Why does Williamson synthesis of *tert*-butyl ethyl ether fail if you try to make it from *tert*-butyl bromide plus sodium ethoxide?

#### Common Mistakes Recap

- Using a 3° alkyl halide in Williamson synthesis causes E2 elimination, not ether formation.
- Bromine water does not react with alcohols — only phenols give the white 2,4,6-tribromophenol precipitate.
- FeCl₃ is a sensitive but not exclusive test; confirm phenols with bromine water or esterification.
