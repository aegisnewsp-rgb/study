# Academic Passage Reading and Skimming — IELTS Reading Notes

The IELTS Academic Reading test has 3 long passages with a total of 2,150-2,750 words, drawn from authentic books, journals, magazines and newspapers on topics of general interest. The candidate has 60 minutes for 40 questions. Three core skills underpin the test: skimming for gist, scanning for specific information, and reading for detail. Most candidates need to improve at least one of these.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Format:** 60 minutes, 40 questions, 3 passages (2,150-2,750 words total).
- **Passage sources:** books, journals, magazines, newspapers on topics of general interest.
- **Audience:** non-specialist test-takers entering undergraduate or postgraduate programmes.
- **Skimming:** read quickly to grasp the main idea of a paragraph or passage.
- **Scanning:** search for specific information (a name, a date, a number).
- **Reading for detail:** focus on a particular sentence or phrase to extract meaning.
- **Time per question:** 60 minutes / 40 questions = 1.5 minutes per question on average.

#### Examiner traps

- Spending too long reading the passage in detail — skim first, then go back for details.
- Not transferring answers to the answer sheet — on paper, there is no extra time for transfer.

---

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Skimming

Read the first sentence of each paragraph to get the main idea. The first sentence of a paragraph usually states the topic; the last sentence summarises or transitions.

#### Scanning

Move your eyes quickly down the page looking for keywords. Use the question stem to identify what to scan for. Stop when you find the relevant section.

#### Reading for detail

Once you have located the relevant section, read carefully to extract the answer. Do not try to read the whole passage in detail — it is too time-consuming.

---

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where reading skills appear across IELTS Reading

All 40 questions depend on these three skills. Skim first, then scan, then read for detail. The most efficient candidates follow this sequence.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "I need to understand every word." No — you need to catch the keywords from the questions. Other words can be skipped.
- "Reading the whole passage first is faster." No — it is slower and less efficient. Skim first.
- "I should read the questions after reading the passage." No — read the questions first to know what to look for.

#### Exam technique

- For all passages, skim first (1 minute), then read the questions, then scan for the answer.
- For vocabulary-in-context questions, locate the keyword in the passage and read the surrounding sentence.
- For inference questions, identify the relevant passage and read carefully for the implied meaning.

#### Timing and paragraph mapping protocol for Passage 1

In IELTS Academic and General Training Reading, Passage 1 is the most straightforward text, but candidates frequently squander time by reading every sentence slowly:

1. **2-Minute Skim**: Spend at most two minutes reading the title, subtitle, and first sentence of each paragraph. Form a mental outline of where topics live (e.g., Paragraph A: Origins, Paragraph B: Manufacturing process, Paragraph C: Modern applications).
2. **Scan by Keyword Clusters**: Never search for a single isolated word; keywords in question stems are almost always paraphrased in the text. Search for conceptual synonyms (e.g., *increase dramatically* -> *surged*, *climbed steeply*).
3. **Strict 17-Minute Limit**: Complete all 13 or 14 questions in Passage 1 within 17 minutes. Transfer answers directly onto the answer sheet as you work, leaving 20 minutes for Passage 2 and 23 minutes for the significantly more challenging Passage 3.
