# Biotechnology

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Biotechnology** is the controlled use of biological organisms, cells, or enzymes to make useful products. The two pillars you must memorise for NABTEB are **recombinant DNA technology** and **polymerase chain reaction (PCR)**.

- A **restriction enzyme** (e.g., EcoRI) cuts DNA at a specific palindromic sequence, producing **sticky ends**. **DNA ligase** joins the cut gene into a **plasmid vector**, forming **recombinant DNA** that is inserted into a host (commonly *Escherichia coli*).
- **PCR** has three temperature-dependent steps — **denaturation (≈95 °C)**, **annealing (≈55 °C)**, and **extension (≈72 °C)** — and amplifies a chosen DNA region exponentially.
- High-yield NABTEB pointers: recombinant human **insulin** from *E. coli*, **Bt cotton** (Bacillus thuringiensis toxin gene), **golden rice** (β-carotene), and **bioremediation** of oil spills by *Pseudomonas*.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Recombinant DNA Technology
A **restriction endonuclease** recognises a 4–8 base palindromic sequence and hydrolyses the phosphodiester bond, generating either **sticky (cohesive) ends** or blunt ends. The target gene and a bacterial **plasmid** are cut with the same enzyme, mixed so their sticky ends base-pair, and sealed by **DNA ligase**. The recombinant plasmid is then introduced into a host cell by **transformation**, where it replicates along with the host. Selection is done using **antibiotic resistance markers** (e.g., ampicillin resistance gene).

#### Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR)
PCR amplifies a specific DNA sequence *in vitro* without a living cell. The cycle:
1. **Denaturation** (~95 °C) — double helix separates.
2. **Annealing** (~55 °C) — short **primers** bind the flanking regions.
3. **Extension** (~72 °C) — heat-tolerant **Taq polymerase** synthesises new strands.
Each cycle doubles the DNA copy number, so after *n* cycles there are 2ⁿ copies.

#### Plant and Animal Applications
- **Bt cotton** carries the *cry* gene from *Bacillus thuringiensis*, producing an insecticidal protein against bollworm.
- **Golden rice** is engineered with *phytoene synthase* and *crtI* genes to produce β-carotene (provitamin A).
- **Transgenic animals** such as goats secrete pharmaceutical proteins in milk ("**biopharming**").

#### Industrial and Environmental Uses
**Fermentation** uses yeast (*Saccharomyces cerevisiae*) anaerobically for bread, beer, and wine; aerobic fermentation in a **bioreactor** produces antibiotics (penicillin from *Penicillium*), enzymes, and citric acid. **Bioremediation** exploits microbes such as *Pseudomonas putida* to degrade oil hydrocarbons and heavy metals.

#### Tissue Culture
Based on **totipotency** — the ability of a differentiated plant cell to regenerate a whole plant. An **explant** is sterilised and placed on **MS medium** (Murashige and Skoog) with auxins and cytokinins, forming an undifferentiated **callus**, then shoots and roots.

#### Typical NABTEB Question Patterns
- 1-mark objectives asking for the enzyme that joins DNA fragments (DNA ligase).
- 2-mark structured questions listing steps of gene transfer.
- 4-mark essays comparing **selective breeding** vs **genetic engineering** or discussing **bioethics** of GMOs.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Worked Example — Recombinant Insulin Production
Human insulin is a polypeptide of 51 amino acids encoded by the *INS* gene on chromosome 11. The gene is excised using a restriction enzyme and ligated into a plasmid carrying the *lac* operon promoter. The recombinant plasmid transforms *E. coli* K-12. On IPTG induction, **insulin chains A and B** are expressed separately, purified, and mixed in vitro to form functional **human insulin (Humulin)** — the first FDA-approved recombinant drug (1982). NABTEB loves the contrast with older **porcine insulin** extracted from pig pancreas.

#### Edge Cases and Distinctions
- **Cloning (e.g., Dolly the sheep, 1996)** transfers a whole somatic nucleus into an enucleated egg — this is **nuclear transfer**, **not** the same as producing a transgenic organism.
- **Gene therapy** (e.g., SCID, RPE65-mediated Leber congenital amaurosis) delivers functional genes via viral vectors (AAV) to somatic cells; it does not alter germline DNA.
- **Monoclonal antibodies** are produced by fusing B-lymphocytes with myeloma cells to form **hybridomas**, immortalising single-antibody specificity — applied in pregnancy tests and cancer therapy (trastuzumab).

#### Connections to Adjacent Topics
Link **meiosis and crossing-over** to natural recombination, **mutation** to source of variation for selection, **enzyme specificity** to restriction enzyme behaviour, and **ecology** to risks of **gene flow** from GM crops to wild relatives.

#### Common Mistakes
1. Saying PCR copies whole chromosomes — it amplifies only the region flanked by primers.
2. Confusing **sticky ends** with blunt ends; only complementary sticky ends can be ligated efficiently.
3. Assuming fermentation is always aerobic — yeast alcoholic fermentation is anaerobic.
4. Believing tissue culture uses soil — it requires sterile **MS medium** with sucrose and plant growth regulators.
5. Ignoring that **bioethics** questions on NABTEB expect mention of **biosafety, GM labelling, and patenting of life forms**.
