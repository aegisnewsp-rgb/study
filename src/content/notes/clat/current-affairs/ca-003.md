# Legal News

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Legal News is the slice of CLAT Current Affairs that tests your awareness of Indian judicial pronouncements, parliamentary statutes, ordinances, and constitutional developments reported in the **12-month window** before the exam. CLAT conducted by the Consortium of NLUs allocates roughly **5% weightage** inside the larger 25% Current Affairs & GK section, so two to three questions almost always come from this area.

The three criminal codes — **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS)**, **Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS)**, and **Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA)** — replaced the IPC, CrPC and Indian Evidence Act from **1 July 2024**, and CLAT 2025 and 2026 papers already test the new section numbers.

- Track Supreme Court and High Court **landmark judgments** of the previous year — note bench strength, judge names, citation and date.
- Read **The Hindu / Indian Express** daily and a law outlet such as **LiveLaw, Bar & Bench, or Supreme Court Observer** for verified reporting.
- Distinguish **ratio decidendi** (binding law) from **obiter dicta** (judicial comments) when a judgment is summarised.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### What CLAT actually tests under "Legal News"

The Current Affairs section in CLAT is passage-based and factual, but Legal News questions are almost always grounded in three document types: a Supreme Court judgment, a recent Act of Parliament with presidential assent, or an Ordinance promulgated under **Article 123**. You must read the *legal issue*, not the headline. A question will frequently give you a 200-word factual passage about a ruling and then ask which Article, which section, or which doctrine was applied.

#### Mapping the core vocabulary

| Concept | Key point for CLAT |
| --- | --- |
| Ratio decidendi | The binding reason for the decision; only this is the "law declared" under Article 141. |
| Obiter dicta | Persuasive observations, not binding on lower courts. |
| PIL (Public Interest Litigation) | Filed under Article 32 (SC) or Article 226 (HC); CLAT tests whether admission or disposal is reported. |
| Article 142 | Complete justice power of the Supreme Court in pending matters. |
| Article 370 abrogation | Read with the 2019 Constitution (Application to Jammu and Kashmir) order; re-notified under the J&K Reorganisation Act. |
| Ordinance | Executive legislation under Article 123 (Centre) / Article 213 (State), valid for six weeks after Parliament reassembles. |

#### Reading strategy that actually works

A reliable weekly routine is to follow one national daily, one law-specific portal, and the Supreme Court "Today's Orders" page. When you log an item, write four lines under it: **issue, court/bench, provision interpreted, relief granted**.

- Convert every reported IPC, CrPC and IEA reference into the corresponding **BNS/BNSS/BSA section** — IPC 302 → BNS 302, IPC 376 → BNS 63, IPC 420 → BNS 318.
- Tag each item with a subject head — criminal, constitutional, commercial, environmental, family, labour, tax, cyber — because CLAT groups questions by topic clusters.
- Note **Law Commission Reports** and **Standing Committee recommendations** separately; they are advisory, not law.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge cases and traps examiners exploit

CLAT setters deliberately mix three traps in this section. First, they cite a **High Court division bench** ruling but frame the question as if it were a Supreme Court judgment, testing whether you read the attribution. Second, they quote the **old IPC section** while the offence now resides in the BNS after **1 July 2024**, catching candidates who studied only pre-2024 material. Third, they paraphrase **obiter dicta** as if it were the ratio, rewarding students who can tell binding law from judicial commentary.

#### Worked legal-news reading

Take a reported headline: *"Supreme Court holds that the GST Council's recommendations are not binding on the States."* A correct CLAT-ready note reads — **Issue:** federal fiscal autonomy under Article 246 read with Article 279A. **Bench:** Constitution Bench of five judges. **Provision interpreted:** Article 279A(4) — recommendation vs. decision-making power. **Relief:** States retain fiscal autonomy; Council recommendations are advisory. Writing this in four lines is what separates a topper from a middle-rank scorer.

#### Connections to adjacent CLAT areas

Legal News overlaps directly with **Constitution, Criminal Law, and Jurisprudence** in the Legal Reasoning section. A PIL question in Legal Reasoning may require you to recall a current affairs judgment. Same-sex marriage, **live-in relationships**, **PMLA** amendments, **RTI Act** amendments, and **ADR** notifications from NALSA are recurring clusters.

| Cluster | Recent anchor points |
| --- | --- |
| Criminal law transition | BNS 2023, BNSS 2023, BSA 2023 — effective 1 July 2024. |
| Constitutional benches | Article 370 abrogation, basic structure, GST Council. |
| Personal law | Same-sex marriage, live-in relationship, maintenance jurisprudence. |
| Governance | RTI Act amendments, PMLA, Election Commission notifications. |

#### Practice prompts

1. A passage states that "the Court observed the policy was unconstitutional." Rewrite it specifying whether the remark is **ratio** or **obiter**, and identify the Article interpreted.
2. A news item says "Parliament passed the X Bill, 2025." Identify whether it is now an **Act**, the date of presidential assent, and the implementing ministry — then map it to the relevant BNS/BNSS/BSA provision if it amends criminal procedure.
