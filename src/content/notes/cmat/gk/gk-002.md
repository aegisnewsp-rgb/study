# Medieval Indian History

### 🟢 Lite

Medieval India ran **three stories at once**: a Muslim-ruled north (the Delhi Sultanate, 1206–1526, then the Mughals, 1526–1857), a rival Deccan (Vijayanagara and Bahmani, 14th–17th c.), and a devotional tide (Bhakti and Sufi) that ignored both courts. CMAT's General Awareness section tests exactly these three threads.

#### The five Delhi Sultanate dynasties — one word each

| Dynasty | Years | One-word hook |
|---|---|---|
| **M**amluk (Slave) | 1206–1290 | Qutub Minar |
| **K**hilji | 1290–1320 | Alauddin's market control |
| **T**ughlaq | 1320–1414 | Token currency |
| **S**ayyid | 1414–1451 | Weak |
| **L**odi | 1451–1526 | Panipat |

**Memory hook — "MKTSL, 1206 → 1526":** read the initials M-K-T-S-L in order and the five boundary years run 1206, 1290, 1320, 1414, 1451, 1526. If you can recite that sequence you can place any Sultanate question in time before you answer it.

#### The four dates that organise the whole chapter

- **1192** — Second Battle of Tarain; Ghori beats Prithviraj Chauhan.
- **1526** — First Battle of Panipat; Babur ends the Sultanate and founds the Mughal Empire.
- **1565** — Battle of Talikota; Vijayanagara's capital Hampi is plundered and abandoned.
- **1757** — Battle of Plassey; the Mughal Empire becomes a British puppet.

### 🟡 Standard

#### How the Sultanate was built, and why it kept collapsing

Muhammad Ghori defeated Prithviraj Chauhan at the **Second Battle of Tarain (1192)**, and his slave-general **Qutb-ud-din Aibak** captured Delhi in **1206**. That year is the conventional starting point of the Delhi Sultanate.

**Iltutmish (1211–1236)** organised the **iqta** system — land grants to nobles in return for military service — and completed the Qutub Minar. **Balban (1266–1287)** tightened discipline, took the title *Sultan*, and created the **naib** (deputy governor) post to hold distant provinces.

**Muhammad bin Tughlaq (1325–1351)** is the dynasty's most examinable failure. He introduced **token currency** — bronze coins to replace silver — which was counterfeited at scale and wrecked the economy. He shifted the capital from Delhi to **Daulatabad** and forced the population to march. He disbanded much of the army. He still founded a **dak** (postal system) and protected scholars. Examiners like this ruler precisely because the verdict is mixed.

**Firoz Tughlaq (1351–1388)** reversed the experiment: reduced the land revenue demand, regranted land to nobles for service, and built Firoz Shah Kotla.

#### Why Babur won Panipat in 1526

Ibrahim Lodi fielded a numerically larger but poorly organised army. Babur's force was smaller, drilled, and used field artillery and the **tulughma** flanking formation. The margin was not manpower — it was gunpowder combined with discipline.

#### Sher Shah's reforms — the template Akbar copied

- Built the **Grand Trunk Road**, running Bengal to Afghanistan (over 2,500 km), still in use as the NH-1 corridor.
- Established the **dak** relay — horse-mounted couriers changing at fixed stations.
- Standardised the silver **rupiya**, from which the word "rupee" descends.
- Surveyed and recorded land to fix revenue demand, replacing iqta.

When Akbar took power in 1556 he inherited Sher Shah's administrative skeleton, not a vacuum.

#### Three powers, side by side

| Axis | Delhi Sultanate | Vijayanagara & Bahmani | Mughal Empire |
|---|---|---|---|
| Region | North India | Deccan plateau | North + Deccan |
| Years | 1206–1526 | 1336–1646 / 1347–1526 | 1526–1857 |
| Ruling elite | Turko-Afghan, then Mongol | Vijayanagara: Hindu; Bahmani: Muslim | Turco-Mongol |
| Power base | Land revenue + iqta | Temple economy + trade | Mansabdari + zabt |
| Turning point | Panipat 1526 | Talikota 1565 | Plassey 1757 |
| Ended by | Mughal invasion | Deccan Sultanate alliance | British political control |

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Vijayanagara and Bahmani look like one "Deccan" entry but they were enemies. Vijayanagara was Hindu and Bahmani was Muslim; they fought over the Krishna river region for most of two centuries, and only allied *after* 1565 to destroy Vijayanagara at Talikota. A question pairing them as allies before 1565 is wrong.

#### Architecture you can identify from the description alone

- **Qutub Minar** (73 m) — begun by Aibak, completed by Iltutmish; tallest brick minaret in the world.
- **Alai Darwaza** — Alauddin Khilji; first true dome in India.
- **Ghiyasi Masjid** — Tughlaq style: plain, massive, sloping towers, grey sandstone.
- **Balban's tomb**, Delhi — ornate arches.
- **Firoz Shah Kotla** — built by Firoz Tughlaq.

#### The Mughal line

**Babur (1526–1530)** — Timurid prince from Fergana, descended from Timur and Genghis Khan. Panipat 1526 beat Ibrahim Lodi; **Khanwa 1527** beat Rana Sanga of Mewar; **Ghaghra 1529** beat the Bengal–Awadh alliance. His memoir, the **Baburnama**, was written in Chagatai Turkish.

**Humayun (1530–1540, 1555–1556)** — lost at **Chausa 1539** and **Kannauj 1540** to Sher Shah, spent fifteen years at the Safavid court in Persia, and returned in 1555. He died in 1556 after a fall from his library terrace.

**Akbar (1556–1605)** — acceded at thirteen. At **Panipat 1556** his regent Bairam Khan defeated **Hemu**, who had won twenty consecutive engagements. Akbar's **mansabdari** ranked officials from 10 to 10,000, each rank carrying a *zat* (personal status) and a *sawar* (cavalry strength). He recorded agricultural data in the **Ain-i Akbari**, imposed the **zabt** land system, held debates at the **Ibadat Khana**, and introduced **Din-i Ilahi**. His Navratnas included Birbal, Abul Fazl, Faizi and Todar Mal.

**Jahangir (1605–1627)** — married **Nur Jahan** (Mehr-un-Nisa), who shaped the Taj Mahal's layout. His Persian autobiography, **Tuzuk-i Jahangiri**, is unusually candid. He executed Sikh Guru Arjan in 1606.

**Shah Jahan (1628–1658)** — the peak of Mughal architecture: the **Taj Mahal** (1632–1653), the **Red Fort**, and Delhi's **Jama Masjid**. Deccan wars against Bijapur and Golconda drained the treasury. Aurangzeb deposed him in 1658 and confined him to the Moti Masjid.

**Aurangzeb (1658–1707)** — reversed Akbar's toleration, reimposed the **jaziya**, and spent his reign fighting Marathas under Shivaji. His death in 1707 conventionally marks the empire's terminal phase.

#### How it unravelled

- **1739** — Nader Shah sacked Delhi and carried off the Peacock Throne and Koh-i-Noor.
- **1757** — Plassey: Clive bribed Mir Jafar to defeat Siraj-ud-Daulah.
- **1764** — Buxar: the British gained the right to collect Bengal revenue and govern.

#### Deccan kingdoms

**Vijayanagara (1336–1646)** — founded by Harihara I and Bukka Raya I; capital **Hampi** on the Tungabhadra, a UNESCO site since 1986. Its peak under **Krishna Deva Raya (1509–1529)**, a scholar who had the Vedas rendered into Telugu. Dravidian temples with towering **gopurams**, pillared *mandapas*, and temple tanks (*kalyanis*). Destroyed after **Talikota (1565)**.

**Bahmani (1347–1526)** — founded by Ala-ud-din Hasan Bahmani; capital Gulbarga, later Bidar. After 1526 it fragmented into **Bijapur, Golconda, Ahmadnagar, Berar and Bidar**, all absorbed by the Mughals in the 17th century.

#### Bhakti and Sufi currents

**Bhakti** stressed direct devotion over ritual and priestly mediation, and rejected caste.

| Saint | Region | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| Narsi Mehta | Gujarat | "Vaishnav Jan to" |
| Namdev | Maharashtra | Verses in Guru Granth Sahib |
| Ramananda | North India | Taught Kabir; equality of souls |
| Kabir (1440–1518) | Varanasi | Weaver; Hindi over Sanskrit; *Nirgun* |
| Guru Nanak (1469–1539) | Punjab | Founded Sikhism; "Ik Onkar"; *langar* |
| Tulsidas (1532–1623) | Varanasi | *Ramcharitmanas*; Hanuman Chalisa |
| Surdas (1478–1581) | Braj | Blind poet of Krishna; *Sur Sagar* |

**Sufism** entered India in the 12th century seeking direct experience of God through love. The **Chishti** order (Ajmer Sharif, via Khwaja Moin-ud-din Chishti) was the most influential; **Suhrawardi** was closer to courts; **Naqshbandi** was stricter on Sharia observance. Dargahs at Ajmer, Nizamuddin, Faridkot and Bulleh Shah drew followers across religious boundaries.

### 🔴 Extended

#### Worked item

**Question.** The Battle of Talikota (1565) is most directly associated with which outcome?

- (A) The end of the Delhi Sultanate
- (B) The plunder and abandonment of Hampi
- (C) The Mughal annexation of the Deccan
- (D) Babur's defeat at Khanwa

**Resolution.**
1. Talikota (1565) was fought in the Deccan against **Vijayanagara** by an alliance of the Deccan Sultanates.
2. Vijayanagara's capital **Hampi** was sacked and effectively destroyed, which ended the empire's power.
3. Check the distractors: (A) describes **Panipat 1526**; (D) describes **Khanwa 1527**; (C) describes Akbar's 17th-century campaigns after the Bahmani successor states weakened.
4. Only (B) survives.

**Answer: B**

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** candidates frequently swap **Hampi** (Vijayanagara, Karnataka, Tungabhadra) with **Daulatabad** (Muhammad bin Tughlaq's relocated capital, Maharashtra). They are 400 km apart and belong to different centuries. A second trap is treating Talikota as a Mughal battle — the Mughals were not a party to it.

#### Edges that change the answer

- **Sayyid and Lodi were weak, not absent.** Khizr Khan (Sayyid) held Delhi from 1414 and Sikandar Lodi expanded the empire before Ibrahim Lodi lost it. "Weak" in a mnemonic never means "skip the dynasty".
- **Din-i Ilahi was not a state religion.** It had a tiny following and Akbar never made it official. Present it as a personal experiment with debates at the Ibadat Khana.
- **Muhammad bin Tughlaq had genuine successes.** The note worth keeping alongside the failures is the *dak* — he did not invent it, but he extended it.
- **Two Battles of Panipat appear here.** 1526 (Babur over Ibrahim Lodi) and 1556 (Bairam Khan over Hemu). Both matter; neither is optional.
- **Sher Shah is not a Mughal.** He was an Afghan who briefly held Delhi between Humayun's reigns. Akbar's debt to him is administrative, not dynastic.

#### CMAT strategy

Medieval Indian history is high-yield in General Awareness because it is narrative — dates chain into causation. Learn the skeleton (1206 Sultanate → 1336 Vijayanagara → 1526 Mughals → 1757 Plassey), then attach one word per ruler. Most options in an exam are wrong because they attach the right ruler to the wrong achievement: Alauddin did not build the Qutub Minar, Aurangzeb did not found the Mansabdari, Bahmani did not ally Vijayanagara before 1565.
