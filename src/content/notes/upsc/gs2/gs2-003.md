# Social Justice

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

- **Social Justice** in the Indian constitutional sense is the triad of removing economic-social-political inequalities, **equalising opportunity**, and guaranteeing a **life with human dignity** — anchored in **Article 38(1)** of the DPSPs and operationalised through **Articles 14, 15, 16, 17, 21**.
- The **Preamble** resolves to secure "Social, Economic and Political Justice" — *social* specifically targets caste, class, gender, and disability-based hierarchies.
- The **Indra Sawhney (1992)** 9-judge verdict capped reservations at **50%** (with the **EWS 10%** carved out as a separate category by the **103rd Amendment, 2019**).
- **Creamy Layer exclusion** applies to OBCs and EWS — not to SCs/STs.
- Must-know commissions: **NCBC** (Art. 338B), **NCST** (Art. 338A), **NCW**, **NCM**, **NCRPD**.
- High-yield points: **Article 15(4)/15(5)/15(6)**, **Article 16(4)/16(4A)/16(4B)**, **Article 46**, **PESA 1996**, **FRA 2006**, **RTE 2009**, **NFSA 2013**, **JAM Trinity (Jan Dhan + Aadhaar + Mobile)**.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Constitutional Architecture

The Indian model of social justice rests on a **two-pillar design**. **Part III (Fundamental Rights)** supplies the justiciable guarantees, while **Part IV (DPSPs)** supplies the welfare-state roadmap. Article 14's *equality before law* is read with **Article 15 (non-discrimination)** and **Article 16 (equal opportunity in employment)** to permit, not contradict, **compensatory discrimination** for historically excluded groups.

The decisive judicial move was *State of Madras v. Champakam Dorairajan (1951)*, where the Supreme Court struck down caste-based reservation in education under Article 15(1). Parliament responded with the **1st Amendment (1951)** inserting **Article 15(4)** — the textual seed of protective discrimination. **Article 15(5)** (93rd Amendment, 2005) extended it to private-aided minority and professional institutions; **Article 15(6)** (103rd Amendment, 2019) added the **10% EWS quota** in higher education.

#### Reservation Framework

| Category | Provision | Cap | Creamy Layer? |
|---|---|---|---|
| SC/ST (vertical) | Art. 16(4), 330/332 | Aggregate 50% ceiling (Indra Sawhney) | No |
| OBC (vertical) | Art. 16(4) read with Indra Sawhney | 27% (Mandal, 1990) | Yes — *Jarnail Singh v. Lachhmi Narain Gupta* (2018) |
| EWS (vertical) | Art. 15(6) & 16(6), 103rd Amend. | 10% | Yes — *Janhit Abhiyan v. Union of India* (2022) upheld |
| Women (horizontal) | Art. 15(3) | Cuts across categories | N/A |

The **50% rule of Indra Sawhney** has exceptions: **extraordinary circumstances** (Tamil Nadu's 69% under the Ninth Schedule, now struck down except for the 50% portion) and **far-flung/remote areas** (*Balaji v. State of Mysore* legacy). *M. Nagaraj v. Union of India* (2006) held that the 50% ceiling is not inflexible but requires the State to demonstrate **quantifiable data** on backwardness, inadequacy of representation, and overall administrative efficiency.

#### Welfare-State Mechanisms

- **Article 46** mandates the State to promote the educational and economic interests of SCs/STs/weaker sections with special care.
- **Article 38(2)** obliges the State to minimise income inequalities and remove inequalities of status, opportunity and facility.
- **Article 39** lists equal pay, equal opportunity, and prevention of concentration of wealth.

The **JAM Trinity** (Jan Dhan–Aadhaar–Mobile) enables **Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT)** that has saved the exchequer over ₹2.7 lakh crore (figures cited in Economic Survey 2023-24) by cutting leakage — a structural social-justice reform, not merely a subsidy tool.

#### Exam Patterns

UPSC Prelims frames this through **Match-the-Following** on commissions, **Statement-Based MCQs** on creamy-layer rulings, and **constitutional-article pairing**. Mains GS-II questions typically demand a 150-word distinction between **equality, equity and dignity**, a 250-word critique of reservation policy, or a 15-marker essay linking social justice to **Sustainable Development Goals 1, 5 and 10**.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Doctrinal Tensions

The **horizontal vs vertical reservation** debate surfaced decisively in *Saurav Yadav v. State of UP* (2020), where the Allahabad High Court struck down UP's OBC quota within the OBC category as double dipping. The Supreme Court has clarified that women reservation under **Article 15(3)** and the recently enacted **Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam, 2023** (33% in Lok Sabha and Assemblies) is **horizontal** — it applies within each vertical category, not over it.

The **creamy layer** doctrine (*Indra Sawhney*, 1992) operates on the premise that once a backward class member attains a certain socio-economic threshold, the rationale for compensatory State action lapses. The ₹8 lakh income threshold (revised to ₹12 lakh, with asset conditions, in 2024) excludes only OBCs and EWS — a position criticised by the **National Commission for Backward Classes (NCBC)** as a *reverse discrimination* against the genuinely deprived within the OBC universe.

#### Interlinkages

- **Basic Structure Doctrine** (*Kesavananda Bharati*, 1973): the Supreme Court held that while reservation itself is not a basic feature, equality and the rule of law are — meaning reservation cannot destroy the *equality code* itself.
- **Article 21 expansion**: *Olga Tellis v. Bombay Municipal Corporation* (1985) read **right to livelihood** into Article 21, *Bandhua Mukti Morcha* (1984) read **right to live with dignity** to mean freedom from bonded labour, and *Unni Krishnan v. State of AP* (1993) made **right to education** (later codified as **RTE, 86th Amendment, 2002**) justiciable.
- **Tribal self-governance**: **PESA 1996** extends Part IX provisions to Scheduled Areas; **FRA 2006** grants forest-dwellers' rights over individual and community forest resources — both are social-justice instruments targeted at tribal marginalisation.
- **Gender Budgeting** in India (since 2005-06) tracks allocation across ministries; the **Beti Bachao Beti Padhao**, **Mahila Shakti Kendra**, and **Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana** triangulate with SDG 5 (Gender Equality).

#### Common Mistakes

- Conflating **equality** (formal, identical treatment) with **equity** (differential treatment to achieve substantive equality) — UPSC Mains specifically tests this distinction.
- Treating EWS as a *quota within OBC*; it is a **separate vertical category** (Janhit Abhiyan, 2022).
- Forgetting that **Article 17 abolishes untouchability** and the **SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989** (amended 2015, upheld in *Subhash Kashinath Mahajan*, 2018) carries stringent bail and anticipatory-bail restrictions.

#### Practice Prompts

1. "Reservation is a means, not an end." Critically examine in the context of the Indra Sawhney ceiling, the 103rd Amendment, and the 2024 *Janhit Abhiyan* review. (250 words)
2. Discuss how the JAM Trinity, SECC-based targeting, and Gender Budgeting collectively re-engineer welfare delivery as an instrument of social justice rather than a tool of patronage. (150 words)
