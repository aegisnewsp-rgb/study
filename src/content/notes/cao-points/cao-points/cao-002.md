# Higher Level Maths Bonus Points

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your CAO application.

**HL Maths Bonus Points** — Quick Facts for Irish Leaving Certificate students

**What is the bonus?**
If you take Higher Level Mathematics in the Leaving Certificate and achieve grade H6 or above, CAO adds 25 bonus points to your aggregate — exactly 25, applied once, regardless of whether you score H6, H5, H4, H3, H2, or H1. The bonus is not multiplied, not stacked, and not available at Ordinary Level.

**At a glance:**
- Bonus amount: +25 points, applied once
- Threshold: grade H6 or above in HL Maths (H6, H5, H4, H3, H2, H1 all qualify)
- Subject level: Higher Level Maths only (Ordinary Level Maths does NOT trigger the bonus)
- Cap: your total aggregate is capped at 625 points even after the bonus
- Application order: your six best subjects are summed first, then the +25 is added, then the 625 cap applies (see topic cao-003)

**Why it matters for offers:**
The 25-point bonus is the single biggest predictable points swing in the Irish admissions system. It can move you from below a course cut-off to comfortably above it without changing any of your other grades. Many Level 8 STEM programmes (engineering, science, computing, pharmacy, and some health-science programmes) expect applicants to have the bonus; some courses use it as a published course-specific requirement.

⚡ **Quick rule:** If you can realistically aim for H6 or above in HL Maths, the 25-point bonus is essentially free 25 points. The same 25 points would require moving an H3 to an H1 in another subject, or an O1 to an H2 — both substantially harder.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

**HL Maths Bonus Points** — CAO Points Study Guide

**Who qualifies for the bonus?**
You qualify if and only if you meet ALL THREE of these conditions:

1. **Subject level:** you sat Mathematics at **Higher Level** in the Leaving Certificate (not Ordinary Level, not Foundation Level).
2. **Grade threshold:** your final HL Maths grade is **H6 or above** — meaning H1, H2, H3, H4, H5, or H6.
3. **Application timing:** you apply to CAO by the published deadline and list at least one Level 7 or Level 8 undergraduate course in your course choices.

**The bonus is exactly +25, regardless of grade band:**
| HL Maths grade | Bonus points |
|----------------|-------------:|
| H1 | +25 |
| H2 | +25 |
| H3 | +25 |
| H4 | +25 |
| H5 | +25 |
| H6 | +25 |
| H7 | 0 |
| H8 | 0 |

The bonus does NOT scale with grade — a H6 in HL Maths earns the same +25 as a H1. CAO deliberately structured the bonus this way so that the threshold (H6 or above) is the only thing that matters. There is no "bonus for bonus" for higher grades.

**What the bonus is added to:**
The +25 is added to your raw six-best-subjects total BEFORE the 625-point cap is applied. The sequence is:

1. Convert each of your Leaving Certificate subject grades into CPS values (see topic cao-001).
2. Pick your **six best** subjects (excluding Foundation Level Maths entirely — see cao-004).
3. Sum those six CPS values into a raw aggregate.
4. **Add the +25 HL Maths bonus IF** you achieved H6 or above in HL Maths.
5. Cap the result at **625** (the maximum aggregate; see the cap section below).

**What the bonus is NOT:**
- It is **not** added at Ordinary Level. OL Maths at any grade does NOT trigger the bonus.
- It is **not** added at Foundation Level. No Foundation grade earns the HL Maths bonus — F1, F2 and F3 all sit outside it.
- It is **not** added per-subject. It is exactly +25, applied once across the entire aggregate, regardless of how many other maths modules you take.
- It is **not** added to international applicants whose maths is presented under a non-Leaving Certificate scheme (GCE A-Level Maths, GCSE Maths, QQI FET Maths — see the international-applicants section below).

**The 625-point cap:**
The maximum CAO aggregate is 625 points. This is the sum of six H1 grades (6 × 100 = 600) PLUS the +25 HL Maths bonus (600 + 25 = 625). The cap means:

- Six H1s is the only way to reach 600 before the bonus, and 600 + 25 lands exactly on 625. So the cap never actually clips anyone — it defines the ceiling rather than truncating a lower total.
- If your six best subjects sum to anything below 600, the cap is simply irrelevant and your aggregate is six-subject-sum + 25.

In other words "capped at 625" is not a rule you can be caught out by. It only ever describes a perfect six-subject sheet.

**Worked example 1:** Grades: H1, H1, H2, H2, H3, H3, H4 in seven subjects (six best counted). Six-best CPS = 100 + 100 + 88 + 88 + 77 + 77 = 530. HL Maths is H1 — bonus applies. Aggregate = 530 + 25 = 555. Cap does not apply (555 < 625).

**Worked example 2:** Grades: H1, H1, H1, H1, H1, H1 in six subjects. Six-best CPS = 6 × 100 = 600. HL Maths is H1 — bonus applies. Raw aggregate = 600 + 25 = 625. **Capped at 625.**

**Worked example 3 — the bonus depends on Maths landing in your six:**
Grades: English H1, Irish H1, Biology H1, Chemistry H1, Physics H1, History H1, plus Maths Higher Level H2 as a seventh subject. The six highest are 6 × 100 = **600**. Maths H2 is worth 88, the seventh value, so it is not counted — and because Mathematics is not one of the best six, **the bonus is not added either.** Aggregate = **600**.

Move Maths into the six and the same candidate scores 625. Replace six H1s with five H1s and Maths H1, and the six become 100 + 100 + 100 + 100 + 100 + 100 = 600 with the bonus added → **625**.

So the bonus is not a flat award for sitting HL Maths. It is conditional: you must achieve H6 or above *and* Mathematics must be one of the six subjects counted. A candidate with a strong maths grade but six stronger subjects elsewhere does not collect it — which is why Maths is often the subject to upgrade when you already sit it.

**When the bonus was introduced:**
The +25 HL Maths bonus is not a recent addition. It was carried into the points-based offer system as a fixed 25 points at grade H6 and above, and it predates the 2017 Common Points Scale change of grid values. Every CAO applicant is scored on the same +25 rule — there is no in-year variation.

**Course-specific treatment:**
Many Level 8 STEM programmes (engineering, science, computing, pharmacy, some health-science programmes) use the HL Maths bonus as a course-specific entry requirement. Some programmes require that the bonus be applied to the published course cut-off — meaning the published cut-off already has +25 baked in for HL Maths applicants. The CAO Handbook publishes the course-specific requirements each year; always read the handbook for your specific course.

**Foundation Level Maths applicants:**
Foundation Level Maths does NOT earn the bonus and does NOT count toward the six-best-subjects aggregate. Some Level 8 STEM courses require either a pass in OL Maths at O6 or above, OR a minimum grade in Foundation Maths, as a matriculation requirement (see cao-005). Foundation Level Maths applicants who do not meet the OL/HL requirement for a STEM course are not eligible for the bonus under any pathway.

**International applicants:**
The HL Maths bonus applies ONLY to applicants who took the Irish Leaving Certificate with Mathematics at Higher Level. Applicants presenting GCE A-Levels, GCSEs, QQI FET/FETAC awards, or other international qualifications do not get the +25 under the Irish-Leaving-Certificate rule. Each non-CE pathway has its own scoring grid (published by CAO) which may or may not include its own bonus points — always check the parallel-scoring rules for your specific qualification entry route.


### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> For students deciding how hard to push HL Maths, and whether switching level is worth it.

**HL Maths Bonus Points** — Advanced CAO Points Analysis

**The grading-direction trap — the most common error on this topic:**
The Leaving Cert scale runs *downwards*. H1 is the highest grade and H8 the lowest passing grade. So when the rule says "H6 or above", it means the six grades H1 through H6 — and an H4 is *better* than an H6, so an H4 qualifies. Only **H7 and H8** miss out.

Students read "H6 or above" as the numbers 6 and upwards, conclude that an H4 is too low, and give up 25 points for nothing. The bonus does not scale with the grade; every qualifying grade earns exactly +25.

| HL Maths grade | CPS value | Bonus earned |
|---|---:|---:|
| H1 | 100 | +25 |
| H2 | 88 | +25 |
| H3 | 77 | +25 |
| H4 | 66 | +25 |
| H5 | 56 | +25 |
| H6 | 46 | +25 |
| H7 | 37 | 0 |
| H8 | 0 | 0 |

**What the bonus is worth in grade terms:**
+25 is roughly two and a half HL steps in a single subject. H6 (46) to H4 (66) is +20; H6 to H3 (77) is +31. The bonus sits between those two jumps, which makes it larger than any single one-band move available anywhere on the scale.

**Is switching from OL Maths to HL Maths worth it?**
Run the comparison as effective points, not raw grades:

| Route | Best case | Worst qualifying case |
|---|---:|---:|
| OL Maths | O1 = 56, no bonus | 56 |
| HL Maths | H1 = 100, +25 → 125 | H6 = 46, +25 → **71** |

The lowest bonus-qualifying HL result (71 effective) still beats the *highest possible* OL result (56) by 15 points. So any HL Maths result at H6 or above is worth more than any OL Maths result, before you count the higher ceiling.

The decision is still not automatic, because the risk runs the other way: a candidate who would comfortably score an O1 at OL and is likely to land H7 or H8 at HL scores 37 or 0 and has lost 19 to 56 points. Switching pays if H6+ is realistic; it costs if it is not.

**Foundation Maths is a different question again:** no Foundation grade earns the bonus, but Foundation Maths is not necessarily worthless for points — the grid awards F1 = 20 and F2 = 12 (F3 = 0), and CAO states these are awarded by certain institutions rather than all. Where an institution does award them, Foundation Maths can take a place in the six; where it does not, it contributes nothing. Foundation Maths remains a coherent choice mainly where your target courses accept it as a matriculation requirement — so check the points treatment and the entry requirement separately, per course.

**The bonus has two conditions, not one:**
You must achieve H6 or above **and** Mathematics must be among the six subjects counted. CAO's published guidance is explicit that the bonus is included in the overall calculation only where Higher Level Mathematics is one of the applicant's best six subjects. A candidate whose maths grade is their seventh-best result does not collect it, however strong that grade is — the sixth-best subject displaces both the grade and the bonus.

This is the rule most often misremembered, and it changes the decision in cao-003: with a fixed set of results, moving Maths into the counted six is worth 25 points even before any grade improvement.

**Headroom arithmetic against a cut-off:**
Cut-offs move each year, so the useful question is margin rather than a yes/no verdict:
- If the bonus is the only thing standing between you and a cut-off, maths is by a wide margin the highest-leverage subject on your sheet.
- If you are 15 points short *after* the bonus, no maths outcome closes that gap on its own — you need a band elsewhere. Knowing which situation you are in stops you from re-sitting or re-targeting maths for no gain.
- Treat a published cut-off as a floor to clear, not a target to graze. Offer rounds move.

**What the bonus does not do:**
- It does not stack, multiply, or scale with grade.
- It is not paid twice for two maths modules.
- It does not apply to OL Maths, Foundation Maths, the QQI FET/FETAC route, or GCE A-Level/GCSE applicants.
- It does not satisfy a course's own minimum-grade requirement in Mathematics — entry requirements are assessed separately from the aggregate.

**Verify before you commit:** the bonus threshold, the grid values and the 625 cap are all on the single CAO scoring page listed under Official Resources. The bonus rule has been stable for many years, but course-specific requirements change annually — read the current handbook for your course.

### 💡 Pro Tips
- The threshold H6 in HL Maths unlocks +25 — there is no point trying to claw back +25 by improving a different subject, it is one of the best point-arbitrage opportunities in the Irish system
- Take HL Maths if you can realistically achieve H6 — even a H6 in HL Maths + the +25 bonus is worth more points than a H1 in many other subjects
- Plan your six best subjects around the +25 — if you are at or near the cap, an extra H1 in another subject is wasted (capped at 625) but the +25 still applies
- Read the CAO Handbook for your specific course — some STEM programmes REQUIRE HL Maths at a specific threshold as a course entry condition, independent of the +25 bonus
- The Common Points Scale Calculator (cao.ie/index.php?bb=cps&page=newcps) lets you model your aggregate with and without the +25 bonus
