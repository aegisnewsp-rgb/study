# Ledger Posting

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Ledger posting** moves each debit and credit entry from the **journal** and the **daybooks** into the matching accounts in the **general ledger**, so that every T-account carries its up-to-date running balance. Under the **double-entry rule**, each transaction produces equal debits and credits, and posting simply honours that rule inside the ledger. Daybook totals — **sales daybook**, **purchases daybook**, **sales returns daybook**, **purchases returns daybook** — are transferred at period-end, with credit sales posted to the **Sales Account** (cr) and the **Trade Receivables Control Account** (dr), and credit purchases to **Purchases** (dr) and the **Trade Payables Control Account** (cr). After posting, every account is **balanced c/d**, the figure carried as **balance b/d** to start the next period, and the balances are listed in the **trial balance**. Two exam pointers: (1) remember that a contra entry touches *both* sides of the ledger — never record it once, and (2) credit balances mean a liability, equity, or income; debit balances mean an asset or expense. In ACCA/CA Pakistan papers, posting errors typically surface as a disagreement in the suspense account during the trial balance.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### The Posting Workflow

Posting is the bridge between the **prime books of entry** — the journal and the daybooks — and the **ledger**. Each transaction enters the ledger as a debit in one account and a credit in another, in equal amounts. When entries reach the ledger from the journal, the **date** of the journal entry is carried forward, the amount is written on the correct side of the named account, and the **ledger folio (LF)** column of the journal is filled in with the ledger page number to maintain a clear cross-reference.

#### Posting from the Daybooks

Analysed daybooks avoid one-line-per-entry clutter in the ledger:

| Daybook | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Sales daybook total | Trade Receivables Control | Sales |
| Purchases daybook total | Purchases | Trade Payables Control |
| Sales returns daybook total | Sales Returns | Trade Receivables Control |
| Purchases returns daybook total | Trade Payables Control | Purchases Returns |
| Cash book (receipts side) | Cash | Relevant account (dr source) |
| Cash book (payments side) | Relevant account | Bank |

Totals are struck at the end of the accounting period (monthly or weekly in practice).

#### Balancing and the Trial Balance

After all postings are complete, each account is closed by totalling both sides. The side with the smaller total is increased by the **balance c/d** — the balancing figure — so the two columns agree. The same balance appears on the opposite side as **balance b/d** when a new period opens.

#### Common Pitfalls

- Swapping dr/cr when posting, leading to a trial-balance error and a **suspense account** entry.
- Postings made only on one side for **contra entries** such as cash banked immediately or set-offs between a debtor and creditor. Both sides must be recorded.
- Forgetting to transfer daybook totals to the **control accounts**, which makes the personal-ledger control and the general-ledger control fail to agree.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Contra Entries: The Two-Sided Edge Case

A **contra entry** is unique because it touches two ledger accounts but bypasses the double-entry logic of “one debit, one credit in different accounts.” Depositing cash straight into the bank, for example, debits **Bank** and credits **Cash**. The transaction is recorded in both the debit and credit sides of the *cash book* and is also posted into the ledger on the same date, normally as a **C** in the journal’s LF column. Omitting one side produces a half-posting and a disagreement in the trial balance — examiners love testing this.

#### Subsidiary Ledgers vs the General Ledger

Large organisations split the ledger:

- The **general ledger** holds nominal accounts (income, expenses) and real accounts (assets, liabilities, equity).
- **Subsidiary ledgers** hold the detailed personal accounts of every debtor and creditor, controlled by the **Trade Receivables Control Account** and **Trade Payables Control Account** in the general ledger.

The control account’s balance must equal the aggregate of the subsidiary ledger balances; reconciling them is a frequent practical-task question.

#### Edge Cases Worth Memorising

- Opening a new accounting period requires b/d entries before new postings; the previous c/d automatically becomes the next b/d, so **do not** book the opening balance twice.
- A **compound journal entry** posting must be split across multiple ledger accounts on the correct sides — the total of all debits still equals the total of all credits.
- Discounts allowed/received appear in the **discounts allowed** and **discounts received** accounts, not netted into receivables/payables.

#### Common Mistakes

- Posting a discount column total as a single figure when it actually belongs to two nominal accounts.
- Treating an end-of-year **stock** adjustment as a ledger account — closing inventory is a year-end adjustment on the trial balance, not a posted ledger balance.
- Forgetting to date the posting, which breaks the audit trail even if the figures are correct.

#### Practice Prompts

1. Given a list of daybook totals and a contra entry, prepare the postings to the relevant control accounts and show the resulting balances at period-end.
2. From a suspense account figure, identify which type of posting error (transposition, single-sided omission, contra-entry omission) is most likely and show the correcting entries.
