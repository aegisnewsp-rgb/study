# Nepal's Legal System and Court Structure

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Nepal's legal system is a **mixed (hybrid) civil-law tradition** anchored by the Constitution of Nepal 2015. It blends statutory acts of Parliament, codified laws (Muluki Dewani Samhita 1910 BS replaced by Civil Code 2074 BS), Supreme Court precedent (नजिर), and customary law (जात्री/कुटुम्बी प्रथा). The court architecture is a **three-tier unitary hierarchy**: District Court (first instance) → High Court (appellate, 7 in number) → Supreme Court (apex, single national body).

- **Apex court:** Supreme Court (सर्वोच्च अदालत), Kathmandu — only court empowered to issue the five constitutional writs under Article 133.
- **Sources of law (descending authority):** Constitution → Acts of Parliament → Case law (नजिर) → Customary law → Justice, equity, good conscience.
- **Writs (अनुच्छेद 133):** Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, Quo Warranto.
- **Key body:** Judicial Council (न्यायिक परिषद्) — appoints, transfers, and recommends the Chief Justice.
- **Anti-corruption body (NOT a court):** CIAA — Commission for the Investigation of Abuse of Authority.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** "**DHS**" = **D**istrict → **H**igh → **S**upreme (the three tiers, bottom-up). For writs, memorise "**He Met Pro Cert & Quo**" = Habeas, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, Quo Warranto — the five constitutional remedies exclusive to the Supreme Court.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Sources of Nepali Law in Hierarchy

| Rank | Source | Authority Status | Example |
|------|--------|------------------|---------|
| 1 | Constitution of Nepal 2015 | Supreme / overriding | Article 133 (writs) |
| 2 | Acts of Parliament (Statutes) | Binding if constitutional | Civil Code 2074 BS |
| 3 | Supreme Court precedent (नजिर) | Persuasive, followed by lower courts | Reported SC decisions |
| 4 | Customary law (जात्री/कुटुम्बी) | Valid only if not repugnant to Constitution | Community dispute practices |
| 5 | Justice, equity, good conscience | Gap-filler when law silent | Used rarely, residually |

The Constitution overrides every contradictory source. A statute struck down by the Supreme Court under **judicial review** becomes void *ab initio* — a hallmark feature tested in LOE Nepal objective questions.

#### Court Structure at a Glance

| Tier | Court | Number | Jurisdiction |
|------|-------|--------|--------------|
| Apex | Supreme Court | 1 (Kathmandu) | Constitutional interpretation, final appeal, writs, advisory |
| Appellate | High Court (उच्च अदालत) | 7 (with benches) | Provincial appellate + supervisory over District Courts |
| First Instance | District Court (जिल्ला अदालत) | 77 districts | Original civil, criminal, and specialised matters |
| Special | Tribunals & Special Courts | Multiple | Revenue, Labour, Election, Administrative, Debt Recovery, Special Court (Corruption) |

The seven High Courts sit at Biratnagar, Janakpur, Hetauda, Patan (Lalitpur), Pokhara, Nepalgunj, and Surkhet; the Supreme Court has constitutional benches and a full bench division.

#### Constitutional Writs — Article 133

1. **Habeas Corpus** — "produce the body"; illegal detention remedy.
2. **Mandamus** — "we command"; orders a public official to perform duty.
3. **Prohibition** — prevents an inferior court from exceeding jurisdiction.
4. **Certiorari** — transfers a case from lower court to Supreme Court.
5. **Quo Warranto** — questions the legality of holding a public office.

Only the Supreme Court can issue these. High Courts cannot — a frequent LOE MCQ distractor.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** A citizen of Nepal is illegally detained by a police officer for 72 hours without producing him before a magistrate. Which constitutional writ, and which court, should his advocate file?
(A) Mandamus in the High Court
(B) Habeas Corpus in the Supreme Court
(C) Certiorari in the District Court
(D) Quo Warranto in the High Court

**Solution:** Identify the grievance: **illegal detention** → remedy is **Habeas Corpus** ("produce the body"). Identify the writ-issuing authority: under Article 133 of the Constitution of Nepal 2015, **only the Supreme Court** can issue the five writs. Therefore the correct option is **(B) Habeas Corpus in the Supreme Court**.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students frequently pick Mandamus because they confuse "commanding release" with Mandamus. Mandamus orders performance of a public duty; it does **not** release a detainee. Also watch for options offering "Habeas Corpus in the High Court" — High Courts lack writ jurisdiction under Article 133.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Historical Evolution of Nepali Law

| Year (BS / AD) | Code / Statute | Significance |
|----------------|----------------|--------------|
| 1910 BS (1854 AD) | **Muluki Dewani Samhita** | First unified civil code under Jung Bahadur Rana |
| 1974 BS (1917 AD) | **Muluki Aparadh Samhita** | Unified criminal/penal code |
| 2007 BS (1950 AD) | First codified procedural laws | Beginning of modern statutory framework |
| 2047 BS (1990 AD) | Constitution of Nepal 1990 | Multi-party democracy, independent judiciary |
| 2072 BS (2015 AD) | **Constitution of Nepal 2015** | Federal structure, current supreme law |
| 2074 BS (2017 AD) | **Civil Code 2074** | Replaced Muluki Dewani Samhita of 1910 BS |

> ⚠️ **High-Yield Revision Hook:** When LOE MCQs reference "1910 BS Civil Code", the answer is **Muluki Dewani Samhita**, not the Constitution. The current Civil Code is **2074 BS (2017 AD)**.

#### Advanced Traps and Exceptions

1. **CIAA is NOT a court** — the Commission for the Investigation of Abuse of Authority is an executive anti-corruption body; it cannot adjudicate cases. The "Special Court" (विशेष अदालत) handles CIAA-prosecuted corruption cases.
2. **One Supreme Court only** — Federal restructuring did NOT create provincial supreme courts. There is a single apex court sitting in Kathmandu with benches.
3. **Customary law rank** — Customary practices yield to any contradictory statute or constitutional provision; they sit below statute in hierarchy.
4. **Writ exclusivity** — High Courts exercise supervisory jurisdiction under Articles 138–141 but **cannot** issue the five Article 133 writs.
5. **Judicial Council composition** — Chaired by the Chief Justice, with the Minister for Law, senior Supreme Court Justice, two legal-scholarly members, and the Attorney General — appoints and recommends judicial appointments per Article 142.

#### Connections and Cross-References

- **Preamble of Constitution 2015** explicitly adopts **Rule of Law** and **Due Process of Law** — frequently tested as GK statements.
- **Article 33 (Right to Constitutional Remedies)** guarantees free legal aid for indigent litigants and direct access to the Supreme Court for writ petitions.
- **Article 144** empowers the Supreme Court to render **advisory opinions** to the President on constitutional questions — a unique jurisdiction rarely found in civil-law systems.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. **Sequence question:** Arrange in ascending hierarchy of authority: Supreme Court precedent, Constitution, Customary law, Acts of Parliament, Justice-equity-good-conscience.
2. **Identification question:** Match each High Court location (Biratnagar, Pokhara, Nepalgunj) with the province it primarily serves under the federal structure — and identify which High Court also acts as the **Revenue Tribunal** appellate bench.
