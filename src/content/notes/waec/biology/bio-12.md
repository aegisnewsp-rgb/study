# Growth and Development

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Growth** is a permanent, quantitative increase in size, mass, or volume of an organism, driven by **mitosis** (cell division) and **cell enlargement**. **Development** is the qualitative, progressive change in structure, function, and complexity that an organism undergoes from zygote to maturity, involving **cell differentiation** and **morphogenesis**.

- **Growth rate** = (final size − initial size) / time, expressed in cm/day or g/day.
- **Relative growth rate** = (change in size / initial size) / time, expressed in day⁻¹, useful for comparing organisms of different initial sizes.
- The growth curve is typically **sigmoid (S-shaped)**: lag → log (exponential) → stationary phases.
- Plant growth is **indeterminate** (continuous at **apical** and **lateral meristems**); animal growth is **determinate** (genetically limited).
- **Auxins** and **gibberellins** are plant hormones that promote cell elongation and stem growth.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Distinction Between Growth and Development

Growth is **measurable and irreversible** — you can record a seedling's height in centimetres or mass in grams and confirm an increase over days. Development is **descriptive and qualitative** — you describe the formation of new tissues, organs, and physiological capabilities that did not exist before (e.g., a seedling producing flowers, a caterpillar acquiring wings).

#### The Three Stages of Growth

1. **Cell division (mitosis)** in meristematic regions increases cell number.
2. **Cell enlargement** expands each daughter cell through water uptake (vacuolation in plants) and cytoplasmic synthesis.
3. **Cell differentiation** specialises cells into tissues such as xylem, phloem, or muscle.

#### Measurement of Growth

| Parameter | Tool / Method | Accuracy Note |
|---|---|---|
| Length / height | Ruler, metre rule, calibrated graph paper | Suitable for stems, roots, seedlings |
| Fresh mass | Electronic balance | Includes water — fluctuates daily |
| **Dry mass** | Oven at 80–105 °C until constant mass | **Most accurate** growth measure |
| Volume | Water displacement, eureka can | Used for irregular organs |

> **Exam tip:** WASSCE examiners prefer "dry mass" as the correct answer when asked which method gives the truest measure of growth, because fresh mass changes with hydration.

#### Sigmoid Growth Curve

A typical population or organism produces an **S-shaped curve** when size is plotted against time. The **lag phase** shows slow initial growth, the **log (exponential) phase** shows rapid increase, and the **stationary phase** shows growth slowing as resources deplete or maturity is reached. WASSCE questions often ask candidates to label these phases on a graph.

#### Plant vs Animal Growth

| Feature | Plants | Animals |
|---|---|---|
| Growth pattern | **Indeterminate** (continuous) | **Determinate** (stops at maturity) |
| Growth zones | Apical & lateral meristems | Diffuse — most tissues |
| Hormones | Auxins, gibberellins, cytokinins | Growth hormone (somatotropin), thyroxine |
| Examples | Bamboo keeps elongating yearly | Humans stop growing after adolescence |

#### Typical WASSCE Question Patterns

- "Differentiate between growth and development in two sentences" (2 marks).
- "State three regions of cell division in a flowering plant" — apical, lateral, intercalary meristems.
- "List four factors affecting growth" — nutrients, water, light, temperature, hormones, genetics.
- "Sketch and label a sigmoid growth curve" (3–4 marks).

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Allometric vs Isometric Growth

Not all parts of an organism grow at the same rate. **Isometric growth** occurs when body parts grow at the same proportional rate (e.g., a fish larva enlarging uniformly). **Allometric growth** occurs when different parts grow at different rates — the human brain reaches 90 % of its adult size by age 5, while the reproductive organs grow mainly during puberty. WASSCE Theory questions sometimes describe an insect whose wings enlarge disproportionately after the final moult to test understanding of allometry.

#### Metamorphosis as Development

Metamorphosis is a **developmental**, not a growth, event because it reshapes form and function. **Complete metamorphosis** (egg → larva → pupa → adult) occurs in butterflies, mosquitoes, and houseflies. **Incomplete metamorphosis** (egg → nymph → adult) occurs in cockroaches and grasshoppers. Amphibians such as the toad show metamorphosis from aquatic, gilled tadpole to terrestrial, lunged adult.

#### Senescence — The Final Stage

**Senescence** (ageing) is the terminal phase of development, marked by declining metabolic activity, reduced cell division, and programmed tissue death. In plants it appears as autumn leaf fall (abscission), fruit ripening, and bark shedding. Confusing senescence with disease is a common essay error — senescence is genetically programmed, while disease is environmentally induced.

#### Roles of Key Plant Hormones

| Hormone | Site of Production | Principal Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Auxin (IAA)** | Shoot apex | Cell elongation, apical dominance |
| **Gibberellin** | Young leaves, roots | Stem elongation, seed germination |
| **Cytokinin** | Roots | Cell division, delay of senescence |
| **Abscisic acid** | Leaves, root cap | Inhibits growth, promotes dormancy |
| **Ethylene** | Ripening fruit, ageing tissues | Fruit ripening, leaf abscission |

> **Examiner trap:** WASSCE essay questions frequently test whether candidates confuse **abscisic acid (inhibitor)** with **auxin (promoter)**. Memorising the function of each hormone by name prevents easy marks lost.

#### Common Mistakes Students Make

- Treating **germination** as development alone — it is the resumption of both growth and development in the embryo.
- Saying growth stops at puberty in plants — only determinate animal growth stops; plants continue until death.
- Confusing **cell enlargement** with **cell elongation against gravity** (geotropism) — enlargement is a growth process, tropism is a directional response.

#### Practice Prompts

1. A student recorded the heights of 20 bean seedlings daily for 14 days. Explain why plotting the **mean dry mass** would be a more reliable indicator of growth than mean height.
2. A grasshopper emerges from its final moult with disproportionately long hind legs. Identify the type of growth pattern illustrated and justify your answer with two reasons.
