# World Geography

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

World Geography for CMAT tests factual recall of Earth's physical and political layout. Earth's surface is **510.1 million km²**, of which **~71% is water** across five oceans and **~29% land** across seven continents. The framework rests on **latitude** (0°–90° N/S, measures distance north/south of the Equator) and **longitude** (0°–180° E/W, measures distance east/west of the Prime Meridian through Greenwich, UK).

| Key parallel / meridian | Latitude / Longitude | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| Equator | 0° latitude | Divides Earth into Northern and Southern hemispheres |
| Tropic of Cancer | 23.5° N | Northern limit of Sun's vertical overhead |
| Tropic of Capricorn | 23.5° S | Southern limit of Sun's vertical overhead |
| Arctic / Antarctic Circle | 66.5° N / 66.5° S | Boundary of polar day/night |
| Prime Meridian | 0° longitude | Reference for Greenwich Mean Time |

- **Highest land:** Mt. Everest (8,848.86 m, Nepal-China border)
- **Lowest land:** Dead Sea shoreline (~−430 m)
- **Longest river:** Nile (~6,650 km); **Amazon** carries the most water
- **Largest country:** Russia; **most populous:** India (since 2023)
- **Earth rotates 360° in 24 hours → 15° longitude = 1 hour time difference**

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"CAPE"** for the five parallels from the Equator outward — **C**ancer, **A**rctic, **P**olar (Antarctic), **E**quator — paired with **23.5, 66.5, 23.5, 0**. Mnemonic for the seven continents: **"Eat An Aspirin After A Nice Tasty Sandwich"** = Europe, Asia, Africa, Australia (Australasia), Antarctica, North America, South America.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Coordinate System and Time

Latitude lines run **east-west** and measure angular distance north or south of the Equator; they never converge. Longitude lines (meridians) run **north-south** and converge at the poles. The Earth rotates 360° in 24 hours, so the local-time difference between two meridians equals their longitude difference **× 4 minutes per degree**. India uses a single time zone based on **82.5° E** (IST = UTC + 5:30) despite spanning about 30° of longitude.

#### Climate Belts and Controls

Climate is driven by latitude, altitude, distance from the sea, ocean currents, and prevailing winds. The **Köppen system** groups climates into tropical, arid, temperate, continental, and polar categories. The **Mediterranean climate** (dry summer, wet winter) hugs the Mediterranean Sea, California, central Chile, Cape Town, and southwestern Australia — opposite in phase to the **monsoon climate** (wet summer, dry winter) of South and Southeast Asia.

#### Tectonics and Landforms

The lithosphere is split into **seven major plates**: Pacific, North American, South American, Eurasian, African, Indo-Australian, and Antarctic. Plate boundaries generate the **Pacific Ring of Fire** (≈90% of the world's earthquakes and 75% of active volcanoes), the Mid-Atlantic Ridge, and the Himalayan uplift (India–Eurasia collision).

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** Two cities, X (located at 30° W) and Y (located at 45° E), are observed simultaneously. If local solar time at X is 12:00 noon, what is the local solar time at Y?

#### Solution:
1. Longitude difference between Y and X = 45° E − (−30° W) = **75° eastward**.
2. Time gained moving east = 75° × 4 minutes/° = **300 minutes = 5 hours**.
3. Local time at Y = 12:00 + 5 hours = **17:00 (5:00 PM)**.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students often subtract instead of add when moving east, or forget that longitude values carry sign (east is +, west is −). Direction matters: **eastward = time ahead**, **westward = time behind**.

#### Geopolitical Blocs and Resource Geography

CMAT frequently links geography with current affairs. The **G20** (19 countries + EU + AU) drives macroeconomic coordination; **BRICS** (Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa, plus new members from 2024) represents emerging economies; **OPEC** (Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries) controls about 40% of global oil supply. **Strategic straits and canals** to memorise:

| Strait / Canal | Connects | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Suez Canal | Mediterranean ↔ Red Sea | Europe–Asia trade shortcut |
| Panama Canal | Atlantic ↔ Pacific | Americas maritime shortcut |
| Strait of Hormuz | Persian Gulf ↔ Gulf of Oman | ~20% of global oil transit |
| Strait of Malacca | Indian Ocean ↔ South China Sea | China/Japan oil lifeline |
| Strait of Gibraltar | Atlantic ↔ Mediterranean | Entry to Mediterranean basin |
| Bab-el-Mandeb | Red Sea ↔ Gulf of Aden | Suez traffic chokepoint |

#### Edge Cases and Common Examiner Traps

1. **Australia lies in BOTH the Eastern and Southern hemispheres** — questions saying "the country entirely in the Southern Hemisphere" exclude it; those saying "entirely in the Eastern Hemisphere" also exclude it.
2. **Greenwich Meridian supremacy** is only by 1884 international agreement; France used the Paris Meridian until 1911. Don't assume "universal" = "historically uncontested."
3. **Tropic of Cancer passes through 16 countries** including India (through eight states: Gujarat, Rajasthan, MP, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura, Mizoram). Don't confuse the Tropic of Capricorn's 23.5° S with the Arctic Circle's 66.5° N.
4. **Amazon vs Nile length** is a perennial trap. Traditional textbooks cite **Nile ≈ 6,650 km** as longest; some 2007+ studies argue Amazon is longer when including its Apurímac source streams — answer according to **NCERT/standard school geography** unless the question specifies.
5. **Largest desert = Antarctica** (cold desert), not the Sahara; the Sahara is the largest **hot** desert.
6. **Pacific Ocean contains the deepest point** (Mariana Trench, ~10,994 m at Challenger Deep), but the **Atlantic is the busiest trade corridor**.
7. **IST vs GMT:** India is 5 hours 30 minutes ahead of GMT — the half-hour offset is unusual and frequently tested.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. A cargo ship leaves Lagos (3° E) on 1 June at 08:00 local time and sails to New York (74° W). Ignoring travel duration, what is the corresponding local time and date in New York at the moment of departure? *(Answer: longitude gap = 77° westward → 308 minutes = 5 h 8 min behind → 02:52, 31 May.)*
2. Why does the Atacama Desert (≈20° S, Chile coast) receive almost no rainfall despite lying near the Equator-side Tropic? *(Answer: cold Peruvian Humboldt Current, rain-shadow from the Andes, and subtropical high-pressure belt — a classic cold-current desert trap.)*
