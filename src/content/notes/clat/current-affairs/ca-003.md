# Legal News

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Legal News in CLAT covers recent judicial pronouncements, parliamentary statutes, ordinances, and constitutional events reported in the **12-month window** before the exam. It sits inside the **Current Affairs & General Knowledge** section and carries roughly **5% weightage** of the 150-mark paper.

- **Three new criminal codes** — BNS, BNSS, BSA — replaced the IPC, CrPC, and Indian Evidence Act from **1 July 2024**.
- **Article 142**, **Article 32**, and **Article 226** rulings dominate the constitutional segment.
- **Ratio decidendi** binds lower courts; **obiter dicta** is persuasive only — examiners test this distinction directly.

| Legal News Pillar | What CLAT Tests |
| --- | --- |
| Supreme Court / HC judgments | Bench strength, citation, relief granted |
| Parliamentary enactments | Date of assent, replacing statute |
| Ordinances under Article 123 | Duration, subject matter |
| Constitutional amendments | Article number, basic structure impact |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Sources You Must Track
CLAT expects familiarity with at least one national daily (**The Hindu** or **Indian Express**) plus a law-specific outlet such as **LiveLaw**, **Bar & Bench**, or **Supreme Court Observer**. The Consortium of NLUs restricts the syllabus to news reported in mainstream English dailies and recognised law journals — avoid political blogs and opinion columns.

#### Core Concepts to Master
Every news item carries a *legal* spine: the petition, the statutory provision, the bench, the relief, and the citation. Memorising only the headline loses you the comprehension marks in passage-based items.

- **Ratio decidendi** — the binding reasoning on which the decision rests.
- **Obiter dicta** — incidental remarks, persuasive but not binding.
- **PIL disposal vs admission** — only disposed PILs create precedent.
- **BNS / BNSS / BSA** mapping — Section 302 IPC is now Section 302 BNS; Section 376 IPC is Section 63 BNS; Section 420 IPC is Section 318 BNS.
- **Article 142** — complete justice powers of the Supreme Court.
- **Ordinance route** — Article 123 (Centre) and Article 213 (State), valid for six weeks after Parliament reassembles.

#### Standard Question Types
| Question Format | What It Asks |
| --- | --- |
| Factual MCQ | Name of the judge, date of judgment, or section number |
| Passage-based | Apply the ruling's holding to a fresh fact pattern |
| Principle-application | State whether the ratio governs a described scenario |
| Assertion-reason | Distinguish binding law from political commentary |

#### Common Traps
- Citing the **High Court** when the matter was actually decided by the **Supreme Court**, or vice versa.
- Quoting **IPC sections** after 1 July 2024 instead of the BNS equivalents.
- Writing the *political* headline instead of the *legal* issue and relief.
- Citing judgments outside the 12-month qualifying window.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Mapping Cases to Subject Heads
Build a personal tracker divided into criminal, constitutional, commercial, environmental, family, labour, tax, and cyber law. For each entry record: case title, citation, bench, date, statutory provision interpreted, and the narrow holding. This structure turns revision into retrieval practice and exposes gaps within a subject head.

#### Worked Example — Article 370 Cases
In the post-5 August 2019 rulings, the Supreme Court (5-judge Constitution Bench) upheld the abrogation and directed timelines for restoration of statehood. CLAT will not ask *whether* the Court upheld it; it will ask **which bench strength heard it**, **what relief was granted regarding statehood**, and **which Article was the basis**. Memorising the political slug loses the mark — naming the bench and the relief wins it.

#### Edge Cases and Adjacent Topics
- **Law Commission Reports** vs **Standing Committee Reports** vs enacted statutes — only an Act of Parliament or a notified Ordinance binds.
- **Interim orders** do not create precedent; cite the **final judgment**.
- **NALSA** decisions and **Election Commission of India** notifications appear under constitutional and administrative headings respectively.
- **ADR**, **PMLA**, and **RTI Act** amendments are tested through the specific section changed, not the broad theme.

#### Practice Prompts
1. A passage describes a ruling on Section 63 BNS. Identify the *corresponding* IPC section, the bench strength, and whether the ratio or obiter governs a fresh fact pattern.
2. An MCQ names an Ordinance promulgated under Article 123. Choose the correct maximum duration, the re-promulgation rule, and which House's resolution can disapprove it.

| Mistake | Correction |
| --- | --- |
| Quoting IPC sections after 1 July 2024 | Use BNS / BNSS / BSA equivalents |
| Citing the political headline | Cite the legal issue, relief, and statutory provision |
| Treating obiter as binding | Identify the ratio decidendi of the judgment |
| Ignoring the 12-month window | Filter every note by the qualifying date range |
