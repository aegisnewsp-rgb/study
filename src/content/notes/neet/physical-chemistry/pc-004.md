# Classification

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Classification** organises all matter into a hierarchy: **matter → pure substances (elements, compounds) or mixtures (homogeneous solutions, heterogeneous suspensions/colloids)**, with a parallel axis dividing it into **solids, liquids, and gases**. Solids are subdivided into **crystalline (sharp melting point, long-range order)** and **amorphous (no sharp MP, short-range order)**, and crystalline solids split by bonding into **ionic, covalent (network), metallic, and molecular** types. The two must-know concentration expressions for NEET are **Molarity M = n_solute / V_solution(L)** and **Molality m = n_solute / mass_solvent(kg)**, with **mass % = (m_solute / m_solution) × 100**. Tyndall-positive systems are **colloids**, not true solutions. High-yield points: colloids show Brownian motion and scatter light; suspensions settle; true solutions do neither.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### The Matter Hierarchy
**Matter** is anything with mass and volume. It first branches into **pure substances** (fixed composition, e.g. NaCl, O₂) and **mixtures** (variable composition). Pure substances are of two kinds: **elements** (one type of atom, cannot be decomposed chemically) and **compounds** (two or more elements in fixed ratio, chemically combined).

#### Homogeneous vs Heterogeneous Mixtures
A **homogeneous mixture (solution)** has uniform composition throughout a single phase — sugar in water, air, brass. A **heterogeneous mixture** has visibly distinct phases. Heterogeneous mixtures in NEET are further split by particle size:

| Type | Particle size | Tyndall effect | Settles? | Example |
|------|---------------|----------------|----------|---------|
| True solution | < 1 nm | No | No | NaCl(aq) |
| Colloid | 1–1000 nm | **Yes** | No | Milk, fog, blood |
| Suspension | > 1000 nm | Yes (or just cloudy) | **Yes** | Muddy water |

The **Tyndall effect** — scattering of a light beam by colloidal particles whose size is comparable to the wavelength of visible light — is the single most-tested discriminator in this block.

#### Concentration Expressions
NEET routinely tests inter-conversion of concentration terms. The six standard forms:

- **Mass %** = (w_solute / w_solution) × 100
- **Volume %** = (V_solute / V_solution) × 100 (used for liquids-in-liquids)
- **ppm** = (w_solute / w_solution) × 10⁶ (for trace-level impurities)
- **Mole fraction** x_A = n_A / (n_A + n_B); x_A + x_B = 1
- **Molarity (M)** = n_solute / V_solution(L) — **temperature-dependent** (volume changes with T)
- **Molality (m)** = n_solute / mass_solvent(kg) — **temperature-independent** (mass fixed)

A useful bridge: **M = (m × d × 1000) / (1000 + m × M_solute)**, where *d* is solution density in g/mL and M_solute is the solute's molar mass. Molarity is asked more often than molality because it is easier to measure volumetrically.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Classification of Crystalline Solids
Once matter is sorted, NEET pushes deeper into the **four classes of crystalline solids**, distinguished by the bonding that holds the lattice together:

1. **Ionic solids** (NaCl, KNO₃) — cations + anions in a 3-D lattice held by **electrostatic (Coulombic) forces**. Properties: high MP & BP, **hard but brittle**, conduct **only in the molten state or in aqueous solution** (not as solids), high solubility in polar solvents.
2. **Covalent / network solids** (diamond, SiC, quartz SiO₂) — atoms joined by a continuous **covalent bond network**. Properties: **very high MP**, extremely **hard** (diamond is the hardest natural substance), **electrical insulators** (except graphite, which conducts along its layers because of delocalised π-electrons).
3. **Metallic solids** (Cu, Fe, alloys) — metal cations in a **sea of delocalised valence electrons** (the "electron gas" model). Properties: **good electrical and thermal conductors** in solid and molten states, **malleable and ductile** (electron sea lets layers slide), lustrous.
4. **Molecular solids** (I₂, ice, sucrose) — discrete molecules held by **van der Waals forces, dipole-dipole interactions, or hydrogen bonds**. Properties: **soft**, **low MP**, **electrical insulators** in all phases. Polar molecular solids (e.g. ice, H₂O) dissolve in polar solvents; non-polar ones (I₂, naphthalene) dissolve in non-polar solvents — a direct application of **"like dissolves like"**.

#### Colloid Sub-types
Colloids are classified by **dispersed phase + dispersion medium**: **sol** (solid in liquid, e.g. paint), **gel** (liquid in solid, e.g. jelly), **emulsion** (liquid in liquid, e.g. milk), **aerosol** (solid/liquid in gas, e.g. smoke, mist), and **foam** (gas in liquid, e.g. shaving cream). **Coagulation** is the process of precipitating a colloid by adding an electrolyte — the classic "river-water mud settling with alum" example.

#### Common Traps and Connections
- **False claim**: "Colloids are homogeneous." They are **heterogeneous** in the strict sense; only the *optical naked-eye* appearance is uniform.
- **Trick**: A solution showing Tyndall effect is *not* a true solution — it is a colloid, regardless of clarity.
- **Molarity vs Molality**: Numerical values are **not** equal unless the solution is extremely dilute. NEET often gives a problem where the student must convert one to the other using the bridge formula above.
- **Graphite** is a network solid but conducts electricity — an exception that NEET loves to repeat.
- **Ionic solids don't conduct as solids** — the ions are locked; conduction needs the lattice to break (molten state) or a solvent to separate charges (aqueous solution).

#### Practice Prompts
1. A 5.85 g sample of NaCl (M = 58.5 g/mol) is dissolved in 500 mL of water. Calculate molarity and molality. *(Ans: M = 0.20 mol/L; assuming ρ_water = 1 g/mL, m = 0.20 mol/kg — a dilute solution where the two nearly coincide.)*
2. A beam of light is passed through three samples: salt water, milk, muddy water. Which shows the Tyndall effect, and why? *(Milk only — colloid with particle size 1–1000 nm; salt water is a true solution; muddy water is a suspension that scatters light but settles.)*
