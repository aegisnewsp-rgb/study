# Vectors — H2 Mathematics (9758) Notes

Vectors is one of the six content strands in the H2 Mathematics 9758 syllabus. At H2 the topic is taught in 3D, which is a step up from the 2D treatment at O-Level. The strand covers three-dimensional vector operations, the dot and cross products, lines and planes in 3D, and the concept of a normal vector. Vectors items appear on Paper 1 and are a common second-paper item after Calculus and Statistics.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### The seven facts every JC2 student needs

1. **Vector**: a quantity with magnitude and direction, represented in print by a bold letter or a letter with an arrow above it.
2. **Position vector**: the vector from the origin to a point.
3. **Unit vector**: a vector of magnitude 1, often denoted î, ĵ, k̂ for the x, y, z directions.
4. **Dot product** a · b = |a||b| cos θ (where θ is the angle between a and b). Gives a scalar.
5. **Cross product** a × b gives a vector perpendicular to both a and b, with magnitude |a||b| sin θ.
6. **Vector equation of a line**: r = a + t d, where a is a point on the line and d is the direction vector.
7. **Vector equation of a plane**: r · n = d, where n is the normal vector to the plane.

#### Key notations

- î, ĵ, k̂ — unit vectors in the x, y, z directions
- |a| — the magnitude of a
- a · b — the dot (scalar) product of a and b
- a × b — the cross product of a and b
- r — the position vector of a point

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Vector operations

A vector in 3D can be written as a column vector (x, y, z) or as a sum of unit vectors x î + y ĵ + z k̂.

**Magnitude**: |a| = √(x² + y² + z²).

**Addition and subtraction**: a + b = (x₁ + x₂, y₁ + y₂, z₁ + z₂); a − b = (x₁ − x₂, y₁ − y₂, z₁ − z₂).

**Scalar multiplication**: λa = (λx, λy, λz).

**Position vector**: the vector from the origin O to a point P. Often written as OP or r_P.

**Vector between two points**: PQ = OQ − OP.

#### Dot product

The dot product (or scalar product) of two vectors a = (a₁, a₂, a₃) and b = (b₁, b₂, b₃) is:

a · b = a₁b₁ + a₂b₂ + a₃b₃

Equivalently, a · b = |a||b| cos θ, where θ is the angle between a and b.

**Angle between vectors**: cos θ = (a · b) / (|a| |b|).

**Perpendicular vectors**: a ⊥ b if and only if a · b = 0.

#### Cross product

The cross product (or vector product) of two vectors a = (a₁, a₂, a₃) and b = (b₁, b₂, b₃) is:

a × b = (a₂b₃ − a₃b₂, a₃b₁ − a₁b₃, a₁b₂ − a₂b₁)

The cross product is a vector perpendicular to both a and b; its magnitude is |a||b| sin θ, where θ is the angle between a and b. The direction is given by the right-hand rule.

**Parallel vectors**: a ∥ b if and only if a × b = 0.

**Area of a parallelogram**: the parallelogram spanned by a and b has area |a × b|.

**Volume of a parallelepiped**: the parallelepiped spanned by a, b, c has volume |a · (b × c)|.

#### Lines in 3D

A line in 3D can be described by:

- A point on the line (e.g. A = (1, 2, 3)).
- A direction vector (e.g. d = (2, −1, 4)).

The parametric form is: r = a + t d, where a is the position vector of the point and t is a parameter.

Two lines in 3D can be:

- **Intersecting**: there is a point common to both.
- **Parallel**: their direction vectors are scalar multiples.
- **Skew**: not parallel and not intersecting.

The shortest distance between a point P and a line r = a + t d is |(AP × d)| / |d|, where AP is the vector from a point on the line to P.

#### Planes in 3D

A plane in 3D can be described by:

- A point on the plane and a normal vector (perpendicular to the plane).
- Three non-collinear points.

The vector form of a plane is: r · n = d, where n is the normal vector and d is a constant.

Equivalently, the Cartesian form is: ax + by + cz = d, where (a, b, c) is the normal vector.

**Angle between a line and a plane**: sin θ = |d · n| / (|d| |n|), where d is the direction vector of the line and n is the normal to the plane.

**Angle between two planes**: cos θ = (n₁ · n₂) / (|n₁| |n₂|), where n₁ and n₂ are the normals to the two planes.

**Distance from a point to a plane**: |ax₀ + by₀ + cz₀ − d| / √(a² + b² + c²).

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where Vectors appears across the H2 Mathematics papers

- **H2 Mathematics 9758 Paper 1**: short structured items on vector operations (magnitude, dot product, angle) and on the vector equation of a line. 3D geometry items often combine with the Cartesian equation of a line / plane.
- **H2 Mathematics 9758 Paper 3**: longer structured items on lines and planes in 3D, distance calculations, and angle-between-line-and-plane problems.
- **Across the papers**: drawing a clear 3D diagram with the relevant vectors and angles labelled is the highest-leverage exam technique for 3D geometry items.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "A vector is just a list of numbers." A vector has both magnitude and direction; the list is one representation. Two vectors with the same magnitude and direction are equal.
- "The dot product is a vector." The dot product is a scalar. The cross product is a vector.
- "a × b = b × a." The cross product is anti-commutative: a × b = −(b × a).
- "The angle between two lines is the same as the angle between their direction vectors." The angle between two lines is the acute angle between them, so take |cos θ| or |sin θ| as appropriate.
- "Two lines in 3D always intersect." Two lines in 3D can be skew — not parallel and not intersecting. This is a 3D-specific behaviour.

#### Exam technique

- For vector-equation questions, write out the parametric form explicitly: r = a + t d. The parametric form is the method mark.
- For dot-product questions, state the formula (a · b = |a||b| cos θ) before substituting. The formula is the method mark.
- For cross-product questions, set up the determinant form of the cross product before computing. The determinant is the method mark.
- For distance-to-plane questions, write the formula with the values substituted. The substituted formula is the method mark.
