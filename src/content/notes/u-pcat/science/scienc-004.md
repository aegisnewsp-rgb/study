# Earth's interior

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Earth's interior is layered into **crust** (solid rock), **mantle** (hot semi-solid rock), and **core** (liquid outer + solid inner, mostly iron–nickel). The four **spheres** — lithosphere, hydrosphere, atmosphere, biosphere — interact continuously. **Weather** is the short-term atmospheric state; **climate** is the average over 30+ years. **Rotation** (24 h) causes day and night plus the Coriolis deflection. **Revolution** (365.25 d) combined with the **23.5° axial tilt** produces the seasons.

- Rotation period: **24 hours**; revolution period: **365.25 days**
- Axial tilt: **23.5°** — the actual cause of seasons, not distance from the Sun
- Solar system: **8 planets** (Pluto is a dwarf planet); Earth–Sun distance ≈ **1 AU (150 million km)**

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Earth's internal layers

The planet behaves like a giant stratified sphere. The thin **crust** (5–70 km) floats on the hotter **mantle**, which flows slowly enough to drive plate tectonics. Below the mantle, the **liquid outer core** swirls to generate Earth's magnetic field, while the **inner core** stays solid because of extreme pressure despite higher temperature.

| Layer | State | Main composition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crust | Solid | Silicate rock (granite, basalt) |
| Mantle | Semi-solid / plastic | Silicates rich in iron and magnesium |
| Outer core | Liquid | Iron–nickel alloy |
| Inner core | Solid | Iron–nickel alloy |

#### The four spheres

- **Lithosphere** — solid land (crust + upper mantle); site of tectonic activity.
- **Hydrosphere** — all water: oceans, rivers, groundwater, ice caps.
- **Atmosphere** — gaseous envelope; divided into troposphere, stratosphere, mesosphere, thermosphere.
- **Biosphere** — every living organism interacting with the other three.

#### Rotation vs. revolution

Earth spins eastward on its tilted axis once every 24 hours, exposing each longitude to the Sun and creating **day and night**. This eastward spin also deflects moving air and water to the right in the Northern Hemisphere — the **Coriolis effect**, which shapes wind patterns and ocean currents. The annual **revolution** around the Sun, paired with the persistent 23.5° tilt, changes the solar angle received at each latitude over the year, producing **seasons**.

#### Weather versus climate

- **Weather**: temperature, humidity, wind, precipitation at a specific place and time.
- **Climate**: statistical average of weather for a region over at least 30 years.
- The Philippines lies inside the tropical climate belt; its wet–dry pattern is driven by monsoon winds, not by axial-tilt distance.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Why seasons actually occur

Earth maintains a fixed tilt direction in space as it orbits. In June the Northern Hemisphere leans toward the Sun, receiving more direct rays and longer daylight — that is **summer in the north** even though Earth is actually farther from the Sun. In December the tilt reverses and the Northern Hemisphere receives oblique rays. Therefore **axial tilt**, not orbital distance, governs seasons.

| Hemisphere | Tilted toward Sun | Season |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Northern | June solstice | Summer |
| Northern | December solstice | Winter |
| Southern | December solstice | Summer |
| Both | March / September equinox | Spring or autumn |

#### Common mistakes examined by UPCAT

1. Stating seasons come from changing Earth–Sun distance.
2. Listing Pluto as the ninth planet (it is a dwarf planet since 2006).
3. Confusing weather with climate when the item gives a multi-decade rainfall average.
4. Placing the mantle below the core or forgetting the inner core is solid.
5. Saying the Philippines gets hotter because it is closest to the Sun — closeness varies only by about 3% and does not drive Philippine seasonal swings.

#### Solar system at a glance

From the Sun outward: **Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars** (terrestrial/rocky), then the asteroid belt, then **Jupiter, Saturn** (gas giants), and **Uranus, Neptune** (ice giants). Comets originate mainly from the **Kuiper Belt** and **Oort Cloud**.
