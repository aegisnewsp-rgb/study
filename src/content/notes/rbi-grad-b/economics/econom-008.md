# Factor Markets

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

> One-line takeaway for the hall: factor markets price **land, labour, capital and enterprise**, and each factor earns the value of what it adds at the margin.

- The four factors and their typical incomes: **land → rent**, **labour → wages**, **capital → interest**, **entrepreneurship → profit**.
- Firms are **demanders**; households are **suppliers**, so demand for any factor is a **derived demand** pulled from the demand for the final good it helps make.
- A firm hires up to the point where the extra revenue from the last unit equals the extra cost: **MRP = MFC**, with MRP in ₹ per unit of factor and MFC in ₹ per unit of factor.
- Under perfect competition in the output market, **MRP equals VMP**, because price equals marginal revenue; under monopoly/imperfect competition, **MRP < VMP** since MR is below price.
- Wage in a competitive labour market equals the MRP of labour; interest is the price of capital's services; rent is paid to factors with inelastically fixed supply.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** Think **"LLEC"** for the four factors — **L**and, **L**abour, **E**nterprise, **C**apital — and pair them with **"RWIP"** — **R**ent, **W**ages, **I**nterest, **P**rofit. Land→Rent, Labour→Wages, Capital→Interest, Enterprise→Profit. State the worker as **"MRP = MFC"**; the firm stops hiring one unit before that.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### The circular flow framing

Factor markets sit on the bottom loop of the circular flow. Households own the factors and supply them; firms demand factors to combine with technology and produce goods and services in the product market above. What flows opposite to factor services is **factor income** — rent, wages, interest, profit — and these are the four lines you will be tested on for ~3% weight in the Economics paper, usually as 1–2 MCQs in Phase I and possibly a 10-mark item in Phase II ESI.

#### Profit-maximising employment of any factor

The firm treats every factor like any other input. It hires the next unit if the revenue that unit generates exceeds the cost it imposes, and stops one unit before the point where the two are equal.

- MRP (Marginal Revenue Product) = MPP × MR, measured in **₹ per unit of factor**; MPP in **units of output per unit of factor**, MR in **₹ per unit of output**.
- MFC (Marginal Factor Cost) = ΔTC / ΔF, in **₹ per unit of factor**; ΔTC is the change in total cost (₹), ΔF the change in factor quantity.
- VMP (Value of Marginal Product) = MPP × P, in **₹ per unit of factor**; P is output price in **₹ per unit**.

| Concept | Formula | When MRP = VMP | When MRP < VMP |
|---|---|---|---|
| MRP | MPP × MR | Product market is perfectly competitive (MR = P) | Imperfect competition (MR < P) |
| VMP | MPP × P | Always defined this way | Always defined this way |
| MFC | ΔTC / ΔF | Flat under perfect factor competition | Upward-sloping under monopsony |
| Hiring rule | MRP = MFC | Standard result | Hiring rule unchanged |

#### Comparing the three demand concepts

Students regularly swap these because the acronyms overlap.

| Term | What it measures | Units | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Demand for a factor** | Schedule of quantities a firm hires at different factor prices | Units of the factor | Direct concept (derived from MRP curve) |
| **Derived demand** | Demand that originates from final-goods demand | — | Explains shift factors of factor demand |
| **MRP curve** | Extra revenue from one more factor unit | ₹ / unit of factor | Underlying the firm's factor demand |

#### Wage determination across market forms

In a perfectly competitive labour market the equilibrium wage equals **MRP_L**, and everyone willing to work at that wage is employed. A **minimum wage fixed above MRP_L** cuts a wedge between the wage workers are paid and the wage at which firms want to hire, generating a surplus of labour (unemployment). Under **monopsony**, the firm faces an upward-sloping labour supply curve, so MFC > wage; the firm hires where MFC = MRP_L and pays a wage below MRP_L, producing both monopsony profit and deadweight loss — RBI examiners test this contrast because it links to labour regulation policy in India.

#### Rent: Ricardian versus modern

Ricardian rent is the surplus accruing to land differing in fertility, after the worst land in use earns zero rent. Modern (Marshallian) economic rent is the payment to any factor above its **transfer earnings** — i.e. what it could earn in its next-best use. With perfectly inelastic supply, the entire payment is rent; with elastic supply, rent and transfer earnings mix. This distinction matters when you read about agricultural policy or mineral rights in RBI's report on the economy.

#### Interest: three competing theories

| Theory | What interest is | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **Loanable Funds ** | Price equilibrating supply and demand for loanable funds | Real + monetary factors determine the rate |
| **Liquidity Preference (Keynes)** | Reward for parting with liquidity | Money demand vs money supply sets the short rate |
| **Time Preference / Böhm-Bawerk** | Reward for waiting / time preference | Future goods discounted against present goods |

#### Euler's Theorem, applied cautiously

Euler's theorem states that under constant returns to scale, **total product is exactly exhausted by total factor payments** at their marginal product values — i.e. land gets its marginal product, labour gets its marginal product, and so on, and the sum equals total output. Outside CRS, with increasing returns **total product exceeds total factor payment at marginal rates** (a residual profit appears); with decreasing returns the opposite holds. Always state the CRS assumption before you invoke Euler.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

#### Mathematical boundaries and edge cases

- Pure **economic rent** corresponds to the area above the factor's supply curve, bounded by the price line and the y-axis. With a perfectly inelastic supply (vertical line), the *entire* factor income is rent — a useful framing for landowner income analysis in Indian land economics.
- Euler exhaustion fails the moment you drop **CRS**. Under IRS, payment at marginal products leaves a residual equal to total profit; under DRS, marginal-product payments exceed total product, so the result is infeasible without bargaining over shares.
- In a **monopsony with a minimum wage**: a wage floor set *below* the monopsonistic wage but *above* the competitive wage can raise both employment *and* the wage simultaneously — the canonical Monopsony Paradox, occasionally tested.

#### Indian policy adjacencies

- **MNREGA** fixes a statutory minimum wage floor in rural areas; understanding the disequilibrium surplus in labour markets explains why implementation slips below headline coverage.
- **PDS and MSP** influence transfers to agricultural factor income, mixing Ricardian rent with policy transfer rent.
- **RBI's Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF)** and the **Liquidity Preference** theory appear together: the repo rate is the short-term interest rate the banking system clears at, mirroring Keynesian money-demand logic.

#### Common mistakes in the exam room

- Confusing MRP and VMP under imperfect competition — the safe move is to write **MRP = MPP × MR** and **VMP = MPP × P**, then say **MRP < VMP** whenever MR < P.
- Using **APP** (average physical product) instead of **MPP** when computing MRP — the hiring decision always uses the marginal curve.
- Reading **derived demand** as direct demand. The demand for, say, typists is derived from the demand for the firm's output, not from a desire to "buy typist services" directly.

#### Advanced practice prompts

1. Suppose a firm produces 100 units at P = ₹50. Hiring one extra worker raises output by 4 units and raises total wage bill from ₹3,000 to ₹3,600. The output market is monopolistically competitive, with MR = ₹40. Should the firm hire the worker? Show MRP, VMP and MFC, then decide. *(Hint: MRP = 4 × 40 = ₹160; VMP = 4 × 50 = ₹200; MFC = ₹600; hire because MRP > MFC.)*
2. The government imposes a wage floor above the competitive wage in a monopsonistic labour market. Use MRP_L = MFC and a comparison of areas to show why employment may rise *and* wage rise. Sketch the curves and identify the new wage as the highest of (i) the minimum wage, (ii) the competitive wage, (iii) the old monopsony wage.

#### Exam-specific strategy

For Phase I MCQs, lock the rule **MRP = MFC** as your first filter and treat VMP/MRP distinction as a tie-breaker. For Phase II, structure a 10-mark answer as: define the factor market, state the hiring rule, derive wage = MRP_L for the competitive case, contrast with monopsony, and close with one sentence on policy linkage (e.g. minimum wage). Use NCERT's introductory microeconomics chapter as the authoritative base; the RBI Grade B ESI paper accepts NCERT-orthodox terminology.
