# Topic 5 — Politics: Meaning, Approaches and Scope

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Politics**, in the TNPSC Group 1 sense, is the disciplined study of **power**, **authority**, the **State** and **governance** — not merely the activity of parties and elections. David Easton defined it as the "authoritative allocation of values for a society"; Robert Dahl framed it as "who gets what, when, how"; Harold Lasswell called it the study of "influence and the influential". Aristotle treated politics as the **master science**, focused on the State.

| Thinker / School | Core Idea |
|---|---|
| Easton | Authoritative allocation of values |
| Dahl | Who gets what, when, how |
| Lasswell | Study of influence and the influential |
| Weber | Traditional, charismatic, rational-legal authority |
| Marx & Engels | Politics as class struggle; State as bourgeois instrument |
| Locke / Mill / Rawls | Liberty, consent, constitutionalism |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Traditional and Modern Views

The **Traditional view** confines politics to the State, government and formal institutions. Aristotle called politics the "master science" because the State encloses all other associations, and the goal of the polis is the "good life". Within this view, the discipline studies the **organs of the State** — Legislature, Executive and Judiciary — their functions and inter-relationships.

The **Modern view** broadens politics to **power relations in every social sphere** — family, workplace, caste associations, religious bodies. Easton's "authoritative allocation of values" definition captures this: politics binds society by deciding, authoritatively, which values are distributed, to whom, and in what quantity.

#### Power, Authority and Legitimacy

**Power** is the ability of an actor to influence another's behaviour. **Authority** is power recognised as legitimate. **Legitimacy** is the general belief that a ruler's commands ought to be obeyed. Max Weber classified legitimate domination into three ideal types:

| Weber's Authority Type | Basis of Obedience | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Traditional | Sanctity of long-standing custom | Hereditary monarchy |
| Charismatic | Personal charisma of leader | Revolutionary prophet |
| Rational-Legal | Legally enacted rules | Modern bureaucracy |

#### Liberal and Marxist Approaches

The **Liberal approach** (Locke, J.S. Mill, John Rawls) centres on **individual liberty**, **consent of the governed**, **constitutionalism**, **rule of law** and **pluralism**. The State exists to protect rights, not to dictate life-plans. Rawls's *A Theory of Justice* extends this with the "veil of ignorance" and the difference principle.

The **Marxist approach** (Marx, Engels, Lenin) treats politics as a **superstructure** resting on the **economic base**. The State is an instrument of **class domination**; class struggle is the motor of history. In a classless communist society, the State — and politics — is expected to **"wither away"**.

#### Politics vs Political Science

**Politics** is the practice — contestation, voting, lobbying, governing. **Political Science** is the systematic, empirical and normative study of that practice, drawing on Political Theory, Comparative Politics, Public Administration and International Relations.

**Exam pattern:** TNPSC frames this topic as 2-mark definitions ("Define Politics"), short notes on thinkers (10 marks), and comparison questions — Traditional vs Modern, Liberal vs Marxist, Power vs Authority.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Sub-fields of Political Science

| Sub-field | Focus |
|---|---|
| Political Theory | Normative ideas — justice, liberty, equality |
| Comparative Politics | Systems across countries — UK, US, France, India |
| Public Administration | Bureaucracy, budgeting, policy implementation |
| International Relations | War, diplomacy, IOs, global governance |
| Political Sociology | Political culture, socialisation, participation |
| Political Economy | State–market interaction, development |

#### Relationship with Allied Disciplines

Politics borrows from and overlaps with several social sciences. From **Economics** it draws theories of public choice and rent-seeking; from **Sociology** the concepts of stratification and political socialisation; from **History** the comparative method and case studies of states; from **Psychology** the analysis of voting behaviour and political personality; from **Law** the framework of constitutionalism; from **Geography** the influence of territory and resources on power. Without these linkages, Political Science becomes a closed vocabulary.

#### Edge Cases and Common Mistakes

- Quoting Easton's definition as Lasswell's, or Dahl's "who gets what, when how" as Easton's — examiners mark strictly.
- Confusing **Liberalism** (a political philosophy) with the **Liberal faction** inside the Indian National Congress.
- Reducing Marxism to "economics only" — its **political** content (State, revolution, dictatorship of the proletariat) is what the syllabus tests.
- Treating authority and legitimacy as synonyms — authority is a relation, legitimacy is the belief that underwrites it.

2. Compare Traditional and Modern approaches to the study of Politics. Why is the distinction between Politics and Political Science important for civil services aspirants? (15 marks)
