# Nuclei

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

- A nucleus contains **Z protons** and **N neutrons**, with mass number **A = Z + N**. Protons and neutrons are collectively **nucleons**.
- Nuclear radius scales as **R = R₀A^(1/3)**, with R₀ ≈ 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁵ m; nuclear density is roughly constant at ~2.3 × 10¹⁷ kg/m³.
- **Binding energy** BE = Δm·c² where Δm = [Z·m_p + N·m_n − m_nucleus]; using 1 u ≈ 931.5 MeV/c² converts mass defect directly to MeV.
- **Decay law**: N(t) = N₀e^(−λt); half-life **T₁/₂ = 0.693/λ**; activity A = λN in becquerel (Bq), where 1 Ci = 3.7 × 10¹⁰ Bq.
- **Q-value** of a reaction = (Σm_initial − Σm_final)c²; **Q > 0** releases energy, **Q < 0** absorbs it.

> **Exam pointers:** JEE Advanced asks 1–2 questions (~5% weight). Expect mass-defect arithmetic, λ–T₁/₂–activity conversions, and Q-value with threshold energy for endoergic reactions.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Nuclear Composition and Size

Every nuclide is specified by (Z, A): Z is the proton count (atomic number) and A = Z + N is the mass number. Nucleons are held by the **strong nuclear force**, which is short-ranged (~few fm), charge-independent, and saturates — each nucleon interacts mainly with nearest neighbours.

The empirical **R = R₀A^(1/3)** (R₀ ≈ 1.2 fm) implies volume ∝ A, hence nuclear density is nearly independent of A. Memorise this — JEE often asks the density given R and A.

#### Binding Energy Curve

Mass defect Δm = [Z·m_p + N·m_n − m_nucleus] is converted via E = Δm·c². Working in atomic mass units (u), **1 u ≡ 931.5 MeV/c²** so BE in MeV = Δm(u) × 931.5.

Plotting **BE/A vs A** gives a curve peaking near **Fe-56 (~8.75 MeV/nucleon)**. The shape dictates energetics:

| Region | Behaviour on moving toward peak | Released how? |
|---|---|---|
| Light (A < 20) | Fusion | Energy released when light nuclei combine |
| Heavy (A > 200) | Fission | Energy released when heavy nucleus splits |

#### Radioactive Decay Law

Decay is statistical. The differential form **dN/dt = −λN** integrates to **N(t) = N₀e^(−λt)** and activity A(t) = A₀e^(−λt). Key relations:

- Mean life τ = 1/λ
- Half-life T₁/₂ = ln 2 / λ ≈ 0.693/λ
- Activity A = λN; 1 Ci = 3.7 × 10¹⁰ Bq; 1 Bq = 1 disintegration/s

For a parent–daughter chain, **secular equilibrium** (when T_parent ≫ T_daughter) gives λ_p·N_p = λ_d·N_d.

#### Q-Value and Threshold Energy

For any reaction, **Q = (Σm_reactants − Σm_products)c²**. Use **atomic masses** consistently (electrons included) when β-decay is involved. For endoergic reactions (Q < 0), the **threshold kinetic energy** in the lab frame is

$$E_{\text{th}} = |Q|\left(1 + \frac{m_{\text{projectile}}}{m_{\text{target}}}\right)$$

> **Standard trap:** Many students write E_th = |Q|. The extra factor accounts for recoil of the target nucleus and is frequently tested.

#### Decay Modes

- **α-decay**: A ≥ 150 region; tunnelling through Coulomb barrier (Gamow factor); discrete energy spectrum, daughter recoils.
- **β-decay**: Weak interaction; **β⁻** (n → p + e⁻ + ν̄) and **β⁺** (p → n + e⁺ + ν); continuous electron spectrum up to E_max = Q.
- **γ-decay**: De-excitation of the daughter nucleus, E_γ = hν.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Worked Mechanics

**Alpha recoil:** In α-decay, KE_α : KE_daughter = m_daughter : m_α. JEE sometimes asks the daughter KE, which is much smaller than KE_α because of inverse mass sharing. Total kinetic energy released equals Q.

**Beta spectrum:** The neutrino carries away variable energy, so the electron/positron shows a continuous spectrum bounded by E_max = Q. Energy conservation still holds; momentum is balanced by the antineutrino/neutrino.

#### Connections to Adjacent Topics

- **Modern Physics (Photoelectric effect):** The photon picture hν links directly to γ-ray energies from nuclear de-excitation.
- **Atoms (Bohr model):** Electron binding energies are eV scale; nuclear binding is MeV scale — the ~10⁶ factor explains why nuclear processes release roughly a million times more energy per event than chemistry.
- **Semiconductors / detectors:** Geiger–Müller tubes and semiconductor counters exploit the ionising power of α, β, γ — same decay law drives the count rate.

#### Common Mistakes (Exam Traps)

| Mistake | Correction |
|---|---|
| Using nuclear mass instead of atomic mass in Q-value | Use atomic masses; β-decay electron bookkeeping must balance |
| Setting decay rate constant over time | dN/dt = −λN decreases as N decreases |
| Threshold = \|Q\| for endoergic reactions | Multiply by (1 + m₁/m₂); recoil matters |
| Saying "heavier always splits to release energy" | Only A ≳ 56; very light nuclei release on fusing |
| Confusing τ and T₁/₂ | τ = T₁/₂/0.693, not equal |

#### Two Practice Prompts

1. A sample has activity 8000 Bq at t = 0 and 500 Bq after 12 hours. Find the half-life and the number of radioactive nuclei initially.
2. For ⁷Li(α, n)¹⁰B, given atomic masses m(⁷Li) = 7.01600 u, m(α) = 4.00260 u, m(¹⁰B) = 10.01294 u, m(n) = 1.00866 u, compute the Q-value in MeV and comment on whether the reaction is exoergic.

> **Strategy tip:** In JEE Advanced, Nuclei questions are often the "quick wins" — short numericals with unit conversions. Carry 1 u = 931.5 MeV/c² mentally and double-check isotope vs isobar vs isotone labelling.
