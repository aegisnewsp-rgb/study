# Heredity

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

> Most-tested single fact: ratios, not definitions, decide marks in MDCAT Heredity MCQs.

**Heredity** is the transmission of genes from parents to offspring through gametes, governed by Mendelian laws and modified by linkage, dominance patterns, and sex-linkage. The five ratios that recur in past PMDC papers sit on one card.

| Cross type | F2 phenotypic ratio | Genotypic ratio |
|---|---|---|
| Monohybrid (complete dominance) | 3 : 1 | 1 : 2 : 1 |
| Monohybrid (incomplete dominance) | 1 : 2 : 1 | 1 : 2 : 1 |
| Dihybrid (independent assortment) | 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 | 1 : 2 : 1 : 2 : 4 : 2 : 1 : 2 : 1 |
| Test cross (monohybrid) | 1 : 1 | 1 : 1 |
| Test cross (dihybrid) | 1 : 1 : 1 : 1 | — |

> 💡 **Memory Hook (Mnemonic): "SADD-ICE":** **S**egregation, **A**ssortment, **D**ominance patterns (complete, incomplete, co-), **D**ihybrid = 9:3:3:1, **I**ndependent assortment, **C**rossing over, **E**pistasis. Read it as "sad-ice" and the seven traps arrive in one breath.

A **gene** is a DNA segment coding for a trait; **alleles** are its variant forms at a given **locus**. Diploid cells carry two alleles per locus, restoring the 2n state after fertilisation.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Mendel's Two Laws and What They Predict

Mendel's **Law of Segregation** states that the two alleles of a gene separate during meiosis I, so each gamete receives only one allele. Fertilisation randomly recombines them. A monohybrid cross Aa × Aa therefore produces the 3 : 1 phenotypic ratio when A is completely dominant over a.

The **Law of Independent Assortment** applies when genes sit on different chromosomes: alleles of two genes segregate independently into gametes, producing the 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 phenotypic ratio in F2 of AaBb × AaBb.

Gamete genotype proportion from a heterozygote AaBb follows (½)ⁿ, where n is the number of heterozygous gene pairs. For AaBb, n = 2 and ¼ of the gametes are AB.

#### Dominance Patterns Compared

| Pattern | Cross | F2 ratio | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Complete dominance | Tt × Tt | 3 tall : 1 short | Mendel's pea height |
| Incomplete dominance | Rr × Rr | 1 red : 2 pink : 1 white | Snapdragon flower colour |
| Co-dominance | I^A I^B × I^A I^B | 1 I^A I^A : 2 I^A I^B : 1 I^B I^B | Human ABO blood groups |

Incomplete dominance produces a phenotype intermediate between the two homozygotes, so the genotypic and phenotypic ratios are both 1 : 2 : 1. Co-dominance expresses both alleles simultaneously.

#### Sex-Linked Inheritance and Criss-Cross Pattern

Genes on the X chromosome show **criss-cross inheritance**: a father transmits his X-linked allele only to daughters, and a mother passes it to sons as well as daughters. X-linked recessive disorders (haemophilia, red-green colour blindness) appear far more often in males (XY, hemizygous) than in females (XX).

For carrier mother X^H X^h × normal father X^H Y, each male child has a ½ chance of being affected and each female child has a ½ chance of being a carrier.

#### Worked Practice with the 9:3:3:1 Ratio

Consider two true-breeding pea plants: RRYY (round, yellow) × rryy (wrinkled, green). The F1 is RrYy — round and yellow, because R is dominant over r and Y is dominant over y. Self-crossing F1 (RrYy × RrYy) yields:

- Round, yellow (R_Y_) = 9/16
- Round, green (R_yy) = 3/16
- Wrinkled, yellow (rrY_) = 3/16
- Wrinkled, green (rryy) = 1/16

The complementary ratio 9 + 3 + 3 + 1 = 16 confirms that all four gamete combinations form with equal probability when the two genes assort independently.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Past MDCAT papers list "9 : 3 : 3 : 1" as the answer even when the genes are **linked** on the same chromosome. Linked genes produce mostly parental phenotypes with only a small recombinant fraction, so the 9:3:3:1 ratio collapses. Spot the question stem — if linkage is implied, the ratio will not fit.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

#### Exceptions That Break the Standard Ratios

| Mechanism | Cross | Observed ratio | Why it differs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Linked genes | AaBb (cis) × aabb | Mostly parental, ~7% recombinants | Loci on same chromosome assort together unless crossover occurs |
| Epistasis (9 : 7) | AaBb × AaBb | 9 : 7 | One gene masks another's expression |
| Lethal alleles | Yy × Yy | 2 : 1 instead of 3 : 1 | Homozygous dominant or recessive dies |
| Sex-limited / sex-influenced | Various | Skewed male:female | Hormonal environment alters phenotype |

#### Chromosomal Errors and Pedigree Logic

**Non-disjunction** during meiosis I or II produces gametes with n+1 or n−1 chromosomes. Fertilisation with a normal gamete yields **aneuploidy** — Down syndrome (trisomy 21, karyotype 47, XY, +21) is the classic case. Turner's syndrome (45, X) and Klinefelter's (47, XXY) are also tested.

Pedigree symbols follow a strict convention: square = male, circle = female, filled = affected, half-filled = carrier, horizontal line = mating, vertical line = offspring. A pedigree showing affected males with unaffected parents and carrier daughters points directly to X-linked recessive inheritance.

#### Adjacent Topics Worth Linking

- **Molecular basis:** each gene → mRNA → protein via transcription and translation; a **point mutation** (substitution) may be silent, missense or nonsense.
- **Variation source:** mutation is the ultimate source of new alleles; recombination and independent assortment reshuffle existing ones.
- **Population genetics:** Hardy-Weinberg extends Mendelian ratios to large populations where genotype frequencies stabilise at p² + 2pq + q² = 1.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. A colour-blind man marries a woman whose father was colour-blind. What proportion of their sons will be colour-blind? (Answer: ½, because the mother must be a carrier X^C X^c with probability 1.)
2. In sweet peas, two complementary genes (A and B) both produce purple flowers; absence of either gives white. Self-cross AaBb × AaBb. What is the phenotypic ratio? (Answer: 9 purple : 7 white, a classic 9 : 7 epistatic ratio.)

> 📌 **Formula Check:** Probability of an independent combination of two events uses the **product rule** P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B). For mutually exclusive phenotypes use the **sum rule** P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B). Wrong application is the most common source of error in pedigree-based MCQs.
