# XIII. Industrial Chemistry (Elective) — HKDSE Chemistry Notes

This topic covers Elective Topic XIII of the HKDSE Chemistry Elective Part. Candidates sit any two of the three electives on Paper 2. The topic addresses bulk chemicals (production of ammonia, sulfuric acid, sodium hydroxide, sodium carbonate); petrochemicals; plastics and synthetic polymers; detergents; pharmaceuticals and the pharmaceutical industry.

> Verify the live HKDSE Chemistry Assessment Framework on https://www.hkeaa.edu.hk/ before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Bulk chemicals**: produced in large quantities; common examples include ammonia, sulfuric acid, sodium hydroxide, sodium carbonate.
- **Ammonia (NH₃)**: produced by the Haber process (N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃, Fe catalyst, ~450°C, ~200 atm). Used in fertilisers, explosives, nitric acid production.
- **Sulfuric acid (H₂SO₄)**: produced by the Contact process (2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃, V₂O₅ catalyst, ~450°C, then SO₃ + H₂O → H₂SO₄). Used in fertilisers, detergents, batteries.
- **Sodium hydroxide (NaOH)**: produced by the chlor-alkali process (electrolysis of brine: 2NaCl + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂ + Cl₂). Used in soap, paper, textiles.
- **Sodium carbonate (Na₂CO₃)**: produced by the Solvay process (ammonia-soda: NaCl + CO₂ + NH₃ + H₂O → NaHCO₃ + NH₄Cl, then 2NaHCO₃ → Na₂CO₃ + H₂O + CO₂). Used in glass, soap, water softening.
- **Petrochemicals**: chemicals derived from petroleum. Key building blocks (C1-C8 alkenes and aromatics): ethene, propene, butadiene, benzene, toluene, xylene.
- **Plastics and synthetic polymers**: addition polymers (poly(ethene), poly(propene), PVC, polystyrene, PTFE), condensation polymers (nylon, polyester).
- **Detergents**: surfactants — long hydrocarbon tail (hydrophobic) + polar head (hydrophilic). Soap (carboxylate) vs. synthetic detergent (sulfonate).
- **Pharmaceuticals**: drugs — aspirin, paracetamol, penicillin, ibuprofen, statins, antidepressants, antiretrovirals. The pharmaceutical industry involves drug discovery, clinical trials, manufacturing and regulation.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing the Haber process (ammonia) with the Contact process (sulfuric acid).
- Forgetting that the Solvay process produces sodium carbonate, not sodium bicarbonate (which is an intermediate).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Haber process (ammonia)

Reaction: N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g), ΔH = -92 kJ/mol.

Catalyst: iron (Fe) with promoters (K₂O, Al₂O₃, CaO).

Conditions: ~450°C, ~200 atm. Compromise conditions: low T favours yield but slow rate; high P favours yield but expensive equipment.

Production:
1. Hydrogen: produced from natural gas (steam reforming: CH₄ + H₂O → CO + 3H₂, then water-gas shift: CO + H₂O → CO₂ + H₂) or from coal gasification.
2. Nitrogen: from fractional distillation of air.
3. Synthesis: the mixed gases (1:3 N₂:H₂) are passed over the catalyst at high T and P. NH₃ is condensed and separated; unreacted gases are recycled.

Uses: fertilisers (ammonium nitrate, ammonium sulfate, urea), nitric acid (Ostwald process: NH₃ → NO → NO₂ → HNO₃), explosives, refrigerants, synthetic fibres (nylon).

#### Contact process (sulfuric acid)

Reactions:
1. S(s) + O₂(g) → SO₂(g) (or from sulfide ores: 4FeS₂ + 11O₂ → 2Fe₂O₃ + 8SO₂).
2. 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g), ΔH = -197 kJ/mol. Catalyst: V₂O₅.
3. SO₃(g) + H₂SO₄(l) → H₂S₂O₇(l) (oleum).
5. H₂S₂O₇(l) + H₂O(l) → 2H₂SO₄(l).

Conditions: ~450°C, 1-2 atm. Two-stage absorption (SO₃ is absorbed in H₂SO₄ to form oleum, then diluted with water).

Uses: fertilisers (ammonium sulfate, superphosphate), detergents, petroleum refining, batteries (lead-acid), mineral processing.

#### Chlor-alkali process (sodium hydroxide)

Reaction: 2NaCl(aq) + 2H₂O(l) → 2NaOH(aq) + H₂(g) + Cl₂(g).

Methods:
- **Membrane cell** (most common): ion-exchange membrane separates anode and cathode compartments; Na⁺ migrates from anode to cathode compartment; NaOH forms at cathode; Cl₂ forms at anode.
- **Diaphragm cell**: porous diaphragm separates anode and cathode compartments.
- **Mercury cell**: mercury cathode forms sodium amalgam; the amalgam is hydrolysed to NaOH and H₂ (mostly phased out due to mercury pollution).

Uses: soap, paper, textiles, water treatment, aluminium extraction (Bayer process), petroleum refining.

#### Solvay process (sodium carbonate)

Reactions:
1. NaCl(aq) + CO₂(g) + NH₃(g) + H₂O(l) → NaHCO₃(s) + NH₄Cl(aq).
2. 2NaHCO₃(s) → Na₂CO₃(s) + H₂O(g) + CO₂(g) (calcination).

The less soluble NaHCO₃ precipitates; it is filtered, washed and calcined. The CO₂ produced in calcination is recycled.

Uses: glass (the largest single use), soap, detergents, water softening, paper, food (sodium carbonate as an additive).

#### Petrochemicals

Petrochemicals are chemicals derived from petroleum and natural gas. Key building blocks:

- **Olefins (alkenes)**: ethene (ethylene), propene (propylene), butadiene.
- **Aromatics**: benzene, toluene, xylene.

These are produced by cracking and reforming (Topic V). They are the feedstocks for plastics, synthetic fibres, synthetic rubber, detergents, pharmaceuticals and many other products.

#### Plastics and polymers

See Topic XI for polymer structures. Industrial applications:

- Poly(ethene): packaging films, plastic bags, bottles.
- Poly(propene): containers, automotive parts, fibres.
- PVC: pipes, window frames, cable insulation.
- Polystyrene: packaging, insulation, disposable cups.
- PTFE: non-stick coatings, chemical-resistant seals.
- Nylon: fibres, engineering plastics.
- Polyester (Terylene): fabrics, bottles.

#### Detergents

Soap: sodium salts of long-chain fatty acids. Saponification: fat + NaOH → glycerol + soap.

Synthetic detergents (syndets): sodium salts of long-chain alkylbenzene sulfonic acids (LAS). They work in hard water (unlike soap, which forms scum with Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺).

#### Pharmaceuticals

Drug classes: analgesics (aspirin, paracetamol), antibiotics (penicillin, cephalosporins), anti-inflammatory drugs (ibuprofen, aspirin), antivirals (oseltamivir, antiretrovirals for HIV), antidepressants (SSRIs), statins (cholesterol-lowering).

Drug development stages: discovery, preclinical testing, clinical trials (Phase I safety, Phase II efficacy, Phase III comparison), regulatory approval (e.g. by FDA, EMA, the Pharmacy and Poisons Board of Hong Kong), manufacturing.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-topic links

- **IX. Rate of Reaction and X. Chemical Equilibrium**: industrial processes balance rate and equilibrium.
- **V. Fossil Fuels and Carbon Compounds**: petrochemicals come from petroleum.
- **XI. Chemistry of Carbon Compounds**: polymer structure determines properties.

#### Exam technique

- **For Haber/Contact process questions**: state the reaction, the catalyst, the compromise conditions (T, P) and the reason for each choice.
- **For Solvay process questions**: write the two key reactions; explain how NaHCO₃ precipitates.
- **For pharmaceutical questions**: identify the drug class, the mechanism (where known), and the development stages.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Ammonia is the same as ammonium." Ammonia is NH₃; ammonium is NH₄⁺.
- "Sulfuric acid is dry." Concentrated sulfuric acid is a viscous liquid; oleum is the SO₃·H₂SO₄ mixture.
- "All drugs are safe." All drugs have side effects; benefit/risk assessment is part of the regulatory process.
