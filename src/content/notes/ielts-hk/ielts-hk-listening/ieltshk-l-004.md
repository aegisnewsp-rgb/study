# Part 4 — Academic Lecture — IELTS Listening (Hong Kong) Notes

Part 4 of the IELTS Listening test (administered in Hong Kong by the British Council or IDP) is a monologue on an academic subject — typically a university lecture, a research summary, or a presentation. The recording runs about 5-7 minutes and has 10 questions. Tests sentence completion, summary completion, note completion, table completion and flow-chart completion on dense academic vocabulary.

> Verify the live Listening format on https://ielts.org/ before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Format:** 10 questions.
- **Transcript style:** one speaker (lecturer or researcher), monologue. Long, dense, academic content.
- **Test types:** sentence completion, summary completion, note completion, table completion, flow-chart completion.
- **Vocabulary:** academic and subject-specific.
- **Structure:** the lecture typically has an introduction, body, and conclusion; uses signposting language to mark the transitions.
- **Difficulty:** the highest-difficulty section of the Listening test.

#### Examiner traps

- Missing key information because the pace is fast and the content is dense.
- Confusing the terms used in the lecture with the options in completion items.
- Forgetting to follow the word limit on completion items.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Common Part 4 topics

- **Sciences:** biology, chemistry, physics, geology, astronomy.
- **Social sciences:** sociology, psychology, history, geography, anthropology.
- **Business and economics:** management, marketing, finance, economic history.
- **Technology:** computer science, engineering, biotechnology.
- **Arts and humanities:** linguistics, philosophy, art history.

#### Following the lecture structure

- **Introduction:** the lecturer typically states the topic, the scope, and the main thesis.
- **Body:** the main content, divided into sections with clear signposting.
- **Conclusion:** the lecturer summarises the main points and may suggest further research.

Signposting language to listen for:

- "Today I'll be talking about..."
- "Let's start with..."
- "Moving on to..."
- "Now let's consider..."
- "To summarise..."
- "In conclusion..."

#### Note-taking strategy

During the recording (and on the 10-minute transfer time on paper), take structured notes:

- Use headings to match the lecture structure.
- Use abbreviations for common words.
- Use symbols and arrows to show relationships.
- Focus on the main ideas, not every word.

#### Vocabulary strategies

- **Predict topic vocabulary:** before listening, identify the topic and predict the key vocabulary.
- **Listen for definitions:** the lecturer often defines key terms ("X is a type of Y...").
- **Listen for examples:** the lecturer often gives examples to illustrate a concept.

#### Common Part 4 question types

- **Sentence completion:** complete a sentence with a word or short phrase from the recording.
- **Summary completion:** complete a summary using words from the recording.
- **Note completion:** complete notes using words from the recording.
- **Table completion:** complete a table with categories and missing entries.
- **Flow-chart completion:** complete a process diagram.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-paper links

- **Part 3**: academic discussion; same vocabulary but conversational.
- **Reading**: academic vocabulary; long passages.
- **Writing Task 1**: describing visual information in academic style.

#### Exam technique

- For sentence completion, identify the keywords in the question and listen for them.
- For summary completion, follow the structure of the summary (it usually mirrors the lecture structure).
- For note/table/flow-chart completion, identify the order of items (the recording usually follows the visual structure).

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Part 4 is impossible to understand." With practice, the academic vocabulary becomes familiar.
- "I need to write down every word." No — focus on the main ideas and key terms.
- "The lecturer speaks too fast." The pace is moderate, but the content is dense; practice with longer academic lectures.
