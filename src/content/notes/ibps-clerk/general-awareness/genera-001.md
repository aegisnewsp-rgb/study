# Indian Banking System and Structure

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The **Indian Banking System** operates under the supervisory umbrella of the **Reserve Bank of India (RBI)**, the apex central bank established on 1 April 1935 under the **RBI Act, 1934**. Banks are classified as **Scheduled** (listed in the Second Schedule of the RBI Act, eligible for RBI borrowings) or **Non-Scheduled** (deposits below ₹5 lakh historically). Scheduled Commercial Banks split into **Public Sector Banks (PSBs)**, **Private Sector Banks**, **Foreign Banks**, and **Regional Rural Banks (RRBs)**.

> The **Banking Regulation Act, 1949** is the principal statute governing banking companies — do not confuse it with the RBI Act.

| Body / Scheme | Year | Key Fact |
|---|---|---|
| RBI nationalisation of 14 banks | 1969 | First major wave |
| Second nationalisation (6 banks) | 1980 | Took PSB count to 20 |
| Deposit insurance (DICGC) | 2020 revision | **₹5,00,000 per depositor per bank** |
| Priority Sector Lending | Ongoing | **40% of ANBC or CEOB** |

**High-yield pointers:** RBI HQ is **Mumbai**; NABARD HQ is **Mumbai**; SIDBI HQ is **Lucknow**; IRDAI HQ is **Hyderabad**; SEBI HQ is **Mumbai**; PFRDA HQ is **New Delhi**.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Structure of the Indian Banking System

The architecture rests on three pillars: **commercial banks**, **cooperative banks**, and **development financial institutions (DFIs)**. Commercial banks handle deposits, loans, and payment services under licences issued by RBI. Cooperative banks operate on a mutual-aid model and, since the **Banking Regulation (Amendment) Act, 2020**, Urban Cooperative Banks (UCBs) are fully under RBI supervision alongside the Registrar of Cooperative Societies.

#### Types of Scheduled Commercial Banks

A bank whose paid-up capital and reserves satisfy RBI norms and whose net demand and time liabilities exceed the prescribed threshold is added to the **Second Schedule**. Within this category:

- **Public Sector Banks** — Government of India (GoI) holding > 50%. After the 2019 mega-merger of 10 PSBs into 12, India now has **12 PSBs** (the 12th being the merged entity of PNB + OBC + United Bank).
- **Private Sector Banks** — Equity held by private entities (e.g., HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, Axis Bank, Kotak Mahindra).
- **Foreign Banks** — Indian branches of banks incorporated outside India.
- **Regional Rural Banks** — Established under the **RRB Act, 1976** for rural credit; equity held 50% by GoI, 35% by sponsor bank, 15% by state government.

#### New-Age Bank Categories

- **Small Finance Banks (SFBs)**: Minimum capital **₹300 crore** (raised from ₹200 crore in 2022), 75% of lending must be to Priority Sector.
- **Payments Banks**: Minimum capital **₹100 crore**, can accept deposits up to ₹2 lakh per account, cannot lend.
- **NARCL (National Asset Reconstruction Company Limited)**: Bad bank set up in 2021 to consolidate stressed assets above ₹500 crore.

#### Priority Sector Lending (PSL) Targets

Total **40% of ANBC** (Adjusted Net Bank Credit) or **CEOB** (Credit Equivalent of Off-Balance Sheet Exposures). Sub-targets:

| Sub-target | Share of ANBC |
|---|---|
| Agriculture | 18% |
| Micro Enterprises | 7.5% |
| Small Enterprises | 2.5% |
| Education | 2% (of which overseas 1%) |
| Housing | 3% |
| Social Infrastructure | 1.5% |

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Regulatory Architecture vs. Statute

Two distinct statutes operate in parallel. The **RBI Act, 1934** established the Reserve Bank and empowers it to issue currency, manage monetary policy via the **6-member Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)** (4 RBI insiders + 3 external, amended 2016), and act as banker to the government. The **Banking Regulation Act, 1949** governs licensing, capital adequacy, management, and winding-up of banking companies. The 2017 and 2020 amendments to the BR Act placed UCBs fully under RBI, empowered RBI to issue directions on stressed assets, and authorised the **FSIB (Financial Services Institutions Bureau)** — successor to the Bank Board Bureau — to recommend PSB board appointments.

#### Reserve Requirements and Liquidity Norms

| Ratio | Statutory Range | Current Norm |
|---|---|---|
| **CRR (Cash Reserve Ratio)** | 3–15% under Section 42 of RBI Act | ~4% (revised periodically) |
| **SLR (Statutory Liquidity Ratio)** | Up to 40% under Section 24 of BR Act | ~18% |
| **LCR (Liquidity Coverage Ratio)** | Basel III mandate | Minimum 100% |

> Examiner trap: CRR is maintained as **cash with RBI** (no interest), while SLR is held as gold, government securities, or cash — students often interchange them.

#### NPA Classification and Recent Reforms

An asset becomes a **Non-Performing Asset** when interest or instalment remains overdue for **more than 90 days**. The 4R framework — **Recognition, Resolution, Restructuring, and Recovery** — governs resolution. The **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC), 2016** established the **IBBI (Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India)** and the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) as the primary recovery forum, with IBC overriding the SARFAESI Act, 2002 in some cases.

#### Common Mistakes and Edge Cases

- **IBA is autonomous, not statutory** — the Indian Banks' Association was set up in 1946 as a self-governing body, unlike SEBI or IRDAI which are statutory regulators.
- **NBFCs ≠ Banks** — NBFCs (Chapter IIIB of RBI Act) cannot accept demand deposits and are not part of the payment & settlement system; deposit insurance via DICGC does not cover them.
- **Lead Bank Scheme (1969)** is distinct from the **Service Area Approach (1989)** — the former assigns one bank per district for credit planning; the latter allocates villages to specific bank branches.
- **DICGC premium**: 12 paise per ₹100 of deposits, reviewed annually; insures **principal + interest** up to ₹5 lakh.

#### Practice Prompts

1. Differentiate between Payments Banks and Small Finance Banks across four parameters: minimum capital, deposit ceiling, lending scope, and PSL target. State which one accepts current account deposits — and explain why the other cannot.
2. The RBI uses the **MCLR (Marginal Cost of Funds based Lending Rate)** as the internal benchmark rate since 2016, replacing the Base Rate. Trace how a 25-bps repo rate change typically transmits to MCLR and finally to a home-loan borrower's EMI under the floating-rate regime.
