# Business Ethics

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Business Ethics** is the study of moral principles and standards that govern conduct, decisions, and policies of individuals and organizations operating in commercial environments. It is broader than law, integrating integrity, transparency, fairness, accountability, and corporate social responsibility (CSR) into everyday operations. For RBI Grade B, expect 3–5 questions per cycle, often as case-study items in the Finance & Management paper (Paper-II Phase 2).

| Term | Meaning | Example in RBI context |
|---|---|---|
| **Ethics** | Self-imposed, system-wide moral standards for a profession/organization | RBI's Code of Conduct for employees |
| **Morals** | Personal beliefs about right and wrong held by an individual | A bank officer refusing a bribe privately |
| **Values** | Core guiding beliefs (honesty, fairness) | Listed in a bank's Vision statement |
| **CSR** | Mandatory social spend under Companies Act 2013 Section 135 | A PSU bank's rural literacy programme |

> 📌 **Formula Check:** CSR Spend = 2% × Average Net Profit of preceding 3 financial years (Section 135, Companies Act 2013).

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"ELFIC"** — Ethics, Law, Fairness, Integrity, CSR. Run any business decision through these five filters to choose the right MCQ option. The most-tested phrase in the paper is *"legal but unethical"* — and ELFIC instantly flags it as wrong.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Sources and Principles of Business Ethics

Ethics flows from religion, culture, law, customs, organizational culture, and personal values. The professional frameworks tested in RBI Grade B come from ICAI (Chartered Accountants) and IMA (Management Accountants), built on five principles: **integrity, objectivity, professional competence, confidentiality, and professional behaviour**.

The RBI expects candidates to distinguish between three governing lenses:

- **Deontological** — duty-based; an action is right if it follows a rule (Kant's categorical imperative).
- **Utilitarian** — outcome-based; right action maximises total welfare.
- **Virtue Ethics** — character-based; right action is what a virtuous person would do.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix

| Concept | What it covers | Source/Legal anchor | Common confusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ethics vs Morals** | Ethics = professional/system rules; Morals = personal beliefs | ICAI Code of Ethics | Students treat them as synonyms |
| **Corporate Governance vs Management** | Governance = board oversight; Management = day-to-day execution | Companies Act 2013, Sections 149 & 166 | Often used interchangeably |
| **Stockholder Theory vs Stakeholder Theory** | Friedman: profit for owners only; Freeman: duties to all stakeholders | Freeman (1984) vs Friedman (1970) | Examiner tests which SEBI/MCA prefers |
| **CSR vs Charity** | CSR is a mandatory 2% spend, not voluntary donation | Section 135 + Schedule VII | Charity ≠ CSR under law |
| **Insider Trading** | Trading on Unpublished Price Sensitive Information (UPSI) | SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, 2015 | Seen as legal loophole, but is unethical AND illegal |

#### Key Decision-Making Models

1. **Potter Box** — apply four lenses: Facts, Values, Principles, Loyalties.
2. **Triple Bottom Line (TBL)** — measure success on People, Planet, Profit simultaneously.
3. **Ethics Check (4 tests)** — Legality, Fairness, Pride (would you announce it?), Harm.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** A listed bank's CFO learns from a board meeting that the bank will post a record quarterly profit. Before public disclosure, she buys 50,000 shares of her own bank. Under which regulatory framework is this action actionable, and which ethical theories condemn it? (RBI Grade B, Finance & Management, 15-mark descriptive)

#### Solution:
- Identify the violation: trading on **Unpublished Price Sensitive Information (UPSI)**.
- Cite regulation: SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, 2015 (PIT 2015), read with Section 15G of SEBI Act, 1992.
- Apply ethical theories:
  - **Deontology (Kant)** — duty to disclose truthfully is violated; using private information treats shareholders as means, not ends.
  - **Utilitarianism** — short-term personal gain produces long-term market-wide harm (loss of investor confidence, capital misallocation).
  - **Stakeholder Theory (Freeman)** — directors owe fiduciary duty to all shareholders, not just themselves.
- Consequence: penalty up to ₹25 crore or three times the profit made, whichever is higher, plus criminal prosecution under PIT 2015.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Many candidates answer only "it is illegal" and skip the ethical-theories angle. Marks are split equally between the *legal* framework (PIT 2015) and *ethical* reasoning (deontology + stakeholder theory). Writing only one half caps the answer at 7–8 marks out of 15.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Advanced Traps

| Trap / Edge Case | Correct Position | Why students fail |
|---|---|---|
| "Ethical = always legal" | Wrong — ethics is broader than law | Forget that *legal but unethical* is a real exam question (e.g. aggressive tax avoidance) |
| "Whistle-blowers are disloyal" | Wrong — protected under Companies Act 2013 § 177 | Conflates whistle-blowing with disloyalty |
| "Friedman view is the modern standard" | Wrong — India follows Stakeholder Theory via SEBI/MCA norms | Treat Friedman's 1970 essay as current policy |
| "CSR is voluntary" | Wrong — Section 135 mandates 2% spend for qualifying companies | Confuses CSR with philanthropy |
| "Insider trading is only unethical, not illegal" | Wrong — both SEBI PIT 2015 and ethical codes apply | Treats ethics and law as mutually exclusive |

#### Limits and Boundary Conditions

- **CSR applicability threshold** — mandatory only when a company meets **any three** of these in the preceding FY: turnover ≥ ₹1,000 crore, or net worth ≥ ₹500 crore, or net profit ≥ ₹5 crore.
- **Whistle-blower protection scope** — applies to listed companies and prescribed classes; protection is lost if the disclosure is frivolous or motivated by personal gain.
- **Independent directors** under Section 149(6) must not have any pecuniary relationship exceeding ₹50 lakh or 10% of turnover, depending on threshold.
- **Fiduciary duty (Section 166)** — directors must act in good faith, promote the objects of the company, and avoid conflicts of interest; breach attracts penalties under Section 447 (fraud).

#### Connections to Adjacent RBI Topics

- **Corporate Governance** is tested independently and overlaps heavily — SEBI's LODR Regulations, 2015 mandate the board composition, audit committee, and disclosures discussed here.
- **Banking Ethics** — RBI's 2020 charter on customer fairness, the 2015 **Fair Practices Code** for lenders, and the **BCSBI** (Banking Codes and Standards Board of India) code rest on the same ELFIC principles.
- **ESG and Sustainability** — the Triple Bottom Line feeds directly into RBI's 2021–22 discussion paper on climate-related financial disclosures.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. A PSU bank sanctions a loan to a promoter group firm in which one board member's spouse holds a 12% stake. Apply Section 166, the conflict-of-interest test, and Potter Box to determine whether the sanction is ethical. Recommend the remedial disclosure route under SEBI LODR.
2. A fintech NBFC's CSR budget for FY 2024–25 must be calculated using the average net profits of FY 2021–22, 2022–23, and 2023–24: ₹80 cr, ₹120 cr, and ₹100 cr respectively. Compute the mandatory spend under Section 135 and list two Schedule VII activities the NBFC cannot fund.
