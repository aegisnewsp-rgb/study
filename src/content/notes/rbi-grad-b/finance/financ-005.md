# Ratio Analysis

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Ratio Analysis expresses relationships between items on the Balance Sheet, Profit & Loss account, and Cash Flow Statement to judge a firm's liquidity, solvency, profitability, and efficiency. The four must-know formulas for the RBI Grade B Phase-II Finance paper:

- **Current Ratio** = Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities
- **Quick Ratio** = (Current Assets − Inventory − Prepaid Expenses) ÷ Current Liabilities
- **Debt–Equity Ratio** = Total Debt ÷ Shareholders' Equity
- **Interest Coverage Ratio** = EBIT ÷ Interest Expense

High-yield pointers: DuPont decomposes ROE into Net Profit Margin × Asset Turnover × Equity Multiplier, so a high ROE driven purely by leverage signals risk, not quality. RBI examiners test banking ratios (CRAR, NPA%, NIM) more often than generic industrial ratios — read any Balance-Sheet problem with that lens.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Classification of Ratios

Ratios group into five functional categories. Each answers a distinct decision question a supervisor, lender, or investor asks.

| Category | What it measures | Key ratios |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Liquidity | Short-term debt-servicing ability | Current Ratio, Quick Ratio |
| Solvency / Leverage | Long-term financial risk | Debt–Equity, Interest Coverage |
| Activity / Turnover | Asset utilisation efficiency | Asset Turnover, Inventory Turnover |
| Profitability | Earnings performance | Net Profit Margin, ROE, ROA |
| Market / Valuation | Investor perspective | EPS, P/E Ratio, Dividend Payout |

#### DuPont Decomposition of ROE

DuPont Analysis breaks ROE into three multiplicative drivers, which lets you identify whether profit margin, sales productivity, or leverage is producing the return. The identity:

ROE = (Net Profit ÷ Sales) × (Sales ÷ Total Assets) × (Total Assets ÷ Equity)

This is equivalent to: ROE = Net Profit Margin × Asset Turnover × Equity Multiplier. A 20% ROE achieved through a 5% margin and 4× asset turnover is structurally stronger than the same 20% driven by 2% margin and 5× leverage.

#### Banking-Specific Metrics

When the question stem is a bank's balance sheet, switch to CAMELS-style indicators:

- **CRAR (Capital to Risk-Weighted Assets Ratio)** — must satisfy RBI's Basel III minimum.
- **Gross/Net NPA%** — credit-quality measure.
- **Net Interest Margin (NIM)** = (Interest Earned − Interest Expended) ÷ Average Interest-Earning Assets.
- **Cost-to-Income Ratio** = Operating Expenses ÷ Net Income.

#### Common Exam Traps

- Treating the Current Ratio as the acid-test — Quick Ratio is the stricter test because it strips inventory and prepaid items.
- Using Net Profit in Interest Coverage instead of EBIT, which understates coverage capacity.
- Comparing ratios across industries without a benchmark — bank Current Ratios differ fundamentally from manufacturing norms.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Reading a Ratio in Context

A single ratio is uninformative without two reference points: the firm's own prior-year trend (intra-firm) and the industry cross-section (inter-firm). A Current Ratio of 1.5 is healthy for a retailer but a red flag for a quick-service grocer. Ratio analysis is comparative, never absolute.

#### DuPont with Worked Numbers

Suppose a firm has Net Profit = ₹120 cr, Sales = ₹1,000 cr, Total Assets = ₹800 cr, and Equity = ₹400 cr.

| Driver | Calculation | Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Net Profit Margin | 120 / 1,000 | 12% |
| Asset Turnover | 1,000 / 800 | 1.25× |
| Equity Multiplier | 800 / 400 | 2.0× |
| ROE | 12% × 1.25 × 2.0 | 30% |

Notice that leverage (2.0×) is the largest contributor. Strip out the multiplier and ROE collapses to 15%, signalling that two-thirds of the headline return is debt-financed.

#### Limitations of Ratio Analysis

Ratios depend on historical cost accounts, ignore price-level changes, are vulnerable to window-dressing, and exclude qualitative factors such as management quality and governance. For banks, off-balance-sheet exposures (guarantees, derivatives) sit outside the headline ratios — supervisors therefore rely on supplementary disclosures.

#### Practice Prompts

1. A bank reports Current Assets ₹450 cr, Inventory nil, Current Liabilities ₹300 cr. Compute Current and Quick Ratios and interpret.
2. From the DuPont identity, explain why two firms with identical 25% ROE can have sharply different risk profiles.
