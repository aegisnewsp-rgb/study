# Variety of Life

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Taxonomy** is the branch of biology dealing with the description, identification, naming, and classification of organisms, while **systematics** is the broader study of evolutionary relationships among them. R.H. **Whittaker's** 1969 Five Kingdom Classification sorts life into **Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, and Animalia** based on cell structure (prokaryotic vs eukaryotic), body organization (unicellular vs multicellular), mode of nutrition (autotrophic, heterotrophic, saprotrophic), and phylogenetic relationships. **Binomial nomenclature**, formalised by Carolus Linnaeus in *Species Plantarum* (1753) for plants, gives every species a two-word Latin name: *Homo sapiens* style — *Genus* (capitalized) + *species epithet* (lowercase), both italicised or underlined. NEET typically asks 1–2 questions from this unit, mostly on kingdom features, bacterial groups, and virus structure. Memorise the **peculiar organisms**: *Mycoplasma* (wall-less), diatoms (silica frustules), euglenoids (mixotrophic), TMV (RNA virus), viroids (RNA only).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Taxonomic Hierarchy and Nomenclature
The taxonomic hierarchy arranges organisms in descending rank: **Kingdom → Division (Phylum in animals) → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species**. A *taxon* is any named group at any rank. The rules of plant naming are governed by the **International Code of Nomenclature for algae, fungi, and plants (ICN)**, while bacteria follow the **ICNP** (prokaryotic code). Binomial nomenclature demands: (1) Latin, (2) genus capital, species lowercase, (3) italic/underlined print, (4) authority name in roman after the species, e.g., *Mangifera indica* Linn.

#### Five Kingdom Classification
| Kingdom | Cell | Nutrition | Cell Wall | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Monera** | Prokaryotic | Autotrophic/Heterotrophic | Peptidoglycan (bacteria); pseudopeptidoglycan (archaea) | Bacteria, *Nostoc*, *Mycoplasma* |
| **Protista** | Eukaryotic | Photo/heterotrophic | Variable (cellulose, silica, absent) | Diatoms, *Amoeba*, *Euglena* |
| **Fungi** | Eukaryotic | Saprotrophic/parasitic | Chitin | *Rhizopus*, *Agaricus*, *Aspergillus* |
| **Plantae** | Eukaryotic | Autotrophic | Cellulose | Algae to angiosperms |
| **Animalia** | Eukaryotic | Heterotrophic | Absent | Sponges to mammals |

#### Salient Features of Microbial Groups
- **Monera**: Bacteria may be cocci, bacilli, spirilla; cyanobacteria (blue-green algae) fix nitrogen; *Mycoplasma* lacks a wall.
- **Protista**: Chrysophytes (diatoms) have indestructible silica walls forming diatomaceous earth; dinoflagellates have two flagella and cellulose plates; euglenoids lack a wall and are mixotrophic; slime moulds are saprotrophic.
- **Fungi**: Filamentous body of *hyphae* forms *mycelium*; classes are *Phycomycetes, Ascomycetes, Basidiomycetes, Deuteromycetes*.

#### Viruses, Viroids and Lichens
Viruses are **acellular**, consisting of a protein **capsid** enclosing **DNA or RNA**; they are obligate intracellular parasites. *Bacteriophage* T4 has head, collar, sheath, tail, tail fibres. **Viroids** are free infectious RNA without capsid (e.g., potato spindle tuber). **Lichens** are symbiosis of alga (phycobiont) + fungus (mycobiont); **Mycorrhiza** is fungal–root symbiosis aiding phosphorus uptake.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Whittaker vs Carl Woese
While Whittaker's 1969 system placed prokaryotes in a single Monera, **Carl Woese's** rRNA-based **Three-Domain System** split Monera into **Archaea** (unique membrane lipids, pseudopeptidoglycan wall, methanogens, halophiles, thermoacidophiles) and **Bacteria** (true peptidoglycan). This is the modern accepted view and is occasionally asked as an assertion–reason in NEET. The "sixth kingdom" idea adds Archaebacteria as separate.

#### Numerical Taxonomy and Cladistics
**Numerical taxonomy** (phenetics) uses computer-assisted clustering of many characters with equal weight, producing a **phenogram**; **cladistics** groups organisms by *shared derived* (synapomorphic) characters, producing a **cladogram**. Cladistics is now standard, but in NEET stick to the classical hierarchy unless specifically asked.

#### Common Mistakes and Traps
1. **Mycoplasma** is often miscalled a virus — it is the smallest *cellular* organism (no wall), insensitive to penicillin.
2. **Diatom** frustules leave **diatomaceous earth** used in polishing and filtration.
3. **Euglena** is **not** a plant — no cellulose wall, has pellicle and flagellum.
4. **TMV** is RNA-based; do not write "DNA virus".
5. **Lichens** are *pioneer colonisers* of bare rock and bioindicators of SO₂ pollution; **mycorrhiza** is a *root association*, not a stem association.

#### Worked Practice Prompts
**Q1.** Identify the organism: acellular, protein coat, genetic material is ssRNA, obligate parasite — and name the disease of potato spindle tuber.
*Answer*: Virus in general; the potato spindle tuber agent is a **viroid** (free RNA, no capsid).
**Q2.** Place *Nostoc* and *Aspergillus* in their kingdoms with one key feature each.
*Answer*: *Nostoc* — Monera, prokaryotic nitrogen-fixing cyanobacterium; *Aspergillus* — Fungi, septate mycelium reproducing by conidia (Deuteromycetes).

#### Exam Strategy
In NEET, this unit contributes ~1–2 MCQs (out of 90), often disguised with a microbial name and asking for the kingdom. Learn **prefix-meaning** cues (*-mycota* = fungus, *-phyta* = plant, *-zoa* = animal). Pair this note with "Cell: The Unit of Life" and "Biological Classification" revision for cross-questions on prokaryotic cell envelope.
