# Genetic Disorders

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Genetic disorders are diseases produced by heritable alterations in DNA sequence, chromosomal structure, or chromosome number, manifesting as single-gene (Mendelian), chromosomal, or multifactorial traits. INI CET Pathology questions cluster around three families: Mendelian pedigree interpretation, chromosomal syndromes (especially the trisomies and imprinting defects), and trinucleotide repeat disorders with anticipation.

- **Must-know formula:** Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium — p² + 2pq + q² = 1, with p + q = 1; disease prevalence = q², carrier frequency = 2pq.
- **High-yield one-liners:** Down = trisomy 21; Edwards = trisomy 18; Patau = trisomy 13; Turner = 45,X; Klinefelter = 47,XXY; cri-du-chat = 5p deletion; DiGeorge = 22q11.2 deletion; Philadelphia = acquired t(9;22), **not** inherited.
- **Repeat triplets:** Huntington = CAG, myotonic dystrophy = CTG, fragile X = CGG, Friedreich ataxia = GAA.
- **Imprinting contrast:** Prader–Willi = paternal 15q defect (hyperphagia, obesity); Angelman = maternal 15q defect (ataxia, laughter).

| Pattern | Risk per pregnancy (one affected/carrier parent) | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Autosomal dominant | 1/2 each pregnancy | Marfan, Huntington |
| Autosomal recessive (both carriers) | 1/4 affected, 1/2 carrier | Cystic fibrosis, PKU |
| X-linked recessive | 1/2 of sons affected | Haemophilia A, DMD |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Classification and inheritance patterns

Genetic disorders are conventionally grouped into single-gene (Mendelian), chromosomal (numeric and structural), and multifactorial categories. Mendelian disorders follow autosomal dominant (heterozygous loss- or gain-of-function, e.g., Marfan syndrome from FBN1 mutation, Huntington disease from CAG expansion in HTT), autosomal recessive (both alleles defective, e.g., cystic fibrosis from CFTR ΔF508, phenylketonuria from PAH deficiency, Tay–Sachs HEXA mutation), X-linked dominant (Rett syndrome MECP2, incontinentia pigmenti), X-linked recessive (Duchenne muscular dystrophy dystrophin, haemophilia A factor VIII, G6PD deficiency), and mitochondrial patterns (heteroplasmy, maternal-only transmission: MELAS, MERRF, LHON).

#### Hardy–Weinberg application

The equation p² + 2pq + q² = 1 is applied to autosomal recessive disease where q is the disease allele. If disease prevalence = q², the carrier frequency is **2pq**, not q² — a common inversion error.

#### Chromosomal syndromes

Numeric aneuploidies arise from meiotic nondisjunction. Down syndrome is full trisomy 21 in ~95% of cases (maternal meiosis I), with ~4% translocation (typically Robertsonian 14;21) and ~1% mosaic forms; recurrence risk differs markedly across these subtypes. Sex chromosome aneuploidies include Turner (45,X) and Klinefelter (47,XXY). Structural lesions — deletions (cri-du-chat 5p−, DiGeorge 22q11.2), inversions, isochromosomes, and ring chromosomes — produce contiguous-gene syndromes.

#### Trinucleotide repeats, imprinting, and lysosomal storage

Repeats expand across generations producing anticipation: Huntington (CAG), myotonic dystrophy (CTG), fragile X (CGG in FMR1), Friedreich ataxia (GAA in frataxin). Imprinting disorders at 15q11–q13 split by parent-of-origin into Prader–Willi (paternal) and Angelman (maternal/UBE3A). Lysosomal storage diseases (Gaucher, Niemann–Pick, Tay–Sachs, Fabry, Pompe, MPS) accumulate substrate within lysosomes and present with hepatosplenomegaly and neurodegeneration.

- Penetrance vs expressivity: penetrance = proportion expressing phenotype; expressivity = severity spectrum.
- Pleiotropy = one gene → multiple organ effects (e.g., Marfan).
- Mosaicism = somatic cells with differing genotypes from one zygote.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Oncogenetics and the two-hit hypothesis

Proto-oncogenes require only **one** activating allele (gain-of-function, dominant): RAS, MYC, BCR–ABL1 (Philadelphia t(9;22)(q34;q11) creating the chimeric tyrosine kinase in CML), HER2/ERBB2, BCL2. Tumour-suppressor genes require **two** inactivated alleles (loss-of-function, recessive at the cellular level) per Knudson's two-hit model: RB1 (retinoblastoma), TP53 (Li–Fraumeni), BRCA1/BRCA2, APC (FAP), NF1/NF2, VHL, WT1. Distinguishing these mechanisms is a frequently tested point in INI CET stems on familial cancer syndromes.

#### Mitochondrial inheritance pitfalls

Mitochondrial DNA is maternally inherited; affected fathers do **not** transmit. Heteroplasmy means the mutant load varies across oocytes, producing a threshold effect where high-energy tissues (brain, muscle, heart, retina) manifest first. MELAS (mitochondrial encephalomyopathy, lactic acidosis, stroke-like episodes) and MERRF (myoclonic epilepsy with ragged-red fibres) are classic stems.

#### Detection methods and newborn screening

Karyotyping detects aneuploidy and large rearrangements; FISH identifies specific loci; chromosomal microarray (CGH/SNP) finds microdeletions/microduplications; MLPA detects exon-level copy changes; Sanger sequencing confirms single-gene mutations; NGS panels and whole-exome/genome sequencing are increasingly the standard for undiagnosed cases. Prenatal diagnosis uses chorionic villus sampling (10–12 weeks) and amniocentesis (15–18 weeks); newborn screening employs tandem mass spectrometry for aminoacidopathies, organic acidurias, and fatty-acid oxidation defects.

#### Common INI CET traps

- Treating the Philadelphia chromosome as a constitutional germline translocation.
- Inverting q² and 2pq in Hardy–Weinberg calculations.
- Confusing parent-of-origin for Prader–Willi vs Angelman.
- Forgetting that 21;21 Robertsonian translocation carriers transmit trisomy 21 with near-100% recurrence risk.

#### Practice prompts:

1. A 25-year-old woman, asymptomatic, has a brother with haemophilia A. Calculate the probability her son is affected using Hardy–Weinberg assumptions if carrier frequency = 2pq = 1/5,000.
2. A newborn with 45,X karyotype, webbed neck, and coarctation — identify the syndrome and explain why recurrence risk in the next pregnancy is not markedly increased.
