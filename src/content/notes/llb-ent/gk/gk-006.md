# Key South African Legislation

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Three acts carry one in three marks on this topic. Memorise their numbers before anything else.

The Constitution of the Republic of South Africa, 1996 is the supreme law; every statute is read subject to its terms, and section 172 lets a court strike down inconsistent legislation. The Promotion of Administrative Justice Act 3 of 2000 (PAJA) sets the grounds on which administrative action can be reviewed — lawfulness, reasonableness, and procedural fairness are the three legs. The Labour Relations Act 66 of 1995 governs unfair dismissal and collective bargaining.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Reading order matters: constitutional supremacy first, then administrative law, then the labour and consumer statutes that test its application.

#### Constitutional Supremacy and the Bill of Rights
Section 2 of the Constitution declares it supreme; section 172 empowers the Constitutional Court, the High Court, and the Supreme Court of Appeal to invalidate inconsistent law. Section 39(2) obliges every court to interpret legislation in a way that promotes the spirit, purport, and objects of the Bill of Rights. The Bill of Rights binds the State directly, and binds private parties where the right is "just and applicable" between them — Du Plessis v De Klerk 1996 (3) SA 850 (CC) is the leading authority on direct versus indirect horizontal application.

#### PAJA: The Three-Legged Review Test
PAJA 3 of 2000 codifies review under section 33 of the Constitution. The action must be lawful (authorised by the empowering provision), reasonable (rationally connected to the purpose, no less restrictive means available), and procedurally fair (notice, opportunity to be heard). Section 7 requires internal remedies to be exhausted within 180 days before review is launched — a procedural bar examiners love to test.

#### The Labour Trilogy
LRA 66 of 1995 protects employees against unfair dismissal and unfair labour practices; it establishes the CCMA and bargaining councils. BCEA 75 of 1997 sets minimum conditions of employment (working hours, leave, overtime, pay). EEA 55 of 1998 drives transformation: designated employers must submit employment equity plans and report on workforce demographics to the Department of Employment and Labour.

#### Worked Scenario: Reviewing a Refusal
A municipal housing officer refuses to allocate a subsidized house to an applicant without giving reasons, citing a waiting list policy. The applicant wants to challenge the refusal. The applicant first lodges an internal appeal with the municipality's housing committee within 90 days of the refusal. If the internal appeal fails, the applicant must launch review proceedings in the High Court within 180 days of the original refusal — that 180-day window is set by section 7(1) of PAJA, not by the Promotion of Administrative Justice Act's general limitation rules.

The High Court will test the refusal against three grounds. Was the officer authorised to allocate houses? Was the policy rationally connected to the waiting list criteria, or did it exclude eligible applicants arbitrarily? Was the applicant given notice of the refusal and a chance to respond? If any leg fails, the refusal is set aside under section 8 of PAJA.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Applicants who skip internal remedies or who file in court before the 180-day clock starts are barred — section 7(1) reads "must" exhaust, not "may". Examinees often invent a "reasonable time" extension that the Act does not provide.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Edge cases surface in scenario questions, not in straight recall. The four traps below earn marks students lose most often.

#### Advanced Traps and Boundary Conditions

1. **Two CPA acronyms.** The Criminal Procedure Act 51 of 1977 (CPA — criminal) is unrelated to the Consumer Protection Act 68 of 2008 (CPA — consumer). Reading the stem carefully prevents this collapse.
2. **POPIA commencement phasing.** POPIA 4 of 2013 was signed in 2013, but most processing provisions only took effect from 1 July 2021. An answer citing the signature date is correct; citing full operational date as 2013 is wrong.
3. **Customary marriages.** The Recognition of Customary Marriages Act 120 of 1998 validates polygamous customary marriages entered into before its commencement, subject to registration — a frequent source of confusion with the Marriage Act 25 of 1961, which regulates civil marriages.
4. **Companies Act 71 of 2008 — business judgment rule.** Section 76(4) presumes a director acted on an informed basis, in good faith, and in the best interests of the company. A plaintiff must rebut this presumption, not the other way round.

#### Connections Worth Linking
- PAJA review grounds overlap with constitutional review under section 33 — a litigant can approach PAJA first and constitutional review in the alternative.
- The LRA's unfair-dismissal test (substantive fairness + procedural fairness) borrows language from the BCEA's procedural codes.
- POPIA and the Consumer Protection Act intersect where suppliers process customer data; POPIA controls the data side, CPA controls the commercial side.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts
1. A private security company dismisses a guard without a hearing. Does the Constitution apply directly to the company, or only indirectly through the LRA's unfair-dismissal machinery? Apply *Du Plessis v De Klerk* and section 8(2) of the Constitution to construct the answer.
2. A credit provider grants a loan without assessing the consumer's income. Under which Act is the loan potentially declared reckless, and what remedy is available to the consumer? Distinguish the role of the National Consumer Tribunal from that of a court under that Act.
