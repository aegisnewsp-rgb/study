# Six Best Subjects Rule

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your CAO application.

**Six Best Subjects Rule** — Quick Facts for Irish Leaving Certificate students

**What is the rule?**
Your CAO points are calculated from your **six best Leaving Certificate subjects** only. If you sit seven or more subjects, only the six with the highest CPS values count toward your aggregate. Foundation Level Mathematics does NOT contribute to your points even though the grade appears on your results.

**At a glance:**
- Subjects counted: exactly **six** best-scoring subjects
- Subjects required for Leaving Certificate: 6+ (most students sit 7–8)
- Foundation Level Maths: grade recorded, but NOT counted toward the six best
- Order of application: pick six best → add +25 HL Maths bonus (if H6+) → cap at 625
- Tie-breaking: identical CPS values across subjects — the system takes the six highest by CPS first, then by a pre-published random tie-breaker

**Why it matters for offers:**
The six-best-subjects rule means strategically weak subjects do not hurt you if you have six stronger ones. Conversely, dropping below six scoring subjects (because some are Foundation or below O-level pass) means your aggregate is capped before the bonus is applied.

⚡ **Quick rule:** Sit out to qualify for the six best — every extra subject you sit gives you one more chance of pushing a weak grade out of the six best. Most students sit seven subjects so they have a spare; some sit eight.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

**Six Best Subjects Rule** — CAO Points Study Guide

**Who is affected?**
Every Irish Leaving Certificate candidate submitting a CAO application is scored under this rule. You are scored under the rule regardless of whether you sit six, seven, or eight+ subjects. The six-best calculation always uses the **six highest CPS values** from your set of subjects.

**The standard set of subjects:**
Most Leaving Certificate candidates take:
- English (compulsory, OL or HL)
- Mathematics (compulsory — OL, HL, or Foundation)
- A modern European language (and optional classical languages)
- Three or four additional subjects at Higher or Ordinary Level
- (Many add a seventh or eighth subject for breadth or as a fallback)

The Leaving Certificate minimum is six subjects, including English, but most students sit seven or eight to give themselves a buffer for the six-best rule.

**What is counted and what is NOT counted:**

| Subject level | Counting rule |
|---|---|
| Higher Level subject | Counts at its full CPS value (H1 = 100 down to H8 = 0) |
| Ordinary Level subject | Counts at its full CPS value (O1 = 56 down to O8 = 0) |
| Foundation Level subject | Grade recorded on the transcript, but contributes **0 points** to the aggregate |
| LCVP / JCPA link module | Counts as one subject: Distinction = 66, Merit = 46, Pass = 28 |
| Other vocational / non-exam subjects | Counts only if approved by CAO for that entry route |

**How the six best are selected:**
CAO takes every points-bearing subject on your result set, converts each grade to its CPS value, and keeps the six highest. Order is irrelevant — only the *sum* of the six highest values is used, so two subjects with identical CPS values are interchangeable and there is never a tie to break. Those six values are summed into a **raw six-subject aggregate**.

**Foundation Level Maths treatment:**
Foundation Level Mathematics is recorded on your transcript as F1, F2, or F3 (similar to other grades). However:
- F1, F2, F3 do NOT contribute any CPS value to your aggregate
- Foundation Level Maths is NOT counted as one of your six best subjects
- A pass in Foundation Maths does NOT unlock the +25 HL Maths bonus — that bonus is a Higher Level award, and Foundation results carry no H-grades
- Foundation Maths does NOT meet the minimum OL Maths (O6) matriculation requirement for many Level 8 STEM programmes (see cao-005)

Foundation Maths is, in effect, recorded but invisible to CAO points.

**Worked example 1:** Subjects and grades: English H2, Maths H4, Irish H3, Biology H3, Chemistry H2, Physics H1, History H4, Art H2. CPS values: Physics 100, English 88, Chemistry 88, Art 88, Irish 77, Biology 77, Maths 66, History 66. The six highest are 100 + 88 + 88 + 88 + 77 + 77 = **518**. HL Maths is **H4**, which is *above* the H6 threshold — H1 is the highest Leaving Cert grade and the scale runs downwards, so H1 to H6 all qualify. The bonus applies. Aggregate = 518 + 25 = **543**.

**Worked example 2:** Subjects and grades: English O1, Maths F2, Irish O1, Biology O1, Chemistry O1, Physics H2, History O1, Geography O1. CPS values: Physics 88, then six subjects at 56 (English, Irish, Biology, Chemistry, History, Geography), and Maths F2 at 0. The six highest are 88 + 56 + 56 + 56 + 56 + 56 = **368** — the seventh 56 is dropped, and Foundation Maths is 0 so it can never take a place. There is no Higher Level Maths result at all, so no bonus applies. Aggregate = **368**.

**Worked example 3:** Subjects and grades: English H3, Maths H6, Irish H4, Biology H5, Chemistry H6, Physics H5, Music H5. CPS values: English 77, Irish 66, Biology 56, Physics 56, Music 56, Maths 46, Chemistry 46. The six highest are 77 + 66 + 56 + 56 + 56 + 46 = **357** — the spare Chemistry H6 is the seventh value and is dropped. HL Maths is **H6, exactly the threshold, so the bonus applies.** Aggregate = 357 + 25 = **382**.

**How Foundation Maths compares to OL Maths:**
- OL Maths: O1 = 56; O2 = 46; O3 = 37; O4 = 28; O5 = 20; O6 = 12; O7 = 0
- Foundation Maths F1, F2, F3 = 0 points (regardless of grade)
- The +25 bonus is a Higher Level award only — no OL or Foundation Maths result unlocks it, however good
- Some Level 8 STEM programmes require OL Maths O6 minimum, NOT Foundation Maths

Foundation Maths applicants often apply to Level 7/Level 8 courses that accept Foundation Maths as a matriculation requirement (see cao-005).

**Edge case — six subjects only:**
If you sit exactly six subjects and your Foundation Maths is one of them, you have only **five scoring subjects** because Foundation Maths contributes 0 points. The aggregate is the sum of those five plus the +25 bonus (if HL Maths H6+ is in the five). For this reason, candidates who anticipate a Foundation Maths result typically sit **seven subjects**, ensuring six scoring subjects even if Maths is filtered.

**Special provision for LCVP link modules:**
An LCVP (or JCPA) link module is scored on the points scale and can take one of your six places: **Distinction = 66, Merit = 46, Pass = 28**. A link module is not a token pass — a Distinction beats an O1 (56) and sits alongside a solid H4, making it one of the strongest single results available outside the traditional exam subjects. It counts only when it lands inside your six highest values.

**Special provision for modern European languages:**
Modern European languages at HL or OL contribute the full CPS value. There is no language-specific reduction or bonus in the six-best calculation (some French / Spanish entrants expected a language bonus historically — that was retired under the 2017 Common Points Scale reform).

**The random number allocation and tie-breaking:**
If two candidates tie on aggregate, the tie is broken by a **random number** generated when the application is submitted (see cao-006). The random number is independent of the order of course choices and is fixed at the application submission moment. The six-best rule never breaks a tie by subject choice.

**When the rule was introduced:**
The six-best-subjects rule was introduced alongside the 2017 Common Points Scale reform. Before 2017, candidates could use the **best five subjects** for some courses and **all seven subjects** for others; the 2017 reform collapsed this to a single six-best rule for all Level 7 and Level 8 courses. The six-best rule has been stable since 2017.

**Course-specific variations:**
Some Level 8 courses (e.g., medicine, veterinary, dental science) have **specific subject requirements** that must be met regardless of the six-best CPS calculation. The six-best rule calculates your aggregate; the course-specific requirements determine whether you are eligible to be considered. For example, a medicine course might require H6 in Chemistry and H6 in Biology; even with 625 points, you are not eligible if you lack H6 in either. See cao-005 for the full matriculation minimum requirements.

**Combined with the 625-point cap:**
The six-best-subjects sum is calculated, the +25 HL Maths bonus is added (if applicable), and the aggregate is capped at 625. For most candidates, the cap does not apply because six H1s plus the bonus is the only way to reach 625. See cao-002 for the bonus rule and the cap.

**Frequently asked questions:**
- "If I take seven subjects and four are H1, does the six-best rule help me?" — Only if your four H1s are among your six best, which they would be by definition; the six-best rule just gives you a buffer to drop a weak subject.
- "Does sitting more than six subjects ever hurt me?" — No. The rule always selects the six best by CPS value; an extra subject can only HELP your aggregate by displacing a weaker subject.
- "Can I pick which six subjects count?" — No. CAO automatically selects the six with the highest CPS values; you do not have the option to drop a strong subject in favour of a weaker one.


### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> For students choosing a full subject set and protecting against a bad exam day.

**Six Best Subjects Rule** — Advanced CAO Points Analysis

**The mechanism in one line:** convert every points-bearing result to its CPS value, sum the six highest, add 25 if HL Maths is H1–H6, cap the total at 625. Nothing else enters the calculation.

**Two consequences that are routinely misunderstood:**

1. **An extra subject can never cost you points.** A seventh result either raises your six highest values or leaves them unchanged. There is no penalty, no averaging and no dilution — the weakest results simply are not selected. Candidates often drop a subject they are unsure about, believing it will count against them. It cannot.
2. **Subjects with identical CPS values are interchangeable.** Only the sum is used, so an O1 (56) and an H5 (56) occupy the same slot. Level of study matters enormously for your ceiling (56 versus 100) but makes no difference to the selection once the values are equal.

**When a seventh subject genuinely pays:**
It only pays if its value beats the current weakest of your six.

- Weakest of your six at 37 → a seventh subject worth 56 or more improves your aggregate.
- Weakest of your six at 56 → only a subject worth 66+ moves the total.
- Weakest of your six at 88 or more → a seventh subject is very unlikely to add points.

Past that point extra subjects earn their place through **insurance**, not arithmetic: if one of your six underperforms, the seventh result replaces it automatically. A candidate with seven strong subjects has a buffer a candidate with six has no use for.

**Foundation Maths is a subject-count risk, not only a points loss:**
Foundation Maths contributes 0 points, so it never occupies one of the six. Sitting it among six subjects leaves you with only five scoring results — one bad exam away from a materially lower aggregate. Candidates expecting a Foundation result generally sit seven subjects so six scoring results remain available.

**LCVP and JCPA link modules are a real points route, not a token pass:**
A link module scores **Distinction 66, Merit 46, Pass 28** and can take one of your six places. A Distinction is worth more than an O1 (56) and can displace a weak result in your six. The binding constraint is usually not the points but the entry requirement — many Level 7 courses will not accept a link module in place of a required subject, so it only helps where your other subjects already satisfy the stated requirements.

**The aggregate is not eligibility:**
Course entry requirements sit outside the points calculation. A medicine course may require H6 in Chemistry and H6 in Biology; no aggregate, however high, substitutes for a missing requirement. Read the requirements, not only the cut-off.

**Other bonus-point rules exist:**
CAO's published grid also sets out separate bonus points for E grades in certain subjects. Those rules are not covered here, and both the eligible subjects and their values have changed over time — check the grid page directly rather than relying on anyone's summary, including this one.

**Rehearse the arithmetic, not the concept:**
Most candidates who genuinely understand this rule still lose marks on arithmetic under pressure. Before you submit, recalculate your own aggregate from the published grid using only the grades you actually have, then check it a second time against the CAO calculator. Write the six values out in descending order and sum them by hand — a running total is where the errors hide.

### 📝 Previous Year Question Patterns
- Q: "How many Leaving Certificate subjects are counted toward a candidate's CAO aggregate?" — A: Six, selected automatically by CAO from the candidate's full subject set by highest CPS value [2024 CAO Handbook §2.2]
- Q: "Does Foundation Level Mathematics contribute to a candidate's six-best-subjects aggregate?" — A: No — Foundation Maths is recorded on the transcript but contributes 0 points and is not counted toward the six best [2024 CAO Handbook §2.2]
- Q: "If a candidate sits seven subjects and one is Foundation Maths, how many scoring subjects does the candidate have for the six-best calculation?" — A: Six scoring subjects (Foundation Maths is the seventh but contributes 0 points); the six best are the six scoring subjects with the highest CPS values [2024 CAO Handbook §2.2]
- Q: "If a candidate's six best subjects include both Higher and Ordinary level grades, are both included?" — A: Yes — both HL and OL grades count toward the six best at their full CPS values; the rule does not favour HL over OL except for the HL Maths bonus [2024 CAO Handbook §2.2]

### 💡 Pro Tips
- Sit 7 subjects minimum if you anticipate a Foundation Maths result — you need 6 scoring subjects, and Foundation Maths is invisible to CAO points
- Take HL Maths if you can — even at H6, the +25 bonus effectively makes HL Maths the highest-value subject per unit of effort
- Plan your 7-subject or 8-subject set so that your "drop six" is by choice, not by accident — pick a 7th subject with strong grade potential to give yourself a buffer
- Foundation Maths applicants often apply to Level 7/Level 8 courses that accept Foundation Maths as a matriculation requirement (see cao-005) — those courses usually publish the minimum Foundation Maths grade
- The six-best rule NEVER grades against you for sitting more subjects — extra subjects only help, never hurt
- For Level 8 STEM courses (engineering, science, computing, pharmacy), HL Maths is usually a course-specific requirement INDEPENDENT of the +25 bonus — check the course handbook for your specific course
