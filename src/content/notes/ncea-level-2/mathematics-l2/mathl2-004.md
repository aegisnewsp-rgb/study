# Apply trigonometric relationships in solving problems (91259) — NCEA Level 2 Mathematics Notes

Achievement Standard 91259 carries 3 credits and tests trigonometric relationships including the sine rule, cosine rule, area of a triangle using trigonometry, and 3D trigonometry. It is internally assessed by the school and is the prerequisite for 91575 (Apply trigonometric methods) at Level 3. Most schools schedule 91259 in Term 1 or early Term 2 so the techniques are available for the rest of the year.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **3 credits**, internally assessed by the school, NZQA moderated.
- Topics: sine rule, cosine rule, area of a triangle (½ab sin C), 3D trigonometry (angles and distances in 3D space), exact trig values for 0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 90°.
- **Merit** requires relational thinking — choosing the right rule for the given information.
- **Excellence** requires extended abstract thinking — connecting the rules, justifying method selection.

#### Examiner traps

- Using the sine rule when the triangle is not angle- or side-opposite (the rule requires care with the ambiguous case).
- Forgetting that the cosine rule works for all triangles (sine rule has a restriction).
- Mixing radians and degrees — NCEA trigonometry uses degrees.
- 3D errors — confusing the horizontal plane angle with the angle of elevation.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Sine rule

For any triangle with sides a, b, c opposite to angles A, B, C:

a / sin A = b / sin B = c / sin C

Use the sine rule when you have:

- Two angles and a side (AAS, ASA), to find another side.
- Two sides and a non-included angle (SSA — the ambiguous case), to find another angle.

The ambiguous case: if you have a, b, and A, you can find sin B = (b sin A)/a. If sin B > 1, no triangle exists. If sin B ≤ 1, there are two possible values for B (B and 180° − B), giving zero, one, or two triangles.

#### Cosine rule

For any triangle with sides a, b, c opposite to angles A, B, C:

a² = b² + c² − 2bc cos A

The cosine rule generalises Pythagoras' theorem. Use it when you have:

- Three sides (SSS), to find any angle.
- Two sides and the included angle (SAS), to find the third side.

The cosine rule works for all triangles, no ambiguous case.

#### Area of a triangle

Area = ½ × a × b × sin C

where a and b are two sides and C is the included angle (the angle between a and b).

Alternatively:

Area = ½ × base × height

Use the ½ab sin C form when the height is not given but two sides and the included angle are.

#### 3D trigonometry

3D trigonometry problems embed a triangle in 3D space. The standard approach:

1. Identify the triangle that contains the angle or distance asked for.
2. Reduce the 3D problem to a 2D triangle by drawing a 2D cross-section.
3. Apply the sine rule, cosine rule, or right-angled trigonometry as appropriate.

**Angle of elevation:** the angle measured up from the horizontal to a point above.

**Angle of depression:** the angle measured down from the horizontal to a point below.

**Bearing:** the angle measured clockwise from north. A bearing of 060° means 60° clockwise from north.

#### Exact trig values

Memorise the exact values for 0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 90°:

| Angle | sin | cos | tan |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0° | 0 | 1 | 0 |
| 30° | 1/2 | √3/2 | 1/√3 = √3/3 |
| 45° | √2/2 | √2/2 | 1 |
| 60° | √3/2 | 1/2 | √3 |
| 90° | 1 | 0 | undefined |

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Choosing the right rule

For a triangle problem, the rule selection heuristic:

- **Right angle present?** Use Pythagoras' theorem and SOH-CAH-TOA.
- **Two angles and a side (AAS) or two sides and a non-included angle (SSA)?** Sine rule.
- **Three sides (SSS) or two sides and the included angle (SAS)?** Cosine rule.
- **Need area, have two sides and the included angle?** ½ab sin C.
- **Need area, have base and height?** ½ × base × height.

The Excellence move is to recognise that the cosine rule reduces to Pythagoras' theorem when the angle is 90° (because cos 90° = 0), and to use this to check calculations.

#### The ambiguous case at Level 2

The ambiguous case (SSA) appears in NCEA Excellence questions. The check is:

- If (b sin A) / a > 1, no triangle.
- If (b sin A) / a = 1, one right-angled triangle.
- If (b sin A) / a < 1, two possible triangles with B and 180° − B (both angles less than 180°).

A common Excellence-grade problem gives a side-side-angle setup and asks for the number of triangles and the third side in each case.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Sine rule always works." No — only for AAS or SSA. For SAS, use cosine rule.
- "Cosine rule is only for obtuse angles." No — cosine rule works for any triangle, acute or obtuse.
- "3D trigonometry is a different topic." It is the same topic applied to a 3D cross-section.
- "Area = ½ab sin C only works when C is the right angle." No — it works for any included angle.
