# Parliament and Central Legislature

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The Indian Parliament consists of the **President**, the **Lok Sabha** (House of the People), and the **Rajya Sabha** (Council of States) under Articles 79–122. The President is an integral part but neither House can meet without his summons. Money Bills originate and pass only in the Lok Sabha; the Rajya Sabha can recommend amendments within 14 days but cannot reject them (Article 108 does not apply to Money Bills). Anti-defection disqualification under the **10th Schedule** is decided by the Presiding Officer, subject to judicial review after *Kihoto Hollohan* (1992). An Ordinance under Article 123 lapses six weeks from Parliament's reassembly, not six weeks from promulgation.

- **Lok Sabha (max 552):** 530 from States + 20 from UTs + 2 Anglo-Indians (Anglo-Indian seats abolished by the 104th Amendment, 2019)
- **Rajya Sabha (max 250):** 238 elected + 12 nominated by the President for expertise in art, science, literature, or social service
- **Vice-President** is ex-officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha but is NOT a member of either House
- **Speaker's casting vote** is exercised only when the House is equally divided

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Composition and Constitutional Position

Article 79 declares that there shall be a Parliament for the Union consisting of the President and two Houses. The Rajya Sabha represents the states through proportional representation by single transferable vote; the Lok Sabha represents the people directly. The Anglo-Indian nomination clause in Article 333 was effectively abolished by the 104th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2019, which expired the provision after 25 January 2020.

| House | Max Strength | Election / Nomination | Term |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lok Sabha | 552 (530+20+2) | Direct adult suffrage; 2 Anglo-Indian seats lapsed in 2020 | 5 years |
| Rajya Sabha | 250 (238+12) | Single transferable vote by elected state legislators; 12 nominated | 6 years, staggered |

#### Legislative Procedure and Money Bills

A **Money Bill** under Article 110(1) must deal with imposition or abolition of tax, borrowing, custody of the Consolidated or Contingency Fund, appropriation, or audit. It is introduced only in the Lok Sabha on the President's recommendation and bears the Speaker's certificate, which is final. The Rajya Sabha may only recommend amendments within 14 days; the Lok Sabha may accept or reject these.

- **Ordinary bills** can originate in either House and require the President's assent
- **Constitutional Amendment Bills** under Article 368 require a special majority in each House (no Joint Sitting is allowed)
- **Joint Sitting** under Article 108 is summoned by the President and presided over by the Speaker when an ordinary bill is deadlocked

#### Parliamentary Control Devices

The House exercises executive oversight through Question Hour, Zero Hour, Calling Attention Motion, Half-an-Hour Discussion, Adjournment Motion, and No-Confidence Motion. The Speaker admits or rejects notices for these motions; Adjournment and No-Confidence Motions require prior leave of the House.

#### Anti-Defection Law (10th Schedule)

A member is disqualified under Paragraph 2(1)(a) for voluntarily giving up party membership or 2(1)(b) for voting or abstaining contrary to the party whip. The 91st Amendment, 2003, inserted Para 4, exempting splits where one-third of a party forms a separate group; mergers are exempt only if two-thirds of members concur.

#### Ordinance-Making Power

Article 123 empowers the President to promulgate an Ordinance when immediate action is necessary and Parliament is not in session. The Ordinance ceases to operate six weeks from the reassembly of Parliament, or earlier if both Houses pass a resolution disapproving it.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Worked Example — Anti-Defection Disqualification

Consider a 10-member legislative party in a state assembly. Three members split off on grounds of internal dissent and form a separate group. Under Para 2(1)(a), each individual faces disqualification unless the Speaker holds that giving up membership is "voluntary". Under Para 4 (inserted by the 91st Amendment, 2003), the three-member breakaway is below the one-third threshold of the original party (10/3 ≈ 3.33), so the split is NOT exempt; each member is disqualified on the Speaker's ruling. If instead four members broke away, the one-third exemption would apply because 4/10 ≥ 1/3.

#### Joint Sitting Mechanics

The Joint Sitting under Article 108 does NOT cover Money Bills or Constitutional Amendment Bills. Quorum is one-tenth of the total membership of both Houses (Article 118(2)). The Speaker presides; in his absence, the Deputy Speaker; if both are absent, the Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha. The guillotine cuts short debate and forces a vote on all pending amendments at once.

#### Parliamentary Privileges and Article 105

Article 105 confers freedom of speech in Parliament, immunity from legal proceedings for votes and speeches, and collective privileges necessary for the House's functioning. Courts cannot inquire into the validity of House proceedings under Article 122. The Supreme Court in *Pioneer Sugar Mills v. UP* (1965) ruled that privileges are subject to constitutional limitations.

#### Common Exam Traps

| Trap | Correct Position |
| --- | --- |
| Vice-President is a member of Parliament | He is ex-officio Chairman of RS but NOT a member |
| Ordinance lapses 6 weeks after promulgation | Lapses 6 weeks from Parliament's reassembly |
| All bills can go to Joint Sitting on deadlock | Money Bills and Constitutional Amendment Bills cannot |
| 10th Schedule disqualification decided by courts | Presiding Officer decides; courts review after *Kihoto Hollohan* (1992) |
| Joint Sitting presided by Vice-President | Presided by Speaker (or Deputy Speaker, then Deputy Chairman of RS) |
