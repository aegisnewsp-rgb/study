# Number Patterns and Sequences

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

A **number pattern** is an ordered list of numbers following a fixed rule. The three families tested most on NCEE Quantitative Reasoning are **Arithmetic Progression (AP)**, **Geometric Progression (GP)**, and **special sequences** (square, cube, triangular).

- **AP**: add a constant *d* each step. nth term: **Tn = a + (n − 1)d**.
- **GP**: multiply by a constant *r* each step. nth term: **Tn = a × rⁿ⁻¹**.
- **AP sum**: **Sn = n/2 × [2a + (n − 1)d]** or **n/2 × (a + l)**.

> Tip: When differences are constant → AP. When ratios are constant → GP.

Always verify the rule on three terms before extrapolating the next term.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Recognising the Pattern Type

Check the **difference** between consecutive terms first. If the gap stays the same, the sequence is arithmetic and *d* equals that gap. If the gap itself changes in a steady way (e.g., 2, 4, 6, 8…), the underlying sequence is **quadratic** — the rule sits on the differences-of-differences.

If dividing each term by the one before yields the same number, you have a **geometric** progression with common ratio *r*. NCEE questions often mix the two by adding a small twist: start by adding 1, then multiply by 2, then add 1, then multiply by 2 (alternating operations).

#### Core Formulas

| Concept | Formula | Meaning of symbols |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AP nth term | Tn = a + (n − 1)d | *a* = first term, *d* = common difference, *n* = position |
| AP sum of n terms | Sn = n/2 × [2a + (n − 1)d] | *a*, *d*, *n* as above |
| AP sum (alt.) | Sn = n/2 × (a + l) | *l* = last term (Tn) |
| GP nth term | Tn = a × rⁿ⁻¹ | *a* = first term, *r* = common ratio (r ≠ 0) |
| GP sum (r ≠ 1) | Sn = a(1 − rⁿ) / (1 − r) | *a*, *r*, *n* as above |

#### Typical NCEE Question Types

- **Missing-term problems** (e.g., 3, 7, 11, __, 19 → answer 15).
- **Next-term prediction** after showing 4–5 terms.
- **nth-term computation** ("find the 12th term of 5, 9, 13, …").
- **Applied AP/GP** in savings, populations, or measurements.
- **Pattern decoding** using triangular (n(n+1)/2), square (n²), or cube (n³) numbers.

> Tip: For "find the middle term of an AP with 21 terms," the middle term equals the mean of the first and last terms — no need to list them.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Worked Example (AP)

Find the 20th term and the sum of the first 20 terms of 4, 7, 10, 13, …

Here *a* = 4 and *d* = 3. T20 = 4 + (20 − 1) × 3 = 4 + 57 = **61**. The 20th term is 61.
S20 = 20/2 × [2(4) + (20 − 1) × 3] = 10 × [8 + 57] = 10 × 65 = **650**.

The sum equals 650. Note how the alternative formula n/2 × (a + l) gives 10 × (4 + 61) = 650 — same answer, fewer steps when *l* is already known.

#### Edge Cases and Common Traps

| Trap | What goes wrong | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Indexing | Using *n* instead of *(n − 1)* in AP | T1 = a (not a + d); T2 = a + d |
| Constant GP | Applying Sn = a(1 − rⁿ)/(1 − r) when r = 1 | Use Sn = n × a |
| Two-term guess | Extrapolating after only 2 terms | Verify on a 3rd term first |
| Hidden context | Missing that a word problem hides an AP or GP | Translate words into first term + rule |
| Alternating pattern | Treating mixed operations as one rule | Split into two sub-patterns and alternate |

#### Practice Prompts

1. The 5th term of an AP is 23 and the 12th term is 58. Find *a* and *d*, then the 20th term.
2. A GP has *a* = 3 and *r* = 2. Compute S10 and verify against the formula a(rⁿ − 1)/(r − 1).

> Exam tip: NCEE Quantitative Reasoning devotes roughly 2–4 questions to this topic per paper. Spend under 90 seconds per item — recognise AP/GP on sight, plug into the formula, and move on.
