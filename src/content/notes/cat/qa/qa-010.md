# Circles

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

A circle is the locus of points in a plane at a fixed distance (radius **r**) from a fixed point (the **centre**). The standard equation is **(x − h)² + (y − k)² = r²**, with centre **(h, k)** and radius **r** (units of length). The general form **x² + y² + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0** has centre **(−g, −f)** and radius **√(g² + f² − c)**.

- **Tangent length** from an external point at distance **d** from the centre: **l = √(d² − r²)**.
- **Chord length** subtending central angle **θ** at the centre: **chord = 2r·sin(θ/2)** (arc length is **rθ** in radians — different formula).
- **Power of a point**: **PT² = PA · PB** for tangent **PT** and secant through **A, B** from external point **P**.
- CAT weight ~4% of QA — expect 1–2 direct questions on equation conversion, centre/radius, or tangent length.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Equation Forms and Centre–Radius Extraction

Converting between the two forms uses completing the square. Given **x² + y² + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0**, group as **(x² + 2gx + g²) + (y² + 2fy + f²) = g² + f² − c**, yielding **(x + g)² + (y + f)² = g² + f² − c**. The centre is **(−g, −f)** — note the minus signs — and the radius is **√(g² + f² − c)**, valid only when **g² + f² − c > 0**.

| Concept | Key Point |
| --- | --- |
| Standard form | (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r², centre (h, k), radius r |
| General form | x² + y² + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0, centre (−g, −f), radius √(g² + f² − c) |
| Real circle condition | g² + f² − c > 0 (strictly positive) |
| Point on circle | (x − h)² + (y − k)² equals r² exactly |
| Point inside / outside | Value is less than r² / greater than r² |

#### Line–Circle Relative Position

Substitute the line equation (in **y = mx + c** form) into the circle equation to get a quadratic in **x**. The discriminant **Δ** of that quadratic decides the position:

- **Δ > 0** → line cuts the circle at two points (**secant**).
- **Δ = 0** → line touches the circle at one point (**tangent**); the repeated root gives the tangency point.
- **Δ < 0** → line does not meet the circle (no real intersection).

This is the standard CAT trap: students use the *condition* for tangency without checking whether the resulting quadratic is genuinely quadratic (coefficient of x² must not vanish).

#### Family of Circles Through Two Given Circles' Intersection

If **S₁ = 0** and **S₂ = 0** are two intersecting circles, every circle through their two intersection points has equation **S₁ + λS₂ = 0** for some real constant **λ ≠ −1**. Setting λ = −1 gives the common chord line (radical axis). The technique lets you write a circle's equation when you know it passes through two specific points that also lie on a known circle.

- Pick **λ** by plugging a third given condition (e.g., a specified centre, radius, or another point on the circle).
- **Common chord** of two circles = equation obtained by subtracting one circle equation from the other.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Power of a Point and Tangent–Secant Configurations

For an external point **P**, draw tangent **PT** and a secant through points **A** and **B**. The power of **P** with respect to the circle equals **PT² = PA · PB = (constant)** — independent of which secant you draw. For an internal point, the same product holds for the two chord segments: **PA · PB = PC · PD** (intersecting chords theorem). CAT questions typically pack this into a cyclic quadrilateral, so recognise the configuration first.

#### Inscribed Angle Theorem and Cyclic Quadrilaterals

An inscribed angle is half the central angle subtending the same arc. Equivalently, angles in the **same segment** are equal. For a cyclic quadrilateral (vertices on one circle), **opposite angles sum to 180°** and the **exterior angle equals the opposite interior angle**. A diameter subtends a **90°** angle at every point on the circle — use this to spot right-triangle setups instantly.

| Trap | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Reporting centre as (g, f) instead of (−g, −f) | Rewrite general form, then negate both coefficients |
| Using chord formula 2r sin(θ/2) when the answer needs arc length | Arc length = rθ, with θ in **radians**, not degrees |
| Setting Δ < 0 for tangency | Tangency needs Δ = 0 exactly |
| Treating S₁ + λS₂ = 0 as the radical axis | The radical axis corresponds to λ = −1 only |
| Mixing tangent length with full secant length | Tangent length uses √(d² − r²); secant uses PA · PB = PT² |

#### Worked Micro-Example

Circle: **x² + y² − 6x + 4y − 12 = 0**. Complete the square: **(x − 3)² + (y + 2)² = 25**, so centre **(3, −2)**, radius **5**. Point **P(8, −2)** lies outside (distance from centre = 5 = radius; actually on the circle — adjust: use **P(10, −2)**, distance = 7). Tangent length from **P**: **l = √(7² − 5²) = √24 = 2√6**.

#### Practice Prompts

1. Find the equation of the circle through **(1, 0)** and **(0, 1)** that is concentric with **x² + y² − 4x + 6y − 12 = 0**, then compute its radius.
2. For point **(0, 0)** and circle **x² + y² = 25**, a secant through the origin meets the circle at **A(3, 4)** and **B**. Find **OB** using power of a point.
