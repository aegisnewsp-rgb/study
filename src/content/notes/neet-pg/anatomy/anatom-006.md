# Cardiovascular System

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The **cardiovascular system** is a closed circulatory loop: a muscular four-chambered **heart** pumps blood through **arteries → arterioles → capillaries → venules → veins**. The right heart handles deoxygenated blood; the left heart handles oxygenated blood. Cardiac output is governed by **CO = SV × HR**, where stroke volume (mL/beat) equals **EDV − ESV**.

- **Chambers**: RA, RV, LA, LV separated by interatrial and interventricular septa.
- **Valves**: Tricuspid (right AV) and Mitral/Bicuspid (left AV) anchored by **chordae tendineae** to **papillary muscles**; **Aortic** and **Pulmonary** semilunar valves have no chordae.
- **Conducting system**: SA node → AV node (0.3 s delay) → Bundle of His → bundle branches → **Purkinje fibres**.

| Structure | Function |
|---|---|
| SA node | Pacemaker (~70–75/min), upper RA near SVC opening |
| AV node | Delays impulse; located in interatrial septum near coronary sinus |
| Purkinje fibres | Modified cardiac muscle; transmit impulse to ventricular myocardium |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Heart Wall and Pericardium
The heart wall has three layers: **endocardium** (inner, endothelium-lined), **myocardium** (middle, cardiac muscle, thickest in the LV), and **epicardium** (outer, visceral layer of serous pericardium). The pericardium comprises a tough outer **fibrous pericardium** and an inner **serous pericardium** split into parietal and visceral layers, with pericardial fluid between them. Two sinuses matter surgically: the **transverse pericardial sinus** (between aorta/pulmonary trunk anteriorly and SVC/atria posteriorly — used to clamp great vessels) and the **oblique sinus** (posterior to the left atrium).

#### Chambers and Great Vessels
The **right atrium** receives the SVC, IVC, and coronary sinus. The **left atrium** receives four pulmonary veins. The **interventricular septum** has a small **membranous part** (just below the aortic valve) and a larger **muscular part**.

#### Coronary Circulation
- **Left coronary artery (LCA)** → **LAD** (anterior 2/3 of septum, anterior LV) + **circumflex** (left atrium, lateral LV).
- **Right coronary artery (RCA)** → right atrium, RV, posterior 1/3 of septum, **SA node (~60%)** and **AV node (~80%)**.
- **Dominance** is defined by which artery gives the **posterior interventricular artery (PDA)**; ~70–80% of people are right-dominant.

#### Fetal Shunts and Their Closure
Three shunts bypass the non-functional fetal lung:

| Shunt | Connects | Postnatal fate |
|---|---|---|
| Foramen ovale | Right atrium → Left atrium | Fossa ovalis |
| Ductus arteriosus | Pulmonary trunk → Aortic arch | Ligamentum arteriosum |
| Ductus venosus | Umbilical vein → IVC | Ligamentum venosum |

#### Nerve Supply and Referred Pain
Sympathetic fibres from **T1–T5** (accelerator nerves) increase rate and force; parasympathetic fibres via the **vagus nerve (CN X)** slow the SA node. Visceral afferent pain follows sympathetic fibres back to **T1–T5 dermatomes**, producing the classic **left arm/jaw pain in myocardial infarction**.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Auscultation Areas vs Anatomical Position
A frequent trap: **auscultation sites do not match valve anatomy**.

| Valve | Anatomical location | Auscultation area |
|---|---|---|
| Aortic | Left side, behind sternum at level of 3rd ICS | Right 2nd ICS, sternal border |
| Pulmonary | Left side, behind sternum at level of 3rd ICS | Left 2nd ICS, sternal border |
| Tricuspid | Right side, behind sternum at 4th–5th ICS | Lower left sternal border, 4th–5th ICS |
| Mitral | Left side, behind sternum at 4th ICS | Apex, 5th ICS mid-clavicular line |

#### Common Mistakes
1. Treating **Purkinje fibres as nerves** — they are modified cardiomyocytes, conducting faster than ordinary ventricular muscle.
2. Believing **chordae tendineae attach to semilunar valves** — they attach only to AV (tricuspid, mitral) valves.
3. Saying the **ductus arteriosus connects aorta to pulmonary vein** — it joins the pulmonary trunk to the aortic arch.
4. Forgetting that **left ventricular pressure (~120 mmHg)** vastly exceeds right (~25 mmHg), explaining wall thickness differences.
5. Confusing the **transverse pericardial sinus** with the oblique sinus; the former is anterior, the latter is posterior to the left atrium.

#### Applied Correlates and Worked Example
#### Worked numeric — Cardiac output at rest:
If HR = 75 beats/min, EDV = 120 mL, ESV = 50 mL, then SV = 120 − 50 = 70 mL. **CO = 70 × 75 = 5250 mL/min (≈ 5.25 L/min)**. **Ejection fraction = (70/120) × 100 ≈ 58%** (normal 55–70%).

#### Practice prompts:
1. A patient has an infarct in the posterior 1/3 of the interventricular septum with bradycardia and PR prolongation. Which coronary artery is occluded, and which node is most likely ischaemic?
2. During cardiac surgery the surgeon passes a clamp behind the aorta and pulmonary trunk to isolate them. Name the sinus used and the structures forming its anterior and posterior walls.

#### NEET PG Strategy
Cardiovascular anatomy carries ~3% weight and is heavily image-based — expect angiograms, valve cross-sections, and fetal-circulation diagrams. Prioritise **coronary dominance**, **conducting system order**, **auscultation sites**, and **fetal shunt closure** — these four areas account for most repeated MCQs.
