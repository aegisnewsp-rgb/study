# Biology of Behaviour: Neuroanatomy and Neurochemistry — MCAT Psych/Soc

The biology of behaviour spans AAMC Foundational Concept 10 of the Psych/Soc section. The MCAT tests the neuron, action potentials, synapses and neurotransmitters, the central and peripheral nervous systems, the brainstem, limbic system, cerebral cortex, the endocrine system, and the biological basis of behaviour. These topics underpin every brain-behaviour passage on the exam.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### Core concepts

- **Neuron structure:** dendrites (receive), soma (cell body), axon (transmit), axon terminals, myelin sheath.
- **Action potential:** all-or-none depolarisation along the axon. Phases: resting, depolarisation, repolarisation, hyperpolarisation.
- **Synaptic transmission:** electrical signal converted to chemical signal at the synapse. Neurotransmitters bind to receptors on the postsynaptic neuron.
- **Neurotransmitters:** acetylcholine (ACh), dopamine, serotonin, norepinephrine, GABA, glutamate, endorphins.
- **Central nervous system (CNS):** brain and spinal cord.
- **Peripheral nervous system (PNS):** somatic (voluntary) and autonomic (involuntary). Autonomic: sympathetic (fight-or-flight) and parasympathetic (rest-and-digest).
- **Brain structures:** brainstem (vital functions), cerebellum (movement coordination), limbic system (emotion, memory), cerebral cortex (higher cognition).
- **Endocrine system:** pituitary, thyroid, adrenal, pancreas, gonads. Hormones travel through the blood.

#### One-pass drill

1. Identify the topic: neuron, action potential, synapse, brain region, or hormone.
2. Recall the relevant structure or molecule.
3. Apply to the specific scenario.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing sympathetic and parasympathetic. Sympathetic = fight-or-flight; parasympathetic = rest-and-digest.
- Treating all neurotransmitters as excitatory. GABA is inhibitory; glutamate is excitatory.
- Forgetting that the pituitary is the master gland but is controlled by the hypothalamus.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Neuron and action potential

**Neuron:** the basic functional unit. Dendrites receive signals; the soma integrates them; the axon transmits the action potential; the axon terminals release neurotransmitters.

**Action potential:** all-or-none. Resting potential (~-70 mV) maintained by the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase. Stimulus opens Na⁺ channels; Na⁺ rushes in (depolarisation). At threshold, voltage-gated Na⁺ channels open fully (rising phase). Na⁺ channels inactivate; voltage-gated K⁺ channels open (falling phase). K⁺ flows out (hyperpolarisation). Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase restores resting potential.

**Saltatory conduction:** in myelinated axons, the action potential "jumps" between nodes of Ranvier, increasing speed.

#### Synaptic transmission

**Electrical-to-chemical-to-electrical:** the action potential reaches the axon terminal; voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels open; Ca²⁺ triggers vesicle fusion; neurotransmitter is released into the synaptic cleft; neurotransmitter binds to receptors on the postsynaptic neuron.

**Excitatory postsynaptic potential (EPSP):** depolarises the postsynaptic neuron; increases the likelihood of firing.

**Inhibitory postsynaptic potential (IPSP):** hyperpolarises the postsynaptic neuron; decreases the likelihood of firing.

#### Neurotransmitters

- **Acetylcholine (ACh):** neuromuscular junction; autonomic nervous system; memory. Alzheimer disease involves loss of ACh neurons.
- **Dopamine:** reward, movement, motivation. Parkinson's disease involves loss of dopamine neurons. Schizophrenia involves excess dopamine.
- **Serotonin:** mood, sleep, appetite. SSRIs treat depression by blocking serotonin reuptake.
- **Norepinephrine:** arousal, alertness. Locus coeruleus is the main source.
- **GABA:** main inhibitory neurotransmitter. Benzodiazepines and alcohol enhance GABA.
- **Glutamate:** main excitatory neurotransmitter. Involved in learning and memory.
- **Endorphins:** pain relief, pleasure. Opioids bind to endorphin receptors.

#### Brain structures

- **Brainstem:** medulla (breathing, heart rate), pons (sleep, respiration), midbrain (vision, hearing, motor control).
- **Cerebellum:** movement coordination, balance, motor learning.
- **Limbic system:** amygdala (fear, aggression), hippocampus (memory formation), hypothalamus (homeostasis, hormones), cingulate cortex (attention, emotion).
- **Cerebral cortex:** frontal (executive function, motor), parietal (somatosensory, spatial), temporal (auditory, memory, language comprehension), occipital (vision).

#### Endocrine system

The MCAT tests the major endocrine glands and hormones:

- **Pituitary:** master gland; controlled by hypothalamus. Releases GH, ACTH, TSH, FSH, LH, prolactin.
- **Thyroid:** T3 and T4 (metabolism).
- **Adrenal:** cortex (cortisol, aldosterone, androgens); medulla (epinephrine, norepinephrine).
- **Pancreas:** insulin (beta cells, lowers glucose), glucagon (alpha cells, raises glucose).
- **Gonads:** oestrogen, progesterone (ovaries); testosterone (testes).

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How this topic connects to the other Psych/Soc topics

- **Sensation and perception** depend on sensory neurons and brain pathways.
- **Learning and memory** depend on synaptic plasticity (long-term potentiation).
- **Psychopathology** is linked to neurotransmitter imbalances (dopamine in schizophrenia, serotonin in depression).

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"GABA is excitatory."** GABA is the main inhibitory neurotransmitter in the CNS.
- **"The sympathetic system is calming."** The sympathetic system activates fight-or-flight; the parasympathetic system promotes rest-and-digest.
- **"Hormones act quickly."** Hormones act more slowly than neurotransmitters because they travel through the blood.

#### Exam technique

- For neuron items, identify the part by its function.
- For action potential items, identify the phase by the ion movements.
- For neurotransmitter items, identify the neurotransmitter by its function.
- For brain items, identify the structure by its function.
- For endocrine items, identify the gland by the hormone.
