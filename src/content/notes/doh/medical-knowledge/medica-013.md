# Health Promotion and Patient Education

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Health promotion enables individuals and communities to gain control over the determinants of health, while patient education is the one-to-one clinical process that supports behaviour change and self-management. Both are governed in the UAE by **DOH Patient Education standards** and align with national initiatives such as **Weqaya** and **FitforLife**.

The single most-tested framework is the **Levels of Prevention**: primary (before disease — vaccination, smoking cessation), secondary (early detection — screening), and tertiary (reducing complications — diabetic foot care, post-MI rehab). Pair this with the **Ottawa Charter (1986)** five action areas and the **Transtheoretical (stages of change)** model for behaviour-change counselling.

- **Patient-education cycle**: assess → set objectives → plan → deliver (with teach-back) → evaluate → document in the medical record.
- **Counselling technique**: motivational interviewing (**OARS** — Open questions, Affirmations, Reflections, Summaries).
- **Domains of learning (Bloom's)**: cognitive, affective, psychomotor — chronic-disease teaching must cover all three.

| Level of prevention | Clinical example |
| --- | --- |
| Primary | HPV vaccine, smoking cessation |
| Secondary | Mammography, HbA1c screening |
| Tertiary | Foot care in diabetic neuropathy |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Definition and Scope

Health promotion is the broader socio-behavioural and environmental strategy outlined in the **Ottawa Charter (1986)** — building healthy public policy, creating supportive environments, strengthening community action, developing personal skills, and reorienting health services toward prevention. Patient education is the **clinical, encounter-based** teaching that supports an individual to manage illness, adhere to treatment, and adopt protective behaviours. The two overlap but are not identical: education transfers knowledge; promotion also addresses **social determinants of health** such as housing, food access, and health literacy.

#### Levels of Prevention

The DOH exam repeatedly asks candidates to classify interventions:

| Level | Timing | DOH/UAE example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary | Before disease onset | National tobacco-cessation drive, childhood immunisation per MOHAP schedule |
| Secondary | Early/asymptomatic disease | Type 2 diabetes screening, breast and colorectal screening |
| Tertiary | Established disease | Cardiac rehab post-MI, diabetic foot-care programme, asthma action plans |

#### The Patient-Education Process

Effective education follows a deliberate cycle rather than handing out a leaflet:

1. **Assess** the learner's needs, prior knowledge, language, and health literacy.
2. **Set objectives** mapped to a Bloom's domain (cognitive, affective, or psychomotor).
3. **Plan** content and method — verbal counselling, written material, demonstration, video.
4. **Deliver** with **plain language**, avoiding medical jargon; offer culturally and linguistically appropriate material (Arabic, English, Hindi, Urdu, Tagalog, Malayalam).
5. **Verify comprehension** using the **teach-back method**.
6. **Document** the session in the medical record — this is a **DOH clinical-governance and licensing requirement**, not an informal courtesy.

#### Behaviour-Change Counselling

Motivational interviewing uses **OARS** to resolve ambivalence about lifestyle change. The clinician should match the intervention to the patient's **stage of change** (precontemplation → contemplation → preparation → action → maintenance). For example, a smoker in *precontemplation* benefits from reflective listening; one in *action* benefits from relapse-prevention planning.

- Knowles' **andragogy** assumptions: adults learn best when content is relevant, problem-centred, and respects their autonomy and prior experience.
- Health literacy is a **modifying factor**: low-literacy patients require pictograms, short sentences, and confirmation cycles.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Mechanism and Theoretical Anchors

Health promotion rests on the **Ottawa Charter** action areas and operates upstream of clinical care, while patient education operates downstream within consultations. Behaviour change is mediated by constructs from the **Transtheoretical Model** (decisional balance, self-efficacy, processes of change) and **Social Cognitive Theory** (reciprocal determinism between person, behaviour, and environment). In the UAE, the **Weqaya cardiovascular screening** programme and **MOHAP immunisation drives** are concrete instantiations of primary and secondary prevention.

#### Adult-Learning and Domain Mapping

A common exam error is writing a learning objective in the **cognitive** domain when the clinical requirement is **psychomotor** — for instance, stating "the patient will understand insulin use" instead of "the patient will correctly demonstrate insulin injection using an insulin pen". Selecting the correct Bloom's domain drives the choice of teaching method (discussion vs. return-demonstration vs. attitude exploration).

#### Common Mistakes and DOH-Specific Traps

| Mistake | Why it loses marks |
| --- | --- |
| Labelling diabetic foot care as primary prevention | It limits complications of **existing** disease — tertiary |
| Treating "patient education" as leaflet handover | Misses the required **teach-back** verification and **documentation** |
| Using English-only material in a multilingual UAE clinic | Ignores **health literacy** and cultural appropriateness |
| Skipping the stages-of-change match | MI is stage-specific; one-size-fits-all counselling is ineffective |
| Forgetting to record education in the chart | Breaches **DOH clinical-governance** standards |

#### Worked Micro-Example

A 52-year-old male with newly diagnosed Type 2 diabetes (HbA1c 8.9%), overweight, Arabic-speaking, presents for follow-up. The physician should: (1) **assess** his literacy and prior knowledge; (2) **set objectives** — cognitive (understands glycaemic targets), affective (accepts lifestyle change), psychomotor (demonstrates glucose monitoring); (3) **deliver** in Arabic using a pictorial plate-model and pen demonstration; (4) **teach-back** the hypoglycaemia plan; (5) **document** all teaching points; (6) **plan follow-up** for medication adherence and weight reduction — mapping to **secondary** prevention of complications and **tertiary** care for the established disease.

#### Practice Prompts

1. A DOH MCQ describes a breast-screening awareness campaign. Classify it under the correct level of prevention and name the Ottawa Charter action area it primarily supports.
2. An OSCE station asks you to counsel a precontemplative smoker. Outline the OARS micro-skills and the stage-matched intervention you would use.

#### Exam Strategy

Within the **3% Medical-Knowledge** allocation, expect 1–2 questions per paper on this topic, often scenario-based. Allocate ~30 seconds per MCQ; for OSCE stations, structure answers using **assess → educate → verify → document** to score full marks.
