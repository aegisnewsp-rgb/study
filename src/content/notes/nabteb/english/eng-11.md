# Summary Writing

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your NABTEB paper.

Summary Writing is the controlled condensation of a given passage into a shorter version that keeps the original meaning while dropping examples, repetitions, and stylistic flourishes. In NABTEB English Language the question is compulsory and accounts for roughly 4% of the paper, normally appearing as Paper III Essay / Condensation.

- **Target length:** about **one-third** of the original word count.
- **Language:** **your own words** — never lift more than 7 consecutive words from the source.
- **Structure:** logical paragraphs joined with linkers such as *however*, *in addition*, *therefore*.
- **Tense and tone:** preserve the author's tense, point of view, and register.

| Must-do | Must-avoid |
| --- | --- |
| Read the passage twice, underline the topic sentence | Copying whole sentences verbatim |
| Write in continuous prose | Adding personal opinion or outside facts |
| Count words and stay near the limit | Quoting statistics or examples at length |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Reading the passage strategically

Before writing a single sentence of the summary, scan the passage to locate the **topic sentence** (usually the first or last sentence of a paragraph) and the **central theme** that ties every paragraph together. Mark supporting ideas that directly expand the theme; ignore anecdotes, dialogues, and illustrative examples that merely colour the argument. NABTEB examiners reward candidates who can separate the skeleton of the argument from its flesh.

#### Paraphrasing rules

Paraphrasing is substitution of equivalent words and structures without changing meaning. Keep the **same tense** as the original, retain the author's **point of view** (first, second, or third person), and preserve any cause-and-effect or comparison relationships. Use connectives such as *also, however, in addition, therefore, on the other hand, as a result* to bind your sentences into coherent paragraphs.

| Dos | Don'ts |
| --- | --- |
| Replace nouns with synonyms and verbs with equivalents | Switch past tense to present |
| Combine related ideas into one sentence | Insert your own opinion or judgement |
| Keep technical terms and proper nouns unchanged | Invent statistics or examples |

#### Word count and layout

Aim for **one-third of the original length**, usually 80–120 words for a 300-word passage, and write in **two or three short paragraphs**. Indicate the word count at the end, e.g. *(115 words)*. Organise your summary so the order of ideas mirrors the source's logical flow.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Condensation techniques

When you compress a passage, work in three passes: (1) delete repetitions and examples, (2) merge sentences that share a topic, (3) substitute long noun phrases with shorter equivalent constructions. A useful rule of thumb is to ask, *"Would removing this sentence change the author's main argument?"* If the answer is no, cut it.

#### Common NABTEB examiner traps

- **Verbatim lifting:** any string of 7 or more consecutive words directly copied from the passage is penalised.
- **Tense drift:** switching the past tense to present distorts the time frame the author set.
- **Additive commentary:** sentences beginning with *"In my view…"* or *"This is because…"* introduce outside material and are marked down.
- **Fragmentary layout:** a single block of disconnected sentences without paragraph breaks loses coherence marks.

#### Worked micro-example

Original (42 words): *"Many students fail exams because they read without understanding. They memorise facts the night before and forget them the morning after. However, students who plan their reading consistently tend to perform better."*

Summary (14 words): *"Many students fail exams because they read without understanding, while consistent planners perform better."* — tense, meaning, and contrast preserved.

#### Practice prompts

1. Condense a 300-word passage on rural-urban migration into 100 words, preserving the author's three main causes.
2. Rewrite a 250-word expository passage on renewable energy in about 80 words, keeping the cause-effect order intact.
