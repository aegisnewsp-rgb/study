# Reading Conventions, Vocabulary and Visual Information — OSSLT Reading

This topic covers the conventions, vocabulary and visual-information items that appear across the four reading selection types on the OSSLT. The EQAO Framework names spelling, grammar, and punctuation as assessed skills within the reading component, and OSSLT selections are often paired with captioned photos, sidebars, tables, and small charts that test visual literacy alongside reading comprehension.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### What this topic covers

- **Spelling.** Recognising the correct spelling of high-frequency and subject-specific words inside reading items and conventions items.
- **Grammar.** Subject-verb agreement, pronoun-antecedent agreement, consistent verb tense, correct modifier placement, correct word form (noun vs verb vs adjective).
- **Punctuation.** Capital letters, end punctuation (period, question mark, exclamation point), internal punctuation (commas, apostrophes, quotation marks), and dialogue-specific punctuation (commas inside quotation marks, end punctuation placement).
- **Vocabulary in context.** Determining the meaning of an unfamiliar word from the way it is used in the selection.
- **Visual information.** Reading captioned photos, sidebars, small tables, simple charts, and labels that accompany the selection.

#### One-pass drill for conventions items

1. Read the conventions item stem carefully. Note whether it asks about spelling, grammar, punctuation, or vocabulary.
2. If it asks about spelling, eliminate choices that look plausible but use a wrong letter pattern (e.g., "definately" instead of "definitely").
3. If it asks about grammar, read the sentence aloud in your head. The choice that makes the sentence read smoothly is usually right.
4. If it asks about punctuation, recall the rule (comma before "and" in a list, apostrophe for possession, capital letter at the start of a sentence).
5. If it asks about vocabulary in context, substitute each choice for the unfamiliar word. The choice that keeps the sentence making sense is right.

#### Examiner traps

- A spelling choice that looks right phonetically but is wrong orthographically. The OSSLT tests the written form, not the sound.
- A grammar choice that is technically grammatical but changes the meaning. The right answer preserves the writer's intended meaning.
- A punctuation choice that adds a comma where none is needed. Commas have specific rules; do not add them by feel.
- A vocabulary choice that is a real word but does not fit the sentence. Vocabulary-in-context items test meaning in the sentence, not the dictionary.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Strategy for conventions items

**Learn the rules, not the feel.** Conventions items are graded against a fixed set of rules (capitalisation, subject-verb agreement, comma usage, apostrophe usage). Memorise the rules, then apply them.

**For spelling items, eliminate choices that match the wrong pattern.** Common misspellings swap the order of vowels (recieve instead of receive), double a wrong consonant (definately instead of definitely), or change a silent letter (priviledge instead of privilege). Eliminate any choice that contains one of these patterns.

**For grammar items, identify the subject and verb first.** Subject-verb agreement is the most-tested grammar item on the OSSLT. Find the subject, find the verb, check that they agree in number.

**For punctuation items, recall the rule before reading the choices.** The rule is usually clear (capital at start of sentence, period at end, comma in a list, apostrophe for possession). The right answer is the choice that follows the rule.

**For vocabulary-in-context items, read the whole sentence.** The unfamiliar word is surrounded by clues — synonyms, contrasts, examples. Use the clues to choose the meaning.

#### Strategy for visual information

**Read the caption or label.** The caption or label of a graphic element usually states what the element shows. Reading 1 items about the graphic can usually be answered from the caption.

**Identify the type of graphic.** A captioned photo illustrates the news event; a sidebar gives additional information; a small table compares two or three items; a chart shows a trend over time. Knowing the type tells you what to look for.

**Match the item to the graphic.** If the item asks "according to the chart," find the chart, then find the specific data point the item targets.

#### Practice routine

- Take five EQAO-style conventions items (one each of spelling, grammar, punctuation, vocabulary, visual). Time yourself on conventions alone.
- Take one OSSLT-style selection with an accompanying graphic element. Read the graphic element first, then the selection, then the items.
- Keep a personal "missed-conventions" list. Re-take the items on the list the next day.

#### How this topic connects to the OSSLT writing tasks

The same conventions rules are tested in the Writing component and in the scoring of the long-writing tasks. Practising conventions in Reading reinforces them in Writing.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How this topic connects to the other OSSLT items

- **The writing tasks** depend on the same conventions rules. A student who misses comma usage in a Reading conventions item will also miss it in the long-writing tasks.
- **Vocabulary in context** is a skill used across all four reading selection types. It is also useful for the Series-of-Paragraphs task, where the student must interpret the source's vocabulary to support an opinion.

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"This sentence looks fine without a comma."** Some comma rules are not optional. A compound sentence (two independent clauses joined by "and," "but," "or") requires a comma before the conjunction. Memorise the rule.
- **"The choice adds more information, so it is better."** Conventions items test correctness, not informativeness. The choice that fixes the error is right, even if it adds no new information.
- **"The vocabulary item is asking what the word means in general."** The item asks what the word means in this sentence. The right answer is the meaning that fits the sentence, not the dictionary's broadest definition.

#### Exam technique

- Read the item stem carefully. Note what rule is being tested.
- Apply the rule. Eliminate choices that break the rule.
- If two choices remain, read the whole sentence and pick the choice that keeps the sentence reading smoothly.
- For visual information items, read the caption first, then the data.
- Use the platform's flag-for-review function. Do not leave blanks on selected-response items.
