# Magnetic Field and Electromagnetism

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

- **Magnetic field (B)** is the region around a magnet or current-carrying conductor where magnetic force acts; measured in **tesla (T)**.
- **Force on a conductor**: F = BIL sinθ (N), where I = current (A), L = length (m), θ = angle between conductor and field.
- **Force on a moving charge**: F = qvB sinθ (N); a stationary charge feels no magnetic force.
- **Flux**: Φ = BA cosθ (Wb); **Faraday's law**: emf = −N(dΦ/dt); **Lenz's law** fixes the minus sign (induced current opposes change).
- **Solenoid**: B = μ₀nI, where μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ T·m/A and n = turns per metre.
- **Transformers** work on AC only; ideal: E_p/E_s = N_p/N_s = I_s/I_p.

> JAMB tests this topic with 1–3 MCQs combining F = BIL sinθ, flux, Faraday's law, transformer ratio, and the right-hand grip rule.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Magnetic Fields Around Magnets and Currents

Field lines run **north → south** outside a magnet and never cross. A straight wire carrying current I produces circular field lines; the **right-hand grip rule** gives direction — thumb points along current, fingers curl along B. For a solenoid (a long coil), B at its centre is uniform and equals **μ₀nI**, where n is turns per metre. Permeability of free space μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ T·m/A.

#### Motor Effect: Force on a Current

A conductor of length L carrying current I at angle θ to a uniform field B experiences **F = BIL sinθ**. The force direction follows Fleming's left-hand rule (thumb = force, forefinger = field, middle finger = current). Maximum force occurs at θ = 90°; zero at θ = 0°. This is the operating principle of the **DC motor**, where a coil on a split-ring commutator rotates in a magnetic field.

#### Electromagnetic Induction

A changing magnetic flux Φ through a coil of N turns induces an emf given by **Faraday's law**: emf = −N(dΦ/dt), with Φ = BA cosθ. The minus sign is **Lenz's law** — the induced current opposes the flux change that created it. JAMB often presents a bar magnet moving into/out of a coil: speed up → faster flux change → larger induced emf.

#### Generators and Transformers

| Device | Input | Output | Key Feature |
|---|---|---|---|
| AC generator | Mechanical rotation | Sinusoidal emf | Slip rings; continuous alternation |
| DC generator | Mechanical rotation | Unidirectional emf | Split-ring commutator |
| Step-up transformer | Low AC voltage | High AC voltage | N_s > N_p |
| Step-down transformer | High AC voltage | Low AC voltage | N_s < N_p |

For an ideal transformer: **E_p/E_s = N_p/N_s = I_s/I_p**. Real transformers lose energy to copper resistance and eddy currents; efficiency η = (P_out/P_in) × 100%. Transformers **only work with AC** because steady DC produces no changing flux.

#### Force on a Moving Charge

A charge q moving at velocity v at angle θ to B feels **F = qvB sinθ**. In a uniform field perpendicular to v, the charge follows a circular path of radius **r = mv/(qB)** — used in mass spectrometers and cyclotrons.

> Common JAMB trap: writing Φ when the question asks for B (or vice versa). Flux is in weber, flux density in tesla.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Deeper Mechanism of Induction

Induction is not a "magnetic effect" alone — it is an **electric field induced by a changing magnetic field** (Maxwell–Faraday equation: ∮E·dl = −dΦ/dt). The induced emf exists whether or not a conductor is present; a coil simply provides charges that respond. The magnitude depends on **rate of change of flux linkage NΦ**, not on B alone. This is why spinning a coil faster, using stronger magnets, or adding more turns all increase the induced emf.

#### Edge Cases and Common Mistakes

- **θ in F = BIL sinθ** is measured from the conductor axis to B, not from the surface. Students often default to sin90° = 1 and miss partial-force questions.
- **Transformers with DC input** produce only a brief emf spike when the switch closes or opens; steady DC yields zero emf.
- **Back-emf in motors**: a running DC motor generates its own opposing emf proportional to its speed; current drawn = (V − back-emf)/R.
- **Self-inductance**: a changing current in a coil induces an emf in the same coil; Lenz's law makes the coil resist current changes.
- **Energy in a magnetic field**: stored as U = ½LI², with L in henries.

#### Connections to Adjacent Topics

- Links to **current electricity** through resistance and power loss in transformers.
- Links to **waves** via electromagnetic waves: changing E produces changing B, propagating at c = 1/√(μ₀ε₀).
- Links to **modern physics** through the Lorentz force F = qE + qv×B, the full force on a charged particle.

#### Practice Prompts

1. A coil of 200 turns has its flux change from 0.5 Wb to 0.1 Wb in 0.02 s. Find the average induced emf and state its direction relative to the flux change.
2. A transformer has 500 primary turns, 1500 secondary turns, and a primary voltage of 220 V. Find the secondary voltage and the turns-ratio classification; explain why connecting DC instead of AC would not produce a steady output.
