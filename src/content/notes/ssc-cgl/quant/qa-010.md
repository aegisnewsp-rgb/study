# Partnership

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

A **partnership** is a business arrangement in which two or more persons (called **partners**) pool capital, and sometimes skill, and divide the resulting profit or loss in an **agreed ratio** over a fixed time. The single governing rule for SSC CGL is: **profit is shared in the ratio of (Capital × Time)** for each partner, never by capital alone when the investment durations differ.

#### Must-know formulas
- Profit share of a partner = (His capital × His months) ÷ (Sum of all partners' capital × months) × Total profit
- When all partners invest for the same number of months, profit ratio reduces to the simple capital ratio x : y.
- When a partner adds or withdraws money mid-year, use **time-weighted (cumulative) capital** = Σ (amount × number of months it stayed invested).

#### High-yield pointers
- SSC CGL almost always frames partnership as a single MCQ asking for a profit share, an investment ratio, or a new partner's contribution — solving time should be under 90 seconds.
- Watch for "joined after 3 months" or "withdrew ₹x after 6 months" — these change the effective months, not just the principal.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core principle
In any partnership question, the share of profit a partner receives is proportional to the **product of the capital he kept in the business and the duration for which it remained invested**. This is the bedrock rule; every variant in SSC CGL is built on top of it.

#### Case 1 — Same time period
If A puts ₹60,000 and B puts ₹40,000, both for 12 months, the profit-sharing ratio is simply 60,000 : 40,000 = **3 : 2**. The time factor cancels out because it is identical for both.

#### Case 2 — Different time periods
If A invests ₹50,000 for 6 months and B invests ₹75,000 for 8 months, the ratio of profits is:
(50,000 × 6) : (75,000 × 8) = 3,00,000 : 6,00,000 = **1 : 2**.

Always convert months and years to a **common unit** before multiplying.

#### Case 3 — Changing capital mid-period
A invests ₹1,00,000 on 1 Jan, adds ₹50,000 on 1 July, and the year ends on 31 Dec. His effective capital is:
(1,00,000 × 6) + (1,50,000 × 6) = 6,00,000 + 9,00,000 = ₹15,00,000 (capital-months).
This sum is then compared with other partners' capital-month totals.

#### Case 4 — Withdrawal
If a partner withdraws ₹20,000 after 4 months out of a 12-month year, the ₹20,000 counts for 4 months and the balance ₹80,000 (or whatever remains) for the remaining 8 months.

#### Common SSC CGL patterns
- "A and B start a business. A invests ₹x for *p* months, B invests ₹y for *q* months. Find profit ratio." → Direct (xp) : (yq).
- "A, B, C invest in ratio 5:7:8 for 12, 8, 6 months respectively" → Multiply each capital by its month count, then simplify.
- "A and B start a business. C joins after 3 months with ₹z" → A and B have 12 months, C has 9 months.
- "A starts with ₹5 lakh. After 6 months B joins with ₹3 lakh. Total profit ₹… Find A's share." → Build the (Cap × Time) table and apply the formula.

#### Quick comparison table

| Situation | Formula to use |
|---|---|
| Same duration for all | Ratio of capitals |
| Different durations | Ratio of (Capital × Months) |
| Capital changes mid-year | Sum of (Capital × Months) for each slab |
| New partner joins late | Reduce his months; others keep full term |

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge case — "Working" vs "Sleeping" partners
A **working partner** may receive an extra salary or commission *in addition to* the profit share; this is usually deducted from profits *before* dividing the balance. A **sleeping partner** contributes only capital and takes a pure profit share. SSC CGL occasionally tests whether the candidate mistakenly includes the salary in the profit pool or omits it altogether.

#### Edge case — Profit-sharing ratio ≠ Investment ratio
The question may explicitly state "profits are shared in ratio 2 : 3 even though capitals are 1 : 1." In that case, the given ratio **overrides** the capital-time calculation. Conversely, if the problem asks for a partner's investment given the profit ratio and time, invert the working: Capital ratio = (Profit ratio) ÷ (Time ratio) when times are equal.

#### Connection to ratio & proportion
Partnership questions are essentially applied ratio problems. A two-step solve is common: first build the (Capital × Time) ratio, then use it to find a missing investment, a missing time, or a missing total profit.

#### Common mistakes (the ones SSC examiners exploit)
1. Dividing profit in the simple capital ratio even when partners join at different months.
2. Forgetting that when a partner withdraws money, the *withdrawn* amount gets multiplied only by the months it was *present*, not the full year.
3. Using the final capital figure instead of the time-weighted average when capital changes mid-period.
4. Mixing units — writing 1 year 6 months as "6 months" or treating 18 months as 1.8 years without consistent scaling.
5. Assuming the question's "profit ratio" equals the "investment ratio" when in fact the question deliberately differentiates them.

#### Worked micro-example
A starts with ₹4,00,000. After 4 months he adds ₹2,00,000. B starts with ₹6,00,000 and withdraws ₹1,00,000 after 6 months. Profits are ₹2,30,000 for the year.
- A: (4,00,000 × 4) + (6,00,000 × 8) = 16,00,000 + 48,00,000 = 64,00,000 capital-months
- B: (6,00,000 × 6) + (5,00,000 × 6) = 36,00,000 + 30,00,000 = 66,00,000 capital-months
- Ratio A : B = 64 : 66 = 32 : 33
- A's share = 32/65 × 2,30,000 = ₹1,13,231 (approx)

#### Practice prompts
1. A, B and C invest ₹60,000, ₹80,000 and ₹1,00,000 for 12, 8 and 6 months respectively. If profit is ₹45,000, find B's share.
2. X starts a business with ₹5,00,000. Y joins after 3 months with ₹4,00,000. Z joins after 6 months with ₹3,00,000. Year-end profit is ₹2,02,500. Find each partner's share.
