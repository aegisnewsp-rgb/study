# Legal Maxims

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Legal Maxims are short Latin or English phrases that encapsulate a rule of law used across common-law and Indian jurisprudence. CLAT tests them in three ways: meaning recall, maxim-to-fact matching, and consequence inference. Master the 25-30 most-tested maxims and anchor each to a statute (e.g., *Mens Rea* → Section 6 IPC, *Free Consent* → Section 10 Indian Contract Act).

- **Mens Rea** = guilty mind; **Actus Reus** = guilty act; both required for most offences under the IPC.
- **Audi Alteram Partem** = hear the other side; **Nemo Judex In Causa Sua** = no one should be a judge in their own cause (natural justice pair).
- **Caveat Emptor** = let the buyer beware (Sale of Goods Act, 1930), now narrowed by the Consumer Protection Act, 2019.

| Maxim | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Ignorantia Juris Non Excusat | Ignorance of law is no excuse |
| Volenti Non Fit Injuria | No injury to one who consents |
| Res Ipsa Loquitur | The thing speaks for itself |
| Bona Fide / Mala Fide | In good faith / in bad faith |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Categories of Maxims Tested

CLAT groups Legal Maxims into four functional buckets. Mapping a maxim to its bucket is the fastest way to eliminate wrong options.

#### Criminal Liability Maxims

These govern culpability and excuses. The IPC, Section 6, codifies *Mens Rea* (except for strict-liability offences). *Actus Non Facit Reum Nisi Mens Sit Rea* — an act alone does not convict unless the mind is also guilty — is the doctrinal anchor.

| Maxim | Legal Effect | Statutory Anchor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mens Rea | Subjective guilty intention | Section 6 IPC |
| Actus Reus | Physical or external element of crime | Sections 33–34 IPC |
| Ignorantia Juris Non Excusat | Mistake of law is no defence | Section 76 IPC (limited) |
| Volenti Non Fit Injuria | Consent to risk bars negligence claim | Indian Contract Act framework |
| In Pari Delicto | Equal fault; courts refuse relief | Indian Contract Act, 1872 |

#### Procedural & Evidence Maxims

Natural justice drives this cluster. *Audi Alteram Partem* (hear the other side) and *Nemo Judex In Causa Sua* (no bias) together form the two pillars of administrative fairness. *Res Ipsa Loquitur* shifts the burden to the defendant when an accident would not occur without negligence.

#### Common Application Errors

- Picking *Volenti* for plain negligence or trespass; the defence fails unless consent was free and with full knowledge of the risk.
- Mixing up *Audi Alteram Partem* and *Nemo Judex* — they are complementary, not synonyms.
- Assuming *Caveat Emptor* survives untouched — Section 14 of the Sale of Goods Act and the 2019 Consumer Protection Act create implied conditions and consumer rights.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Worked Example — Applying the Right Maxim

**Fact pattern:** A hires B to repair a terrace. During the work, B knowingly skips the safety harness. The terrace collapses and B is injured. Can B claim compensation from A?

**Analysis:** *Volenti Non Fit Injuria* applies only when the plaintiff consented to the risk freely and with full knowledge. A did not consent to B's negligent method; A consented to ordinary risk of repair. The defence fails, and the correct maxim for B's liability to A is *Res Ipsa Loquitur* — the collapse itself points to negligence unless rebutted.

#### Exam Strategy and Weightage

Legal Maxims account for roughly 4% of the CLAT paper, typically 4-6 questions inside the Legal Reasoning section. The Consortium of NLUs (the CLAT conducting body) frames them as either standalone one-liners or as part of a passage where the maxim must be matched to a clause. Reading time per question should stay under 90 seconds.

#### High-Yield Maxim List

1. Pacta Sunt Servanda — agreements must be honoured.
2. Expressio Unius Est Exclusio Alterius — mentioning one implies exclusion of others.
3. Noscitur a Sociis — a word is known by its associates.
4. Ejusdem Generis — general words are limited to their class.
5. Ubi Jus Ibi Remedium — where there is a right, there is a remedy.
6. Stare Decisis — stand by things decided (binding precedent).
7. Ratio Decidendi — the binding reason for a decision.
8. Obiter Dicta — incidental remarks, not binding.
9. Prima Facie — on its face; accepted unless contradicted.
10. Habeas Corpus — produce the body; against unlawful detention.

#### Practice Prompts

1. A factory spews smoke onto a residential colony. The residents sue. Which maxim establishes that a right carries a remedy — *Ubi Jus Ibi Remedium* or *Actus Reus*?
2. Two parties sign a contract, then both break it. The court refuses relief to either. Identify the operative maxim and state its effect.
