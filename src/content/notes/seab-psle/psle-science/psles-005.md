# Interactions — PSLE Science Notes

Interactions is the Primary 6–specific theme in the 2023 MOE Primary Science syllabus. The strand covers types of forces, ecosystems and food chains, adaptation for survival, and the impact of human activity on the environment. The strand is assessed through a mix of multiple-choice items on Paper 1 and structured and open-ended questions on Paper 2, and is a common OEQ topic at the upper end of the mark range.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### What every Primary 6 student must know

1. **Types of forces**: gravitational force, friction, magnetic force, elastic spring force, electrostatic force.
2. **Effects of forces**: a force can change the shape, size, speed, or direction of motion of an object.
3. **Ecosystem**: a community of living things together with the non-living parts of their environment.
4. **Food chain**: shows the feeding relationship between organisms (producer → primary consumer → secondary consumer → …).
5. **Food web**: a network of food chains in an ecosystem.
6. **Adaptation**: a feature of an organism that helps it survive in its environment.
7. **Man's impact on the environment**: pollution (air, water, land), deforestation, climate change, extinction of species.

#### Key terms

- **Producer**: an organism (usually a plant) that makes its own food by photosynthesis.
- **Consumer**: an organism that eats other organisms to get energy.
- **Decomposer**: an organism (e.g. bacteria, fungi) that breaks down dead organisms and waste into simpler substances.
- **Predator**: an animal that hunts and eats other animals.
- **Prey**: an animal that is hunted and eaten by a predator.
- **Conservation**: the protection of living things and their habitats.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Forces

A force is a push or a pull. A force can change the shape of an object (e.g. stretching a spring), the size of an object (e.g. compressing a spring), the speed of an object (e.g. a push makes a stationary object move), or the direction of motion of an object (e.g. a kick changes the direction of a moving ball).

**Gravitational force** is the force of attraction between any two objects due to their masses. The Earth's gravity pulls everything toward the centre of the Earth, which is why objects fall downward when dropped.

**Friction** is a force that opposes motion between two surfaces in contact. It acts in the opposite direction to motion. Rough surfaces produce more friction than smooth surfaces. Friction is useful (walking, braking) and inconvenient (wear on machine parts).

**Magnetic force** is the force of attraction or repulsion between magnets, or between a magnet and a magnetic material (e.g. iron, steel, nickel, cobalt). Like poles repel; unlike poles attract.

**Elastic spring force** is the force exerted by a stretched or compressed spring. A stretched spring pulls back toward its original length; a compressed spring pushes back.

**Electrostatic force** is the force between charged objects. Like charges repel; unlike charges attract. A comb rubbed on dry hair can pick up small pieces of paper because the comb becomes charged and attracts the neutral paper.

#### Ecosystems

An ecosystem is a community of living things (biotic factors) together with the non-living parts of their environment (abiotic factors like water, sunlight, temperature, soil). A pond is an ecosystem; a forest is an ecosystem; a rotting log is even a small ecosystem.

In an ecosystem, organisms interact with each other and with the non-living environment. Plants use sunlight, water and carbon dioxide to grow (photosynthesis). Animals eat plants or other animals. Decomposers break down dead matter into simpler substances, returning nutrients to the soil.

#### Food chains and food webs

A **food chain** shows the feeding relationship between organisms in an ecosystem. Each arrow points from the organism that is eaten to the organism that eats it.

Example: grass → grasshopper → frog → snake → eagle.

The **producer** (grass) makes its own food by photosynthesis. The **primary consumer** (grasshopper) eats the producer. The **secondary consumer** (frog) eats the primary consumer. The **tertiary consumer** (snake) eats the secondary consumer. The **quaternary consumer / apex predator** (eagle) is at the top of the chain.

A **food web** is a network of food chains in an ecosystem, showing how multiple organisms interact. In a food web, most organisms have more than one food source and more than one predator.

A change in one population in a food web can affect many other populations. If the frogs in a food web are killed by pollution, the grasshopper population may increase (no predator), the snake population may decrease (less food), and so on.

#### Adaptation

An adaptation is a feature of an organism that helps it survive in its environment. Adaptations are matched to the organism's habitat.

- Camels in deserts have wide hooves to walk on sand, long eyelashes to keep sand out of their eyes, and the ability to go without water for long periods.
- Fish in water have streamlined bodies, fins and gills.
- Arctic animals have thick fur or a layer of fat to keep warm.
- Plants in dry climates have thick waxy leaves to reduce water loss and long roots to reach water deep underground.

#### Man's impact on the environment

Human activities affect the environment in many ways.

- **Air pollution**: burning fossil fuels releases gases that cause smog and contribute to climate change.
- **Water pollution**: industrial waste, sewage and agricultural runoff can pollute rivers, lakes and oceans.
- **Land pollution**: litter, plastic waste and chemical spills damage the land and harm wildlife.
- **Deforestation**: clearing forests for agriculture or development destroys habitats and reduces biodiversity.
- **Climate change**: the release of greenhouse gases (carbon dioxide, methane) is causing the Earth to warm, which changes weather patterns and threatens many species.
- **Extinction**: when a species dies out completely. Many species are endangered due to habitat loss, hunting and pollution.

Conservation efforts include protecting habitats, reducing pollution, recycling, using renewable energy sources, and breeding endangered species in captivity.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where Interactions appears across the paper

- **PSLE Science Paper 1**: multiple-choice items on types of forces, food chains, adaptation, and man's impact on the environment.
- **PSLE Science Paper 2**: structured and open-ended questions that test the student's ability to interpret a passage about an ecosystem, predict the consequences of a change in a food web, or explain how an organism's features help it survive.
- **Across both papers**: drawing a labelled food web is the highest-leverage exam technique for the food-web portion of the strand.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Heavier objects fall faster." In a vacuum, all objects fall at the same rate due to gravity. In air, air resistance makes lighter objects with a larger surface area fall more slowly.
- "Friction is always bad." Friction is useful for walking, braking and gripping. It is only inconvenient when it causes wear on machine parts.
- "Plants get their food from the soil." Plants make their own food by photosynthesis; they absorb water and minerals from the soil, but the food itself is made in the leaves using sunlight, water and carbon dioxide.
- "All animals are consumers." Animals that eat plants or other animals are consumers; decomposers (bacteria, fungi) are a separate group.
- "Apex predators are not affected by changes lower in the food web." Apex predators depend on the populations below them for food; a decline lower in the web eventually affects the apex predator.

#### Exam technique

- For food-chain questions, draw the arrows in the right direction (from the eaten to the eater). The arrow direction is a common single-mark loss.
- For adaptation questions, link the adaptation to the function explicitly: "the camel's wide hooves spread its weight on the sand". The link is the method mark.
- For "man is harming the environment" questions, name the activity AND the consequence: "burning fossil fuels releases carbon dioxide, which contributes to climate change".
- For force questions, name the type of force and the direction of its action. "Friction opposes motion" is more precise than "friction slows things down".
