# Moving Charges and Magnetism

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

> Core vector identities, force equations, and field configurations for rapid JEE Advanced revision.

The interaction between moving electric charges and magnetic fields forms the foundation of classical electrodynamics:

**1. Lorentz Force on Point Charges:**
$$\vec{F} = q(\vec{E} + \vec{v} \times \vec{B})$$
- Magnetic component: $\vec{F}_m = q(\vec{v} \times \vec{B})$, with magnitude $F_m = |q|vB\sin\theta$.
- Since $\vec{F}_m \perp \vec{v}$, instantaneous power is identically zero:
  $$P = \vec{F}_m \cdot \vec{v} = q(\vec{v} \times \vec{B}) \cdot \vec{v} = 0$$
  *(Static magnetic fields alter only the velocity vector direction, never changing kinetic energy or speed).*

**2. Trajectory Regimes in Uniform $\vec{B}$:**
- $\vec{v} \parallel \vec{B}$ ($\theta = 0^\circ \text{ or } 180^\circ$): Straight line with constant velocity.
- $\vec{v} \perp \vec{B}$ ($\theta = 90^\circ$): Uniform circular motion with:
  $$r = \frac{mv}{qB} = \frac{p}{qB} = \frac{\sqrt{2mK}}{qB}, \quad \omega = \frac{qB}{m}, \quad T = \frac{2\pi m}{qB}$$
- General angle $\theta$: Helical trajectory with radius $r = \frac{m(v\sin\theta)}{qB}$ and pitch $p = (v\cos\theta)T = \frac{2\pi m v\cos\theta}{qB}$.

**3. Magnetic Force on Current-Carrying Conductors:**
$$\vec{F} = I(\vec{L} \times \vec{B})$$
For an arbitrary curved wire connecting points $A$ and $B$ in a uniform $\vec{B}$:
$$\vec{F} = I\left(\int_A^B d\vec{l}\right) \times \vec{B} = I(\vec{L}_{effective} \times \vec{B})$$
*(For any closed planar loop in a uniform magnetic field, $\vec{L}_{effective} = \oint d\vec{l} = 0 \implies \vec{F}_{net} = 0$).*

**4. Biot-Savart Law for Current Elements:**
$$d\vec{B} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \frac{I(d\vec{l} \times \hat{r})}{r^2} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \frac{I(d\vec{l} \times \vec{r})}{r^3}$$
where $\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} = 10^{-7}\,\text{T}\cdot\text{m/A}$.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

> Standard derivations, field symmetries, Ampère's circuital law, and comparison frameworks.

Magnetic fields generated by steady electrical currents reflect the underlying spatial symmetries of the charge flow.

#### Magnetic Field Configurations
1. **Finite Straight Wire of Length $L$:**
   At perpendicular distance $d$ from a wire subtending angles $\alpha$ and $\beta$ to the endpoints:
   $$B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi d}(\sin\alpha + \sin\beta)$$
   - Infinitely long straight conductor ($\alpha = \beta = 90^\circ$):
     $$B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi d}$$
   - Semi-infinite wire at endpoint ($\alpha = 90^\circ, \beta = 0^\circ$):
     $$B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi d}$$

2. **Circular Current Loop of Radius $R$:**
   Along the central axis at distance $x$ from the ring center:
   $$B_{axial} = \frac{\mu_0 I R^2}{2(R^2 + x^2)^{3/2}} = \frac{\mu_0 M}{2\pi (R^2 + x^2)^{3/2}}$$
   where $M = I(\pi R^2)$ is the magnetic dipole moment.
   - At center ($x = 0$):
     $$B_{center} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2R}$$
   - For an arc subtending angle $\theta$ radians at its center:
     $$B_{arc} = \frac{\mu_0 I \theta}{4\pi R}$$

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

#### Ampère's Circuital Law
For any closed planar or non-planar loop $\mathcal{C}$ enclosing net steady conduction current $I_{enc}$:
$$\oint_{\mathcal{C}} \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{l} = \mu_0 I_{enclosed}$$

To apply Ampère's Law effectively:
1. Choose an Amperian loop where $\vec{B}$ is either parallel to $d\vec{l}$ with constant magnitude, or perpendicular to $d\vec{l}$ ($\vec{B} \cdot d\vec{l} = 0$).
2. Evaluate enclosed current using surface integral of current density:
   $$I_{enclosed} = \iint_{\mathcal{S}} \vec{J} \cdot d\vec{A}$$
   For non-uniform current densities (e.g. $J(r) = J_0(r/R)$), integrate over concentric differential annuli:
   $$I_{enc}(r) = \int_0^r J(r') \cdot 2\pi r'\,dr'$$

> Gyromagnetic ratio of rotating distributions, magnetic dipole torque, non-uniform field gradients, and relativistic magnetic corrections.

#### Gyromagnetic Ratio & Magnetic Moments of Rotating Rigid Bodies
Consider a rigid body carrying total charge $Q$ distributed proportionally with total mass $M$ (so charge-to-mass density ratio $\rho_q / \rho_m = Q/M$ is spatially uniform throughout).
When the body rotates with angular velocity $\vec{\omega}$:
- A differential volume element $dV$ with mass $dm$ and charge $dq = \frac{Q}{M}dm$ moves in a circle of radius $r_\perp$ with speed $v = \omega r_\perp$.
- Equivalent orbital current: $dI = \frac{dq}{T} = \frac{dq \cdot \omega}{2\pi}$.
- Differential magnetic dipole moment:
  $$d\vec{M} = dI \cdot \vec{A} = \left(\frac{\frac{Q}{M}dm \cdot \omega}{2\pi}\right)(\pi r_\perp^2) = \frac{Q}{2M}(dm \cdot r_\perp^2)\vec{\omega} = \frac{Q}{2M}d\vec{L}$$
Integrating over the entire body yields the universal relation:
$$\vec{M} = \frac{Q}{2M}\vec{L}$$

where $\vec{L} = I_{rot}\vec{\omega}$ is the mechanical angular momentum.
- For a rotating thin ring ($I_{rot} = MR^2$): $M = \frac{Q}{2M}(MR^2\omega) = \frac{1}{2}Q R^2 \omega$.
- For a rotating uniform disc ($I_{rot} = \frac{1}{2}MR^2$): $M = \frac{Q}{2M}\left(\frac{1}{2}MR^2\omega\right) = \frac{1}{4}Q R^2 \omega$.
- For a rotating solid sphere ($I_{rot} = \frac{2}{5}MR^2$): $M = \frac{Q}{2M}\left(\frac{2}{5}MR^2\omega\right) = \frac{1}{5}Q R^2 \omega$.
- For a rotating spherical shell ($I_{rot} = \frac{2}{3}MR^2$): $M = \frac{Q}{2M}\left(\frac{2}{3}MR^2\omega\right) = \frac{1}{3}Q R^2 \omega$.

*(This gyromagnetic relationship holds for any classical body with identical geometric mass and charge distributions).*

## Worked JEE Advanced Exam Problems

### Problem 1: Cyclotron Motion Across a Discontinuous Magnetic Boundary

**Statement:** A uniform magnetic field $\vec{B} = B_0 \hat{k}$ exists only in the semi-infinite region $x > 0$. A particle of positive charge $q$ and mass $m$ is projected from the origin $(0,0,0)$ with initial velocity $\vec{v}_0 = v_0(\cos\alpha \hat{i} + \sin\alpha \hat{j})$, where $0 < \alpha < \frac{\pi}{2}$.
1. Find the coordinates of the particle when it exits the magnetic field region.
2. Calculate the total time $\Delta t$ spent by the particle inside the magnetic field.
3. Determine the velocity vector $\vec{v}_{exit}$ at the moment of exit.

**Step-by-Step Solution:**

1. **Trajectory in the Region $x > 0$:**
   - The particle enters at $t = 0$ with velocity $\vec{v} = v_0\cos\alpha\hat{i} + v_0\sin\alpha\hat{j}$.
   - Magnetic force at entry:
     $$\vec{F} = q(\vec{v}_0 \times \vec{B}) = q(v_0\cos\alpha\hat{i} + v_0\sin\alpha\hat{j}) \times (B_0\hat{k}) = q v_0 B_0 (-\cos\alpha\hat{j} + \sin\alpha\hat{i})$$
   - The force is in the $xy$-plane and perpendicular to velocity, producing a circular arc of radius:
     $$R = \frac{m v_0}{q B_0}$$
   - The center of the circle $(x_c, y_c)$ lies along the normal to the entry velocity vector:
     $$\hat{n}_{inward} = \sin\alpha\hat{i} - \cos\alpha\hat{j}$$
     $$x_c = 0 + R\sin\alpha = R\sin\alpha$$
     $$y_c = 0 - R\cos\alpha = -R\cos\alpha$$

2. **Exit Geometry:**
   - The circular path is governed by $(x - x_c)^2 + (y - y_c)^2 = R^2$.
   - The boundary of the magnetic field is the line $x = 0$.
   - Setting $x = 0$ in the circle equation:
     $$(0 - R\sin\alpha)^2 + (y - y_c)^2 = R^2 \implies R^2\sin^2\alpha + (y - y_c)^2 = R^2$$
     $$(y - y_c)^2 = R^2(1 - \sin^2\alpha) = R^2\cos^2\alpha \implies y - y_c = \pm R\cos\alpha$$
   - At entry, $y = y_c + R\cos\alpha = 0$ (the origin).
   - Therefore, at exit:
     $$y_{exit} = y_c - R\cos\alpha = -R\cos\alpha - R\cos\alpha = -2R\cos\alpha$$
   - The particle exits at point $(0, -2R\cos\alpha, 0) = \left(0, -\frac{2mv_0\cos\alpha}{qB_0}, 0\right)$.

3. **Time of Flight Inside Field ($\Delta t$):**
   - The angle subtended by the circular chord connecting $(0,0)$ and $(0, -2R\cos\alpha)$ at the center of curvature is:
     $$\sin\left(\frac{\Phi}{2}\right) = \frac{\text{half-chord}}{R} = \frac{R\cos\alpha}{R} = \cos\alpha = \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - \alpha\right)$$
     $$\implies \frac{\Phi}{2} = \frac{\pi}{2} - \alpha \implies \Phi = \pi - 2\alpha$$
   - The time spent is proportional to the orbital period $T = \frac{2\pi m}{q B_0}$:
     $$\Delta t = \frac{\Phi}{2\pi} T = \frac{\pi - 2\alpha}{2\pi} \left(\frac{2\pi m}{q B_0}\right) = \frac{m(\pi - 2\alpha)}{q B_0}$$

4. **Exit Velocity Vector:**
   - Because magnetic force does zero work, speed remains strictly equal to $v_0$.
   - By symmetry of the circular arc intersecting the line $x = 0$:
     $$v_{x,exit} = -v_0\cos\alpha, \quad v_{y,exit} = +v_0\sin\alpha$$
     $$\vec{v}_{exit} = v_0(-\cos\alpha\hat{i} + \sin\alpha\hat{j})$$

### Problem 2: Magnetic Field of a Truncated Conductor with Off-Center Cylindrical Cavity

**Statement:** An infinitely long solid cylindrical conductor of radius $R$ carries a total current $I$ uniformly distributed across its cross-section. A cylindrical hole of radius $a$ ($a < R/2$) is drilled parallel to the cylinder's axis, with its center displaced by vector $\vec{d}$ from the conductor axis ($|\vec{d}| + a < R$).
1. Determine the magnitude and direction of the magnetic field $\vec{B}$ at any point inside the cavity.
2. Calculate the magnetic field at the origin (center of the solid conductor).
3. Discuss the spatial uniformity of the field within the cavity.

**Step-by-Step Solution:**

1. **Current Density and Principle of Linear Superposition:**
   - Cross-sectional area of remaining conductor: $A_{cond} = \pi R^2 - \pi a^2 = \pi(R^2 - a^2)$.
   - The uniform current density is:
     $$\vec{J} = \frac{I}{\pi(R^2 - a^2)}\hat{k}$$
   - By the principle of linear superposition, the configuration is equivalent to:
     - A solid cylinder of radius $R$ carrying uniform current density $+\vec{J}$.
     - A smaller cylinder of radius $a$ occupying the cavity region carrying uniform current density $-\vec{J}$.

2. **Magnetic Field Formulation for a Uniform Solid Cylinder:**
   - For an intact cylinder of radius $R$ carrying current density $+\vec{J} = J\hat{k}$, Ampère's Law inside the cylinder at position vector $\vec{r}_1$ from its axis gives:
     $$\oint \vec{B}_1 \cdot d\vec{l} = \mu_0 I_{enc} \implies B_1 (2\pi r_1) = \mu_0 (J \pi r_1^2) \implies B_1 = \frac{\mu_0 J r_1}{2}$$
   - In vector notation (where $\hat{k} \times \hat{r}_1$ points along azimuthal direction $\hat{\phi}_1$):
     $$\vec{B}_1(\vec{r}_1) = \frac{\mu_0}{2} (\vec{J} \times \vec{r}_1)$$

3. **Superposition Inside the Cavity:**
   - Let $\vec{r}$ be the position vector of an arbitrary point $P$ inside the cavity relative to the main cylinder axis.
   - The position vector of the same point $P$ relative to the cavity's axis is $\vec{r}_2 = \vec{r} - \vec{d}$.
   - The magnetic field due to the negative current cylinder of radius $a$ is:
     $$\vec{B}_2(\vec{r}_2) = \frac{\mu_0}{2} ((-\vec{J}) \times \vec{r}_2) = -\frac{\mu_0}{2} (\vec{J} \times (\vec{r} - \vec{d}))$$
   - Total magnetic field at point $P$:
     $$\vec{B}_{total} = \vec{B}_1 + \vec{B}_2 = \frac{\mu_0}{2}(\vec{J} \times \vec{r}) - \frac{\mu_0}{2}(\vec{J} \times (\vec{r} - \vec{d}))$$
     $$\vec{B}_{total} = \frac{\mu_0}{2} [\vec{J} \times \vec{r} - \vec{J} \times \vec{r} + \vec{J} \times \vec{d}] = \frac{\mu_0}{2}(\vec{J} \times \vec{d})$$

4. **Analysis of the Cavity Field:**
   - Notice that position vector $\vec{r}$ cancels completely!
   - This proves that the magnetic field everywhere inside the cavity is **strictly uniform in both magnitude and direction**:
     $$B_{cavity} = \frac{\mu_0 J d}{2} = \frac{\mu_0 I d}{2\pi(R^2 - a^2)}$$
   - Direction: If current $\vec{J}$ is out of the page ($\hat{k}$) and cavity displacement $\vec{d}$ is along $+\hat{i}$, then $\vec{B}_{cavity} \propto \hat{k} \times \hat{i} = +\hat{j}$ (vertically upward throughout the entire cavity).
   - At the origin ($\vec{r} = 0$), point lies on the main cylinder axis where $\vec{B}_1 = 0$. The net field is purely from the negative current cavity cylinder:
     $$\vec{B}(0) = -\frac{\mu_0}{2}(\vec{J} \times (-\vec{d})) = \frac{\mu_0}{2}(\vec{J} \times \vec{d})$$
     which matches the uniform cavity value identically.

## Critical JEE Advanced Traps & Heuristic Checkpoints

- **Trap 1: Magnetic Force Work**: Never allow a non-zero work answer for a pure magnetic Lorentz force on a free charge. Static magnetic fields cannot change kinetic energy ($\Delta K = 0$). If particle speed changes, an electric field (either electrostatic or induced $-d\vec{B}/dt$) is necessarily present.
- **Trap 2: Curved Conductor Force in Uniform Field**: Do not integrate along every arc segment if the field $\vec{B}$ is uniform. Replace the curved path with the straight vector displacement $\vec{L}_{eff}$ between endpoints: $\vec{F} = I(\vec{L}_{eff} \times \vec{B})$.
- **Trap 3: Gyromagnetic Ratio Uniformity Requirement**: The relation $\vec{M} = \frac{Q}{2M}\vec{L}$ is valid only if charge density and mass density have identical spatial distributions throughout the body. For a non-uniform sphere where charge is on the surface but mass is in the volume, the numerical factor deviates from $1/2$.
- **Trap 4: Cyclotron Period Speed Independence**: In non-relativistic regimes, cyclotron period $T = \frac{2\pi m}{qB}$ is strictly independent of speed $v$. As particle velocity approaches relativistic thresholds ($v > 0.1c$), relativistic mass expansion ($m = \gamma m_0$) increases orbital period, causing classical cyclotrons to lose synchronization (requiring frequency-modulated synchrocyclotrons).
