# Laws of Motion

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Newton's three laws govern the link between force and motion. The second law in vector form is **F_net = dp/dt**, which reduces to **F = ma** only for constant mass. Momentum **p = mv** has units kg·m/s, and the impulse–momentum theorem gives **J = Δp = F_avg · Δt**.

- **First law** defines inertia; a body at rest or in uniform motion stays that way unless a net external force acts.
- **Third law** pairs every action with an equal and opposite reaction — apply it action-reaction pair-wise on separate free body diagrams (FBDs).
- For an Atwood machine on a frictionless pulley, a = (m₁ − m₂)g / (m₁ + m₂).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Force, Momentum, and Impulse

Newton's second law in its most general form is **F = dp/dt**. When mass is constant, dp/dt = m(dv/dt) = ma; when mass varies (a chain piling up, a leaking cart, a rocket ejecting fuel), the **F = ma** shortcut fails and you must use the momentum form. Impulse **J = ∫F dt** equals the change in momentum, which is why a cushioned landing over a longer time reduces the average force on the body.

#### Free Body Diagrams and Constraints

Solve every connected-body problem by isolating each mass, drawing only the forces acting **on that body**, and writing Newton's second law along two independent axes. A constraint equation links the accelerations of bodies joined by an inextensible string — for example, on a single movable pulley the lifted mass accelerates at half the rate of the pulling block.

#### Friction at an Interface

Static friction self-adjusts up to a maximum: **f_s ≤ μ_s N**. Once the applied force exceeds μ_s N, motion begins and kinetic friction takes over: **f_k = μ_k N**, where μ_k < μ_s on the same surfaces. The angle of friction satisfies **tan θ = μ_s**; if the applied force's line of action lies within this angle, the block stays in equilibrium without any external horizontal push.

#### Apparent Weight in a Lift

A spring balance reads the normal force on a person. In a lift accelerating upward with acceleration a, the reading is **T = m(g + a)**; accelerating downward it is **T = m(g − a)**; in free fall it is zero.

| Concept | Governing relation |
| --- | --- |
| Newton's 2nd law (constant m) | F = ma |
| Newton's 2nd law (general) | F = dp/dt |
| Spring force | F = −kx, k in N/m |
| Static friction limit | f_s ≤ μ_s N |
| Impulse | J = Δp = F_avg · Δt |

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Non-Inertial Frames and Pseudo Forces

In an accelerating frame the observer introduces a fictitious force **F_pseudo = −m a_frame** on every body to make Newton's second law hold in that frame. A passenger pushed back into the seat when a car accelerates forward is not a real backward force — it is the inertia of the body described in the car's frame. Pseudo forces vanish only in inertial frames.

#### Spring Systems and SHM Link

Hooke's law **F = −kx** gives a position-dependent force whose equation of motion is m(d²x/dt²) = −kx, yielding simple harmonic motion with **ω = √(k/m)**. A block on a horizontal spring on a frictionless floor performs SHM; add friction and the amplitude decays. Two springs in parallel give k_eff = k₁ + k₂; in series, 1/k_eff = 1/k₁ + 1/k₂.

#### Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Drawing one combined FBD for two bodies instead of two separate FBDs, which double-counts internal tension or normal force.
- Forgetting the pseudo force in lift, wedge-on-accelerating-cart, or rotating-frame problems.
- Treating static friction as fixed at μ_s N — it equals the applied tangential force up to its maximum.
- Sign errors when projecting forces along inclined axes; resolve weight into mg sin θ along the slope and mg cos θ perpendicular to it.

**Worked micro-example.** Two blocks m₁ = 4 kg and m₂ = 1 kg hang from a massless frictionless pulley. Acceleration a = (4 − 1)·10 / (4 + 1) = 6 m/s². Tension T = 2m₁m₂g/(m₁+m₂) = 2·4·1·10/5 = 16 N. Both m₁ and m₂ have the same magnitude of acceleration because the string is inextensible.

#### Practice Prompts

1. A 5 kg block rests on a 30° incline (μ_s = 0.4, μ_k = 0.3). Find the minimum horizontal push P to start it moving up the plane, and its acceleration once moving.
2. A chain of length L and mass M lies coiled; a constant force F pulls it vertically upward from rest. Find the velocity after the entire chain has just left the pile, using F = dp/dt.
