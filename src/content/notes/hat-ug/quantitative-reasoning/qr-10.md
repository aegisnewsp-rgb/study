# Geometry: Lines, Angles, Triangles

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Geometry: Lines, Angles, Triangles covers three building blocks of plane geometry tested in **HAT-UG Quantitative Reasoning**. A **line** is a one-dimensional straight path extending infinitely; a **line segment** is a finite portion; a **ray** starts at a point and extends one way. When two lines meet, they form **angles** measured in degrees, classified as acute (<90°), right (=90°), obtuse (90°–180°), straight (=180°), or reflex (>180°).

Must-know facts:

- **Angle sum property:** A + B + C = **180°** in any triangle.
- **Exterior angle theorem:** The exterior angle equals the sum of the two opposite interior angles.
- **Pythagoras theorem:** In a right triangle, a² + b² = c² (c = hypotenuse).
- **Vertically opposite angles** are equal; a **linear pair** sums to 180°.
- **Triangle congruence rules:** SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, RHS.

High-yield HAT-UG pointers: expect 1–2 MCQs combining angle-chase logic with a numeric answer (e.g. find an unknown angle), and one item pairing **Pythagoras** with a right-triangle word problem.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Lines, Rays, and Basic Angle Pairs

A **point** has position but no size; a **line** has length but no thickness and is denoted by two points (AB) or a single lowercase letter. The **collinearity** of three points means they lie on the same line. When two lines intersect, four angles are produced, and the **vertically opposite** pair is always equal. Adjacent angles on a straight line form a **linear pair** summing to 180°; angles summing to 90° are **complementary**; those summing to 180° are **supplementary**.

#### Parallel Lines and the Transversal

When a **transversal** cuts two **parallel lines**, it creates eight angles grouped into three named pairs:

- **Corresponding angles** — equal.
- **Alternate interior angles** — equal.
- **Co-interior (same-side interior)** — supplementary (sum = 180°).

These equalities reverse when the original two lines are NOT parallel, so confirming parallelism is the first step in any angle-chase question.

#### Triangle Fundamentals

Triangles are classified two ways:

| By sides | By angles |
|---|---|
| **Scalene** — all sides unequal | **Acute** — all angles < 90° |
| **Isosceles** — two equal sides; base angles equal | **Right** — one angle = 90° |
| **Equilateral** — all sides equal; all angles 60° | **Obtuse** — one angle > 90° |

The **angle sum property** A + B + C = 180° is the most frequently invoked fact. The **exterior angle theorem** states that any exterior angle equals the sum of the two remote interior angles — useful when the problem gives an exterior angle and one interior angle.

#### Area Formulas

- **Base × height / 2**: Area = ½ × b × h.
- **Two sides and included angle**: Area = ½ × ab × sin C.
- **Heron's formula** (when all three sides a, b, c are known, with semi-perimeter s = (a + b + c)/2): Area = √[s(s − a)(s − b)(s − c)].

#### HAT-UG Question Patterns

1. Angle chase: an irregular figure is split into triangles; apply 180° repeatedly.
2. Parallel-line reasoning: identify which angle pair is being asked, then set up an equation.
3. Right-triangle numeric: combine **Pythagoras** with a perimeter or area statement to solve for a side.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study

#### Congruence vs Similarity

Two triangles are **congruent** if they have identical shape and size, verified by:

- **SSS**, **SAS**, **ASA**, **AAS** — three matching parts (sides or angles) in the right combination.
- **RHS** — right angle, hypotenuse, and one side equal (restricted to right triangles).

Two triangles are **similar** if they share the same shape but differ in size. The criteria are **AA** (two matching angles automatically give the third), **SSS similarity** (proportional sides), and **SAS similarity** (proportional sides with equal included angle). If the similarity ratio of corresponding sides is **k**, the ratio of perimeters is k and the ratio of areas is **k²** — a frequent trap in MCQs that asks for area given a side ratio.

#### Special Lines Inside a Triangle

- **Median** — joins a vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side (centroid is their common point of intersection).
- **Altitude** — perpendicular from a vertex to the opposite side.
- **Angle bisector** — divides an angle into two equal halves.
- **Perpendicular bisector** — perpendicular line through a side's midpoint.

Confusing these four is a common HAT-UG trap; only the angle bisector relates to equal angles, and only the perpendicular bisector / altitude involves a 90°.

#### Worked Micro-Example

In triangle ABC, ∠A = 50°, and the exterior angle at B is 130°. Find ∠C.

By the **exterior angle theorem**, the exterior angle at B = ∠A + ∠C, so 130° = 50° + ∠C, giving ∠C = 80°. Sanity check: 50° + ∠B + 80° = 180° → ∠B = 50°, so the triangle is isosceles with AB = AC.

#### Common Mistakes

- Forgetting that **co-interior** angles sum to 180° (not equal) when lines are parallel.
- Using the **hypotenuse** as a leg in Pythagoras — the hypotenuse is always opposite the right angle and is the longest side.
- In similarity problems, comparing sides that are NOT corresponding.

#### Practice Prompts

1. Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. One corresponding angle is 65°. List all eight angles with their measures and identify the co-interior pair.
2. Triangle PQR has sides 5, 12, 13. Verify the right angle, then use Heron's formula to confirm the area equals ½ × 5 × 12 = 30.
