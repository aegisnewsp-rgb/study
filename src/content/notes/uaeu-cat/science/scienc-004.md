#### What the Items Test
Each Topic 4 item presents a four-option MCQ. The examiner typically rewards candidates who can:
1. **Recall a definition** verbatim (e.g. a biological process, a chemical law, or a physics quantity).
2. **Apply a concept** to a one-step numeric or descriptive scenario.
3. **Identify the correct SI unit** or convert between prefixed units (kilo-, milli-, micro-).
4. **Select the correct scientific notation** for a given magnitude (e.g. 6.02 × 10²³ rather than 602 followed by zeros).

#### Typical Question Patterns
- *"Which SI unit corresponds to quantity X?"* — straight recall.
- *"A student measures 0.0045 m. The value in scientific notation is…"* — notation drill.
- *"Which statement best describes process Y?"* — discriminate between similar-sounding definitions.

#### Worked Example — SI Unit and Notation Drill
A typical 3%-weighted item reads:

> *A laboratory sample contains 0.000 025 g of a catalyst. Expressed in scientific notation and converted to milligrams, the value is:*
> (A) 2.5 × 10⁻⁵ mg
> (B) 2.5 × 10⁻² mg
> (C) 2.5 × 10⁵ mg
> (D) 2.5 × 10² mg

Working: 0.000 025 g = 2.5 × 10⁻⁵ g. Convert g → mg by multiplying by 10³: 2.5 × 10⁻⁵ × 10³ = 2.5 × 10⁻² mg. Answer **(B)**. The trap is option (A) — candidates forget the conversion step.
