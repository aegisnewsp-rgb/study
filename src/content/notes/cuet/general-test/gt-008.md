# Economics

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Economics for CUET UG General Test covers microeconomic foundations (demand, supply, elasticity, market forms) and macroeconomic aggregates (GDP, GNP, NNP, money, banking, fiscal and monetary policy, BoP) at the NCERT Class XI–XII level. The section contributes ~15–25 discrete items to the General Test paper (code 501), with national income accounting and money-banking as the highest-yield sub-units.

- **Micro block:** Law of Demand (inverse P–Q), Law of Supply (direct P–Q), equilibrium where Q_d(P) = Q_s(P), and price elasticity E_d = (%ΔQ)/(%ΔP).
- **Macro block:** GDP_MP = C + I + G + (X − M); GNP_MP = GDP_MP + NFIA; NNP_FC (National Income) = GNP_MP − Depreciation − Net Indirect Taxes.
- **Policy block:** Fiscal policy = taxation, expenditure, subsidies, borrowings. Monetary policy = repo rate, reverse repo, CRR, SLR, OMO, MSF (all administered by the RBI).
- **Reform block:** LPG = **L**iberalisation, **P**rivatisation, **G**lobalisation — three distinct post-1991 pillars, not synonyms.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"GDP → GNP → NNP"** flows in that order; remember the three subtractions as **"Minus-Depreciation-Minus-NIT"** to land at National Income. For demand, recall **"Price up → Quantity down → E_d negative, take |E|"** so a "−2" elasticity is reported as elastic.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Microeconomics: Demand, Supply, Elasticity
The Law of Demand states an inverse relationship between price (P, ₹/unit) and quantity demanded (Q_d, units), ceteris paribus. The Law of Supply gives a direct relationship for Q_s. Market equilibrium price P* satisfies Q_d(P*) = Q_s(P*); a demand shift (income, taste, price of related goods) moves both P* and Q*, while a movement *along* a curve is triggered only by the good's own price change. Price elasticity of demand E_d = (ΔQ/Q) ÷ (ΔP/P) is dimensionless [M⁰L⁰T⁰]; |E_d| > 1 is elastic, < 1 is inelastic, = 1 is unit-elastic. Total revenue TR = P × Q (₹) is maximised where E_d = −1.

#### Market Forms and Consumer Behaviour
Perfect competition, monopoly, oligopoly, and monopolistic competition differ on number of sellers, product differentiation, and price control. For a monopoly facing linear demand P = a − bQ, the MR curve has the same intercept but twice the slope: MR = a − 2bQ. Consumer equilibrium under Cardinal Utility requires MU_x/P_x = MU_y/P_y = λ (marginal utility per rupee equalised across goods).

#### National Income Accounting
National income flows are measured over one financial year. The identities (all in ₹) follow:

| Aggregate | Formula | Key adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| GDP_MP | C + I + G + (X − M) | Add consumption, investment, govt. spending, net exports |
| GNP_MP | GDP_MP + NFIA | Add net factor income from abroad |
| NNP_MP | GNP_MP − Depreciation | Subtract capital consumption allowance |
| NNP_FC (National Income) | NNP_MP − Net Indirect Taxes | Indirect taxes minus subsidies |

#### Money, Banking, and Policy Instruments
RBI controls money supply M1 (currency + demand deposits), M2, M3, M4. Commercial banks create credit with a multiplier = 1/LRR, where LRR = CRR + SLR. CRR is the cash reserve ratio held with RBI; SLR is the statutory liquidity ratio held in gold and government securities. Repo rate is the rate at which RBI lends short-term funds to banks.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix
The following commonly confused pairs cost marks every year:

| Concept A | Concept B | Distinction |
|---|---|---|
| GDP at factor cost | GDP at market price | FC excludes net indirect taxes; MP includes them |
| GNP | NNP | NNP = GNP − Depreciation |
| Movement along demand curve | Shift of demand curve | Movement = own-price change; shift = change in a non-price determinant |
| CRR | SLR | CRR held as cash with RBI; SLR held as gold/G-secs with the bank itself |
| Fiscal deficit | Revenue deficit | Revenue deficit = Revenue expenditure − Revenue receipts (no borrowings for assets) |
| Current account (BoP) | Capital account (BoP) | Current = visible + invisible trade; Capital = FDI, FPI, ECBs, banking capital |

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem
**Question:** In an economy, Consumption = ₹400 cr, Investment = ₹100 cr, Government Spending = ₹80 cr, Exports = ₹60 cr, Imports = ₹90 cr, Net Factor Income from Abroad = ₹20 cr, Depreciation = ₹30 cr, Net Indirect Taxes = ₹10 cr. Calculate (a) GDP_MP, (b) GNP_MP, and (c) National Income (NNP_FC).

#### Solution:
- GDP_MP = C + I + G + (X − M) = 400 + 100 + 80 + (60 − 90) = **₹550 cr**.
- GNP_MP = GDP_MP + NFIA = 550 + 20 = **₹570 cr**.
- NNP_FC = GNP_MP − Depreciation − NIT = 570 − 30 − 10 = **₹530 cr**.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students often stop at GDP and report "national income" without subtracting both depreciation and net indirect taxes. Note that NIT = Indirect Taxes − Subsidies; if the problem gives subsidies separately, subtract the difference. Also, X − M = −30 here (trade deficit), not +30 — keep the sign.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Advanced Traps and Edge Cases

| Trap | Correct treatment |
|---|---|
| Treating GDP as a stock | GDP is a flow measured over one financial year; wealth/stock measures include money supply M1, capital stock |
| Reporting E_d as positive | Price and quantity demanded move opposite, so E_d is negative; report \|E_d\| when classifying elastic/inelastic |
| BoP classification of FDI | FDI is a capital account entry, never current account — even if it crosses borders for production |
| LPG conflation | Liberalisation (deregulation, end of licence raj), Privatisation (disinvestment of PSUs), Globalisation (trade and FDI openness) are three distinct reforms |
| Inflation rate formula sign | π = [(Index_t − Index_{t−1}) / Index_{t−1}] × 100; do not divide by Index_t |
| Index base-year | CPI and WPI are periodically rebased by the MoSPI; the base-year revision does not change the formula, only the reference |

#### Producer Behaviour and PPC
The Production Possibility Curve (PPC) shows maximum combinations of two goods the economy can produce with given resources and technology. Points on the curve are productively efficient; inside the curve indicates unemployed resources; outside is unattainable today. The slope of the PPC at any point gives the Marginal Rate of Transformation (opportunity cost). Outward shifts of the PPC come from capital accumulation, technological progress, or labour-force growth — relevant when an exam item links LPG reforms to "shift of PPC rightward".

#### Balance of Payments and the Rupee
Current Account = Visible Trade (merchandise) + Invisible Trade (services, transfers). Capital Account records FDI, FPI (portfolio), External Commercial Borrowings (ECBs), and banking capital. A current-account deficit (CAD) is typically financed by capital inflows; CAD + capital account net = overall BoP, balanced by changes in forex reserves with RBI. Surplus/deficit terminology applies to each account separately — never call BoP itself "deficit" without naming the sub-account.

#### Inflation, Index Numbers, and RBI Tools
Inflation π can be computed via CPI (consumer), WPI (wholesale), or GDP deflator. The repo rate (rate at which RBI lends to banks), reverse repo (rate RBI pays on bank deposits), MSF (penalty overnight window), CRR, SLR, and Open Market Operations (buying/selling G-secs) are the standard monetary policy levers. An *increase* in repo rate tightens liquidity, raises bank lending rates, and cools demand-pull inflation.

#### Common Mistakes to Audit Before the Exam
1. Mixing up *change in demand* (curve shifts) with *change in quantity demanded* (movement along the curve).
2. Computing budget deficit with borrowings included — the textbook definition excludes borrowings.
3. Stating that SLR earns no interest — historically CRR did not, but SLR securities do.
4. Calling the Planning Commission still active — it was replaced by **NITI Aayog** in January 2015; questions referencing the planning body since 2015 refer to NITI Aayog.
5. Reporting the BoP surplus/deficit on the wrong side — always specify *current account* or *capital account*.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts
1. If price falls from ₹20 to ₹18 and quantity demanded rises from 100 to 130 units, compute E_d using the point method and classify elasticity. (Answer key: E_d = −3, elastic.)
2. An economy has GDP_MP = ₹1,000 cr, Depreciation = ₹80 cr, Net Indirect Taxes = ₹50 cr, NFIA = −₹20 cr (net factor income paid abroad). Find National Income. (Answer key: ₹850 cr.)
