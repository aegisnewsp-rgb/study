# Paper 2 — Writing — HKDSE English Language Notes

Paper 2 of the HKDSE English Language examination tests Writing. It is 2 hours long and carries 25% of the subject mark (10% for Part A and 15% for Part B). Part A is a short guided task of about 200 words; Part B is a longer, more open-ended task of about 400 words with a choice of 1 out of 4 questions. The paper tests the ability to write for different contexts, audiences and purposes with relevant content; use varied vocabulary, linguistic devices and language patterns; plan and produce coherent, structured texts; and use appropriate tone, style, register and genre features.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Paper 2 weight**: 25% of the subject mark (Part A 10% + Part B 15%).
- **Duration**: 2 hours.
- **Part A**: a short guided task of about 200 words; the situation, purpose and relevant information are given.
- **Part B**: a longer task of about 400 words; choose 1 out of 4 questions.
- **Tested abilities**: relevant content for the context/audience/purpose; varied vocabulary and linguistic devices; coherent structure; appropriate tone, style, register and genre features; ability to plan and revise.
- **Genre coverage**: typically articles, letters (formal/informal), emails, reports, reviews, proposals, speeches, narratives.

#### Examiner traps

- Exceeding the recommended word count for Part A — the time spent on Part A costs marks on Part B, which carries more weight.
- Choosing a Part B question because the topic is "interesting" rather than because the candidate can handle its genre.
- Generic templates — examiners recognise memorised openings ("Nowadays, more and more people...") and the band drops.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Part A — the guided task (about 200 words)

Part A gives you the situation, the purpose and the relevant information. You are expected to write a short text (about 200 words) using the given information, for the given audience and purpose.

Common Part A genres: an email to a teacher, a notice for a school bulletin board, a short report, a brief article, a letter of apology or complaint, a proposal summary.

Approach:

1. Read the situation, the audience and the purpose carefully.
2. Identify the bullet points or required content.
3. Draft an outline: opening sentence addressing the audience, the main content in 2-3 paragraphs, a closing sentence.
4. Write the draft, aiming for about 200 words.
5. Check: does it address all the given content? Is the tone appropriate? Have I used varied vocabulary?

#### Part B — the longer task (about 400 words, choose 1 of 4)

Part B offers 4 questions on different themes and genres. Each question typically asks for a specific text type (article, letter, report, review, story, speech, proposal). Read all four options before choosing.

Approach:

1. Read all four Part B questions.
2. Choose the question whose genre and topic you can handle best.
3. Plan: introduction (point or argument), 2-3 body paragraphs (each with a topic sentence and supporting details), conclusion (link back to the question).
4. Write the draft, aiming for about 400 words.
5. Revise: vary your sentence length, use linking words, check spelling and grammar.

#### Common Part B genres and structures

- **Article**: catchy title, introduction with a question or strong statement, body paragraphs with examples, conclusion with a call to action or reflection.
- **Letter (formal)**: sender's address, date, recipient's address, salutation, introduction stating the purpose, body paragraphs, closing, signature line.
- **Letter (informal)**: greeting, news or update, question to the reader, closing.
- **Report**: title, introduction stating the purpose, findings in sub-headed sections, conclusion with recommendations.
- **Review**: title, summary of the work, evaluation of strengths and weaknesses, recommendation.
- **Speech**: greeting, hook, main points with personal anecdotes, strong closing (call to action).

#### Assessment criteria

The HKDSE English Writing paper rewards:

- **Content**: addressing the question, providing relevant and developed ideas.
- **Organisation**: clear introduction, body and conclusion; logical paragraph breaks.
- **Language**: varied vocabulary, accurate grammar, appropriate register.
- **Genre awareness**: using the conventions of the chosen genre (e.g. salutation for a letter; sub-headings for a report).

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-paper links

- **Reading (Paper 1)**: the texts in Paper 1 model the expected register and vocabulary range. Read widely — broadsheet newspapers, magazine articles, opinion pieces — to absorb different writing styles.
- **Listening (Paper 3)**: the integrated writing tasks in Paper 3 require note-taking and information selection; the same skills underpin Part A.
- **SBA**: the SBA reading/viewing programme is the venue to build the writing stamina needed for the exam.

#### Exam technique

- **Allocate time**: Part A ~30 minutes (including planning), Part B ~75 minutes (including planning), review ~10-15 minutes.
- **Plan before writing**: a 5-minute plan saves 15 minutes of revision and produces a more coherent text.
- **Variety in sentence length and structure**: alternate long and short sentences; use complex structures (relative clauses, participial phrases, conditional sentences) sparingly and accurately.
- **Topic-specific vocabulary**: avoid generic words. Use specific words ("myriad", "commendable", "alleviate") where they fit, not as filler.
- **Cohesive devices**: vary your linking words (furthermore, conversely, nonetheless, in light of this). Do not start every paragraph with "Also".

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "The more words I write, the higher my grade." No — irrelevant content and padding cost marks. Stay close to the recommended word count.
- "Fancy vocabulary improves my grade." Inappropriate fancy vocabulary lowers the grade. Use sophisticated vocabulary where it fits naturally.
- "Memorising an essay template guarantees a high grade." Examiners recognise memorised phrases; the band drops for Lexical Resource and Task Response.
