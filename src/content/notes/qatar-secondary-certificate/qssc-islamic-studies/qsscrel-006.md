# Fiqh — Ahkam al-Jihad and Masadir al-Tashri' — Qatar Secondary Certificate Islamic Studies Notes

The MoEHE Grade 12 Islamic Studies Fiqh (Islamic Jurisprudence, الفقه الإسلامي) domain opens in Term 1 with "Ahkam al-Jihad" (Rulings of Jihad, أحكام الجهاد) and continues in Term 2 with "Masadir al-Tashri' al-Islami" (Sources of Islamic Legislation, مصادر التشريع الإسلامي). These are the foundational fiqh modules of the Grade 12 curriculum.

> Verify the live Grade 12 textbook edition on https://www.edu.gov.qa/ before planning revision.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Jihad in Islamic law:** struggle in the path of Allah — the greater jihad (jihad al-nafs, against the lower self) and the lesser jihad (jihad al-qital, armed struggle in defined conditions).
- **Conditions for jihad al-qital:** just cause, legitimate authority, proportional response, no harm to non-combatants, no mutilation, no forced conversion.
- **Masadir al-Tashri':** the four sources of Islamic law — Quran, Sunnah, Ijma' (consensus), Qiyas (analogy). Some scholars add Istihsan, Maslahah Mursalah, Istishab, 'Urf.
- **Fiqh methodology:** the four Sunni schools (Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i, Hanbali) and their distinct approaches to evidence and method.

#### Examiner traps

- Reducing jihad to qital only — the Quranic and Prophetic usage covers both meanings.
- Treating the four sources as equal in weight — the Quran and Sunnah are primary; Ijma' and Qiyas are secondary and derived.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Jihad in the Quran and Sunnah

Quranic usage: jihad in the Quran primarily means struggle, striving, and effort in the path of Allah. The armed struggle (qital) is one meaning — " أذن للذين يقاتلون بأنهم ظلموا وإن الله على نصرهم لقدير " (Al-Hajj 22:39) — but the broader concept covers all forms of effort.

Prophetic usage: the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) returned from a battle and said "رجعنا من الجهاد الأصغر إلى الجهاد الأكبر" — we have returned from the lesser jihad (battle) to the greater jihad (the struggle against the self).

Conditions for jihad al-qital in classical fiqh: (1) just cause — defence of the Muslim community, removal of oppression, defence of religious freedom; (2) the ruler's call — armed struggle is waged by the state, not individuals; (3) proportional response; (4) no harm to non-combatants, women, children, the elderly, places of worship, crops, or animals except by necessity; (5) no forced conversion — " لا إكراه في الدين " (Al-Baqarah 2:256).

Classical rulings: the rules of engagement, treatment of prisoners, distribution of war booty, the status of treaties and truces, and the conditions for peace. The classical manuals (al-Shaybani's al-Siyar al-Kabir, al-Awza'i's al-Siyar, Ibn al-Qayyim's Ahkam Ahl al-Dhimma) develop these in detail.

#### Masadir al-Tashri' — the four sources

**1. The Quran (القرآن):** the literal word of Allah revealed to Muhammad (peace be upon him). The primary source. It contains explicit rulings (ahkam) and general principles (usul).

**2. The Sunnah (السنة):** the words, actions, and tacit approvals of the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him). The second source. It explains and details the Quran. Types: qawliyya (verbal), fi'liyya (action), taqririyya (tacit approval).

**3. Ijma' (الإجماع):** the consensus of the scholars of the Muslim community on a particular issue. Conditions: unanimity, after the Prophet's time, on a legal (not theological) matter, by qualified scholars. Valid by consensus of the four Sunni schools; debated by some Mu'tazili and Shi'i schools.

**4. Qiyas (القياس):** analogical reasoning — applying a known ruling to a new case that shares the same legal cause ('illa). Example: the prohibition of khamr (wine) is extended to all intoxicants based on the 'illa of causing loss of reason.

#### Secondary sources

Some schools accept additional sources:

- **Istihsan (الاستحسان):** moving from a strict analogy to a more equitable ruling — accepted by the Hanafi school.
- **Maslahah Mursalah (المصلحة المرسلة):** unrestricted public interest — accepted by the Maliki school under conditions.
- **Istishab (الاستصحاب):** presumption of continuity — the existing state continues until evidence changes it.
- **'Urf (العرف):** custom — accepted when it does not contradict the Sharia.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### Why fiqh carries marks at the MoEHE terminal

The MoEHE Fiqh domain tests the student's understanding of the rulings of jihad (Term 1) and the methodology of Islamic legislation (Term 2). These are central to the Grade 12 Islamic Studies curriculum and appear in the terminal assessment as structured questions.

#### Exam technique

- For jihad questions, distinguish the greater jihad (spiritual) from the lesser jihad (armed struggle) — both are valid uses of the term.
- For Masadir al-Tashri', rank the sources in order (Quran > Sunnah > Ijma' > Qiyas) and explain the role of each.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Jihad = holy war." The Quranic and Prophetic usage is broader; "holy war" is a Western medieval coinage (cruzada, croisade) that does not map directly to the Arabic.
- "The four schools disagree on everything." The four Sunni schools agree on the sources (Quran, Sunnah, Ijma', Qiyas) and on the broad principles; they differ on methodology and specific rulings.
