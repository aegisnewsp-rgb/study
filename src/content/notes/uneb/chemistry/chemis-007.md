# Electronic Configuration and Periodicity

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Treat the orbital diagram as a packing instruction: lowest energy first, two electrons per box, all parallel spins before any pairing.

Electron shells build up by the **Madelung rule**: lowest *(n + ℓ)* value fills first, breaking ties by smaller *n*. Periodicity is the recurrence of similar valence-shell patterns at fixed atomic-number intervals.

| Quantum number | Symbol | Allowed values | Physical meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Principal | *n* | 1, 2, 3, … | Shell size |
| Azimuthal | *ℓ* | 0 to *n* − 1 | Subshell shape (s, p, d, f) |
| Magnetic | *mℓ* | −*ℓ* … +*ℓ* | Orbital orientation |
| Spin | *ms* | +½ or −½ | Electron spin direction |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook (the "2-4-D-H" hook):** 2 electrons per orbital · 4 quantum numbers describe each · Degenerate orbitals singly first · Hund's rule = "spread out before pairing".

Atomic radius shrinks left-to-right and grows top-to-bottom. Ionisation energy climbs across a period but dips sharply between groups 2→13 (entering a new subshell) and 15→16 (paired-electron repulsion in 2p).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> The 60-second version above lists facts; this tier shows why each fact holds so a UNEB Paper 2 essay question on periodic trends earns full marks.

#### How orbitals fill and why the exceptions exist

Three rules govern every configuration you will write. The **Aufbau principle** orders orbitals by *(n + ℓ)*, giving the canonical sequence 1s → 2s → 2p → 3s → 3p → 4s → 3d → 4p … The **Pauli exclusion principle** caps occupancy at two electrons per orbital, with *ms* = +½ and −½. **Hund's rule** requires degenerate orbitals to be singly occupied with parallel spins before any pairing, minimising electron-electron repulsion.

Two configurations routinely catch candidates out. Chromium is **[Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹**, not 3d⁴ 4s²; copper is **[Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹**, not 3d⁹ 4s². The half-filled and fully-filled d-subshell gains extra exchange-stabilisation energy that outweighs the small cost of promoting a 4s electron.

#### Trends driven by effective nuclear charge

Effective nuclear charge is **Zeff = Z − σ**, where *Z* is the proton count (dimensionless, in elementary-charge units) and *σ* is the shielding constant from inner electrons (dimensionless). Across a period, *Z* rises while *σ* stays roughly constant, so Zeff climbs and pulls electrons inward — atomic radius shrinks. Down a group, an extra shell adds distance from the nucleus; radius grows despite higher Zeff.

| Property | Across period 3 (left → right) | Down group 1 (top → bottom) |
|---|---|---|
| Atomic radius | Decreases | Increases |
| First ionisation energy | Increases (with Be→B and N→O dips) | Decreases |
| Electronegativity | Increases (Cl highest in period 3) | Decreases |
| Metallic character | Decreases | Increases |

#### A worked Paper 2-style problem

A UNEB structured question often reads: *"Explain why the first ionisation energy of nitrogen (1402 kJ mol⁻¹) is higher than that of oxygen (1314 kJ mol⁻¹)."*

Nitrogen has the configuration 1s² 2s² 2p³, so each of the three 2p orbitals holds one electron. The half-filled 2p³ arrangement gives maximum exchange energy and zero pairing repulsion. Oxygen is 1s² 2s² 2p⁴, forcing one orbital to hold a paired set. Removing one electron from oxygen relieves that pairing repulsion, so less energy is needed.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Candidates write "because nitrogen has a half-filled subshell" without quantifying the exchange-stabilisation argument or naming the pairing penalty in oxygen. State both effects explicitly — the marker awards marks for the mechanism, not the label.

Ionisation energy itself is **IE₁(g) = E(X⁺(g)) − E(X(g))**, measured in kJ mol⁻¹ for one mole of gaseous atoms. Use the correct sign convention: a positive value means energy is absorbed (endothermic).

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> The Lite and Standard tiers give you a working model; this tier tests its limits where UNEB Paper 2 essay questions are most often lost.

#### Edge cases that break the naive trend

1. **Isoelectronic comparisons** — Na⁺ (1s² 2s² 2p⁶, 11 protons), Ne (1s² 2s² 2p⁶, 10 protons), and F⁻ (1s² 2s² 2p⁶, 9 protons) share the same electron count but differ in nuclear charge. Ionic radius shrinks as *Z* rises across the isoelectronic series: F⁻ > Ne > Na⁺.
2. **d-block contraction** — From Sc to Zn, the poor shielding of 3d electrons makes Zeff climb faster than expected, pulling radii down. This is why period 4 d-block atoms are similar in size to period 3 p-block atoms and why post-transition metals (Ga, In, Tl) ride unusually high densities.
3. **f-block contraction (lanthanide contraction)** — Poor 4f shielding across the lanthanides produces a cumulative shrinkage that affects the 5d series, so Zr and Hf have nearly identical atomic radii.

#### Common trap list for revision

1. Writing 4s before 3d in the fill order is correct; removing 4s before 3d during ionisation is also correct. Do not invert these — many candidates do.
2. Cr (Z = 24) and Cu (Z = 29) are the only period-4 neutral atoms whose ground-state configuration deviates from Madelung. Do not generalise the exception to Mo/Ag/Pd without checking; Mo is [Kr] 4d⁵ 5s¹, but Pd is [Kr] 4d¹⁰ 5s⁰ — a different anomaly.
3. Electron affinity trends resemble electronegativity trends but are not identical. Pauling's electronegativity is **χ = 0.359 × √(IE − EA)**, with IE and EA in kJ mol⁻¹; noble gases are missing from most EA tables because their measured EA values are either very low or undefined.
4. Across period 2, two dips exist: Be→B (899 → 801 kJ mol⁻¹) because B's 2p electron is partly shielded by the filled 2s subshell, and N→O (1402 → 1314 kJ mol⁻¹) due to paired 2p repulsion in O. Do not state that IE "always increases".
5. Transition metals show variable oxidation states because (n−1)d and ns energies are close. Fe²⁺ is [Ar] 3d⁶; Fe³⁺ is [Ar] 3d⁵. Note that ionisation removes 4s before 3d.
6. Electronegativity has no units on the Pauling scale; it is a derived dimensionless number with F at 3.98 as the practical maximum.

#### Two advanced prompts

1. *Essay prompt:* Account for the variation in first ionisation energy across period 3 (Na → Ar), using configurations and Zeff arguments, and explain any discontinuities you encounter.
2. *Numerical prompt:* Rank Na⁺, Mg²⁺, Al³⁺, and Ne by ionic radius, justifying your order using *Z* and the shared 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ configuration.
