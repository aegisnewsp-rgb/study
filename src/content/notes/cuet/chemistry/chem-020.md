# Solid State

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your CUET UG Chemistry paper.

Solid State covers the classification, internal architecture, and defects of solids in which particles (atoms, ions, molecules) vibrate about fixed mean positions under negligible translational freedom. CUET UG Chemistry typically asks 1–2 questions from this unit (≈4% weightage), most often numericals on density or packing efficiency.

- **Crystalline solid**: long-range order, sharp melting point, definite geometry (e.g., NaCl, diamond).
- **Amorphous solid**: only short-range order, no sharp melting point, called *pseudo-solids* (e.g., glass, rubber).
- **Unit cell**: smallest repeating 3D block of a crystal lattice; characterised by edge lengths *a, b, c* and angles *α, β, γ*.
- **Coordination number**: number of nearest neighbours around a particle.
- **Density formula**: ρ = ZM / (N_A · a³), where Z = formula units per cell, M = molar mass (kg mol⁻¹), N_A = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹, a = edge length (m).
- **Three cubic cells**: SCC (Z = 1, packing 52.4%), BCC (Z = 2, packing 68%), FCC (Z = 4, packing 74%).

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** *"SCC = 1, BCC = 2, FCC = 4"* for Z, and *"Packing jumps 52 → 68 → 74"* for efficiency — read left-to-right as you go from loose to closest packing.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months to revise.

#### Classification and Crystal Systems

Crystalline solids are classified into seven crystal systems based on axial ratios (a ≠ b ≠ c or a = b ≠ c, etc.) and inter-axial angles (α, β, γ = 90° or not). These give rise to **14 Bravais lattices** when primitive and non-primitive variants are counted.

- Molecular (e.g., ice), ionic (NaCl), covalent/network (diamond, SiO₂), and metallic (Cu, Fe) are the four bonding-based sub-types.
- Amorphous solids lack long-range periodicity; they soften gradually over a temperature range rather than melting sharply.

#### Cubic Unit Cells Compared

| Property | Simple Cubic (SCC) | Body-Centred Cubic (BCC) | Face-Centred Cubic (FCC) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Atoms per cell (Z) | 1 | 2 | 4 |
| Coordination number | 6 | 8 | 12 |
| Nearest-neighbour distance | a / 2 | (√3 / 2) a | a / √2 |
| Packing efficiency | 52.4 % | 68.0 % | 74.0 % |
| Relation r vs a | r = a / 2 | r = (√3 / 4) a | r = a / (2√2) |

> 📌 **Formula Check:** Packing efficiency (%) = (Z × V_particle / V_cell) × 100; both volumes must be in m³ so the ratio stays dimensionless.

#### Close Packing and Voids

In hexagonal close packing (HCP) and cubic close packing (CCP = FCC), each sphere touches 12 neighbours. The interstitial voids formed are:

| Void type | Number per N spheres | Radius ratio r_void / r_sphere |
|---|---|---|
| Tetrahedral | 2 N | 0.225 |
| Octahedral | N | 0.414 |

#### Point Defects

1. **Schottky defect**: equal cation + anion vacancies → preserves stoichiometry, **lowers density**, occurs when cation and anion sizes are similar (NaCl, KCl).
2. **Frenkel defect**: small cation displaced to an interstitial site → preserves stoichiometry, **density unchanged**, common in AgCl, AgBr (small cation, large anion).
3. **F-centres**: anion vacancy trapping an electron → gives non-stoichiometric colour (NaCl yellow, LiCl pink).
4. **Impurity / doping defects**: introduce Group 13 (p-type) or Group 15 (n-type) atoms into Group 14 lattices (Si, Ge).

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** A metal crystallises in a face-centred cubic lattice. Its edge length is 408.6 pm and molar mass is 107.87 g mol⁻¹. Calculate its density in g cm⁻³. (Given N_A = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹)

#### Solution:
For FCC: Z = 4.
Convert units: a = 408.6 pm = 408.6 × 10⁻¹⁰ cm = 4.086 × 10⁻⁸ cm, M = 107.87 g mol⁻¹.
Using ρ = ZM / (N_A · a³):
a³ = (4.086 × 10⁻⁸)³ = 6.823 × 10⁻²³ cm³.
ρ = (4 × 107.87) / (6.022 × 10²³ × 6.823 × 10⁻²³)
ρ = 431.48 / (4.109 × 10¹)
ρ ≈ 10.50 g cm⁻³.
This matches the accepted density of silver (Ag), confirming FCC structure.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Forgetting to convert pm → cm (or to m) before cubing the edge length inflates a³ by a factor of 10³⁰ and crashes the answer. Always convert first.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Radius-Ratio Rule and Geometry Selection

| Radius ratio (r⁺ / r⁻) | Coordination geometry | Void occupied |
|---|---|---|
| 0.225 – 0.414 | Tetrahedral | Tetrahedral |
| 0.414 – 0.732 | Octahedral | Octahedral |
| 0.732 – 1.000 | Cubic (CsCl-type) | Cubic |

Outside these limits, the cation "rattles" in its hole (unstable) or cannot fit (forces lower coordination). Use this rule to predict structure from ionic radii.

#### Electrical and Magnetic Properties

Conductors have overlapping valence and conduction bands; insulators have a wide band gap (Eg > ~4 eV); semiconductors have a moderate gap (Eg ~1–3 eV). Intrinsic conductivity: σ = n e (μₑ + μₕ), where n = carrier density (m⁻³), e = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, μₑ, μₕ = mobilities (m² V⁻¹ s⁻¹).

- **n-type**: Si doped with P/As (Group 15) → electrons are majority carriers.
- **p-type**: Si doped with B/Ga (Group 13) → holes are majority carriers.

Magnetic behaviour depends on alignment of permanent magnetic dipoles:

1. **Diamagnetic**: all electrons paired, weakly repelled by field (Bi, Cu⁺, most organic solids).
2. **Paramagnetic**: unpaired electrons, weakly attracted (O₂, Fe³⁺ salts).
3. **Ferromagnetic**: parallel alignment below Curie temperature (Fe, Co, Ni).
4. **Ferrimagnetic**: anti-parallel but unequal moments (Fe₃O₄, ferrites).
5. **Antiferromagnetic**: anti-parallel equal moments (MnO, NiO) — net magnetisation zero below Néel temperature.

#### Edge Cases and Advanced Traps

1. **HCP ≠ FCC stacking order**: HCP has ABAB… stacking, CCP (FCC) has ABCABC… — yet both reach 74% packing efficiency.
2. **Density paradox**: A Schottky-defected crystal has more volume per formula unit because vacancies expand the lattice slightly; the combined effect always *decreases* bulk density.
3. **Coordination number ≠ Z**: Z is the count of atoms *belonging* to one unit cell after sharing; coordination number is geometric neighbours.
4. **Pseudo-solids flow**: Glass and polymers are amorphous and show slow time-dependent deformation — hence "supercooled liquids".
5. **F-centre vs Schottky**: F-centres need an anion vacancy *plus* a trapped electron (often from a dopant) to colour a crystal; a plain Schottky vacancy alone does not produce colour.

#### Two Advanced Practice Prompts

1. A cubic crystal has a = 4.077 Å, ρ = 2.165 g cm⁻³, M = 58.44 g mol⁻¹. Identify the unit-cell type and the element.
2. Pure silicon has Eg = 1.12 eV. After doping with boron, what type of semiconductor forms, and which carriers dominate conduction at room temperature?
