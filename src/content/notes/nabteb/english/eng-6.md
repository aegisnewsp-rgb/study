# Direct and Indirect Speech

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Direct speech** reproduces the speaker's exact words inside quotation marks, paired with a reporting verb (e.g., *said*, *asked*). **Indirect speech** (reported speech) conveys the same meaning without quotation marks, forcing systematic changes in tense, pronouns, and time/place expressions. NABTEB tests this skill through 4–7 multiple-choice items worth roughly 3% of the English paper.

- **Reporting verb**: introduces the spoken words — *say, tell, ask, reply, exclaim*.
- **Tense back-shift**: present → past, past → past perfect, *will* → *would*, *can* → *can*.
- **Pronoun shift**: 1st person follows the reporting subject; 2nd person becomes 3rd; 3rd person stays.
- **Time/place shifts**: *today → that day, tomorrow → the next day, here → there, now → then*.

| Change category | Direct form | Indirect form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tense | "I **am** tired." | He said he **was** tired. |
| Pronoun | "**I** will come." | She said **she** would come. |
| Time word | "**today**" | that day |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Mechanism of Reported Speech

When the reporting verb sits in the simple past (*said, told, asked*), the reported clause must back-shift one tense step. Present perfect becomes past perfect (*have done → had done*), and modals slide backward (*can → can, must → had to*). If the reporting verb is present (*says, says*), the reported tense stays unchanged — NABTEB items sometimes hinge on spotting this.

#### Pronoun and Adverbial Shifts

First-person pronouns (*I, me, my, we, us*) adopt the person of the reporting subject. Second-person pronouns (*you, your*) in most cases convert to third-person (*he, she, they, his, her, their*) unless the listener remains the same. Demonstratives also shift: *this → that, these → those*. Time and place adverbials slide backward by one reference point.

| Direct element | Indirect equivalent | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| today | that day | "Today is hot." → He said that day was hot. |
| yesterday | the day before / the previous day | "I came yesterday." → She said she had come the day before. |
| tomorrow | the next day / the following day | "I will travel tomorrow." → He said he would travel the next day. |
| now | then / at that time | "I am busy now." → She said she was busy then. |
| here | there | "It is cold here." → He said it was cold there. |
| ago | before | "I met him two days ago." → She said she had met him two days before. |

#### Transforming Questions, Commands, and Exclamations

Yes/no questions introduce *if* or *whether*, while wh-questions keep the wh-word but flip to statement word order. Imperatives use *to + infinitive* for affirmative commands and *not + to + infinitive* for negatives. Exclamations convert to statements introduced by *exclaimed with/expressing + noun* (joy, anger, surprise), with the exclamation mark removed.

- Always drop quotation marks and the comma after the reporting verb in indirect form.
- Use *told* (with object) for statements, *asked* for questions, *ordered/commanded* for emphatic imperatives.
- Insert *that* after *said/told* in statements, but never before an imperative or a *yes/no* question.
- Reporting verbs in the present tense (*says, is saying*) freeze the reported verb — a frequent NABTEB trap.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Examiner Traps

NABTEB objective items in many papers test whether candidates over-shift modals that need no change. *Would, can, should, can, used to, ought to, had better* already carry past-time meaning, so they in most keys remain stable. Likewise, eternal truths and habitual facts stay in the present even inside a past reporting verb: *"The sun rises in the east"* → *He said the sun rises in the east*. Conditional type 2 (*If I were you, I would…*) also resists back-shifting.

#### Worked Micro-Examples

1. Direct: *"I am writing the report now," Chinedu said.* → Chinedu said that he was writing the report then.
2. Direct: *"Have you finished the task?" she asked him.* → She asked him if/whether he had finished the task.
3. Direct: *"Don't touch the machine!" the foreman shouted.* → The foreman ordered/told them not to touch the machine.
4. Direct: *"What a beautiful workshop!" the visitor exclaimed.* → The visitor exclaimed with admiration that it was a very beautiful workshop.

#### High-Yield Practice Prompts

- Convert: *"We shall deliver the goods tomorrow," the manager assured us.*
- Spot the error: *Ade told that he had completed the project.* (Correct by deleting *that*, since *told* requires an object: *Ade told us/me that…*.)

#### Exam Strategy for NABTEB

Direct and Indirect Speech appears in the objective section (Paper 1) as single-sentence transformations, and occasionally surfaces in the comprehension passage as reported dialogue. Allocate under 30 seconds per item: identify the reporting verb's tense, apply the three shifts (tense, pronoun, adverbial), then verify question/imperative handling. Skip multi-rule sentences first; return only after clearing the easier mechanical shifts.

| Common mistake | Correction |
| --- | --- |
| "He said he is coming." | He said he **was** coming. |
| "She asked was I ready?" | She asked **if** I **was** ready. |
| "He told that he was tired." | He told **me/us** that he was tired. |
| "She said to go immediately." | She told him **to** go immediately. |
