# Introduction to Economics

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your UPPSC PCS prelims or mains paper.

Economics is the science of **scarcity**: how society rations limited resources against unlimited wants. Adam Smith framed it as the study of **wealth**; Marshall reframed it as the study of **human welfare** in ordinary business life; Robbins tightened the modern definition to a **science of scarcity**; Samuelson added the social-choice layer. UPPSC repeats these names in matching-type MCQs.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **S**mith = **S**tudy of **W**ealth. **M**arshall = **M**ankind / Welfare. **R**obbins = **R**ationing scarce means. **S**amuelson = **S**ociety + scarce resources. Associate the first letter of each economist with the first letter of the keyword.

| Central Problem | Question it asks |
|---|---|
| What to produce | Which goods and in what quantities |
| How to produce | Which technique (labour-intensive vs capital-intensive) |
| For whom to produce | Who gets the output (distribution) |

| Branch | Unit of analysis | Example variable |
|---|---|---|
| Microeconomics | Individual consumer, firm, market | Individual demand curve |
| Macroeconomics | Economy-wide aggregates | GDP, inflation rate |

The **Production Possibility Curve (PPC)** plots two goods, $Q_A + Q_B = \text{constant}$, where $Q_A$ and $Q_B$ are output quantities (units). Points on the curve show efficient use; points inside show idle resources; points outside are unattainable today.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Scope and Definitions

The word *Economics* comes from the Greek *oikonomikos* (household management). Four definitions dominate the UPPSC paper:

- **Adam Smith (1776, Wealth of Nations)** — inquiry into the nature and causes of the **wealth of nations**. Focus: production and accumulation.
- **Alfred Marshall (1890, Principles of Economics)** — a study of mankind in the **ordinary business of life**; examines both wealth and welfare.
- **Lionel Robbins (1932, An Essay on the Nature and Significance of Economic Science)** — economics as the **science which studies human behaviour as a relationship between ends and scarce means which have alternative uses**. Scarcity is the centre.
- **Paul Samuelson (1948)** — the study of how people and society **use scarce resources** to produce valuable commodities and distribute them.

#### Factors of Production and Factor Payments

Land yields **rent**, labour yields **wages**, capital yields **interest**, entrepreneurship yields **profit**. UPPSC sometimes asks the matching of factor → reward.

#### Production Possibility Curve in Working Form

A PPC is drawn with Good A on the X-axis and Good B on the Y-axis. Its **concave** shape (bowed outward from origin) reflects **increasing opportunity cost** — as more of A is produced, increasingly larger quantities of B must be sacrificed because resources are not equally productive in both uses.

Slope of the PPC at any point: $\frac{dQ_B}{dQ_A}$ = opportunity cost of one extra unit of A, expressed in units of B.

#### Worked Problem (Prelims Style)

> An economy can produce a maximum of 200 units of food (F) or 100 units of cloth (C), or any linear combination along the PPC. It currently produces 150 F and 25 C. Identify the position of this point and calculate the opportunity cost of one extra unit of food.

**Step 1 — Slope of the linear PPC.** With endpoints (0, 100) on the cloth axis and (200, 0) on the food axis, the trade-off ratio is 100 cloth sacrificed for 200 food gained, so 1 F costs 100/200 = **0.5 C**.

**Step 2 — Locate (150 F, 25 C).** Maximum cloth available if all resources go to cloth = 100 C. At 150 F the economy sacrifices (150 × 0.5) = 75 C, leaving 100 − 75 = 25 C. The point (150, 25) lies **on** the curve, so resources are **fully and efficiently employed**.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Many candidates assume the economy is "wasting" resources because the figure 25 looks small. Check by substitution into the line equation $C = 100 - 0.5F$. The point satisfies it exactly — inside/outside the curve decisions must always be verified against the equation, not eyeballed.

#### Utility and Demand Foundations

- **Total Utility (TU)** = $\sum_{i=1}^{n} MU_i$, measured in utils.
- **Marginal Utility (MU$_n$)** = $TU_n - TU_{n-1}$, utils per unit consumed.
- **Average Utility (AU)** = $TU / Q$, utils per unit.
- **Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility**: as consumption of a good rises, MU falls, ceteris paribus.

#### Elasticity of Demand

$$E_d = \frac{\%\Delta Q_d}{\%\Delta P} = \frac{\Delta Q_d / Q_d}{\Delta P / P}$$

$E_d$ is **dimensionless**. $|E_d| > 1$ = elastic, $< 1$ = inelastic, $= 1$ = unit elastic.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

#### Edge Cases and Limits

1. **Linear PPC fallacy.** A straight-line PPC implies constant opportunity cost, which only holds when resources are perfectly adaptable between uses — a textbook limit case. Real-world PPCs are concave; treat linearity as a simplification, not the rule.
2. **Robbins and the "wants" question.** Robbins deliberately excluded wants from economic science because they are non-scarce by definition. Samuelson's later expansion re-introduced the study of *how* wants are satisfied under scarcity, not whether they exist.
3. **Marshall's "welfare" ambiguity.** Marshall treated welfare economics as normative but used positive tools (consumer surplus). UPPSC may pair a Marshall quote with a normative-sounding claim — judge by the economist named, not by the framing word.
4. **Mixed economy boundary.** India post-1991 is classified as a mixed economy with a dominant private sector and a strategic public sector. Ownership share is set by current disinvestment policy — verify the latest figure in the official UPPSC notification rather than memorising a stale ratio.
5. **PPC vs PPF terminology.** Some texts call the same diagram the *Production Possibility Frontier*. Both refer to the boundary where resources are fully and efficiently used.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. A closed economy moves from point P (on the PPC) to point Q (outside the PPC) within two years. Identify two real-world mechanisms through which this could occur and state the assumption about technology or resource stock that each mechanism violates if treated as a one-time jump.
2. Robbins' definition rejects welfare considerations while Samuelson's definition embraces them. Reconstruct the argument in 150 words and decide which definition better fits the analysis of India's NREGA wage-employment scheme. Justify with two criteria drawn from the definitions.

#### Common Mistakes Table

| Mistake | Why it fails | Correct framing |
|---|---|---|
| Equating scarcity with poverty | Poverty is a distribution outcome; scarcity is a universal resource-wants gap | Scarcity exists in every economy, rich or poor |
| Treating Smith's "wealth" and Marshall's "welfare" as synonyms | Wealth is stock of value; welfare is human well-being | They differ in objective: accumulation vs life-quality |
| Calling GDP a micro variable | GDP is an aggregate national-income measure | Macro only |
| Assuming PPC is always linear | Constant opportunity cost is a special case | Concavity reflects heterogeneous resource productivity |
| Writing MU as dimensionless | MU has units (utils per unit) and is positive until the satiety point | Cite utils/unit and the law of diminishing MU |
