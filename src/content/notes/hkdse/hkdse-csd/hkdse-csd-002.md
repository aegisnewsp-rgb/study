# Theme 1 — National Identity and Cultural Diversity — HKDSE CSD Notes

This topic covers Topics 2 and 3 of Theme 1 of the HKDSE Citizenship and Social Development curriculum. Topic 2 covers the contemporary situation of the country, the sense of national identity, the national symbols and achievements, and the benefits and contributions of the HKSAR's participation in national affairs. Topic 3 covers the factors shaping the characteristics of cultural diversity in Hong Kong society and the positive significance of that diversity.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Topic 2 — situation of the country and national identity**: political structure of the country (people's congress system, multi-party cooperation); the Chinese nation; Chinese nationality and citizens; national symbols (flag, emblem, anthem); the country's achievements in technology, medical care, culture, education, infrastructure and poverty eradication; benefits of HKSAR's participation in national affairs; contributions of Hong Kong to national development.
- **Topic 3 — cultural diversity of Hong Kong society**: Chinese culture as the mainstay; historical development of Hong Kong; the impact of Chinese traditional culture integrating with different cultures; positive significance (promoting inclusion, facilitating cultural innovations, promoting harmony, nurturing citizenship rooted in Chinese culture with a global perspective).
- **Assessment**: data-response and short-essay items on the 2-hour CSD paper.

#### Examiner traps

- Treating the national symbols as decorative items — they are legal symbols with stipulations in Annex III to the Basic Law.
- Conflating "the country" and "the government" — the country refers to the People's Republic of China as a whole; the government is the Central People's Government.
- Reducing cultural diversity to "East meets West" — the topic requires specific examples and the impact on Hong Kong's identity.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### The country's contemporary situation

**Political structure**: The People's Republic of China is governed through a system of people's congresses. The National People's Congress (NPC) is the highest organ of state power, with the Standing Committee exercising its powers between sessions. The State Council is the executive body. The Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference (CPPCC) is a political consultation body. The multi-party cooperation system is led by the Communist Party of China (CPC) and includes eight democratic parties.

**The Chinese nation**: A unified concept comprising the 56 ethnic groups recognised in the People's Republic of China, with the Han majority and 55 ethnic minorities. Chinese nationality is governed by the Nationality Law of the PRC.

**National symbols**: The five-star red flag (red field with five yellow stars in the upper-left canton — one large star and four small stars in an arc); the national emblem (Tiananmen Gate with five stars, encircled by ears of grain with a cogwheel at the base); the national anthem (the March of the Volunteers). Stipulations for use are in Annex III to the Basic Law.

#### The country's achievements

Examples to cite in a CSD short essay:

- **New high-end technologies**: 5G networks, BeiDou navigation system, Tianwen-1 Mars mission, Tiangong space station, AI and big data industries.
- **Medical care and public health**: the COVID-19 response (testing, vaccination, the dynamic-zero policy and subsequent adjustment), National Healthcare Security Administration, Healthy China 2030 initiative.
- **Culture and education**: Confucius Institutes internationally, UNESCO World Heritage sites, the expansion of higher education and vocational training.
- **Infrastructure**: high-speed rail (the world's largest network), Hong Kong-Zhuhai-Macau Bridge, the Belt and Road Initiative infrastructure.
- **Poverty eradication**: declared in February 2021 — the elimination of absolute poverty in rural China, a key milestone of the reform era.

#### HKSAR's participation in national affairs

**Benefits to Hong Kong**: the country's policies supporting Hong Kong's development (CEPA — Closer Economic Partnership Arrangement, the Greater Bay Area development, support for Hong Kong as an international financial centre, the Shanghai-Hong Kong Stock Connect and Shenzhen-Hong Kong Stock Connect schemes).

**Contributions by Hong Kong**: Hong Kong promotes development and exchange with the Mainland (financial services, legal and dispute resolution services, professional services including accounting and architecture); donations for natural disasters (the Hong Kong Jockey Club and the HKSAR Government have made significant donations for events including the 2008 Sichuan earthquake and the 2020 COVID-19 response); support for developing regions of the Mainland.

#### Cultural diversity of Hong Kong society

**Chinese culture as the mainstay**: Hong Kong's cultural foundation is Chinese culture, particularly Cantonese culture with roots in Lingnan. Traditional festivals (Lunar New Year, Mid-Autumn Festival, Dragon Boat Festival) are widely celebrated.

**Impact of Chinese traditional culture integrating with different cultures**: Hong Kong's history as a port city brought British colonial influence, Southeast Asian connections (many residents trace origins to Guangdong, Fujian and other southern Chinese provinces) and global trade links. The result is a hybrid culture that blends Cantonese, Hakka, Teochew, Western and other influences.

**Positive significance**: promoting inclusion of cultures, facilitating cultural innovations and reflections (Hong Kong cinema, Cantopop, Cantonese opera, design), promoting harmony among different ethnic groups (the South Asian, Indonesian and Filipino communities in Hong Kong), and nurturing citizenship rooted in Chinese culture with a global perspective.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-topic links

- **Theme 2**: the development plans and Five-Year Plans continue the story of national development begun in Topic 2.
- **Theme 3**: economic globalisation (Topic 1 of Theme 3) connects with the role of Hong Kong as an international city.
- **National Security Education**: the cultural-diversity topic connects to the holistic approach to national security through social security and identity security.

#### Exam technique

- **For short-essay items**: structure with a clear thesis, supporting evidence and a balanced conclusion. Use specific examples (e.g. the Hong Kong-Zhuhai-Macau Bridge, CEPA, the 2008 Sichuan earthquake donations).
- **For data-response items**: identify the issue, the context, the key stakeholders, the policy framework, and the implications. Use the source material directly.
- **For "national identity" questions**: distinguish the legal/national identity (Hong Kong residents are Chinese nationals) from the cultural identity (Chinese culture with global influences).

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Hong Kong culture is separate from Chinese culture." No — Hong Kong culture is rooted in Chinese culture (Cantonese, Lingnan, Chinese traditional values) with added global influences.
- "The Chinese nation is one ethnicity." No — the Chinese nation includes 56 ethnic groups in a unified concept.
- "Cultural diversity is a Western concept." Cultural diversity exists across all societies; Hong Kong's particular expression is the integration of Chinese culture with other influences.
