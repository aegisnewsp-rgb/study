# Part 3 — Two-Way Discussion — IELTS Speaking Notes

IELTS Speaking Part 3 is a two-way discussion linked to the Part 2 topic. The examiner asks abstract questions about issues related to the Part 2 topic, and the candidate is expected to express and justify opinions, speculate, and compare. Part 3 lasts about 4-5 minutes and is the strongest band discriminator at band 7+.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Format:** examiner-led discussion of abstract issues linked to the Part 2 topic.
- **Time:** 4-5 minutes.
- **Question types:** opinion, speculation, comparison, evaluation.
- **Answer length:** developed responses, 3-5 sentences per answer.
- **Common distractor:** the examiner challenges the candidate's view to extend the discussion.

#### Examiner traps

- Giving short, undeveloped answers — Part 3 expects developed responses.
- Avoiding the examiner's challenges — they are opportunities to extend the discussion.
- Memorised answers — examiners recognise them and the band for lexical resource drops.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Answer structure for Part 3

A good Part 3 answer has:

1. **Direct response:** answer the question.
2. **Reason:** explain why.
3. **Example:** give a specific example.
4. **Compare/speculate:** add a contrasting view or future possibility.

Example:

- Q: "Do you think technology has made people more isolated?"
- A: "In some ways, yes. With the rise of social media, many people spend more time on their phones than with friends in person. However, technology has also enabled people to maintain long-distance relationships and connect with communities they wouldn't otherwise have access to. So I think it's a mixed picture — it depends on how individuals use it."

#### Handling challenges

When the examiner challenges a view, acknowledge the challenge and provide a nuanced response:

- "I see your point. On the other hand..."
- "That's true to some extent, but..."
- "I can understand that view, however I still believe..."

#### Speculation vocabulary

For speculating about the future, use:

- **Modal expressions:** would, will probably, is likely to, is predicted to.
- **Future forms:** will, going to, is expected to, points toward.
- **Analytical phrasing:** it appears that, one perspective indicates, evidence suggests that.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where Part 3 appears across IELTS Speaking

Part 3 is the third and most difficult section, accounting for about a third of the Speaking band. It is the strongest discriminator at band 7+ — candidates who score 7+ in Speaking consistently achieve Band 7+ in Part 3.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Long answers always score higher." Quality matters more than length.
- "Avoiding challenges is safer." Challenges are opportunities to extend the discussion.
- "Memorised answers score high." Examiners recognise them.

#### Exam technique

- Listen to the question carefully and answer directly.
- Develop the answer with a reason and example.
- Use speculation vocabulary for future-oriented questions.
- Acknowledge challenges and provide nuanced responses.

#### Developing Abstract Answers

In Part 3, move beyond personal anecdotes to broader societal trends. Use signposting structures such as "From an economic perspective...", "If you look at urban areas compared to rural regions...", or "Historically, this trend was less pronounced, but today...". This directly demonstrates your ability to analyze macro issues systematically.
