# History of Tanzania and Independence Struggle

> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Tanganyika became independent on 9 December 1961** under Julius Kambarage Nyerere, then merged with the newly-independent Zanzibar (10 December 1963) on **26 April 1964** to form the **United Republic of Tanzania**. Memorise six fixed dates — they recur in nearly every multiple-choice paper on this topic.

- 1885 — German East Africa established under Karl Peters / DOAG
- 1919 — Treaty of Versailles cedes the territory to Britain as a League of Nations Mandate
- 1946 — reclassified as a UN Trust Territory under Chapter XI of the UN Charter
- 7 July 1954 — TANU founded (Nyerere president); TAA of 1929 is the *predecessor*, not TANU itself
- 9 December 1961 — Tanganyika independence (Dominion), Republic on 9 December 1962
- 12 January 1964 — Zanzibar Revolution; 22 April Articles of Union; **1 November 1964** the name *Tanzania* is adopted

| Period | Status | Administering power |
|---|---|---|
| 1885–1919 | German colony | German East Africa Company (DOAG) |
| 1920–1945 | League of Nations Mandate | Britain |
| 1946–1961 | UN Trust Territory | Britain |
| 1961–present | Independent state | Tanzanians (Nyerere → Karume → successors) |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook — "Pet-Mand-Tru-Ind-Rev-Un":** **PET**ers 1885 → **MAND**ate 1919 → **TRU**steeship 1946 → **IND**ependence 1961 → **REV**olution 12 Jan 1964 → **UN**ion 26 Apr 1964. Six pegs, six dates, one phrase.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Colonial Origins and the Mandate Question

German East Africa was carved out in 1885 through treaties signed by Carl (Karl) Peters on behalf of the Deutsch-Ostafrikanische Gesellschaft. The territory passed to Britain after Germany's defeat in the First World War, formalised by the Treaty of Versailles (1919) and administered from 1920 under a Class B League of Nations Mandate. Britain's administration was never a "colony" in the strict legal sense — this distinction matters because Tanganyika's path to sovereignty ran through UN trusteeship machinery, not decolonisation of a colony. In 1946 the territory became a UN Trust Territory under Chapter XI of the UN Charter, which obliged Britain to prepare Tanganyika for self-government and to submit annual petitions to the Trusteeship Council.

> 📌 **Exam Point:** Examiners repeatedly test the difference between Mandate, Trusteeship, and colony. Use the words *Mandate* (1920) and *Trust Territory* (1946), never *colony*, when answering.

#### Nationalism: TAA to TANU

The Tanganyika African Association (TAA) was formed in **1929** as a welfare and cultural body for civil servants and clerks. On **7 July 1954** it was reconstituted as the Tanganyika African National Union (TANU) at a meeting in Dar es Salaam; **Julius Kambarage Nyerere** was elected president and held the post until 1985. TANU's strategy combined constitutional negotiation with mass mobilisation. Internal self-government was granted in 1958–1959, with Nyerere serving as **Chief Minister**. General elections followed in 1958, 1959, and 1960. Full independence arrived on 9 December 1961 as a Dominion within the Commonwealth, then a Republic exactly one year later on 9 December 1962.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** The 1929 date belongs to the **TAA**, not TANU. Candidates who write "TANU was founded in 1929" lose marks. TANU's founding date is **7 July 1954**.

#### Zanzibar and the 1964 Revolution

Zanzibar was a separate British protectorate from 1890. The Afro-Shirazi Party (ASP), led by **Abeid Karume**, and the Zanzibar Nationalist Party mobilised competing constituencies. Elections in 1957, 1961, and 1963 produced shifting coalitions. On **10 December 1963** Zanzibar gained independence as a constitutional monarchy under Sultan Jamshid bin Abdullah. Just **33 days later**, on **12 January 1964**, an ASP-led uprising overthrew the Sultan. This was an internal revolution, not a mainland TANU operation.

#### Unification

Karume and Nyerere signed the **Articles of Union on 22 April 1964**; the **United Republic of Tanganyika and Zanzibar** came into being on 26 April 1964. The compound name *Tanzania* was adopted on **1 November 1964**.

| Concept | Tanganyika | Zanzibar | United Republic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Status pre-1964 | UN Trust Territory | British protectorate | — |
| Independence date | 9 Dec 1961 | 10 Dec 1963 | Formed 26 Apr 1964 |
| Key party | TANU (1954) | ASP | Union of both |
| Head at union | Nyerere | Karume (Rev. President) | Nyerere, President |
| Documents | — | — | Articles of Union, 22 Apr 1964 |

> 💡 **Revision Anchor — "Two rivers, one bank":** Tanganyika (mainland, African majority, TANU-led) and Zanzibar (island, Arab-African mix, ASP-led after revolution) flowed into one state. The Articles of Union are the "bank" that joined them; Zanzibar retains its own House of Representatives.

#### Worked Essay Question

#### "Assess the role of TANU and the Zanzibar Revolution in shaping the United Republic of Tanzania." (20 marks)

*Outline:* (i) TANU's constitutional path — petitions to the Trusteeship Council, internal self-government 1958, election victories 1958/1959/1960, dominion status 1961, republic 1962. (ii) The Zanzibar Revolution of 12 January 1964 as the catalyst that made union urgent rather than optional. (iii) Negotiations between Nyerere and Karume culminating in the Articles of Union (22 April 1964). (iv) The compromise: Zanzibar retains internal autonomy (its own House of Representatives and Revolutionary Council), the Union government handles union matters. (v) Evaluation: TANU provided the political vehicle; the Revolution provided the *timing*. Without either, the United Republic in its 1964 form would not exist.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Do not write that TANU *caused* the Zanzibar Revolution. The Revolution was an internal ASP-led uprising. TANU's role was to negotiate the union *after* the Revolution had already changed Zanzibar's politics.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

#### Beyond the Headline Dates

The 1967 **Arusha Declaration** (Ujamaa na Kujitegemea) is post-independence but examiners at law-entry level sometimes fold it into "independence struggle" essays. Treat it as the philosophical capstone: nationalisation of banks, leading-party doctrine under TANU, self-reliance, and adult-literacy drives. The **East African Common Services Organisation (EACSO, 1961)** grew out of the colonial King's African Rifles and customs union; it was the forerunner of the **East African Community (EAC)**, which collapsed in 1977 and was **revived on 7 July 2000** (the date echoes TANU's founding — examiners notice). The 1995 multiparty reforms ended single-party legalised and produced the multiparty constitution still in force.

#### Federation That Never Was

The **Federation of Rhodesia and Nyasaland** (1953–1963) was contemporaneous with Tanganyika's transition. Several proposals (1948 Buganda-style federation, 1953 Groundnut Scheme) flirted with merging East African territories; Nyerere consistently opposed federation as a vehicle for settler dominance. Understanding *why* Tanganyika rejected federation explains why Tanganyika, not Kenya or Uganda, became the natural anchor for a sovereign East African cooperation later.

| Trap | Wrong answer | Right framing |
|---|---|---|
| "Tanganyika was a British colony" | Colony | League of Nations Mandate (1920), then UN Trust Territory (1946) |
| "TANU founded 1929" | 1929 | TAA was 1929; TANU was founded 7 July 1954 |
| "Nyerere was first PM in 1961" | First PM | He was Chief Minister from 1958; became PM at independence; Rashidi Kawawa also briefly held the post |
| "Name Tanzania used at independence" | 1961 | Adopted 1 November 1964, after the union |
| "Zanzibar Revolution led by TANU" | TANU | Internal ASP-led uprising; TANU negotiated the *aftermath* |
| "EAC founded 1961" | EAC | EACSO was 1961; EAC revived 7 July 2000 |

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. *"Mandate versus colony: did the international legal status of Tanganyika accelerate or delay its independence compared with Kenya?"* Compare Trusteeship Council petitions, UN visiting missions (1948, 1951, 1954, 1957, 1959), and the contrasting colonial land-grant politics of Kenya's White Highlands.
2. *"Why did union happen in 1964 and not 1963 or 1965?"* Trace the Zanzibar Revolution's 33-day window, Karume's need for mainland protection against counter-revolution, and Nyerere's long-standing federal proposal. Add the Cold War context: the USS *Beale* incident on Zanzibar in February 1964, and British/American recognition timelines.

Lead with **dates**, follow with **personalities**, close with the **legal status** (Mandate/Trusteeship) — that order matches how markers tick their sheets.
