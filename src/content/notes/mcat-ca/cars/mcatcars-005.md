# Social Sciences Passages — MCAT CARS

Social Sciences passages are one of the two passage-content groups on the MCAT CARS section, the other being humanities. AAMC draws social sciences passages from anthropology, archaeology, economics, education, geography, history, linguistics, political science, population health, psychology, and sociology. Social sciences passages tend to be more factual and scientific in tone, presenting interpretations, implications, or applications of historical accounts, theories, observations, or trends of human society, a specific population group, or a specific country. About half of CARS passage content is Social Sciences.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### What Social Sciences passages look like

- **Psychology:** studies of behaviour, cognition, emotion, social interaction, or development.
- **Sociology:** studies of social structure, institutions, stratification, or culture.
- **Political science:** analyses of government, policy, power, or political behaviour.
- **Economics:** analyses of markets, incentives, trade, or economic systems.
- **Education:** studies of teaching, learning, or educational systems.
- **Anthropology:** analyses of culture, kinship, ritual, or human evolution.

#### What Social Sciences items test

- The author's central claim or hypothesis.
- The evidence offered and the methodology used.
- Strengthen/weaken items based on the author's argument.
- Assumptions the author's reasoning depends on.
- Application of the author's reasoning to new situations.

#### One-pass drill

1. Read the passage in 4 minutes. Identify the discipline and the central claim.
2. Identify the methodology: is the author reporting a study, reviewing literature, or arguing a position?
3. Answer the items. Focus on the author's argument and the evidence.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing the author's claim with the study's findings. The author may interpret the findings in a specific way; the items may ask about the author's interpretation, not the study.
- Picking an answer that uses general knowledge instead of the passage's content.
- Misreading methodological language (e.g., correlation versus causation).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Strategy for psychology and sociology passages

- Identify the study or argument: what is the author reporting or arguing?
- Identify the variables: independent, dependent, control.
- Identify the methodology: experiment, survey, case study, ethnography, archival research.
- For items, focus on the author's interpretation and the limits of the evidence.

The MCAT tests psychology and sociology passages for argument evaluation, methodological reasoning, and application to new situations.

#### Strategy for political science and economics passages

- Identify the author's position on the policy or economic question.
- Identify the evidence and the reasoning.
- For items, focus on the argument's structure and the implications of the author's position.

#### Strategy for education and anthropology passages

- Identify the educational system or cultural practice discussed.
- Identify the author's claim about the practice.
- For items, focus on the author's interpretation and the cultural context.

#### Practice routine

- Take one Social Sciences passage from each discipline. Read each in 4 minutes.
- Identify the methodology and the author's claim.
- Answer the items. Pay attention to argument structure and evidence.
- Time yourself: under 8 minutes per passage.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How Social Sciences passages connect to the rest of the CARS section

- **Humanities passages** share the argument-evaluation skill with political science and economics passages; what separates them is tone, not method. Social sciences writing is more factual and scientific in tone.
- **Studies of Diverse Cultures passages** file under social sciences when the passage reasons about trends, reconstructions, or population-level outcomes.
- **The Psych/Soc MCAT section** shares content with Social Sciences CARS passages, but CARS items test reasoning skills, not content knowledge — nothing in a CARS passage requires you to know the underlying science.

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"CARS Social Sciences passages need content knowledge."** CARS items can be answered from the passage alone. The content is provided; the test is reasoning.
- **"Correlation implies causation."** Social Sciences passages often distinguish correlation from causation. Items test this distinction.
- **"A study's findings are the same as the author's claim."** The author may interpret the findings in a particular way. Items test the interpretation.

#### Exam technique

- Read the passage in 4 minutes. Identify the discipline and the central claim.
- For methodology items, identify the type of study (experiment, survey, case study) before picking the answer.
- For argument items, identify the structure (claim, evidence, assumption) before picking the answer.
- Use the platform's flag-for-review function for items you cannot answer.

#### Specification reference

Social Sciences passages are one of the three CARS passage categories defined by AAMC. The MCAT CARS section draws roughly one-third of its passages from social sciences disciplines.
