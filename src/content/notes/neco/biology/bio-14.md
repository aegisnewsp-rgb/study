# DNA, RNA and Protein Synthesis

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**DNA** is a double-helical polymer of **deoxyribonucleotides** that stores the cell's genetic information. Each nucleotide contains a **deoxyribose** sugar, a **phosphate group**, and one of four bases: **adenine (A)**, **guanine (G)**, **cytosine (C)**, or **thymine (T)**. The two strands run **antiparallel** and pair by complementary base pairing: **A=T (2 H-bonds)**, **G≡C (3 H-bonds)**.

**RNA** is a single-stranded polymer of **ribonucleotides** that uses **uracil (U)** in place of thymine. Three functional types exist: **mRNA** (carries the genetic message), **tRNA** (carries amino acids and has an **anticodon**), and **rRNA** (forms ribosomes with proteins).

**Protein synthesis** has two stages: **transcription** (DNA → mRNA in the nucleus, catalysed by **RNA polymerase**) and **translation** (mRNA → polypeptide at the ribosome). The genetic code is a **triplet code** read from a **start codon (AUG)** to a **stop codon (UAA, UAG, or UGA)**.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Nucleotide Structure
Every nucleic-acid monomer is a **nucleotide** made of three parts: a pentose sugar (**deoxyribose** in DNA, **ribose** in RNA), a **phosphate group** attached to the 5' carbon, and a **nitrogenous base** attached to the 1' carbon. Bases are either **purines** (A, G — double ring) or **pyrimidines** (C, T, U — single ring).

#### DNA Double Helix
DNA has two **antiparallel polynucleotide strands** wound around a common axis. The **sugar-phosphate backbone** sits on the outside; bases project inward and pair through hydrogen bonds. Base pairing is strictly complementary: **A pairs with T (2 H-bonds)** and **G pairs with C (3 H-bonds)**. This explains **Chargaff's rule** (%A = %T; %G = %C). The two strands are oriented 5'→3' and 3'→5', which is critical because nucleic acids are synthesised only in the **5'→3'** direction.

#### The Three Types of RNA

| Type | Function | Distinguishing feature |
|------|----------|------------------------|
| **mRNA** | Carries the gene's message from DNA to ribosome | Linear, short-lived, capped and poly-A tailed |
| **tRNA** | Delivers the correct amino acid during translation | Clover-leaf shape, carries an **anticodon** loop and a 3' CCA amino-acid attachment site |
| **rRNA** | Catalytic and structural component of ribosomes | Combined with proteins to form the large and small ribosomal subunits |

#### Transcription (in the nucleus)
1. **RNA polymerase** binds the **promoter** region of a gene.
2. **DNA helicase** activity unwinds a short stretch of the double helix.
3. One strand, called the **template (antisense) strand**, is read 3'→5'.
4. Free **ribonucleotides** align by complementary base pairing (A→U, T→A, G→C, C→G).
5. RNA polymerase joins them, releasing a single-stranded **mRNA** that grows **5'→3'** and is **identical in base sequence to the coding (sense) strand** (with U replacing T).
6. In eukaryotes, the mRNA is spliced (introns removed) before export to the cytoplasm.

#### The Genetic Code
The code is read three bases at a time. Each **codon** on the mRNA specifies one amino acid (or a stop signal). It is **degenerate** (e.g. both UUU and UUC code for phenylalanine), **universal** (the same in nearly all organisms), and **non-overlapping**. **AUG** is the universal **start codon** (methionine) and **UAA, UAG, UGA** are the three **stop codons**.

#### Translation (at the ribosome)
1. The ribosome binds mRNA and the first **tRNA** charged with methionine at the **P site**.
2. A second tRNA, whose **anticodon** is complementary to the next mRNA codon, enters the **A site** carrying its amino acid.
3. A **peptide bond** forms between the two amino acids; the ribosome translocates one codon along the mRNA.
4. The process repeats until a stop codon enters the A site; **release factors** terminate translation and the **polypeptide** is released.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Directionality and Antiparallel Logic
DNA strands are **antiparallel** because the 5' phosphate of one strand aligns with the 3' hydroxyl of the other. This geometry is what forces the two strands to be synthesised by different mechanisms during DNA replication (leading strand continuous, lagging strand as Okazaki fragments), but in **transcription** only the mRNA strand is produced and it grows strictly **5'→3'** by addition to the free 3'-OH of the last nucleotide. NECO questions frequently test this by asking why transcription does not need Okazaki fragments.

#### Coding vs Template Strand — A Frequent Trap
Students often confuse which strand is "read". The mRNA sequence is **complementary to the template (antisense) strand** and **identical to the coding (sense) strand**, except that **U replaces T**. For example, a DNA coding-strand triplet `TAC` produces an mRNA codon `AUG`, which codes for methionine. If asked "what is the mRNA from the template strand `ATG`?", the answer is `UAC` (tyrosine).

#### Edge Cases and Exceptions
- Some codons do not code for any amino acid — they are **stop codons**, recognised by release factors rather than tRNAs.
- A few mitochondria use slight variants of the code (e.g. UGA codes for tryptophan, not stop) — NECO may mention this as a note on the code being "nearly universal".
- Antibiotics such as **rifampicin** and **streptomycin** exploit bacterial transcription/translation machinery without harming human ribosomes — a useful link to applied biology.

#### Connections to Other Topics
- Links directly to **cell division** (DNA must be replicated before mitosis) and to **gene mutation** (base substitutions, frameshifts from insertions/deletions).
- Connects to **inheritance** through the **one gene–one polypeptide** principle, which underlies Mendelian and molecular genetics.

#### Common Mistakes
- Writing that **RNA contains thymine** or that **DNA contains uracil** — both are wrong.
- Forgetting that **transcription occurs in the nucleus** and **translation in the cytoplasm** (in eukaryotes).
- Stating that tRNA "reads" the mRNA — it is the **ribosome** that reads codons; tRNA delivers the matching amino acid.

#### Worked Example
Given a template DNA strand reading `3'-TAC GGA TTT-5'`, the mRNA produced is `5'-AUG CCU AAA-3'`. Codon by codon: **AUG** = methionine (start), **CCU** = proline, **AAA** = lysine. So the tripeptide is **Met–Pro–Lys**.

#### Practice Prompts
1. A segment of mRNA reads `5'-AUG UGC UAA-3'`. Identify the start codon, the stop codon, and the number of amino acids in the resulting polypeptide.
2. Explain why a substitution of the third base of a codon often does not change the amino acid, and describe the term that describes this property of the genetic code.
