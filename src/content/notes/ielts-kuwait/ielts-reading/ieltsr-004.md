# Identifying Writer's Views/Claims (Yes/No/Not Given) — IELTS Reading Notes

Yes/No/Not Given tests whether each statement agrees with the writer's view or claim (Yes), contradicts the writer's view (No), or whether the writer expresses no view (Not Given). It is different from True/False/Not Given: True/False tests the passage's content; Yes/No tests the writer's opinion.

> Verify the live Reading format on https://ielts.org/ before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Yes:** the statement agrees with the writer's view or claim.
- **No:** the statement contradicts the writer's view or claim.
- **Not Given:** the writer expresses no view on the statement.
- **Common confusion:** confusing this question type with True/False/Not Given.
- **Focus:** what the writer believes, not what the passage says about the world.
- **Order of answers:** questions follow the passage in order.

#### Examiner traps

- Choosing Yes when the statement agrees with the passage's content but the writer has expressed no view.
- Choosing No when the statement contradicts the passage's content but the writer has expressed no view (it should be Not Given).
- Mixing up the question type with True/False/Not Given.

---

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Strategy

1. Read the statement carefully — identify the claim being made about the writer's view.
2. Scan the passage for the writer's view on the claim.
3. Compare the statement to the writer's view:
   - Does it agree? → Yes.
   - Does it contradict? → No.
   - Does the writer say nothing? → Not Given.

#### Distinguishing this from True/False/Not Given

- True/False tests whether a statement about the world agrees with the passage.
- Yes/No tests whether a statement about the writer's view agrees with what the writer believes.

Example:

- Passage: "I believe that climate change is the most pressing issue of our time."
- Statement 1: "Climate change is the most pressing issue of our time." → True (the passage says it).
- Statement 2: "The writer believes that climate change is the most pressing issue of our time." → Yes (the writer says so).
- Statement 3: "Climate change is a serious issue." → True (the passage says it is the most pressing) but Not Given for Yes/No (the writer says it is "the most pressing," not "serious" — these are different views).

---

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where Yes/No/Not Given appears across IELTS Reading

Yes/No/Not Given appears in IELTS Academic Reading, typically in one of the three passages. It is less common than True/False/Not Given but equally important.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Yes/No is the same as True/False." No — they test different things.
- "If the passage is silent, it is No." No — Not Given, even if the statement seems plausible.
- "The writer's view is always in the first paragraph." Not necessarily — it can be anywhere in the passage.

#### Exam technique

- For each statement, locate the writer's view in the passage first.
- Look for evaluative language ("I believe," "in my view," "clearly," "obviously").
- If the writer expresses no view on the statement, choose Not Given.

#### Specification reference

Yes/No/Not Given is one of the official IELTS Academic Reading question types. Source: IELTS Academic Reading test format, https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test/ielts-academic-format-reading. Re-check the live format on ielts.org before committing a revision plan.

#### Practical Pacing and Scan Technique

Allocate at most 90 seconds per statement in this section. Under actual examination timing, candidates frequently lose time reading entire paragraphs when a single qualifying adverbial phrase ("while many argue", "it is plausible that") establishes the writer's deliberate stance. Mark qualifying adverbs immediately during your first scan.
