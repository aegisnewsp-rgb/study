# Legal Aptitude: Intellectual Property Rights

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Intellectual Property Rights (IPR) protect **creations of the mind** — inventions, artistic works, brand identifiers, and confidential business information — granting the creator exclusive exploitation rights for a limited period. LAT tests IPR through fact-pattern passages under Legal Reasoning, not by asking you to recite statutes.

- **Patent**: 20 years from filing, for new, useful, non-obvious inventions.
- **Copyright**: automatic on creation, lasts life of author + 60 years.
- **Trademark**: renewable every 10 years, protects distinctive signs identifying source.
- **Infringement** triggers civil damages, injunctions, and sometimes criminal prosecution.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### What Each Branch Protects

IPR divides into statutory regimes, each guarding a different category of mental creation. Patent law safeguards functional inventions (a new machine, process, or composition of matter) only after substantive examination and grant. Copyright protects the original **expression** of ideas — novels, songs, films, software code — the instant the work is fixed in a tangible medium. Trademark law guards distinctive signs (logos, words, shapes) that identify the commercial source of goods. Geographical Indications shield product names tied to a specific territory whose quality flows from that origin. Trade Secrets cover confidential know-how such as formulas, customer lists, and manufacturing processes.

#### Duration, Subject Matter, and Registration

| Branch | Protectable Subject | Duration | Registration Required? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Patent | New, useful, non-obvious invention | 20 years from filing | Yes, after examination |
| Copyright | Original literary, artistic, musical works | Life of author + 60 years | No — automatic |
| Trademark | Distinctive sign identifying source | 10 years, renewable indefinitely | Yes (registration creates stronger rights) |
| GI | Product with quality tied to geographical origin | 10 years, renewable | Yes |
| Trade Secret | Confidential commercial information | As long as secrecy is maintained | No |

#### Key Application Pointers

- "Fair **dealing**" is the operative phrase in Pakistan's Copyright Ordinance 1962 — narrower than the US "fair use" doctrine and limited to enumerated purposes (private study, criticism, review, news reporting, research).
- The Berne Convention secures automatic copyright across member states without formalities; the Paris Convention covers industrial property.
- TRIPS (1994) sets WTO minimum standards, harmonising protection levels internationally.
- Trade secrets evaporate the moment secrecy is lost — there is no registration safety net.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Examiner Traps

LAT passages often hinge on subtle distinctions candidates routinely blur. A software algorithm alone is **not** patentable — only its concrete technical application is. A recipe idea is unprotected; the written cookbook is copyrighted. Brand colours, shapes, or sounds can be trademarks if acquired distinctiveness is proven. A GI cannot be monopolised merely by adding a place name; the link between geography and quality must be inherent.

The biggest trap is importing US "fair use" language into a Pakistani fact pattern. LAT expects "fair dealing" with its closed list of defences. Another trap: assuming unregistered marks carry no rights. Common-law passing-off actions survive even without IPO Pakistan registration, but the scope is narrower than a registered mark.

#### Common Mistakes Summary

1. Conflating copyright (automatic, expression-based) with patent (registered, functional).
2. Citing Paris Convention when Berne Convention applies (and vice versa).
3. Treating trade secrets as registrable property.
4. Forgetting that bare ideas receive no protection — only their expression or embodiment does.
5. Quoting the wrong duration — life+60 vs 20 vs 10 years.

#### Worked Scenario Walkthrough

> Principle: *"Copyright subsists in original literary works and does not require registration."*
> Fact: Aisha drafts a textbook chapter, emails it to a peer for review, and the peer publishes it commercially.
> Application: Aisha owns copyright the moment the chapter was written and fixed in digital form. Registration is irrelevant. The peer's commercial publication without licence constitutes infringement, exposing him to damages and injunction. Sending the draft does not constitute "fair dealing" because no enumerated purpose (criticism, review, research, news reporting, private study) is served by commercial republication.

#### Practice Prompts

1. A factory uses a recipe identical to a competitor's, claiming the competitor never registered it. Which IPR branch protects the competitor, and what remedy follows if the information was clearly kept confidential?
2. A foreign film is shown on Pakistani cable without a licence. Identify the treaty that protects the film automatically, the branch of IPR violated, and the available remedy under the Copyright Ordinance 1962.
