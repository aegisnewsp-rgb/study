# Communicable Diseases and Epidemiological Determinants

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

- **Communicable disease**: an illness caused by a specific pathogenic agent (bacteria, virus, parasite, fungus) transmissible from a **reservoir** or **source** to a **susceptible host**.
- **Epidemiological triad** = **agent + host + environment**; the determinants of occurrence and distribution.
- **Reservoir** = natural habitat where the agent lives and multiplies (e.g., soil for *Clostridium tetani*); **source** = the immediate origin of infection for a particular host.
- **Spectrum of disease occurrence**: **sporadic** (occasional), **endemic** (constant usual level), **epidemic** (excess over expected), **pandemic** (worldwide epidemic).
- **Herd Immunity Threshold (HIT, %)** = (R0 − 1) / R0 × 100; measles ≈ 93–95%, pertussis ≈ 92–94%, polio ≈ 80–86%.
- **Primary case** introduces infection; **index case** is the first one identified; **secondary cases** arise among contacts.
- **CFR** = (deaths ÷ cases) × 100; **mortality rate** = (deaths ÷ total population) × 1000 — these are not the same.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Agent, Host, Environment
The **epidemiological triad** frames every outbreak. **Agent** factors include *infectivity* (ability to establish infection), *pathogenicity* (ability to cause disease), *virulence* (severity), and *immunogenicity* (ability to induce immunity). **Host** factors are age, sex, genetics, nutrition, immune status (vaccination, prior infection, IgA, cell-mediated immunity), and occupation. **Environment** includes physical (climate, crowding, sanitation), biological (vectors, reservoirs), and social (income, literacy, healthcare access) dimensions.

#### Reservoir, Source, and Carriers
A **reservoir** maintains the agent in nature; a **source** is the person/object from whom the current host acquired the organism. **Carriers** harbour the agent without symptoms: *healthy* (no symptoms ever, e.g., polio), *incubatory* (transmitting before symptoms, e.g., measles, hepatitis A), *convalescent* (recovering but still shedding, e.g., typhoid, diphtheria), and *chronic* (months–years, e.g., typhoid gall-bladder carriage, hepatitis B surface-antigen carriers).

#### Modes of Transmission
- **Direct**: droplet, skin contact, sexual, transplacental, transplacental/perinatal.
- **Indirect**: **fomite** (inanimate object), **vehicle-borne** (water, food, blood), **airborne** (droplet nuclei < 5 µm), **vector-borne** (biological — agent multiplies in vector, e.g., *Anopheles* for malaria; mechanical — passive transfer, e.g., houseflies for diarrhoeal disease).

#### Frequency Measures

| Measure | Numerator | Denominator | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Incidence rate** | New cases | Population at risk (person-time) | Risk over time |
| **Prevalence** (point) | All existing cases at one time | Total population at that time | Disease burden |
| **Attack rate** | New cases in outbreak period | Population at risk at start | Outbreak settings |
| **Secondary attack rate (SAR)** | Cases among contacts in 1 incubation | Total susceptible contacts | Transmission intensity |
| **Case fatality rate (CFR)** | Deaths from disease | Cases of same disease | Severity |

> **Trap**: CFR is per *case*, mortality rate is per *population* — examiners swap these often.

#### Control and Prevention
**Primary** removes the source, interrupts transmission, and protects the susceptible host (immunisation, sanitation, vector control). **Secondary** is early diagnosis, prompt treatment, **isolation** of the ill, and **quarantine** of contacts (40 days historically for plague; current rules are disease-specific). **Tertiary** limits disability and rehabilitates.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### R0, Re, and Herd Immunity
**R0** (basic reproduction number) is the average number of secondary cases generated by one primary case in a **fully susceptible** population. **Re** (effective reproduction number) accounts for the actual susceptible fraction: Re = R0 × (proportion susceptible). When Re < 1, transmission declines. **HIT** is the critical immune proportion needed to push Re below 1, derived as (R0 − 1) / R0. For R0 = 2, HIT = 50%; for R0 = 12 (measles), HIT ≈ 91.7% — explaining why high-coverage MMR campaigns are mandatory.

#### Incubation vs Communicable Period
**Incubation period** = infection to first symptom; **communicable period** = time during which the host can transmit. They often overlap but are not identical: in chickenpox, communicability starts 1–2 days *before* the rash, and in hepatitis B the communicable period can persist for years in chronic carriers. SAR calculations use the incubation period as the observation window.

#### Cold Chain and Vaccines
The **cold chain** preserves vaccine potency from manufacturer to recipient. OPV and measles must be stored at **−20 °C**; DPT, DT, TT, BCG, Hepatitis B, and Hib at **+2 °C to +8 °C** in ILR (ice-lined refrigerator). Breaks in the chain (freeze damage to DPT/TT, thaw damage to OPV) render doses ineffective and must be discarded.

#### Common Pitfalls
- **Index case ≠ primary case**: primary brought the agent in; index is the first *detected*.
- **Reservoir vs source** conflation is the single most-tested distinction in FMGE.
- **Sporadic vs endemic vs epidemic** depend on the *expected baseline*; an isolated case of a notifiable disease is still reportable.
- Indian eliminations: **polio (2014)**, **neonatal tetanus (2015)**; current targets — measles and rubella elimination by 2026, and sustained TB decline.

#### Practice Prompts
1. In a hostel of 200 students, 50 develop food poisoning within 24 hours. Calculate the attack rate. *(Answer: 50/200 × 100 = 25%.)*
2. R0 of varicella is approximately 8. What is the herd immunity threshold? *(Answer: (8 − 1)/8 × 100 ≈ 87.5%.)*
