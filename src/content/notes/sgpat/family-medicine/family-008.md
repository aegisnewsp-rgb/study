# Levels of Prevention Applied in the Saudi

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> One-sentence frame: in Saudi family medicine, prevention is a five-rung ladder from population to patient, and the most-missed rung sits at the very top.

The five levels run **primordial → primary → secondary → tertiary → quaternary**. Each targets a different stage of disease, and the SCFHS blueprint expects residents to slot any intervention into exactly one rung.

- **Primordial** prevents risk factors from appearing in whole populations. Saudi examples: tobacco taxation under Vision 2030, school nutrition standards, walkable-city planning.
- **Primary** reduces incidence in healthy people. Immunisation, smoking-cessation counselling, statins in diabetics.
- **Secondary** finds disease early in the asymptomatic. Saudi MOH screening: breast 40–69, colorectal 45–75, DM/HTN from 18 with risk factors.
- **Tertiary** cuts complications and disability in established disease. Cardiac rehab post-MI, structured diabetic foot clinics.
- **Quaternary** shields patients from over-medicalisation, over-diagnosis, and iatrogenic harm. The rung most candidates omit.

| Level | Target | Saudi GP example |
|---|---|---|
| Primordial | Risk factors not yet born | Salt/sugar policy, anti-tobacco taxation |
| Primary | Healthy individuals | Childhood immunisation, HPV catch-up |
| Secondary | Asymptomatic disease | Mammography, HbA1c, FIT |
| Tertiary | Established disease | Post-MI rehab, diabetic foot care |
| Quaternary | Excessive medicine | Avoiding low-value imaging in low back pain |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook (Memory Hook — Five P's):** **P**opulation policy, **P**revent disease onset, **P**ick it up early, **P**revent complications, **P**rotect from over-treatment. The first **P** belongs to a *population*; the last **P** belongs to a *patient already in your clinic*.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> The five-level model is Leavell and Clark extended; the exam regularly traps candidates on whether an action is primordial or primary, and on whether quaternary even exists.

#### Where the framework comes from

Leavell and Clark's 1950s model named three stages (primary, secondary, tertiary). Modern public health — and the SCFHS Family Medicine curriculum — added **primordial** upstream of primary, and **quaternary** downstream of tertiary, because two real clinical problems were missing from the original triad: populations where risk factors had not yet emerged, and patients being harmed by the system meant to help them.

#### How the five levels differ

The discriminator is **what the action is acting on**: the population, the risk factor, the asymptomatic disease, the established disease, or the medical encounter itself.

- **Primordial** acts on the *determinants* of risk: pricing, regulation, environment, education. Vision 2030's Quality-of-Life Program and the Health Sector Transformation Program sit here.
- **Primary** acts on *known* risk factors or boosts resistance: vaccines, chemoprophylaxis, behaviour counselling.
- **Secondary** acts on *occult* disease via screening tests. Wilson and Jungner's criteria (1968) and the Saudi MOH/PSSD screening lists are the reference.
- **Tertiary** acts on *manifest* disease to limit impairment, disability and recurrence — the bread and butter of chronic-disease clinics.
- **Quaternary** acts on the *clinical encounter* itself, protecting the patient from unnecessary tests, drugs, and labels.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix

| Feature | Primordial | Primary | Secondary | Tertiary | Quaternary |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Subject | Whole population | Healthy individuals | Asymptomatic at-risk | Patients with disease | Patients at risk of over-medicalisation |
| Aim | Prevent emergence of risk factors | Prevent onset of disease | Early detection | Reduce complications, recurrence | Avoid iatrogenic harm |
| Tool | Policy, taxation, health promotion | Vaccines, counselling, chemoprophylaxis | Screening (Mammography, FIT, HbA1c) | Rehab, disease management, foot care | Rational test ordering, shared decision-making |
| Saudi MOH example | Tobacco tax, school nutrition | Childhood + HPV immunisation | Breast 40–69, CRC 45–75 | Cardiac rehab post-MI | Avoiding routine LBP imaging <6 weeks |
| Risk of confusion | Often mislabelled "primary" | Confused with primordial | Confused with primary | Confused with secondary | Frequently omitted entirely |

#### Worked Exam Scenario

A 35-year-old non-smoking man with a BMI of 27 and a strong family history of type 2 diabetes attends a Well-Man visit. His fasting glucose is 5.9 mmol/L and blood pressure 132/84. The GP discusses a Mediterranean diet, prescribes structured physical-activity advice, and defers metformin because the glycaemic threshold is not yet met. He documents BMI, blood pressure and glucose for re-check in 12 months.

- The **diet and activity counselling** is **primary** prevention (intervening on a known risk factor before disease onset).
- The **planned re-screening** is **secondary** prevention (early detection of yet-undiagnosed dysglycaemia or hypertension).
- The **deferral of metformin** is **quaternary** prevention — withholding a drug whose benefit does not yet exceed its potential harm.
- The fact that he is a *non-smoker in a smoke-free workplace* sits within **primordial** protection maintained by national anti-tobacco policy.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** candidates who read "lifestyle counselling" instantly tick "primary" and stop. The vignette stacks three interventions at three different levels; the SCFHS blueprint rewards the answer that names **all three** plus the screening plan. Also, do not file the deferred metformin under "doing nothing" — deferring a non-indicated drug is a *positive* quaternary act.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Two situations trip senior residents more than any other: a population-wide policy misfiled as primary, and a treatment decision that should be quaternary filed as tertiary.

#### Edge cases and boundary conditions

- **Population strategy vs high-risk strategy.** Rose's distinction (1981) maps onto the framework cleanly: the population strategy is **primordial/primary** (shift the whole distribution), the high-risk strategy is **primary/secondary** (treat the tail). Saudi Vision 2030 Quality-of-Life indicators (physical-activity prevalence, dietary salt, tobacco exposure) are population-strategy metrics.
- **Vaccination straddles two rungs.** Childhood immunisation is **primary** (preventing onset). Travel or post-exposure rabies prophylaxis is still **primary** by mechanism, even though the exposure has happened. HPV catch-up at age 26 is **primary**; cervical cytology at 25–49 is **secondary**.
- **Statins in diabetes.** Initiating a statin in a patient with established type 2 diabetes is **tertiary** (preventing a *complication* of existing disease) in many guidelines, but **primary** in others when framed as preventing a first cardiovascular event in a high-risk person. The exam typically expects **tertiary** because disease is established — read the stem, not your memory.
- **Quaternary is not "no treatment".** It is an active clinical act: choosing watchful waiting for mild otitis media, avoiding PSA screening in men over 70 with limited life expectancy, declining whole-body CT for an asymptomatic patient, or stopping a long-term PPI without indication.
- **Screening rules.** Wilson and Jungner: condition is an important health problem, accepted treatment exists, facilities available, recognisable latent phase, suitable test, acceptable to the population, natural history understood, agreed policy on whom to treat, cost balanced against outcomes, continuous process. Saudi MOH adds: register-based call-recall, KPI-linked payment, PHC performance dashboards.
- **Health promotion is a tool, not a level.** Health promotion is the principal *instrument* of primordial prevention; it can also support primary and secondary goals. The exam answer that conflates "health promotion" with a prevention level is wrong.

#### Common Mistakes and Saudi-Specific Errors

1. Filing the Saudi soft-drinks sugar tax, the school nutrition standards, and the smoke-free-city regulations as **primary** — they are **primordial** because the risk factors themselves have not yet appeared in the population.
2. Omitting **quaternary** when the stem mentions over-investigation, polypharmacy review, or de-prescribing.
3. Calling any asymptomatic check-up "screening" without applying the Wilson-Jungner criteria — opportunistic BP measurement in a feverish child is not a screening programme.
4. Treating **tertiary prevention as curative** — tertiary reduces *impairment and recurrence*, it does not cure the underlying disease.
5. Missing the **Saudi MOH screening ages** (breast 40–69, colorectal 45–75, cervical 25–49, DM/HTN from 18 with risk factors) and the **expanded immunisation schedule** (childhood EPI plus seasonal influenza, COVID-19, HPV catch-up).
6. Confusing **population strategy KPIs** (tobacco prevalence, salt intake) with **high-risk KPIs** (HbA1c <7% in known diabetics) when interpreting PHC dashboards.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. A 52-year-old woman with well-controlled type 2 diabetes, BMI 31, and stable microalbuminuria asks about "full-body screening" advertised privately. Construct a quaternary-prevention response that acknowledges her concern, refuses low-value imaging, and redirects to her evidence-based MOH screening schedule.
2. A PHC medical director asks you to draft a one-page teaching sheet for residents on why the Saudi sugar tax, the school nutrition standards, and the smoke-free public-space policy are **primordial** rather than primary. Produce the rationale in three short paragraphs, with one Saudi data reference per paragraph (refer to the current MOH Health Sector Transformation Program indicators without quoting invented numbers).

> 📌 **Exam Strategy (Saudi GP Board, Family Medicine, 3% weight):** the topic is a high-yield *discriminator* rather than a high-volume one. Most candidates handle primary, secondary, and tertiary. The score jumps when you correctly identify **primordial** population-policy items and **quaternary** over-medicalisation vignettes. In MCQs, scan the stem for three signals: *who is the subject* (population vs individual), *is disease present* (no/yes/asymptomatic), and *is the action a test, a treatment, or a policy*. The combination fixes the level.
