# Protein Chemistry

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Proteins are **polymers of L-α-amino acids** joined by **peptide (amide) bonds** formed by condensation between the α-carboxyl of one residue and the α-amino of the next, releasing H₂O. They exhibit four structural levels: **primary** (linear amino acid sequence held by covalent peptide bonds), **secondary** (α-helix with 3.6 residues/turn or β-pleated sheet, stabilised by backbone H-bonds), **tertiary** (overall 3D fold driven by R-group interactions — hydrophobic, H-bonds, ionic, disulfide, van der Waals), and **quaternary** (assembly of subunits, e.g. haemoglobin tetramer). Average residue mass ≈ **110 Da**. Peptide bonds are **planar, trans, and non-rotatable** due to partial double-bond character. **Denaturation** disrupts 3°/2° structure without cleaving peptide bonds. **High-yield**: Biuret detects peptide bonds; Ninhydrin (Ruhemann's purple) detects free α-amino groups.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Amino Acid Backbone
An **L-α-amino acid** has the general formula H₂N–CHR–COOH. At physiological pH it exists as a **zwitterion** (⁺H₃N–CHR–COO⁻). The **isoelectric point (pI)** is the pH at which net charge = 0; for a neutral amino acid, **pI = (pKa₁ + pKa₂)/2**.

#### Classification of Amino Acids
Grouped by side-chain (R) character: **non-polar** (Ala, Val, Leu, Ile, Pro, Phe, Trp, Met), **polar uncharged** (Ser, Thr, Cys, Tyr, Asn, Gln), **positively charged** (Lys, Arg, His), and **negatively charged** (Asp, Glu). **Nutritionally, essential amino acids** (His, Ile, Leu, Lys, Met, Phe, Thr, Trp, Val) must come from diet; the rest are synthesised endogenously.

#### Peptide Bond
Formed by **condensation (amidation)** with loss of water. The C–N bond has **~40% double-bond character** (resonance between C=O and C=N), forcing **planarity** and **trans geometry**. Rotation is allowed only at the α-carbon (φ, ψ angles), visualised by the **Ramachandran plot**; allowed regions correspond to α-helix and β-sheet conformations.

#### Levels of Protein Structure
- **Primary**: amino acid sequence; read from **N-terminus to C-terminus**. Determines all higher folding (Anfinsen's ribonuclease refolding experiment).
- **Secondary**: local H-bonded patterns — **α-helix** (right-handed, 3.6 residues/turn, 1.5 Å rise/residue, H-bond between residue *i* C=O and *i+4* N–H) and **β-pleated sheet** (parallel or antiparallel strands).
- **Tertiary**: 3D fold stabilised by **hydrophobic core, H-bonds, salt bridges, disulfide (–S–S–) bonds, van der Waals forces**.
- **Quaternary**: association of folded subunits — classical example is **HbA (α₂β₂)** showing **cooperative O₂ binding** (sigmoidal curve) and the **Bohr effect** (H⁺ and CO₂ reduce O₂ affinity).

#### Denaturation
Loss of tertiary/secondary organisation (no peptide bond hydrolysis). Causes: heat, urea, guanidinium Cl⁻, extremes of pH, detergents. Usually **irreversible** (cooked egg white); some cases are **reversible** (ribonuclease).

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Fibrous vs Globular Proteins
**Fibrous** (collagen, keratin, elastin) are long, insoluble, structural — collagen is a triple helix of Gly-X-Y repeats (Pro, Hyp) with hydroxyproline essential for stability. **Globular** (Hb, myoglobin, enzymes) are compact, water-soluble, with hydrophobic cores.

#### Protein Folding & Disease
**Anfinsen's dogma**: primary sequence dictates native conformation. Misfolding produces **amyloidoses** — e.g. amyloid-β in Alzheimer disease, prion PrPˢᶜ in Creutzfeldt–Jakob disease, **sickle-cell HbS** (Glu6Val) from a single nucleotide substitution producing hydrophobic patches and polymerisation.

#### Protein Estimation
**Beer–Lambert**: A = ε·c·l. Standard assays include **Biuret, Lowry, BCA, Bradford** — each exploits peptide bonds, Tyr/Cys residues, or dye-binding to aromatic residues, with differing interference profiles.

#### Amino Acid Catabolism Connections
The α-amino group is funnelled via **transamination** (PLP-/B₆-dependent, AST/ALT) to glutamate, then **oxidative deamination** (glutamate dehydrogenase) releasing NH₃, which enters the **urea (Krebs–Henseleit) cycle** as carbamoyl phosphate. Carbon skeletons feed gluconeogenesis (glucogenic) or ketogenesis (ketogenic — Leu, Lys).

#### Plasma Proteins
Electrophoresis separates **albumin** (~60%, maintains colloidal osmotic pressure and transports bilirubin/free fatty acids/drugs), **α₁-, α₂-, β-globulins** (transport, complement, lipoproteins), **γ-globulins** (immunoglobulins), and **fibrinogen** (clotting). The **A/G ratio** falls in liver disease and rises with dehydration.

#### Common Mistakes
Confusing **denaturation** (non-covalent disruption, reversible sometimes) with **hydrolysis** (peptide bond cleavage). Treating all amino acids as zwitterions in strongly acidic/alkaline solutions. Forgetting that proline introduces a **kink** disrupting α-helices and gives a **yellow** ninhydrin product.

#### Practice Prompts
1. A solution treated with Biuret reagent turns violet but with Ninhydrin gives no Ruhemann's purple. Deduce the nature of the sample.
2. A tetrameric protein loses cooperative O₂ binding when dissociated into dimers — what level of structure does this implicate, and which force normally couples the subunits?
