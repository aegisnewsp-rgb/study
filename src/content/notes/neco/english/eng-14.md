# Essay and Paragraph Writing

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

NECO SSCE Paper II (Essay) tests continuous writing of **450–500 words** on a single topic drawn from everyday life, civic life, or national issues. The required form is the **expository or argumentative essay**, organised in **three parts: introduction, body, and conclusion**. The **introduction** carries a **thesis statement** — one sentence announcing the writer's main argument or position. The **body** consists of two to four paragraphs, each opening with a **topic sentence** that states one sub-idea, followed by **supporting sentences** (facts, examples, definitions, or reasons) and a **concluding/linking sentence**. The **conclusion** restates the thesis in fresh words and summarises the main points. Unity, coherence, and register are the three marks awarded for organisation: keep every sentence tied to the topic sentence, link ideas with **transition words** (however, therefore, furthermore, moreover, in addition, on the other hand), and maintain **formal English** — no slang, no contractions like "can't", no abbreviations.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Structure of the NECO Essay

A NECO essay follows a fixed **three-block structure**. The **introduction** (about 80–100 words) opens broadly, narrows to the topic, and ends with the **thesis statement** — a single sentence that states exactly what the essay will argue or explain. The **body** (about 280–340 words, split into 2–4 paragraphs) develops the thesis. Each body paragraph must obey the **unity rule**: every sentence must relate directly to its **topic sentence**, which is usually the first sentence of the paragraph. After the topic sentence, 2–4 **supporting sentences** provide evidence, examples, statistics, definitions, or reasons. A short **concluding/linking sentence** wraps the paragraph and bridges to the next. The **conclusion** (about 70–90 words) restates the thesis without copying it word-for-word and ends with a final thought, recommendation, or prediction.

#### Methods of Paragraph Development

| Method | When to use | Example trigger words |
|---|---|---|
| **Chronological order** | Narrating events, processes | first, then, finally |
| **Cause-and-effect** | Explaining why something happens | because, as a result, therefore |
| **Comparison-and-contrast** | Showing similarities/differences | similarly, however, on the other hand |
| **Problem-and-solution** | Proposing fixes | the problem is, one solution |
| **Exemplification** | Illustrating an idea with cases | for example, for instance |
| **Definition/Classification** | Clarifying terms or categories | is defined as, can be grouped into |

#### Pre-Writing: The Outline

Before drafting, spend 5–7 minutes on a **brief outline** — write the thesis, then for each body paragraph note the topic sentence plus two or three supporting points. Outlining prevents repetition, off-topic drift, and the common trap of running out of ideas halfway through. NECO examiners reward candidates whose essays show clear planning in the final product (logical paragraph breaks, no repetition, balanced coverage of all parts of the question).

#### Register and Tone

NECO essays must be written in **formal English**. Avoid contractions (*don't, can't, won't*), slang, texting abbreviations (*u, 2day, pls*), first-person over-use, and rhetorical questions in formal arguments. Maintain **audience awareness** — picture a literate adult reader and write to inform or persuade that reader.

#### Common Essay Prompts

Typical NECO titles include "The Role of the Youth in Nation Building", "The Causes and Effects of Examination Malpractice", "How to Solve the Problem of Insecurity in Nigeria", "My Ambition in Life", and "If I Were the President of Nigeria". Note that letter- or article-format questions may appear and require the same paragraph logic inside the appropriate format.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Marking Criteria and How Marks Are Earned

NECO Paper II is marked out of **50**, distributed roughly as: **Content/Task Fulfilment (10)**, **Organisation/Coherence (10)**, **Mechanical Accuracy — grammar, spelling, punctuation (15)**, **Expression/Vocabulary (10)**, and **Style/Register (5)**. A candidate who writes a well-organised but grammatically weak essay usually scores in the low 20s; a fluent essay with poor paragraphing caps around 30. The lesson: paragraph structure alone does not pass the paper, but ignoring it will block the upper bands.

#### Edge Cases and Examiner Pet Peeves

1. **One-block essays** — writing the entire 500 words as a single paragraph collapses the organisation mark. Always indent and use at least four paragraphs.
2. **Narrative essays** — NECO favours expository/argumentative writing; pure storytelling on a reflective topic (*"What I did during the holidays"*) often scores low because the rubric rewards analysis.
3. **Quoting the question verbatim** as the opening sentence wastes the introduction and shows no thesis awareness.
4. **Repeating the same example** across paragraphs suggests the candidate ran out of ideas — outlining solves this.
5. **Mixing formal and informal registers** mid-essay (e.g., beginning formally then switching to "guys, let us look at this") breaks audience awareness and costs the register marks.

#### Worked Micro-Example

Title: *"The Causes and Effects of Examination Malpractice in Nigeria."*
- **Thesis:** Examination malpractice in Nigeria is fuelled by pressure, weak supervision, and a results-driven culture, and it corrodes the credibility of certificates.
- **Body P1 topic sentence:** *The first major cause is the intense pressure placed on students by parents and schools.* Support: comparisons with peers, fear of failure, league tables.
- **Body P2 topic sentence:** *Another cause is the poor supervision and porous arrangement in many centres.* Support: leaked question papers, external helpers, compromised invigilators.
- **Body P3 topic sentence:** *The effects are equally damaging.* Support: devalued certificates, unemployable graduates, legal consequences.
- **Conclusion:** Restate thesis, recommend a national shift in assessment culture, end with a forward-looking sentence.

#### Practice Prompts

1. *"If I were to be the President of Nigeria for one day, the three problems I would solve are…"* (problem-and-solution + cause-and-effect).
2. *"Discuss the role of the young generation in combating corruption in Nigeria."* (argumentative, with exemplification and comparison-and-contrast).

#### Exam Strategy

- Spend **5 minutes planning** (outline + thesis), **35 minutes writing**, **5 minutes proofreading** for subject-verb agreement, spelling, and paragraph indentation.
- Aim for **4–5 paragraphs** in a 450–500-word essay; the standard ratio is one short intro, three body paragraphs, one short conclusion.
- Underline your thesis statement in your rough work; if you cannot locate it, the structure is weak.
