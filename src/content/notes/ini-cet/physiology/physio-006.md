# Respiratory Mechanics and Gas Exchange

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Respiratory mechanics describes the pressure–volume–flow relationships that drive ventilation, while gas exchange covers diffusion-driven transfer of O₂ and CO₂ across the alveolar–capillary membrane. INI CET (AIIMS PG) Physiology items (≈3% weightage) often test compliance, airway resistance, the alveolar gas equation, V/Q matching, and the O₂–Hb curve.

- **Compliance C = ΔV / ΔP** (L/cmH₂O); reduced in pulmonary fibrosis, raised in emphysema.
- **Alveolar gas equation:** PAO₂ = PiO₂ − (PaCO₂ / R); R ≈ 0.8 at rest.
- **Fick's law:** V̇gas = (A × D × ΔP) / T — diffusion falls as membrane thickness rises (pulmonary oedema, fibrosis).
- Surfactant lowers surface tension inversely with alveolar radius, stabilising small alveoli.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Mechanical properties of the lung

The lungs behave as a viscoelastic structure opposing inflation. **Transpulmonary pressure** (alveolar − intrapleural) is the distending force, and **compliance** is its slope on the pressure–volume curve. Surfactant, secreted by type II pneumocytes, lowers surface tension and reduces the work needed to inflate small alveoli; deficiency causes neonatal respiratory distress syndrome. **Airway resistance (Raw)** follows Poiseuille's law (∝ 1/r⁴), making bronchoconstriction in asthma disproportionately raise Raw.

| Parameter | Symbol / Unit | Physiological value | Clinical change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Compliance | C = ΔV/ΔP (L/cmH₂O) | ~0.2 | ↓ fibrosis, ↑ emphysema |
| Airway resistance | Raw = ΔP/Flow (cmH₂O·L⁻¹·s) | 1–2 | ↑ asthma, COPD |
| Alveolar ventilation | VA = (TV − VD) × f (L/min) | ~4 | ↓ if VD rises |
| Laplace alveolar pressure | P = 2T/r | — | Surfactant stabilises |

#### Ventilation–perfusion matching

Normal **V/Q ≈ 0.8**. V/Q = 0 indicates shunt (atelectasis, ARDS), while V/Q = ∞ marks dead space (pulmonary embolism). V/Q mismatch is the most frequent cause of hypoxaemia and is partially corrected by hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction.

#### Gas transport and acid–base link

O₂ content = (1.34 × Hb × SaO₂) + (0.003 × PaO₂) mL/dL. The **Bohr effect** (right-shift of the O₂–Hb curve when pH falls or PCO₂ rises) enhances tissue unloading, while the **Haldane effect** promotes CO₂ release as Hb oxygenates in the lungs. An INI CET favourite is integrating these with renal compensation of respiratory alkalosis at high altitude.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Worked derivation — alveolar PAO₂

At sea level, PiO₂ = FiO₂ × (Patm − PH₂O) = 0.21 × (760 − 47) ≈ 150 mmHg. With PaCO₂ = 40 mmHg and R = 0.8, **PAO₂ ≈ 150 − (40/0.8) = 100 mmHg**, leaving a normal A–a gradient of ≈10 mmHg. At altitude Patm falls, reducing PiO₂; the body responds via carotid-body–driven hyperventilation, dropping PaCO₂ and producing a primary respiratory alkalosis compensated over days by renal bicarbonate excretion.

#### Edge cases and exam traps

1. **Shunt is unresponsive to O₂ therapy**; only raising FiO₂ to 1.0 may blunt hypoxaemia partially.
2. **Chronic hypercapnia** in COPD shifts the CO₂ response curve; relying on PaCO₂ alone misreads acid–base status.
3. **Fetal Hb (HbF)** binds 2,3-BPG poorly, giving P50 ≈ 19 mmHg versus adult 27 mmHg — relevant for neonatal oxygenation.
4. **Specific conductance (sGaw = Raw/Thoracic gas volume)** corrects Raw for lung size — quoting Raw without units or volume correction is a common MCQ distractor.

#### Common mistakes

- Forgetting anatomical dead space (~150 mL) in alveolar ventilation calculations.
- Equating ↑PaCO₂ with acidosis without checking HCO₃⁻ compensation.
- Ignoring the **0.003 × PaO₂** dissolved-O₂ term in oxygen content.

#### Practice prompts

1. A 55-year-old with COPD: Raw 6, PaCO₂ 55, pH 7.34, HCO₃⁻ 31 — classify the acid–base status and explain chronic compensation.
2. Calculate PAO₂ at Patm 520 mmHg, FiO₂ 0.21, PaCO₂ 30 mmHg; predict the shift in the O₂–Hb curve and renal compensation.
