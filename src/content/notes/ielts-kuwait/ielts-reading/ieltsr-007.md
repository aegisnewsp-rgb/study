# Sentence, Summary, Note, Table, Flow-Chart and Diagram Completion — IELTS Reading Notes

The completion family of IELTS Academic Reading question types includes six formats: sentence completion, summary completion, note completion, table completion, flow-chart completion, and diagram label completion. Each format has a structured text with gaps and a strict word limit. The answers come from the passage.

> Verify the live Reading format on https://ielts.org/ before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Sentence completion:** complete sentences with words from the passage.
- **Summary completion:** fill gaps in a summary paragraph.
- **Note completion:** fill gaps in informal notes.
- **Table completion:** fill cells of a table.
- **Flow-chart completion:** fill gaps in a process flow.
- **Diagram label completion:** label items on a diagram.
- **Word limits:** strict — "NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS" means exactly that.
- **Order of answers:** questions follow the passage in order.

#### Examiner traps

- Writing more words than the limit.
- Paraphrasing the answer instead of using the passage's exact words.
- Spelling errors on names or technical terms.

---

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Strategy

1. Read the instruction carefully — note the word limit.
2. Skim the text to understand its structure.
3. Scan the passage for the keyword in each gap.
4. Read the surrounding sentence carefully.
5. Write the exact words from the passage (within the word limit).

#### Word limit discipline

The instruction "NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS" means exactly that — one or two words, never three. The instruction "NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER" allows two words plus a number (e.g. "approximately 15 minutes"). Read the instruction before answering.

---

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where the completion family appears across IELTS Reading

The completion family appears in IELTS Academic Reading, often with 4-6 questions per passage. It is a high-frequency question type and a strong band discriminator.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Paraphrasing is fine." No — write the exact words from the passage (within the word limit).
- "Numbers can be written as words." Sometimes — follow the question's format. If the box requires digits, write digits.
- "Abbreviations are acceptable." Sometimes — follow the question's instruction. If the instruction says "NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS," an abbreviation counts as one word.

#### Exam technique

- For sentence completion, identify the keyword in the gap and scan the passage for it.
- For summary completion, the answer is the next word the passage uses after the trigger phrase.
- For table completion, follow the table's order — the passage matches the table row by row.
