# Topic 2

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Topic 2** of the TNPSC Group 1 History paper covers the History and Cultural Heritage of Telangana, from the Satavahana–Ikshvaku period through the Kakatiyas, the Bahmani and Qutb Shahi sultanates, and the Asaf Jahi Nizams, up to the formation of Telangana state in 2014.

- **Dynastic anchor dates:** Kakatiyas 1163–1326, Qutb Shahis 1518–1687, Asaf Jahis 1724–1948.
- **Two peaks to memorise:** the **Telangana Armed Struggle (1946–1951)** led by P. Sundarayya and Ravi Narayana Reddy against the Nizam, and the **Telangana statehood movement (2009–2014)** under K. Chandrashekar Rao.
- **Common 2–3 mark items:** founding of Hyderabad (Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah, 1591), State Reorganisation Act **1956**, Gentlemen's Agreement violations, and Jai Andhra (1972).
- **Architecture trio:** Golconda Fort, Warangal Fort, Thousand Pillar Temple.

> **Exam tip:** Tie every answer to a date and a place — Telangana questions are graded on factual precision, not narrative flair.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Political Evolution of Telangana

The region's early political geography was shaped along the Godavari and Krishna river valleys. The **Satavahanas** (c. 2nd century BCE – 2nd century CE) gave Telangana its first pan-Deccan state with capitals at Amaravati and later Paithan. Successors — the **Ikshvakus** at Nagarjunakonda, **Vishnukundins** (5th–6th century), **Eastern Chalukyas** of Vengi, and **Rashtrakutas** of Manyakheta — maintained a continuous cultural corridor linking Telugu-speaking lands.

#### Kakatiya Period

The Kakatiya dynasty (1163–1326), founded by **Prola II**, peaked under Ganapati Deva, Rudrama Devi, and Prataparudra II. The Kakatiyas consolidated the **Samsthana** system, fortified Warangal, and patronised Kakatiya Kala, a fusion of Dravidian and early Deccan styles. Defeat by the Delhi Sultanate in 1323 ended the dynasty but not its administrative legacy, which the Vijayanagara and later sultanates absorbed.

#### Deccan Sultanates and Qutb Shahis

After the Bahmani Sultanate fragmented (early 16th century), the **Qutb Shahi dynasty (1518–1687)** established the Golconda Sultanate. Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah founded **Hyderabad in 1591**. The Qutb Shahis patronised Urdu, Telugu, and Deccani art — Charminar and Mecca Masjid date from this period. Mughal emperor Aurangzeb annexed Golconda in 1687.

#### Asaf Jahi Rule and Anti-Feudal Stirrings

Mir Qamar-ud-din Khan became **Nizam-ul-Mulk Asaf Jah I in 1724**, founding the Asaf Jahi dynasty that ruled Hyderabad till 1948. The Nizams' **Jagir** and **Vetti** systems produced sharp feudal pressures. Peasant revolts at Palnadu, Peddapuram, and Jagityala preceded the mass mobilisation of the **Telangana Armed Struggle (1946–1951)** led by communists against the Nizam and Razakars.

| Aspect | Armed Struggle (1946–51) | Statehood Movement (2009–14) |
|---|---|---|
| Leadership | P. Sundarayya, Ravi Narayana Reddy | K. Chandrashekar Rao (TRS) |
| Goal | Land reform, end Nizam's feudalism | Separate Telangana state |
| Outcome | Annexation by India (Operation Polo, 1948) | AP Reorganisation Act, 2014 |

> **Trap to avoid:** Confusing **Operation Polo** (1948, military annexation) with the **States Reorganisation Act (1956)**.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Mechanisms Behind the 1956 Backstory

The 1956 **Gentlemen's Agreement** between Telangana and Andhra leaders promised **Mulki rules** — local domicile for government jobs and education — to safeguard Telangana's economy. Systematic dilution of these Mulki protections from the 1960s created regional resentment. The **1969 Joint Action Committee** under Marri Chenna Reddy articulated a separate-Telangana demand for the first time in independent India, triggering **Jai Andhra (1972)** as a counter-movement.

#### Architectural and Cultural Heritage

Telangana's **composite culture** is best read in monuments: Warangal's Kakatiya Fort and Thousand Pillar Temple (Rudrama Devi), Golconda Fort's acoustic engineering, the Qutb Shahi Tombs, and Osman Sagar reservoirs. Telugu's evolution from a Chalukyan-era literary register into a modern state language was formalised by the **1956 linguistic reorganisation**.

#### Worked Synthesis — Framing a 10-Mark Question

When asked *"Analyse the Telangana Armed Struggle as a precursor to the statehood demand,"* structure the answer as: (i) feudal context — Jagir/Vetti; (ii) communist-led peasant mobilisation 1946–51; (iii) failures of post-1956 Mulki rules to address rural distress; (iv) linkage to 1969 and 2009–14 movements. End with the **AP Reorganisation Act, 2014**.

#### Common Mistakes Table

| Mistake | Correction |
|---|---|
| Treating 1946–51 and 2009–14 as one movement | Distinct phases — armed anti-feudal vs. democratic statehood |
| Crediting Hyderabad's founding to Asaf Jah I | Hyderabad was founded by Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah in 1591 |
| Ignoring tribal revolts (Bhumia, Fituri, Ramdhas) | These shaped the anti-feudal climate |
| Omitting the Gentlemen's Agreement | The 1956 Mulki framework is the structural bridge between both movements |

#### Practice Prompts

1. **2 marks:** Name the founder of the Qutb Shahi dynasty and the city he established in 1591.
2. **10 marks:** Critically assess how the Telangana Armed Struggle (1946–1951) laid the political and ideological groundwork for the 2009–2014 statehood movement.
