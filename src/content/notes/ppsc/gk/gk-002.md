# Sindh Province and Its Administration

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Sindh** is the southeastern province of Pakistan, with **Karachi** as its capital and largest city. It is listed in the **First Schedule of the Constitution of Pakistan, 1973**, making the Provincial Assembly of Sindh a permanent constitutional body. Executive authority flows from the **Governor** (ceremonial head, Articles 129 and 137), exercised in practice through the **Chief Minister** (executive head, Article 130) and the **Chief Secretary** (senior-most bureaucrat heading the Sindh Secretariat).

Administratively, Sindh is divided into **divisions**, each headed by a **Divisional Commissioner**, subdivided into **districts** led by **Deputy Commissioners (DCs)**, then **talukas** (Assistant Commissioners and Mukhtiarkars), down to **Union Councils**. The **Board of Revenue Sindh** manages land revenue, while the **Sindh Public Service Commission (SPSC)** runs provincial competitive exams.

#### Must-Know Facts
- Capital: **Karachi** (Hyderabad is the historical capital of pre-1936 Sindh).
- Constitutional basis: **Article 1, 116, 129, 130, 137** of the 1973 Constitution.
- Civil service: officers from the **Pakistan Administrative Service (PAS/DMG)** and **Provincial Management Service (PMS)** staff key posts.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Constitutional Framework
Sindh's administration rests on the **1973 Constitution**, which devolves powers through the **18th Amendment (2010)**. Under **Article 116**, the Provincial Assembly of Sindh may make laws on the subjects in the Provincial Legislative List. Bills passed are sent to the **Governor**, who grants assent in the name of the Province. The **Provincial Cabinet**, headed by the **Chief Minister**, aids and advises the Governor (Article 137).

The **Governor** is appointed by the President and acts as the ceremonial head; real executive authority lies with the **Chief Minister** and the cabinet. The **Chief Secretary**, a senior PAS officer, coordinates all provincial departments through the **Sindh Secretariat**, which houses administrative departments — Home, Finance, Education, Health, S&GAD (Services & General Administration), Revenue, and Law.

#### Administrative Hierarchy
The field administration mirrors the Secretariat chain. Sindh has **seven administrative divisions** (Karachi, Hyderabad, Sukkur, Larkana, Mirpurkhas, Shaheed Benazirabad, and Malir). Each division is supervised by a **Divisional Commissioner**, a federalised DMG officer. Districts under each division are run by **Deputy Commissioners**, who also act as District Magistrates.

#### Local Government
The **Sindh Local Government Act, 2013** (as amended) establishes a three-tier system: City/District Governments, Municipal Committees, and **Union Councils** at the grassroots. Revenue matters are handled separately by the **Board of Revenue** under the Sindh Revenue Act and the Land Revenue Code.

| Tier | Head | Key Function |
|---|---|---|
| Province | Governor / Chief Minister | Executive and legislative authority |
| Secretariat | Chief Secretary | Policy coordination among departments |
| Division | Commissioner | Supervises districts within the division |
| District | Deputy Commissioner | Revenue, magistracy, law & order |
| Taluka | Mukhtiarkar / AC | Land records and local revenue |
| Union Council | Nazim / Secretary | Grassroots civic services |

**Trap:** candidates often call the Governor the "head of government" — he is the constitutional/ceremonial head; the **Chief Minister** is the head of government.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Articles to Memorise
PPSC papers repeatedly test the exact constitutional provision behind each post:
- **Article 129** — executive authority of the Province exercised in the Governor's name.
- **Article 130** — appointment of the Chief Minister by the Governor on the majority party's advice.
- **Article 137** — the Cabinet's collective responsibility to the Provincial Assembly.
- **Article 116** — extent of provincial legislative competence and assent procedure.
- **Article 186** — jurisdiction of the **High Court of Sindh**, with principal seat at Karachi and a Circuit Bench at Sukkur.

#### Cadre and Recruitment
Provincial services are recruited through two main channels. The **Sindh Public Service Commission (SPSC)** conducts competitive examinations (syllabus aligned with FPSC patterns) for posts such as **Assistant, Lecturer, ASI, PMS, and CSS-related provincial cadres**. All-Pakistan services officers of the **Pakistan Administrative Service (PAS)** — formerly DMG — are posted to Commissioners and DCs by the federal Establishment Division, but their performance is assessed provincially.

#### Edge Cases and Common Errors
- The **Muhajir Sooba Movement** and pending new provincial units make candidates cautious about treating the current 7-division map as permanent. Always attach "as per present notifications" to any number.
- Hyderabad was capital during the **Bombay Presidency era** (until 1936) but is no longer capital; historic MCQs exploit this by offering both options.
- Do not confuse the **Chief Election Commissioner** with the **Chief Secretary** — both abbreviate as "Chief" but one supervises elections federally, the other provincial administration.
- The **Board of Revenue** is provincial, not part of **FBR**; land mutation, record-of-rights, and land-revenue recovery fall under it.
- Union Councils are a **provincial/local-government tier**, not federal.

#### Practice Prompts (PPSC style)
1. *Which constitutional article empowers the Provincial Assembly of Sindh to make laws on provincial subjects?* — Answer: **Article 116**.
2. *Who heads the Sindh Secretariat, and through what service is that post normally filled?* — Answer: the **Chief Secretary**, a senior officer of the **Pakistan Administrative Service (PAS)**.

> Exam tip: pair each official with their **Article**, not just their job title — PPSC option lists deliberately swap "Governor" and "Chief Minister" definitions to catch rote learners.
