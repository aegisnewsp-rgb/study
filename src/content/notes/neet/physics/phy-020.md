# Electromagnetic Induction and Alternating Current

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Faraday's law, ε = −N·dΦ/dt, governs every induction problem on NEET. Magnetic flux Φ = B·A·cosθ is in weber (Wb), ε in volt (V), and the negative sign encodes Lenz's law. For AC, three numbers come up repeatedly: peak I₀, RMS I₀/√2, and average 2I₀/π over a half cycle. Power formula always uses RMS: P = V_rms·I_rms·cosφ.

| Quantity | Formula | SI unit |
|---|---|---|
| Magnetic flux | Φ = B·A·cosθ | Wb |
| Induced emf | ε = −N·dΦ/dt | V |
| Self-inductance | L = μ₀N²A/ℓ | H |
| RMS current | I_rms = I₀/√2 | A |
| Impedance | Z = √(R² + (X_L − X_C)²) | Ω |
| Resonant frequency | f₀ = 1/(2π√(LC)) | Hz |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook (Lite):** "**FLeX**" — **F**lux changes → **L**enz opposes → e**X**ists as emf. Pair it with "**RMS = Peak / √2**" and "resonance = X_L − X_C = 0" for the last 60 seconds.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard depth for steady semester-level preparation.

#### Faraday's, Lenz's, and Motional emf
A changing flux through N turns induces ε = −N·dΦ/dt. The minus sign is Lenz's law: the induced current opposes the very change that produced it, conserving energy. A rod of length ℓ moving at velocity v through a uniform field B develops motional emf ε = B·ℓ·v·sinθ, with the −ve end identified by the right-hand rule.

#### Self and Mutual Induction
Self-inductance L = NΦ/I stores energy U = ½L·I² in its magnetic field (joule, J). Mutual inductance M = μ₀N₁N₂A/ℓ links two coaxial solenoids. Both L and M carry SI unit henry (H), dimensions M·L²·T⁻²·I⁻².

#### AC Quantities
A sinusoidal source i(t) = I₀·sin(ωt) gives I_rms = I₀/√2. Average over a half cycle is I_avg = 2I₀/π. Peak-to-peak V_pp = 2V₀ — easy to confuse with V₀ itself.

#### Reactance, Impedance, and Phase
Inductive reactance X_L = ωL and capacitive reactance X_C = 1/(ωC), both in ohm. Series impedance combines them by subtraction: Z = √(R² + (X_L − X_C)²). The phase angle φ obeys tanφ = (X_L − X_C)/R, with cosφ = R/Z as the power factor.

| Element | Voltage–Current phase | Power dissipated (avg) |
|---|---|---|
| Pure resistor R | In phase (φ = 0) | V_rms·I_rms |
| Pure inductor L | V leads I by π/2 | 0 |
| Pure capacitor C | I leads V by π/2 | 0 |

#### Resonance and Transformer
At f₀ = 1/(2π√(LC)), X_L = X_C, Z is minimum (= R), and current peaks. A transformer steps voltage by the turns ratio V_s/V_p = N_s/N_p, with the inverse current relation I_p/I_s = N_s/N_p. It works only on AC — DC produces no changing flux.

#### Comparison Matrix: Lenz's Law vs Faraday's Law
| Aspect | Faraday's law | Lenz's law |
|---|---|---|
| What it gives | Magnitude of induced emf | Direction of induced current |
| Equation form | ε = N·dΦ/dt (magnitude) | ε = −N·dΦ/dt (with sign) |
| Basis | Experimental (flux change) | Energy conservation |
| NEET use | Numerical value of ε | Polarity check in rod/moving coil |

#### Worked Exam-Style Problem
A rectangular coil of 200 turns, area 0.02 m², rotates at 50 Hz in a uniform field of 0.5 T. Find the peak emf.

Peak emf for a rotating coil: ε₀ = NBA·ω, where N = 200, B = 0.5 T, A = 0.02 m², ω = 2π·50 = 100π rad/s.

ε₀ = 200 × 0.5 × 0.02 × 100π = 200π V ≈ 628 V. The rms value is ε₀/√2 ≈ 444 V.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students frequently write ω = 50 instead of ω = 2πf, or use N·B·A without ω and obtain only the flux linkage (NBA), which is in weber-turns, not volt. Always multiply by ω.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Mastery layer covering edge cases, adjacent links, and exam traps.

#### Edge Cases Worth Memorising
1. **Zero induced emf despite motion** — when a rod moves parallel to its length, v × B has no component along the rod. Similarly, flux is constant if the coil's plane stays parallel to B (θ = 90°, cosθ = 0).
2. **Eddy-current suppression** — laminating the core breaks conducting loops, reducing I²R loss. NEET often asks *why* transformer cores are laminated: to minimise eddy currents, not hysteresis.
3. **Choke coil preference over resistor** — at AC frequency, X_L = ωL limits current with negligible heat dissipation; a resistor of equal drop would waste the same power as heat.
4. **Average of full cycle is zero, half cycle is 2I₀/π** — power uses RMS over a full cycle, not the half-cycle mean.
5. **Ideal transformer sign** — for step-up (N_s > N_p), current steps down: I_s < I_p. Conflating V_s/V_p with I_s/I_p is the most common NEET error in this unit.

#### Connections to Adjacent Topics
EMI hands off directly to **Electromagnetic Waves** (Maxwell's displacement current closes the loop between changing electric and magnetic fields) and to **Ray Optics** indirectly via the skin effect in conductors carrying high-frequency AC. Mutual inductance is the dual of capacitance in a coupled-circuit analysis.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts
1. A circular loop of radius r is pulled out of a uniform field B at constant speed v. Show that the emf induced equals that of a straight rod of effective length 2r, and compute the retarding force needed to maintain v against the magnetic braking action.
2. An LCR series circuit has R = 30 Ω, X_L = 90 Ω, X_C = 30 Ω at 50 Hz. Find the phase angle φ, the power factor, and the new resonant frequency if a dielectric of relative permittivity 4 is inserted into the capacitor without changing L. State how the Q-factor (X_L/R) changes and what that implies for sharpness of resonance.

#### Numerical Table of Reactances at Common NEET Frequencies (with L = 1 H, C = 1 μF)
| f (Hz) | X_L = 2πfL (Ω) | X_C = 1/(2πfC) (Ω) |

| 50 | 314 | 3183 |

The crossover sits near 318 Hz, where X_L = X_C for these values — useful for sanity-checking resonance problems without a calculator.
