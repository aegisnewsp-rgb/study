# Passage Mapping and Timing Strategy — MCAT CARS

Passage mapping and timing strategy are practical skills for the CARS section. The MCAT gives roughly 90 minutes for the CARS section, distributed across roughly nine to ten passages with five to seven items each. Active reading (passage mapping) and disciplined time management are central to score improvement, especially for the Reasoning Within the Text and Reasoning Beyond the Text skills.

> Verify the live CARS section length and item distribution on https://students-residents.aamc.org/ before planning around the figures below.

---

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### What passage mapping is

Passage mapping is the practice of marking the structure of a passage as you read:

- Underline the central claim of each paragraph.
- Mark the author's argument structure (claim, evidence, counter-argument, response).
- Identify the transitions between paragraphs ("however," "for example," "in contrast").
- Note unfamiliar terms and their context.

#### What the timing strategy is

The MCAT CARS section gives roughly 90 minutes for roughly 53 items. A workable timing strategy:

- Read each passage in 3-4 minutes.
- Answer each item in about 1 minute.
- Aim to leave 5-10 minutes at the end for review.

#### One-pass drill

1. Read the passage in 4 minutes, mapping the structure as you go.
2. Answer each item in about 1 minute. Use the platform's flag-for-review function for hard items.
3. Leave 5 minutes at the end to revisit flagged items.

#### Examiner traps

- Spending too long on a single passage. If you cannot answer an item in 1.5 minutes, flag it and move on.
- Reading too quickly. Reading in under 2 minutes often leads to missing the central claim.
- Skipping the passage map. Without a map, the items become a re-read of the passage.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Passage mapping in detail

A good passage map marks:

- The topic of each paragraph (one or two words).
- The central claim of each paragraph (one sentence).
- The transitions between paragraphs (logical flow).
- The author's argument structure (claim, evidence, counter-argument, response).
- Unfamiliar terms and their context.

The map should fit on the margin of the passage or on a separate sheet. It should take 30-60 seconds to make.

#### Timing strategy in detail

The MCAT CARS section gives roughly 90 minutes for roughly 53 items across roughly nine to ten passages. A practical breakdown:

- Read each passage in 3-4 minutes.
- Answer each item in about 1 minute.
- Aim to leave 5-10 minutes at the end for review.

If a passage is taking longer than 5 minutes to read, you are reading too slowly. If an item is taking longer than 1.5 minutes, flag it and move on.

#### Practice routine

- Take one CARS passage. Read it in 4 minutes with a passage map.
- Answer the items in about 6 minutes (1 minute per item for 5-6 items).
- Total target: under 10 minutes per passage.
- Take 10 passages in a row. Track your time per passage. Aim for consistent timing across passages.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How passage mapping and timing connect to the other CARS skills

- **Foundations of Comprehension** is supported by the passage map: the map makes the central claim and structure visible.
- **Reasoning Within the Text** is supported by the timing strategy: consistent timing across items lets you focus on argument evaluation.
- **Reasoning Beyond the Text** is supported by the map: extending the author's reasoning requires a clear view of the author's argument.

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"I should read the items before the passage."** Reading items first without context often leads to misreading the passage. Read the passage first.
- **"I should read more passages for more practice."** Quality of practice matters more than quantity. One well-mapped passage is more useful than five unmapped ones.
- **"I should not flag items."** Flagging is a tool. Use it for items that would take too long to answer in the moment.

#### Exam technique

- Read the passage in 3-4 minutes. Make a passage map as you read.
- Answer each item in about 1 minute. Flag items that would take too long.
- Leave 5-10 minutes at the end for review.
- Use the platform's flag-for-review function.
- Trust your first instinct on items you cannot answer — change your answer only if you have a clear reason.
