# Environmental Chemistry

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Every JAMB Chemistry paper hides one Environmental Chemistry MCQ. Memorise the high-yield items below and you claim that point.

Environmental Chemistry studies chemical changes in air, water, and soil caused by natural cycles and human activity. Pollutants measured as BOD (mg/L) and pH (dimensionless) tell you how dirty a water sample is, while CO₂, CH₄, CFCs, N₂O, and SO₂ classify as greenhouse gases. Hard water contains Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺ salts; boiling strips the temporary kind (Ca(HCO₃)₂) and leaves the permanent kind (CaSO₄, MgSO₄).

| Term | Quick fact |
|------|------------|
| BOD | Dissolved O₂ consumed in 5 days at 20 °C; > 5 mg/L = polluted water |
| pH of acid rain | < 5.6 (unpolluted rain ≈ 5.6 from dissolved CO₂, not 7) |
| Ozone depletion | CFCs release Cl• which catalytically splits O₃ in the stratosphere |
| Eutrophication | Excess PO₄³⁻/NO₃⁻ → algal bloom → fish kill |
| Hardness removal | Boiling (temporary only); Na₂CO₃ or ion-exchange (both) |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"BODeG"** — **B**iodegradable → raises **O**xygen demand (high **BOD**) → **D**epletes dissolved gas → **G**reenhouse effect worsens. JAMB loves linking BOD directly to "indicates level of water pollution."

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Builds the mechanism behind each pollutant so you can defend any option, not just guess.

#### Atmospheric Composition and Pollutants
Clean dry air holds roughly **78 % N₂, 21 % O₂, 0.03 % CO₂** by volume, plus noble gases. Combustion of fossil fuels and biomass injects **CO, SO₂, NO₂, hydrocarbons, and lead particulates** into that baseline. JAMB tests whether you can separate natural gases (N₂, O₂) from anthropogenic ones (SO₂, NO, NO₂). The gaseous oxides collectively labelled **SOₓ** (sulfur oxides) and **NOₓ** (nitrogen oxides) form the raw ingredients of acid rain.

#### Greenhouse Effect vs Ozone Depletion
Both involve atmospheric chemistry but target different layers.

| Feature | Greenhouse effect | Ozone-layer depletion |
|---------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Layer | Troposphere (lower) | Stratosphere (15–35 km) |
| Gases involved | CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, CFCs, H₂O | CFCs, halons, NOₓ |
| Mechanism | Absorb outgoing IR radiation | Cl• catalyses O₃ → O₂ + O₂ |
| Consequence | Global warming | Increased UV-B reaching Earth |

#### Acid Rain Formation
SO₂ + H₂O → H₂SO₃, then ½O₂ + H₂SO₃ → H₂SO₄ (sulfuric acid rain). Separately, 2NO₂ + H₂O → HNO₃ + HNO₂ gives nitric acid rain. The benchmark **pH < 5.6** distinguishes acid rain from naturally slightly acidic unpolluted rain (~5.6 from dissolved CO₂ forming H₂CO₃).

#### Water Hardness and BOD
**Temporary hardness** = Ca(HCO₃)₂ + Mg(HCO₃)₂; boiling decomposes the bicarbonates to insoluble carbonates. **Permanent hardness** = CaSO₄, MgSO₄, CaCl₂, MgCl₂; boiling fails, so add **Na₂CO₃** (washing soda) or pass through an ion-exchange resin. **BOD₅** in mg/L = (D₁ − D₂) / sample volume (mL), where D₁ and D₂ are dissolved-oxygen readings in mg/L before and after five days at 20 °C. Potable water should sit below about 1–2 mg/L BOD; rivers downstream of sewage outfalls spike well past 5 mg/L.

#### Worked Example with Numbers
A 250 mL water sample is diluted to 1 L with oxygen-saturated water. Initial dissolved O₂ = 8.4 mg/L. After 5 days at 20 °C, dissolved O₂ = 4.0 mg/L. Calculate the BOD₅ of the original sample.

The BOD of the diluted sample = 8.4 − 4.0 = **4.4 mg/L**. Because only 250 mL of the 1 L dilution came from the original wastewater, multiply by the dilution factor (1000 / 250 = 4): BOD₅ (original) = 4.4 × 4 = **17.6 mg/L**. This value lies far above the 1–2 mg/L potable benchmark, so the source water is heavily polluted — consistent with raw domestic sewage.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students forget the dilution factor and write "4.4 mg/L." JAMB often frames the question with a diluted BOD bottle and expects you to back-calculate to the undiluted strength.

#### Bioaccumulation versus Biomagnification

| Process | Where it happens | Example |
|---------|-----------------|---------|
| Bioaccumulation | Inside **one** organism over time | DDT building up in a tilapia's fatty tissue |
| Biomagnification | Across **trophic levels** in a food chain | DDT concentration rising from plankton → small fish → large fish → bird of prey |

Both depend on **non-biodegradable** substances (DDT, mercury, lead). Biodegradable pollutants (domestic sewage, food waste) break down and do not accumulate.

#### Eutrophication
Phosphates from detergents and nitrates from fertiliser runoff trigger algal bloom. Decaying algae consume dissolved O₂ and the water body suffocates. Removing PO₄³⁻ at the treatment stage (precipitation as Ca₃(PO₄)₂ or biological uptake) breaks the cycle.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Where to push when one MCQ is not enough — connections, edge cases, and the traps JAMB sets in the harder alternatives.

#### Volumetric Calculation Edge Cases
When a flue gas contains 80 mL of SO₂ in 4.00 L of total gas at STP, the percent by volume is (80 / 4000) × 100 = 2.0 %. Watch the **molar mass of SO₂ = 64 g mol⁻¹**, not 48 — using S = 32 and O = 16 × 2 keeps the math honest. Converting that volume percent into mass per cubic metre requires the ideal-gas relation **n = PV/RT** at STP (P = 101 325 Pa, T = 273 K), giving n ≈ 0.0446 mol of SO₂ per litre × 64 g mol⁻¹ ≈ 2.86 g per cubic metre of flue gas. Exam answers usually stop at the volume-percent step.

For acid-rain titration, JAMB occasionally asks the **molarity of H⁺** from a 0.001 M H₂SO₄ sample. Each mole of H₂SO₄ yields 2 moles of H⁺, so [H⁺] = 2 × 0.001 = 0.002 mol L⁻¹, giving pH = −log₁₀(0.002) ≈ 2.70. Forgetting the factor of 2 is the classic slip.

#### Where This Topic Touches Other JAMB Areas
- **Acids, Bases and Salts** — water softening exploits carbonate precipitation, the same reaction used in qualitative analysis for Group 2 cations.
- **Rates of Reaction** — CFC breakdown in the stratosphere is photochemically driven; the rate depends on UV flux, not thermal activation energy.
- **Hydrocarbons** — incomplete combustion of alkanes yields CO, while catalytic converters oxidise CO to CO₂ and reduce NOₓ to N₂.
- **Extraction of Metals** — recycling aluminium saves ~95 % of the energy of primary extraction from bauxite; JAMB may frame this as an environmental benefit.

#### Numbered List of Advanced Traps and Exceptions
1. **"CO₂ is a pollutant"** — flag this as ambiguous on objective tests. Natural background CO₂ (~0.03 %) is not pollution; anthropogenic excess is. JAMB's safest option choice accepts CO₂ as a greenhouse gas rather than a classical toxic pollutant.
2. **Ozone distinction** — tropospheric O₃ (ground level) is a respiratory pollutant; stratospheric O₃ is the UV shield. Do not let "ozone" alone decide your answer; read the layer.
3. **"Boiling removes hardness"** — only temporary hardness. Permanent hardness demands Na₂CO₃ or ion exchange.
4. **BOD vs COD** — BOD captures only biodegradable organics (5 days); COD captures everything an oxidising agent can attack, including non-biodegradables, so COD > BOD for the same sample.
5. **Recycling** — PET (polyethylene terephthalate) and HDPE are thermoplastic and recyclable; thermosetting plastics (Bakelite, melamine) cross-link on heating and are **not** recyclable by remelting.
6. **Lead in petrol** — phased out globally; JAMB still mentions it because Nigerian syllabus texts reference historical leaded gasoline as a lead-pollution source.
7. **Catalytic converter chemistry** — 2CO + O₂ → 2CO₂ (oxidation catalyst); 2NOₓ → xN₂ + yO₂ (reduction catalyst). Both happen on the same honeycomb monolith coated with Pt/Rh/Pd.

#### Practice Prompts
- A river sample shows BOD₅ = 9 mg/L. Classify it, justify the classification with reference to the potable benchmark, and suggest one treatment step that would lower it.
- 1.50 g of CaCO₃ equivalent is obtained from 500 mL of hard water. Express the hardness in mg/L CaCO₃ and decide which removal method (boiling vs Na₂CO₃) fully eliminates it.

Spend ten minutes on it: pin the pH-of-acid-rain threshold, lock the temporary/permanent hardness removal methods, and nail the bioaccumulation/biomagnification contrast.
