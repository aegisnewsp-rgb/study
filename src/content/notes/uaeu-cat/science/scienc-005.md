# Scientific Literacy & Core Physical Measurements

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Scientific Literacy & Core Physical Measurements** in the UAEU CAT Science section measures core scientific competence at a senior-secondary general level, blending physics, chemistry, biology, and data-reading in roughly 3% of the composite score. The fastest-scoring items rely on the formulas in the table below plus disciplined graph reading.

- **Master the core formulas**: `v = d/t`, `F = ma`, `W = mg`, `KE = ½mv²`, `ρ = m/V`, `pH = −log₁₀[H⁺]`, and `PV = nRT`.
- **Apply the scientific method**: hypothesis → independent/dependent/control variables → evidence-based conclusion.
- **Spot the trap**: confuse *mass (kg)* with *weight (N)*, treat pH as linear, or misread graph axes.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Scientific method and variables
A testable **hypothesis** must be falsifiable. Identify the **independent variable** (what you change), the **dependent variable** (what you measure), and the **control variables** (kept constant). UAEU CAT items often describe a short experiment and ask which variable is independent or which setup is a fair test.

#### Mechanics essentials
Use **Newton's second law** `F = ma` (F in newtons, m in kg, a in m/s²) and the weight equation `W = mg` with `g ≈ 9.8 m/s²` near Earth's surface. **Kinetic energy** `KE = ½mv²` grows with the square of speed, a fact that appears in energy-comparison MCQs.

| Quantity | Formula | SI unit | Common trap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Speed | v = d / t | m/s | Mixing km/h with m/s |
| Force | F = m·a | N (kg·m/s²) | Forgetting to convert g → kg |
| Weight | W = m·g | N | Confusing with mass (kg) |
| Kinetic energy | KE = ½mv² | J | Treating v as constant when comparing |
| Density | ρ = m / V | kg/m³ | Swapping m and V |

#### Chemistry building blocks
Classify changes as **physical** (reversible, no new substance — sugar dissolving, ice melting) or **chemical** (new substance — iron rusting, wood burning). Use the **periodic table** for group/period position and metal/non-metal character. Treat **pH = −log₁₀[H⁺]** as dimensionless and logarithmic: each unit is a 10× change in hydrogen-ion concentration.

#### Biology and ecosystems
The **cell** is the structural and functional unit of life; recognise key organelles (nucleus, mitochondria, chloroplast). **Photosynthesis** (6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂) and **cellular respiration** are complementary energy-conversion pathways. In an **ecosystem**, energy flows from producers → consumers → decomposers, with roughly 10% transfer between trophic levels.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Worked example: kinetic energy comparison
A 2 kg ball moves at 3 m/s; a 1 kg ball moves at 5 m/s. Compute each KE and decide which has more.
- Ball A: `KE = ½ × 2 × 3² = 9 J`.
- Ball B: `KE = ½ × 1 × 5² = 12.5 J`.
Despite A being heavier, B wins because KE scales with **v²**. This is a classic CAT distractor pattern: weight misleads, speed squared decides.

#### Graph interpretation strategy
Always check the **axis label, unit, and scale** before reading a value. A straight line on a d–t graph gives speed from the **slope**; a v–t graph gives acceleration the same way. Misreading 0.5 km as 500 m is the most common numerical error in data items.

#### Common mistakes to neutralise
1. Treating pH as linear — pH 3 is **1000×** more acidic than pH 6, not 3×.
2. Believing heavier objects fall faster — in idealised free-fall they fall at the same rate.
3. Confusing chemical vs. physical change for reversible processes like dissolving.
4. Using `PV = nRT` with the wrong gas constant or non-SI pressure units.

#### Practice prompts
1. A 1500 kg car accelerates from rest to 20 m/s in 10 s. Find the average force and compare with its weight.
2. A solution has `[H⁺] = 1 × 10⁻⁴ mol/L`. Calculate pH and state whether it is acidic, neutral, or basic.
