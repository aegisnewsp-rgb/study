# Consumer Behaviour

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Consumer Behaviour** studies how a rational household divides limited money income across goods to reach the highest attainable satisfaction, working inside a fixed budget.

- **Total Utility (TU)** is the full satisfaction from a bundle; **Marginal Utility (MU)** is the addition to TU from one more unit, computed as MU_n = ΔTU/ΔQ_n ≈ dTU/dQ_n.
- The **Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility** (Gossen's First Law) says each successive unit consumed adds smaller increments of satisfaction, holding consumption of all other goods constant.
- **Equilibrium rule (cardinal):** MU_x / P_x = MU_y / P_y = λ (marginal utility of income, in utils per rupee), subject to the budget constraint P_x·x + P_y·y = M.
- **Ordinal view:** the consumer picks the bundle where the **Indifference Curve (IC)** is tangent to the **Budget Line**, so MRS_xy = MU_x/MU_y = P_x/P_y.
- **High-yield exam pointers:** distinguish Giffen (negative income effect on an inferior good) from Veblen (conspicuous consumption); remember Slutsky keeps purchasing power constant, Hicks keeps utility constant; aggregation of individual demand yields market demand.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Cardinal and Ordinal Frameworks

The **cardinal utility** approach measures satisfaction in utils and applies the **equimarginal principle** (Gossen's Second Law): the consumer equates the last rupee spent on every good, so MU_x/P_x = MU_y/P_y = λ. Here λ is the marginal utility of income in utils per rupee, and falling MU is what guarantees an interior maximum rather than a corner solution.

The **ordinal utility** approach drops the utils assumption and ranks bundles via **indifference curves**. Three properties define well-behaved preferences: ICs slope downward (more of x means less of y for equal satisfaction), they are convex to the origin (preference for balanced bundles — diminishing MRS), and they never cross (transitivity of choice).

#### Budget Constraint and Consumer Equilibrium

The **budget line** P_x·x + P_y·y = M shows all affordable bundles; its slope is −P_x/P_y. **Attainable bundles** lie on or inside the line, **inefficient bundles** lie strictly inside, and **unattainable bundles** lie outside.

#### Price Effects: Income and Substitution

A fall in P_x generates two effects:

| Effect | Mechanism | Sign for normal good | Sign for inferior good |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Substitution** | Relative price change tilts budget line; consumer substitutes toward cheaper x | Positive | Positive |
| **Income** | Real income (M/P) rises; demand rises for normal, falls for inferior | Positive | Negative |

| Decomposition | What is held constant | Use case |
|---|---|---|
| **Hicks** | Utility (original IC) | Welfare analysis, CPI |
| **Slutsky** | Original bundle (purchasing power) | Empirical demand estimation |

#### Common Exam Traps

- Giffen good (strong negative income effect overwhelms substitution) is **not** the same as Veblen good (status-driven upward-sloping demand).
- Conflating diminishing TU (false in normal range) with diminishing MU (true) loses marks.
- Off-budget bundles cannot be equilibrium even if MRS matches the price ratio.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Special Preference Structures and Corner Solutions

Preference shape dictates demand response. With **perfect substitutes** (linear ICs), equilibrium is at a corner unless MRS equals the price ratio exactly. With **perfect complements** (L-shaped ICs, e.g. left and right shoes in fixed proportion), demand for x is x = M/(P_x + kP_y), independent of relative price. **Cobb-Douglas preferences** U = x^α y^(1−α) yield constant expenditure shares α and 1−α, satisfying Euler's condition P_x·x + P_y·y = M at every interior optimum.

#### Revealed Preference and Intertemporal Choice

**Samuelson's Revealed Preference Theorem** infers preferences from observed budget-constrained choices: if bundle A is chosen when B was affordable, A is revealed preferred to B. Violations imply inconsistent behaviour. **Intertemporal choice** extends the model to two periods with budget C₁ + C₂/(1+r) = Y₁ + Y₂/(1+r), where r is the real interest rate; borrowers consume more today when r is low, lenders save more when r is high.

#### Worked Micro-Example

Suppose U(x,y) = xy, P_x = ₹10, P_y = ₹20, M = ₹400. From MU_x = y, MU_y = x, equilibrium requires y/x = 10/20 = 0.5, so y = 0.5x. Budget: 10x + 20(0.5x) = 20x = 400, giving **x\* = 20, y\* = 10**, with utility 200 and λ = MU_x/P_x = 10/10 = 1 util per rupee.

#### Practice Prompts

1. Derive Marshallian and Hicksian demands for U = √x + y under a P_x change, and compute the substitution and income effects numerically.
2. Use revealed preference to test whether observed choices (A chosen with B affordable, then B chosen with C affordable, then A chosen with C affordable) violate the Weak Axiom.
