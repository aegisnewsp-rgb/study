# Data Interpretation

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Data Interpretation (DI) tests your ability to read numbers from charts, tables and caselets, then compute percentages, ratios and averages to answer a question. In CUET UG General Test, it contributes about **10–15 questions out of 75**, and each correct answer carries **+5 marks** under the current NTA marking scheme. The format is MCQ-based and Class 10–12 level.

- **Bar, line and pie charts** → read the axis label and unit first, then pull the value.
- **Tables and caselets** → underline every numeric fact before forming a ratio.
- **Percentage change** = ((New − Old) / Old) × 100, **always with old as base**.
- **SI** = P·R·T/100; **CI** = P[(1 + R/100)^T − 1]. Use SI for under-1-year or split-year cases.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Chart types and how to read them

DI passages arrive in four formats: bar graphs, line graphs, pie charts, and tables or caselets. **Bar graphs** compare absolute quantities across categories — the height (or length) of the bar maps to the y-axis scale. **Line graphs** show trends over time, so questions usually ask about slope, peak or trough. **Pie charts** represent 100% as a full circle, and every sector's central angle equals its percentage share.

| Format | What it shows | Typical question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bar graph | Discrete category values | Difference between two bars |
| Line graph | Trend over time | Highest growth year |
| Pie chart | Proportional share of a whole | Percentage of one sector |
| Table / Caselet | Mixed raw numbers | Ratio, average or ratio comparison |

#### Core arithmetic to master

Almost every DI question reduces to one of three operations: percentage, ratio, or average.

- **Percentage** lets you convert between raw numbers and standardised shares.
- **Ratio** A : B is just A/B in lowest terms — keep it in that form until a comparison forces conversion.
- **Average** = sum ÷ count, and a missing value can be back-solved from a known average.

#### Data Sufficiency and caselets

A **Data Sufficiency** question gives you two statements and asks whether they alone or together let you find the answer. The decision grid is: statement 1 alone? statement 2 alone? both together? neither? Never assume — test each branch.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Percentage change and growth comparison

When comparing growth between two series with different bases, raw differences mislead. Use **Percentage Change = ((New − Old) / Old) × 100**. For multi-year growth, use a CAGR-like approach: ((End/Start)^(1/n) − 1) × 100, where n is the number of compounding years. A common CUET trap gives two years' figures and asks which grew faster — students divide by the wrong base and pick the larger absolute change.

#### Interest applications inside DI

Profit and Loss, SI and CI appear as caselet sub-problems. Use **SI** when interest is computed only on the original principal, and **CI** when each year's interest is added to the principal. A 2-year CI at 10% is not 20% — it is 21% because of compounding on interest.

| Quantity | Formula | When to use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Simple Interest | P·R·T/100 | Fractional years, single period |
| Compound Interest | P[(1 + R/100)^T − 1] | Full integer years, reinvestment |
| Profit % | (Profit / CP) × 100 | Marked price problems |
| Population growth | P(1 + r/100)^t | Caselet with yearly % rise |

#### Common traps and how to avoid them

- **Scale confusion**: a bar at "20" on a y-axis labelled "in thousands" equals 20,000, not 20.
- **Unit mixing**: crores vs lakhs vs millions — convert before adding.
- **Wrong base**: percentage change always uses the **old** value as denominator.
- **Partial-information assumption**: in Data Sufficiency, mark "cannot be determined" only after testing every branch.

#### Practice prompts

1. A pie chart shows sector angles 90°, 120°, 60°, 90°. If the total value is ₹4,80,000, find the value of the 60° sector and its share as a percentage.
2. A company's revenue rises from ₹80 lakh to ₹92 lakh over two years. Compute the simple average annual growth rate and compare it with the CAGR.
