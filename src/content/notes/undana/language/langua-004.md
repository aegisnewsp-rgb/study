# Writing and Expression

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Three sentence-and-paragraph rules decide roughly 80% of marks in this 3%-weighted Language subtest.

A solid answer in **Writing and Expression** rests on three pillars: a *paragraf* whose idea pokok sits in the right slot, *kalimat efektif* with a visible subject–predicate pair, and *PUEBI*-clean spelling from the first word to the last.

| Pillars to lock in before the exam | What examiners look for |
|---|---|
| Paragraf structure (deduktif / induktif / campuran) | Idea pokok in the **first** sentence for deduktif, in the **last** for induktif |
| Kalimat efektif (6 syarat) | Kesepadanan, paralelisme, ketepatan diksi, kehematan, kelogisan, kepaduan |
| PUEBI basics | Huruf kapital, *di/ke/dari*, partikel *pun*, koma vs titik koma |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook — "PADU-KSP"**: **P**aragraf deduktif (idea pokok depan), **A**khir = induktif; **D**iksi tepat, **U**bah variasi dengan sinonim; **K**alimat efektif = **S**ubjek-Predikat jelas, **P**UEBI tanpa cela. Whisper "PADU-KSP" while reading the stem and the answer type surfaces itself.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Read this tier once, then practise five *melengkapi paragraf rumpang* items against a timer.

#### Paragraf organisation: where the idea pokok lives

UNDANA items rarely test grammar in isolation; they hand you a four-sentence *paragraf rumpang* and ask which sentence A, B, C, or D belongs in the blank. The discriminator is recognising the **development pattern** — that is, deduktif moves *umum → khusus*, so the idea pokok sits in sentence one and supporting details expand afterwards. Induktif does the opposite, climbing from a specific datum to a conclusion in the closing line. Campangan (*mixed*) threads both: an opening generalisation, evidence in the middle, and a sharpened closing claim.

Sentence-level cohesion matters as much as paragraph shape. **Kohesi gramatikal** uses conjunctions (*dan, tetapi, karena, sehingga*), reference pronouns (*ia, mereka, hal itu*), and ellipsis to tie clauses together. **Kohesi leksikal** ties them through repetition, synonym pairs (*besar–luas, masalah–isu*), and antonym contrast. A paragraph whose sentences all begin with the same noun phrase, or that lacks any conjunctive glue, will read fragmented to the marker.

#### Effective-sentence checklist

A *kalimat efektif* satisfies six mutually reinforcing requirements, often grouped as **kesepadanan, keparalelan, ketepatan diksi, kehematan, kelogisan, kepaduan**. The first one — *kesepadanan* — is the most tested: the predicate must match the subject in number, person, and presence. A sentence beginning with a long prepositional phrase and ending with no explicit subject (e.g., *Di Desa Oebobo, sejak pagi hari, …*) breaks *kesepadanan* even though every word is spelled correctly.

| Syarat kalimat efektif | What it forbids | Quick diagnostic |
|---|---|---|
| Kesepadanan | Hidden / missing subject | Cross out the fronted adverbial — does a subject still appear? |
| Keparalelan | Mixing noun + verb-ing forms in a list | All items in a series share one grammatical shape |
| Ketepatan diksi | *Besar sekali* instead of *sangat besar*; English code-switches | Prefer locally precise Indonesian lexemes |
| Kehematan | Redundant pairs (*balik kembali*), filler (*pada dasarnya*) | Every word carries information |
| Kelogisan | Cause-after-effect wording | Time, cause, and consequence in expected order |
| Kepaduan | Two ideas glued by *dan* but unrelated | One dominant idea per sentence |

#### PUEBI traps that recur every year

The examiners at **pmb.undana.ac.id** lean on a fixed handful of PUEBI slips rather than obscure orthography. Master these and a noticeable number of "find the wrong sentence" items becomes automatic. Words that look prepositional but function as prefixes (*di-, ke-, -nya, -pun*) are written as one token; only *di/ke/dari* as standalone prepositions split. Capitalisation is reserved for the first word of a sentence, proper nouns, and honorifics — not common nouns like *pemerintah* or *universitas*. The semicolon joins two equal clauses; the comma never can.

#### Worked problem on the paragraph-completion pattern

Consider the stem:

> *“Penduduk desa mengelola sampah rumah tangga dengan cara yang sederhana. _____ Hasil fermentasi tersebut dapat digunakan sebagai pupuk organik pada musim tanam berikutnya.”*

Options: (A) *Sampah organik dikumpulkan dan difermentasi dalam lubang tanah selama beberapa minggu.* (B) *Karena sampah plastik sulit diurai, sehingga masyarakat membakarnya di pekarangan.* (C) *Sampah anorganik seperti botol dan sachet dipisahkan sejak di sumbernya.* (D) *Tidak semua desa memiliki bank sampah yang aktif, tetapi inisiatif Rukun Tetangga tetap berjalan.*

Two of the four sentences are factually true, but only one completes the deduktif→prosedur flow. Option B fails a *kelogisan* test: *karena … sehingga* is a doubled conjunction. Option C drifts sideways — it is about inorganic waste, not the fermentable organic stream that the frame already names. Option D introduces a new topic (*bank sampah*), so the paragraph loses cohesion. Option A continues the procedural chain ("dikumpulkan dan difermentasi"), which is precisely the supporting detail the *umum* sentence at the top needs.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap**: Most candidates choose option D because it "sounds smart" and adds information. Markers reward **coherence with the existing frame**, not topical novelty.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Use this tier when you have at least six weeks before the exam and want to push past *lulus* into *lolos dengan nilai aman*.

#### Boundary cases that separate top scorers

Three conditions repeatedly decide the top quartile. First, when a paragraph opens with a long adverbial or a quote, candidates must verify that the *kalimat utama* is not the second sentence by mistake. Second, *translanguaging* — mixing English or regional Kupang-Malay lexemes into a formal answer — costs marks even when the meaning is clear, because the rubric scores against *bahasa Indonesia baku*. Third, *paduan paragraf* breaks when the writer inserts a totally new idea between two tightly related clauses; the fix is either to cut the new sentence or to move it into a new paragraph.

| Trap | Why it slips under the rubric | Specific correction |
|---|---|---|
| Frontal adverbial without subject | Examiner reads "no subject" before noting hidden subject | Insert explicit subject after the comma |
| *Di mana / dari mana* as one token | Preposition used as prefix by instinct | Split: *di mana, ke mana, dari mana* |
| Comma splices two main clauses | Both halves are full sentences joined by koma alone | Replace koma with semicolon, *tetapi*, or a full stop |
| Idea pokok buried mid-paragraph | Rater scans first sentence, finds a detail, deducts | Re-order so the *general claim* leads |
