# Analysing Argument — VCE English (VCAA 2023) Notes

Analysing Argument is the Unit 4 Area of Study 2 of VCE English. Students analyse the ways argument is constructed in contemporary media texts and produce a written commentary on a selected text. The Area of Study is assessed as part of the Unit 4 School-Assessed Coursework and is one of the three sections of the end-of-year examination.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Area of Study.** Analysing argument in contemporary media texts and producing a written commentary.
- **Outcomes.** Students identify argument techniques; analyse the construction of argument; produce a commentary.
- **Assessment.** Unit 4 SAC and one of three sections on the end-of-year examination.
- **Texts.** Typically a media text (article, opinion piece, editorial, blog post, advertisement, speech transcript).

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing Analysing Argument with Reading and Responding — argument analysis focuses on persuasive techniques rather than literary features.
- Confusing analysis of argument with analysis of media — the question is about HOW argument is built, not just about the medium.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Argument techniques

Appeals to logic (logos): evidence, statistics, logical chains, expert authority. Appeals to emotion (pathos): anecdote, vivid imagery, rhetorical questions, hyperbole. Appeals to credibility (ethos): expert voice, balanced tone, transparent reasoning.

Fallacies: ad hominem (attacking the person), straw man (misrepresenting the argument), false dilemma (only two options), slippery slope (one thing leads to another without justification), appeal to authority (without relevant expertise), appeal to popularity (bandwagon).

#### Structure of an argument analysis

1. Identify the thesis (the central claim).
2. Identify the premises (the supporting claims).
3. Identify the argument techniques (logos, pathos, ethos, or fallacies).
4. Evaluate the argument (does the evidence support the premises? do the premises support the thesis?).
5. State the argument's overall effectiveness.

#### Commentary structure

A commentary integrates the analysis above into a coherent piece of writing, typically 600-800 words. Use topic sentences, evidence from the text, and evaluation of the argument.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Why Analysing Argument is examined separately

- **Argument analysis is a distinct skill.** It overlaps with Reading and Responding but focuses on persuasive technique rather than literary meaning.
- **University study requires argument analysis.** First-year humanities and social science courses reward students who can spot argument techniques and evaluate evidence.

#### Exam technique

- Identify the thesis BEFORE identifying the techniques.
- Quote specific passages of the argument.
- Evaluate the argument's effectiveness, not just describe it.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Argument = disagreement." Argument can be a reasoned defence of a position; disagreement is one form.
- "Fallacies are always wrong." Fallacies are always flawed reasoning; an argument built on fallacies is unsound even if the conclusion happens to be true.
- "All arguments are equal." No — well-constructed arguments have evidence, clear reasoning and consideration of counter-arguments.
