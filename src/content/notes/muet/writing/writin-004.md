# Paragraph Development — The PEEL Technique

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your MUET Writing paper.

PEEL is a four-move paragraph structure used in MUET Writing Paper 4 continuous essays. Every body paragraph should contain **Point → Evidence → Explanation → Link** in that exact order. Getting the order right is what lifts a candidate from MUET Band 3 to Band 5 on the Organisation criterion.

- **P — Point:** an arguable topic sentence that previews the paragraph's main idea and ties back to the thesis statement.
- **E — Evidence:** a concrete example, statistic, quotation, or case-study fact that supports the Point.
- **E — Explanation:** analyst-style commentary showing *how* and *why* the Evidence proves the Point (this is the band-mover).
- **L — Link:** a sentence that returns to the thesis and/or bridges into the next paragraph using a discourse marker such as *Consequently*, *Building on this idea*, or *In contrast*.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"PeeEL — Prove, Explain, Extend, Loop back."** Think of L as a literal *loop* knot that ties the paragraph to your thesis before you move on.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months before Paper 4.

#### What Examiners Are Actually Scoring

MUET Paper 4 is marked by trained Malaysian Examinations Council (MPSM) assessors using four band criteria: Task Fulfilment, Communicative Achievement, Organisation, and Language. Each body paragraph contributes most heavily to **Organisation** because the marker scans for clear progression and cohesive devices. A paragraph that states an idea but never *develops* it caps the essay at Band 3 ("some organisational features"). PEEL is the fastest mechanical fix because it forces the Explanation step — the exact move examiners reward.

#### PEEL vs. Common Alternatives

| Feature | PEEL Paragraph | Topic-Only Paragraph | TEEL Variant | Free-Write Paragraph |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Topic sentence | Required, arguable | Required, in many papers descriptive | Required | Optional, unclear |
| Evidence | Cited/example-based | In many papers anecdotal or missing | Cited/example-based | Unsourced or fabricated |
| Explanation step | Mandatory (~2–3 sentences) | In most keys absent | Mandatory | Absent |
| Linkage to thesis | Explicit final sentence | Implicit, in many papers missing | Explicit | None |
| Typical MUET band | Band 4–5 | Band 2–3 | Band 4–5 | Band 1–2 |
| Best used for | Argumentative / discursive Task 2 | Narrative Task 1 only | Argumentative Task 2 | Draft brainstorming only |

#### Step-by-Step Construction

1. Draft your thesis statement first so every Point has something to loop back to.
2. Write one **Point** per body paragraph — never two competing claims in one paragraph.
3. Insert **Evidence** immediately after the Point: a statistic, a quoted expert, a real Malaysian case study.
4. Spend 2–3 sentences on **Explanation**, naming the *mechanism*: cause-and-effect, comparison, consequence, or rebuttal.
5. Close with a **Link** sentence using a discourse marker that signals either continuation (*Furthermore*) or contrast (*Conversely*).
6. Aim for 90–130 words per body paragraph — long enough for development, short enough to stay focused.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** *A friend has written the following body paragraph for an MUET Task 2 essay on "Social media does more harm than good to Malaysian youth." Identify the two PEEL errors and rewrite the paragraph using correct PEEL order.*

> *"Social media exposes teenagers to misinformation. According to a 2023 KKM report, 62% of Malaysian adolescents encountered fake health news online. Many young people share these posts without checking. In conclusion, social media is bad."*

#### Solution:

- **Error 1 — Missing Explanation.** The statistic (62%) is dropped in but never analysed. The marker awards zero marks for *development of ideas* because the writer never explains *how* the statistic proves the Point.
- **Error 2 — Weak Link.** "In conclusion" is a *conclusion* discourse marker, not a link; it also prematurely closes the essay by restating the thesis instead of bridging to the next paragraph.

#### Corrected PEEL paragraph (~110 words):
> *(P)* Social media exposes Malaysian teenagers to large volumes of misinformation that can shape unsafe health decisions. *(E)* A 2023 KKM report found that 62% of Malaysian adolescents encountered fake health news online in the past year, and many admitted forwarding the posts unchecked. *(E)* This matters because adolescent users in many papers treat viral content as expert advice, so false claims about diet pills or unproven remedies spread faster than official MOH corrections can counter them. *(L)* Consequently, the harm of social media cannot be measured by screen time alone, which links directly to the next point: the erosion of face-to-face support networks.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Reusing the word *conclusion* or *in short* inside a body paragraph telegraphs to the marker that the candidate has run out of ideas — it silently caps your Organisation score at Band 3.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students refining for Band 5.

#### Edge Cases and Boundary Conditions

Not every MUET essay paragraph accepts vanilla PEEL. Skilled writers adapt the order without losing the four moves.

- **Argumentative Task 2** — use straight PEEL; do not invert the order.
- **Discursive Task 2 (For/Against)** — the *Against* paragraph can flip the structure to **Point → Counter-Evidence → Rebuttal-Explanation → Link**, which still satisfies all four PEEL moves in substance.
- **Narrative/descriptive Task 1** — PEEL becomes **Point → Descriptive detail → Reflection → Link**, because Evidence is replaced by sensory or narrative detail.
- **Cause-and-effect prompts** — the Explanation step must show a *mechanism*, not just repeat the cause.

#### Advanced Traps Candidates Miss

1. **Quantifying Explanation is the band-mover.** Sentences like "this shows that social media is harmful" repeat the Point; "this reduces the time adolescents spend on critical cross-checking of sources, per the MOH 2023 framework" demonstrates *analysis*.
2. **Link sentence function matters.** A Link must either **return** to the thesis or **forward** to the next paragraph. A Link that only re-states the Point wastes its slot.
3. **Discourse-marker placement.** Place *consequently*, *therefore*, or *in contrast* at the **start** of the Link sentence so the marker instantly recognises logical flow.
4. **One Point per paragraph.** Two competing Points in one paragraph split the Explanation budget and signal poor planning.
5. **Link must not contradict the Point.** If your Point says X is harmful but your Link implies X is beneficial, cohesion breaks and the marker caps the band.

#### Cross-Connections and Adjacent Topics

- **Cohesion vs. coherence** — PEEL produces *coherence* (logical sense); discourse markers produce *cohesion* (grammatical connection). MUET awards both under Organisation.
- **Thesis statement drafting** — a vague thesis makes every Link sentence vague. Practice pairing PEEL with the *three-prong thesis* (claim + reason + counter-acknowledgement).
- **Lexical resource** — varying your discourse markers across paragraphs (*Consequently, In contrast, Admittedly*) raises the Language band independently of PEEL.

#### Two Advanced Practice Prompts

1. Write a 120-word PEEL paragraph on "Online learning narrows the rural-urban education gap in Malaysia," using one statistic from a Malaysian Ministry of Education source and ending with a forward-link to a paragraph on "limitations of broadband access."
2. Rewrite the following paragraph by swapping any structural errors you spot, then justify in one sentence why each move earned or lost a band: *"Many people say volunteering is good. Studies show volunteers are happier. This proves volunteering helps mental health. Volunteering is therefore good for Malaysia."*
