# XII. Patterns in the Chemical World — HKDSE Chemistry Notes

This topic covers Topic XII of the Compulsory Part of HKDSE Chemistry. It addresses the variation of properties across a period and down a group; periodicity and chemical bonding; selected inorganic chemistry of Group I, Group VII, transition metals and noble gases; and selected organic chemistry of plastics, detergents and pharmaceuticals.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Periodicity**: trends across periods and down groups (atomic radius, ionisation energy, electronegativity, melting point, reactivity).
- **Across a period (left to right)**: atomic radius decreases; ionisation energy generally increases (with some exceptions at group 2-13 and 15-16); electronegativity increases; reactivity of metals decreases; reactivity of non-metals increases.
- **Down a group**: atomic radius increases; ionisation energy decreases; electronegativity decreases; reactivity of metals increases (Groups 1, 2); reactivity of non-metals decreases (Group 17).
- **Group 1 (alkali metals)**: soft, low melting points, react with water to form hydroxides + H₂, form ionic compounds with non-metals.
- **Group 17 (halogens)**: diatomic molecules (F₂, Cl₂, Br₂, I₂); oxidising agents; reactivity decreases down the group.
- **Noble gases**: Group 18 (He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe, Rn); inert (generally unreactive due to full valence shells).
- **Transition metals**: d-block metals; variable oxidation states; form coloured compounds; act as catalysts; form complex ions.
- **Plastics**: synthetic polymers (Topic XI); thermoplastics, thermosets, elastomers.
- **Detergents**: surfactants — long hydrocarbon tail (hydrophobic) + polar head (hydrophilic). Soap (carboxylate) vs. synthetic detergents (sulfonate).
- **Pharmaceuticals**: aspirin (analgesic), paracetamol (analgesic and antipyretic), penicillin (antibiotic), ibuprofen (anti-inflammatory).

#### Examiner traps

- Treating "transition metal" as synonymous with "d-block metal" — strictly, transition metals are those with partially filled d subshells in the ground state or in common oxidation states.
- Confusing Group 1 reactivity trend (Li most reactive, going down to Cs actually more reactive due to lower ionisation energy) with the standard "more reactive as you go down" rule.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Group 1 — alkali metals

Properties:

- Soft, silvery, low density.
- Low melting and boiling points (decreasing down the group).
- Reactivity with water increases down the group: 2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂.
- Reactivity with oxygen: 4Li + O₂ → 2Li₂O; Na forms Na₂O₂ (peroxide); K, Rb, Cs form KO₂ (superoxide).
- Form ionic compounds (with non-metals) that are soluble in water.

#### Group 17 — halogens

Properties:

- Diatomic molecules (X₂).
- Physical state: F₂ (yellow gas), Cl₂ (green-yellow gas), Br₂ (red-brown liquid), I₂ (purple solid).
- Reactivity decreases down the group.
- Strong oxidising agents.
- React with alkali metals: 2Na + Cl₂ → 2NaCl.
- React with hydrogen: H₂ + Cl₂ → 2HCl.
- Halide ions (Cl⁻, Br⁻, I⁻) are reducing agents; their reducing power increases down the group.

#### Noble gases

Properties:

- Monoatomic gases (He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe, Rn).
- Colourless, odourless, inert (very low reactivity).
- Used in lighting (Ne, Ar), welding (Ar), balloons (He), and as inert atmospheres for chemical reactions.
- Xenon forms a few compounds (e.g. XeF₂, XeF₄, XeF₆) with very strong oxidising agents.

#### Transition metals

Properties:

- D-block metals.
- Variable oxidation states (e.g. Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺; Cu⁺ and Cu²⁺; Mn²⁺, Mn⁴⁺, Mn⁷⁺).
- Form coloured compounds (e.g. Cu²⁺ is blue; Fe³⁺ is yellow-brown; Cr³⁺ is green).
- Form complex ions (e.g. [Cu(NH₃)₄]²⁺ is deep blue; [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ is colourless).
- Act as catalysts (Fe in Haber process; Pt/Pd/Rh in catalytic converters; MnO₂ in H₂O₂ decomposition).

#### Selected organic chemistry

**Plastics**:

- Addition polymers: poly(ethene) (PE), poly(propene) (PP), poly(vinyl chloride) (PVC), poly(styrene) (PS), PTFE.
- Condensation polymers: polyamides (nylon), polyesters (Terylene).
- Properties vary: density, hardness, flexibility, melting point, chemical resistance.

**Detergents**:

- Soap: sodium or potassium salts of long-chain fatty acids (from hydrolysis of fats/oils with NaOH or KOH).
- Synthetic detergents: sodium salts of long-chain alkylbenzene sulfonic acids (LAS).
- Mechanism of cleaning: surfactants lower the surface tension of water; the hydrophobic tail dissolves grease, the hydrophilic head dissolves in water.

**Pharmaceuticals**:

- Aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid): analgesic, antipyretic, anti-inflammatory. Inhibits prostaglandin synthesis by inhibiting cyclooxygenase.
- Paracetamol (acetaminophen): analgesic and antipyretic. Mechanism similar to aspirin (COX inhibition) but less anti-inflammatory effect.
- Penicillin: antibiotic discovered by Alexander Fleming (1928). Inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis by inhibiting transpeptidase. Bacterial resistance is a growing problem.
- Ibuprofen: anti-inflammatory. Inhibits COX-1 and COX-2.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-topic links

- **II. Microscopic World I**: periodicity is explained by atomic structure.
- **VII. Redox reactions, chemical cells and electrolysis**: transition metal variable oxidation states underpin electrochemistry.
- **XIII. Industrial Chemistry (Elective)**: industrial production of polymers, detergents and pharmaceuticals.

#### Exam technique

- **For periodicity questions**: state the trend and explain it in terms of atomic structure (number of shells, nuclear charge, shielding).
- **For Group 1/17 questions**: state the trend, give specific reactions with water water (Group 1) or as oxidising agents (Group 17).
- **For transition metal questions**: cite specific examples of variable oxidation states, coloured compounds, complex ions, and catalytic activity.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Group 1 reactivity increases as you go down." Correct: down the group, atomic radius increases, ionisation energy decreases, so the metal is more easily oxidised.
- "Noble gases are completely inert." They are mostly inert but xenon forms compounds with very strong oxidising agents (e.g. XeF₂, XeF₄).
- "All transition metal compounds are coloured." Most are, but some are colourless (e.g. Zn²⁺ is colourless because d¹⁰ configuration gives no d-d transitions).
