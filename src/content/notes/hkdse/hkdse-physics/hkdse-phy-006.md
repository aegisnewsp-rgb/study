# VI. Astronomy and Space Science (Elective) — HKDSE Physics Notes

This topic covers Elective Topic VI of the HKDSE Physics Elective Part. Candidates sit any two of the four electives on Paper 2. The topic addresses the universe as seen in different scales (celestial coordinates, distance measurement); orbital motions under gravity (Kepler's laws, satellites, escape velocity); and stars and the universe (stellar classification, the Hertzsprung-Russell diagram, stellar evolution, the Big Bang and cosmology).

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **The universe in scale**: from Earth to the observable universe, spanning more than 40 orders of magnitude in size.
- **Celestial sphere**: an imaginary sphere with the observer at the centre; celestial objects appear projected on its surface.
- **Coordinate systems**: the equatorial system uses right ascension (RA) and declination (Dec), analogous to longitude and latitude.
- **Distance measurement**:
  - Astronomical Unit (AU):): 1 AU ≈ 1.5 × 10¹¹ m, the average Earth-Sun distance.
  - Light-year (ly):): the distance light travels in one year, ~9.46 × 10¹⁵ m.
  - Parsec (pc):): 1 pc ≈ 3.26 ly, defined as the distance at which 1 AU subtends 1 arcsecond.
- **Kepler's three laws**: 1. The orbit of a planet is an ellipse with the Sun at one focus. 2. The line joining a planet to the Sun sweeps out equal areas in equal times. 3. The square of the orbital period is proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis (T² ∝ a³).
- **Newton's law of gravitation**: F = Gm₁m₂/r².
- **Circular orbit**: v = √(GM/r); T = 2π√(r³/GM).
- **Escape velocity**: v_esc = √(2GM/r). At Earth's surface, v_esc ≈ 11.2 km/s.
- **Stellar classification**: by spectral type (O, B, A, F, G, K, M) and luminosity class (I-V).
- **Hertzsprung-Russell (H-R) diagram**: plot of luminosity vs. temperature (or spectral type). Most stars are on the main sequence.
- **Stellar evolution**: nebula → protostar → main sequence → red giant/supergiant → planetary nebula/supernova → white dwarf/neutron star/black hole.
- **Big Bang theory**: the universe began ~13.8 billion years ago from a hot hot state; the universe is expanding (Hubble's law); evidence includes cosmic microwave background radiation, abundance of light elements, galaxy distribution.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing mass and weight — mass is constant; weight is the gravitational force on an object.
- Treating the H-R diagram as a position-versus-time chart.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Celestial coordinates

The equatorial system uses right ascension (RA, measured eastward along the celestial equator from the vernal equinox, in hours from 0 to 24) and declination (Dec, measured north/south of the celestial equator, in degrees from -90° to +90°). The hour angle (HA) is the angle between the celestial meridian and the object.

#### Distance measurement

**Trigonometric parallax**: measure the apparent shift of a nearby star against the background as Earth orbits the Sun. The distance d = 1 AU / tan(p), where p is the parallax angle. The parsec is defined as the distance at which p = 1 arcsecond.

**Standard candles**: objects of known luminosity used to determine distance. Cepheid variable stars (period-luminosity relation), Type Ia supernovae.

**Redshift**: distant galaxies show redshifted spectral lines (Hubble's law: v = H₀d). The farther the galaxy, the greater the recession velocity.

#### Orbital mechanics

**Kepler's laws**:

1. **First law**: planets move in ellipses with the Sun at one focus.
2. **Second law**: equal areas in equal times (planets move faster near perihelion, slower near aphelion).
3. **Third law**: T² ∝ a³ (for objects orbiting the same central body). T²/a³ = 4π²/(GM).

**Circular orbit speed and period**: for circular orbit around mass M at radius r:

- v = √(GM/r)
- T = 2π√(r³/GM)

**Escape velocity**: v_esc = √(2GM/r). At Earth's surface (r = R_Earth ≈ 6,370 km), v_esc ≈ 11.2 km/s. To escape the Solar System from Earth's orbit, v_esc from the Sun ≈ 42 km/s.

**Satellites**: geosynchronous orbit (T = 24 hours, above the equator), low Earth orbit (~500 km altitude, ~90 minute period). GPS satellites orbit at ~20,200 km altitude with a 12-hour period.

#### Stellar properties

**Luminosity L**: the total energy radiated per per unit time (W). L = 4πR²σT⁴ (Stefan-Boltzmann law).

**Spectral classification**: by surface temperature. Order: O (hottest, blue, > 30,000 K) > B > A > F > G (Sun, ~5,800 K) > K > M (coolest, red, < 3,500 K). Mnemonic: "Oh Be A Fine Girl/Guy Kiss Me".

**Hertzsprung-Russell diagram**: L vs. T. Main sequence is a band from upper-left (hot, luminous) to lower-right (cool, faint). Giants and supergiants are upper-right; white dwarfs are lower-left.

**Stellar evolution**:

- **Low-mass stars (≤ 8 M_sun)**: main sequence → red giant → planetary nebula → white dwarf.
- **High-mass stars (> 8 M_sun)**: main sequence → red supergiant → supernova → neutron star or black hole.

The Sun is ~4.6 × 10⁹ years old, midway through its ~10 billion-year main sequence lifetime.

#### Cosmology

**Big Bang theory**: the universe began ~13.8 billion years ago from a hot, dense state and has been expanding ever since.

**Evidence**: cosmic microwave background (CMB) radiation (the afterglow of the Big Bang), abundance of light elements (H, He), Hubble's law, large-scale structure of galaxies.

**Hubble's law**: v = H₀ × d (recession velocity is proportional to distance). H₀ ≈ 70 km/s/Mpc.

**Critical density and fate**: if the average density of the universe equals the critical density (Ω = 1), the universe is flat and will expand forever but at a decelerating rate. If Ω > 1 (closed), the universe will eventually recollapse. If Ω < 1 (open), the universe will expand forever. Current measurements suggest Ω ≈ 1 with dark energy dominating.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-topic links

- **II. Force and Motion**: gravitational force, circular motion, escape velocity.
- **V. Radioactivity and Nuclear Energy**: stellar nucleosynthesis.
- **VII. Atomic World (Elective)**: spectral lines of atoms.

#### Exam technique

- **For orbital mechanics questions**: apply Newton's gravitation law; use the appropriate formula for v or T.
- **For stellar evolution questions**: trace the life cycle; state the end state.
- **For cosmology questions**: cite evidence; apply Hubble's law.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "The Big Bang was an explosion." The Big Bang is the expansion of space itself, not an explosion in pre-existing space.
- "Light-years measure time." Light-years measure distance.
- "Stars twinkle because they flicker." Stars twinkle because of atmospheric turbulence, not because of intrinsic variation.
