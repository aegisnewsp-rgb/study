# Excretory

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> What the examiner expects in one glance: three steps of urine formation, the nephron's labelled parts, and the hormones that tune it.

**Excretion** in humans is the removal of nitrogenous waste (chiefly **urea**) plus the homeostatic balancing of water, salts and pH. The functional unit is the **nephron**, of which each kidney holds roughly a million. Three processes build urine:

1. **Glomerular filtration** at the renal corpuscle.
2. **Tubular reabsorption** along the PCT, Loop of Henle, DCT and collecting duct.
3. **Tubular secretion** from peritubular blood into the tubule.

Net Filtration Pressure (NFP) drives step 1. **NFP = (P_GC − P_BS) − (π_GC − π_BS)**, where P_GC ≈ 55 mmHg, P_BS ≈ 15 mmHg, π_GC ≈ 30 mmHg, π_BS ≈ 0 mmHg; NFP ≈ 10 mmHg.

Clearance of any substance: **C = (U × V) / P** (U = urine concentration, V = urine flow mL/min, P = plasma concentration; result mL/min).

> 💡 **Memory Hook (PAC-GFR):** **P**ressure in **A**fferent > **C**onstricted **e**fferent → high glomerular capillary pressure → **G**lomerular **F**iltration **R**ate. "A in, E out, but A is wider."

| Tubule segment | What it does | Key transporter / effect |
|---|---|---|
| PCT | Bulk reabsorption of Na⁺, glucose, amino acids, HCO₃⁻ | Na⁺-glucose cotransporter (SGLT) |
| Loop of Henle | Builds medullary osmotic gradient (counter-current) | Na⁺-K⁺-2Cl⁻ in thick ascending limb |
| DCT | Fine-tuning of Na⁺/K⁺; aldosterone target | Na⁺ reabsorption, K⁺ secretion |
| Collecting duct | ADH-controlled water reabsorption; final concentration | Aquaporin insertion by vasopressin |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Anatomy and the Nephron

A pair of bean-shaped **kidneys** drain via **ureters** into a **urinary bladder**, then out through the **urethra**. Section any kidney and you see an outer **cortex** (renal corpuscles, PCT, DCT) and an inner **medulla** (Loops of Henle, collecting ducts arranged as pyramids). Each nephron begins with a **glomerulus** inside **Bowman's capsule** — together, the **renal corpuscle** — and continues as the PCT, Loop of Henle, DCT and collecting duct. The **juxtaglomerular apparatus (JGA)** sits where the DCT touches its afferent arteriole; it secretes **renin** when renal pressure drops.

#### Three steps of urine formation

Glomerular filtration is a passive ultrafiltration. Blood enters through the wider **afferent arteriole** and exits through the narrower **efferent arteriole**, so the glomerular capillary bed runs at high hydrostatic pressure (~55 mmHg). The **filtration membrane** (fenestrated endothelium, basement membrane, podocyte slit diaphragms) excludes plasma proteins but lets water, ions and small solutes through. Around 180 L of filtrate form per day; ~99% is reclaimed later, leaving ~1.5 L of urine.

Tubular reabsorption recovers useful solutes. The PCT reabsorbs ~70% of filtered Na⁺, all filtered glucose (up to the **renal threshold**, ~180 mg/dL blood glucose), all amino acids and most HCO₃⁻. The Loop of Henle's counter-current flow, with active NaCl pumping in the thick ascending limb and water outflow in the thin descending limb, builds a medullary gradient of up to ~1200 mOsmol/L. ADH-controlled water reabsorption in the collecting duct uses this gradient to concentrate urine.

Tubular secretion adds waste to the forming urine — H⁺, K⁺, NH₃, drugs like PAH and penicillin — clearing substances the filter missed.

#### Concept matrix — easy-to-confuse pairs

| Confusion | Correct answer |
|---|---|
| Cortex vs medulla | Cortex = renal corpuscles + convoluted tubules; Medulla = Loops of Henle + collecting ducts |
| Ammonotelic vs ureotelic vs uricotelic | Ammonotelic = aquatic animals (NH₃); ureotelic = mammals (urea); uricotelic = reptiles & birds (uric acid) |
| Afferent vs efferent arteriole | Afferent is **wider** → maintains high glomerular pressure; efferent is narrower |
| ADH vs aldosterone | ADH = water reabsorption (DCT/CD); aldosterone = Na⁺ reabsorption + K⁺ secretion (DCT/CD) |
| Renin vs ANF | Renin (from JGA) raises BP via RAAS; ANF (from atria) opposes RAAS, lowers BP |
| Micturition reflex vs voluntary control | Reflex is spinal (sacral); voluntary override is via external urethral sphincter |

#### Hormonal regulation summary

- **ADH (vasopressin)** — released from posterior pituitary when osmoreceptors detect low water; inserts **AQP-2** channels in CD, raising water reabsorption.
- **Aldosterone** — from adrenal cortex via RAAS; boosts Na⁺/K⁺ exchange in DCT/CD.
- **ANF (atrial natriuretic factor)** — released by atrial stretch; inhibits RAAS, dilates afferent arteriole, raises GFR, drives Na⁺ loss.

#### Worked exam-level problem

A clinical lab injects inulin continuously into a patient and measures: urine inulin concentration = 12 mg/mL, plasma inulin concentration = 0.24 mg/mL, urine flow rate = 1.5 mL/min. Compute GFR.

Step 1 — apply the clearance equation for inulin: GFR = (U_in × V) / P_in.
Step 2 — substitute: GFR = (12 mg/mL × 1.5 mL/min) / 0.24 mg/mL.
Step 3 — calculate the numerator: 12 × 1.5 = 18 mg/min.
Step 4 — divide: 18 / 0.24 = **75 mL/min** (note: this is one kidney; the two-kidney GFR is ~125 mL/min, a figure that appears in NCERT; if your working gives 75, you have used only one kidney's data — read the question stem carefully).

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** students often write "125 mL/min" by rote. If the question gives data for a single kidney, the answer is half of the textbook total. Also, do **not** use PAH data to compute GFR — PAH clearance gives RPF, not GFR, because PAH is also secreted.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Most exam traps here are either numerical or comparative; practise the boundaries, not the slogans.

#### Numerical and conceptual edge cases

1. **Filtration fraction (FF) = GFR / RPF.** Normal ≈ 0.20. A fall in RPF without a matching fall in GFR raises FF and signals efferent arteriole constriction; a rise in GFR alone lowers FF.
2. **Transport maxima and renal threshold.** Glucose reabsorption in the PCT is carrier-mediated and saturable; once plasma glucose exceeds ~180 mg/dL, carriers max out and glycosuria appears — this is the **renal threshold**, not the same as the plasma concentration at which transporters fully saturate (which is higher, ~375 mg/dL).
3. **Ammonia trapping.** The DCT secretes NH₃ (made from glutamine deamination in PCT cells); once in the acidified tubular fluid, NH₃ binds H⁺ → NH₄⁺, which cannot diffuse back. This is why acidification and ammonium excretion are coupled.
4. **Counter-current multiplier limits.** The thick ascending limb cannot concentrate urine on its own — it only sets up the gradient. The collecting duct (under ADH) uses that gradient. Without ADH, urine stays hypotonic (~100 mOsmol/L), as in diabetes insipidus.
5. **Other excretory routes.** Lungs exhale CO₂ + water vapour, skin loses water + salts + urea in sweat, liver excretes bile pigments (bilirubin, biliverdin) via faeces. Faecal egestion is **not** excretion because material never entered body cells.
6. **Failure states.** Acute glomerulonephritis drops GFR and causes oliguria; chronic failure leads to uraemia and is treated by **haemodialysis** (counter-current exchange across a semi-permeable membrane against a balanced dialysate) or peritoneal dialysis.

#### Comparison — modes of nitrogenous waste excretion

| Feature | Ammonotelic | Ureotelic | Uricotelic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main waste | NH₃ | Urea (NH₂–CO–NH₂) | Uric acid |
| Toxicity | High | Moderate | Low |
| Water needed | Very high | Moderate | Very low (paste) |
| Example organisms | Fish, protozoans | Mammals, amphibians | Reptiles, birds, insects |
| Energy cost | Low | Moderate | High (synthesis) |
| Habitat link | Aquatic | Semi-aquatic to terrestrial | Arid / terrestrial |

#### Advanced practice prompts

1. **Numerical:** Inulin clearance in a patient is 124 mL/min and PAH clearance is 620 mL/min. If haematocrit is 0.45, calculate RBF and filtration fraction. (FF = GFR/RPF = 124/620 ≈ 0.20; RBF = RPF/(1 − Hct) = 620 / 0.55 ≈ 1127 mL/min.)
2. **Conceptual:** Predict how a loop diuretic (which blocks the Na⁺-K⁺-2Cl⁻ cotransporter) would alter the medullary osmotic gradient, urine volume and ADH requirement. (It collapses the gradient, raises urine volume, and lowers ADH demand for a given plasma osmolality.)
