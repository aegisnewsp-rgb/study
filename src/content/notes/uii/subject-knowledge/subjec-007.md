# Question format and scoring

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The seven clusters inside **UI Entrance Subject Knowledge — Topic 7 (Advanced English)** are: main idea identification, inference, vocabulary-in-context, error identification, sentence completion, tense consistency, and idiomatic usage. The subtest sits inside the **SIMAK UI Kemampuan Dasar / Bahasa Inggris** paper and targets **CEFR C1** reading-analytic skills, not grammar recall.

| Cluster | What the stem asks | Fast rule |
|---|---|---|
| Main idea | Single best summary sentence | Find the recurring noun phrase, ignore examples |
| Inference | Conclusion supported but not stated | Must be *logically forced*, not merely plausible |
| Vocabulary | Word meaning in context | Replace the word — if the sentence breaks, drop it |
| Error ID | Underlined fragment with 4 options | Four families: SVA, parallel, pronoun, tense |

Three highest-yield traps: **EXCEPT** wording, look-alike distractors, and idiom literal-translation errors.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Question format and scoring

SIMAK UI Subject Knowledge blends **multiple-choice reading comprehension, error identification, and sentence-completion** items, each with four options and exactly one correct answer. The paper contributes roughly **3%** of the total weighted score, but English items routinely appear across other subtests, so Topic 7 performance cascades. Negative marking is generally absent on Kemampuan Dasar sections, so attempting every item is rational.

#### The four error families examiners exploit

| Family | Typical trigger | Diagnostic clue |
|---|---|---|
| Subject–verb agreement | Inverted subject, collective nouns, indefinite pronouns | Locate the *real* subject, ignore prepositional phrases |
| Parallel structure | Correlative conjunctions | Match forms after **both…and, either…or, not only…but also** |
| Pronoun–antecedent | Ambiguous or shifted reference | Antecedent must agree in number and gender |
| Tense consistency | Mixed time signals in one paragraph | Anchor on the *dominant* time frame first |

#### Reading strategy: skim then scan

For long passages (≈300–500 words), read the first and last sentence of each paragraph to build a topic skeleton in under 90 seconds, then scan for the specific detail the stem requests. Inference questions reward **text-anchored conclusions** — if the passage does not contain the supporting evidence, the option is wrong even if it sounds reasonable.

> **Tip:** Treat every “NOT true” and “EXCEPT” stem as a four-way search for the single true statement; mark it, then pick the opposite.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Mechanism behind the C1 difficulty calibration

SIMAK UI passages are drawn from editorial and academic prose with **lexical density above 15%** (low-frequency words per 100 tokens) and **mean clause length near 18 words**, placing them at CEFR C1 on the **Cambridge text-profile scale**. Items therefore probe *collocation* and *register* (formal vs academic vs neutral) rather than dictionary definitions. A word like **“substantial”** in an economic-passage context means *large in degree*, not *physical mass* — a distinction the four-option list exploits by offering a dictionary match that breaks the collocation.

#### Edge cases worth drilling

- **Reduced relative clauses** (*the method developed by…*) where the relative pronoun is omitted — examiners hide SVA errors in the post-modifier.
- **Not only X but also Y** — if X and Y differ in grammatical form, the sentence is wrong regardless of meaning.
- **Idioms with opaque etymology**: *hit the books* (study), *break the ice* (start conversation), *cost an arm and a leg* (expensive). Always decode idiom meanings as fixed units.
- **Shifted pronoun reference** across two clauses — Indonesian writers often default to *dia* regardless of gender, so UI items test English *he/she/they* discipline.

#### Common mistakes mapped to score loss

| Mistake | Approx. cost | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Translating the stem into Indonesian | 1–2 min lost per item, drift on inference | Read in English, summarise in English |
| Choosing a look-alike word | Most-frequent wrong option | Re-read the sentence with your pick substituted |
| Confusing EXCEPT / NOT | Guaranteed wrong answer on that item | Underline the polarity word before reading options |
| Treating every detail as the main idea | −1 to −2 per passage | Main idea is *general*, details are *specific* |

#### Two practice prompts

1. **Inference drill:** Read a 250-word op-ed on urban transit, then write two inferences and one *plausible-but-unsupported* claim. Verify the third is not stated or implied.
2. **Error-ID drill:** From any past SIMAK UI paper, isolate all four error families and classify each underlined fragment before peeking at the key — train pattern recognition, not memorisation.

> **Strategy note:** Because Topic 7 carries only ~3% weight, spend at most **8–10 hours** of total prep here; over-investing crowds out higher-weight sections.
