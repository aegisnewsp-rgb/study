# Krebs Cycle

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> The single thing that decides your FMGE score on this topic is whether you can place every enzyme, coenzyme and carbon loss in the right order.

The Krebs cycle (citric acid cycle / TCA cycle) runs inside the **mitochondrial matrix** and oxidises the 2-carbon acetyl group of acetyl-CoA into **two molecules of CO₂**, releasing electrons onto **3 NADH + 1 FADH₂** and forming **1 GTP** per acetyl unit. One full turn delivers the reducing equivalents that the electron transport chain later converts to ≈10 ATP under modern P/O ratios (2.5 ATP/NADH, 1.5 ATP/FADH₂).

#### Memory Hook — Citrate Is Krebs' Starting Substrate

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** The word **"Can I Keep Selling Sex For Money, Officer?"** maps onto the eight intermediates in sequence: **C**itrate → **I**socitrate → (α-)**K**etoglutarate → **S**uccinyl-CoA → **S**uccinate → **F**umarate → **M**alate → **O**xaloacetate. The capital letters **C, I, K, S, S, F, M, O** are the only eight intermediates you must write on the answer sheet.

| Output per acetyl-CoA | Modern (P/O 2.5/1.5) | Legacy (3/2) |
|---|---|---|
| NADH | 3 | 3 |
| FADH₂ | 1 | 1 |
| GTP | 1 | 1 |
| ATP equivalent | ≈10 | 12 |
| CO₂ released | 2 | 2 |

Two CO₂ leaves the cycle; the carbons that started in glucose have now fully exited as gas.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> The Krebs cycle is the metabolic crossroad where carbohydrate, fat and protein catabolism converge — every FMGE question on this topic reduces to "which enzyme, which cofactor, which yield, which regulation?"

#### Why the cycle is amphibolic, not just catabolic

The pathway supplies precursors outward as well as oxidising acetyl-CoA inward. **Oxaloacetate** drains into gluconeogenesis, **α-ketoglutarate** and **oxaloacetate** feed amino-acid biosynthesis (glutamate, aspartate), **succinyl-CoA** enters heme synthesis, and **citrate** leaves the matrix to deliver acetyl groups for fatty-acid synthesis in the cytosol. Anaplerotic reactions — chiefly **pyruvate carboxylase** (biotin-dependent) — refill oxaloacetate when intermediates are pulled out.

#### The eight steps and their catalytic logic

| Step | Substrate → Product | Enzyme | Cofactor / Product |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Acetyl-CoA + OAA → Citrate | Citrate synthase | CoA-SH released |
| 2 | Citrate → Isocitrate | Aconitase | Fe-S cluster |
| 3 | Isocitrate → α-Ketoglutarate + CO₂ | Isocitrate dehydrogenase | NAD⁺ → NADH (rate-limiting) |
| 4 | α-KG → Succinyl-CoA + CO₂ | α-Ketoglutarate dehydrogenase | NAD⁺ → NADH; requires TPP, lipoate, FAD, CoA |
| 5 | Succinyl-CoA → Succinate | Succinyl-CoA synthetase | GDP + Pi → GTP (substrate-level phosphorylation) |
| 6 | Succinate → Fumarate | Succinate dehydrogenase | FAD → FADH₂ (Complex II, inner membrane) |
| 7 | Fumarate → Malate | Fumarase | H₂O added |
| 8 | Malate → OAA | Malate dehydrogenase | NAD⁺ → NADH |

Two decarboxylations (steps 3 and 4) discharge the carbons originally supplied by acetyl-CoA; three NADH (steps 3, 4, 8) and one FADH₂ (step 6) feed the ETC; one GTP (step 5) carries a high-energy phosphate equivalent to ATP.

#### Vitamin cofactors the examiners love to ask about

- **Thiamine (B1)** → TPP, used by α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase (and pyruvate dehydrogenase). Deficiency (beriberi, Wernicke-Korsakoff) blocks the cycle at step 4.
- **Riboflavin (B2)** → FAD, used by succinate dehydrogenase and by the α-KG dehydrogenase dihydrolipoamide dehydrogenase subunit.
- **Niacin (B3)** → NAD⁺, used in steps 3, 4, 8.
- **Pantothenic acid (B5)** → CoA, the carrier that brings acetyl groups into step 1 and carries succinyl through step 5.

#### Worked resolution of a typical FMGE stem

A stem describes a chronic alcoholic on a polished-rice diet who develops lactic acidosis and confusion. Neurologically, the syndrome is Wernicke encephalopathy; biochemically, the question targets the cofactor lost when thiamine is absent. The cycle stalls at **step 4** because α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase cannot function without **TPP**. Pyruvate then backs up into lactate via anaerobic glycolysis, explaining the lactic acidosis.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students answer "pyruvate dehydrogenase is inhibited" — true, but the FMGE option that scores the mark is the *Krebs* enzyme blocked, which is **α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase**, the only TCA enzyme that uses the same five cofactors (TPP, lipoate, FAD, NAD, CoA) as PDH. Pyruvate dehydrogenase is not part of the cycle.

#### Rate control and regulation

The primary regulated enzyme is **isocitrate dehydrogenase (step 3)** — activated by **ADP** and **Ca²⁺** (the muscle-contraction signal), inhibited by **ATP** and **NADH**. Citrate synthase (step 1) and α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase (step 4) act as secondary control points, both suppressed by their own downstream products (NADH, ATP, succinyl-CoA). High NADH/NAD⁺ or ATP/ADP ratios signal "stop oxidising"; falling energy charge plus rising Ca²⁺ in contracting muscle says "go".

#### Compartmentalisation point that recurs in MCQs

Seven of the eight enzymes float freely in the **mitochondrial matrix**. The exception is **succinate dehydrogenase**, which is embedded in the **inner mitochondrial membrane as Complex II** of the ETC — this is why its product (FADH₂) does not need a shuttle and enters the chain directly at ubiquinone.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> The 3% weight in FMGE hides the fact that two of the three MCQs you will see are regulation/enzyme-location traps, not recall questions.

#### Energy yield — why the legacy 12 ATP is obsolete

Modern bioenergetics uses **2.5 ATP per NADH** and **1.5 ATP per FADH₂** because the H⁺/ATP stoichiometry of the F₁F₀-ATP synthase is closer to 2.7 and the cost of transporting cytoplasmic NADH via the glycerol-phosphate shuttle (used in skeletal muscle and brain) is 1.5 ATP rather than the historical 3.0. The arithmetic then becomes 3×2.5 + 1×1.5 + 1×1 = **10 ATP per acetyl-CoA**. For one glucose (two acetyl-CoA) the cycle contributes 20 ATP; add glycolysis (net 2 ATP) and the pyruvate-dehydrogenase step (2 NADH ≈ 5 ATP) for the aerobic total of ~30–32 ATP, depending on the shuttle.

#### Anaplerosis vs cataplerosis — what controls the OAA pool

- **Anaplerotic input:** pyruvate carboxylase (OAA from pyruvate, requires acetyl-CoA as allosteric activator and biotin as cofactor), PEPCK running in reverse, aspartate and glutamate transamination feeding OAA and α-KG.
- **Cataplerotic drain:** OAA → PEP for gluconeogenesis during fasting; α-KG → glutamate (ammonia detoxification); succinyl-CoA → δ-aminolevulinate → heme; citrate → cytosolic acetyl-CoA for lipogenesis.

When cataplerosis exceeds anaplerosis, the cycle stalls because oxaloacetate is the acceptor for acetyl-CoA. Clinically, this is why prolonged starvation or uncontrolled diabetes (oxaloacetate diverted to gluconeogenesis) causes lactic acidosis despite normal oxygen delivery.

#### Inherited and toxic blocks you may see in vignettes

| Lesion | Site | Clinical pearl |
|---|---|---|
| Arsenite (lipoyl poison) | Lipoamide cofactor of α-KG dehydrogenase | "Rice-water" vomiting mimics, mimics PDH block |
| Mercury iodoacetate | Sulphydryl groups of succinate dehydrogenase | Historically used to map glycolysis vs cycle |
| Malonate | Competitive inhibitor of succinate dehydrogenase | Classic Krebs-cycle inhibitor from biochemistry textbooks |
| Fluoroacetate | Aconitase (after conversion to fluorocitrate) | "1080" pesticide, lethal dose ≈ 5 mg/kg, citrate accumulates |
| Thiamine deficiency | α-KG dehydrogenase (and PDH) | Lactic acidosis, Wernicke encephalopathy |
| Leigh disease | PDH, α-KG dehydrogenase or Complex I/IV defects | Infantile lactic acidosis with bilateral basal-ganglia lesions |

#### Two advanced practice prompts

1. A patient is given **fluoroacetate**. Predict (a) which metabolite accumulates in the urine, (b) why citrate concentrations rise in the cytosol, and (c) the direction of the change in mitochondrial NADH/NAD⁺ ratio. (Answer skeleton: fluorocitrate blocks aconitase → citrate and fluorocitrate accumulate; upstream oxaloacetate and acetyl-CoA accumulate; downstream NADH production falls because the cycle cannot run, so the NADH/NAD⁺ ratio drops.)
2. **β-oxidation of palmitate (C16)** yields 8 acetyl-CoA. Calculate total Krebs-derived ATP from those acetyl-CoA units using the modern P/O values, and identify the two Krebs enzymes that require FAD-derived and NAD-derived vitamin cofactors, respectively. (Answer skeleton: 8 × 10 = 80 ATP from Krebs; succinate dehydrogenase uses FAD from riboflavin; isocitrate dehydrogenase uses NAD⁺ from niacin.)

> 📌 **Revision Anchor for the Cycle:** Eight intermediates, three NADH, one FADH₂, one GTP, two CO₂, one rate-limiting enzyme, one membrane-bound enzyme, four B-vitamin cofactors — write this column on rough paper before the bell rings.
