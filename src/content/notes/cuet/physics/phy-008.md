# Mechanical Properties

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your CUET UG Physics paper.

**Mechanical Properties of Matter** describes how solids deform when external forces act on them and how they respond once the load is removed. The two foundational quantities are **stress** (force per unit area) and **strain** (fractional change in dimension).

- **Stress** σ = F / A, units N/m² or Pa.
- **Strain** ε = ΔL / L, dimensionless.
- **Hooke's law**: σ = Y·ε, where Y is **Young's modulus** in Pa.
- Within the **elastic limit**, the body returns to its original shape; beyond the **yield point**, deformation becomes **plastic** and permanent.
- Ductile materials (copper, aluminium) show a long plastic region; brittle materials (glass, ceramics) fracture shortly after the elastic limit.

| Quantity | Formula | Unit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stress | σ = F / A | Pa |
| Longitudinal strain | ε = ΔL / L | none |
| Young's modulus | Y = σ / ε = FL / (A·ΔL) | Pa |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Stress, Strain and Hooke's Law

Stress is the internal restoring force per unit area developed inside a body when an external load is applied. Strain is the relative deformation produced by that stress. For a metallic wire stretched by force F along its length, σ = F/A and ε = ΔL/L. Within the proportional limit, stress is directly proportional to strain; this statement is **Hooke's law** and the constant of proportionality is Young's modulus Y.

#### The Three Elastic Moduli

Different modes of deformation require different moduli. **Young's modulus (Y)** governs longitudinal stretch, **bulk modulus (K)** governs uniform compression (K = −ΔP / (ΔV/V), with the negative sign because volume falls when pressure rises), and **shear modulus (G)** governs shape change under tangential force. These three are linked: Y = 3K(1 − 2ν) and Y = 2G(1 + ν), where ν is Poisson's ratio.

#### Stress–Strain Curve for a Ductile Metal

A typical ductile material passes through six stages: A→B proportional (Hooke's law holds), B→C elastic limit reached, C→D yielding begins, D→E strain hardening up to the **ultimate tensile strength (UTS)**, E→F necking, and finally fracture at F. Energy stored per unit volume in the elastic region equals ½·σ·ε = σ²/(2Y).

| Region of curve | Behaviour | Reversible? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A → B | Linear, Hooke's law valid | Yes |
| B → C | Elastic limit | Yes |
| C → D | Yielding, plastic flow begins | No |
| D → E | Strain hardening to UTS | No |
| E → F | Necking then break | No |

#### Exam Pointers for CUET UG

CUET UG (NCERT Class 11, Chapter 9) asks 1–2 questions per paper, most often a numerical on wire elongation ΔL = FL/(AY) and a concept MCQ on the stress–strain graph. Watch for the brittle-vs-ductile distinction and the sign convention in K.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Poisson's Ratio and Elastic Constants

When a rod is stretched, it lengthens along the axis and contracts laterally. **Poisson's ratio** ν = −(lateral strain) / (longitudinal strain). For stable isotropic solids, 0 < ν < 0.5; most metals lie in the range 0.2–0.4, while rubber approaches 0.5. The negative sign makes ν positive because longitudinal elongation produces lateral shrinkage. Compressibility, defined as C = 1/K, is the reciprocal of the bulk modulus and is useful in fluid problems.

#### Worked Numerical

A steel wire of length 2.0 m and cross-sectional area 4 × 10⁻⁶ m² is stretched by a 200 N load. Given Y = 2 × 10¹¹ Pa, find the elongation:
ΔL = FL / (AY) = (200 × 2.0) / (4 × 10⁻⁶ × 2 × 10¹¹) = 5 × 10⁻⁴ m = 0.5 mm.
The longitudinal strain is ΔL/L = 2.5 × 10⁻⁴, and the resulting stress is F/A = 5 × 10⁷ Pa.

#### Common Mistakes

- Dividing force by diameter instead of cross-sectional area; doubling the radius quadruples A, so stress falls by a factor of four.
- Dropping the minus sign in K = −ΔP / (ΔV/V); pressure up and volume down must yield a positive K.
- Treating Hooke's law as valid all the way to fracture; it fails past the proportional limit.
- Writing Young's modulus in newtons; strain is dimensionless, so Y must be in Pa (N/m²).
- Labelling the fracture point as the yield point on a stress–strain graph.

#### Practice Prompts

1. Two wires of the same material and length have radii in the ratio 1:2. Which carries more stress under the same axial load, and by what factor?
2. A copper rod (ν = 0.34) has its diameter measured as 10.000 mm before loading and 9.998 mm after a longitudinal strain of 8 × 10⁻⁴. Verify consistency and predict the lateral strain from ν.
