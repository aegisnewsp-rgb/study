# Motion in 1D

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Motion in one dimension** describes an object's changing position along a single straight-line axis (the x-axis) with respect to time. The four quantities that drive every problem are **displacement** (s, vector), **distance** (scalar), **velocity** (v = dx/dt, vector), and **acceleration** (a = dv/dt, vector).

For **uniformly accelerated motion** — the case examiners test 90% of the time — three equations govern everything:

- **v = u + at**
- **s = ut + ½ at²**
- **v² = u² + 2as**

where *u* is initial velocity, *v* is final velocity, *a* is constant acceleration, *s* is displacement, and *t* is elapsed time. A useful shortcut: the average velocity over a uniformly accelerated interval equals **(u + v)/2**. In free fall, *a = g ≈ 9.8 m/s²* directed downward along the chosen negative axis. **Relative velocity** in 1D follows **v_AB = v_A − v_B**.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Distance vs Displacement
**Distance** is the total path length covered — always positive, always scalar. **Displacement** is the straight-line change in position from start to end — a vector carrying both magnitude and sign along the chosen axis. A ball thrown up to 20 m and falling back to the launch point has *distance = 40 m* but *displacement = 0*. This distinction is the single most common CUET trap in this topic.

#### Average vs Instantaneous Quantities
**Average velocity** = total displacement ÷ total time, while **instantaneous velocity** is the limit as Δt → 0, written **v = dx/dt**. The same logic gives **a = dv/dt** for instantaneous acceleration. CUET MCQs frequently ask students to identify when an average value equals an instantaneous value — this happens only when the quantity is constant over the interval.

#### The Three Equations of Motion
Derived by integrating *a = constant*, these relations are **valid only under uniform acceleration**:

| Equation | Use it when… |
|---|---|
| v = u + at | time *t* is given or asked |
| s = ut + ½at² | displacement *s* is asked and *t* is known |
| v² = u² + 2as | time is absent from the problem |

A fourth useful identity: the displacement in the *n*th second is **sₙ = u + (a/2)(2n − 1)**.

#### Graphical Interpretation
- **Slope of x-t graph** = instantaneous velocity (signed).
- **Area under v-t graph** = displacement (signed area; negative area below the axis subtracts).
- **Slope of v-t graph** = acceleration.

A horizontal x-t segment means zero velocity, not zero distance traveled.

#### Free Fall
With upward as positive, g = −9.8 m/s². A stone thrown up at 20 m/s returns to the hand with v = −20 m/s; maximum height uses v² = u² + 2as with v = 0.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Worked Example: Ball Thrown Vertically Upward
A ball is launched at *u = 30 m/s*. Maximum height: 0 = (30)² + 2(−9.8)s → **s ≈ 45.9 m**. Time to apex: 0 = 30 − 9.8t → **t ≈ 3.06 s**. Total flight time back to launch level = 2t ≈ 6.12 s. Note that displacement at return = 0 but distance traveled ≈ 91.8 m.

#### Edge Cases and Common Mistakes
1. **Non-uniform acceleration** invalidates all three kinematic equations. If *a* depends on *x* or *v*, integration or energy methods are required.
2. **Sign of g**: keep −9.8 m/s² when up is positive; switch to +9.8 m/s² if down is positive. Mixing conventions is the #1 error.
3. **Average speed vs |average velocity|**: average speed = total distance ÷ total time, which differs from |displacement/time| whenever the object reverses direction.
4. **Relative velocity sign**: v_AB = v_A − v_B. Two cars each at 60 km/h toward each other approach at 120 km/h, not 0.

#### Connections to Later Topics
Motion in 1D is the scaffold for **Motion in a Plane** (vector decomposition), **Laws of Motion** (force = ma), and **Work-Energy** (W = ½mv² − ½mu²). Mastering sign conventions here prevents errors throughout mechanics.

#### Practice Prompts
1. A train decelerates uniformly from 72 km/h to rest over 50 m. Find the stopping time using v = u + at and v² = u² + 2as, and verify consistency.
2. From the v-t graph of an object moving with v(t) = 4 − 2t, compute displacement from t = 0 to t = 3 s using the area method and confirm with integration.
