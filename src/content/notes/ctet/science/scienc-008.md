# Natural Resources and Environment

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your CTET Science/EVS paper.

- **Natural resources** are biotic (flora, fauna) or abiotic (land, water, air, minerals) materials drawn from the environment to meet human needs.
- **Renewable** resources (solar, wind, forests, water, wildlife) regenerate naturally; **non-renewable** ones (coal, petroleum, natural gas, metallic minerals) form over geological timescales and are exhaustible.
- The four environmental **spheres** — lithosphere, hydrosphere, atmosphere, biosphere — interact within an **ecosystem** whose stability depends on **biodiversity**.
- **Population density** D = P ÷ A, where P is total population (persons) and A is land area (km²).
- The **4R principle** (Reduce, Reuse, Recycle, Recover) governs waste management; **sustainable development** balances present needs with future generations (Brundtland Report).
- CTET frequent: classification MCQs, 4R items, Chipko movement, and ozone-depleting **CFCs**.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months before CTET.

#### Classifying Natural Resources

Resources are **biotic** (living — forests, fisheries, livestock) or **abiotic** (non-living — minerals, fossil fuels, water, air). A second axis splits them by regeneration time. **Renewable** sources (solar radiation, wind, tidal energy, fresh water, forest biomass) replenish within a human lifespan, while **non-renewable** sources (coal, crude oil, natural gas, bauxite, iron ore) took millions of years to form.

> Trap: "Renewable" ≠ "inexhaustible." Groundwater and forest timber renew only when extraction is below the recharge rate.

#### Spheres and Biogeochemical Cycles

The **lithosphere** (solid earth), **hydrosphere** (water), **atmosphere** (gaseous envelope up to ~10,000 km), and **biosphere** (all living zones) constantly exchange matter through **biogeochemical cycles** — water, carbon, oxygen, and nitrogen. Photosynthesis reaction:
#### 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂
This transfers carbon from atmosphere to biomass and replenishes atmospheric oxygen.

#### Pollution and the 4R Response

| Pollution type | Major source | Typical CTET answer |
|---|---|---|
| Air | Vehicle/industrial emissions, stubble burning | Particulate matter, SO₂, NOₓ |
| Water | Sewage, industrial effluents, agricultural run-off | Eutrophication, heavy metals |
| Soil | Pesticides, plastic, e-waste | Loss of soil fertility |
| Noise | Traffic, loudspeakers, construction | >85 dB unsafe |
| Thermal | Power-plant cooling water | Aquatic life disturbance |

The **4R principle** — **R**educe, **R**euse, **R**ecycle, **R**ecover — is the standard CTET-recommended waste hierarchy, applied in the **Swachh Bharat Mission** context.

#### Conservation Laws and Movements

Indian environmental legislation to memorise:

- **Wildlife Protection Act, 1972** — schedules of protected species
- **Forest Conservation Act, 1980** — restricts diversion of forest land
- **Environment Protection Act, 1986** — umbrella law post-Bhopal
- **Biological Diversity Act, 2002** — access and benefit sharing

The **Chipko movement** (1973, Uttarakhand) led by **Sunderlal Bahuguna** is a hallmark MCQ on community-led conservation.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for aspirants preparing three months or more in advance.

#### Afforestation vs Reforestation vs Social Forestry

These three terms are routinely mixed in MCQs. **Afforestation** establishes forest cover on land that has never historically been forested (e.g., degraded wasteland). **Reforestation** replants trees on previously forested but currently degraded land. **Social forestry** grows trees on community and roadside lands for local fuel, fodder, and fruit — a people-centric variant. **Agroforestry** integrates trees with crop and livestock systems on farmland.

#### Biosphere Reserve Zones

India has 18 notified **biosphere reserves** (e.g., Sundarbans, Nilgiri, Pachmarhi). Each has three concentric zones:

| Zone | Human activity permitted |
|---|---|
| **Core** | None — strict nature reserve |
| **Buffer** | Limited research and education |
| **Transition** | Sustainable human use, settlements |

> Exam alert: CTET questions often flip the wording — "maximum activity zone" is the Transition zone, not the Core.

#### Greenhouse Effect vs Ozone Depletion

Both are atmospheric changes but have different mechanisms. The **greenhouse effect** traps outgoing infrared radiation through CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, CFCs, and water vapour; atmospheric CO₂ has risen from ~280 ppm (1750) to ~420 ppm (2024). **Ozone depletion** is the catalytic destruction of stratospheric O₃ by **CFCs** and **halons**, releasing Cl• radicals. CFCs do *not* "cause global warming" directly in the classical CTET framing — they are linked to the **ozone hole**.

2. Two statements: (a) "Hydroelectric power is pollution-free." (b) "CFCs deplete the ozone layer." Identify the limitation in (a) and the category of pollutant in (b).
