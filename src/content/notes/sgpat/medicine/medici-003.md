# Pathophysiology

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

This section addresses **acute coronary syndromes (ACS)**, a high-yield domain in the Saudi GP Board Medicine blueprint (≈3% of the paper, typically 1–2 MCQs). ACS is the clinical spectrum spanning **unstable angina (UA)**, **non-ST-elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI)**, and **ST-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI)**, all driven by **myocardial ischemia** from reduced coronary flow. The unifying pathophysiology is **plaque rupture or erosion → platelet aggregation → thrombus formation → partial or complete coronary occlusion**. Key diagnostic anchors are the **12-lead ECG** (ST elevation ≥1 mm in two contiguous limb leads or ≥2 mm in precordial leads, or new LBBB = STEMI) and the **high-sensitivity troponin I or T** curve (rise-and-fall pattern above the 99th percentile). Door-to-balloon time for STEMI must stay **≤90 minutes** in a PCI-capable center. Memorize the **MONA-B** initial bundle (Morphine, Oxygen if SpO₂ <90%, Nitroglycerin, Aspirin 300 mg chewed, Beta-blocker) and the dual antiplatelet regimen (**aspirin + P2Y12 inhibitor**: clopidogrel, ticagrelor, or prasugrel).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Pathophysiology
Atherosclerotic plaques develop silently over decades via **endothelial dysfunction**, LDL infiltration, foam-cell formation, and a **fibrous cap**. When the cap thins, it ruptures exposing the lipid core; **tissue factor** triggers the coagulation cascade, **glycoprotein IIb/IIIa receptors** on activated platelets bind fibrinogen, and an occlusive thrombus forms. The downstream consequence is a **supply-demand mismatch** — coronary flow drops below myocardial oxygen demand, producing ischemic chest pain, regional wall-motion abnormality, and, if unrelieved, myocyte necrosis beginning subendocardially and spreading transmurally.

#### Clinical Differentiation
- **STEMI**: persistent ST elevation, transmural injury, requires emergent reperfusion.
- **NSTEMI/UA**: ST depression, T-wave inversion, or non-diagnostic ECG; differentiation depends on **troponin elevation** (NSTEMI positive; UA normal).
- **Braunwald classification** stratifies UA by severity and clinical circumstances.

#### Diagnostic Workup
| Test | Finding | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| 12-lead ECG | ST Δ, T-wave inversion, new LBBB | Within 10 min of arrival |
| High-sensitivity troponin | Rise/fall above 99th percentile | 0 h and 1–3 h delta |
| Echocardiogram | Regional wall-motion abnormality | Within 24 h |
| Coronary angiography | Culprit lesion identification | Urgent for STEMI; risk-stratified for NSTE-ACS |

#### Initial Management
MONA-B covers symptom relief; definitive therapy depends on ST elevation. **Primary PCI** is preferred over fibrinolysis when achievable within 120 min of first medical contact. Fibrinolytics (tenecteplase, alteplase) are reserved when PCI is unavailable, given within **30 min** of arrival.

#### Typical Exam Question Patterns
- ECG interpretation of inferior STEMI (II, III, aVF).
- Selecting antithrombotic regimen based on bleeding risk.
- Calculating **GRACE** or **TIMI** score for NSTE-ACS risk stratification.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases & Atypical Presentations
**Diabetic and elderly patients** often present with **silent MI** — dyspnea, fatigue, or epigastric discomfort rather than classic crushing chest pain. **Right ventricular infarction** accompanies inferior STEMI in 30–40% of cases; avoid nitrates and preload-reducing agents, and obtain **right-sided leads V4R**. **Wellens syndrome** signals critical proximal LAD stenosis with deep symmetric T-wave inversion in V2–V3 during pain-free intervals — a contraindication to stress testing.

#### Adjacent Topics Worth Linking
- **Heart failure**: post-MI LV remodeling → reduced ejection fraction.
- **Arrhythmias**: ventricular fibrillation peaks in the first 24 h post-MI; reperfusion reduces risk.
- **Anticoagulation overlap** when switching from unfractionated heparin to warfarin or DOACs in atrial fibrillation developing post-ACS.

#### Common Traps
- Confusing **early repolarization** (concave ST elevation in young men) with STEMI.
- Misreading **reciprocal ST depression** in inferior leads as the primary abnormality.
- Prescribing **NSAIDs** (except aspirin) in the acute phase — increases mortality.
- Overlooking **cardiogenic shock** as a STEMI complication requiring urgent revascularization ± mechanical circulatory support (IABP, Impella, ECMO).

#### Practice Prompts
1. A 58-year-old man presents 2 h after onset of crushing retrosternal pain; ECG shows ST elevation in II, III, and aVF with reciprocal depression in I and aVL. Which coronary territory is occluded, and what is the next step if PCI is unavailable within 2 hours?
2. A 65-year-old diabetic woman presents with vague nausea and diaphoresis. Initial troponin is borderline; a 3-hour delta shows a 5-fold rise. How does GRACE score alter your disposition decision, and which P2Y12 inhibitor would you choose given her HbA1c of 9.2% and eGFR of 55 mL/min/1.73 m²?
