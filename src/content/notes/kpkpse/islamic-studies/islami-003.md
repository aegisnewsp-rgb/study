# Islamic Ethics, Morality, and the Islamic Worldview

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

- **Definition**: *al-Akhlaq wa al-Tasawwur al-Islami* integrates beliefs, values, and conduct rooted in **Tawhid** (Oneness of God), **Risalah** (prophethood), and **Akhirah** (accountability), shaping a Muslim's perception of reality.
- **Core formulation**: Islam as **Deen** (way of life) — not merely ritual — unites spiritual (*ibadah*), ethical (*akhlaq*), legal (*fiqh*), economic, and political dimensions under divine guidance.
- **Hierarchy of acts**: Fard → Mustahabb → Mubah → Makruh → Haram, where **niyyah** (intention) determines the moral value of every act.
- **Maqasid al-Shariah** (higher objectives) preserve five necessities: **Din, Nafs, Aql, Nasl, Mal**.
- **Wasatiyyah** (Qur'an 2:143) is the Qur'anic principle of moderation, not political centrism.
- **Exam tip**: Memorise the verse reference for Wasatiyyah (2:143), Taqwa (49:13), and the Hadith on Ihsan (Sahih Muslim).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Foundations of the Islamic Worldview

The Islamic worldview rests on three pillars: **Tawhid** (the absolute Oneness of God, free from shirk), **Nubuwwah** (prophethood as the channel of divine guidance culminating in Muhammad ﷺ), and **Akhirah** (life after death with accountability). Together they reorient human consciousness — life becomes a *test* (ibtila'), the world a *field of action* (mazra'at al-akhirah), and moral agency a *trust* (amanah) from Allah.

#### Sources of Islamic Ethics

Ethics is derived from a graded hierarchy of sources, each with a defined epistemic role:

| Source | Type | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Qur'an** | Primary revelation | Establishes universal moral axioms (adl, ihsan, rahma) |
| **Sunnah** | Prophetic practice | Operationalises Qur'anic values into concrete conduct |
| **Ijma** | Scholarly consensus | Provides communal certainty on emerging issues |
| **Qiyas** | Analogical reasoning | Extends rulings to new cases |
| **Istislah** | Public interest | Protects maslahah when texts are silent |
| **Istihsan** | Juristic preference | Abandons analogy for stronger equity |
| **Urf** | Custom | Recognises socially accepted norms that contradict no text |

#### Akhlaq Mahmudah versus Akhlaq Madhmumah

The Prophetic tradition (*innama bu'ithtu liutammima makaarim al-akhlaq* — Sahih al-Bukhari) frames morality as the very purpose of the mission. Praiseworthy traits (*mahmudah*) include **sidq** (truthfulness), **amanah** (trustworthiness), **sabr** (patience), **shukr** (gratitude), and **tawadu'** (humility); blameworthy traits (*madhmumah*) include **kadhb** (lying), **ghiba** (backbiting), **kibr** (arrogance), **hasad** (envy), and **riya'** (ostentation).

#### Akhlaq versus Fiqh

Fiqh regulates outward acts through legal rulings; akhlaq internalises intention and character. A valid prayer (sahih fiqhi) without humility is ritually correct but spiritually deficient — hence the hadith that Allah accepts only that which is pure (*tayyib*) and done solely for His sake.

> **Scoring tip**: In essay questions, always cite Surah al-Hujurat 49:13 ("Indeed the most noble of you before Allah is the most taqwa") to anchor the link between equality and moral excellence.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Maqasid al-Shariah: The Five Necessities

Formulated by al-Shatibi (*Al-Muwafaqat*), the Maqasid classify human interests into three tiers: **Dharuriyyat** (essentials — Din, Nafs, Aql, Nasl, Mal), **Hajiyyat** (complementaries), and **Tahsiniyyat** (embellishments). Any ruling that endangers a Dharuriyyat is abrogated in application, not in text. This framework refutes the misconception that Islamic ethics is purely otherworldly — preserving *nafs* (life) and *mal* (wealth) is itself a sacred objective.

#### Wasatiyyah and the Comprehensive Worldview

Surah al-Baqarah 2:143 designates the *ummah wasatan* — a community of *qist* (justice) and *shahada* (witness-bearing to mankind). Wasatiyyah is neither extremism nor liberalism; it is a teleological balance between *haqq* and *khayr*, between rights and duties, between ruh (spirit) and jism (body). It governs economics (prohibition of *riba* alongside permission of trade), family ethics (balance of rights between spouses), and international relations (justice even toward enemies, 60:8).

#### Common Examiner Traps

- Treating **Wasatiyyah** as political centrism or secular moderation — it is theologically grounded.
- Conflating **Urf** with un-Islamic customs; valid *urf* must not contradict the Qur'an or Sunnah.
- Reducing **akhlak** to *ibadah* rituals; ethics extends to *mu'amalat* (transactions) and *mu'asharah* (social conduct).
- Forgetting that **niyyah** differentiates identical acts — a sword drawn for injustice vs. for justice.

#### Worked Mini-Example

Consider a trader selling halal goods. Fiqh requires honest description (sahih contract). Akhlaq adds **niyyah** of *ibadah* — earning a livelihood (*kasb al-halal*) as an act of *ibadah* when done with *sidq* and *amanah*. The same outward transaction, if tainted by *gharar* (deception) or *riba*, becomes *haram* — proving that fiqh and akhlaq are two expressions of one divine guidance.
