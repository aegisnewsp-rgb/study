# Kinetics and Thermodynamics — MCAT Chem/Phys

Kinetics and thermodynamics form the largest single content block of AAMC Foundational Concept 5 in the Chem/Phys section. The MCAT tests reaction rates, rate laws, activation energy, catalysis (with enzymes as the biological context), and the thermodynamics of enthalpy, entropy, and Gibbs free energy. These topics underpin almost every biochemistry and metabolism passage in the Bio/Biochem section.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### Core formulas

- **Rate law:** Rate = k × [A]^m × [B]^n. Order is m + n; k is the rate constant.
- **Arrhenius equation:** k = A × e^(-Ea / RT). Ea in J/mol; R = 8.314 J/(mol·K); T in K.
- **First-order integrated rate law:** ln[A] = ln[A]₀ - k × t.
- **Half-life:** t₁/₂ = 0.693 / k for first-order reactions.
- **Enthalpy change:** ΔH = H_products - H_reactants. Negative ΔH = exothermic.
- **Entropy change:** ΔS = S_products - S_reactants. Positive ΔS = increase in disorder.
- **Gibbs free energy:** ΔG = ΔH - T × ΔS. Negative ΔG = spontaneous.

#### One-pass drill

1. Identify the topic: kinetics or thermodynamics.
2. List the known quantities and convert to consistent units.
3. Pick the formula; solve algebraically.
4. Check the sign of ΔG to determine spontaneity.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing reaction order and molecularity. Order is empirical; molecularity is the number of molecules in an elementary step.
- Forgetting that enzymes are catalysts — they lower Ea but do not change ΔG.
- Treating ΔS as always positive for gas-producing reactions.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Chemical kinetics

The MCAT tests:

- **Rate law:** determine the order from data, then write the rate law. Zero order: rate independent of concentration. First order: rate proportional to one concentration. Second order: rate proportional to concentration squared or to two concentrations.
- **Rate constant (k):** depends on temperature via the Arrhenius equation. Higher T → higher k → faster reaction.
- **Activation energy (Ea):** the energy barrier between reactants and products. Catalysts (including enzymes) lower Ea.
- **Reaction mechanisms:** elementary steps sum to give the overall reaction. The rate-determining step sets the overall rate law.
- **Integrated rate laws:** first-order reactions show a linear ln[A] vs t plot; zero-order show a linear [A] vs t plot.

The MCAT frames kinetics in biological contexts: enzyme-catalysed reactions, drug metabolism, and radioactive decay.

#### Chemical thermodynamics

**Enthalpy (H):** the heat content of a system. ΔH is negative for exothermic reactions, positive for endothermic reactions. The MCAT tests ΔH in the context of bond breaking (endothermic) and bond forming (exothermic).

**Entropy (S):** the disorder of a system. ΔS is positive when disorder increases (gas from solid, more particles, higher temperature). The MCAT tests ΔS in the context of protein folding (decrease in entropy) and hydrophobic effect.

**Gibbs free energy (G):** the energy available to do work. ΔG = ΔH - TΔS. A reaction is spontaneous if ΔG < 0. The MCAT tests ΔG in the context of metabolic reactions (catabolism is exergonic; anabolism is endergonic).

**Temperature dependence:** for reactions with positive ΔS, increasing T makes ΔG more negative. For reactions with negative ΔS, increasing T makes ΔG less negative.

**Equilibrium:** ΔG° = -RT × ln(K_eq). A negative ΔG° corresponds to K_eq > 1 (favours products). The MCAT tests the relationship between ΔG° and K_eq.

#### Practice routine

- Take 10 rate-law items. Determine the order from data, then write the rate law.
- Take 5 Arrhenius items. Compute k at two temperatures; compute Ea from a plot.
- Take 10 thermodynamics items. Compute ΔH, ΔS, ΔG, and predict spontaneity.
- Take 5 equilibrium items. Compute ΔG° from K_eq, or K_eq from ΔG°.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How this topic connects to the other Chem/Phys topics

- **Acid-base chemistry** uses Gibbs free energy to predict reaction direction.
- **Electrochemistry** relates ΔG to cell potential: ΔG° = -n × F × E°_cell.
- **Biochemistry metabolism** (in the Bio/Biochem section) depends on thermodynamics: ATP hydrolysis is exergonic and drives endergonic reactions.

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"Catalysts change the equilibrium constant."** No — catalysts change the rate but not the equilibrium. They lower Ea for both forward and reverse reactions equally.
- **"ΔS is always positive for gas-producing reactions."** ΔS depends on the total change in disorder. A reaction that produces one gas molecule from two gas molecules has negative ΔS (decrease in particle number).
- **"ΔG° tells you about a specific reaction mixture."** ΔG° is the standard free energy change. The actual ΔG depends on the concentrations of reactants and products.

#### Exam technique

- For kinetics items, identify the order from data (graph or table) before writing the rate law.
- For Arrhenius items, use R = 8.314 J/(mol·K) and convert Ea to J/mol if given in kJ/mol.
- For thermodynamics items, compute ΔH, ΔS, ΔG in order; predict spontaneity from the sign of ΔG.
- For equilibrium items, use ΔG° = -RT × ln(K_eq). R = 8.314 J/(mol·K); T in K.
- Use the platform's calculator.
