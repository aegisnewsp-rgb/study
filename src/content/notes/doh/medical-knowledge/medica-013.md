# Health Promotion and Patient Education

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Health promotion is the socio-behavioural and policy effort to enable people to gain control over health determinants, whereas patient education is the one-to-one clinical teaching of an individual about their condition, medication, or self-care. Both are scored under DOH Medical-Knowledge (~3% weight).

- **Three prevention levels:** primary = before disease (vaccination, smoking cessation), secondary = early detection (screening, mammography), tertiary = reducing complications (cardiac rehab, diabetic foot care).
- **Patient-education cycle:** assess → set objectives → plan → deliver (verbal, written, demo, teach-back) → evaluate.
- **Counselling toolkit:** motivational interviewing (OARS) and the **Transtheoretical Model** (precontemplation → maintenance).
- **UAE focus areas:** diabetes, obesity, tobacco, mental health, vaccine uptake under Weqaya and FitforLife.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Definitions and Scope

Health promotion is the WHO-endorsed process of enabling individuals, families, and communities to increase control over the determinants of health. Patient education is the clinician-delivered component: structured teaching during the consultation about diagnosis, medication, lifestyle, or self-monitoring.

#### Levels of Prevention

| Level | Aim | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary | Stop disease before it starts | HPV vaccination, tobacco cessation |
| Secondary | Detect disease early | HbA1c screening, mammography, FIT test |
| Tertiary | Limit complications in established disease | Cardiac rehab post-MI, diabetic foot care |

Misclassifying tertiary care as "primary" is a common DOH MCQ trap, e.g. insulin education for a known diabetic is tertiary, not primary.

#### The Patient-Education Process

- **Assess** learning needs, literacy, language, and cultural background.
- **Set objectives** across Bloom's domains — cognitive (knowledge), affective (attitude), psychomotor (skill).
- **Plan** content, format, and media (verbal, written, pictogram, video).
- **Deliver** using adult-learning principles (Knowles' andragogy: relevance, autonomy, prior experience).
- **Evaluate** with the **teach-back method** — DOH standards require documented verification of comprehension.

#### Behaviour-Change Counselling

- **Motivational interviewing** uses OARS (Open questions, Affirmations, Reflections, Summaries) to resolve ambivalence.
- **Stages of change** (precontemplation, contemplation, preparation, action, maintenance) must guide intervention matching.
- Match the strategy to the stage — pushing "action" advice on a patient in precontemplation fails.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Ottawa Charter and Health-Promotion Framework

The 1986 Ottawa Charter defines five action areas: build healthy public policy, create supportive environments, strengthen community action, develop personal skills, and reorient health services. UAE programmes such as Weqaya (cardiovascular screening for Emiratis) and FitforLife (community physical-activity) operationalise these action areas at the national level.

#### Domains of Learning and Health Literacy

Patient-education objectives should target the correct Bloom's domain. Teaching inhaler technique is a **psychomotor** objective, not cognitive; "explain the role of β2-agonists" is cognitive; "accept long-term controller use" is affective. Plain language, avoidance of jargon, and culturally adapted materials are mandatory in multilingual UAE settings (Arabic, English, Hindi, Urdu, Tagalog, Malayalam).

#### Common Mistakes

1. Confusing health education (information transfer) with health promotion (policy + environment + community action).
2. Treating "handed the patient a leaflet" as completed patient education — teach-back is required.
3. Ignoring documentation in the medical record, which the DOH clinical-governance framework audits.
4. Recommending action-stage tasks for precontemplation-stage patients.
5. Using English-only materials for non-English-speaking patients.

#### Worked MCQ Logic

Scenario: A 52-year-old with type 2 diabetes and peripheral neuropathy is taught daily foot inspection. Level = tertiary prevention; objective domain = psychomotor; documentation of teach-back required.

#### Practice Prompts

1. A smoker in **contemplation** stage visits the clinic — apply OARS and propose a stage-matched intervention.
2. An asthma patient receives a written action plan only — identify the missing element per DOH standards and the appropriate Bloom's domain for correct inhaler technique.
