# Organising Paragraphs and Using Transitions — OSSLT Writing

Organisation is one of the four dimensions of the EQAO OSSLT rubric for the long-writing tasks. The rubric scores paragraph structure (topic sentences, paragraph separation, logical flow) and the use of transitions to connect ideas within and between paragraphs. This skill is central to both the News Report and the Series-of-Paragraphs long-writing tasks.

> Verify the live rubric and any in-year task changes on https://www.eqao.com/the-assessments/osslt/ before planning around the figures below.

---

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### What "organisation" means on the OSSLT

- **Paragraph separation.** Each main idea gets its own paragraph. The Series-of-Paragraphs task requires at least two distinct paragraphs; the News Report task requires a lead, supporting paragraphs, and a closing paragraph.
- **Topic sentences.** Each body paragraph opens with a sentence that states the paragraph's main idea. The topic sentence tells the reader what the paragraph is about.
- **Logical flow.** The paragraphs are arranged in a logical order — most important first, then supporting, then wrap-up.
- **Transitions.** Words and phrases connect ideas within and between paragraphs. "However," "for example," "in addition," "as a result," "on the other hand" are common transitions.

#### Common transition words and phrases

- **Addition:** "in addition," "furthermore," "also," "another reason is..."
- **Contrast:** "however," "on the other hand," "in contrast," "although..."
- **Cause and effect:** "because," "as a result," "therefore," "this led to..."
- **Sequence:** "first," "next," "finally," "then..."
- **Example:** "for example," "for instance," "such as," "in particular..."

#### Examiner traps

- A response with one long paragraph. The OSSLT rubric scores single-paragraph responses down on organisation.
- A response with paragraphs but no topic sentences. Each paragraph should open with a clear statement of its main idea.
- A response with transitions inside paragraphs but not between paragraphs. The first sentence of a body paragraph should signal the connection to the previous paragraph.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Strategy for paragraph organisation

**Plan the paragraph order before writing.** Decide which paragraph goes first, which goes second, and so on. The most important idea usually goes first; the wrap-up goes last.

**Open each body paragraph with a topic sentence.** The topic sentence is the first sentence of the paragraph and states the paragraph's main idea. Example: "The first reason the school should adopt the program is that it improves attendance."

**Use one paragraph per main idea.** Do not stack multiple main ideas in one paragraph. A paragraph that tries to do two things scores lower on organisation than two paragraphs that each do one thing.

**Use transitions between paragraphs.** The first sentence of a body paragraph should signal the connection to the previous paragraph. "Another reason is..." "Furthermore..." "On the other hand..." These transitions make the logical flow visible.

**Close with a short wrap-up paragraph or sentence.** The closing should restate the main idea in light of the supporting paragraphs. Do not introduce a new idea in the closing.

#### Practice routine for paragraph organisation

- Take one EQAO sample Series-of-Paragraphs task. Read the prompt and source. List the supporting reasons.
- Plan the paragraph order: opinion first, then reasons in order of importance, then wrap-up.
- Write the response. Use a topic sentence at the start of each paragraph. Use a transition between paragraphs.
- Read the response aloud. Each paragraph should flow into the next without abrupt shifts.
- Time yourself: under 20 minutes for the long-writing task.

#### How this skill connects to the OSSLT reading tasks

The OSSLT reading items on Information Paragraph and News Report selections test the same organisational skills: identifying the topic sentence, recognising the order of supporting details, and following the logical flow from lead to body to wrap-up.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How organisation connects to the other OSSLT items

- **Reading 1** (explicit information) supports organisation by helping the student locate the supporting details to use.
- **Reading 2** (inference) supports organisation by helping the student interpret the supporting details and decide which ones to use.
- **Reading 3** (making connections) supports organisation by helping the student connect the response to the reader's experience in the wrap-up sentence.

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"The topic sentence can go anywhere in the paragraph."** The topic sentence goes at the start of the paragraph. Readers expect it there.
- **"Transitions are optional."** Transitions make the logical flow visible. A response without transitions feels like a list of paragraphs, not a coherent argument.
- **"A closing paragraph is unnecessary."** The closing ties the response together. Without it, the response feels unfinished.

#### Exam technique

- Plan the paragraph order before writing. Most important first; wrap-up last.
- Open each body paragraph with a topic sentence. State the paragraph's main idea clearly.
- Use transitions between paragraphs. Use transitions within paragraphs to connect ideas.
- Close with a short wrap-up paragraph or sentence that restates the main idea.
- Re-read the response. Each paragraph should flow into the next without abrupt shifts.
