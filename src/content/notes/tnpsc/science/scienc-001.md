# Living World and Cell Biology

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid revision summary of cell structure, organelles, and taxonomic hierarchy for TNPSC Group 1 Preliminary examination.

**Cell Biology Foundations — High-Yield Essentials**

1. **Cell Theory:** Postulated by Matthias Schleiden (1838) and Theodor Schwann (1839). Rudolf Virchow (1855) added the Latin aphorism *Omnis cellula-e-cellula* (all cells arise from pre-existing cells). Viruses remain a classic exception to cell theory because they lack cellular machinery and reproduce exclusively inside host cells.
2. **Prokaryotes vs Eukaryotes:** Prokaryotic cells (bacteria, cyanobacteria, mycoplasma) lack membrane-bound organelles and a true nuclear membrane; their 70S ribosomes consist of 50S and 30S subunits. Eukaryotic cells possess 80S ribosomes (60S and 40S subunits) and compartmentalized organelles.
3. **Key Organelles and Nicknames:**
   - **Mitochondria:** Powerhouse of the cell; site of Krebs cycle in matrix and ATP synthase on inner membrane cristae. Contains 70S ribosomes and circular maternal DNA.
   - **Ribosomes:** Protein factories; non-membrane bound structures discovered by George Palade.
   - **Lysosomes:** Suicidal bags; contain acid hydrolases operating optimally at pH ~4.5 to 5.0.
   - **Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER):** Rough ER (studded with ribosomes) synthesizes secretory proteins; Smooth ER synthesizes lipids and detoxifies drugs/xenobiotics in hepatocytes.
   - **Golgi Complex:** Packaging and glycosylation center of secretory vesicles.
4. **Cell Division:** Mitosis produces two genetically identical diploid (2n) daughter cells for somatic growth. Meiosis produces four non-identical haploid (n) gametes through two reductional divisions, introducing genetic variation via crossing over during the Pachytene stage of Prophase I.

⚡ **TNPSC Group 1 Exam Trap:** Candidates often confuse the site of ribosome biogenesis with the site of protein synthesis. Ribosomes are manufactured in the **nucleolus**, while protein synthesis takes place on ribosomes in the **cytoplasm** and **rough endoplasmic reticulum**.

## 1. Organelle Structure and Physiological Function

The eukaryotic cell maintains structural compartmentalization to prevent enzymatic cross-reactions. The table below details key organelle functions tested in TNPSC General Science MCQs:

| Organelle | Structural Features | Primary Function | TNPSC Exam Association |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Plasma Membrane** | Fluid Mosaic Model (Singer & Nicolson, 1972); phospholipid bilayer | Selective permeability; active and passive transport | Cholesterol stabilizes fluidity; Na+/K+ ATPase pumps 3 Na+ out, 2 K+ in |
| **Nucleus** | Double membrane with nuclear pores; contains chromatin and nucleolus | Stores genetic code; coordinates cell division and gene expression | Chromatin condenses into chromosomes; histones are basic proteins rich in lysine and arginine |
| **Mitochondria** | Semi-autonomous organelle; double membrane, inner folds form cristae | Oxidative phosphorylation; generates ATP via electron transport system (ETS) | Cytochrome c oxidase, F0-F1 particles; endosymbiotic bacterial origin |
| **Chloroplast** | Semi-autonomous plastid; thylakoids stacked into grana surrounded by stroma | Photosynthesis: light reactions in thylakoid membranes, Calvin cycle in stroma | Contains green pigment chlorophyll; Mg2+ ion sits at the center of the porphyrin ring |
| **Peroxisomes** | Single-membrane microbody containing catalase and urate oxidase | Detoxification of hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) and photorespiratory glycolate oxidation | Catalase converts 2 H2O2 into 2 H2O + O2 |
| **Centrosome** | Composed of two mutually perpendicular centrioles (9+0 microtubule triplet pattern) | Organizes spindle apparatus during animal cell division | Absent in higher plant cells; plant spindles form without centrioles |

## 2. Cell Cycle and Mitotic vs Meiotic Regulation

The eukaryotic cell cycle progresses through four sequential stages: **G1** (gap 1: RNA and protein synthesis), **S** (synthesis: DNA replication and centrosome duplication), **G2** (gap 2: tubulin synthesis and checkpoint verification), and **M** (mitosis: nuclear and cytoplasmic division). Non-proliferating cells exit G1 into a quiescent state termed **G0** (such as mature neurons and cardiac myocytes).

### Phases of Meiosis I (Pachytene and Crossing Over)
Prophase I of meiosis is subdivided into five distinct cytological stages:
- **Leptotene:** Chromatin begins condensation into visible single strands.
- **Zygotene:** Homologous chromosomes pair up along their lengths in a process called synapsis, mediated by the synaptonemal complex.
- **Pachytene:** Non-sister chromatids of homologous pairs exchange genetic segments at recombinant points. This process—crossing over—is catalyzed by the enzyme recombinase and generates genetic diversity.
- **Diplotene:** Synaptonemal complex dissolves; homologous chromosomes remain attached at X-shaped intersections called chiasmata.
- **Diakinesis:** Terminalization of chiasmata and breakdown of nuclear envelope.

## 3. High-Yield Diagnostic Problem Solving

#### Worked Micro-Example: DNA Replication and Chromatid Quantification
A somatic cell of an organism possesses 2n = 24 chromosomes. Determine the exact count of chromosomes and chromatids across cell cycle milestones:
1. **G1 Phase:** 24 chromosomes, 24 chromatids (unreplicated single double-helix per chromosome).
2. **Post-S Phase (G2):** 24 chromosomes, 48 chromatids (each chromosome now has two sister chromatids joined at the centromere; chromosome number remains 24, but total DNA content doubles from 2C to 4C).
3. **Metaphase of Mitosis:** 24 chromosomes aligned at equatorial plate, 48 chromatids.
4. **Anaphase of Mitosis:** Centromeres split; sister chromatids migrate to opposite poles, momentarily yielding 48 independent chromosomes.
5. **Telophase Daughter Cells:** Each daughter cell inherits 24 chromosomes and 24 chromatids.

## 4. TNPSC Group 1 Previous Year Questions (PYQs)

**Question 1 (TNPSC Group 1 Prelims):** Which organelle is responsible for the formation of acrosome in mammalian sperm cells?  
*(A) Mitochondria (B) Golgi apparatus (C) Endoplasmic reticulum (D) Ribosome*  
**Answer:** (B) Golgi apparatus (Explanation: The acrosome of the spermatozoon is a modified cap-like lysosomal vesicle derived directly from the Golgi apparatus, loaded with hyaluronidase and acrosin to penetrate the ovum zona pellucida).

**Question 2 (TNPSC Group 1 Prelims):** In which stage of meiotic division does genetic recombination take place?  
*(A) Leptotene (B) Zygotene (C) Pachytene (D) Diplotene*  
**Answer:** (C) Pachytene (Explanation: Crossing over between non-sister chromatids of homologous chromosomes occurs during the Pachytene stage, mediated by the recombinase enzyme complex).

**Question 3 (TNPSC Group 1 Prelims):** The metal ion present at the center of chlorophyll is:  
*(A) Iron (B) Magnesium (C) Zinc (D) Manganese*  
**Answer:** (B) Magnesium (Explanation: The porphyrin ring of chlorophyll coordinates a central Mg2+ ion. Iron (Fe2+) is found in haemoglobin and cytochromes; cobalt is present in cyanocobalamin / vitamin B12).
