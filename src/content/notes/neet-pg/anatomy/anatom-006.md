# Cardiovascular System

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The cardiovascular system is a closed circulatory network built around a four-chambered muscular pump (the heart) connected to arteries, capillaries, and veins. Its anatomical core for NEET PG covers chambers, valves, conducting tissue, coronary vessels, pericardial layers, and fetal shunts.

- **Chambers:** 2 atria (receiving) + 2 ventricles (pumping), separated by interatrial and interventricular septa.
- **Valves:** AV valves (tricuspid right, mitral left) anchored by chordae tendineae to papillary muscles; semilunar valves (pulmonary, aortic) are chord-free.
- **Conducting system order:** SA node → AV node (0.3 s delay) → Bundle of His → bundle branches → Purkinje fibres.
- **Fetal shunts:** foramen ovale, ductus arteriosus, ductus venosus — all close functionally post-birth.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Chambers and Septa

The right atrium receives the SVC, IVC, and coronary sinus; its posterior wall is smooth (sinus venarum) while the anterior wall shows pectinate muscles. The left atrium has four pulmonary vein openings and a smooth interior. The interventricular septum has a thin membranous part (just below the aortic valve) and a thick muscular part.

#### Valves and their supports

AV valves open during ventricular filling and close during systole; chordae tendineae from the papillary muscles prevent eversion. Semilunar valves (aortic, pulmonary) have three cusps but no chordae — closure produces the second heart sound.

#### Coronary Dominance

Coronary dominance is defined by which artery gives the posterior interventricular branch (PDA), not by the size of the vessel.

| Feature | Right Coronary Artery (RCA) | Left Coronary Artery (LCA) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Supplies (most hearts) | RA, RV, posterior 1/3 septum, SA node (60%), AV node (80%) | LA, LV, anterior 2/3 septum (via LAD), left atrium |
| Branches | SA nodal, AV nodal, marginal, PDA in right-dominant | LAD, circumflex |
| Dominance prevalence | Right-dominant ~70–80% | Left-dominant ~10% |

#### Pericardium and Sinuses

The fibrous pericardium is tough and outer; the serous pericardium has parietal and visceral (epicardium) layers. The transverse sinus lies between the aorta/pulmonary trunk anteriorly and the SVC/atria posteriorly — the surgical landmark for clamping great vessels.

- SA node is the pacemaker (~70–80/min intrinsic rate).
- AV node delay allows complete atrial emptying before ventricular contraction.
- Purkinje fibres are modified cardiomyocytes, not nerves.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Applied Anatomy and Surgical Landmarks

The transverse pericardial sinus is used in cardiac surgery to cross-clamp the aorta and pulmonary trunk during coronary bypass. The oblique sinus lies behind the left atrium — distension suggests left atrial enlargement. Referred cardiac pain travels via sympathetic afferents (T1–T5) to the left arm and ulnar side of the forearm, explaining why MI presents as left arm pain.

#### Fetal Shunts and Closure

| Shunt | Connects | Postnatal fate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Foramen ovale | Right atrium → left atrium | Fossa ovalis |
| Ductus arteriosus | Pulmonary trunk → aortic arch | Ligamentum arteriosum |
| Ductus venosus | Umbilical vein → IVC | Ligamentum venosum |

Prostaglandin E1 keeps the ductus arteriosus open — clinically used in transposition of great vessels until surgical correction; indomethacin closes a patent ductus.

#### Auscultation vs Anatomical Site

| Valve | Anatomical location | Auscultation site |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Aortic | Left of sternum, 3rd ICS | Right 2nd ICS (Erb's point nearby) |
| Pulmonary | Left of sternum, 2nd ICS | Left 2nd ICS |
| Tricuspid | Right sternal border, 4th ICS | Lower left sternal border (4th–5th ICS) |
| Mitral | Apex, 5th ICS midclavicular | Apex (5th ICS midclavicular) |

#### Common Errors

1. Marking the aortic valve as "right-sided" because its auscultation site is on the right — anatomy is left, sound radiates right.
2. Calling Purkinje fibres "nerves"; they are specialised cardiomyocytes conducting at ~2–4 m/s.
3. Assuming LAD supplies the AV node — the RCA does in ~80% of hearts.
