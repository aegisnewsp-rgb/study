# Legal News

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Legal News** in CLAT is the subset of **Current Affairs & GK** that tests awareness of judicial pronouncements, statutory amendments, ordinances, and constitutional developments reported in the **12-month window** before the exam. The Consortium of NLUs assigns roughly **5% weight** inside the ~25% Current Affairs section, in most keys as passage-comprehension or factual MCQs.

#### Must-Know Anchors
- **Three new criminal codes** — **BNS, BNSS, BSA** replaced IPC, CrPC and the Indian Evidence Act from **1 July 2024**. Map old sections to new ones: IPC 302 → BNS 302; IPC 376 → BNS 63; IPC 420 → BNS 318.
- **Article 32, 226, 142** — routes for **PILs**, writ jurisdiction, and complete justice.
- **Ratio decidendi** is binding; **obiter dicta** is persuasive commentary.
- **Article 123** empowers the President to promulgate ordinances when Parliament is not in session.

| --- | --- |
| BNS in force | 1 July 2024 |
| PIL entry point | Article 32 (SC) / 226 (HC) |
| Ordinance route | Article 123 |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### What CLAT Tests Under "Legal News"
The Consortium expects candidates to read **mainstream national dailies** (The Hindu, Indian Express) and at least one **law outlet** (LiveLaw, Bar & Bench, Supreme Court Observer). Items tested in most keys carry a **legal core**: a court ruling, a parliamentary act, an ordinance, a constitutional bench decision, or a notification from the **Ministry of Law and Justice**, **Election Commission of India**, or **NALSA**.

#### How to Read a Judgment
A reported Supreme Court order in standard papers carries a **bench strength**, **judge names**, **citation**, and **date of judgment**. The **ratio decidendi** is the rule that binds lower courts. The **obiter dicta** is incidental observation. CLAT passages in many papers quote a paragraph and ask which part is the binding principle — most students misread the obiter as law.

#### Mapping the New Criminal Codes
From **1 July 2024**, the **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS)**, **Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS)**, and **Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA)** replaced the colonial-era codes. Old IPC sections do **not** apply to offences committed after that date.

| Old Code | New Code | Example mapping |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IPC | BNS | Murder: IPC 302 → BNS 302 |
| CrPC | BNSS | Procedure & arrest reforms |
| Indian Evidence Act | BSA | Digital & electronic evidence |

#### Sources to Track Daily
- Supreme Court of India case status portal
- PRS Legislative Research bill trackers
- LiveLaw / Bar & Bench daily case digest
- PIB releases from the Ministry of Law and Justice

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Examiner Traps
1. **Wrong court attribution** — quoting a **High Court division bench** ruling as a Supreme Court judgment. Always check the citation prefix.
2. **Pre-1 July 2024 law on post-1 July 2024 facts** — applying IPC where BNS governs.
3. **Ordinance vs Act** — a Law Commission recommendation or Standing Committee report is **not** enacted law.
4. **PIL interim order vs final judgment** — interim stays are routinely misread as binding precedent.
5. **Political slug vs legal issue** — headlines like "SC slams Centre" tell you nothing about the relief or the statute interpreted.

#### Worked Example
A CLAT passage reports: *"In *XYZ v. State*, a five-judge Constitution Bench of the Supreme Court read down Section 377 in 2018 and later expanded Article 21 to recognise same-sex marriage in 2023."*
- Identify the **bench strength** (5 judges), **court** (SC), **doctrine** (transformative constitutionalism under **Article 21**), and the **date of judgment**.
- Note the **legal issue** (right to marry, equality under **Article 14**) and the **relief** (read down / directive to legislature).
- Avoid writing the political slug; cite the case, the year, and the binding paragraph.

#### Practice Prompts
1. *"The BNS came into force on 1 July 2024. A murder committed on 15 June 2024 is governed by which statute, and which section applies?"*
2. *"An ordinance is promulgated when Parliament is not in session. Name the constitutional provision, the recommending authority, and the maximum lifetime of such an ordinance."*
