# Organic Chemistry Structure and Reactivity

The Survey of the Natural Sciences is the largest section of the DAT at 100 items, and the ADA's Candidate Guide divides it into Biology (40 items), General Chemistry (30 items) and Organic Chemistry (30 items). It runs for 90 minutes inside a total administration time the ADA states as five hours and 15 minutes.

Before you revise anything: the ADA announced updated Organic Chemistry Test Specifications for 2026, and its official DAT page states that candidates planning to test from May 2026 onward should review the new specifications. The 2026 Candidate Guide contains both the pre-update and post-update specifications and directs candidates to use the version in effect on their administration date. Revising from the wrong version wastes a third of your largest section.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

**Every organic chemistry item is one of four questions.** Identify the functional group → match the reagent → predict the product and mechanism → compare rate or stability. The type tells you the method before you read the options.

**Memory hook — GROUPE, then REACT, then PRODUCT.** G-group identified from the structure, R-reagent matched to that group, O-outcome predicted (substitution vs elimination), P-product and mechanism named, E-error mode checked (wrong group read, or stereochemistry dropped). The chain runs in that order every item, and the two most common losses both happen at step one and the last one.

| Functional group | Mild oxidant | Strong oxidant | Distinctive test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alcohol (1°/2°) | → aldehyde | → carboxylic acid | Reacts with Na, releases H₂ |
| Alcohol (3°) | no reaction | no reaction | Converts to alkene with conc. H₂SO₄ |
| Aldehyde | → carboxylic acid | → carboxylic acid | **Silver mirror** with Tollens' |
| Ketone | no reaction | no reaction | No silver mirror, no Fehling's red |
| Carboxylic acid | — | — | Ester + water with an alcohol, acid-catalysed |
| Alkene | — | — | Decolourises Br₂ water |
| Primary/secondary halide | Substitution with OH⁻ | — | Depends on substitution pattern |
| Tertiary halide | — | Elimination favoured | Heat + weak base → alkene |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

### The four questions organic chemistry asks

An organic chemistry item is almost always one of four types, and the type determines the method.

1. **What is it?** Identify the structure or functional group. Reactions are decided by the group, so misreading the structure loses the whole item.
2. **What will react with this?** Match the reagent to the group. Knowing the standard reagent set cold is worth more than knowing more reactions.
3. **What happens?** Predict the product and name the mechanism type.
4. **Which is faster, or more stable?** Compare two structures on rate or equilibrium.

Run the four in order every time. Steps 1 and 4 are where items are actually lost: a misread structure sends you into a correct reaction with the wrong substrate, and an unstated comparison answers only half of what was asked.

### Functional groups and their reactions

Know a closed set. An alcohol oxidises to a carbonyl with a mild oxidant and to a carboxylic acid with a strong one; it converts to an alkene with concentrated sulfuric acid; it reacts with sodium to release hydrogen. An aldehyde oxidises to a carboxylic acid and a ketone does not — that is the classic identification test. A carboxylic acid reacts with an alcohol under acid catalysis to give an ester and water. A primary or secondary halide undergoes substitution with hydroxide; a tertiary halide cannot, and goes for elimination instead.

That last point is the mechanism decision. Substitution needs a better leaving group or a better nucleophile; elimination needs heat and a weak base. For a tertiary halide with hydroxide in the conditions given, elimination is the product.

### Stereochemistry

Stereoisomers have the same connectivity and a different spatial arrangement. Three relationships matter, and the distinction between them decides most of what follows:

- **Enantiomers** — non-superimposable mirror images. Identical physical properties except for how they rotate plane-polarised light.
- **Diastereomers** — stereoisomers that are not mirror images. Different physical properties.
- **Conformers** — interconvert by bond rotation. The same molecule.

Assigning R and S requires the same three steps every time, in order:

1. Find the highest-priority group (CIP rules).
2. Orient the lowest-priority group away from the viewer.
3. Read clockwise as **R**, anticlockwise as **S**.

Candidates lose these by reordering the steps, not by getting the chemistry wrong.

The carbon with four different groups is the chiral centre. A molecule with an internal plane of symmetry is achiral even if it looks asymmetric in a flat drawing, which is the meso compound case and a recurring trap.

### Spectroscopy and the rest

Infrared identifies functional groups by their characteristic absorptions — broad O–H stretch, carbonyl stretch, N–H — and it tells you which group is present, not how the molecule is arranged. Proton NMR tells you about the number and environment of hydrogen environments: chemical shift, integration, splitting. Together they will let you identify a structure from a partial formula, which is a common DAT item type.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

### Worked item 1 — assigning R and S

**Structure.** 2-butanol: CH₃–CH(OH)–CH₂–CH₃. The stereocentre is the second carbon.

**Step 1 — list the four groups and rank them by CIP priority.**

| Group | First atom | Atoms attached to it | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| –OH | O | — | 1 |
| –CH₂CH₃ (ethyl) | C | C, H, H | 2 |
| –CH₃ (methyl) | C | H, H, H | 3 |
| –H | H | — | 4 |

Ethyl beats methyl at the first point of difference: both attach through carbon, so compare what hangs off that carbon — (C, H, H) against (H, H, H), and carbon outranks hydrogen. Oxygen and hydrogen need no comparison, they sit at the ends.

**Step 2 — orient priority 4 (H) pointing away from you.**

**Step 3 — read 1 → 2 → 3.** Clockwise is **R**; anticlockwise is **S**.

> ⚡ **Examiner Trap.** The most common loss is skipping step 2 and reading the order as drawn. Reading 1→2→3 in the flat drawing gives the wrong answer whenever the hydrogen is pointing toward the viewer rather than away. If the lowest priority is toward you, the correct reading is the *reverse* of what the drawing appears to show. Second trap: ranking ethyl below methyl because methyl "looks bigger" on the page — CIP priority is decided by atomic number at the first point of difference, never by drawn size.

### Worked item 2 — aldehyde or ketone

**Question.** Two unknown compounds, A and B, both give an orange precipitate with 2,4-DNP. With warm Tollens' reagent, A produces a silver mirror and B does not. Identify each.

**Step 1 — what 2,4-DNP tells you.** Brady's reagent reacts with *any* aldehyde or ketone to give a yellow-orange precipitate. A positive result establishes a carbonyl group and nothing further. It cannot separate an aldehyde from a ketone.

**Step 2 — what Tollens' tells you.** Tollens' is ammoniacal silver nitrate, and aldehydes reduce Ag⁺ to metallic silver while being oxidised to the carboxylate. Ketones do not.

- **A** gives the silver mirror → **aldehyde**.
- **B** does not → **ketone**.

> ⚡ **Examiner Trap.** The trap is concluding from the 2,4-DNP result that both are aldehydes. 2,4-DNP is the non-discriminating test and Tollens' is the discriminating one; items deliberately present both reagents in this order. Fehling's solution gives a brick-red Cu₂O precipitate with aliphatic aldehydes and can substitute for Tollens' — note that it does *not* work with aromatic aldehydes, so Tollens' is the safer default.

### The identification-test matrix

| Reagent | Aldehyde | Ketone | Alcohol | Carboxylic acid | Use it when |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2,4-DNP (Brady's) | ✅ precipitate | ✅ precipitate | ❌ | ❌ | Confirm a carbonyl exists |
| Tollens' (warm) | ✅ silver mirror | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | Separate aldehyde from ketone |
| Fehling's | ✅ brick-red Cu₂O | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | Same, aliphatic aldehydes only |
| Acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ | ✅ turns orange→green | ❌ | ✅ primary/secondary | ❌ | Oxidising alcohol |
| Sodium metal | ❌ | ❌ | ✅ H₂ released | ✅ H₂ released | Test for O–H |
| Br₂ in water | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | Test for C=C double bond |

### Stereoisomer comparison matrix

| Property | Enantiomers | Diastereomers | Conformers | Meso compound |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Connectivity | Same | Same | Same | Same |
| Mirror images? | Yes | No | — | Superimposable |
| Physical properties | Identical | **Different** | Identical | Identical to mirror |
| Optical rotation | Equal, opposite | Different, not equal | Identical | Zero |
| Separability | Chiral methods only | Ordinary methods | Not separable at all | n/a |
| Formed by | Reaction at a stereocentre | Reaction at ≥2 stereocentres | Bond rotation | Internal mirror plane |
| Interconvert? | No | No | **Yes, freely** | No |

The single row that decides most items is physical properties: enantiomers are identical, diastereomers are not. Choose the wrong row and every subsequent property is reasoned wrongly.

### The substitution-versus-elimination decision

| Carbon bearing the leaving group | Nucleophile + heat | Weak base + heat | Default |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary (1°) | Substitution | Elimination (minor) | Substitution |
| Secondary (2°) | Substitution | Elimination | Context-dependent |
| Tertiary (3°) | Elimination (bulky nu) | **Elimination** | Elimination |

The reasoning is steric, not electric: a tertiary carbon is too crowded for the nucleophile to attack the carbon directly, so the base removes a proton from a neighbouring carbon instead and the double bond forms.

### ⚡ Quick Exam Checklist

- Functional group identified **before** any reagent is considered
- 2026 Organic Chemistry specification version matched to your administration date
- Aldehyde/ketone question answered with Tollens', not with 2,4-DNP
- CIP priorities ranked at the first point of atomic-number difference
- Lowest-priority group oriented away *before* reading clockwise or anticlockwise
- Meso compound recognised by its internal plane of symmetry
- Substitution vs elimination decided by the degree of substitution
- Stereochemistry preserved on the product line of any stereocentre reaction
