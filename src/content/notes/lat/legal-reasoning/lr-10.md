# Legal Maxims and Doctrines

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Legal maxims** are short Latin principles (e.g., *Actus reus non facit reum nisi mens sit rea*) that capture rules of law in a single phrase. **Legal doctrines** are broader frameworks of judicial reasoning — Pakistani courts draw them from English common law, Islamic jurisprudence, and the Constitution (primarily Articles 4, 8, 10A, and 19).

The LAT rewards memorisation of the maxim's literal meaning **and** its application context. For example, *Res Ipsa Loquitur* ("the thing speaks for itself") only **raises a presumption** of negligence in tort matters like medical malpractice — it does not conclusively establish liability.

- **Stare Decisis** — superior court precedents bind lower courts (Supreme Court of Pakistan > High Courts > District Courts).
- **Audi Alteram Partem** — right to be heard; a rule of natural justice protected by Article 10A.
- **Ignorantia Juris Non Excusat** — ignorance of law is no excuse, even in Pakistani criminal trials.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Distinguishing Maxims from Doctrines

A **maxim** is a one-line Latin aphorism cited as authority. A **doctrine** is a structured rule courts apply when reasoning — for instance, the *Doctrine of Pith and Substance* resolves Union-versus-Provincial legislative conflicts by examining the legislation's true nature, not its label. The distinction matters because LAT questions often ask whether you can identify the principle versus recognise when a court would invoke it.

#### Core Categories Tested

| Category | Maxims / Doctrines | LAT Application |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Precedent | Stare Decisis, Ratio Decidendi, Obiter Dicta | Identify which part of a judgment is binding |
| Negligence | Res Ipsa Loquitur, In Pari Delicto | Torts and medical malpractice MCQs |
| Statutory Interpretation | Ejusdem Generis, Noscitur a Sociis | General Clauses Act 1897 questions |
| Criminal Law | Mens Rea, Actus Reus, Ignorantia Juris Non Excusat | Pakistan Penal Code (PPC) scenarios |
| Constitutional Justice | Audi Alteram Partem, Doctrine of Eclipse, Severability | Articles 4, 8, 10A, 19 of the Constitution |

#### High-Yield Pointers

- **Stare Decisis is not absolute in Pakistan.** The Supreme Court can overrule its own decisions (e.g., *Mansoor Ali Shah* and *Muhammad Mansha Rehman* — SCPR 2023), unlike the rigid UK position.
- **Mens Rea + Actus Reus = the guilty act coupled with a guilty mind**; both must coexist for most offences under the PPC.
- **Ejusdem Generis** restricts general words to the class of specific words preceding them; **Noscitur a Sociis** derives meaning from neighbouring words — both involve context but operate differently.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Traps

- **Res Ipsa Loquitur** is **rebuttable**, not conclusive. The defendant can still discharge presumption by showing no negligence.
- **Audi Alteram Partem** yields to national-security and emergency carve-outs under Article 10A — LAT MCQs exploit candidates who treat it as absolute.
- **Uberrima Fides** (utmost good faith) governs insurance contracts in Pakistan, but Islamic jurisprudence layers *riba* and *gharar* restrictions on top — a uniquely Pakistani dimension.

#### Worked Micro-Example

**Scenario:** A surgeon leaves a surgical instrument inside a patient after an operation. The patient sues.

**Analysis:** Apply **Res Ipsa Loquitur** — such an outcome does not occur absent negligence, so the burden shifts to the surgeon to explain. The maxim is functionally distinct from **In Pari Delicto** (equal fault), which would *bar* the plaintiff's claim if both parties were equally at fault.

#### Practice Prompts

1. A Provincial Assembly law on "education standards" is challenged as encroaching on federal currency policy. Which doctrine resolves the jurisdictional conflict?
2. A defendant argues he did not know the PPC section he violated. Does **Ignorantia Juris Non Excusat** apply?

> **Exam tip:** Mislabelling *ratio decidendi* as *obiter dicta* (or vice versa) is the single most common maxim-matching error in LAT past papers.

#### Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Treating *Stare Decisis* as UK-rigid — Pakistani courts retain overruling power.
- Applying *Res Ipsa Loquitur* where the defect could equally stem from plaintiff conduct.
- Confusing *Ejusdem Generis* with *Noscitur a Sociis* — one is class-based, the other is associative.
- Forgetting the *Doctrine of Eclipse* (Article 8) eclipses only pre-Constitution inconsistent laws, not post-Constitution statutes.
