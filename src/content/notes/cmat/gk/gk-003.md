# Modern Indian History and Freedom Struggle

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Modern Indian History spans roughly 1707 (death of Aurangzeb) or 1757 (Battle of Plassey) to 15 August 1947, covering the collapse of Mughal central authority, the East India Company's conquest, Crown rule after 1858, and the Gandhian-led national movement that secured **Purna Swaraj** and Partition. The arc is best remembered as a chain of British Acts, Congress phases, mass movements, and communal milestones.

- **British Acts chronology:** Regulating Act **1773** → Pitt's India Act **1784** → Charter Acts **1813, 1833, 1853** → Government of India Acts **1858, 1909 (Morley-Minto), 1919 (Montagu-Chelmsford), 1935**.
- **Land revenue systems:** Permanent Settlement (Cornwallis, Bengal-Bihar), Ryotwari (Munro, Madras-Bombay), Mahalwari (Northern India).
- **INC phases:** Moderates (1885–1905) → Extremists/Swadeshi (1905–1908) → Gandhian era (1919–1947).
- **Key Gandhian movements:** Champaran **1917**, Kheda **1918**, Non-Cooperation **1920–22**, Civil Disobedience/Dandi March **1930**, Quit India **1942**.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"1773–1784–1858–1919–1935"** — think **R-P-C-G-G** (**R**egulating, **P**itt's, **C**rown takeover, **G**oI Act with Dyarchy, **G**oI Act with Provincial Autonomy). For Acts-vs-Viceroys, recall: Curzon partitioned Bengal (1905) → Lord Irwin (Viceroy) hosted the **"Irwin-D Gandhiji Pact"** of **1931**, signed in **March 1931** right after the **Dandi March (12 March–6 April 1930)**.

#### Timeline of British Constitutional Acts

The CMAT frequently tests the sequence of British Acts that progressively transferred (or retained) power. Each Act introduced a structural shift, and confusing two consecutive Acts is the most common blunder.

| Act / Reform | Year | Key Provision | Person / Authority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Regulating Act | 1773 | First Governor-General of Bengal (Warren Hastings) | British Parliament |
| Pitt's India Act | 1784 | Dual control: Board of Control (political) + Court of Directors (commerce) | William Pitt |
| Charter Act | 1813 | Company's trade monopoly ended except tea/China | Lord Liverpool |
| Charter Act | 1833 | Made Governor-General of India (over all presidencies); Macaulay's education minute | William Bentinck |
| Government of India Act | 1858 | Transferred power from Company to British Crown; Secretary of State for India | Lord Canning (last GG, first Viceroy) |
| Indian Councils Act | 1892 | Indirect election, budget discussion | Lord Lansdowne |
| Morley-Minto Reforms | 1909 | Separate electorates for Muslims | Morley & Minto |
| Montagu-Chelmsford | 1919 | Dyarchy in provinces; central bicameralism | Montagu & Chelmsford |
| Government of India Act | 1935 | Provincial autonomy; federal scheme; All-India Federation | Government of India |
| Indian Independence Act | 1947 | Dominion status; Partition legalised | Mountbatten |

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** Match the following correctly:

1. Cabinet Mission
2. Mountbatten Plan
3. Cripps Mission
4. Wavell Plan

#### Codes:
- (a) 1942, 1946, 1946, 1945
- (b) 1946, 1947, 1942, 1945
- (c) 1946, 1945, 1947, 1942
- (d) 1945, 1942, 1946, 1947

#### Solution:
- **Cripps Mission** was sent in **March 1942** by Stafford Cripps during WWII — it offered Dominion status after the war but was rejected by Congress because key portfolios were denied to Indians.
- **Wavell Plan** was announced in **June 1945** by Viceroy Wavell (Shimla Conference) — proposed an interim government with equal Hindu-Muslim representation.
- **Cabinet Mission** (Pethick-Lawrence, Cripps, A.V. Alexander) arrived in **March 1946** — proposed a united federal India with weak centre; rejected the demand for a separate Pakistan. It led to the formation of the Interim Government under Nehru (September 1946).
- **Mountbatten Plan** (3 June Plan) was announced on **3 June 1947** by Lord Mountbatten — accepted the demand for Partition and led to the Indian Independence Act 1947.

Hence the correct sequence is **1946, 1947, 1942, 1945 → Option (b)**.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students frequently reverse the **Cabinet Mission (1946)** and the **Wavell Plan (1945)** because both attempted to keep India united. Remember: **Cripps (1942) came first chronologically**, then Wavell (1945), then Cabinet Mission (1946), and finally Mountbatten (1947). Memorise the year of every Mission as **C-W-C-M** with **42-45-46-47**.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Common Misconceptions

1. **1857 Revolt — not a planned nationalist war.** It began as a sepoy mutiny at Meerut on **10 May 1857** triggered by the greased cartridges (cow and pig fat). It acquired political character under **Bahadur Shah Zafar** in Delhi and regional character under **Rani Lakshmibai (Jhansi), Tantia Tope (Kanpur/Gwalior), Kunwar Singh (Bihar), and Begum Hazrat Mahal (Lucknow)** — but it lacked unified leadership, modern political ideology, and a unified vision of India. Examiners reward the **balanced interpretation**, not "First War of Independence" as fact.
2. **Two-Nation Theory chronology.** The Muslim League was founded at **Dhaka (1906)** under **Nawab Salimullah** and initially cooperated with Congress — the **Lucknow Pact (1916)** saw joint demands. The Pakistan demand crystallised only with **Jinnah's 14 Points (1929)** and the **Lahore Resolution (March 1940)**. Jinnah's presidential address at Lahore on **23 March 1940** is the official birth point of the separatist demand.
3. **Quit India Movement mechanics.** Launched on **8 August 1942** at Gowalia Tank, Bombay, with the slogan **"Do or Die"**. Within 24 hours, the entire Congress Working Committee — Gandhi, Nehru, Patel, Maulana Azad — was arrested. The movement was sustained underground through Congress Radio (Usha Mehta) and parallel governments in Ballia, Satara, Tamluk, and parts of Bihar.
4. **INA's actual military impact.** The **Azad Hind Fauj** was formed in **Singapore on 4 July 1943** under Subhas Chandra Bose with the help of the Japanese. It fought alongside Japan at **Imphal and Kohima (1944)**, where it was defeated along with Japanese forces. INA's real legacy is the **Red Fort Trials (November 1945)** of Shah Nawaz, Prem Kumar Sahgal, and Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon, whose defence lawyers included Nehru, Patel, and Bhulabhai Desai — this crystallised public sympathy across India.
5. **Government of India Act 1935 — the most-misread Act.** Students wrongly attribute **provincial autonomy** to the **1919 Act**; in reality, **1919 introduced Dyarchy**, while **1935 introduced Provincial Autonomy** (responsible government in provinces) and the **abolition of Dyarchy at the centre**. Federalism was **never fully implemented** because Princely States did not accede.

#### Tricky Distinctions CMAT Tests

| Tricky Pair | Actual Distinction |
|---|---|
| Viceroy vs Governor-General | Governor-General governed **before 1858** (under Company); Viceroy governed **after 1858** (under Crown). **Canning** was the last GG and first Viceroy. |
| Swaraj vs Purna Swaraj | **Swaraj** = self-rule within Empire (Tilak's 1906 demand); **Purna Swaraj** = complete independence (Lahore Session, December 1929, Jawaharlal Nehru as President). |
| Simon Commission vs Round Table Conferences | **Simon Commission (1928)** — all-British, no Indian member, led to "Simon Go Back"; **RTCs (1930–32)** — included Indian delegates (B.R. Ambedkar, Sapru, Tej Bahadur Sapru). |
| Bardoli vs Champaran | **Bardoli (1928)** — peasants of Gujarat led by Sardar Patel against enhanced land revenue; **Champaran (1917)** — indigo planters' exploitation, first Gandhian satyagraha in India. |
| Khilafat vs Non-Cooperation | **Khilafat (1919–24)** was about preserving the Ottoman Caliphate's authority, led by the Ali Brothers; **Non-Cooperation (1920–22)** was broader Indian political movement — they merged under Gandhi in 1920. |
| HSRA vs Anushilan Samiti | **Anushilan Samiti** — earlier revolutionary outfit (1902), Bengal-based; **HSRA (Hindustan Socialist Republican Association)** — founded **1928** in Delhi by **Bhagat Singh, Chandrashekhar Azad, Sukhdev** to fuse nationalism with socialism. |

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. **Chronological sorting:** Place the following in order — Lahore Resolution, Lucknow Pact, Karachi Session, Tripuri Session, Faizpur Session. (Hint: recall that Faizpur 1936 was the **first Congress session held in a village**; Karachi 1931 passed the **Fundamental Rights resolution**; Tripuri 1939 saw the **Bose-Gandhi clash** over the Tripuri presidency.)
2. **Viceroy linkage:** Match the Viceroy to the event — Irwin (Dandi March pact), Linlithgow (Quit India arrests, longest-serving Viceroy), Wavell (Shimla Conference, INA trials), Mountbatten (Partition, 3 June Plan). Linlithgow served from **1936 to 1943** — his tenure saw both the **August Offer (1940)** and **Quit India (1942)**.

> 💡 **High-Yield Revision Hook:** For British Acts use **"RPSC-GG-GI"** (Regulating-Pitt-Charter-Subsidiary alliance-Government of India Acts). For Movements use **"CKRQ"** (Champaran-Kheda-Rowlatt-Quit India). For Congress sessions use **"LF-K-T"** (Lahore 1929, Faizpur 1936, Karachi 1931, Tripuri 1939).
