# The UAEU CAT Expects Students to Label Each

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Topic 1** anchors the UAE University CAT Science component with 3% weight, almost always as 1–2 short conceptual MCQs. Master the **scientific method** (observation → hypothesis → experiment → analysis → conclusion), the seven **SI base units**, and the five core formulas below before moving to higher-weight Science modules.

- Density (ρ) = mass ÷ volume, in **kg/m³**
- Speed (v) = distance ÷ time, in **m/s**
- Force (F) = mass × acceleration, in **N** (kg·m/s²)
- Work (W) = force × distance, in **J** (N·m)
- Power (P) = work ÷ time, in **W** (J/s)

| Term | Quick meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Mass | Amount of matter, in **kg** |
| Weight | Gravitational force, in **N** = mg |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### The scientific method

The UAEU CAT expects students to label each step of an experiment correctly and to distinguish a **hypothesis** (a testable prediction) from a **theory** (a widely supported explanation). A controlled experiment varies one **independent variable**, measures the **dependent variable**, and keeps all other factors constant as **controlled variables**. A **control group** receives no treatment and is mandatory for valid comparison, not optional.

#### SI units and measurement

The seven **SI base units** — metre (m), kilogram (kg), second (s), kelvin (K), mole (mol), ampere (A), candela (cd) — combine into derived units such as newton, joule, and watt. **Accuracy** means closeness to the true value; **precision** means reproducibility of repeated readings.

#### Matter, energy, and the working formulas

Matter divides into **elements** (single-atom species), **compounds** (chemically bonded), and **mixtures** (physically combined). Physical changes are reversible (ice melting); chemical changes form new substances (iron rusting). Energy interconverts between **kinetic**, **gravitational potential**, and **thermal** forms, with total energy conserved.

| Quantity | Formula | SI unit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Density | ρ = m / V | kg/m³ |
| Speed | v = d / t | m/s |
| Force | F = m·a | N |
| Work | W = F·d | J |
| Power | P = W / t | W |

- Treat **mass (kg)** and **weight (N)** as different quantities; weight = mg on Earth.
- Convert all quantities to SI before substituting into any formula.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge cases and common traps

The most frequent UAEU CAT error on this topic is treating hypothesis and theory as synonyms — examiners word options like "a hypothesis becomes a theory after one successful experiment" to trap students. Another classic trap lists weight and mass as interchangeable; remember weight is a **force** measured in newtons, while mass is a **scalar** measured in kilograms. Unit-inconsistency errors appear when candidates keep grams and centimetres instead of converting to kilograms and metres before applying density = m/V.

#### Worked micro-example

A copper block has mass 178 g and volume 20 cm³. Convert: 178 g = 0.178 kg, 20 cm³ = 20 × 10⁻⁶ m³ = 2.0 × 10⁻⁵ m³. Density = 0.178 ÷ 2.0 × 10⁻⁵ = **8900 kg/m³**, matching the accepted value for copper. The same block's weight on Earth is W = mg = 0.178 × 9.8 ≈ **1.74 N**.
