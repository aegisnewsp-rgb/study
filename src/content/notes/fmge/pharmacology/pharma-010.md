# Toxicology, Pharmacology, and Dermatological Agents

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Pharmacology** studies how drugs act on living systems (pharmacodynamics) and how the body handles them (pharmacokinetics: ADME). **Toxicology** quantifies adverse effects through the dose–response curve, while **dermatological agents** are topically applied drugs whose absorption is limited by the stratum corneum.

- **Therapeutic Index (TI) = TD50 / ED50** — a dimensionless safety ratio; higher means wider margin.
- **Hill equation**: E = Emax × [C]ⁿ / (EC50ⁿ + [C]ⁿ) — defines graded dose–response.
- **t½ = 0.693 × Vd / Cl** — half-life links volume of distribution and clearance.

High-yield FMGE triggers: organophosphate → **atropine + pralidoxime**; paracetamol overdose → **N-acetylcysteine**; opioid overdose → **naloxone**; **avoid adrenaline in cocaine toxicity** (benzodiazepines first-line).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Pharmacokinetic Framework
ADME governs drug concentration at the receptor. **Clearance (Cl)** is volume of plasma cleared per unit time (L/min); **Volume of distribution (Vd)** is the apparent volume needed to contain the total drug at plasma concentration (L or L/kg). Together they fix half-life via t½ = 0.693 × Vd / Cl — extending this relationship is a recurring FMGE numerical.

#### Pharmacodynamics & Dose–Response
Receptor agonists possess **intrinsic activity** and **affinity**; antagonists possess affinity without efficacy. The graded response follows the Hill equation. The **quantal dose–response** plots population fraction responding, yielding ED50 (50% effective) and LD50 (50% lethal). The therapeutic window sits between ED50 and LD50, summarised by TI.

#### Mechanisms of Toxicity
Toxicants act via receptor overstimulation, covalent protein binding, oxidative stress (GSH depletion — classic in paracetamol), and mitochondrial dysfunction. **Idiosyncratic reactions** are unpredictable, dose-independent host responses.

#### Topical Pharmacokinetics
Stratum corneum is the rate-limiting barrier. Fick's law dictates flux: J = (D × K × ΔC) / h, where D is diffusion coefficient, K the partition coefficient, and h the stratum corneum thickness. Hydration and occlusion dramatically increase h⁻¹, allowing systemic absorption of potent steroids.

#### Common Mistake
> Mixing up ED50 vs LD50 costs marks instantly — remember ED = Effective, LD = Lethal.

| Antidote | Poison | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| N-acetylcysteine | Paracetamol | Replenishes glutathione |
| Atropine + PAM | Organophosphates | Muscarinic blockade + AChE reactivation |
| Naloxone | Opioids | μ-receptor competitive antagonist |
| Flumazenil | Benzodiazepines | GABA-A receptor antagonist |
| Sodium thiosulphate | Cyanide | Sulphur donor → rhodanese pathway |

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Worked Calculation: Therapeutic Index
A drug has ED50 = 50 mg and LD50 = 1000 mg. **TI = 1000 / 50 = 20**. A second drug has ED50 = 10 mg, LD50 = 120 mg → TI = 12. Drug A is safer by ratio, but absolute safety also depends on plasma protein binding and steepness of the dose–response curve. FMGE often tests whether a wider TI guarantees safety — it does not.

#### Dermatological Agents — Mechanisms
- **Topical corticosteroids** inhibit phospholipase A2, blocking arachidonic acid release; potency is graded Groups I (superpotent: clobetasol propionate) to VII (mild: hydrocortisone 1%).
- **Retinoids** (tretinoin, adapalene) bind retinoic acid receptors (RAR/RXR), normalising keratinocyte differentiation.
- **Azole antifungals** inhibit lanosterol 14α-demethylase (CYP51), depleting ergosterol.
- **Terbinafine** inhibits squalene epoxidase — distinct mechanism, remember this for MCQs.
- **Calcineurin inhibitors** (tacrolimus, pimecrolimus) bind FKBP-12, blocking IL-2 transcription in T-cells.

#### Organophosphate Poisoning Management
Atropine must be **titrated to drying of bronchial secretions**, not given as a fixed total dose. Pralidoxime reactivates acetylcholinesterase only before "aging" occurs (~24 h for most agents). Avoid morphine, aminophylline, and theophylline — they worsen bronchospasm.

#### Adjacent Topic Links
First-pass hepatic metabolism explains why **nitroglycerin** is given sublingual and why oral propranolol has low bioavailability (~25%). This connects to dermatology through transdermal nitroglycerin patches — bypassing first-pass metabolism entirely.

#### Practice Prompts
1. Calculate the TI of a drug where ED50 = 25 mg/kg and LD50 = 750 mg/kg; comment on its safety versus one with TI = 5 but lower ED50.
2. A 30-year-old with organophosphate poisoning receives 2 mg atropine IV with persistent wheeze. Outline next steps and the rationale for adding pralidoxime within 24 hours.

> Exam trap: potent topical steroids under occlusion can suppress the HPA axis — always specify potency class (I–VII) in prescription MCQs.
