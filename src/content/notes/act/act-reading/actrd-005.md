# Integration of Knowledge: Comparative Paired Passages — ACT Reading Notes

Paired passage sets (Passage A and Passage B) require answering questions on each passage separately and then answering synthesis questions comparing both. In the official ACT examination, this topic carries a weight rating of 4 out of 5 and appears consistently across testing administrations. Candidates preparing for high percentiles must master both theoretical fundamentals and rapid practical problem-solving strategies.

- **Core Principle**: Read Passage A and answer Passage A questions; then read Passage B and answer Passage B questions; finish with synthesis questions.

#### Conceptual framework and solving methodology

To secure consistent accuracy on Integration of Knowledge: Comparative Paired Passages, candidates should follow a structured three-step execution framework:

1. **Identify What the Item Actually Asks**:
   Begin by identifying the exact question asked. For paired passages, mark which paired-passage relationship the question tests, such as agree, disagree, or combined stance.

2. **Execute Systematic Solution Protocol**:
   Apply official methodology rather than intuition. Read Passage A and answer Passage A questions; then read Passage B and answer Passage B questions; finish with synthesis questions. Mark your evidence by jotting one claim per passage and noting the author behind each.

### 🔴 Deep Dive — Mastery & Edge Cases (3mo–2yr)

#### Advanced variations and high-difficulty edge cases

When targeting top-decile scores, students must prepare for non-standard variations:

- **Shared Ground**: List the issue both passages address, record where they agree, and reject any response that ignores their common concern when it is the main point.
- **Divergence Map**: Sort each author's stance, method, and evidence into separate columns, then explain the sharpest meaningful difference or disagreement in scope between them.
- **Synthesis Choice**: Select the option that preserves each text's position, cites support from both, and follows the prompt's requested focus rather than a vague common theme.

#### Systematic drill schedule

Consistent mastery requires deliberate retrieval practice:
- **Week 1**: Foundational concept review and untimed drills focusing exclusively on accuracy.
- **Week 2**: Timed sets of 10 to 15 questions, tracking error root causes in an error log.
- **Week 3**: Full section practice integrated into complete mock examinations to build cognitive endurance and pacing discipline.
