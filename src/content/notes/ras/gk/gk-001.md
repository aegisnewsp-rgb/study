# Government Schemes and Welfare Programmes

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Welfare programmes are the delivery arm of Indian governance — they transfer cash, kind, or services from the state to a defined beneficiary group. For RPSC RAS, two facts matter most: schemes split into **Centrally Sponsored (Centre + State share)**, **Central Sector (100% Central)**, and **State (100% State, e.g. Rajasthan)**, and the **JAM Trinity (Jan Dhan + Aadhaar + Mobile)** is the leak-proof delivery pipeline behind DBT.

- **Centrally Sponsored Scheme (CSS)** — shared funding, e.g. MGNREGA, PMAY
- **Central Sector Scheme** — 100% Central funding, e.g. PM-KISAN
- **State Scheme** — 100% State funding, e.g. Mukhyamantri Chiranjeevi Yojana (Rajasthan)
- **DBT** — Direct Benefit Transfer to Aadhaar-linked Jan Dhan accounts
- **JAM Trinity** — Jan Dhan + Aadhaar + Mobile

| Scheme | Sector | Core entitlement |
|---|---|---|
| MGNREGA | CSS | 100 days wage employment per rural household per FY |
| PM-KISAN | Central Sector | ₹6,000/year to landholding farmer families (3 instalments) |
| Ayushman Bharat-PMJAY | CSS | ₹5 lakh/family/year hospital cover |
| Chiranjeevi Yojana | Rajasthan State | ₹10 lakh family + ₹25 lakh accident cover |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook — "C–C–S, like CSS code":** **C**entrally **S**ponsored = **C**entre + **S**tate split; **C**entral **S**ector = **C**entre only. Two words, two C's, one rule.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Working understanding for the bulk of your preparation window.

#### Funding classification (the highest-yield MCQ stem)

Every Indian welfare scheme falls into one of three funding buckets. RPSC repeatedly frames options so that confusing CSS with a Central Sector scheme picks the wrong answer. The share between Centre and State for CSS varies — typically 60:40 or 90:10 (NE/north-eastern States), while Central Sector schemes leave the State only the implementation role.

#### Targeting approaches

Not every scheme targets the same way. Three mechanisms appear on the RAS paper:

- **Universal** — everyone qualifies (e.g. NFSA's coverage of roughly two-thirds of the population under the Public Distribution System)
- **Self-targeting** — only the poor will opt in because the design is unattractive to the non-poor (e.g. MGNREGA's manual rural work)
- **Means-tested** — an explicit income or deprivation cut-off (e.g. SECC-2011 deprivation criteria for PMJAY in rural areas)

#### DBT and the JAM Trinity

**DBT (Direct Benefit Transfer)** pushes subsidies, pensions, and scholarships straight into a beneficiary's bank account. **JAM Trinity** — **J**an Dhan (bank account) + **A**adhaar (identity) + **M**obile (communication) — is the technical stack that makes DBT leak-proof. PMEAC has called this the platform for the "less-cash economy."

#### Comparison matrix: easily confused scheme pairs

| Confused pair | What they actually do | Don't mix these up |
|---|---|---|
| PMAY-U vs PMAY-G | Urban housing vs Gramin (rural) housing; unit size and unit cost norms differ | Tag them by geography, not by acronym |
| Indira Gandhi Smartphone Yojana vs Bhamashah | Free smartphone to female head of family vs DBT family-ID platform | Both Rajasthan; one is a device, the other is a database |
| Palanhaar vs Chhatra Sambal | ₹2,000/month to unmarried girls after Class 10 vs stipend to poor students in professional/college courses | Different life stages, different ministries |
| MGNREGA 100 vs 150 days | 100 days is the general guarantee; 150 days is reserved for notified disaster/special categories | Don't round up the headline figure |

#### A worked Mains-style framing question

"RPSC Mains GS-II asks: critically examine the role of DBT and JAM Trinity in reducing subsidy leakages in India." Here is the resolution shape examiners reward:

1. **State the mechanism.** DBT credits the beneficiary's Aadhaar-linked bank account; JAM Trinity closes the identity-and-payment loop.
2. **Name the measurable wins.** Reduced ghost beneficiaries, lower intermediation cost, faster transfers.
3. **Name the genuine limits.** Aadhaar authentication failures, exclusion errors, last-mile digital access in rural Rajasthan, grievance redressal.
4. **Link to a scheme.** PM-KISAN, PDS kerosene substitution, PAHAL (LPG), and MGNREGA wages are the canonical examples.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Writing "DBT removes all leakages" — full marks will not follow. Examiners want exclusion-error analysis (Aadhaar seeding failures, bank-account dormancy) and a concrete post-2014 scheme reference, not a slogan.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Edge cases, scheme-specific traps, and the Rajasthan-specific layer that carries disproportionate weight in RAS.

#### Rajasthan-specific layer (where RAS gains its edge)

The state paper disproportionately tests Rajasthan schemes. Five appear year after year:

- **Mukhyamantri Chiranjeevi Swasthya Bima Yojana** — combined accident and family-floater health cover; voluntary enrolment with a stated annual premium differentiated by rural/urban category.
- **Indira Gandhi Smartphone Yojana** — smartphone to the woman listed as head of family on the Bhamashah record.
- **Mukhyamantri Chhatra Sambal Yojana** — stipend to economically weak students in approved higher-education and professional courses.
- **Palanhaar Yojana** — monthly support to unmarried girls after Class 10, conditional on continued education and a guardianship bond.
- **Bhamashah Card / Bhamashah Yojana** — family-ID and DBT platform, not a transfer by itself; it is the rails on which other transfers run.

The classic trap here is treating Bhamashah as a "free money" scheme. It is an identity and payment infrastructure; the transfer itself comes from another scheme riding on top of it.

#### Advanced traps and exceptions

1. **PM-KISAN exclusions** — income-tax payers, government employees, and institutional landholders are ineligible; the scheme pays landholding farmer **families**, not every farmer.
2. **NFSA vs PDS** — the National Food Security Act entitles roughly 67% of the population to subsidised grains under TPDS; do not equate PDS with NFSA — NFSA is the legal entitlement, PDS is the delivery channel.
3. **MGNREGA 150-day trigger** — the extra 50 days are for households in notified drought/flood-affected or hard-rock-mining blocks, not a default.
4. **PMAY-U vs PMAY-G implementing ministries** — Urban component sits with MoHUA, Gramin component with MoRD; confusing these breaks a "ministry-based" MCQ.
5. **Sub-Mission naming** — DAY-NRLM (rural livelihoods) and DAY-NULM (urban livelihoods) are siblings under the NRLM umbrella; POSHAN Abhiyaan and Samagra Shiksha are independent verticals, not sub-missions of NRLM.
6. **NITI Aayog vs erstwhile Planning Commission** — SDG-linked scheme monitoring is now a NITI Aayog function; the Planning Commission is dissolved, so any option pairing "Planning Commission approves annual plan of these schemes" is outdated.

#### Two advanced practice prompts

1. Frame a 15-mark Mains answer comparing PMAY-U and PMAY-G on **eligibility**, **unit cost norms**, and **implementing ministry** — conclude with which addresses urban-rural housing inequality better.
2. Construct a 10-MCQ mini-paper on Rajasthan schemes where each wrong option swaps Chiranjeevi with PMJAY, or Palanhaar with Chhatra Sambal; then resolve each swap with one sentence each.
