# JUPEB Subject Combinations

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

JUPEB Subject Combinations is the administrative rulebook that decides which three A'Level subjects you register for and, ultimately, which Nigerian university course you can enter through Direct Entry (DE). Every candidate must pick **two Principal subjects plus one Elective** from the approved matrix published by the Joint Universities Preliminary Examinations Board.

- **Three subjects, two groups rule:** your trio must follow the JUPEB-approved matrix; cross-group pairings are restricted.
- **Minimum DE grades:** A–E across all three subjects, with **at most one D** — two Ds or any F disqualifies you.
- **Combinations lock DE eligibility:** Government + Economics + Accounting unlocks Management courses; Biology + Chemistry + Physics/Mathematics unlocks Medicine and most Sciences.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### How JUPEB groups its subjects

JUPEB classifies every examinable subject into one of three groups. Your Principal–Elective trio must respect these groupings, because universities and JAMB only accept DE applications whose subjects match the course's faculty.

| Group | Representative subjects | Typical DE destination |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sciences | Biology, Chemistry, Physics, Mathematics, Further Maths | Medicine, Engineering, Pharmacy, Sciences |
| Arts / Humanities | Government, History, Literature in English, CRS, IRS, French, Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa | Law, Arts, Social Sciences, Education |
| Management / Commercial | Accounting, Economics, Business Management, Government | Accounting, Business Admin, Banking & Finance |

Government appears in both Arts and Management because of its content overlap with Economics and Political Science faculties.

#### The Principal + Elective structure

Your two Principal subjects carry the heaviest weight on the A'Level result, while the **Elective is compulsory** — not optional — and is graded A–F like the Principals. Further Mathematics is a common Elective for science candidates aiming at Engineering, but it is only offered at selected study centres, so confirm availability before paying fees.

#### Matrix logic and restrictions

Most approved combinations stay within one group or bridge two related groups. Restricted pairings — such as combining Economics with certain Mathematics variants or pairing Literature with non-Arts Electives — are flagged in the JUPEB matrix and must be checked at jupeb.edu.ng/examination-subjects/. A registration that ignores the matrix is treated as invalid, even if you later score A grades.

#### Direct Entry grade floor

| Grade | DE status |
| --- | --- |
| A, B, C, E | Eligible when paired with at most one D across the three subjects |
| Two D grades | Ineligible for DE |
| Any F grade | Ineligible for DE regardless of other scores |

#### Standard exam patterns

JUPEB examiners test this topic as short administrative-theory questions inside General Studies or as scenario items: *"Which combination is valid for a Law DE candidate?"* or *"State the minimum grade condition for DE eligibility."* Expect 1–2 items worth roughly 3% of total marks.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge cases in the matrix

The matrix looks simple until you hit its fringes. **Further Mathematics** is only a valid Elective if your study centre runs it; **Literature in English** paired with an Arts Principal such as Government is accepted, but Literature + Economics is rarely approved. **Government + CRS + Economics** works for many Social Science DE routes, while **Government + Economics + Accounting** is the canonical Management trio — using both Economics and Accounting as Principals is permitted but heavy on quantitative load.

Candidates should also note that **subjects with heavy content overlap** (e.g. Economics alongside certain Management options) may be re-classified by individual universities during DE screening, so the JUPEB matrix alone is not always the final word.

#### Worked example: matching combination to DE course

A candidate wants Medicine through DE. The approved trio is:

1. **Biology (Principal)**
2. **Chemistry (Principal)**
3. **Physics or Mathematics (Elective)**

If the candidate scores B, B, C — that satisfies "A–E with at most one D", so the result is DE-eligible for Medicine, Dentistry, Pharmacy, and most Biological Sciences.

If instead the candidate scores **B, D, D**, the result fails the DE floor (two D grades) and JAMB DE forms will be rejected even though the combination itself was valid.

#### Common registration mistakes

1. Picking Further Maths as Elective at a study centre that does not offer it, leading to forced subject change mid-programme.
2. Assuming any cross-group trio is allowed — Sciences + Arts cross-listing is usually blocked.
3. Treating the Elective as a "free" third subject; it appears on the A'Level transcript and counts toward DE scoring.
4. Switching DE course ambition after registering (e.g. Economics trio → Engineering) — the original combination locks the DE faculty.
