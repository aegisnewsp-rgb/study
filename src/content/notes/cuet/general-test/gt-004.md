# Static GK

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Static GK is the **evergreen, factual** portion of General Knowledge tested in the CUET UG General Test. It covers permanent knowledge — Indian polity, geography, history, economy, general science, national symbols, awards, books and authors — that does **not** change with news cycles.

- **What it is:** Textbook-based, stable facts (e.g., the Constitution came into force on **26 January 1950**, the **Reserve Bank of India** was established on **1 April 1935**).
- **What it is NOT:** Current Affairs (recent schemes, summits, sports results) — those form a separate CUET head.
- **Where it appears:** General Test section of CUET UG, which carries **3% weightage** in overall merit for most participating universities. Roughly **10–15 questions per slot** are drawn from the static heads.
- **Seven core heads to revise:** Indian Polity & Constitution, Indian Geography, Indian History (Ancient/Medieval/Modern), Indian Economy, General Science basics, National Symbols & Awards, Books & Authors + Important Days.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"PHEGS–NAB"** for the 7 static heads — **P**olity, **H**istory, **E**conomy, **G**eography, **S**cience, **N**ational symbols, **A**wards/**B**ooks-and-authors. Revise in this order on exam morning; Polity + Symbols give the densest question bank per minute of reading.

#### Core Definitions

**Static GK** = fixed, reference-book knowledge (textbooks, NCERT class 6–12, official government compendiums). **Dynamic GK / Current Affairs** = news-driven knowledge with a shelf life of weeks or months. The CUET General Test tests both, but Static GK contributes roughly **30–40%** of the paper, making it the highest-return revision segment.

#### Comparison Matrix — Commonly Confused Static GK Pairs

| Confused Pair | Correct Distinction | Examiner-Style Swap |
|---|---|---|
| Constituent Assembly **President** vs Drafting Committee **Chairman** | President = Dr. Rajendra Prasad; Chairman = Dr. B.R. Ambedkar (with 6 other members) | Options swap "President" and "Chairman" |
| National **Anthem** vs National **Song** | Anthem = *Jana Gana Mana* (Tagore); Song = *Vande Mataram* (Bankim Chandra) | "Which is the national song?" often gets *Jana Gana Mana* |
| Highest peak in India vs Highest in the world | India (administered) = Kangchenjunga **8,586 m**; World = Everest 8,849 m; K2 8,611 m lies in PoK | "K2 is the highest peak in India" — wrong |
| Fundamental Rights vs Fundamental Duties | Rights in Part III (originally 7, now **6** after 44th Amendment removed Right to Property, 1978); Duties in Part IV-A (**11 duties**, added by 42nd Amendment, 1976) | Mixing count and Part numbers |
| Planning Commission vs NITI Aayog | Planning Commission = 1950–2014; NITI Aayog = from **1 Jan 2015** | "Planning Commission replaced NITI Aayog in 2020" — wrong direction |
| Tropic of Cancer states | Passes through **8 states**: Gujarat, Rajasthan, MP, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura, Mizoram | "7 states" or "9 states" — both wrong |

#### Key Principles for Answering

1. **Read the exact wording** — "highest peak *in India*" vs "*administered by India*" changes the answer.
2. **Memorise numbers, not vague ideas** — Article 12–35 (Rights), 36–51 (DPSPs), 51-A (Duties), 395 → 470+ Articles.
3. **Match the body to its Article** — CAG (Art. 148), UPSC (Art. 315), Election Commission (Art. 324).
4. **Use dates as anchors** — 26 Nov 1949 (adopted), 26 Jan 1950 (enforced), 1 April 1935 (RBI), 1 July 2017 (GST).
5. **Flag-swap traps first** — in Polity and Symbols, 70% of wrong options are name/role swaps.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** Which of the following statements about the Indian Constitution is **INCORRECT**?
(a) The Constituent Assembly was presided over by Dr. Rajendra Prasad.
(b) The Drafting Committee was chaired by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar.
(c) The Right to Property is a Fundamental Right under Article 31.
(d) Fundamental Duties were added by the 42nd Amendment in 1976.

#### Solution:
- Statement (a): **Correct** — Dr. Rajendra Prasad was President of the Constituent Assembly.
- Statement (b): **Correct** — Dr. B.R. Ambedkar chaired the Drafting Committee (8 members total).
- Statement (c): **Incorrect** — The Right to Property was originally Article 31, but was **removed as a Fundamental Right** by the **44th Amendment, 1978**, and made a legal right under Article 300-A.
- Statement (d): **Correct** — 11 Fundamental Duties in Article 51-A were added by the **42nd Amendment, 1976**.

#### Answer: (c)

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Option (c) is technically true *historically* (Article 31 did exist) but **false *currently***. CUET consistently tests the **present-day** status. The moment you read "Right to Property" with "Fundamental Right," mark it wrong — it was demoted in 1978.

#### Geography Edge Cases

1. **Kangchenjunga vs K2 vs Everest** — Highest peak **on Indian-administered soil** is Kangchenjunga (8,586 m, Sikkim). K2 (8,611 m) is in Pakistan-occupied Kashmir, so it is **not** within India's pre-2019 effective border. Everest is in Nepal.
2. **Largest vs smallest by area** — State: Rajasthan > Goa. UT: Jammu & Kashmir (post-2019 reorganisation) > others.
3. **Largest by population** — Uttar Pradesh leads; among UTs, Delhi.
4. **Tropic of Cancer** crosses **8 Indian states**, not 7 or 9.
5. **Longest river entirely within India** is often contested; the **Ganga (2,525 km)** is the most-cited answer in NCERT.

#### General Science Quick-Facts

- **SI base units (7):** metre (length), kilogram (mass), second (time), ampere (current), kelvin (temperature), mole (amount), candela (luminous intensity).
- **Formulas to recognise:** Water = H₂O; common salt = NaCl; washing soda = Na₂CO₃·10H₂O; baking soda = NaHCO₃.
- **Photosynthesis:** 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ (light-driven, in chloroplasts).
- **pH scale:** 0 (most acidic) → 7 (neutral) → 14 (most alkaline).

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. *Assertion-Reason:* "Assertion (A): The Planning Commission was replaced by NITI Aayog. Reason (R): NITI Aayog was established on 1 January 2015." — Is (A) true, (R) true, and does (R) correctly explain (A)?
2. *Multi-statement elimination:* "Which of the following is/are national symbol(s) of India — (i) Lotus (ii) Ganga (iii) Banyan (iv) King Cobra" — choose the correct combination.

#### Common Traps to Pre-empt

- **Capital-vs-currency** swaps (Canberra not Sydney; Yen for Japan, Yuan for China).
- **Bharat Ratna** — India's highest civilian award; instituted **2 January 1954**; first recipients (1954): C. Rajagopalachari, S. Radhakrishnan, C.V. Raman.
- **Author–book swaps** — *Discovery of India* (Nehru), *Gitanjali* (Tagore), *Arthashastra* (Kautilya/Chanakya).
