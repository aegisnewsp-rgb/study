# Pakistan's Security Challenges: Terrorism, Militancy, and Internal Security

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Pakistan's internal security challenge is a three-layered problem: terrorism (TTP, ISKP), militancy/insurgency (Balochistan, merged ex-FATA), and sectarian extremism (SC-LeJ nexus). The state responds through an institutional architecture — NACTA (coordinator), provincial CTDs (operational), FIA, PNRA/FinTRACA (financial) — guided by the **National Action Plan (NAP, December 2014)** under the **National Internal Security Policy (NISP 2014–18)**, now superseded by the broader **National Security Policy 2022 ("comprehensive security")**. Key operations: **Zarb-e-Azb (2015)** and **Radd-ul-Fasaad (2017)**.

- **NAP 2014:** 20-point counter-terror agenda — military action, madrassa reform, hate-speech curbs, counter-financing.
- **FATF:** Pakistan grey-listed **June 2018**, fully **delisted 21 October 2022** (Paris plenary).
- **Economic cost:** Terrorism estimated to shave **6–7% off GDP** (early-2010s figure, frequently tested).
- **NSP 2022:** shift from securitised counter-terror to economy + human-security framing.

| Term | Acronym | Role |
|---|---|---|
| NACTA | National Counter-Terrorism Authority | Federal coordinator (not operational) |
| CTD | Counter-Terrorism Department | Provincial investigation/operations |
| FinTRACA | Financial Monitoring Unit | Suspicious-transaction reporting |
| TTP | Tehrik-i-Taliban Pakistan | Domestic Taliban insurgency |
| NISP / NSP | Internal / National Security Policy | Doctrinal frameworks |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"N-C-F-Z-R"** = **N**ACTA (coordinate), **C**TD (catch), **F**inTRACA (freeze money), **Z**arb-e-Azb + **R**add-ul-Fasaad (kill/clear). Pakistan's counter-terror chain in five letters.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Threat Typology: Three Streams
Pakistani security literature — and NACTA briefings — divide internal threats into three streams that examiners expect candidates to label clearly:

1. **Terrorism / jihadist violence** — TTP, Islamic State Khorasan Province (ISKP), Al-Qaeda affiliates; tactic is mass-casualty bombings, suicide attacks, target killings of security personnel.
2. **Ethno-nationalist insurgency** — Balochistan Liberation Army (BLA) and Balochistan Liberation Front (BLF) demanding resource-share and political autonomy; active in Balochistan and parts of southern KPK.
3. **Sectarian extremism** — Sipah-e-Sahaba Pakistan (SSP/SC) as the political/mass wing, Lashkar-e-Jhangvi (LeJ) as its armed militant offshoot; concentrated in southern Punjab (Jhang, Multan corridor) and urban Sindh (Karachi).

#### Institutional Architecture
The federal architecture layers four functions: coordination, investigation, finance, and operations.

| Institution | Established | Mandate |
|---|---|---|
| NACTA | NACTA Act 2013 | Federal coordinator, policy adviser; **not operational** |
| Provincial CTDs | 1990s onward | Investigation, arrests, prosecution of terrorism cases |
| FIA (Cyber Crime Wing) | 1975 (reformed) | Financial/cyber-crime, terror-financing probes |
| PNRA + FinTRACA (under SBP) | Anti-Money Laundering Act 2010; PNRA Act 2019 | Freeze terror assets, STR/CTR reporting |
| Armed forces operations | Zarb-e-Azb 2015 / Radd-ul-Fasaad 2017 | Kinetic phase; cleared North Waziristan, Swat, Karachi |

#### Concept Comparison Matrix
Examiners love to mix up these adjacent-sounding terms — keep them apart.

| Pair | Key Distinction | Exam Gotcha |
|---|---|---|
| TTP vs Afghan Taliban | TTP is Pakistani; fights Islamabad; opposed Afghan-Talihan peace | "Same organisation" is wrong |
| NACTA vs CTD | NACTA plans; CTD operates | "NACTA makes arrests" — false |
| SC (Sipah-e-Sahaba) vs LeJ | SC = political party; LeJ = armed wing | Treating them as the same body |
| NISP 2014 vs NSP 2022 | NISP = internal-security-only; NSP = economy + human security | Quoting NISP as current doctrine |
| PATA vs FATA | PATA = Provincially Administered Tribal Areas; FATA = Federally Administered Tribal Areas; merged into KPK in 2018 | FATA continued post-2018 |
| Grey list vs Black list | Grey = increased monitoring; Black = call for counter-measures | Calling delisting "black-list removal" |

#### NAP 2014: The 20-Point Backbone
Issued after the APS Peshawar attack (December 2014), NAP's 20 pillars span: (i) military operations, (ii) counter-financing of terror, (iii) madrassa registration and reform, (iv) hate-speech and anti-terror media regulation, (v) prosecution reform and speedy ATCs, (vi) Afghan-border management, (vii) foreign-fighter interdiction, and (viii) disarmament of sectarian militias. The official NAP scorecard has repeatedly shown **partial completion** — pillars on madrassa reform and hate-speech enforcement remain stalled, a frequent short-essay angle.

#### FATF Trajectory
Pakistan entered FATF's grey list in **June 2018**, was upgraded to "Jurisdiction under Increased Monitoring" in **June 2021**, and was **fully delisted on 21 October 2022** at the Paris plenary — only the second jurisdiction in FATF history to be removed after Sri Lanka. Any PMS answer citing "Pakistan is still grey-listed" loses marks.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem
**Question:** *"Distinguish between NACTA and the Counter-Terrorism Department (CTD). Why is it incorrect to describe NACTA as the lead operational counter-terrorism agency in Pakistan?"* (KPK PMS 2022, GK Paper II)

#### Solution:
- **NACTA** was set up under the **National Counter-Terrorism Authority Act, 2013**. Its statutory role is to (a) coordinate counter-terrorism policy across federal and provincial stakeholders, (b) prepare the National Counter-Terrorism Strategy, and (c) act as the central hub for information-sharing.
- **CTDs** are **provincial** police units (Punjab CTD, Sindh CTD, Balochistan CTD, KPK CTD) that conduct **arrests, raids, forensic collection, and prosecution support** through Anti-Terrorism Courts (ATCs).
- Operational lead therefore rests with the **provincial CTDs and the armed forces**, while NACTA's role is **policy and coordination**, not field operations.
- Concluding sentence: "NACTA is the coordinator; CTDs are the operational hands."

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Candidates who write *"NACTA carries out raids and arrests"* or *"NACTA is the FBI of Pakistan"* lose marks. NACTA has no arrest power; CTDs do. Also, do not confuse NACTA (2013) with NACTA's parent — the Ministry of Interior.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Doctrinal Shift: NISP 2014 → NSP 2022
The **National Internal Security Policy (2014–18)** was a security-first document focused on counter-terrorism and kinetic operations. The **National Security Policy 2022**, Pakistan's first-ever public NSP, reframes security through **"comprehensive security"** — covering economy, climate, food, energy, and human security alongside defence. Exam answers should note that NSP 2022 is the **current doctrine** and that NISP is now historical context.

#### Advanced Traps and Edge Cases

| Trap | Why It Fools Students | Correct Position |
|---|---|---|
| Quoting GDP cost as "current" | The 6–7% figure is from early-2010s estimates | Frame it as "estimated in early-2010s"; current cost is lower |
| Calling Karachi violence "sectarian only" | Karachi 2008–14 had ethnic, sectarian, criminal, and political-party mafias | Use "multi-vector urban violence" |
| Treating FATA as still separate post-2018 | FATA was merged into KPK via the 25th Amendment in May 2018 | Reference "former FATA / merged tribal districts" |
| Saying NAP "has succeeded" | Scorecards show ~12 of 20 pillars materially progressed | Use "partial implementation" |
| Ignoring South Punjab | Southern Punjab (Jhang, Bhakkar, D.G. Khan) is the SC-LeJ heartland | Always pair Balochistan + South Punjab |
| Calling BLA a "terrorist" outfit only | BLA is an ethno-nationalist insurgency; LeJ added sectarian colour later | Distinguish motive |
