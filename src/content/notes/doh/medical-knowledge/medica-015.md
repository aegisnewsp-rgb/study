# UAE Health System and Healthcare Regulations

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your DOH Medical Knowledge exam.

The UAE runs a **federalised health system**: the **Department of Health (DOH)** regulates Abu Dhabi, the **Dubai Health Authority (DHA)** regulates Dubai, and the **Ministry of Health and Prevention (MOHAP)** regulates the five northern emirates (Sharjah, Ajman, Umm Al Quwain, Ras Al Khaimah, Fujairah). Healthcare regulations are issued as a three-tier hierarchy of **Policies** (mandatory), **Standards** (measurable specifications) and **Circulars** (time-bound updates).

Clinicians are licensed through **Dataflow** primary-source verification, followed by an authority-specific assessment, then issuance of a professional licence and facility privilege to practise. Hospitals require **JCI / JCIA accreditation** as a condition of their operating licence.

- **Federal Law No. 11 of 2013** governs medical liability and malpractice.
- **Federal Law No. 14 of 2014** mandates notification of communicable diseases.
- **Malaffi** (Abu Dhabi) and **NABIDH** (Dubai) are the emirate-level Health Information Exchanges.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Regulatory authorities and jurisdiction

Each emirate has its own licensing authority. The **Emirates Health Authority (EHA)** was created to coordinate national policy, but day-to-day regulation remains emirate-specific. A clinician cannot practise on a DHA licence in Abu Dhabi, nor on a MOHAP licence in Dubai, without the relevant authority's separate licence.

| Authority | Jurisdiction | Key portal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DOH | Abu Dhabi (public + private) | Sheryan |
| DHA | Dubai (public + private) | Sheryan / DHA Portal |
| MOHAP | Sharjah, Ajman, UAQ, RAK, Fujairah | MOHAP e-licensing |
| DHCR | Dubai Healthcare City free zone | DHCR portal |

#### Hierarchy of regulatory instruments

Policies, Standards and Circulars are not interchangeable. **Policies** are permanently binding rule statements. **Standards** specify measurable technical specifications (e.g. staffing ratios, equipment). **Circulars** communicate time-bound changes, fee revisions or outbreak responses.

#### Licensing pathway for expatriate clinicians

1. **Dataflow** primary-source verification of degree, internship, license and good standing.
2. Authority-specific **assessment / Prometric exam** (DOH, DHA or MOHAP).
3. Issuance of **professional licence**.
4. Activation through a facility to obtain **privilege to practise**.
5. Annual **CPD hours** and licence renewal per the issuing authority's rules.

#### Federal legal framework

Three federal laws underpin clinical practice: Federal Law No. 4 of 1983 regulates pharmaceuticals, Federal Law No. 11 of 2013 codifies medical liability and consent, and Federal Law No. 14 of 2014 imposes mandatory notification and isolation powers for communicable diseases.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge cases and high-yield traps

The most common DOH MCQ traps involve confusing regulators with adjacent entities. **DHA** is the Dubai-wide regulator, while **DHCR** regulates only the Dubai Healthcare City free zone. **e-claim** is the insurance pre-authorisation and coding platform, not the same as **Malaffi** (Abu Dhabi HIE) or **NABIDH** (Dubai HIE), which store clinical records. JCI accreditation is not optional: hospital operating licences are conditional on current JCI/JCIA status, and primary healthcare centres must meet JCI PHC standards.

#### Scope of Practice under the PQR

The **Professional Qualification Requirements (PQR)** define tiered roles — **Consultant, Specialist, General Practitioner, Nurse and Allied Health** — each with required qualifications, years of experience and supervised practice hours. Practising outside the assigned PQR tier is a common source of Medical Liability Tribunal cases.

#### Common mistakes in MCQs

- Selecting DHA for a Sharjah facility (correct: MOHAP).
- Treating JCI accreditation as advisory rather than mandatory for hospital licensure.
- Assuming a single Dataflow report transfers across authorities (it does not — each authority runs its own verification cycle).
- Forgetting that **Federal Law No. 14 of 2014** makes non-notification of communicable diseases a punishable offence, separate from facility-level incident reporting.
- Confusing CPD-hour requirements, which differ between DOH, DHA and MOHAP and are not interchangeable.

#### Practice prompts

1. A patient in Sharjah experiences a wrong-site surgery. Identify the licensing authority for the surgeon, the federal law governing liability, and the platform used for credential verification.
2. A private hospital in Abu Dhabi applies for a new operating licence. List the two mandatory prerequisites (JCI accreditation status and Health Information Exchange integration via Malaffi) and the regulatory instrument hierarchy that will govern its inspections.
