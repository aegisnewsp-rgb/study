# Ray Optics

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Where to start when the chapter has only one day: lock the two formulas below and the sign rule, then practise five mirror + five lens numerics.

Light bends at a glass–air boundary, converges through a convex lens, and vanishes inside a prism — all of it is Ray Optics, the branch of physics that traces light as straight rays obeying the laws of reflection and refraction.

- **Snell's law (refraction):** n₁ sin i = n₂ sin r, where n₁ and n₂ are refractive indices (dimensionless), i is the angle of incidence with the normal (radians), r is the angle of refraction (radians).
- **Mirror formula:** 1/v + 1/u = 1/f, where u is object distance, v is image distance, f is focal length, all in metres, and the New Cartesian sign convention (pole as origin, distances measured in the direction of incident light as negative) gives f = R/2.
- **Lens formula (thin lens):** 1/v − 1/u = 1/f, where f is positive for convex and negative for concave, all in metres.
- **Power of a lens:** P = 1/f (in metres), expressed in dioptres (D = m⁻¹); a +2 D lens is convex, −2 D concave.

> 💡 **Memory Hook — "SILVER" for the lens sign rule:** **S**ame side as object ⇒ virtual, **I**nverted ⇒ real image, **L**arger ⇒ magnified, **V**irtual for object inside f of a convex lens, **E**rect for concave lens ray on object side, **R**emember convex = converging, concave = diverging.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Reflection, refraction and the two governing equations
A ray reflects off a plane mirror with i = r and the incident ray, reflected ray, and normal coplanar. For refraction across a smooth boundary, n₁ sin i = n₂ sin r, where n = c/v. Refractive index is dimensionless; its dimensional formula is M⁰ L⁰ T⁰. The denser-to-rarer direction matters for TIR: sin i_c = n₂/n₁ applies only when light moves from medium 1 (denser) to medium 2 (rarer).

#### Mirror vs lens — same algebra, opposite sign
The mirror formula 1/v + 1/u = 1/f and the thin-lens formula 1/v − 1/u = 1/f look identical, but the lens version uses the New Cartesian sign convention (light travels left to right, object distance u is negative for a real object, f positive for convex and negative for concave). The lensmaker's formula 1/f = (n − 1)[1/R₁ − 1/R₂] connects focal length to the two radii of curvature R₁ and R₂ (metres) and the refractive index of the material (dimensionless). For combinations in contact, 1/f_eq = 1/f₁ + 1/f₂ and powers in dioptres simply add: P_eq = P₁ + P₂.

#### Concept comparison matrix

| Quantity / rule | Concave mirror | Convex mirror | Convex lens | Concave lens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Focal length sign | −f | +f | +f | −f |
| Image of distant object | Real, inverted, at F | Virtual, erect, behind mirror | Real, inverted, at F (beyond 2F for object > 2F) | Virtual, erect, same side |
| Object inside f | Real, inverted, magnified | Virtual, erect, diminished | Virtual, erect, magnified | Virtual, erect, diminished |
| Power (D) | — | — | Positive | Negative |
| Used in | Shaving/makeup, dentist mirror | Rear-view mirror | Magnifier, camera objective | Spectacles for myopia |

#### Standard problem types you must drill
1. Mirror/lens formula with sign convention — find image position, magnification m = h_i/h_o = −v/u.
2. Apparent depth: apparent shift = real depth × (1 − 1/n), where n is the medium's refractive index (object viewed normally).
3. TIR check: confirm denser-to-rarer travel and i > i_c, then calculate i_c.
4. Prism at minimum deviation: n = sin[(A + δ_m)/2] / sin(A/2), where A is the prism angle and δ_m the minimum deviation (both in radians).
5. Optical instruments: simple microscope M = 1 + D/f; compound M ≈ (L/f_o)(1 + D/f_e); astronomical telescope in normal adjustment M = −f_o/f_e, where D = 25 cm is the least distance of distinct vision.

#### A worked numerical that recurs every year
**Problem.** An object is placed 30 cm in front of a concave mirror of focal length 15 cm. Find the image position, magnification, and state the image nature.
**Given.** u = −30 cm (real object), f = −15 cm (concave).
**Step 1.** Mirror formula: 1/v + 1/u = 1/f ⇒ 1/v = 1/f − 1/u = 1/(−15) − 1/(−30) = −2/30 + 1/30 = −1/30 ⇒ v = −30 cm. The image forms at 30 cm in front of the mirror.
**Step 2.** Magnification: m = −v/u = −(−30)/(−30) = −1. Height of image equals the object but inverted.
**Step 3.** Nature: real (v negative in front), inverted (m negative), same size (|m| = 1). This is the classic "object at C, image at C" outcome since C = 2f for a spherical mirror.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** students drop the sign on u for a real object and write u = +30 cm; the formula then yields v = +30 cm, "behind the mirror," which is unphysical for a concave mirror and flips the image type from real to virtual. Always fix u negative for a real object first, then solve.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

#### Where the geometric-optics approximation breaks
Ray optics assumes λ ≪ aperture size so diffraction can be ignored. A slit a few wavelengths wide will produce visible fringes; the same slit described as a "ray" will silently violate Snell's law in a CUET numerical. The wavefront model — the locus of points of equal phase — survives this limit and remains useful for Huygens' construction of refraction.

#### Advanced traps and exceptions

1. **Lensmaker's sign rule for R.** A convex lens has R₁ > 0 and R₂ < 0 in the New Cartesian convention; flipping the order of surfaces reverses the sign of (1/R₁ − 1/R₂) and changes the lens type. Treat R₁ as the surface the light hits first.
2. **Silvering a lens turns it into a mirror system.** The equivalent focal length of a silvered lens combines refraction (twice) and reflection (once): 1/f_eq = 2/f_lens + 1/f_mirror, where f_mirror = R/2 of the silvered surface. This is a classic 2-mark trap in CUET.
3. **Prism on its minimum-deviation condition.** δ is minimum only when the ray passes symmetrically; at this point i = e (angle of emergence). Using n = sin A / sin δ at non-minimum deviation is the most common numerical error.
4. **Combination not in contact.** When lenses are separated by d, use 1/f_eq = 1/f₁ + 1/f₂ − d/(f₁ f₂) only if both focal lengths are small and separation is comparable.
5. **Refraction at a single spherical surface.** n₂/v − n₁/u = (n₂ − n₁)/R, with R signed by the centre of curvature's direction. This is the building block the lensmaker's formula assumes.
6. **Optical-fibre numerics.** Numerical aperture NA = √(n₁² − n₂²); maximum acceptance angle θ₀ satisfies sin θ₀ = NA. CUET usually asks for the condition rather than a numerical value.
7. **Dispersion and the Cauchy's relation.** n decreases with wavelength for glass, so red bends least and violet most — VIBGYOR emerges from white light. Angular dispersion δ_v − δ_r ≈ (n_v − n_r) A for a thin prism at small A.

#### Connection to adjacent CUET chapters
Ray Optics links directly to Wave Optics (diffraction single-slit formula, Young's double-slit) because the boundary between them is purely λ/aperture. It also feeds Electromagnetic Waves (v = c/n, refractive index as a property of bound-electron response) and Modern Physics (photoelectric effect uses photon energy, but the wavelength that selects the metal comes from dispersion in a prism or grating).

#### Two advanced practice prompts
- An equilateral prism (A = 60°) shows minimum deviation δ_m = 60°. Compute its refractive index and the angle of refraction inside the prism at this setting.
- A thin convex lens of focal length 20 cm is silvered on one side. Treating it as an equivalent mirror, find the position and nature of the image of an object placed 30 cm in front of the unsilvered surface.
