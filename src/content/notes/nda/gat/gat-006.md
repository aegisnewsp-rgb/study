# General Science Biology

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Cell is the structural and functional unit of life. NDA GAT Biology tests qualitative recall: cell organelles, Mendel's laws, blood groups ABO, human physiology (digestive, circulatory, excretory, nervous, endocrine), plant transport, photosynthesis, and diseases. No derivation work. Conceptual statements only.

- **Cell**: prokaryote (no nucleus, e.g., bacteria) vs eukaryote (nucleus + membrane-bound organelles).
- **Genetics**: DNA double helix, A=T, G≡C; Mendel's three laws govern inheritance.
- **Human physiology**: heart has four chambers; pulmonary artery is the only artery carrying deoxygenated blood.
- **Plant physiology**: xylem transports water upward (transpiration pull); phloem transports sucrose (Münch mass flow).
- **Classification**: Whittaker's five kingdoms — Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia.

| Vitamin | Solubility | Deficiency disease |
|---|---|---|
| A | Fat | Night blindness |
| D | Fat | Rickets (children), osteomalacia (adults) |
| K | Fat | Slow blood clotting |
| B-complex | Water | Beriberi (B₁), Pellagra (B₃) |
| C | Water | Scurvy |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Working understanding of mechanisms, derivations, and standard problem types.

#### Cell and Genetic Material

A prokaryotic cell lacks a true nucleus (nucleoid region only) and membrane-bound organelles; eukaryotic cells contain both. Plant cells differ from animal cells by having a cell wall, chloroplasts, and a large central vacuole. Mature mammalian RBCs are **anucleate** and biconcave — this maximises surface area for oxygen binding.

DNA follows Watson–Crick base pairing: adenine pairs with thymine via two hydrogen bonds (A=T), guanine pairs with cytosine via three (G≡C). RNA replaces thymine with uracil. Chromosomes carry genes; humans have 46 chromosomes (44 autosomes + 2 sex chromosomes: XX in females, XY in males).

#### Cell Division and Mendelian Inheritance

Mitosis produces two genetically identical diploid daughter cells and serves growth/repair. Meiosis produces four genetically varied haploid gametes through two successive divisions and crossing-over during prophase I.

Mendel's three laws:
1. **Dominance** — one allele masks the other in a heterozygote.
2. **Segregation** — allele pairs separate during gamete formation.
3. **Independent assortment** — genes on different chromosomes assort independently.

Blood group ABO is a multiple-allele case: I^A and I^B are codominant, i is recessive. The Rh factor (+/−) is inherited independently. X-linked recessive disorders (colour blindness, haemophilia) appear more often in males because they have only one X chromosome.

#### Human Physiology at a Glance

| System | Functional unit | Key mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Digestive | Enzymes (ptyalin, pepsin, trypsin, lipase) | Pepsin works in acidic stomach; trypsin in alkaline duodenum with bile |
| Circulatory | Heart (4 chambers) | Double circulation — pulmonary and systemic; cardiac output = SV × HR |
| Excretory | Nephron | Filtration → reabsorption → secretion → excretion |
| Nervous | Neuron / reflex arc | Synapse transmits via neurotransmitters (acetylcholine, dopamine) |
| Endocrine | Ductless glands | Insulin lowers glucose; thyroxine regulates BMR; adrenaline triggers fight-or-flight |

The nephron filters blood under hydrostatic pressure in the glomerulus, reabsorbs useful glucose and ions in the proximal tubule, secretes wastes in the distal tubule, and concentrates urine via the loop of Henle.

#### Plant Physiology and Photosynthesis

Xylem vessels conduct water and minerals upward driven by transpiration pull (cohesion–tension theory). Phloem translocates sucrose from source (leaf) to sink (root/fruit) by the Münch mass-flow hypothesis.

Photosynthesis summary equation:
$$6\text{CO}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{light}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\text{O}_2$$
Units: moles; light reaction occurs in thylakoid membranes producing ATP and NADPH; the Calvin cycle (dark reaction) fixes CO₂ in the stroma using the enzyme RuBisCO.

#### Disease and Nutrition

Vitamins A, D, E, K are fat-soluble; B-complex and C are water-soluble. Communicable diseases spread via pathogens (TB, malaria); non-communicable diseases arise from lifestyle or genetic factors (diabetes, hypertension). Vaccination stimulates active immunity by exposing the body to weakened/inactivated antigens.

#### Worked Resolution: Blood Group Inheritance

A father has blood group A (genotype I^A i) and the mother has group B (genotype I^B i). Predict the possible blood groups of their children.

The cross I^A i × I^B i yields four equally likely combinations: I^A I^B (group AB), I^A i (group A), I^B i (group B), ii (group O). Each child has a 25% probability of being A, B, AB, or O. Parents homozygous I^A I^A or I^B I^B cannot produce an O child, but heterozygous parents can.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Candidates often wrongly assume blood group inheritance follows a simple dominant–recessive pattern. ABO actually shows **codominance** between I^A and I^B — both alleles express simultaneously, producing the AB phenotype. Forgetting this leads to missing one-quarter of the offspring possibilities.

#### Classification and Ecology

Whittaker's five-kingdom system: Monera (bacteria, blue-green algae), Protista (amoeba, paramecium), Fungi (yeast, moulds), Plantae, Animalia. A food chain shows linear energy flow (producer → primary consumer → secondary consumer → tertiary consumer); a food web is the interconnected network of multiple food chains in an ecosystem. Energy transfer follows the 10% rule — only about 10% of energy moves up each trophic level.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Edge cases, adjacent links, and exam-specific traps for the high-scorer.

#### Hardy–Weinberg and Population Genetics

Although NDA rarely tests calculations, the Hardy–Weinberg principle underlies conceptual questions on allele frequency. The relation
$$p^2 + 2pq + q^2 = 1$$
where p = frequency of the dominant allele, q = frequency of the recessive allele (dimensionless, since both p and q are pure ratios), gives expected genotype frequencies under random mating, no mutation, no selection, no migration, and infinite population size. The common trap is forgetting that p² and q² are **homozygous** frequencies while 2pq is the **heterozygous** (carrier) frequency — a heterozygote is phenotypically normal but carries one recessive allele.

#### Reflex Arc and Nervous Coordination

A reflex arc has five components: receptor → sensory (afferent) neuron → interneuron (in spinal cord) → motor (efferent) neuron → effector (muscle/gland). The knee-jerk (patellar) reflex is **monosynaptic** — only one synapse between sensory and motor neuron. Most other reflexes (withdrawal from heat) are polysynaptic with at least one interneuron. Synaptic transmission is unidirectional because neurotransmitters are released only from the axon terminal of the presynaptic neuron.

#### Endocrine vs Nervous Coordination

Nervous coordination is fast (milliseconds), short-lived, and electrical. Endocrine coordination is slower (seconds to hours), prolonged, and chemical via hormones in blood. Both maintain homeostasis: the hypothalamus–pituitary axis links them — the hypothalamus produces releasing hormones that regulate the anterior pituitary, which in turn controls the adrenal cortex, thyroid, and gonads.

#### Common High-Scorer Traps

1. Confusing mitosis (somatic, 2 diploid cells) with meiosis (germ cells, 4 haploid gametes).
2. Saying all arteries carry oxygenated blood — pulmonary artery is deoxygenated; pulmonary vein is oxygenated.
3. Mixing up pepsin (stomach, acidic, protein) with trypsin (duodenum, alkaline, protein).
4. Marking all bacteria as harmful — Rhizobium fixes nitrogen, Lactobacillus makes curd.
5. Calling transpiration "transport of food" — that is translocation; transpiration is water loss driving xylem ascent.
6. Treating vitamins C, B-complex as fat-soluble — they are water-soluble; A, D, E, K are fat-soluble.
7. Believing mature RBCs have a nucleus — they are anucleate to maximise oxygen loading.

#### Adjacent Topics and Cross-Links

- **Cell biology ↔ Genetics**: chromosome behaviour in meiosis explains Mendel's laws.
- **Digestive enzymes ↔ Chemistry**: optimal pH maps to acid–base equilibria.
- **Photosynthesis ↔ Ecology**: producers anchor every food chain and drive oxygen cycling.
- **Endocrine ↔ Nervous system**: homeostasis requires both; diabetes (insulin deficiency) links endocrine failure to circulatory glucose imbalance.
- **Five-Kingdom classification ↔ Disease**: knowing that bacteria (Monera) are prokaryotes explains why antibiotics target them but not human (eukaryotic) cells.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. A colour-blind man marries a carrier woman (X^C X^c). Using a Punnett square, determine the probability that their son is colour-blind. *(Answer: 50%, since the son inherits Y from father and X from mother — half the X alleles are X^c.)*
2. Why does a person at high altitude show increased RBC count and breathing rate? *(Answer: low atmospheric pO₂ reduces O₂ diffusion gradient; kidneys release erythropoietin stimulating RBC production; chemoreceptors trigger faster breathing.)*

> 📌 **Exam Strategy:** In NDA GAT Paper-II, expect roughly 8–12 biology questions per attempt. Target the high-recall clusters first — blood groups, vitamin deficiency, hormone–gland pairing, Mendelian crosses — and finish them in under 5 minutes. Save physiology reasoning questions for the second pass.
