# Urinary System

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The urinary system (systema urinarium) eliminates nitrogenous waste, regulates fluid-electrolyte balance, and maintains acid-base equilibrium. It comprises two kidneys, two ureters, one bladder, and one urethra, supported by accessory structures such as the juxtaglomerular apparatus, renal corpuscle, and bladder trigone. The **nephron** (~1 million per kidney) is the structural and functional unit, executing urine formation through three sequential processes.

| Term | Definition | Anatomical Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Renal corpuscle** | Glomerulus + Bowman's capsule | Located in cortex |
| **Loop of Henle** | Generates medullary osmotic gradient | Up to 1200 mOsm/L |
| **Trigone** | Smooth triangular area of bladder floor | Bounded by two ureteric + one urethral orifice |
| **Detrusor muscle** | Smooth muscle of bladder wall | Contracts via parasympathetic S2–S4 |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"A**rtery **P**elvis **V**ein" — At the renal hilum, the **Renal Vein is Anterior**, the **Renal Artery is middle**, and the **Renal Pelvis is Posterior**. Memorise as **V-A-P** (Van-A-P).

Urine production involves: **GFR** = (U_inulin × V) / P_inulin (mL/min), **FF** = GFR/RPF (~0.16–0.20), and **NFP** = (P_GC − P_BS) − (π_GC − π_BS) ≈ 10 mmHg. The right kidney lies ~1.5 cm lower than the left due to hepatic displacement.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Renal Anatomy and the Nephron

Each kidney is retroperitoneal from the outset of development and lies against the posterior abdominal wall at the T12–L3 vertebral level. The kidney has an outer **cortex** (containing renal corpuscles and convoluted tubules) and an inner **medulla** (containing pyramids with loops of Henle and collecting ducts). A nephron consists of the **renal corpuscle**, **proximal convoluted tubule (PCT)**, **loop of Henle** (descending thin, ascending thick), **distal convoluted tubule (DCT)**, and **collecting duct**.

The **juxtaglomerular apparatus (JGA)** comprises macula densa (chemoreceptor cells of the DCT), juxtaglomerular (JG) cells — modified smooth muscle of the afferent arteriole that secrete **renin**, and extraglomerular mesangium. The countercurrent multiplier mechanism in the loop of Henle establishes the corticomedullary osmotic gradient essential for urine concentration.

#### Ureter and Bladder

The **ureter** is a 25 cm muscular tube with three anatomical constrictions: the **pelviureteric junction**, the **pelvic brim** (crossing common iliac vessels), and the **ureterovesical junction**. These are the classic sites of renal calculus impaction.

The **urinary bladder trigone** lies in the bladder floor (fundus), bounded by the two ureteric orifices superiorly and the internal urethral orifice inferiorly. Its mucosa is smooth even when the bladder is distended, contrasting with the rugose mucosa of the rest of the bladder wall.

#### Comparison Matrix: Urethral Anatomy by Sex

| Feature | Male Urethra | Female Urethra |
|---|---|---|
| Length | ~18–20 cm | ~4 cm |
| Parts | Prostatic, membranous, spongy | Single short tube |
| Sphincters | Internal (sympathetic T11–L2) + external (pudendal) | Internal only |
| Clinical correlate | Catheter injury at membranous part | Ascending UTI risk |
| Opens into | External urethral meatus at glans | Vestibule anterior to vagina |

#### Urine formation involves three sequential processes

1. **Glomerular filtration** — bulk, non-selective ultrafiltration at the renal corpuscle driven by NFP ≈ 10 mmHg.
2. **Tubular reabsorption** — selective retrieval of water, glucose, amino acids, Na⁺ in PCT (~65% of filtrate).
3. **Tubular secretion** — active transport of H⁺, K⁺, drugs from peritubular capillaries into DCT and collecting duct.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** A 45-year-old man presents with colicky flank pain radiating to the groin. Non-contrast CT shows a 6 mm calculus in the proximal ureter. At which anatomical site is the stone most likely impacted, and which nerve root provides parasympathetic innervation to the detrusor muscle that produces ureteric peristalsis?

#### Solution:

- **Site of impaction:** The proximal ureter is at the level of the **pelviureteric junction (PUJ)** — the first of three anatomical constrictions. Other classic sites are the pelvic brim (crossing common iliac vessels) and the ureterovesical junction.
- **Parasympathetic supply to detrusor:** **S2–S4** spinal segments via the **pelvic splanchnic nerves** (nervi erigentes). These cause detrusor contraction for micturition. Sympathetic supply from T11–L2 (hypogastric plexus) relaxes the detrusor and contracts the internal urethral sphincter during bladder filling.
- **Clinical reasoning:** Flank pain radiating to the groin follows the pattern of referred pain via the genitofemoral nerve (L1–L2) sharing spinal segments with the ureteric sympathetic supply.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students often answer that the *external* urethral sphincter is relaxed by parasympathetics. In reality, the external sphincter is **somatic** and supplied by the **pudendal nerve (S2–S4)**, voluntarily relaxed during micturition. The parasympathetic system only governs the **detrusor**.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Advanced Edge Cases and Embryological Cues

The metanephric blastema gives rise to the functional nephron from the 5th week, while the ureteric bud (from the mesonephric duct) forms the ureter, renal pelvis, calyces, and collecting ducts. Failure of ascent from the pelvis produces a **pelvic kidney**; failure of caudal migration of the urachus leaves a **urachal fistula** at the bladder dome.

#### Neurovascular Architecture in Detail

| Structure | Arterial supply | Venous drainage | Lymphatic drainage |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kidney** | Renal artery (L2, branch of aorta) | Renal vein → IVC (left longer, crosses aorta) | Para-aortic nodes |
| **Upper ureter** | Renal artery branches | Renal vein | Para-aortic nodes |
| **Lower ureter** | Superior vesical, uterine, gonadal | Internal iliac tributaries | Internal iliac + para-aortic |
| **Bladder (superior)** | Superior vesical arteries | Vesical venous plexus | External iliac nodes |
| **Bladder (inferior/lateral)** | Inferior vesical arteries | Vesical plexus → internal iliac | Internal iliac nodes |

#### Common Pitfalls — High-Yield Traps

1. The **efferent arteriole has a smaller diameter** than the afferent arteriole, generating the high glomerular hydrostatic pressure (~55 mmHg) essential for filtration. Many candidates invert this.
2. **JG cells** are modified smooth muscle cells of the **afferent arteriole**, not part of the DCT — the macula densa belongs to the DCT.
3. The **right kidney is lower** (~1.5 cm) because of liver displacement, not spleen displacement.
4. The **membranous urethra is the shortest and least dilatable** part of the male urethra — a common site of catheter perforation.
5. **Filtration Fraction (FF)** is GFR/RPF (~0.16–0.20), not RPF/GFR; rising FF indicates efferent constriction (e.g., angiotensin II).
6. The **internal urethral sphincter is sympathetic** (T11–L2 via hypogastric plexus); the external is somatic (pudendal nerve, S2–S4).

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. A neonate presents with an abdominal mass and hematuria. Imaging reveals a solid renal mass confined to the kidney. Identify the most likely tumour and its embryological cell of origin.
2. Calculate **NFP** given: P_GC = 56 mmHg, P_BS = 15 mmHg, π_GC = 32 mmHg, π_BS = 0 mmHg. Predict the change if efferent arteriolar resistance increases by 20%.

> 📌 **Formula Check:** Clearance Cₓ = (Uₓ × V) / Pₓ, in mL/min. For inulin (filtered only, neither reabsorbed nor secreted), C_inulin = **GFR**. For PAH (cleared fully by one pass), C_PAH ≈ **RPF**. The **filtration fraction FF = GFR/RPF**, dimensionless.
