# Upper Limb

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The **upper limb** extends from the shoulder girdle (clavicle + scapula) to the fingertips and is divided into the **brachium**, **antebrachium**, and **manus**. Innervation flows from the **brachial plexus (C5–T1)**, and arterial supply travels the subclavian → axillary → brachial → radial/ulnar pathway.

- **Rotator cuff** = **SITS**: Supraspinatus, Infraspinatus, Teres minor, Subscapularis.
- **Erb's palsy** (C5–C6): "waiter's tip" — abducted, laterally rotated arm with extended elbow.
- **Klumpke's palsy** (C8–T1): claw hand from intrinsic muscle paralysis.
- **Anatomical snuffbox** tenderness = scaphoid fracture (risk of AVN of proximal fragment).
- **Median cubital vein** is the preferred venepuncture site.
- **Carpal tunnel**: roof = flexor retinaculum; contents median nerve + 9 tendons.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Osteology and Joints
The appendicular skeleton of the upper limb begins with the **clavicle** (first bone to ossify, last to fuse) articulating at the **sternoclavicular joint** — the only synovial joint connecting the limb to the axial skeleton. The **scapula** sits at T2–T7 posteriorly and bears the glenoid, acromion, and coracoid. The **humerus** carries the surgical neck (axillary nerve), radial groove (radial nerve), and medial epicondyle (ulnar nerve).

#### Axilla and Brachial Plexus
The **axilla** is a pyramidal space whose apex is the cervicoaxillary canal (bounded by clavicle, scapula, first rib). Its contents include the **axillary artery** (three parts separated by pectoralis minor), **axillary vein** (medial), cords of the brachial plexus, and **axillary lymph nodes** in five groups: pectoral, lateral, posterior, central, apical.

The **brachial plexus** has five roots (C5–T1), three trunks (upper, middle, lower), six divisions, three cords (lateral, posterior, medial — named by position relative to axillary artery), and five terminal branches: **musculocutaneous, axillary, radial, median, ulnar**.

#### Nerves and Muscles at a Glance

| Nerve | Motor | Key sensory | Common lesion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Musculocutaneous | Anterior arm (biceps, brachialis, coracobrachialis) | Lateral forearm skin | Rare |
| Axillary | Deltoid, teres minor | Regimental badge area | Surgical neck fracture |
| Radial | Posterior arm + forearm extensors | Dorsum of hand (1st web) | Saturday night palsy |
| Median | Forearm flexors (except FCU), lateral 2 lumbricals | Lateral 3½ digits palm | Carpal tunnel |
| Ulnar | Intrinsic hand (most), FCU, FDP medial half | Medial 1½ digits | Cubital tunnel, claw hand |

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Clinical Correlates and Surface Anatomy
The **cubital fossa** has the mnemonic for its contents (lateral to medial): **T**endon of biceps, **B**rachial artery, **M**edian nerve — "TAN" laterally; the **R**adial nerve lies deep between brachioradialis and brachialis. Floor = brachialis + supinator; roof = bicipital aponeurosis. Its medial border is **pronator teres**, not flexor carpi radialis.

The **anatomical snuffbox** is bounded by abductor pollicis longus + extensor pollicis brevis (radial) and extensor pollicis longus (ulnar); the floor is the scaphoid and trapezium. Tenderness here demands scaphoid X-ray because its **retrograde blood supply** enters distally, making the proximal fragment vulnerable to **avascular necrosis**.

#### Hand Spaces and Intrinsic Muscles
**Thenar muscles** (opponens pollicis, abductor pollicis brevis, flexor pollicis brevis — superficial head) are median (recurrent branch); the deep head of FPB and **adductor pollicis** are ulnar. **Lumbricals**: lateral two by median, medial two by ulnar; they flex MCP and extend IP joints. **Interossei** (3 palmar, 4 dorsal) — "PAD" (palmar = ADduct), "DAB" (dorsal = ABduct).

#### Practice Prompts
1. A patient cannot initiate abduction up to 15° but can continue beyond. Which muscle/tendon is torn and which nerve is involved?
2. After a fracture of the medial epicondyle, a patient cannot adduct the little finger. Trace the nerve injured, the deformity produced, and the muscles paralysed.

#### Common Mistakes
- Mixing up **Erb's point (C5–C6)** roots with Klumpke's (C8–T1) — Erb's gives "waiter's tip," Klumpke's gives claw hand.
- Attributing all lumbricals to ulnar — only the medial two.
- Calling the cord of the brachial plexus by its root origin — cords are named by relation to the axillary artery.
- Forgetting that **profunda brachii** accompanies the **radial nerve** in the radial (spiral) groove.
