# Coordinate Geometry and Graphs

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Coordinate geometry links algebra and geometry using the **Cartesian plane**, where every point is written as **(x, y)** with x = **abscissa** and y = **ordinate**. Two points P(x₁, y₁) and Q(x₂, y₂) give **distance** d = √((x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)²) and **midpoint** M = ((x₁+x₂)/2, (y₁+y₂)/2). The **gradient** between them is m = (y₂−y₁)/(x₂−x₁), and a straight line obeys y = mx + c, where c is the **y-intercept**. **Parallel** lines share equal gradients; **perpendicular** lines satisfy m₁ × m₂ = −1. For a quadratic y = ax² + bx + c, the **axis of symmetry** is x = −b/(2a). In NECO SSCE, expect 2–3 objective questions and one 5-mark structured question on graphs and line equations.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### The Cartesian Plane and Key Points
The plane is divided by a horizontal **x-axis** and vertical **y-axis** meeting at the **origin** (0, 0). Each point has an ordered pair (x, y); x measures horizontal distance from the origin, y measures vertical distance. Quadrants are labelled I (x>0, y>0), II (x<0, y>0), III (x<0, y<0), IV (x>0, y<0).

#### Distance and Midpoint
Given P(x₁, y₁) and Q(x₂, y₂):
- **Distance** d = √((x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)²)
- **Midpoint** M = ((x₁+x₂)/2, (y₁+y₂)/2)

Example: P(2, 3) and Q(6, 11) → M = (4, 7) and d = √(16 + 64) = √80 ≈ 8.94 units.

#### Gradient and Line Equations
The **gradient** (slope) measures steepness: m = (y₂−y₁)/(x₂−x₁). Three forms of a line's equation:
- **Slope-intercept:** y = mx + c, where c = y-intercept
- **Point-slope:** y − y₁ = m(x − x₁)
- **Two-point form:** (y−y₁)/(y₂−y₁) = (x−x₁)/(x₂−x₁)

#### Parallel and Perpendicular Lines
Lines L₁ and L₂ with gradients m₁ and m₂ satisfy:
- **Parallel:** m₁ = m₂ (and different y-intercepts)
- **Perpendicular:** m₁ × m₂ = −1

To find a line through (3, 5) perpendicular to y = 2x + 1: gradient = −1/2, so y − 5 = −½(x − 3) → y = −½x + 6.5.

#### Quadratic Graphs
A quadratic y = ax² + bx + c produces a **parabola**. Key features:
- **Axis of symmetry:** x = −b/(2a)
- **Vertex (turning point):** ((−b/2a), substitute back)
- **y-intercept:** c (set x = 0)
- **Roots (x-intercepts):** solutions to ax² + bx + c = 0

If a > 0, the parabola opens upward (minimum at vertex); if a < 0, it opens downward (maximum).

#### Exam Patterns in NECO SSCE
Typically appears in **Paper II** as: (i) MCQs testing gradient or distance calculations, (ii) a structured question deriving the equation of a line through two given points, (iii) sketching y = ax² + bx + c and labelling the axis of symmetry and turning point, and (iv) solving **simultaneous equations** both graphically (reading intersection) and algebraically.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Connection to Other Topics
Coordinate geometry bridges algebra (equations, simultaneous solutions) with geometry (shapes, loci). The distance formula is essentially the **Pythagorean theorem** applied to horizontal and vertical differences. Gradient is closely tied to **trigonometry** because m = tan θ, where θ is the angle a line makes with the positive x-axis. This link appears in NECO SSCE when a question asks for the angle of inclination of a line.

#### Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them
1. **Sign errors in the distance formula:** squared terms make signs irrelevant for distance, but mixing up (y₂−y₁) with (y₁−y₂) when transferring into the midpoint formula changes the answer. Always subtract consistently in one direction.
2. **Gradient direction confusion:** m = (y₂−y₁)/(x₂−x₁) requires the same point's coordinates to be used as the subtrahend throughout. If you swap once, you flip the sign.
3. **Perpendicular gradient ≠ 1/m:** the correct form is m₁ × m₂ = −1, so the perpendicular to gradient 3 is −1/3, not 1/3.
4. **Vertex vs. roots:** roots are x-intercepts (solve ax² + bx + c = 0); the vertex is the turning point at x = −b/(2a). Many students substitute b/(2a) instead of −b/(2a) and miss the maximum/minimum.
5. **Scale errors on graphs:** NECO often uses non-uniform axes (e.g. 1 cm = 2 units on x but 1 cm = 5 units on y). Re-read axis labels before reading coordinates.

#### Worked Example
Find the equation of the perpendicular bisector of the segment joining A(1, 2) and B(7, 10).
- Gradient of AB = (10−2)/(7−1) = 8/6 = 4/3
- Perpendicular gradient = −3/4
- Midpoint of AB = ((1+7)/2, (2+10)/2) = (4, 6)
- Equation: y − 6 = −¾(x − 4) → y = −¾x + 9

#### Practice Prompts
1. A line passes through (2, −1) and is parallel to y = 3x − 5. Find its equation and the point where it crosses the y-axis.
2. Sketch y = x² − 4x + 3, label the vertex, axis of symmetry, y-intercept, and roots. State whether the parabola opens upward or downward and justify using the value of *a*.
