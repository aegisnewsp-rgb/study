# Fill in the Blanks

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

Fill-in-the-blank questions in CUET UG English Language evaluate precise syntactic competence, idiomatic prepositional collocations, and contextual vocabulary selection.

Core Grammatical Invariants:
- Subject-Verb Concord: The finite verb must agree with its grammatical subject in number and person, ignoring intervening prepositional modifiers:
  - *"The bouquet of yellow roses (was / were) presented."* $\implies$ Subject is singular *bouquet*, requiring singular *was*.
  - Indefinite pronouns (*each, neither, either, everyone, somebody, nobody*) take singular verbs: *"Each of the candidates has submitted the credentials."*
- Proximity Rule with Correlative Conjunctions: With *either...or*, *neither...nor*, and *not only...but also*, the verb agrees with the closest subject:
  - *"Neither the director nor the actors were present on set."*
- Prepositional Collocations: Verbs and adjectives govern fixed prepositions that cannot be altered:
  - *adhere to*, *abstain from*, *comply with*, *deprive of*, *proficient in*, *eligible for*, *allergic to*, *congruent with*.
- Distinctions in Quantifiers:
  - *Fewer* modifies countable plural nouns (*fewer applicants*).
  - *Less* modifies uncountable singular quantities (*less time, less water*).

⚡ **CUET UG Exam Strategy:** Read the complete sentence before inspecting options. Identify the part of speech required by the blank (noun, verb, adjective, adverb) through syntactic positioning, then eliminate grammatically incompatible options before analyzing semantic nuance.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

Standard preparation for CUET English focuses on dependent clauses, prepositional government, discourse markers, and pronoun case selection.

#### 1. High-Frequency Fixed Prepositional Collocations

| Verb / Adjective | Governed Preposition | Contextual Meaning | Example Sentence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Abstain** | *from* | Restrain oneself from doing | Citizens must abstain from violating traffic laws. |
| **Comply** | *with* | Act in accordance with rules | The manufacturer must comply with safety standards. |
| **Adhere** | *to* | Stick firmly to a belief or protocol | Scholars must adhere to academic integrity. |
| **Deprive** | *of* | Prevent from possessing | Poverty should not deprive children of schooling. |
| **Endowed** | *with* | Blessed with a quality or talent | The pianist is endowed with remarkable pitch. |
| **Congruent** | *with* | In agreement or harmony | The verdict is congruent with statutory precedent. |
| **Detrimental** | *to* | Tending to cause harm | Excess sugar is detrimental to metabolic health. |
| **Refrain** | *from* | Stop oneself from an action | Attendees must refrain from mobile phone usage. |

#### 2. Pronoun Case and Relative Clause Selection

| Relative Pronoun | Grammatical Role | Permissible Antecedent | Example Sentence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Who** | Subjective (performs action) | Persons | The researcher who designed the protocol received the medal. |
| **Whom** | Objective (receives action) | Persons | The delegate whom the president appointed delivered the address. |
| **Whose** | Possessive | Persons, Animals, Inanimate objects | The author whose novel won the accolade spoke yesterday. |
| **Which** | Subjective / Objective in non-restrictive clauses | Animals, Inanimate objects | The treaty, which took years to negotiate, was ratified. |
| **That** | Restrictive clauses (essential definition) | Persons, Animals, Things | The laboratory that discovered the compound published the data. |

#### 3. Step-by-Step Worked Example: Correlative Structure

**Problem:** Complete the sentence by choosing the most appropriate option:
*"Not only the principal but also the senior faculty members _____ in favor of revising the academic curriculum."*
(a) was  
(b) is  
(c) were  
(d) has been  

**Solution Steps:**
1. Identify the coordinating conjunction: The correlative conjunction pair is *not only ... but also*.
2. Recall the governing concord rule: When two subjects are connected by *not only ... but also*, the verb agrees in number and person with the second (closer) subject.
3. Identify the subjects:
   - First subject: *the principal* (singular)
   - Second subject: *the senior faculty members* (plural)
4. The second subject is plural (*members*), requiring a plural verb.
5. Eliminate singular verb options: (a) *was*, (b) *is*, and (d) *has been* are all singular.
6. The correct plural form is (c) *were*.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

Extended study targets subjunctive mood inversions, conditional clauses, and formal rhetorical connectors.

#### 1. Comparison of Conditional Sentence Types

| Conditional Category | If-Clause Structure | Main Clause Structure | Intended Temporal Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **First Conditional** | If + Simple Present | Will + Base Verb | Realistic future possibility |
| **Second Conditional** | If + Simple Past (*were*) | Would + Base Verb | Hypothetical present or improbable future |
| **Third Conditional** | If + Past Perfect (*had + V3*) | Would have + Past Participle (*V3*) | Unfulfilled past counterfactual |
| **Inverted Past Counterfactual** | Had + Subject + Past Participle | Would have + Past Participle | Formal rhetorical conditional without *if* |
| **Inverted Present Hypothetical** | Were + Subject + to-infinitive | Would + Base Verb | Elevated hypothetical formal statement |

#### 2. The Mandative Subjunctive Construction

Verbs of recommendation, demand, and urgency (*insist, recommend, demand, suggest, stipulate, mandate*) governing a *that*-clause require the base subjunctive form of the verb (infinitive without *to*), regardless of subject number or tense:
- Correct: *"The committee recommended that the candidate **be** interviewed immediately."* (Not *is* or *was*).
- Correct: *"The judge insisted that the witness **answer** the inquiry."* (Not *answers* or *answered*).

#### 3. Step-by-Step Worked Example: Inverted Conditional Blank

**Problem:** Complete the sentence with the grammatically correct verbal sequence:
*"_____ the weather forecast warned of impending storms, the mountaineering expedition would have reached the summit safely."*
(a) Unless  
(b) Had not  
(c) If not  
(d) Because  

**Solution Steps:**
1. Analyze the main clause: *"the mountaineering expedition would have reached the summit safely"*.
2. The main clause structure (*would have + past participle*) signals an unfulfilled third conditional counterfactual.
3. The subordinate clause contains subject (*the weather forecast*) followed by past participle (*warned*).
4. In formal English, dropping the conjunction *if* requires subject-auxiliary inversion: *Had the weather forecast not warned...* or *Had not the weather forecast warned...*
5. Option (b) *Had not* provides the inverted past perfect auxiliary required to pair with *would have reached*.
6. Correct complete sentence: *"Had not the weather forecast warned of impending storms, the mountaineering expedition would have reached the summit safely."*
