#### Core Economic Indicators

Pakistan's macroeconomic stabilisation programme shapes roughly 30% of current-affairs questions. The IMF Extended Fund Facility (EFF) of $3 billion over 37 months was approved in September 2023, with subsequent tranches tied to structural benchmarks including tax-base expansion (FBR reforms), energy-sector circular-debt containment, and State Bank of Pakistan (SBP) autonomy.

GDP growth is reported on the fiscal year basis (July-June), not the calendar year — a frequent source of mis-attribution. FY2024 growth came in at roughly 0.6% on the PBS base year of 2015-16, while FY2025 projections from the IMF and World Bank cluster between 1.6% and 2.4%, contingent on IMF programme continuity and flood-recovery spending.

> 📌 **Formula Check:** **Inflation Rate (CPI)** = [(CPIₜ − CPIₜ₋₁) / CPIₜ₋₁] × 100, where CPI is the Consumer Price Index published monthly by PBS (base 2015-16 = 100). Units: dimensionless percentage. The SBP Policy Rate is set in % per annum and serves as the policy floor; KIBOR (Karachi Interbank Offered Rate) is the actual interbank lending rate that tracks above the Policy Rate.

#### Political Developments & Judicial Milestones

The federal coalition government (PML-N-led, with PPP and coalition partners) took office in March 2024 after the 8 February 2024 general elections. Major Supreme Court of Pakistan rulings during 2024-2025 included the reserved-seats judgment (PTI eligibility) and Article 63-A disqualification interpretations — both heavily tested in MCQs.

The National Assembly composition shifted following PTI independent candidates' allocation, affecting committee chairmanships and opposition status. Cabinet reshuffles occurred multiple times; track the Finance Minister's exact designation (currently "Minister for Finance and Revenue"), the Foreign Minister (Islah Dar / later portfolios), and the Minister for Petroleum portfolios.

#### Bilateral Relations & Multilateral Forums

CPEC Phase-II prioritises ML-1 (Mainline-1) railway upgrade from Karachi to Peshawar, alongside Special Investment Facilitation Council (SIFC) projects channeling Saudi and UAE investment. Pakistan joined BRICS as a partner state in late 2024 and continues SCO participation. The GSP Plus status with the EU remains contingent on 27 UN convention ratifications, renewed periodically.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** In which year did Pakistan exit the FATF grey list, and what is the installed capacity (gross) of the Karachi Nuclear Power Plant Unit K-3 (Hualong-One)?

(A) 2019; 1,017 MW
(B) October 2022; 1,100 MW (approx.)
(C) 2023; 1,200 MW
(D) October 2022; 2,200 MW

#### Solution:
- Step 1: Pakistan exited the FATF grey list in **October 2022** after the FATF Plenary in Paris. This eliminates (A) 2019 and (C) 2023.
- Step 2: K-3 (KARACHI-3) is a Hualong-One (ACP1000/HRP1000 family) reactor with a gross capacity of approximately **1,100 MW** (net ~1,014 MW after station-service load). It achieved grid connection in March 2023 and commercial operation in 2024. This eliminates (D) 2,200 MW (which is a common trap — students double K-2 + K-3 capacity).
- Step 3: Correct answer is **(B) October 2022; 1,100 MW (approx.)**.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Option (D) 2,200 MW is the trap — students conflate the combined capacity of K-2 and K-3 (each ~1,100 MW) with K-3 alone. Always quote single-unit capacity unless the question specifies "combined" or "twin-reactor complex."

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases & Boundary Conditions

1. **IMF Tranche Mechanics:** Each EFF disbursement is ~$500M-1B and is contingent on quarterly reviews. The 2nd and 3rd tranches (early 2024) faced delays over tax-reform legislation; examiners test whether you know programme *milestones*, not just the headline figure.
2. **Circular Debt Quantum:** The energy-sector circular debt accumulated to over PKR 2.3 trillion by mid-2024; the IMF benchmark targets containment, not full retirement. Confusing "stock" with "flow" leads to wrong answers.
3. **SBP Autonomy Amendment:** The SBP Act amendments (2022) granted operational autonomy; this is frequently tested under "which institution was granted autonomy in 2022" — the answer is SBP, not the Election Commission.
4. **K-3 vs. Chashma C-5 (if announced):** China's CNNC has signalled interest in C-5; students confuse "operational" reactors with "planned/announced" ones.
5. **GSP Plus Renewal:** The EU granted GSP Plus renewal in 2023 covering 2024-2027, conditional on continued convention compliance. Distinguish renewal year from initial grant (2014).
6. **BRICS vs. SCO:** Pakistan joined BRICS as a partner state (not full member) in 2024; it has been an SCO member since 2017. This distinction appears in MCQs.

#### Common Mistakes Specific to This Topic

- Quoting FY2024 GDP as "calendar 2023 GDP" — always specify fiscal year.
- Stating "Pakistan left FATF in 2023" — actual exit was October 2022.
- Mixing up **Chief Justice of Pakistan** with **Chief Election Commissioner** — separate offices, separate appointments.
- Confusing the **Federal Minister for Finance and Revenue** with the **Minister of State for Finance** — full Federal Minister designation carries different weight in MCQs.
- Citing population as "220 million" — outdated; PBS 2024 projection exceeds 240M.
- Attributing ML-1 to Phase-I — it is firmly Phase-II.
