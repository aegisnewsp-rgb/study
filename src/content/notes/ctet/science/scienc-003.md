# Respiration and Circulation

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Respiration** is a two-fold process: *breathing* (ventilation of lungs) and *cellular oxidation* of glucose inside mitochondria to release ATP. **Circulation** is the continuous flow of blood through the **four-chambered human heart**, arteries, capillaries, and veins, driven by cardiac systole and diastole.

- **Vital Capacity (VC)** = ERV + TV + IRV ≈ 4000–4600 mL in a healthy adult.
- **Cardiac Output (CO)** = Stroke Volume × Heart Rate ≈ 5 L/min at rest (SV ≈ 70 mL, HR ≈ 72 bpm).
- **Cellular respiration**: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + ≈ 38 ATP (aerobic).
- Human heart shows **double circulation** — pulmonary (heart ↔ lungs) and systemic (heart ↔ body).
- **Arteries** carry blood away from the heart (thick elastic walls); **veins** carry blood toward it (thin walls, valves).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Mechanism of Breathing

Breathing is a mechanical process controlled by the **diaphragm** and **intercostal muscles**. During **inspiration**, the diaphragm contracts and flattens, while external intercostals lift the rib cage upward and outward. Thoracic volume increases, intrapulmonary pressure drops below atmospheric pressure (≈ −1 cm H₂O), and air rushes into the alveoli.

During **expiration**, the diaphragm relaxes and domes upward, internal intercostals pull the ribs down, thoracic volume decreases, pressure rises above atmospheric (≈ +1 cm H₂O), and air is expelled. This is a passive process at rest; forced expiration recruits abdominal muscles.

#### Gas Exchange and Transport

At the **alveoli**, O₂ diffuses down its partial pressure gradient (alveolar PO₂ ≈ 104 mmHg, blood PO₂ ≈ 40 mmHg) into pulmonary capillaries and binds **haemoglobin** to form **oxyhaemoglobin** (HbO₂). Each gram of Hb binds ≈ 1.34 mL of O₂.

CO₂ is transported in three forms: ≈ 70% as **bicarbonate ions (HCO₃⁻)** in plasma via carbonic anhydrase, ≈ 20–25% as **carbaminohaemoglobin**, and ≈ 5–10% dissolved in plasma (Henry's Law).

#### Human Heart and Double Circulation

The human heart has **two atria** (receiving chambers) and **two ventricles** (pumping chambers) separated by a complete septum. **Pulmonary circulation** sends deoxygenated blood from the right ventricle to the lungs via the pulmonary artery and returns oxygenated blood to the left atrium via pulmonary veins. **Systemic circulation** distributes oxygenated blood from the left ventricle through the aorta to body tissues and returns deoxygenated blood via the superior/inferior vena cava to the right atrium. The complete septum prevents any mixing of the two streams.

#### Lung Volumes and Cardiac Parameters

| Parameter | Typical Adult Value | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| Tidal Volume (TV) | ≈ 500 mL | Air moved in a normal quiet breath |
| Inspiratory Reserve Volume (IRV) | ≈ 2500–3000 mL | Extra air inhaled after a normal breath |
| Expiratory Reserve Volume (ERV) | ≈ 1000–1100 mL | Extra air exhaled after a normal breath |
| Residual Volume (RV) | ≈ 1200 mL | Air remaining after maximal expiration |
| Cardiac Output (CO) | ≈ 5 L/min | SV × HR at rest |
| Normal Heart Rate | 70–80 beats/min | Beats per minute in resting adult |

> **Exam trap:** Pulmonary artery carries *deoxygenated* blood and pulmonary vein carries *oxygenated* blood — the opposite of the systemic naming convention.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Misconceptions

CTET questions frequently hinge on four subtle distinctions. First, **respiration ≠ breathing**: breathing is only the bulk transport of air; cellular oxidation in mitochondria is the energy-yielding half. Second, although systemic arteries carry oxygenated blood, the **pulmonary artery** is the exception — it carries deoxygenated blood from the right ventricle to the lungs. Third, veins contain **valves** to prevent backflow under low pressure, which is why prolonged standing causes varicose veins. Fourth, **lymph** is not plasma — it is interstitial fluid that has entered lymph capillaries and lacks RBCs, returning excess tissue fluid to the subclavian veins.

#### Blood Components and Their Roles

**Plasma** (~55% of blood volume) is the straw-coloured matrix carrying nutrients, hormones, and wastes. **Erythrocytes (RBCs)** lack a nucleus, contain haemoglobin, and survive ≈ 120 days before being recycled in the spleen. **Leukocytes (WBCs)** provide immune defence (neutrophils, lymphocytes, monocytes). **Platelets (thrombocytes)** are cell fragments that aggregate with fibrin to form clots. A typical adult has ≈ 5–5.5 million RBCs/μL and 4000–11000 WBCs/μL.

#### Connection to Health and CTET Pedagogy

**Haemoglobin binds CO** ≈ 200–250 times more avidly than O₂, forming **carboxyhaemoglobin**, the basis of carbon-monoxide poisoning from incomplete combustion. **Hypertension** (BP > 140/90 mmHg) measured by a **sphygmomanometer** strains the left ventricle. The **electrocardiogram (ECG)** traces the P-QRS-T waveform, where QRS marks ventricular depolarisation. For CTET Paper II, expect diagram-based questions asking students to label the heart's chambers, valves (tricuspid, bicuspid), or trace pulmonary vs systemic flow.

#### Worked Example

A resting adult has HR = 75 bpm and SV = 70 mL/beat. Calculate CO and the time taken to circulate the entire blood volume (≈ 5 L) once.
**Solution:** CO = 70 × 75 = 5250 mL/min ≈ **5.25 L/min**. Time = 5000 mL ÷ 5250 mL/min ≈ 0.95 min ≈ **57 seconds** for one complete circulation.

#### Practice Prompts

1. A spirogram shows IRV = 3000 mL, TV = 500 mL, ERV = 1100 mL. Calculate Vital Capacity and identify which volume cannot be measured directly by spirometry.
2. Trace one RBC from the right atrium through the heart and back, naming each chamber, valve, and vessel encountered, and state the oxygen status at each stage.
