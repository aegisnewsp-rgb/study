# Theme 2 — Participation in International Affairs — HKDSE CSD Notes

This topic covers Topic 3 of Theme 2 of the HKDSE Citizenship and Social Development curriculum. It addresses the notion of multifaceted diplomacy since the 21st century, the actions and initiatives of the country in international affairs (including the Belt and Road Initiative, international organisations and forums, rescues and assistance to other countries), and the impact on the country's overall development and the active role played by Hong Kong.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Multifaceted diplomacy**: China's approach to international relations since the 21st century — engaging with governments, international organisations, regional bodies and non-state actors across political, economic, cultural, security and environmental domains.
- **Actions and initiatives**: rescues and assistance to other countries (e.g. responses to the 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami, the 2011 Japan earthquake, the COVID-19 vaccine provision to over 100 countries); setting up international organisations and forums (e.g. the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank, the Belt and Road Forum for International Cooperation); the Belt and Road Initiative (BRI, 2013).
- **Impact on national development**: the BRI is a stated pillar of the country's foreign policy and a key driver of outbound investment; international engagement supports economic growth, technology transfer, energy security, and cultural exchange.
- **Hong Kong's role**: Hong Kong is positioned as an international financial centre and a "super-connector" linking the Mainland with global markets; the city's legal, financial and professional services support the country's international engagement.

#### Examiner traps

- Treating the BRI as a single physical project — it is a framework covering infrastructure, trade, financial integration, digital Silk Road, and people-to-people bonds.
- Reducing multifaceted diplomacy to "soft power" — the topic covers both hard-power elements (peacekeeping, the People's Liberation Army's international role) and soft-power elements (Confucius Institutes, cultural exchange).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Multifaceted diplomacy

The term refers to China's diplomatic approach since the early 2000s, characterised by:

- **Bilateral engagement**: state visits, summit meetings, strategic partnerships with countries across all continents.
- **Multilateral engagement**: active participation in the United Nations system (including the UN Security Council as a permanent member), the World Trade Organization, the G20, the BRICS+, the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation, and the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation forum.
- **Regional initiatives**: the Belt and Road Initiative, the Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP), and the China-ASEAN Free Trade Area.
- **Sectoral engagement**: cultural (Confucius Institutes, the Chinese Cultural Centre in various capitals), technological (the Digital Silk Road), health (the COVID-19 vaccine provision, the Belt and Road Health Silk Road).

#### The Belt and Road Initiative (BRI)

Announced by President Xi Jinping in 2013 (the Silk Road Economic Belt in September 2013 in Astana; the 21st Century Maritime Silk Road in October 2013 in Jakarta). The BRI is a framework, not a single project.

Components include:

- **Infrastructure connectivity**: ports (e.g. the China-built ports in Gwadar, Hambantota and Piraeus), railways (e.g. the China-Laos Railway, the Mombasa-Nairobi Standard Gauge Railway), roads, bridges, pipelines.
- **Trade and investment**: free-trade agreements, bilateral investment treaties, customs cooperation.
- **Financial integration**: the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank (AIIB, 2016), the Silk Road Fund, the New Development Bank (BRICS bank, 2015).
- **Digital Silk Road**: 5G infrastructure, fibre-optic cables, data centres, AI standards.
- **Health Silk Road**: COVID-19 vaccine diplomacy, health-system cooperation.
- **People-to-people bonds**: scholarships, tourism, cultural exchange.

By 2023, over 140 countries and 30+ international organisations had signed BRI cooperation agreements.

#### Hong Kong's role

Hong Kong is positioned by both the HKSAR Government and the Central Government as a key node in the BRI:

- **International financial centre**: Hong Kong provides bond issuance, project finance, insurance, and legal services for BRI projects.
- **Professional services**: Hong Kong-based accountants, lawyers, arbitrators, architects and engineers support BRI projects.
- **Trade and shipping**: Hong Kong's port is one of the busiest container ports in the world; the Hong Kong International Airport is a major logistics hub.
- **Cultural exchange**: West Kowloon Cultural District, the Hong Kong Palace Museum (opened 2022), and other cultural venues support cultural diplomacy.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-topic links

- **Theme 1 (Hong Kong under One Country Two Systems)**: the HKSAR's role in national affairs includes its international engagement.
- **Theme 3 (Interconnected Contemporary World)**: the country's international participation connects to economic globalisation (Topic 1), sustainable development (Topic 3) and public health (Topic 4).
- **National Security Education**: international engagement has a national-security dimension (counter-terrorism, non-traditional security, overseas interests protection).

#### Exam technique

- **For short-essay items on the BRI**: state the BRI's components, give a specific example project, and link to an impact on the country's development or Hong Kong's role.
- **For data-response items**: distinguish between the BRI's announced scope and its actual implementation; cite specific signed cooperation agreements.
- **For "evaluate the impact" items**: balance positive impacts (infrastructure, trade, financial integration) with challenges (debt sustainability, environmental impact, geopolitical reactions).

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "The BRI is a Chinese colonial project." No — it is a cooperation framework based on bilateral agreements. Each project is signed by both parties.
- "Hong Kong is a passive recipient of national policy." No — Hong Kong has an established role as an international financial centre that supports national objectives.
- "Multifaceted diplomacy is purely economic." It covers political, economic, cultural, security, environmental and technological domains.
