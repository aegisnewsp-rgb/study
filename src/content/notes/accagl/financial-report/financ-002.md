# Income Statement & Statement of Comprehensive Income

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

- The **income statement**, or statement of profit or loss, reports revenues, expenses, gains and losses for a reporting period.
- **Profit or loss** includes all income and expenses recognised outside OCI. Items such as sales, cost of sales and operating expenses affect profit or loss.
- **Other comprehensive income (OCI)** includes specified income and expenses recognised directly outside profit or loss, such as revaluation surpluses.
- **Total comprehensive income (TCI)** equals profit for the year plus or minus OCI.
- A two-statement approach presents a separate statement of profit or loss, followed by a statement of comprehensive income beginning with profit for the year.
- A single-statement approach combines both sections in one statement.
- **Gross profit = Revenue − Cost of sales**.
- **Profit for the year = Profit before tax − Income tax expense**.
- OCI must be separated between items that will not be reclassified and items that can be reclassified subsequently.
- Listed entities present basic and diluted earnings per share on the face of the income statement.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core structure

The income statement measures performance for a period. It starts with **revenue**, deducts **cost of sales** to obtain **gross profit**, and then deducts operating expenses to determine **operating profit**.

Finance income, finance costs and results from associates or joint ventures bridge operating profit to **profit before tax (PBT)**. Income tax expense then reduces PBT to **profit for the year**, also called net profit or PAT.

| Measure | Calculation |
| --- | --- |
| Gross profit | Revenue − cost of sales |
| Operating profit | Gross profit − operating expenses |
| Profit before tax | Operating profit + finance income − finance costs ± associate or joint venture results |
| Profit for the year | Profit before tax − income tax expense |

The statement of comprehensive income adds **OCI** to profit for the year. Typical non-reclassifying items include property revaluation surpluses and remeasurements of defined-benefit obligations. Foreign-operation translation differences and cash-flow hedge reserves can later enter profit or loss.

#### Presentation and classification

IAS 1 permits two presentation formats. A two-statement presentation shows profit or loss first and OCI second. A single statement presents profit or loss, followed by OCI and TCI.

| OCI category | Examples and treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Will not be reclassified | Revaluation surplus and certain equity investments measured at FVOCI |
| May be reclassified | Foreign-exchange translation differences and cash-flow hedge reserves |
| Tax allocation | Tax follows the underlying item and can be recognised in profit or loss or OCI |

Expenses can be analysed by nature or by function. Under the functional format, a company presents cost of sales, distribution and administrative expenses separately, while disclosing depreciation, employee benefits and amortisation.

#### Common ACCA and CA traps

- Do not call operating income “other comprehensive income”; it remains part of profit or loss.
- Do not recycle a revaluation surplus through profit or loss merely because the asset is sold.
- Equity investments designated at FVOCI produce OCI changes that are not recycled on disposal.
- Extraordinary items are prohibited.
- Discontinued operations appear as a single amount, with their analysis disclosed in the notes.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Worked relationship

Consider revenue of PKR 1,200 million, cost of sales of PKR 780 million, operating expenses of PKR 190 million, finance costs of PKR 35 million, and income tax expense of PKR 58 million.

| Calculation | Amount (PKR million) |
| --- | ---: |
| Gross profit: 1,200 − 780 | 420 |
| Operating profit: 420 − 190 | 230 |
| Profit before tax: 230 − 35 | 195 |
| Profit for the year: 195 − 58 | 137 |

If OCI contains a PKR 24 million revaluation gain and a PKR 6 million loss on a cash-flow hedge, TCI is PKR 155 million. Tax attributable to the revaluation gain must be recognised in OCI under IAS 12, rather than being charged entirely to profit or loss.

#### Edge cases and presentation strategy

1. **Discontinued operations:** IFRS 5 requires one post-tax amount on the face of profit or loss. A note must disclose revenue, expenses, pre-tax profit or loss, tax expense and the related cash flows.
2. **Earnings per share:** Basic EPS divides profit attributable to ordinary shareholders by the weighted-average ordinary shares. Diluted EPS adds the effects of potentially dilutive instruments, such as options or convertible securities.
3. **OCI recycling:** Translation differences and debt-security FVOCI changes can move to profit or loss later. Revaluation surpluses and designated equity investments at FVOCI are not recycled.
4. **IFRS 18 changes:** For annual periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027, IFRS Accounting Standards require eligible public-interest entities to classify income and expenses in the profit-or-loss statement into operating, investing and financing categories, with income taxes and discontinued operations shown separately. ACCA and CA candidates should check the transition requirements applicable to their exam timetable.

> Exam strategy: compute each subtotal vertically, label OCI items before arithmetic, and place related tax in the same section as the underlying transaction.

Practice prompts:

1. Prepare a two-statement presentation from a trial balance containing a revaluation gain, finance costs, tax, revenue and operating expenses.
2. Calculate basic and diluted EPS where a convertible bond increases attributable profit and the weighted-average share count.
