# Spelling, Grammar and Punctuation Conventions — OSSLT Writing

Conventions (spelling, grammar, and punctuation) are one of the four dimensions of the EQAO OSSLT rubric for the long-writing tasks. The rubric scores the response's accuracy on conventions separately from idea development, organisation, and language. A response with strong ideas but weak conventions scores lower than a response with clear conventions. Conventions are also tested directly in the OSSLT selected-response Writing items.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### What conventions cover on the OSSLT

- **Spelling.** Correct spelling of high-frequency and subject-specific words. The OSSLT spell-check is available on the computer-based platform.
- **Grammar.** Subject-verb agreement, pronoun-antecedent agreement, consistent verb tense, correct modifier placement, correct word form.
- **Punctuation.** Capital letters, end punctuation, internal punctuation (commas, apostrophes, quotation marks), dialogue-specific punctuation.
- **Sentence completeness.** No run-on sentences, no sentence fragments.

#### The high-frequency conventions errors

The OSSLT rubric weights the following errors heavily:

- Subject-verb disagreement (e.g., "the students was" instead of "the students were").
- Comma splices (joining two independent clauses with a comma instead of a period or conjunction).
- Missing end punctuation (a sentence that does not end with a period, question mark, or exclamation point).
- Missing capital letter at the start of a sentence.
- Apostrophe errors (confusing its/it's, your/you're, their/they're/there).

#### Examiner traps

- A sentence that reads smoothly when read aloud but has a subject-verb disagreement. Read the sentence silently, marking the subject and verb separately.
- A compound sentence joined by "and" or "but" without a comma before the conjunction. The comma is required in formal writing.
- A possessive apostrophe that is misplaced or missing. The rule: apostrophe goes before the "s" for singular possessives ("the dog's bone") and after the "s" for plural possessives ("the dogs' bones").

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Strategy for conventions

**Re-read the response for conventions after writing.** After drafting the response, set the ideas aside and re-read for conventions only. Catch spelling errors, grammar errors, and punctuation errors that the drafting pass missed.

**Use the platform's spell-check if available.** The OSSLT computer-based platform has a spell-check tool. Use it before submitting, but do not rely on it alone — spell-check does not catch grammar or punctuation errors.

**Read each sentence aloud.** Reading aloud makes run-on sentences and sentence fragments obvious. If you run out of breath reading the sentence, it is probably a run-on. If the sentence does not express a complete thought, it is probably a fragment.

**Mark the subject and verb in each sentence.** Subject-verb agreement is the most-tested grammar item. Find the subject, find the verb, check that they agree in number.

**Check comma usage against the rules.** Memorise the comma rules: comma in a list, comma after an introductory clause, comma before a coordinating conjunction in a compound sentence, comma around non-essential clauses. Do not add commas by feel.

#### Practice routine for conventions

- Take one EQAO sample long-writing task. Write the response.
- Re-read the response for conventions only. Mark each error you find.
- Keep a personal "conventions miss list" — the errors you make most often. Re-read the list before each writing session.
- Take one OSSLT conventions selected-response item set. Time yourself on conventions alone.
- Target: fewer than three conventions errors per response.

#### How this skill connects to the OSSLT reading tasks

The same conventions rules are tested in the Reading component and in the scoring of the long-writing tasks. Practising conventions in Writing reinforces them in Reading.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How conventions connect to the other OSSLT items

- **The long-writing tasks** score conventions as a separate rubric dimension. A response with strong ideas but many conventions errors scores lower overall than a response with clear conventions and moderate ideas.
- **The selected-response Writing items** test conventions directly. These items require the student to identify the correct spelling, the correct grammar, and the correct punctuation.

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"The spell-check will catch all my errors."** Spell-check catches spelling errors only. It does not catch grammar errors (subject-verb agreement, pronoun case) or punctuation errors (missing commas, misplaced apostrophes).
- **"Run-on sentences are acceptable in informal writing."** The OSSLT is a formal assessment. Run-on sentences score lower on conventions.
- **"I can use exclamation points to make my opinion stronger."** Exclamation points are appropriate in limited contexts. Overuse scores lower on conventions.

#### Exam technique

- Write the response. Set the ideas aside.
- Re-read the response for conventions only. Mark each error.
- Use the platform's spell-check.
- Check subject-verb agreement in every sentence.
- Check comma usage against the rules.
- Check apostrophe usage against the rules.
- Read the response aloud. Catch any run-on sentences or fragments.
