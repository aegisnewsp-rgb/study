# Amines, Amino Acids and Proteins

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Amines are derivatives of ammonia (NH₃) in which one, two, or three hydrogens are replaced by alkyl or aryl groups, classified as **primary (1°)**, **secondary (2°)**, or **tertiary (3°)**. The –NH₂ group carries a lone pair that accepts a proton, making amines basic; alkyl amines are stronger bases than ammonia, while **aniline (C₆H₅NH₂)** is a weak base because the lone pair delocalises into the benzene ring.

Amino acids are bifunctional molecules with both –NH₂ and –COOH on the α-carbon (H₂N–CHR–COOH). At physiological pH they exist as **zwitterions** (H₃N⁺–CHR–COO⁻), and at the **isoelectric point (pI)** carry no net charge. Two amino acids join by a **peptide bond** (–CO–NH–), releasing one H₂O (18 g·mol⁻¹), to form polypeptides and ultimately proteins.

- Classification: 1°, 2°, 3° amines by alkyl groups on N.
- Biuret test (Cu²⁺/NaOH) → violet = peptide bonds = protein.
- Denaturation disrupts 2°/3°/4° structure, NOT peptide bonds.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Classification and IUPAC naming
Amines are named as **alkanamines** in IUPAC system: methanamine (CH₃NH₂), ethanamine (C₂H₅NH₂), and named as **phenylamine** (C₆H₅NH₂) rather than aniline in strict IUPAC. The nitrogen carries a lone pair, so amines behave as **Lewis bases** and form salts with mineral acids (RNH₂ + HCl → RNH₃⁺Cl⁻).

#### Basicity order
Aqueous basicity follows the order: (CH₃)₂NH > CH₃NH₂ > (CH₃)₃N > NH₃ > C₆H₅NH₂. Alkyl groups donate electrons (+I effect), raising electron density on N. In **aniline**, resonance pulls the lone pair into the ring, making it the weakest base in this series.

| Reduction of nitro compound | R–NO₂ + Sn/HCl (or H₂/catalyst) | R–NH₂ |
| Hofmann degradation | R–CONH₂ + Br₂/NaOH | R–NH₂ (one C fewer) |
| Ammonolysis of alkyl halide | RX + NH₃ (excess) | R–NH₂ + R₂NH + R₃N |

#### Amino acids and peptide bonds
α-Amino acids have the general formula **H₂N–CHR–COOH**. The peptide bond forms by condensation: H₂N–CHR₁–COOH + H₂N–CHR₂–COOH → H₂N–CHR₁–CO–NH–CHR₂–COOH + H₂O. The mass lost per bond is **18 g·mol⁻¹**.

- **Essential amino acids** (9): lysine, methionine, tryptophan, valine, leucine, isoleucine, threonine, phenylalanine, histidine — must come from diet.
- **Non-essential**: glycine, alanine, serine, glutamic acid — synthesised by the body.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Protein structure and denaturation
Proteins fold into four levels of structure: **primary** (covalent sequence of peptide bonds), **secondary** (α-helix and β-pleated sheet held by hydrogen bonds), **tertiary** (3D folding from R-group interactions — hydrophobic, ionic, disulfide), and **quaternary** (multiple subunits, e.g., haemoglobin). Denaturation by heat, pH extremes, or heavy-metal ions (Hg²⁺, Pb²⁺) disrupts levels 2°–4° only; peptide bonds survive, which is why boiled egg white is still protein chemically.

#### Worked example — peptide mass
A tripeptide formed from three glycine residues (H₂N–CH₂–COOH, M = 75 g·mol⁻¹ each) loses **2 × 18 = 36 g·mol⁻¹** of water across two peptide bonds. Final mass = (3 × 75) − 36 = **189 g·mol⁻¹**.

| Diagnostic test | Reagent | Positive result | Target |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Biuret | CuSO₄ + NaOH | Violet/Purple colour | Peptide bonds (proteins/polypeptides) |
| Xanthoproteic | Conc. HNO₃, then NH₃ | Yellow → orange | Aromatic R-groups (Phe, Tyr, Trp) |
| Millon's | Hg(NO₃)₂ in HNO₃ | Red precipitate | Tyrosine residues |
| HNO₂ on 1° aliphatic | NaNO₂ + HCl, warm | N₂ gas evolved | Aliphatic primary amine |

#### Common traps in NECO questions
1. Writing the peptide linkage as –C–O–N– instead of **–C(=O)–N–H–** (amide).
2. Treating **aniline** as a strong base because it is "an amine".
3. Saying denaturation "breaks peptide bonds" — it only disrupts non-covalent interactions.
4. Confusing the isoelectric point with pH 7.4 — zwitterions only have net zero charge at pI.
