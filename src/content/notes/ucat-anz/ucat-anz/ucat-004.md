# Situational Judgement — UCAT ANZ Notes

Situational Judgement is the fourth subtest of the UCAT ANZ. It measures the candidate's capacity to understand real world situations and to identify critical factors and appropriate behaviour in dealing with them. Tested through scenarios drawn from healthcare and professional settings; measures integrity, perspective taking, team involvement and resilience.

> Re-check the live test format and any updates on https://www.ucat.edu.au/about-ucat-anz/test-format/ before planning revision around these figures.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Subtest structure.** Scenarios followed by questions about appropriate behaviour. Some items ask for the most appropriate action; others for the most important consideration.
- **Scoring.** Items are scored against a consensus key. There is no penalty for guessing.
- **Time pressure.** Approximately 22 seconds per item.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing "most important" with "most appropriate" — these are different questions.
- Applying personal opinion rather than the consensus answer.

---

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Reading the scenario

Identify the key actors, the conflict or issue, and the underlying professional values (integrity, perspective taking, team involvement, resilience).

#### Answering

For "most appropriate action" questions, choose the response that addresses the issue professionally and ethically. For "most important consideration" questions, choose the principle that the response embodies.

#### SJT principles

Integrity: honesty, transparency, accountability.
Perspective taking: empathy, respect for patients and colleagues.
Team involvement: collaboration, communication, conflict resolution.
Resilience: handling pressure, learning from mistakes.

---

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Why SJT rewards principled reasoning

- SJT tests professional judgement, not domain knowledge. The consensus key is based on what healthcare professionals agree is appropriate.

#### Practice pattern

- One SJT drill per day in the 6-8 weeks before the test.
- Use the official UCAT practice tests.
- Review each question carefully — understand the principle.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Personal opinion matters." No — SJT scores against a consensus key.
- "All options are correct." No — only the most appropriate option earns marks.

#### Specification reference

This subtest is one of four UCAT ANZ subtests. Confirm the live test format and any updates on ucat.edu.au before each cycle.
