# Kayarian ng mga Salita (Word Formation)

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Anchors the four processes tested by UPCAT in one pass. Read this if you have under an hour.

Filipino builds new words through exactly four moves: **Paglapi** (affixation), **Pag-uugnay** (compounding), **Pagbabaybay-salin** (blending/portmanteau), and **Panghihiram na may Pagbabagong-anyo** (borrowing with Filipinization). Paglapi is the most productive — every UPCAT set ships at least one affixation item. The affix slot determines its name: **unlapi** (prefix), **gitlapi** (infix), **hulapi** (suffix), **kabilaan** (circumfix).

| Affix type | Position | Filipino term | Sample build |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prefix | Before the root | **Unlapi** | *ma-* + *lungkot* → **malungkot** |
| Infix | Inside the root | **Gitlapi** | *s-um-ulat* from **sulát** |
| Suffix | After the root | **Hulapi** | *halik* + *-an* → **halikan** |
| Circumfix | Prefix + suffix together | **Kabilaan** | *ka-* + *babae* + *-an* → **kababaihan** |

A **salitang-ugat** (root) is the irreducible core (*takbo*); a **salitang-base** is a derived form used as the base for more derivation (*magtakbo* becomes *tagapagtakbo*). Treat the *-um-* infix with care: it slots in **after the first consonant** of the root — *k-u-ha* from *kuha*, never *\*um-kuha*.

> 💡 **Memory Hook — "U-Ge-Hu-Ka"**: **U**nlapi, **Ge**tlapi, **Hu**lapi, **Ka**bilaan. Singsong order of affix position; sing it once and the four slot names stick for the whole subtest.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Working knowledge for the UPCAT Filipino subtest — affixation rules, root-vs-base discipline, compound and blend recognition.

#### Four processes, one exam

UPCAT Filipino items typically give you a target word and ask *paano ito nabuo?* (how was this formed?). Decide in this order: is an affix attached? → Paglapi. Are two free morphemes joined by a hyphen? → Pag-uugnay. Is part of one word merged with part of another? → Pagbabaybay-salin. Is the whole word a loanword already Filipinized in spelling? → Panghihiram.

#### Verb-focus affixation (most-tested cluster)

Filipino verbs are shaped by focus affixes; recognise the set and you can read any verb form:

| Focus | Affix set | Sample derivation | Plain sentence sense |
|---|---|---|---|
| Actor (Pandatigma) | *mag-*, *nag-*, *-um-* | *mag-* + *aral* → *mag-aral* / *nag-aaral* | The subject does the action |
| Object (Layon) | *-in*, *-hin* | *kain* → *kinain* | The action happens to the object |
| Locative/Directional | *i-*, *-an* | *luto* → *lutu-an* | Direction or place of the action |
| Instrumental | *ipag-* | *bili* → *ipagbili* | Used to do the action |
| Benefactive | *mag-...-an* | *bigay* → *magbigayan* | Done for someone's benefit |
| Circumstantial | *pag-...-an* | *tawa* → *pagtawa-an* | Reason, manner, or circumstance |

Watch the **-um-** slot: it drops in *after* the root's first consonant. *Sulat* becomes *sumulat* (s-u-lat). *Kuha* becomes *kumuha* (k-u-ha). Writing *\*umsulat* or *\*umkuha* is a spelling-grade slip UPCAT love to test.

#### Root versus base — the difference UPCAT rewards

A **salitang-ugat** is the smallest meaningful chunk and carries the dictionary sense. A **salitang-base** already has an affix but is used as the platform for further derivation. Trace *tagapagbigay* (giver): root *bigay*, base *magbigay*, then prefix *tagapag-* stacks on to give *tagapagbigay*. Confusing the two levels is the single most common reason students mis-tag formation type.

#### Compounds, blends, loanwords

- **Pag-uugnay (compound)** — two or more free morphemes, hyphenated: *Bahay-Pamahalaan*, *kapatid-na-babae*, *sari-sari store* (reduplicated form).
- **Pagbabaybay-salin (blend)** — front of word A + tail of word B: *edukasyon* + *internet* → **EdukNet**, *telebisyon* + *advertisement* → **telead**.
- **Panghihiram (loanword)** — adopt and Filipinize per KWF orthography: *computer* → **kompyuter**, *birthday* → **kaarawan**, *-tion* → **-syon** (*istasyon*, *komisyon*).

#### Worked sample for the subtest

A UPCAT-style item: *"Alin sa mga sumusunod ang salitang hulapi (suffix) na idinagdag sa salitang-ugat upang makabuo ng bagong salita?"* with options like *magtakbo*, *kasamahan*, *pagkain*, *sumayaw*.

Walk it:
1. Strip each option to find what was added.
2. *magtakbo* → prefix *mag-* + root *takbo* = **unlapi**.
3. *kasamahan* → *ka-* + root *sama* + *-han/-an* wrapper = **kabilaan**.
4. *pagkain* → *pag-* + root *kain* = **unlapi** (with *-in* acting as a nominalizer; the *pag-* remains a prefix).
5. *sumayaw* → root *sayaw* with infix *-um-* = **gitlapi**.

Surrounding distractors are not always pure hulapi — verify by affix *position*, not by affix *count*.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap**: items showing **-an** at the end are not automatically hulapi. Check whether a *ka-* or *pa-* sits in front. *Ka-…-an* (e.g., *kasamahan*) is kabilaan, not hulapi alone.

> 📌 **KWF Shortcut**: English *-tion/-sion* = Filipino **-syon**. Apply this single rule and most loanword spelling items become mechanical.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Edge cases, reduplication logic, and the items UPCAT writes to break confident guessers.

#### Reduplication is its own formation move

Pag-uugnay includes **inuulit na salita** (reduplicated forms), and UPCAT distinguishes **full** from **partial** reduplication with different grammatical jobs:

| Reduplication | Form | Function | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full (whole-word repeat) | Root + root | Plurality, distribution, or intensity | *kanta-kanta* (various songs), *sari-sari* (variety store) |
| Partial, first-CV/syllable repeat | First consonant + first vowel copied to prefix slot | Non-completed action, attempt, or mildness | *k-kain* (about to eat), *l-lakad* (going to walk) |
| Partial, alternation pattern | Repeating root with *-um-* or *R-* | Repeated or continuous action | *lumingon-lingon* (looking around), *magbasa-basa* (reading repeatedly) |

Partial reduplication with the *R-* pattern is the one students miss entirely — they treat the doubled opener as a typo.

#### Boundary cases that flip the answer

- **Pseudo-affixes.** Some stems already end in *-an* or start with *pa-* by nature (e.g., *ulan*, root of rainfall). Stripping a fake affix to find a non-existent root is a trap.
- **Blends vs. compounds.** *Smoke-free zone* would be a compound; *EdukNet* is a blend. If both halves are full morphemes and the word is hyphenated, it is a compound. If each half has been clipped, it is a blend.
- **Borrowing vs. derivation.** A true loanword like *isko* (from *escuela*) is borrowing. A Filipino-coined term built from a foreign root with native affixes (e.g., *mag-dyip*, *kuma-computer*) is affixation layered on borrowing — call it paglapi, not panghihiram.

#### Adjacent topics worth pairing

- **Pokus ng pandiwa** (focus of the verb) — affixes change meaning with focus; mastering kayarian makes focus questions faster.
- **Salik ng pagbuo ng salita** (word-formation factors) — sociolinguistics angle covers why borrowing accelerates under English contact.
- **Mga bahagi ng pananalita** (parts of speech) — formation type often hints at the part of speech (*-han/-an* nominalizers, *mag-* verbs, *ka-…-an* abstract nouns).

#### Higher-difficulty practice prompts

1. The word **"pang-aapi"** appears in a passage. Walk it down to its root, identify every affix and its type (unlapi/gitlapi/hulapi/kabilaan), and decide whether the *pag-…-in* pattern is actor or object focus.
2. You are given four loanwords: *station*, *computer*, *birthday*, *restaurant*. Write each in its KWF-Filipinized form, state the rule you applied, and flag any that resist regular Filipinization.

> 📌 **Hard limit to remember**: *-um-* always lands **after the first consonant** of a CVC root, but **before the vowel** in vowel-initial roots (*um-iral* from *iral*). Forget the second case and you will mishandle every *um-* + vowel-initial item.
