# Idioms and Phrases

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

An **idiom** is a fixed group of words whose meaning cannot be derived from the literal sense of its parts — "kick the bucket" means *to die*, not to strike a pail. CUET UG tests idioms as **MCQs** worth 5 marks each (1 correct answer, no negative marking) inside the English paper.

- "Once in a blue moon" = rarely; not "during a lunar eclipse".
- "A piece of cake" = something very easy; not a baked dessert.
- "Spill the beans" = reveal a secret; not waste food.

| Idiom | Figurative Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| A blessing in disguise | A misfortune that turns out well |
| Burn the midnight oil | Work late into the night |
| Hit the nail on the head | State something exactly right |

The fastest scoring move: read the **whole sentence first**, then eliminate any literal option. The correct option will match the **emotional tone** (positive, negative, or neutral) of the passage.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### What makes an idiom different from a phrase

The defining property is **non-compositionality**: swapping any word breaks the meaning or destroys it. "By and by" cannot become "by and large" without changing sense. Unlike **proverbs**, idioms do not state a moral; unlike **phrasal verbs** (give up, look after), idioms are non-grammatical and do not inflect.

#### Three CUET question formats

1. **Idiom-to-meaning**: "The underlined idiom means —" followed by four options.
2. **Meaning-to-idiom**: "Choose the idiom that means *to startle someone*."
3. **Sentence replacement**: rewrite the sentence using a suitable idiom from the box.

#### High-yield idiom families

| Family | Sample Idioms | Shared Sense |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Body parts | cold feet, heart of gold, keep an eye on | emotions or vigilance |
| Animals | let the cat out of the bag, dog-tired, horse sense | hidden acts or traits |
| Colours | once in a blue moon, red-handed, green with envy | rarity, guilt, jealousy |
| Action verbs | break the ice, hit the sack, cut corners | beginning, rest, shortcut |

#### Strategy steps for the exam hall

- Read the **surrounding sentence**, not just the idiom in isolation.
- Discard options that read as **literal actions** ("literally hit someone").
- Match the **register**: idioms about anger rarely pair with cheerful contexts.
- Use **family clustering** — if you know "cold feet", "cold shoulder" is easier to recall.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge cases that trip strong candidates

**Homonymic idioms**: two idioms that share a keyword but diverge sharply. "At the drop of a hat" (immediately) and "to talk through one's hat" (speak nonsense) both involve hats but no overlap in meaning exists. Track these in pairs, not alone.

**Tone inversion**: "Cold comfort" sounds warm but means *poor consolation*; "food for thought" sounds edible but means *something worth considering*. The correct answer is always the **figurative reading aligned with sentence polarity**.

#### Worked example

> *Rohan was **on cloud nine** after clearing the entrance test.*

Options: (a) feeling depressed, (b) extremely happy, (c) physically elevated, (d) confused.
Eliminate (a) — "clearing" is positive. Eliminate (c) — literal reading. Eliminate (d) — no contradiction cues remain. **Answer: (b)**.

#### Practice prompts

1. Choose the idiom meaning *to reveal a secret*: spill the beans / bite the bullet / burn bridges / hold your horses.
2. Replace "He postponed the decision at the last moment" using one idiom from: **at the eleventh hour**, **on pins and needles**, **under the weather**, **a storm in a teacup**.

#### Common mistakes to avoid

| Mistake | Why it costs marks | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reading literally | Misses the entire test logic | Always ask "could this be figurative?" |
| Ignoring the cue word | Picks opposite tone | Mark positive/negative in the stem |
| Rote memorisation of 500+ idioms | Low retention under pressure | Learn 8–10 families of 6 idioms each |
