# Mendel's Laws and Cross Patterns

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Mendel's three laws describe how alleles segregate during meiosis and recombine during fertilisation. The **Law of Dominance** states one allele masks the other in the heterozygote. The **Law of Segregation** states each gamete receives only one of the two alleles. The **Law of Independent Assortment** states alleles of different genes assort independently when on different chromosomes.

- **Monohybrid F2 ratio:** phenotype 3 : 1; genotype 1 : 2 : 1.
- **Dihybrid F2 ratio:** 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 phenotype (16-box Punnett square).
- **Test cross:** cross with homozygous recessive reveals unknown genotype (1 : 1 if heterozygous).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### The Three Laws

Mendel crossed true-breeding pea plants and tracked seven traits over successive generations. His conclusions became the foundation of classical genetics.

The **Law of Dominance** says a dominant allele (capital letter, e.g. *A*) expresses its phenotype in both homozygotes and heterozygotes, while a recessive allele (*a*) only appears when homozygous. The **Law of Segregation** describes how homologous chromosome pairs separate in meiosis I, sending one allele into each gamete. The **Law of Independent Assortment** extends this to two or more genes on different chromosomes, producing all possible allele combinations equally.

#### Monohybrid vs Dihybrid Outcomes

A monohybrid cross follows one gene (e.g. *Aa × Aa*), and a dihybrid cross follows two genes independently (e.g. *AaBb × AaBb*).

| Cross type | F2 phenotype ratio | F2 genotype ratio | Gametes per heterozygous parent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monohybrid (Aa × Aa) | 3 : 1 | 1 AA : 2 Aa : 1 aa | 2 (A, a) |
| Dihybrid (AaBb × AaBb) | 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 | 1:2:1:2:4:2:1:2:1 (9 classes) | 4 (AB, Ab, aB, ab) |

#### Test Cross Purpose

- **Goal:** reveal whether a dominant-phenotype individual is *AA* or *Aa*.
- **Cross partner:** always homozygous recessive (*aa*).
- **Result:** all dominant offspring ⇒ parent is *AA*; 1 : 1 ratio ⇒ parent is *Aa*.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Probability Rules Applied to Genetics

Two rules govern Mendelian prediction. The **multiplication rule** computes joint probability of independent events (e.g. probability of an *A* gamete from one parent *and* a *B* gamete from another = ¼ × ¼ = 1/16). The **addition rule** sums probabilities of mutually exclusive outcomes (e.g. *AA* + *Aa* = 3/4 dominant phenotype in F2).

#### Worked Micro-Example

Cross a heterozygous tall, round-seeded plant (*TtRr*) with another identical heterozygote. Each parent produces four gametes (*TR*, *Tr*, *tR*, *tr*) each at probability ¼. Filling the 16-box Punnett square gives 9 *T_R_ : 3 *T_rr : 3 *ttR_ : 1 *ttrr* — exactly the 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 ratio.

#### Common Exam Traps

| Mistake | Correction |
| --- | --- |
| Reporting phenotype as genotype | List both: e.g. "3 tall : 1 short" for phenotype, "1:2:1" for genotype |
| Applying 9:3:3:1 to linked genes | Linked genes distort the ratio toward parental types (repulsion < 50%) |
| Counting only parental gametes in dihybrid | A heterozygote *AaBb* yields all four gamete types equally |
| Assuming dominant parent is homozygous | Without a test cross, *Aa* and *AA* are phenotypically identical |

#### Practice Prompts

1. In a monohybrid F2 of 200 offspring, how many are expected to be homozygous recessive if the cross was *Aa × Aa*? (Answer: 50)
2. A pea plant with yellow round seeds is crossed with a green wrinkled tester (*yyrr*). Offspring: 31 yellow-round, 29 yellow-wrinkled, 32 green-round, 28 green-wrinkled. What is the parent's genotype? (Answer: *YyRr*)
