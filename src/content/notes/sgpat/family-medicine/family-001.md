# The SCFHS Training Pathway

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Family medicine is the **primary care specialty** that delivers comprehensive, continuous, and coordinated care to individuals and families across all ages, sexes, and organ systems, grounded in the **biopsychosocial model**. In Saudi Arabia, the specialty is regulated by the **Saudi Commission for Health Specialties (SCFHS)** and practised within the Ministry of Health (MOH) **primary healthcare centres (PHCs)** that act as **gatekeepers** for the referral pyramid. The SCFHS residency is 4 years (R1–R4) including the Arab Board / Saudi Board final examination. The six core competencies from WONCA are: **primary care management**, **person-centred care**, **specific problem-solving skills**, **comprehensive approach**, **community orientation**, and **holistic modelling**. For the GP Board, weigh preventive services, NCD screening (DM, HTN, dyslipidaemia, breast/colon cancer), antenatal care, immunization schedules, and the appropriate referral threshold.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Definition and Scope
Family medicine provides first-contact, comprehensive, continuing, coordinated, community-oriented, and family-centred care. Unlike organ-defined specialties, family physicians manage **undifferentiated presentations** — chest pain, fatigue, abdominal pain — before a diagnosis exists, and they often manage the patient across decades. The **biopsychosocial model** (Engel) replaces the purely biomedical view by integrating psychological, social, family, occupational, and cultural factors.

#### The SCFHS Training Pathway
The Saudi Board of Family Medicine is a **4-year residency** (R1–R4) under SCFHS, with rotations in internal medicine, paediatrics, obstetrics, emergency, psychiatry, and electives, ending in the **Saudi Board Final Examination (SBFM)** — written (MCQ + EMQ) plus OSCE/structured oral. Holders are eligible for the **Saudi Classification and Registration** as a consultant family physician and the **Arab Board** dual pathway.

#### The WONCA Core Competencies
The 2005 WONCA framework defines six: **primary care management** (gatekeeping, resource use), **person-centred care**, **specific problem-solving** (early, undifferentiated), **comprehensive approach**, **community orientation**, and **holistic modelling**. The GP Board often asks candidates to apply one competency to a vignette.

#### Primary Care Procedures
Within PHCs the family physician performs: **suturing and wound care**, **incision and drainage**, **joint and soft-tissue injections**, **Pap smear**, **basic fracture splinting**, **neonatal resuscitation**, and **cryotherapy**. Procedural competence distinguishes the family physician from a triage nurse and is regularly tested as OSCE stations.

#### Preventive Services and Common Case Mix
Standard preventive packages include **childhood immunization (MOH schedule)**, **antenatal care visits**, **postnatal and well-baby checks**, and adult screening per MOH/CHO guidelines. The most common Saudi PHC presentations are **type 2 diabetes mellitus**, **hypertension**, **asthma**, **upper respiratory tract infections**, **vitamin D deficiency**, **low back pain**, and **depression/anxiety**.

#### Exam Question Patterns
The GP Board commonly tests: (a) choosing the **next step** in an undifferentiated presentation; (b) **screening intervals** (e.g. HbA1c every 3 years > 45 y, mammography biennial 40–69); (c) **referral vs manage in PHC** thresholds; (d) **biopsychosocial assessment** of a frequent attender.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Gatekeeping and Referral Logic
The family physician is the **entry point** of the Saudi health system; ≥80% of encounters should be **closed at PHC**. Correct referral is a balance: under-referral misses red flags (cord compression, ACS mimics, paediatric sepsis); over-referral saturates hospitals. Practical criteria for referral: **diagnostic uncertainty beyond PHC scope**, **failure of first-line therapy after an appropriate trial**, **need for specialised procedure or imaging not available at PHC**, and **red-flag symptoms** (sudden severe headache, neurological deficit, haematemesis, postpartum haemorrhage, neonatal jaundice in first 24 h, suspected child abuse).

#### Cost-Effective, Evidence-Based Practice
EBM at the primary-care level means choosing tests with high **likelihood ratio**, applying **number-needed-to-treat (NNT)**, and avoiding low-yield panels (e.g. annual ECG in low-risk adults). The Saudi PHC formulary restricts antibiotics and combination products; candidates should know rational prescribing of **antibiotics for URTI** (do not give for viral pharyngitis), **stewardship in DM/HTN**, and **deprescribing** in polypharmacy patients > 65 years.

#### Connections to Adjacent Topics
Family medicine overlaps with **community medicine** (population screening, epidemiology), **internal medicine** (chronic NCD management), **paediatrics** (well-child care, immunisations), **psychiatry** (mild–moderate depression, somatisation), and **obstetrics** (low-risk antenatal care, referral of high-risk). It is the **integrator** specialty — most board MCQs are vignettes that cross two domains to test *prioritisation*.

#### Common Mistakes
1. Treating it as a "minor illness" gate — ignoring preventive and chronic care.
2. Skipping **screening** when the chief complaint is acute.
3. Confusing **gatekeeping** with denying care; a refusal to refer when criteria are met is malpractice.
4. Using a biomedical-only assessment, missing **red-flag psychosocial** drivers (intimate partner violence, somatisation).

#### Practice Prompts
- **MCQ:** A 42-year-old woman with BMI 32, no symptoms, normal last fasting glucose two years ago. Next step? (Answer: repeat fasting glucose or HbA1c now — ADA recommends screening every 3 years above age 35 with overweight/obesity.)
- **OSCE:** A mother brings her 6-week-old for routine visit. List the **immunisations, growth, feeding, safety counselling, and maternal-postnatal depression screen** you will deliver in 8 minutes.
