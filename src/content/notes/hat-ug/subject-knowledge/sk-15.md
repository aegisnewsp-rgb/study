# General Science and Technology

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

General Science and Technology is a low-weight (~3%) Subject Knowledge section on HAT-UG that samples Matric/Intermediate-level facts from Physics, Chemistry, Biology, and everyday technology. Questions are MCQs that reward breadth, not depth — one well-known formula or named scientist earns the mark.

#### Must-know formulas and constants

| Formula | Meaning | Units |
| --- | --- | --- |
| F = m × a | Newton's 2nd law: force equals mass times acceleration | N, kg, m/s² |
| V = I × R | Ohm's law: voltage equals current times resistance | V, A, Ω |
| P = V × I | Electrical power | W = V·A |
| E = m × c² | Mass–energy equivalence, c ≈ 3×10⁸ m/s | J, kg |
| PV = nRT | Ideal gas law, R = 8.314 J/(mol·K) | Pa, m³, mol, K |

#### High-yield pointers
- SI base units: metre (m), kilogram (kg), second (s), kelvin (K), ampere (A), mole (mol), candela (cd).
- Newton's three laws, Ohm's law, and Mendel's laws of inheritance appear almost every cycle.
- In a vacuum all objects fall at the same rate — Galileo's result, not Aristotle's.

The HAT-UG Subject Knowledge paper pools questions across multiple disciplines, and General Science and Technology draws from secondary-school syllabi taught by FBISE and provincial boards. #### Physics essentials

- Newton's three laws: inertia, F = ma, action–reaction pair.
- Energy is conserved: kinetic (½mv²), potential (mgh), thermal (mcΔT).
- Ohm's law holds for resistors; it fails for diodes and LEDs.
- SI prefixes to memorise: kilo (10³), mega (10⁶), giga (10⁹), milli (10⁻³), micro (10⁻⁶), nano (10⁻⁹).

#### Chemistry essentials

- Atom = protons + neutrons + electrons; atomic number = proton count.
- Acids donate H⁺, bases donate OH⁻; pH = –log[H⁺].
- Redox: oxidation is electron loss, reduction is electron gain (OIL RIG).
- Periodic trends: atomic radius decreases across a period, ionisation energy increases.

#### Biology essentials

- Cell theory: all organisms are made of cells; the cell is the basic unit of life.
- Mitosis produces two identical diploid cells; meiosis produces four haploid gametes.
- DNA carries genetic information; genes are segments of DNA.
- Ecosystem flow: producers → consumers → decomposers, with energy lost at each trophic level (≈10% rule).

#### Common traps in MCQs

| Trap | Correct principle |
| --- | --- |
| Mass vs weight | Mass (kg) is constant; weight (N) = mg varies with gravity |
| Speed vs velocity | Speed is scalar (m/s); velocity is vector with direction |
| Correlation vs causation | Vaccines prevent disease by biological action, not by association |
| Weather vs climate | Weather = short-term; climate = 30-year pattern |
| Conductors vs insulators | Metals conduct; rubber, plastic, wood insulate |

#### Worked example — applying Ohm's law

A resistor of 20 Ω carries 0.5 A. Voltage across it: V = IR = 0.5 × 20 = **10 V**. Power dissipated: P = VI = 10 × 0.5 = **5 W**. The same calculation fails for an LED because its V–I curve is non-linear.

#### Practice prompts

1. An object of mass 4 kg accelerates at 3 m/s². What force acts on it, and what is its kinetic energy after 5 s starting from rest?
2. List the seven SI base units and identify which ones appear in the formula PV = nRT.
