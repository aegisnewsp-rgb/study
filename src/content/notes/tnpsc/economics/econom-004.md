# Consumer Behaviour

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Consumer behaviour** studies how individuals select, buy, use, and dispose of goods to satisfy **needs** (biogenic, like food) and **wants** (psychogenic, shaped by culture).

**Four determinants** (Kotler): **Cultural** (culture, subculture, social class), **Social** (reference groups, family, roles), **Personal** (age, lifestyle, personality), **Psychological** (motivation, perception, learning, attitude).

**Decision process**: Need Recognition → Information Search → Evaluation → Purchase → Post-Purchase Behaviour.

#### Must-know formulas
- Utility rule: MUx/Px = MUy/Py
- Consumer Surplus = TU − TE
- Engel Coefficient = (Food expenditure / Total consumption expenditure) × 100

**Exam quick hits**: Maslow's five needs, Buyer's Black Box (4Ps → black box → response), Consumer Protection Act 2019 rights.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Determinants of Buyer Behaviour

Four forces shape every purchase decision. **Cultural** factors set the basic wants; **social** factors (family, reference groups) modify them; **personal** factors (age, lifecycle, lifestyle, self-concept) add individual texture; **psychological** factors (motivation, perception, learning, beliefs, attitudes) decide the final response.

| Determinant | Examples | Exam Cue |
|---|---|---|
| Cultural | Caste, religion, region | "Cultural factors influencing rural buyers" |
| Social | Family, peers, online groups | "Reference group influence on brand choice" |
| Personal | Age, occupation, lifestyle | "Affects FMCG targeting" |
| Psychological | Maslow, perception, attitude | "Most-tested in TNPSC" |

#### Consumer Decision-Making Process

The five-stage model is a recurring TNPSC short-note topic. **Problem recognition** triggers when actual state falls short of desired state. **Information search** draws from personal, commercial, public, and experiential sources. **Evaluation of alternatives** uses attributes weighted by attitude. **Purchase decision** is shaped by situational factors, and **post-purchase behaviour** produces satisfaction or cognitive dissonance that feeds back into future decisions.

#### Theories of Motivation

Maslow's hierarchy orders needs as physiological → safety → social → esteem → self-actualization. The hierarchy is generally bottom-up but not rigid. Freudian theory splits the mind into id (desire), ego (reality check), and superego (moral filter), explaining why consumers sometimes buy impulsively.

> **Trap**: Maslow's needs need not be fully satisfied before higher-order needs emerge; charity by the poor contradicts strict sequential reading.

#### Buyer's Black Box Model

Marketing stimuli (4 Ps) plus environmental stimuli enter the **buyer's black box** (buyer characteristics + decision process) and produce a **buyer response**: product choice, brand choice, dealer choice, timing, and quantity.

#### Indian Context and Consumer Rights

The **Consumer Protection Act, 2019** operationalises six rights: Right to Safety, Right to be Informed, Right to Choose, Right to be Heard, Right to Redressal, Right to Consumer Education. The Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA) regulates unfair trade practices, misleading ads, and product liability.

> **Examiner cue**: A 10-mark answer linking consumer rights to e-commerce grievances scores higher than a pure theoretical recall.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Major Behavioural Models

**Howard-Sheth Model** (B = f(P, S, I, T)) integrates psychological and social variables over time, useful for explaining repeat and habitual buying.

**Engel-Kollat-Blackwell (EKB) Model** breaks the decision into Problem Recognition → Information Search → Evaluation → Purchase → Outcome, with explicit feedback loops. Decision Choice = f(Evoked Set, Evaluative Criteria).

**Nicosia Model** treats purchase as a four-field flow: Firm's Message → Consumer Search & Evaluation → Act of Purchase → Feedback to firm.

#### Utility Maximisation and Consumer Surplus

A rational consumer equates **marginal utility per rupee** across goods (MUx/Px = MUy/Py). **Consumer Surplus** is the sum of differences between the maximum price willing to be paid and the price actually paid across all units — not a single number.

#### Engel Coefficient and Living Standards

A lower Engel Coefficient implies a higher standard of living, because a smaller share of income is absorbed by food. India's Engel Coefficient has steadily declined with rising per-capita income, signalling structural change in consumption toward services and durables.

#### Worked Example

A consumer buys 5 units of a good. Total utility = 200 utils; total expenditure = ₹150. Consumer Surplus = TU − TE = 200 − 150 = **50 utils (in monetary equivalent)**. If MUx/Px = 8 and MUy/Py = 6, the consumer should **shift spending from Y to X** until the ratios equalise.

#### Common Mistakes to Avoid

> Treat Maslow as sequential — refuse: pursuit of esteem often precedes physiological comfort in modern urban contexts.

- Writing Engel Coefficient over total income rather than total consumption expenditure.
- Placing lifestyle under social determinants instead of personal.
- Equating satisfaction with performance; satisfaction = perception of performance minus expectation.
- Omitting the post-purchase stage in descriptive answers.
