**Pakistan's History & Independence** — SPSC (Sindh) Study Guide

## Background: Muslim Politics in British India

### Early Muslim Leadership
- **Sir Syed Ahmed Khan (1817–1898)**: founder of Aligarh Muslim University; believed education was key to Muslim advancement
- **Sir Agha Khan III (1877–1937)**: first **permanent president** of the All India Muslim League (AIML), serving until his resignation in **1913**
- **Allama Iqbal (1877–1938)**: poet and philosopher; conceived the idea of a separate Muslim state in 1930 Allahabad Address

### Formation of All India Muslim League (1906)
- Founded at **Dacca (Dhaka)** on **30 December 1906**
- Initially sought protection of Muslim rights within British India
- **Muhammad Ali Jinnah** joined in 1906 and became its most prominent leader

### Key Events Leading to Pakistan

#### 1916 — Lucknow Pact
- Muslim League and Indian National Congress agreed on joint political action
- Jinnah emerged as a bridge between Hindus and Muslims
- First major concession by Congress to Muslim demands

#### 1928 — Nehru Report
- Congress proposed complete dominion status for India within 3 years
- Muslim League rejected it (1929) — Jinnah's **Fourteen Points** (1929) outlined Muslim demands including separate electorates

#### 1930 — Allahabad Address
- **Allama Iqbal** proposed that "the whole of India Muslim majority areas of Punjab, North-West Frontier Province, Sindh and Baluchistan" should form a separate state

#### 1935 — Government of India Act
- Provincial elections gave AIML clear majority in Punjab, Bengal, Sindh, NWFP
- Jinnah's political stature grew significantly

#### 1940 — Lahore Resolution (Pakistan Resolution)
- AIML session at **Lahore (23–24 March 1940)**
- Resolution moved by A.K. Fazlul Huq; seconded by Sardar Abdul Rashid
- Demanded creation of "separate states" for Muslims in the northwest and eastern zones of India
- Not yet called "Pakistan" (the word was coined by Choudhry Rahmat Ali in 1933)

#### 1945–1946 — Simla Conference & Cabinet Mission Plan
- Wavell Plan (1945) called for fresh elections
- Cabinet Mission Plan (1946) proposed a three-tier confederation — Muslims rejected it as inadequate
- **June 1946 — Direct Action Day**: Muslim League called for mass protests; riots in Calcutta (Direct Action Day riots killed 4,000+)

#### 1946 — Interim Government
- Jinnah agreed to join interim government under Nehru (but with reservations)
- Congress's inclusion of only Hindu members and Mountbatten's insistence on Congress primacy led to breakdown

## Partition and Independence (1947)

### Mountbatten Plan (3 June 1947)
- British Parliament passed **Indian Independence Act 1947** (July)
- **Date fixed**: midnight 14–15 August 1947
- Partition of British India into two dominions: **India** and **Pakistan**

### Radcliffe Line
- **Sir Cyril Radcliffe** (British judge) drew boundary between India and Pakistan
- Punjab and Bengal were split — massive riots followed
- **One million+ people killed** in partition violence; 14 million displaced

### 14 August 1947 — Independence Day
- Jinnah sworn in as **Governor-General of Pakistan** (first head of state)
- Liaquat Ali Khan became **Prime Minister**
- **Quaid-e-Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah** delivered his famous speech at the Constituent Assembly

## Quaid-e-Azam's Principles (for SPSC Reference)

Jinnah's vision for Pakistan as a secular, democratic state:
- **Freedom of religion** for all citizens
- **Equality** regardless of race, religion, or caste
- **Justice and fairplay** in all institutions
- **Islamic principles** but not a theocratic state — "you may belong to any religion... that has nothing to do with the business of the state"

## Post-Independence Challenges

- **One million+ refugees** from India required rehabilitation
- **Princely states** of Junagadh, Hyderabad, and Kashmir remained contested
- **Kashmir dispute** erupted immediately — First Indo-Pak War (1947–48)
- **Economic instability** due to assets division disputes with India

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Dive (5/10 — demand, plan and award are three different things, and the exam knows it)

> The yellow tier gives you a chronology. SPSC asks what each step *did*, and most
> lost marks in this topic come from calling a demand a plan, or a boundary award a
> treaty.

#### The decisive ladder: four steps that are not interchangeable

| Step | Year | What it actually was | Not to be confused with |
|---|---|---|---|
| Allahabad Address | 1930 | Iqbal's **proposal** for a separate Muslim state | Jinnah, who is not named in it |
| Lahore Resolution | 1940 | The League's **demand** for "separate states" | A constitution, or a boundary |
| Cabinet Mission Plan | 1946 | A **proposal** for a three-tier confederation, rejected as inadequate | The plan that produced partition |
| Mountbatten Plan | 3 June 1947 | The **plan** that partitioned British India | The boundary itself |

#### Examiner Trap — the Radcliffe Line is an award, not a plan

The note sets out the Mountbatten Plan, the Radcliffe Line and the Indian
Independence Act in sequence, and they are three different instruments. **Radcliffe
drew a boundary** — he was a British judge assigned to that task and authored no
plan. Mountbatten planned the partition. Parliament legislated it in July 1947. If a
stem asks who *drew* the border, the answer is Cyril Radcliffe; if it asks who
*planned* partition, it is Mountbatten. Conflating the two is the most common error
in this topic.

#### The word "Pakistan" predates the demand that created it

Two facts in this note sit seven years apart and are routinely swapped. Choudhry
Rahmat Ali **coined the term** in 1933, and the Lahore Resolution of 1940 demanded
"separate states" **without using the word**. So the name existed for years before the
League formally asked for the thing. A question pairing "who coined the name" with
"who moved the resolution" has two different answers — Rahmat Ali, and **A.K. Fazlul
Huq**, seconded by Sardar Abdul Rashid.

#### Match the name to the contribution, and know who is not in the table

A likelier SPSC question supplies a name and asks for the contribution:

- **Allama Iqbal** — conceived the idea of a separate Muslim homeland; the note calls him "Poet of Pakistan"
- **Jinnah** — founder of Pakistan and its first Governor-General; he *led* the movement but did not *first propose* the separate state
- **Liaquat Ali Khan** — first Prime Minister
- **A.K. Fazlul Huq** — moved the Lahore Resolution; Chief Minister of Bengal
- **Choudhry Rahmat Ali** — coined the name (1933)
- **Sir Syed Ahmed Khan** — Aligarh Muslim University, the education limb of the movement

Lord Mountbatten, Cyril Radcliffe and Wavell appear in the body of this note but are
**not** in the personalities table: they are British officials, not movement leaders.
Nehra appears as the other side of every negotiation. If a question offers Mountbatten
as "a leader of the Pakistan movement", reject that option on the note's own framing.

#### Two figures that describe the boundary, not the negotiation

**One million+ killed** and **14 million displaced** describe what happened once the
Radcliffe boundary was drawn, not the politics that preceded it. Punjab and Bengal
were both split, so the violence concentrated in exactly two provinces. Keep these
figures attached to the *boundary*, never to the Lahore Resolution or the Cabinet
Mission.

#### The scale test — five dates, five different kinds of thing

The note's date table mixes founding, sessions, calls to action, legislation and
commencement. Read what type each entry is before trying to recall it:

| Date | Type |
|---|---|
| 30 December 1906 | Founding of an organisation — AIML, at Dacca |
| 23–24 March 1940 | A resolution session — Lahore |
| 16 June 1946 | A call to mass action — Direct Action Day |
| July 1947 | Legislation — Indian Independence Act |
| 14 August 1947 | Constitutional commencement — independence, midnight 14–15 August |

Direct Action Day belongs to the June 1946 cluster: it is what broke the 1946
negotiations, so the note places it alongside the interim government rather than in
1947. If a question tests whether you can sequence 1946 events at all, that ordering
is the whole answer.
