# Growth and Development

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Growth** is an *irreversible* increase in the **size, mass, and dry weight** of an organism caused by the addition of new protoplasm through cell division and cell enlargement. **Development** is the series of *qualitative* changes — **differentiation** (cells becoming specialised), **morphogenesis** (formation of shape and organs), and **maturation** — that a zygote undergoes to become a complete adult. Plant growth is localised to **meristems** (apical, lateral, intercalary), giving rise to *primary growth* (length) and *secondary growth* (girth). Animal growth typically follows a **sigmoid (S-shaped) curve**: lag → log → deceleration → stationary phase. Five major **plant growth hormones** must be memorised: **auxins** (cell elongation, apical dominance), **gibberellins** (stem elongation, germination), **cytokinins** (cell division), **abscisic acid** (dormancy, stomatal closure), and **ethylene** (fruit ripening, leaf abscission). NABTEB commonly tests hormone functions, sigmoid curve stages, and germination types. Remember: **epigeal** germination lifts cotyledons above ground (bean); **hypogeal** keeps them below (maize, pea).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Plant Growth and Meristems
Plant growth is **localised** to meristematic tissues containing actively dividing cells. The **apical meristem** at root and shoot tips drives *primary growth* (increase in length); the **lateral meristem** (vascular and cork cambium) drives *secondary growth* (increase in girth). An **intercalary meristem** at the base of internodes allows regrowth in grasses after cutting.

#### Plant Growth Hormones (Phytohormones)
| Hormone | Main Function |
|---|---|
| **Auxins** (e.g., IAA) | Cell elongation, apical dominance, phototropism, gravitropism |
| **Gibberellins** | Stem elongation, break seed dormancy, stimulate germination |
| **Cytokinins** | Promote cell division, delay senescence, work antagonistically with auxins |
| **Abscisic acid (ABA)** | Induces dormancy, closes stomata under water stress |
| **Ethylene** | Fruit ripening, leaf abscission, response to stress |

#### Animal Growth Curve
A typical animal shows a **sigmoid growth curve** plotted as mass against time. The **lag phase** (slow initial growth), the **log/exponential phase** (rapid growth), the **deceleration phase** (growth slows as maturity approaches), and the **stationary phase** (growth ceases at adult size). In most vertebrates, growth halts at maturity when **epiphyseal growth plates** ossify; invertebrates (e.g., arthropods) continue moulting through **instars**.

#### Measurement of Growth
- Plants: **auxanometer** (records increase in height).
- Animals: **length, mass, head–body ratio**, and BMI in clinical contexts.

#### Seed Germination
Germination requires **water, oxygen, suitable temperature**, and sometimes **light**. In **epigeal** germination, the hypocotyl elongates and pushes the cotyledons above soil (e.g., *Phaseolus*). In **hypogeal** germination, the cotyledons stay below soil (e.g., maize, pea).

#### Exam Pattern (NABTEB)
Expect 1–2 multiple-choice or short-answer questions on hormone functions, a labelled sigmoid curve, or comparison of epigeal vs hypogeal germination. Numerical questions on growth rate are rare but possible using **Absolute growth = Final mass − Initial mass**.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Growth Rate Calculations
Master three formulas:
- **Absolute growth rate** = (Final size − Initial size) / Time taken
- **Relative growth rate** = (Increase in size / Initial size) × 100
- **Growth rate** = Change in size / Time

*Worked example:* A maize seedling grew from 4 cm to 14 cm in 5 days. Absolute growth rate = (14 − 4) / 5 = **2 cm/day**; relative growth rate = (10/4) × 100 = **250 %**.

#### Deep Dive — Differentiation vs Morphogenesis
**Differentiation** is when genetically identical cells become structurally and functionally distinct (e.g., a meristematic cell becoming a xylem vessel). **Morphogenesis** is the broader process that shapes tissues and organs through coordinated cell movement, division, and death (apoptosis). Both are essential components of **development**; without them, an organism would remain a mass of undifferentiated cells.

#### Metamorphosis
Insects show two patterns:
- **Complete metamorphosis (holometabolous)**: egg → **larva** → **pupa** → adult (e.g., butterfly, mosquito).
- **Incomplete metamorphosis (hemimetabolous)**: egg → **nymph** → adult (e.g., grasshopper, cockroach). Nymphs resemble miniature wingless adults and pass through several **instars**.

#### Common Mistakes to Avoid
1. Treating *growth* and *development* as synonyms — growth is **quantitative**, development is **qualitative**.
2. Believing animals grow throughout life — most **stop at maturity**; arthropods grow only at moulting.
3. Forgetting that high auxin concentrations *inhibit* root growth (used in weedkiller formulations like 2,4-D).
4. Confusing **epigeal** (cotyledons above) with **hypogeal** (cotyledons below) germination.
5. Overlooking that **ethylene** is a gaseous hormone — a frequent MCQ trap.

#### Practice Prompts
1. Sketch a sigmoid growth curve and label the lag, log, deceleration, and stationary phases; state one biological event marking each phase.
2. A student records a chick's mass as 30 g at hatching and 1.5 kg at 8 weeks. Calculate the absolute and relative growth rates, and explain why growth rate declines in the final 2 weeks.
