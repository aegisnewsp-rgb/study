# Major World Events

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Major World Events** in the LAT General Knowledge paper covers landmark political, military, economic and social developments from 1914 to the present that shaped the modern international order. The highest-yield items are: **World War I (1914–1918)**, ended by the **Treaty of Versailles (1919)**; **World War II (1939–1945)**, ended at the **Yalta** and **Potsdam** Conferences; the formation of the **United Nations (1945)** with its six principal organs; the **Cold War** bipolarity between the **USA and USSR**, closed by the **Fall of the Berlin Wall (1989)** and **Dissolution of the USSR (1991)**; **9/11 (2001)**; the **2008 Global Financial Crisis**; the **Arab Spring (2010–2012)**; and **Brexit (2020)**. For LAT, expect 2–3 MCQs: match the event with its year, identify the parties/treaty involved, or name the international body created. Memorise dates and acronyms together — that pairing is the most tested format.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### World Wars and Their Settlements
**World War I (28 July 1914 – 11 November 1918)** erupted from militarism, imperial rivalry, alliance entanglements (Triple Entente: France, Britain, Russia; Triple Alliance: Germany, Austria-Hungary, Italy) and the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand. The **Treaty of Versailles (28 June 1919)** imposed reparations on Germany, redrew European borders via the League of Nations mandates, and created the ill-fated **League of Nations**. **World War II (1 September 1939 – 2 September 1945)** began with Germany's invasion of Poland. The **Yalta Conference (Feb 1945)** and **Potsdam Conference (July–Aug 1945)** partitioned Germany, prosecuted Nazi war criminals at **Nuremberg**, and shaped the post-war order.

#### The United Nations System
The **UN Charter (26 June 1945)** established the United Nations on 24 October 1945. Six principal organs: **General Assembly** (one state, one vote; advisory), **Security Council** (15 members, 5 permanent with **veto power**: USA, UK, France, Russia, China), **International Court of Justice** (The Hague), **Economic and Social Council**, **Secretariat** (led by Secretary-General), and the now-suspended **Trusteeship Council**. Specialized agencies include **IMF, World Bank, WHO, UNESCO**, and the **WTO (1995)**.

#### The Cold War (1947–1991)
Triggered by the **Truman Doctrine (1947)** of containment and the **Marshall Plan (1948)** for European recovery. Bipolarity split the globe into **NATO (1949)** and the **Warsaw Pact (1955)**. Proxy conflicts: **Korean War (1950–53)**, **Cuban Missile Crisis (1962)**, **Vietnam War (1955–75)**, **Soviet–Afghan War (1979–89)**. The **1973 OPEC oil crisis** weaponised energy markets. The Cold War ended with the **Fall of the Berlin Wall (9 November 1989)** and the **Dissolution of the USSR (26 December 1991)**.

#### Decolonization and 21st-Century Events
After 1945, dozens of states in Asia (India 1947, Indonesia 1949) and Africa (1960 "Year of Africa") gained independence, coalescing into the **Non-Aligned Movement (1961)** led by Nehru, Nasser and Tito. In the 21st century: **9/11 (11 September 2001)** triggered the **War on Terror** (Afghanistan 2001, Iraq 2003); the **2008 Global Financial Crisis** began with the US subprime collapse; the **Arab Spring (2010–2012)** toppled regimes in Tunisia, Egypt, Libya; and **Brexit** — the UK's withdrawal from the EU following the 23 June 2016 referendum — was formalised on 31 January 2020.

#### Exam Pattern Snapshot
LAT GK carries 3% weight. Expect one or two questions in MCQ/match-the-following format. High-frequency traps: swapping the Truman Doctrine with the Marshall Plan; placing the Berlin Wall fall (1989) and USSR dissolution (1991) in the same year; and confusing the League of Nations with the UN.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edges, Adjacencies and Common Traps
Several events appear in clusters examiners exploit. The **League of Nations (1920)** is *not* the UN's predecessor in a legal sense — the UN was newly chartered in 1945, though it inherited the League's mandate system. The **Truman Doctrine** was a *political* pledge to contain communism (Greece, Turkey, 1947); the **Marshall Plan** was the *economic* instrument (≈$13 billion in US aid to Western Europe, 1948–52). A persistent error is calling both "containment" interchangeably.

Decolonization is best studied in three waves: 1945–55 (South Asia, Indonesia, Israel); 1960–68 (the **Year of Africa — 1960** — produced 17 new states); and 1974–80 (Portuguese colonies, Zimbabwe). Link this to the **Bandung Conference (1955)** and the formation of **NAM (Belgrade, 1961)** to anchor the Third-World solidarity narrative LAT questions often test.

Post-1991, the unipolar moment produced **NATO expansion (1999, 2004)**, the **Bosnian War**, **Kosovo (1999)**, and the **War on Terror**. Frame 9/11 not merely as an attack but as the trigger for the **USA PATRIOT Act (October 2001)**, the creation of the **Department of Homeland Security (2002)**, and the **Guantanamo detentions** — all relevant to law-admission testing of civil liberties. The 2008 crisis is best memorised via its domino chain: **subprime mortgages → Lehman Brothers collapse (15 Sept 2008) → global credit freeze → G20 summits → Dodd-Frank Act (2010)**.

#### Connections to Other LAT Topics
Major World Events overlaps directly with **International Organizations** (UN organs, veto mechanics, WTO dispute settlement), **Indian Polity** (NAM legacy, Panchsheel, 1971 Bangladesh Liberation War), **Current Affairs** (Russia–Ukraine war, Israel–Hamas, US–China trade), and **Legal Reasoning** (customary international law, ICC jurisdiction post-1998 Rome Statute). Treat the topic as a chronology scaffold; attach institutions and treaties to each phase.

#### Common Mistakes to Eliminate
- **1914 vs. 1939** — WWI began 1914; WWII began 1939.
- **1945** — UN Charter signed 26 June 1945; UN formally established 24 October 1945.
- **1989 vs. 1991** — Berlin Wall fell in 1989; USSR dissolved on 26 December 1991.
- **Veto powers** — the P5 are USA, Russia, China, UK, France; **not** Germany, Japan, India.
- **Brexit referendum** — vote held 23 June 2016; UK formally left the EU on 31 January 2020.

#### Practice Prompts
1. Match the following treaties/conferences to their years: Treaty of Versailles, Yalta, Potsdam, UN Charter, Bandung. Justify why the Yalta and Potsdam decisions are considered the *seedbeds* of the Cold War.
2. The Truman Doctrine and the Marshall Plan are both described as instruments of US containment. Distinguish their mechanisms, geographic scope, and the year each was announced, and explain how a LAT question might frame them as distractors.
