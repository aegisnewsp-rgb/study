# Laws of Motion

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Newton's three laws form the backbone of mechanics. The **first law (law of inertia)** states that a body continues in its state of rest or uniform straight-line motion unless a **net external force** acts on it; this defines an **inertial reference frame**. The **second law** gives the quantitative link: **F_net = dp/dt = m·a** for constant mass, where **p = m·v** is linear momentum. The **third law** says every action has an equal and opposite reaction, but the pair always acts on **two different bodies**, so they never cancel. Two must-know results for JEE Main: (1) impulse **J = F_avg·Δt = Δp** — used in collision and jerk problems; (2) limiting friction **f_s,max = μ_s·N** and kinetic friction **f_k = μ_k·N**, where N is the normal reaction. Always start a problem by drawing a free body diagram (FBD) listing weight mg, normal N, tension T, friction f and applied force — this single habit prevents 80% of sign errors in JEE Main mechanics.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Newton's Three Laws — Precise Statements

**Law 1 (Galilean inertia):** In an inertial frame, a particle with zero net force keeps its velocity constant. Inertia is the property of mass that resists change in motion.

**Law 2:** The rate of change of linear momentum of a body equals the **net external force** applied to it, in the direction of the force. For constant mass, **F_net = m·a**. For variable mass (rockets, chains), the full form **F_net = dp/dt** is mandatory.

**Law 3:** For every force that body A exerts on body B, body B exerts an equal, opposite and collinear force on A. The two forces act on different bodies, so they never appear in the same FBD and never cancel.

#### Building a Free Body Diagram
Identify the body, then list **only** the forces acting **on it**: weight mg (down), normal N (perpendicular to contact surface), tension T (along string, pulling the body), spring force **F_s = −k·x** (Hooke's law, restoring), friction (opposing relative motion or tendency of motion), and any applied push/pull. Choose axes (usually along and perpendicular to the surface or acceleration) and resolve each force.

#### Static vs Kinetic Friction
Static friction is **self-adjusting**: it equals the applied tangential force up to a maximum **f_s,max = μ_s·N**. Beyond this, motion begins and friction drops to the (roughly constant) kinetic value **f_k = μ_k·N**, with μ_k < μ_s typically. The **angle of repose** θ satisfies **tan θ = μ_s** — the steepest incline on which a block does not slide.

#### Constrained Systems and Atwood Machine
For two masses m₁ > m₂ connected by a light inextensible string over a frictionless pulley:
- Acceleration: **a = (m₁ − m₂)·g / (m₁ + m₂)**
- Tension: **T = 2·m₁·m₂·g / (m₁ + m₂)**

Constraint relations come from inextensibility: if one body moves, the string enforces a fixed length, linking accelerations and velocities of the two masses (e.g., for a pulley with movable support, accelerations of connected bodies are related by 2a₁ + a₂ = 0 in the standard arrangement).

#### Pseudo Forces
In a **non-inertial frame** accelerating with **a₀** relative to an inertial frame, every body of mass m experiences a fictitious force **F_pseudo = −m·a₀** in addition to real forces. Example: a passenger in an accelerating car feels pushed backward by **m·a₀** opposite to the car's acceleration.

#### Typical JEE Main Patterns
- Single-block incline with friction: find range of applied force for equilibrium.
- Two-block system pulled horizontally: find acceleration and contact force using **F = ma** on each block separately.
- Lift problems: apparent weight **W_app = m(g + a)** when lift accelerates upward with magnitude a, **m(g − a)** when downward; **0** during free fall.
- Spring-mass pull: maximum extension when impulse is delivered, using **J = Δp** and energy/force balance.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Subtleties

**1. Variable mass and the second law.** For a body whose mass changes (a rocket ejecting fuel, a chain piling up, a raindrop collecting moisture), **F = m·a** is wrong. The correct form is **F_ext + v_rel·(dm/dt) = m·(dv/dt)**, where **v_rel** is the velocity of the ejected/accreted mass relative to the body. The classic JEE Main trap is a falling chain where part of it has already piled up — students forget that the falling part decelerates as it transfers momentum to the pile.

**2. Pseudo-force sign and frame choice.** The pseudo force **−m·a₀** points **opposite** to the acceleration of the reference frame. Switching frames can simplify two-body problems: place yourself in the accelerating frame of one body, and the constraint becomes static. A frequent mistake is adding a pseudo force in an inertial frame (ground) — it is not a real force and must not be drawn there.

**3. Friction is not always μN.** Static friction is whatever is needed to prevent slipping, up to μ_s·N. On a horizontal surface with no applied force, f_s = 0, not μ_s·N. Kinetic friction is independent of contact area and (ideally) of relative speed — many JEE Main numericals hinge on this independence.

**4. Newton's third law pairs and tension.** A string transmits equal tension only if it is **massless and frictionless** and the pulley is frictionless. The tension a string exerts on body A and the tension it exerts on body B are a third-law pair and are equal in magnitude; the body A feels a pull toward B, not away from B.

**5. Connection to Work, Energy and Rotation (Class 11 → advanced).** The constraint **a_cm = F_net / m_total** for a system of particles links directly to the work-energy theorem **W_net = ΔKE_cm + ΔKE_internal**, the foundation for collision problems. Torque and angular momentum are the rotational analogues of F and p, set up in the same chapter.

#### Common Mistakes to Avoid
- Drawing action–reaction forces on the **same** body in the FBD.
- Writing **F = ma** when mass varies (use **F = dp/dt**).
- Resolving tension incorrectly on an inclined pulley problem — tension is along the string, not horizontal/vertical.
- Sign errors in lift problems: use upward as positive and keep a signed (a can be negative when the lift decelerates while moving up).
- Assuming kinetic friction equals the applied force; it is determined by μ_k·N, not by the push.

#### Practice Prompts
1. A 4 kg block on a 30° incline (μ_s = 0.5, μ_k = 0.4) is pushed by a horizontal force F. Find the range of F for which the block remains at rest.
2. Two blocks (2 kg on top of 6 kg) rest on a smooth floor. A horizontal force 16 N is applied to the lower block. If μ between the blocks is 0.4, find the frictional force on the upper block and the system's acceleration.
