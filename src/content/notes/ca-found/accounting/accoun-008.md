# Issue of Shares

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Issue of Shares** is the procedure through which a company raises share capital by allotting new equity or preference shares to the public, existing members, or private parties. The process is governed by Sections 23, 39, 42, 52, 53, 54 and 62 of the Companies Act, 2013, and for listed entities, by the SEBI (ICDR) Regulations, 2018. CA Foundation questions on this topic usually test journal entries under three issue modes (par, premium, discount), forfeiture–reissue accounting, and pro-rata allotment with calls in arrears.

#### Key Terms at a Glance

| Term | Meaning (in ₹ unless stated) |
|---|---|
| Face Value (FV) | Nominal value printed on the share — capitalised to Share Capital A/c |
| Issue Price | FV + Premium (or − Discount where the Act permits) |
| Securities Premium | Excess of issue price over FV — credited to Securities Premium Reserve |
| Calls in Arrears | Call money not received by the due date |
| Calls in Advance | Money received before it is called — interest @ ≤ 12% p.a. |
| Pro-rata Allotment | Fewer shares allotted than applied; excess money adjusted against allotment/calls |
| Forfeited Shares | Shares cancelled for non-payment of call money |
| Capital Reserve | Surplus on forfeiture transferred after reissue — part of reserves, not share capital |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"PPA-FRA"** — **P**ar, **P**remium, **A**dvance/Arrears, **F**orfeiture, **R**eissue, **A**djustment. Run these six checks before writing any journal entry on this topic.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Modes of Issue and the Underlying Entry

A share can be issued at par, at a premium, or (only in restricted cases) at a discount. The journal logic stays consistent: Bank A/c is debited with the total amount received; Share Capital A/c is credited with face value; Securities Premium A/c is credited when issue price exceeds face value. Section 53 of the Companies Act, 2013 expressly forbids public companies from issuing shares at a discount — sweat equity shares under Sec. 54 are the only allowed exception, capped at 25% of paid-up capital or ₹5 crore, whichever is lower.

| Mode | When Permitted | Bank A/c Dr. (₹) | To Share Capital (₹) | To Securities Premium (₹) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| At Par | Always | Issue price × shares | FV × shares | — |
| At Premium | Always | Issue price × shares | FV × shares | Premium × shares |
| At Discount | Sweat equity (Sec. 54); private co. in limited cases | Issue price × shares + discount | FV × shares | — (discount written off to P&L) |

#### Calls in Arrears, Calls in Advance and Interest

Arrears arise when a shareholder fails to pay a call on the due date; the unpaid amount is debited to Calls in Arrears A/c and credited to the relevant Call A/c. Advance money received before a call is credited to Calls in Advance A/c and later adjusted. Interest on arrears is computed at the rate prescribed in the Articles (commonly 10% p.a.); interest on advance is allowed up to **12% p.a.** under Sec. 50 of the Act. The formula for interest on arrears is:

> **Interest on Arrears = Arrears Amount × Rate × (Days Defaulted / 365)** (₹)

#### Pro-rata Allotment — Adjustment Sequence

When applications received exceed shares allotted, surplus application money is *adjusted*, not refunded. The correct order is: **(i) calls already in arrears on application → (ii) allotment money due → (iii) calls in advance, if any, returned**. Wrong sequencing is one of the most heavily-tested traps in CA Foundation paper 1.

#### Forfeiture and Reissue — The Core Logic

Forfeiture reverses only the share-capital and unpaid-call elements. Premium already received is **not refunded** — it remains with the company. On reissue, any discount allowed is debited to the Share Forfeiture A/c itself, but the discount per share cannot exceed the forfeited amount per share. The balance left in Share Forfeiture A/c is transferred to **Capital Reserve**, which is a capital profit and forms part of reserves & surplus on the liability side of the balance sheet.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix

| Concept | Forfeiture of Shares | Surrender of Shares | Buy-back under Sec. 68 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Initiated by | Company (for non-payment) | Shareholder (voluntary) | Board resolution |
| Legal basis | Articles of Association | Articles permitting surrender | Companies Act, 2013 |
| Treatment | Share Forfeiture A/c → Capital Reserve on reissue | Treated as forfeiture if not reissued within 1 month | Reduction of share capital + transfer to Capital Redemption Reserve |
| Refund of premium | Not permitted | Not permitted | Not applicable (separate transaction) |

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

#### Question:
A company issued 50,000 equity shares of ₹10 each at a premium of ₹2 per share, payable as: Application ₹4 (incl. ₹1 premium), Allotment ₹8 (incl. ₹1 premium). Applications were received for 70,000 shares and pro-rata allotment was made. A shareholder holding 1,000 allotted shares failed to pay the allotment money. Pass journal entries for issue, pro-rata adjustment, calls in arrears, forfeiture (assuming the Articles permit forfeiture for non-payment), and reissue of all forfeited shares at ₹8 per share.

#### Solution:

#### 1. Receipt of application money (bank basis):
Bank A/c Dr. ₹3,50,000 (70,000 × ₹5)
 To Share Application A/c ₹3,50,000

#### 2. Application transfer to Share Capital & Securities Premium:
Share Application A/c Dr. ₹3,50,000
 To Share Capital A/c ₹2,00,000 (50,000 × ₹4)
 To Securities Premium A/c ₹50,000 (50,000 × ₹1)
 To Share Allotment A/c ₹1,00,000 (excess adjusted, 20,000 × ₹5)

#### 3. Allotment due (₹8 × 50,000 = ₹4,00,000):
Share Allotment A/c Dr. ₹4,00,000
 To Share Capital A/c ₹3,50,000
 To Securities Premium A/c ₹50,000

#### 4. Calls in arrears on allotment — 1,000 shares × ₹3 (₹8 due − ₹5 already adjusted on application):
Calls in Arrears A/c Dr. ₹3,000
 To Share Allotment A/c ₹3,000

#### 5. Forfeiture (assume full called-up = ₹12 i.e. ₹10 + ₹2 premium received):
Share Capital A/c Dr. ₹10,000 (1,000 × ₹10)
Securities Premium A/c Dr. ₹1,000 (1,000 × ₹1, already received)
 To Calls in Arrears A/c ₹3,000
 To Share Forfeiture A/c ₹8,000 (₹9 received − ₹1 premium = ₹8 forfeited per share)

#### 6. Reissue at ₹8 per share (₹2 discount per share):
Bank A/c Dr. ₹8,000
Share Forfeiture A/c Dr. ₹2,000 (1,000 × ₹2)
 To Share Capital A/c ₹10,000

#### 7. Transfer to Capital Reserve:
Share Forfeiture A/c Dr. ₹6,000
 To Capital Reserve A/c ₹6,000 (₹8 forfeited − ₹2 discount = ₹6 × 1,000 shares)

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students often credit ₹9 per share (full amount received) to Share Forfeiture A/c instead of ₹8, forgetting that ₹1 is premium already capitalised. Including premium inflates the forfeited balance, the reissue discount, and finally the Capital Reserve — and the entry will not balance. Always **exclude securities premium** from the forfeiture credit.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Statutory Limits

| Provision | Statutory Limit | Practical Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Sweat Equity (Sec. 54) | ≤ 25% of paid-up capital or ₹5 crore, whichever is lower | Issued at discount to directors/employees, fair value determined by registered valuer |
| Private Placement (Sec. 42) | ≤ 200 persons per issue, per financial year | Offer letter PAS-4 mandatory; no public advertisement |
| Rights Issue (Sec. 62) | Pro-rata to existing members within 15 days of board resolution | Fully renounceable; Letter of Offer in Form PAS-3 |
| Buy-back (Sec. 68) | ≤ 25% of paid-up capital & free reserves | Funded from free reserves or securities premium; CRR created |
| Issue at Discount (Sec. 53) | Prohibited for public companies | Sweat equity only — debited to "Discount on Issue of Shares" |

#### Forfeiture Premium Treatment — The Subtle Distinction

If premium is received on shares that are subsequently forfeited, that premium is **not refunded** and not part of the forfeited amount. The journal must therefore debit Securities Premium A/c with the premium already credited at the time of original issue. Where premium was *not* received (issue at par), the forfeiture entry is simpler: Share Capital A/c Dr. to Calls in Arrears A/c, and the balance to Share Forfeiture A/c.

#### Advanced Traps and Exceptions

1. **Refund vs Adjustment** — Refunding surplus application money when pro-rata is possible is a recurring error; adjust it instead.
2. **Minimum Subscription** — 90% subscription is mandatory under Sec. 39; failing this, the issue must be refunded within 130 days from the issue date.
3. **Issue Expenses** — Post-2013, preliminary and issue expenses must be charged to the Statement of Profit and Loss; they cannot be written off against Securities Premium Reserve.
4. **Reissue Discount Cap** — Discount allowed on reissue cannot exceed the forfeited amount per share; any excess is borne by the shareholder personally and is not recorded in the books.
5. **Capital Reserve Calculation** — Computed on the *original number of shares forfeited*, not on the number actually reissued, when full forfeiture applies.
6. **Calls in Advance on Forfeited Shares** — If a shareholder has paid in advance and the shares are later forfeited, the advance must be refunded (it is not the company's money to retain).
7. **Surrender vs Forfeiture** — Surrender is only lawful if the Articles permit it AND the shares are reissued within one month; otherwise it is treated as forfeiture.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. **Composite forfeiture:** A company forfeited 2,000 shares (₹10 called-up, ₹8 paid including ₹2 premium) for non-payment of allotment. Of these, 1,500 were reissued at ₹7 per share as fully paid, and the balance 500 were reissued at ₹9 per share, ₹8 called-up. Calculate the final Capital Reserve and prepare all journal entries.
2. **Pro-rata with multiple calls:** XYZ Ltd. issued 1,00,000 shares at ₹12 (incl. ₹2 premium), payable Application ₹5, Allotment ₹4, First & Final Call ₹3. Applications for 1,40,000 shares were received pro-rata. A shareholder allotted 500 shares paid only application and allotment. Pass entries up to forfeiture and compute the Capital Reserve if all shares are reissued at par as fully paid.

> 💡 **High-Yield Revision Hook:** Before any entry, ask four questions — *Is premium involved? Is it pro-rata? Are calls in arrears present? Is forfeiture/reissue in the picture?* Answering these decides whether Securities Premium, Calls in Arrears, and Capital Reserve each appear in your working.
