# Pakistan: Economic Issues

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

> Pakistan's economy runs on four deficits at once — fiscal, current account, energy and savings. Memorise that and half the paper is already half-won.

Pakistan's macroeconomic problem set is unusually concentrated: chronic **fiscal deficit** (government spends more than it earns), **current account deficit** (country imports more than it exports), **circular debt** in the power sector, and persistent **inflation** pressure that devalues the rupee.

| Deficit | What it measures | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| Fiscal | Total expenditure − Total revenue | Rs. crore |
| Current account | Trade + Services + Primary income deficit (net remittances offset) | USD million |
| Circular (energy) | DISCOs/PEPCO payables to generators, fuel suppliers, SBP/PSO | Rs. crore |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook (DEFC-R):** **D**eficits, **E**xternal debt, **F**iscal drag, **C**ircular debt, **R**emittances — the five pillars every LAT passage and essay leans on. Tick all five in your answer and you cover the diagnostic checklist examiners reward.

GDP at market prices (expenditure side) is **C + I + G + (X − M)**, expressed in Rs. crore. Inflation via CPI = {(Current price index − Base price index) ÷ Base price index} × 100, given as a percentage. The **State Bank of Pakistan (SBP)** sets the policy rate; the **Pakistan Bureau of Statistics (PBS)** publishes CPI. For LAT, do not confuse SBP policy rate with realised CPI inflation — examiners swap them in MCQs.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### What the four deficits actually mean

**Fiscal deficit** is the gap between the federal + provincial governments' aggregate expenditure and aggregate revenue (taxes plus non-tax receipts). When this gap is financed by borrowing from SBP or commercial banks, money supply expands and CPI pressure rises.

**Current account deficit** is the BoP sub-account covering goods, services, primary income (interest, dividends) and secondary income (remittances, foreign aid). A persistent deficit drains foreign exchange reserves held at SBP.

**Circular debt** is uniquely Pakistani: power distribution companies (DISCOs) recover less than the cost-of-service billed, then default on payments to generation companies (GENCOs), fuel suppliers (PSO), and SBP — the arrears compound.

**Inflation**, measured through CPI by PBS, ran in double digits through most of the 2020s; the **Policy rate** is SBP's defensive tool against it.

#### Comparative map of easily-confused concepts

| Term | Looks like… | But actually… | Authority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fiscal deficit | Current account deficit | Government revenue vs spending, not trade | Ministry of Finance |
| Current account deficit | Fiscal deficit | Trade + services + income with RoW | SBP BoP statistics |
| External debt | Total public debt | Only foreign-currency liabilities | SBP / Economic Survey |
| Depreciation | Devaluation | Market-driven PKR fall under managed float | SBP |
| Policy rate | Inflation rate | SBP-set benchmark; CPI is realised change | SBP vs PBS |
| Remittances | Exports | Worker transfers, the BoP's largest FX line | SBP |

#### Worked exam-style passage

> *"Pakistan's external debt rose to USD 130 billion in 2024, with debt servicing consuming nearly half of federal revenue. The State Bank of Pakistan, holding reserves of USD 8 billion at one point, raised the policy rate to 22% to anchor the rupee."*

**Question.** Identify the transmission chain from fiscal deficit to rupee depreciation and explain why the SBP's policy-rate response carries a trade-off.

**Resolution.** Government runs a fiscal deficit → it borrows domestically and externally → domestic borrowing crowds out private credit while external borrowing raises the stock of foreign-currency liabilities → debt servicing in USD tightens BoP → demand for USD outstrips supply of FX → rupee depreciates. SBP raises the **policy rate** to slow credit, cool aggregate demand and arrest the FX drain. The trade-off: higher rates also choke private investment and growth, raising the risk of **stagflation** — slow growth combined with persistent inflation. Students frequently forget that the same rate hike that defends the rupee also deepens the fiscal deficit by raising the government's domestic debt-servicing bill.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** A common option reads "depreciation = devaluation". Reject it — under Pakistan's managed float, the PKR moves on market pressure, not a one-shot official re-pegging.

#### Mechanisms the examiner loves

1. **Tax-to-GDP ratio** stays structurally under 11–13%, capping revenue mobilisation. Without broadening the base, every expenditure commitment widens the deficit.
2. **Energy circular debt** cycles back into the fiscal accounts via subsidies, IPP capacity payments and sovereign guarantees.
3. **Remittances**, recorded officially at around USD 27–30 billion in recent years through formal channels, now dwarf services exports and partially offset the trade deficit — yet they remain exposed to host-country visa policy and to the informal **hundi/hawala** channel that under-counts actual inflows.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

#### Edges, boundary cases, and why they bite

**Real vs nominal exchange rate.** Most LAT MCQs offer "PKR/USD = 280" as the nominal rate; the trickier item tests whether you can compute the real exchange rate = nominal rate × (foreign price level ÷ domestic price level). If domestic inflation outpaces foreign inflation, the real exchange rate appreciates even when the nominal rate is constant — eroding export competitiveness.

**Money multiplier.** A single-line definition: money multiplier = 1 ÷ reserve requirement ratio (dimensionless). The catch is its counter-intuitive implication — every rupee of SBP borrowing by the government can become multiple rupees of broad money if reserve requirements are loose.

**Privatisation politics.** The **Privatisation Commission** sells SOEs to retire debt; the **SECP** regulates capital markets post-privatisation. Questions on this sub-topic are usually framed as "Privatisation is a fiscal-deficit solution" — accurate in mechanism, but only if proceeds exceed transaction costs and contingent liabilities.

**Regional trade.** CPEC-related infrastructure arrives as Chinese FDI plus concessional debt. CPEC inflows support BoP temporarily but extend the external-debt maturity profile.

#### Higher-order traps table

| Trap | Why it's wrong | Right framing |
|---|---|---|
| Stating poverty at 21.9% | Pre-2018 line; outdated by post-2022 inflation shock | Use the latest PBS/World Bank revised headcount |
| Equating SBP reserves with gross official reserves | SBP net reserves exclude commercial bank FX | Distinguish gross, net, and reserves cover in months of imports |
| Treating stagflation as only inflation | Requires simultaneous low/negative growth | Cite both growth and CPI components |
| Saying "IMF gives grants" | EFF is a loan facility, repayable in SDR | Identify IMF programmes by their loan instrument (EFF, SBA, RFI) |
| Naming sin/tobacco tax as the revenue fix | Narrow-base, health-policy instrument | Broader VAT base + provincial harmonisation |

#### Two advanced practice prompts

1. **Passage analysis (120–150 words):** A 2024–2026 era editorial excerpt states that remittances "saved Pakistan's BoP". Sketch (i) what counter-factual would have looked like without remittances, and (ii) which three policy levers could substitute partially for them over a five-year horizon. Cover export diversification, services exports (IT, freelancing), and FDI in Special Economic Zones.

2. **Essay outline (350–500 words):** "Pakistan's economic crisis is structural, not conjunctural." Construct a four-paragraph argument: (i) **Diagnosis** — the four deficits and circular debt quantified; (ii) **Structural causes** — narrow tax base, energy tariff differential, export concentration, governance costs; (iii) **Consequences** — debt sustainability, stagflation risk, brain drain; (iv) **Remedies** — FBR reform, IPP renegotiation, export competitiveness, privatisation, regional trade integration.

> 📌 **Formula Check (units matter):** GDP = C + I + G + (X − M), Rs. crore. Inflation = ΔIndex × 100, %. Debt-to-GDP = (External + domestic debt) ÷ GDP × 100, %. Money multiplier = 1 ÷ r, dimensionless. Always quote SBP figures in USD million and fiscal figures in Rs. crore — examiners deduct for unit confusion.
