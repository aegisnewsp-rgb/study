# Puzzles

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Puzzles** in HAT-UG Analytical Reasoning are constrained-arrangement problems: given a list of entities and a set of clues, you deduce the only arrangement satisfying every clue. Formats include **linear seating**, **circular seating**, **scheduling**, **comparison** (height/age/marks), and **grouping-with-conditions**.

- **Skeleton first, placements second.** Read every clue before putting anyone in position.
- **Two clue types only:** *fixed* ("A sits at the extreme left") and *negative/conditional* ("B is not between C and D").
- **Circular rule:** fix one person against clockwise direction; never assume orientation.
- **Verify, then submit.** Check the final arrangement against every clue — a single missed "not" can flip the answer.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Puzzle families you will meet

HAT-UG tests five recurring formats. Each one yields to the same workflow: read all clues → build a skeleton → place the strongest facts → resolve conditionals → verify.

| Format | Skeleton | Key clue pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Linear seating | Straight line, N slots | "A sits 3rd from the left" |
| Circular seating | Circle, N positions | "B sits immediately clockwise to C" |
| Scheduling | Table of days/times | "X teaches on Monday, not Wednesday" |
| Comparison | Ordered line by attribute | "Ali is taller than Bilal but shorter than Cyrus" |
| Grouping | Sets/teams | "Two of P, Q, R go to Group 1" |

#### The clue-typing step

Every clue collapses into one of three buckets:

- **Fixed fact** — pins an entity to a definite slot. Place it immediately.
- **Negative fact** — rules out a slot. Mark it with an **×** in your grid.
- **Conditional** — an *if-then*, *either-or*, or *neither-nor*. These need temporary case analysis: assume one branch, see if a contradiction appears, then keep or discard.

#### Worked micro-example

Six friends A, B, C, D, E, F sit in a row. Clues: (1) A is at the left end. (2) B sits immediately right of A. (3) F is not adjacent to A. (4) D sits somewhere between B and E. From (1)+(2) we lock **A–B** at positions 1–2. (3) eliminates positions 2 and 6 for F, so F ∈ {3, 4, 5}. (4) leaves one consistent chain, which uniquely fixes E at position 6 and F at position 4. Verify (3): F is two seats from A — not adjacent — ✓.

- Linear puzzles: place the **2 anchor clues first**; negatives second; conditionals last.
- Circular puzzles: pin one person against clockwise, then expand outward.
- Grouping puzzles: track each entity with a yes/no tally against each group.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Deep mechanics of deduction

A **constraint** is anything that restricts placement. **Deduction** means converting each clue into allowed positions, then *chaining*: a fact placed in slot *k* immediately restricts slots *k–1* and *k+1*. **Case analysis** is used only when a conditional clue forces two possible worlds; label them **Case 1** and **Case 2** on a scratch copy and eliminate the case that contradicts any later clue.

#### Adjacent-topic links

Puzzles share infrastructure with **Blood Relations**, **Syllogisms** (Venn reasoning), and **Coding-Decoding** (symbol substitution). Specifically, the *negative-elimination* logic you use here is identical to the contrapositive step in syllogisms. **Directional reasoning** (left/right, clockwise/anticlockwise) reappears in clock-angle problems and direction-distance puzzles.

#### Common traps and examiner moves

| Trap | Why it hurts | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Misreading "to the left of" as immediate neighbour | Skips a slot | Mark definite negatives on the line |
| Treating "either-or" as exclusive when it is inclusive | Drops a valid case | Read the verb tense carefully |
| Stopping after first consistent placement | Leaves contradictions | Run a full verification pass |
| Forgetting circular symmetry | Double-counts rotations | Fix one reference person first |

#### Practice prompts

1. **Linear scheduling.** Seven seminars run Monday → Sunday, one per day. Clue: Seminar X is two days after Y; Seminar Z is not on Wednesday; only one seminar sits between W and V. Find the day of X.
2. **Circular grouping.** Eight doctors sit around a table. Clue: Dr. Ali faces Dr. Bilal; Dr. Cyrus is 3rd to the left of Dr. Danial; Dr. Ehsan sits between Dr. Faiq and Dr. Ghani. Identify the person sitting 2nd to the right of Dr. Ali.

Target **≤ 90 seconds** per puzzle after your grid is built — the speed comes from setup discipline, not from re-reading the passage.
