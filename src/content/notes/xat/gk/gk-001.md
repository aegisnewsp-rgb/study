# Indian Economy and Planning

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your XAT.

The Indian economy operates as a **mixed economy** with the private sector dominating output and the public sector steering welfare and infrastructure. Planning shifted from the **Planning Commission** (which ran 12 Five-Year Plans from 1951 to 2014) to **NITI Aayog** on 1 January 2015, an advisory body without fund-allocation powers. National income is measured at three levels: **GDP** (domestic), **GNP** (national, includes Net Factor Income from Abroad), and **NNP** (national income after depreciation).

- **GDP formula:** GDP = C + I + G + (X − M), where C=Consumption, I=Investment, G=Government spending, X=Exports, M=Imports.
- **GNP = GDP + NFWA** (Net Factor Income from Abroad, in ₹).
- **Fiscal Deficit = Total Expenditure − Total Receipts (ex-borrowings)**, targeted at 3% of GDP under the **FRBM Act, 2003**.
- **Money Multiplier = 1 / CRR**, so a 4% CRR gives a multiplier of 25.
- Key 1991 reforms: **LPG** (Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation) following the BoP crisis.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Macroeconomic Aggregates

India's central statistical office (now NSO under MoSPI) publishes national accounts using **2011-12 as the base year** since January 2015. Real GDP strips out price effects using the **GDP deflator**, while nominal GDP reflects current prices. The gap matters because India's nominal growth often exceeds real growth by 4-5 percentage points on inflation differentials.

| Aggregate | Formula | What it captures |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GDP at MP | C + I + G + (X − M) | Value of all final goods produced **within** India |
| NDP | GDP − Depreciation | Net domestic output after asset wear |
| GNP | GDP + NFWA | Output by **Indian residents** anywhere in the world |
| NNP (National Income) | GNP − Depreciation | True income available to the nation |

Per capita income = National Income / Mid-year Population. India crossed the **USD 3 trillion GDP mark in 2024**, but per capita income remains below USD 2,500.

#### Planning and Policy Architecture

The **Planning Commission**, chaired by the Prime Minister, drafted 12 Five-Year Plans modelled on the Soviet template. The last plan (12th, 2012-17) was never completed because NITI Aayog replaced the body on 1 January 2015. NITI Aayog functions as a policy **think-tank**, advising states through competitive federalism frameworks like the NITI States Health Index.

#### Fiscal and Monetary Policy

| Deficit type | Definition | Typical target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fiscal Deficit | Total Expenditure − Non-debt Receipts | 3% of GDP (FRBM) |
| Revenue Deficit | Revenue Expenditure − Revenue Receipts | Below 2% |
| Primary Deficit | Fiscal Deficit − Interest Payments | Indicates new borrowing burden |

The **Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)**, constituted in 2016 under the RBI Act amendment, decides the **repo rate** by majority vote. Currently four members are from RBI and three are external economists appointed by the central government.

- Five-Year Plans ran from **1951 to 2014** (12 plans).
- NITI Aayog replaced Planning Commission on **1 January 2015**.
- GST launched on **1 July 2017** with CGST, SGST, IGST architecture.
- **IBC 2016** created the NCLT/NCLAT framework for insolvency resolution.
- India's base year for national accounts: **2011-12**.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### External Sector Mechanics

The **Balance of Payments (BoP)** records all economic transactions with the rest of the world in two main accounts. The **Current Account** tracks goods, services, and transfers; a deficit here is the **CAD** (Current Account Deficit), typically financed by the **Capital Account**, which records FDI, FPI, and external borrowings. India's CAD stayed below 2.5% of GDP for most of the 2010s but widened during commodity-price spikes. **FDI** brings strategic control (10%+ equity, factory setup) while **FPI** is portfolio investment in stocks and bonds — FPI exits trigger sharp rupee depreciation, as seen in 2013 and 2018.

#### Sectoral Composition and Reforms

Post-1991 LPG reforms dismantled the **License Raj**, reduced import tariffs from peak rates above 300% to weighted averages near 10%, and opened sectors like telecom and aviation to private capital. Agriculture's share of GVA fell from roughly 55% at Independence to about **17-18% by 2023**, while services crossed **50% of GVA**. The **MSME classification** was revised in 2020 to composite investment-plus-turnover criteria: micro (investment ≤ ₹1 cr, turnover ≤ ₹5 cr), small (≤ ₹10 cr, ≤ ₹50 cr), medium (≤ ₹50 cr, ≤ ₹250 cr).

#### Common Pitfalls and Practice Prompts

1. **Conceptual trap:** Confusing GDP with GNP. If NFWA is negative (more foreign income flowing out than in), GNP falls *below* GDP. Recall that during high-interest years, Indian assets abroad earn less than foreign assets held by NRIs, flipping the sign.
2. **Policy trap:** Quoting the old Planning Commission as active. Since 2015 only NITI Aayog exists, and it does **not** allocate plan funds to states.

> Exam tip: For XAT Decision-Making sets, expect a 2-3 paragraph passage quoting RBI bulletin data on repo rates, CAD, or fiscal glide paths. Pre-compute direction of effect, then read the question.

#### Practice Prompts

1. If CRR rises from 4% to 5%, what happens to the money multiplier and explain why RBI uses CRR.
2. State two differences between NITI Aayog and the disbanded Planning Commission and identify which body currently chairs the Inter-State Council.
