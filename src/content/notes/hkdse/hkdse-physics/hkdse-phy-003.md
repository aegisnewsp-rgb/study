# III. Wave Motion — HKDSE Physics Notes

This topic covers Topic III of the Compulsory Part of HKDSE Physics. It addresses the nature and properties of waves (transverse and longitudinal, wavelength, frequency, speed, amplitude, superposition); light (reflection, refraction, total internal reflection, lenses, the eye and optical instruments); and sound (production, speed, intensity, the decibel scale, the Doppler effect).

> Verify the live HKDSE Physics Assessment Framework on https://www.hkeaa.edu.hk/ before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Wave**: a disturbance that transfers energy without net transfer of matter.
- **Transverse wave**: oscillations perpendicular to the direction of propagation (e.g. light, waves on a string).
- **Longitudinal wave**: oscillations parallel to the direction of propagation (e.g. sound, P-waves in earthquakes).
- **Wave parameters**: wavelength λ (distance between adjacent crests), frequency f (oscillations per second), speed v = fλ, amplitude (maximum displacement from equilibrium).
- **Superposition**: when two waves meet, the resultant displacement is the algebraic sum of the individual displacements.
- **Reflection**: angle of incidence = angle of reflection. Image distance from mirror = object distance from mirror.
- **Refraction**: change in direction of a wave at the interface between two media. Snell's law: n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂.
- **Refractive index n = c/v** (speed of light in vacuum/speed in medium).
- **Total internal reflection**: when the angle of incidence exceeds the critical angle, light is totally reflected. Used in optical fibres.
- **Convex lens**: converging. Concave lens: diverging.
- **Lens equation**: 1/u + 1/v = 1/f. Magnification m = v/u.
- **Sound**: a longitudinal wave produced by vibrations. Speed ~340 m/s in air at room temperature.
- **Decibel scale**: a logarithmic scale for sound intensity. Each 10 dB increase corresponds to a 10× increase in intensity.
- **Doppler effect**: the apparent change in frequency when the source and observer move relative to each other.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing wavelength and amplitude — wavelength is the distance; amplitude is the maximum displacement.
- Treating the lens equation as the same for concave and convex lenses — sign conventions matter.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Wave properties

**Wave equation**: v = fλ, where v is wave speed, f is frequency, λ is wavelength.

For sound in air at 20°C, v ≈ 343 m/s. For light in vacuum, v = c ≈ 3 × 10⁸ m/s.

**Wavefront**: a surface of constant phase. For point sources, the wavefronts are spheres; for plane sources, they are planes.

**Superposition and interference**:

- Constructive interference: waves in phase, amplitude adds.
- Destructive interference: waves out of phase, amplitude cancels.

**Standing (stationary) waves**: produced by the superposition of two waves of the same frequency and amplitude moving in opposite directions. Nodes (zero amplitude) and antinodes (maximum amplitude) are formed. Examples: vibrating strings, organ pipes, microwaves in cavities.

#### Light and optics

**Reflection**: angle of incidence = angle of reflection. For a plane mirror, the image is virtual, the same distance behind the mirror as the object is in front, and laterally inverted.

**Refraction (Snell's law)**: n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂. The refracted ray bends toward the normal when entering a denser medium (smaller angle).

**Total internal reflection**: when the angle of incidence exceeds the critical angle θ_c (sin θ_c = n₂/n₁ where n₁ > n₂), light is completely reflected. Used in optical fibres, prisms (binoculars, periscopes), and total internal reflection fluorescence microscopy.

**Lenses**:

- Convex lens (converging): parallel rays converge to the focal point on the far side.
- Concave lens (diverging): parallel rays diverge as if from a focal point on the near side.

**Thin lens equation**: 1/u + 1/v = 1/f (with sign convention).

**Magnification**: m = v/u (positive for upright, negative for inverted).

**The eye**:

- Cornea, aqueous humour, iris, lens, vitreous humour, retina.
- Image forms on the retina; the lens changes shape to focus on near and far objects (accommodation).
- Defects: myopia (near-sighted, image forms in front of retina), hyperopia (far-sighted, image forms behind retina), astigmatism (uneven cornea/lens).

#### Sound

**Sound waves**: longitudinal pressure waves. Speed in air at 20°C ≈ 343 m/s; faster in solids and liquids.

**Intensity**: power per unit area. The threshold of hearing is I₀ ≈ 10⁻¹² W/m².

**Decibel scale**: β = 10 log₁₀(I/I₀) dB. Each 10 dB increase corresponds to a 10× increase in intensity.

**Pitch**: frequency. Higher frequency = higher pitch.

**Loudness**: amplitude. Higher amplitude = louder.

**Doppler effect**: the apparent frequency of a wave changes when the source and observer move relative to each other. f' = f·(v ± v_o)/(v ± v_s). For a stationary observer and a source approaching at speed v_s: f' = f·v/(v - v_s). The shift is toward higher frequencies as the source approaches.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-topic links

- **VI. Astronomy and Space Science (Elective)**: the Doppler effect on light waves is used to measure stellar radial velocities.
- **VII. Atomic World (Elective)**: the wave-particle duality of light.
- **IX. Medical Physics (Elective)**: ultrasound imaging uses sound waves.

#### Exam technique

- **For wave equations**: state the relationship v = fλ, then identify the unknown.
- **For refraction questions**: apply Snell's law; determine the critical angle for TIR.
- **For lens questions**: apply the thin lens equation with sign convention.
- **For Doppler effect questions**: use the appropriate formula with the correct signs for the motion direction.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Higher frequency sound is louder." No — loudness is amplitude; pitch is frequency.
- "Light waves are longitudinal." Light is a transverse electromagnetic wave (with electric and magnetic fields perpendicular to the direction of propagation).
- "The Doppler effect only affects sound." The Doppler effect affects all waves, including light (used in astronomy and radar).
