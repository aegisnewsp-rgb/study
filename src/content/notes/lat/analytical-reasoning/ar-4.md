# Blood Relations

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your LAT paper.

Blood Relations questions on LAT ask you to decode verbal clues about family ties and answer "How is X related to Y?". The method is mechanical: read each statement, fix gender, then build a vertical family tree — eldest at the top, children on the next row, siblings on the same horizontal line.

- A + B → A is the parent of B
- A − B → A is the child of B
- A × B → A is married to B
- A = B → A and B are siblings

> Trap: "uncle" = father's brother, mother's brother, father's sister's husband, or mother's sister's husband. Only the first two are blood uncles.

| Relation | Vertical moves |
| --- | --- |
| Parent / Child | 1 |
| Grandparent | 2 |
| Uncle / Nephew | 1 + diagonal sibling |
| Cousin | 2 generations + sibling branch |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Decoding Notation

Each blood-relation clue on LAT uses one of four coded relations: parent (+), child (−), marriage (×), or sibling (=). When you see "A + B − C", read it left-to-right and stack vertically: A is above B, B is above C. The whole tree is built by chaining these symbols before any "how related?" question is attempted.

#### The Pointing-to-Photograph Trap

Statements of the form *"Pointing to a man, A said, 'He is the son of my wife's father.'"* introduce a third-person observer. The speaker (A) is fixed; the photographed person is fixed; the relationship flows from A's perspective. Misreading who reports the relation is the single most common error, because students assume the photographed person is speaking.

#### Gender Discipline

LAT deliberately uses names such as "Samar", "Kiran", "Sai" that are unisex in Urdu/English transliteration. Until a statement explicitly fixes gender (sister, mother, brother-in-law), leave gender blank. A brother-in-law from the husband's side and from the wife's side are both brother-in-laws, but only one is a blood relation of ego — the answer key often depends on this distinction.

| Code | Meaning | Generation effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| + B | A is parent of B | +1 down |
| − B | A is child of B | −1 up |
| × B | A married to B | same level |
| = B | A sibling of B | same level |

#### Worked Mini-Example

Statement: *P × Q, P + R, R − S, S = T (female).*
Draw P–Q on row 1, their child R on row 2, R's child S on row 3, sibling T beside S (also row 3). Question: *How is T related to Q?* Q is on row 1, T on row 3 — Q is T's grandmother.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Generation Counting and Diagonal Moves

Blood relations resolve into three move types on the tree: vertical (parent/child), horizontal (sibling/spouse), and diagonal (uncle/aunt/nephew/niece/cousin). Cousins sit two vertical steps plus one diagonal step from ego. Counting moves is faster than recalling names: any answer involving "great-" prefix means three or more vertical links.

#### Recursive Chains

When LAT stacks four or five statements, build the tree clause by clause rather than guessing the final link. Each statement introduces one new node or one new edge. Skipping ahead is the most common reason candidates answer with the correct relation but wrong direction ("X is Y's uncle" vs. "Y is X's nephew").

#### Common Pitfalls Catalogue

- Treating "only son" as emphasis; it actually forbids other sons and changes sibling-deduction paths.
- Counting "father's sister's husband" as a blood uncle — he is uncle by marriage only.
- Assuming shared surname implies relation — surname is irrelevant in standard LAT sets.
- Confusing "A is the son of B who is the daughter of C" — B is C's daughter, A is C's grandson, not C's son.

#### Practice Prompts

1. *A + B, A × C, C − D, D = E (male). How is E related to B?*
2. *Pointing to a woman, P said, "She is the daughter of my mother's only son." Who is the woman to P?*

| 1 vertical | Father / Son |
| 2 vertical | Grandfather / Grandson |

| 2 vert + 1 diag | Cousin |
