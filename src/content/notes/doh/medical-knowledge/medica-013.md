# Health Promotion and Patient Education

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your DOH (UAE) exam.

Health promotion enables individuals, families, and communities to act on the determinants of health, while **patient education** is the one-to-one teaching done in the consultation. Both fall under DOH clinical scope and must be documented.

- **Levels of prevention**: primary (vaccination, smoking cessation), secondary (screening for diabetes or breast cancer), tertiary (foot care in diabetic neuropathy, post-MI rehab).
- **Ottawa Charter (1986)**: build healthy public policy, create supportive environments, strengthen community action, develop personal skills, reorient health services.
- **Patient-education process**: assess needs → set objectives → plan content → deliver (verbal, written, demo) → evaluate with **teach-back**.

UAE priorities include tobacco cessation, diabetes/obesity prevention, mental-health awareness, maternal-child health, and vaccine uptake under Weqaya and FitforLife.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Defining the two pillars

Health **promotion** is broader than education: it addresses social determinants and environmental change, anchored in the **Ottawa Charter's five action areas**. Patient **education** is the clinician-led process of transferring knowledge and skills so a patient can self-manage. The DOH Clinical Governance standards require both to be evident in the record.

#### Levels of prevention applied

| Level | Goal | Clinical example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary | Prevent disease before it starts | HPV vaccine, smoking-cessation advice |
| Secondary | Detect disease early | HbA1c screening, mammography, FIT test |
| Tertiary | Reduce complications of established disease | Diabetic foot-care programme, post-MI cardiac rehab |

#### Behaviour-change counselling

The **Transtheoretical Model** (precontemplation → contemplation → preparation → action → maintenance) guides stage-matched advice, while **Motivational Interviewing** uses OARS: Open questions, Affirmations, Reflections, Summaries.

#### Exam pointers
- Match the prevention level to the scenario, not the disease severity label.
- Apply **Bloom's domains**: cognitive (knowledge), affective (attitudes), psychomotor (skills). Insulin injection technique is psychomotor, not cognitive.
- Choose culturally and language-appropriate material; teach-back confirms comprehension.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### The patient-education process in DOH practice

The DOH Patient Education standards treat education as a five-step **nursing/clinical process**: assess learning needs (literacy, language, readiness, beliefs) → write SMART objectives → plan method and materials → deliver using plain language, pictograms, and demonstration → evaluate with the **teach-back method** and document in the medical record. Skipping teach-back is a recurrent OSCE failure.

#### Domains of learning and common traps

| Domain | Aim | Example in DOH exam |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cognitive | Recall/understand | Explaining metformin mechanism |
| Affective | Attitudes/beliefs | Accepting insulin therapy |
| Psychomotor | Physical skill | Drawing up insulin, inhaler spacer use |

#### UAE-specific application

The Weqaya programme targets cardiovascular screening; **FitforLife** addresses obesity and physical activity. Smoking-cessation clinics, mental-health stigma campaigns, and HPV catch-up are active DOH/MOHAP priorities. Patient handouts must accommodate Arabic, English, Hindi, Urdu, Tagalog, and Malayalam speakers; a single English leaflet is rarely sufficient.

#### Common mistakes
1. Calling diabetic foot care "primary prevention" — it is tertiary.
2. Treating education as leaflet handover without teach-back.
3. Ignoring health literacy in multilingual populations.
4. Failing to record the session — DOH licensing requirement.

#### Practice prompts
1. A 52-year-old with newly diagnosed Type 2 diabetes. Write one SMART objective for each Bloom's domain.
2. A smoker in precontemplation. Outline two OARS-style questions and the next stage-matched intervention.
