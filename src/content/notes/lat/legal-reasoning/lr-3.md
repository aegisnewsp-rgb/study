# Legal Aptitude: Criminal Law

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Criminal law** is the branch of public law that defines offences, prescribes punishments, and regulates prosecution. For LAT in Pakistan, the two statutes that govern almost every question are the **Pakistan Penal Code, 1860 (PPC)** — which defines what counts as a crime — and the **Code of Criminal Procedure, 1898 (CrPC)** — which controls how the State investigates, arrests, tries, and punishes. Two Latin maxims anchor the subject: **actus reus** (the physical act or unlawful omission) and **mens rea** (the guilty mind). Most PPC offences require both. **General Exceptions (Sections 76–106 PPC)** — insanity, accident, necessity, consent, good faith, intoxication, and the **right of private defence (Sections 96–106)** — negate mens rea. **Group liability** runs through **Section 34** (common intention, needs a pre-arranged plan) and **Section 149** (common object of an unlawful assembly of five or more). LAT tests these through 1-mark scenario MCQs where you pick the correct exception or section — speed on section numbers wins marks.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Actus Reus and Mens Rea
Every punishable offence under the PPC ordinarily requires a **voluntary act or unlawful omission** (actus reus) coupled with a **guilty mental state** (mens rea) — intention, knowledge, or recklessness as defined in **Section 33 PPC**. A person who performs the physical act without the requisite mind is not guilty of that offence, although the same conduct may still attract a different, **strict liability** provision (e.g. public nuisance, certain motor-vehicle offences). The **mistake of fact (Section 76)** and **accident (Section 80)** exceptions operate on this very logic.

#### General Exceptions (Sections 76–106)
The exceptions are the highest-yield area in LAT Criminal Law. Memorise the section *and* the limit. **Section 76** excuses an act done by a person bound, or by mistake of fact believing himself bound, by law. **Section 80** covers accident in doing a lawful act, in a lawful manner, with lawful means. **Section 84** excuses an act of a person of **unsound mind** at the time, provided he could not understand the nature or wrongfulness of the act — a psychiatrist's later diagnosis is not enough. **Section 85** covers intoxication, but only when the accused was incapable of forming intent *and* did not voluntarily drink knowing the risk. **Sections 87–89** protect acts done with **consent** above 18, in good faith, for a lawful purpose. **Section 95** excuses trifling harm.

#### Right of Private Defence (Sections 96–100)
The right is **co-extensive with the reasonable apprehension of danger**, not the actual harm. It begins the moment apprehension arises and **ends the instant the danger is removed** — continuation of force after that point is itself an offence. Against the body, it covers reasonable apprehension of **death, grievous hurt, rape, or kidnapping** (Section 100). Against property, it is narrower: robbery, house-breaking by night, theft under specified circumstances, and mischief by fire. The right **does not extend** to causing death in defence of mere criminal trespass or theft not amounting to robbery.

#### Group Liability — Section 34 vs Section 149
**Section 34** attributes liability for an act done by several persons in **furtherance of a common intention** — there must be a **pre-arranged plan** and **active or even passive participation** by each accused. Mere presence at the scene is not enough. **Section 149** is different: every member of an **unlawful assembly of five or more**, sharing a **common object**, is liable for any offence committed by *any* member in prosecution of that object, or one the members **knew was likely to be committed**. Section 149 needs no physical participation; Section 34 requires it.

#### Homicide Hierarchy
The PPC distinguishes offences against the human body on a sliding scale of culpability. **Culpable homicide not amounting to murder (Section 299)** is the base. **Murder (Section 300)** is culpable homicide with one of four qualifying circumstances: intention to cause death or bodily injury sufficient in the ordinary course to cause death, or to cause such injury as the offender knows is likely to cause death. **Five Exceptions to Section 300** reduce murder to culpable homicide not amounting to murder: grave and sudden provocation, exceeding the right of private defence, public servant exceeding lawful powers in good faith, sudden fight in the heat of passion, and consent of the victim above 18. LAT frequently asks you to match a fact-pattern to the correct Exception.

#### Property Offences
**Theft (Section 378)** is the dishonest taking of any **movable** property out of possession, **without consent**. **Extortion (Section 383)** is obtaining property or signature by putting a person in **fear of injury** (to person, reputation, or property). **Robbery (Section 390)** is theft or extortion **accompanied by, or attempted with, force causing fear of instant death, hurt, or wrongful restraint**. **Dacoity (Section 391)** is robbery by **five or more** persons. A clean table helps:

| Offence | Force/Fear | Number of offenders |
|---|---|---|
| Theft | None | 1 or more |
| Extortion | Fear of injury | 1 or more |
| Robbery | Fear of instant death/hurt | 1 or more |
| Dacoity | Fear of instant death/hurt | 5 or more |

#### Exam-Style Question Types
LAT typically frames Criminal Law as short fact-patterns asking: (a) which Exception applies, (b) which section defines the offence, (c) whether a right of private defence is available, and (d) liability under Section 34 or 149. Always read the *mens rea* first, then map it to the section.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Common Traps
Examiners love three traps. **First, "unsoundness of mind" is narrower than medical insanity** — Section 84 requires total cognitive incapacity at the exact moment, so schizophrenia in remission or conduct arising from "irresistible impulse" alone is *not* a defence. **Second, the right of private defence of property cannot extend to causing death** unless the offence is one of the three listed in Section 100 — defending a car with a gunshot is not lawful. **Third, Section 34 versus Section 149 confusion**: a person can be convicted under Section 34 only if a specific overt act or active role is proved; mere membership of a mob, without a pre-arranged plan, is charged under **Section 149** (and may attract Section 302 read with Section 149, not Section 302 read with Section 34).

#### Procedural Overlay (CrPC)
LAT also tests a few CrPC classifications that determine police and court powers:

#### Classification of Offences
- **Cognizable** (First Schedule, Part I): police may arrest without warrant and investigate (e.g. murder, theft, kidnapping).
- **Non-cognizable**: police need an arrest warrant (e.g. defamation, forgery not involving valuable security).
- **Bailable** vs **Non-bailable**: in bailable offences, bail is a matter of right; in non-bailable offences, it is the discretion of the court.
- **Compoundable** vs **non-compoundable**: compoundable offences can be settled by the victim (e.g. hurt under Section 323); non-compoundable ones (e.g. murder) cannot.

#### Inchoate and Constructive Liability
**Attempts, abetment, and conspiracy** (Sections 107–120B PPC) are tested through questions on *preparation* versus *attempt* — an act showing intent and going beyond mere preparation is punishable. **Constructive liability** through **Section 34** and **Section 149** is the most-asked topic after Exceptions: a Supreme Court precedent line holds that mere presence, even armed, is not Section 34 liability, but unexplained presence with the rest of the gang acting in concert usually attracts Section 149.

#### Connection to Other Legal Reasoning Topics
Criminal Law overlaps directly with **Tort Law** (right of private defence mirrors the concept of necessity in torts) and **Constitutional Law** (Article 4 protections during arrest, Article 10 safeguards). It also feeds **Contract Law** through the doctrine that agreements obtained by coercion (Section 15 Contract Act) echo the fear-element of extortion. Treat criminal-law reasoning as a *substrate* for public-law and moral-cognition questions in LAT.

#### Worked Micro-Example
A, B, and C plan to beat D for non-payment of a debt. They all carry sticks. A strikes D once; B and C stand nearby. D falls and dies of a head injury. Identify the liability. **Reasoning:** There is a **pre-arranged plan** to beat D and active participation by A; B and C were present in furtherance of the common intention. All three are liable under **Section 34 read with Section 302** for the *natural and probable consequences* of the joint act, even though only A delivered the fatal blow. If, however, B and C had no plan and merely watched, Section 34 would fail — charge would shift to A alone under Section 302. If five or more persons shared the same object, Section 149 read with Section 302 would be the correct framing.

#### Practice Prompts
1. R, a police officer, shoots a fleeing thief at night, killing him. R claims right of private defence of property. Discuss the validity of the defence with reference to Sections 96–100 PPC. *(Hint: defence of property does not extend to causing death unless robbery, house-breaking by night, or similar conditions exist.)*
2. A mob of seven persons attacks a house; one member, unprovoked by the group, sets a neighbouring shop on fire. Are all seven liable for arson? Analyse under **Section 149** — liability attaches only to offences committed in prosecution of the **common object**, or one the members knew was likely to be committed. The arson, if outside the object, may not bind all.
