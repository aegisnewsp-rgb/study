# Paper 4 — Speaking — HKDSE English Language Notes

Paper 4 of the HKDSE English Language examination tests Speaking. It is about 20 minutes long and carries 10% of the subject mark. The paper has two parts: Part A is a Group Interaction in which four candidates prepare for 10 minutes and then discuss a given short text for 8 minutes per group; Part B is an Individual Response in which each candidate responds individually to the examiner's question(s) for about 1 minute. The paper tests the ability to express information and ideas with elaboration, use a range of vocabulary and language patterns, establish and maintain spoken exchanges, produce coherent structured speeches, pronounce clearly and accurately, and use appropriate pace, volume, intonation, stress, eye contact and gesture.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Paper 4 weight**: 10% of the subject mark.
- **Duration**: about 20 minutes.
- **Part A — Group Interaction**: 10 minutes preparation, 8 minutes discussion per group of 4 candidates based on a short text.
- **Part B — Individual Response**: about 1 minute per candidate.
- **Tested abilities**: express information and ideas with elaboration; use a range of vocabulary; establish and maintain spoken exchanges; produce coherent structured speeches; pronounce clearly; use appropriate pace, volume, intonation, stress, eye contact and gesture.
- **Speaking SBA**: separate from Paper 4 — see the SBA topic.

#### Examiner traps

- Speaking too quickly — the examiner needs to hear every word clearly.
- Letting one candidate dominate the group interaction — examiners watch for equal participation.
- Memorising scripted responses for Part B — examiners recognise rehearsed answers and the band drops.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Part A — Group Interaction

In the 10-minute preparation time, you may make notes on the short text (advertisements, book synopses, film reviews, letters, short news reports) and refer to them during the discussion. The discussion lasts 8 minutes for a group of four candidates.

Functions tested in Part A:

- **Making suggestions** ("I think we should consider...", "How about we...?", "Why don't we...?")
- **Giving advice** ("I'd recommend...", "It might be a good idea to...")
- **Making and explaining a choice** ("I'd go for... because...")
- **Arguing for/against a position** ("On the one hand... but on the other hand...", "I see your point, however...")
- **Discussing pros and cons** ("The advantage is... but the disadvantage is...")

Strategy:

1. In the preparation time, identify 2-3 key points you can raise.
2. In the discussion, make 3-4 substantial contributions (not just one-word replies).
3. Listen actively to the other candidates and respond to their points ("Building on what [name] said...").
5. Use linking phrases to show you are following the discussion ("That's a good point, and...").
6. Aim for balanced participation — speak for roughly 2 minutes total across the 8-minute group discussion.

#### Part B — Individual Response

In Part B, the examiner asks each candidate a follow-up question based on the group discussion. Each candidate speaks for about 1 minute.

Functions tested in Part B:

- Making and justifying a choice.
- Deciding and explaining a course of action.
- Arguing for or against a position.
- Evaluating the options discussed in Part A.

Strategy:

1. Take a moment to think about your answer (a 3-second pause is acceptable).
2. Make a clear point.
4. Provide one or two reasons or examples to support your point.
5. Conclude with a final statement.

#### Pronunciation and fluency

For Hong Kong candidates, common pronunciation issues include:

- **Consonant clusters** ("asks" not "ax", "texts" not "texes")
- **Final consonant sounds** (clearly end "asked" with the /t/ or /kt/ sound)
- **Sentence stress** (emphasise content words, de-emphasise function words)
- **Intonation** for question types (rising for yes/no questions, falling for wh-questions)
- **Linking and chunking** at natural phrase boundaries

Fluency markers: discourse markers ("well", "actually", "in fact"), hedging ("sort of", "kind of"), rephrasing. Self-correction is acceptable and shows fluency.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-paper links

- **Listening (Paper 3)**: listening to a variety of speakers builds pronunciation awareness.
- **SBA**: the SBA speaking assessments are the best practice venue for Paper 4 — they use the same task types.
- **Reading and Writing (Papers 1, 2)**: wide reading builds vocabulary range, which feeds directly into Speaking.

#### Exam technique

- **Practice with a partner**: find a classmate or language partner and run mock Part A discussions. Record yourself and listen back for pronunciation and fluency issues.
- **Listen to a range of English**: podcasts, films, news broadcasts — at different accents and speeds. This builds the comprehension that supports Speaking.
- **Use the PEEL pattern in Part B**: Point, Example, Explanation, Link.
- **Speak at a moderate pace**: faster is not better. The examiner needs to hear you clearly.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Speaking fast shows fluency." No — speaking clearly and at a natural pace shows fluency. Fast speech with errors shows a lack of control.
- "I should memorise answers for the common topics." Memorised answers break down when the examiner asks a follow-up question. Build vocabulary and structures instead.
- "Strong pronunciation is the most important thing." Pronunciation is one of four equally-weighted band descriptors. Lexical resource, grammatical range, fluency and coherence all matter.
