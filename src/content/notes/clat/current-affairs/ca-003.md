# Legal News

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Legal News, for CLAT purposes, is the slice of **Current Affairs & General Knowledge** that covers Indian judicial pronouncements, parliamentary statutes, ordinances and constitutional developments reported during the **12-month reference window** preceding the exam. Questions test awareness of **Supreme Court and High Court rulings**, **Central Acts**, and **repealed/replaced colonial-era codes** — the IPC, CrPC and Indian Evidence Act ceased to operate from **1 July 2024**, replaced by **BNS, BNSS and BSA**.

- **Read at least one national daily** (*The Hindu* or *Indian Express*) daily, plus a law outlet such as *LiveLaw*, *Bar & Bench* or *Supreme Court Observer*.
- **Map every case** to its court, bench strength, judge names, citation, date and statutory provisions interpreted.
- **Distinguish binding law from commentary**: *ratio decidendi* binds; *obiter dicta* does not.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### What the Examiner Tests
CLAT frames Legal News items as **passage-based comprehension**, factual MCQs or principle-application sets. Roughly **5%** of the paper — about **2–3 questions** in the 50-question Current Affairs section — fall directly under this head. A single passage often weaves in a recent judgment, the statutory provision interpreted, and the relief granted; aspirants must extract the legal issue, not the political headline.

#### The Three New Criminal Codes
The criminal-law map changed on **1 July 2024**. Familiar IPC citations such as Section 302 (murder), 376 (rape) and 420 (cheating) must now be cited as **BNS Sections 100, 63 and 318** respectively. Procedure moved from CrPC to **BNSS**, and evidence from the Indian Evidence Act to **BSA**. Mixing old and new section numbers is the single most common trap.

#### Reading a Reported Judgment
A news item typically contains four layers: the case name, the bench, the issue, and the relief. The **ratio decidendi** is the binding principle on which the decision rests; everything else said by the court is **obiter dicta** — persuasive, not binding. Aspirants must learn to separate the two, because CLAT principle-application questions deliberately quote obiter as if it were the rule.

| Layer | What to extract | Example reference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Case name | Petitioner v. Respondent | *Manohar Lal Sharma v. Union of India* |
| Bench | Strength + judge names | 2-Judge bench, Justices B.R. Gavai, K.V. Viswanathan |
| Issue | Question of law framed | Validity of electoral bonds scheme under Article 19 |
| Relief | Order + statutory provision | Scheme struck down; RTI Act invoked |

- **Track ordinances** under Article 123, PILs admitted (not merely disposed), and **Article 142** complete justice directions.
- **Watch constitutional-bench rulings** on Article 32, Article 226 and basic structure references.
- **Note Ministry of Law & Justice, ECI and NALSA notifications** — they generate direct MCQs.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### How to Build a Personal Legal News Register
Maintain a **month-wise register** indexed by subject head: criminal, constitutional, commercial, environmental, family, labour, tax, cyber, and IPR. Each entry should record the case name, court, date, judges, statutory provisions cited, and a one-line holding. Cross-reference every criminal-law entry to the **corresponding BNS/BNSS/BSA section** so old IPC memory does not contaminate fresh citations.

#### Adjacent Topics to Link
Legal News intersects with **Constitutional Law** (basic structure doctrine, Article 370 abrogation aftermath), **Family Law** (live-in relationships, same-sex marriage ruling in *Supriyo v. Union of India*), **Commercial Law** (PMLA, IBC amendments), and **ADR** (mediation and lok-adalat notifications by NALSA). A question on *Navtej Singh Johar* flows from Article 19 jurisprudence into current *Article 21* privacy reasoning — studying them as a chain strengthens recall.

#### Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them
- Quoting **IPC Section 302** instead of **BNS Section 100** after 1 July 2024.
- Citing a **High Court division-bench ruling** as Supreme Court law.
- Treating a **Law Commission Report** as enacted legislation.
- Ignoring the **12-month window** and citing older landmarks that are out of syllabus scope.
- Writing only the **political slug** ("SC slams government") instead of the **legal issue and relief**.

| Mistake | Correct practice |
| --- | --- |
| Cite IPC 302 for murder | Cite BNS 100 from 1 July 2024 |
| Quote obiter as binding | Identify ratio decidendi only |
| Report PIL "admitted" | Distinguish admitted vs disposed |
| Skip bench strength | Always record bench + judges |

#### Practice Prompts
1. Draft a 150-word note on the *Navtej Singh Johar* line of cases, distinguishing the ratio from obiter and linking it to later privacy rulings.
2. Re-write a newspaper headline on electoral bonds as a principle-application MCQ, citing the bench, statutory provisions interpreted, and relief granted.
