# Advance Tax & Assessment Procedures

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Advance tax is income tax paid in **four instalments** during the financial year under sections 207–211 of the Income-tax Act, 1961. Liability arises when estimated tax payable (after TDS credit) exceeds **₹10,000**. The assessment procedure (sections 139–158) is the statutory framework by which the **Assessing Officer (AO)** determines total income and tax liability — through Self-Assessment (140A), Summary Assessment (143(1)), Scrutiny Assessment (143(3)), Best Judgement Assessment (144) and Reassessment (147/148).

- **Instalment due dates (15th of each month):** 15-Jun → 15%, 15-Sep → 45% cumulative, 15-Dec → 75% cumulative, 15-Mar → 100% cumulative.
- **Threshold rule:** No advance tax if estimated liability after TDS ≤ ₹10,000; the full amount is then due on or before 15-Mar.
- **Interest u/s 234C:** 1% per month (simple) on cumulative shortfall in each instalment — computed separately for each due date.
- **Interest u/s 234B:** 1% per month on assessed tax minus advance tax paid — applies where self-assessment tax under section 140A is short.
- **Five assessment routes:** 139 (ROI) → 140A (self-assessment) → 143(1) summary / 143(3) scrutiny / 144 best judgement → 147/148 reassessment.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"S-S-S-S-R = 1-4-3-1-4-3-1-4-4-1-4-7"** — think **1**39 → **1**40A → **1**43(1) / **1**43(3) / **1**44 → **1**47/**1**48. Section numbers are the spine of the chapter; the digits **1-4-3** and **1-4-7/1-4-8** are repeated so heavily that recalling the order alone answers most MCQs.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Liability and Instalment Mechanics

An assessee (other than a company computing under the **presumptive taxation** sections 44AD, 44ADA, 44AE, 44B, 44BB, 44BBA, 44BBB, and any assessee with no tax liability after TDS) must pay advance tax whenever the estimated tax on total income exceeds ₹10,000 after adjusting for TDS and relief under sections 89, 90 and 91. The instalment percentages of estimated tax payable are **15% / 45% / 75% / 100%** cumulative. Each instalment falls due on the 15th of June, September, December and March respectively.

> 📌 **Formula Check:** Advance Tax Payable = (Estimated Tax Liability) − (TDS) − Relief u/s 89/90/91. SI unit: ₹ (INR). Conditions: result must exceed ₹10,000; otherwise, single 15-Mar payment.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix

| Aspect | Summary Assessment u/s 143(1) | Scrutiny Assessment u/s 143(3) | Best Judgement Assessment u/s 144 | Reassessment u/s 147/148 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Automatic on ROI upload | Notice u/s 143(2) within 12 months of AY end | Non-compliance with notices u/s 142(1) or 143(2) | AO has "information" of escaped income |
| **Personal hearing** | Not required | Granted on request | Granted on request | Required |
| **Penalty u/s 270A** | May be levied | May be levied | **Expressly barred** by Sec 144 proviso | May be levied if conditions met |
| **Time limit** | Within 1 year from end of AY | No outer statutory limit (practical 12 months notice) | No outer limit | 3 yrs (≤ ₹50 lakh) / 10 yrs (> ₹50 lakh) per Sec 149 |
| **Burden on AO** | Arithmetical matching with AIS/TIS | Examine books, call evidence | Draw adverse inferences | Record reasons in writing |

#### Interest Calculations — u/s 234B and 234C

Interest under section 234C is levied for shortfall in each advance-tax instalment. The interest is computed on the cumulative shortfall — i.e. 15% of estimated tax minus advance tax paid by 15-Jun, an additional 30% by 15-Sep, another 30% by 15-Dec and a final 25% by 15-Mar. Rate is **1% per month or part thereof** from the due date of that instalment up to the date of actual payment (or the date of determining tax by regular assessment).

> 📌 **Formula Check:** 234C Interest = P × 1% × n/12; P = cumulative shortfall; n = months of default. 234B Interest = (Assessed Tax − Advance Tax paid by 31-Mar) × 1% × n/12; n = months from April of AY to date of payment.

#### Return Filing and Self-Assessment

Section 139 obliges every assessee whose total income exceeds the basic exemption limit to file a Return of Income (ROI). Before filing, the assessee must pay any balance tax plus interest under sections 234B and 234C and any fee under section 234F — this payment is called **self-assessment tax** under section 140A. Filing the ROI without paying self-assessment tax renders the return **defective** under section 139(9); the AO issues a notice, and continued default attracts penalty under sections 270A / 270AA and prosecution under section 276CC.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** Mr. Rohit, a resident individual (non-corporate, non-presumptive), has an estimated total income of ₹24,00,000 for FY 2024-25. TDS deducted during the year is ₹45,000. He paid ₹50,000 on 18-Aug-2024, ₹1,00,000 on 22-Dec-2024 and nothing else. Tax on ₹24,00,000 (using the old regime basic slab) is approximately ₹2,60,000 plus 4% cess. Compute his advance-tax shortfall and interest u/s 234C.

#### Solution:

1. **Estimated tax liability:** Tax on ₹24,00,000 ≈ ₹2,60,000. Add 4% Health & Education Cess = ₹10,400. Total = **₹2,70,400**.
2. **Advance tax payable:** ₹2,70,400 − ₹45,000 (TDS) = **₹2,25,400** (exceeds ₹10,000 threshold — liability triggered).
3. **Cumulative due:** 15-Jun = ₹33,810 (15%), 15-Sep = ₹1,01,430 (45% cumulative), 15-Dec = ₹1,69,050 (75% cumulative), 15-Mar = ₹2,25,400 (100%).
4. **Shortfall per instalment:**
   - 15-Jun: due ₹33,810, paid ₹0 → shortfall ₹33,810.
   - 15-Sep: cumulative due ₹1,01,430, paid ₹50,000 → cumulative shortfall ₹51,430 (period of default = 5 months Jun→Oct).
   - 15-Dec: cumulative due ₹1,69,050, paid ₹1,50,000 → cumulative shortfall ₹19,050 (3 months default).
   - 15-Mar: cumulative due ₹2,25,400, paid ₹1,50,000 → cumulative shortfall ₹75,400 (default — interest 234B applies after assessment).
5. **Interest u/s 234C** (1% per month):
   - On ₹33,810 × 1% × 5 = **₹1,690.5**
   - On ₹51,430 × 1% × 3 = **₹1,542.9**
   - On ₹19,050 × 1% × 3 = **₹571.5**
   - **Total 234C interest ≈ ₹3,805**.
6. **Interest u/s 234B** at assessment stage on remaining ₹75,400 shortfall × 1% × months from April 2025 to date of payment.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students frequently forget that 234C interest is calculated on the **cumulative** shortfall, not the per-instalment shortfall. Also, when part payment is made between two due dates, the months of default are counted from the **earlier** due date, not the date of payment.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Reassessment — Finance Act 2021 Regime

The Finance Act 2021 rewrote sections 147–151. Reassessment now requires the AO to possess **"information"** that income has escaped assessment; the AO must record reasons and obtain prior approval of the Principal Commissioner / Commissioner in cases where the escaped income exceeds ₹50 lakh or involves search-related data. Time limits under section 149 are strict: **3 years** from the end of the relevant AY if the escaped income is ₹50 lakh or less, and **10 years** if it exceeds ₹50 lakh and there is evidence of specified concealment. Pre-2021 provisions allowing a 6-year reopening without limit have been retired. Notice u/s 148 must be issued before the limitation expires, and the assessee must file a fresh ROI within the time allowed.

#### Edge Cases and Practical Limits

1. **Presumptive assessees (44AD / 44ADA / 44AE):** Full advance tax is payable on or before **15-Mar** in a single instalment — the 15-Jun / 15-Sep / 15-Dec schedule does not apply. Default still attracts 234C interest, computed on a deemed single instalment basis.
2. **Capital gains and windfall gains:** An assessee can pay the entire advance tax on capital gains arising after 15-Mar in the next 14 days — section 211(2) concession.
3. **Foreign income:** Relief under sections 90 / 91 reduces the advance-tax quantum, but the instalment schedule remains unchanged.
4. **Companies:** Domestic companies follow the same four-instalment schedule, but the first instalment is calculated on the income of the year as estimated by the company.

#### Advanced Traps and Exceptions

| Trap / Exception | Common Misconception | Correct Position |
|---|---|---|
| 234C interest on each instalment | Per-instalment separate computation | Computed on **cumulative** shortfall against cumulative due |
| 143(1) treated as scrutiny | Notice u/s 143(2) required | 143(1) is automatic arithmetical matching — no notice, no hearing |
| Penalty barred under 144 | Best judgement assessment always attracts 270A | Section 144 proviso expressly bars penalty u/s 270A on best-judgement tax |
| Reassessment time limit | Old 6-year / unlimited rule | Post-FA 2021: 3 years (≤ ₹50 lakh), 10 years (> ₹50 lakh + specified concealment) |
| Fee u/s 234F flat | Always ₹5,000 | ₹1,000 if total income ≤ ₹5 lakh but > basic exemption; nil if ≤ basic exemption |
| PY vs AY confusion | PY = AY in shorthand | Previous Year = year of earning (FY 2024-25); Assessment Year = year of taxation (AY 2025-26) |
