# Algebra, Number and Structure (Specialist) — VCE Specialist Mathematics (VCAA 2023) Notes

Algebra, number and structure is one of the four Areas of Study in VCE Specialist Mathematics Units 3 and 4. The 2023 Study Design covers complex numbers (rectangular, polar and exponential forms), arithmetic and geometric sequences, recurrence relations, matrices and matrix algebra, and the formal logic of mathematical proof.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Complex numbers.** z = a + bi where i² = −1. Modulus |z| = √(a² + b²). Argument arg(z) = arctan(b/a).
- **Sequences.** Arithmetic a_n = a + (n−1)d. Geometric a_n = ar^(n−1). Sum to infinity S_∞ = a/(1−r) for |r| < 1.
- **Matrices.** A matrix is a rectangular array of numbers. Matrix operations: addition, scalar multiplication, multiplication (where defined), transpose.
- **Proof.** Direct proof, proof by contradiction, proof by induction.

#### Examiner traps

- Forgetting that matrix multiplication is not commutative — AB ≠ BA in general.
- Confusing geometric series with arithmetic series when computing sums.
- Using degrees instead of radians when finding the argument of a complex number.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Complex numbers

z = a + bi is the rectangular form. i² = −1. The complex conjugate of z = a + bi is z̄ = a − bi. Modulus |z| = √(a² + b²). Argument arg(z) = arctan(b/a) (with quadrant adjustment).

Polar form: z = r (cos θ + i sin θ) where r = |z| and θ = arg(z). Exponential form: z = r e^(iθ).

Multiplication: z₁ z₂ in polar form: r₁ r₂ (cos(θ₁ + θ₂) + i sin(θ₁ + θ₂)). In exponential form: r₁ r₂ e^(i(θ₁+θ₂)).

De Moivre's theorem: (cos θ + i sin θ)^n = cos nθ + i sin nθ.

#### Sequences and series

Arithmetic: a_n = a + (n−1)d. Sum S_n = n/2 (2a + (n−1)d).

Geometric: a_n = ar^(n−1). Sum S_n = a(1 − r^n)/(1 − r) for r ≠ 1. Sum to infinity S_∞ = a/(1 − r) for |r| < 1.

Recurrence: a_(n+1) = r a_n + b has fixed point a/(1 − r) and solution a_n = (a_0 − b/(1 − r)) r^n + b/(1 − r).

#### Matrices

A matrix A is an m × n array. A^T is the transpose. The determinant det(A) is defined for square matrices. For 2×2: det(A) = ad − bc. Inverse A^(−1) exists iff det(A) ≠ 0.

Matrix multiplication AB is defined when columns of A = rows of B. (AB)_ij = Σ_k A_ik B_kj. Matrix multiplication is associative but not commutative.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Why this Area of Study underpins Complex Numbers and Mechanics

- **Complex numbers** appear throughout Specialist — De Moivre's theorem for roots, Argand diagram for visualisation.
- **Sequences and series** underpin applications in finance (compound interest), probability (binomial expansion) and analysis (Taylor series).

#### Exam technique

- For complex number questions, decide whether rectangular, polar or exponential form is most efficient for the operation.
- For matrix multiplication, check dimensions before multiplying.
- For induction proofs, state the base case, the inductive hypothesis, the inductive step and the conclusion.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "AB = BA." No — matrix multiplication is not commutative.
- "All series have finite sums." Only geometric series with |r| < 1.
- "Induction proves only arithmetic examples." Induction is a general proof technique.
