# Percentage, Ratio, and Proportion

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Topic 4 of Qimiyah General Studies covers **quantitative literacy**: the arithmetic, algebra, geometry, and reasoning skills tested through MCQ word problems. The four most-tested operations are **percentages**, **ratios/proportions**, **averages**, and **distance–speed–time**. Memorise these formulas:

- **Average** = Sum of values ÷ Number of values
- **Percentage** = (Part ÷ Whole) × 100
- **Simple Interest (SI)** = P × R × T ÷ 100
- **Distance** = Speed × Time

The Qimiyah section rewards **unit conversion** (grams↔kilograms, minutes↔hours, riyal↔halala) and careful reading of "of" as multiplication by a fraction. Always identify the **base value** before computing a percentage increase or successive discount. Practice **pie charts, bar charts, and tables** — they appear in nearly every paper.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Percentage, Ratio, and Proportion

A **percentage** expresses a part per hundred; convert it by dividing by 100 (20% = 0.20). For a percentage **increase**, multiply the original by (1 + r/100); for a **decrease**, multiply by (1 − r/100). Successive discounts compound multiplicatively: a 20% then 10% discount on 100 SAR equals 100 × 0.80 × 0.90 = 72 SAR, not 70 SAR.

**Ratios** compare two quantities a:b. To divide a total T in ratio a:b, the parts are T·a/(a+b) and T·b/(a+b). A **direct proportion** y = kx; an **inverse proportion** xy = k (e.g., speed and time for a fixed distance).

#### Averages and Series

The **arithmetic mean** is sensitive to outliers; the **median** is the middle value when sorted. A moving average smooths trends in time-series data. Numerical series questions test pattern recognition: arithmetic (constant difference), geometric (constant ratio), or alternating (e.g., +2, ×3, +2, ×3).

#### Financial Math

**Simple Interest** grows linearly: A = P + SI. **Compound Interest** grows exponentially: A = P(1 + R/100)^T. Saudi installment plans often quote a flat monthly rate — convert to annualised rate by (1 + r)^12 − 1 to compare with bank deposits.

#### Geometry Formulas

| Shape | Perimeter / Circumference | Area / Volume |
|-------|---------------------------|----------------|
| Rectangle | 2(l + w) | l × w |
| Triangle | a + b + c | ½ × b × h |
| Circle | 2πr | πr² |
| Rectangular prism | 4(l + w + h) | l × w × h |
| Cylinder | — | πr²h |

Circle problems demand **r vs d discipline**: circumference uses radius (C = 2πr) but a "wire bent into a circle" question requires diameter = C/π.

#### Data Interpretation

Read axes, units, and legend **before** answering. Pie-chart slices sum to 100%; verify totals. Bar-chart comparison questions ask for difference, ratio, or percentage share — each demands a different operation on the same numbers.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Trap Problems

**Successive percentage traps.** When a price rises by a% then falls by b%, the net change is **not** (a − b)%. Net factor = (1 + a/100)(1 − b/100). A 50% rise followed by a 50% fall leaves 75% of the original, not 100%.

**VAT in KSA.** Standard VAT is 15%. A pre-VAT price of X becomes 1.15X post-VAT. To extract pre-VAT from a receipt showing 115 SAR, divide by 1.15, not multiply by 0.85.

**Speed–time–distance traps.** If a car travels 120 km at 60 km/h and returns at 40 km/h, the **average speed** for the round trip is harmonic mean 2·60·40/(60+40) = 48 km/h — not the arithmetic mean 50 km/h.

**Mixture and alligation.** Mixing two solutions of concentration a% and b% (a < b) in ratio (c − a):(b − c) gives concentration c%. Useful for fuel-blending and discount-mix questions.

#### Common Mistakes in Exam Conditions

1. Treating "of" as multiplication by a decimal when the word problem specifies a percentage (e.g., "30% of 200" = 60, not 0.30 × 200 = 60 — same result here, but "three-fifths of 200" = 120, not 0.6 × 200 = 120… still equivalent; the real trap is "increase by 30% of 200" meaning add 60 to 200 = 260, not multiply 200 by 0.30 alone).
2. Forgetting to convert 18 months into 1.5 years before applying SI = PRT/100.
3. Computing average of percentages without weighting by their base populations.
4. Confusing **area** (cm²) with **perimeter** (cm) in shading or fencing problems.

#### Practice Prompts

1. A refrigerator is marked 2,400 SAR. The shop offers a 15% discount, then adds 15% VAT. What is the final price? (Answer: 2,400 × 0.85 × 1.15 = **2,346 SAR**.)
2. Two workers complete a job in 12 days together. Worker A alone takes 20 days. How long does Worker B take alone? (Combined rate 1/12; A's rate 1/20; B = 1/12 − 1/20 = 1/30 → **30 days**.)
