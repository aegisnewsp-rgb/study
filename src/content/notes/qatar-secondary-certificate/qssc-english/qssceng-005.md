# Grammar and Vocabulary in Context — Qatar Secondary Certificate English Notes

The MoEHE English Curriculum Standards at Grade 12 (CEFR B2 Low-Mid exit) expect confident use of complex sentence patterns, modal verbs, passive forms, conditionals, reported speech, phrasal verbs, collocations, and academic vocabulary in context. Grammar and vocabulary are not separate strands but the building blocks across all four skills (L, S, R, W).

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Tenses:** present simple, present continuous, past simple, past continuous, present perfect, past perfect, future (will, going to).
- **Modals:** can, could, may, might, must, should, ought to, had better, would.
- **Conditionals:** zero (if + present, present), first (if + present, will + base), second (if + past, would + base), third (if + past perfect, would have + past participle).
- **Passive:** subject + be + past participle. "The book was written by the author."
- **Reported speech:** tense shifts back one step; modals shift (can -> could, may -> might, must -> had to).
- **Phrasal verbs:** verb + particle with idiomatic meaning ("put up with" = tolerate).
- **Collocations:** word partnerships ("make a decision", "do homework", "heavy rain", "take a photo").

#### Examiner traps

- Mixing conditionals ("If I would have..." is wrong — third conditional is "If I had...").
- Overusing the passive — it is appropriate when the action is more important than the agent.
- Reporting "yesterday" as "yesterday" instead of "the day before".

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Tense review

Present simple for habits and facts. Present continuous for actions happening now. Past simple for completed actions at a specific time. Past continuous for actions in progress in the past. Present perfect for past actions with present relevance (have you ever...). Past perfect for the earlier of two past actions. Future: "will" for predictions and spontaneous decisions, "going to" for plans.

#### Modals in context

"Can" for ability and permission. "Could" for past ability, polite requests, and possibility. "May" for formal permission and possibility. "Might" for weaker possibility. "Must" for strong obligation and logical deduction. "Should" for advice. "Ought to" for moral obligation. "Had better" for urgent advice. "Would" for polite requests and habitual past.

In reported speech: can -> could, may -> might, must -> had to (or must if meaning is logical deduction). "Would, could, might, should, ought to, had better" do not shift.

#### Conditionals

Zero: "If you heat water to 100 degrees, it boils." (general truth).
First: "If it rains tomorrow, we will stay inside." (real future).
Second: "If I had more time, I would learn Arabic." (unreal present).
Third: "If she had studied harder, she would have passed the exam." (unreal past).

Mixed: "If I had studied medicine, I would be a doctor now." (third condition + second result).

#### Passive

Use passive when the action or the result is more important than the agent, or when the agent is unknown. "The window was broken" (by whom? unknown or irrelevant). Avoid passive when the agent matters: "Shakespeare wrote Hamlet" (not "Hamlet was written").

#### Reported speech

Direct: "I am happy." Reported: He said he was happy.
Direct: "Do you like coffee?" Reported: She asked (me) if I liked coffee.
Direct: "Where do you live?" Reported: He asked where I lived.
Direct: "I will go tomorrow." Reported: She said she would go the next day.

Time and place shifts: today -> that day, yesterday -> the day before, tomorrow -> the next day, here -> there, this -> that.

#### Phrasal verbs

Memorise in phrases, not as separate words. Common types: intransitive (the plane took off), transitive separable (turn down the radio / turn it down), transitive inseparable (look after the children), three-word (put up with the noise).

#### Collocations

Word partnerships are arbitrary — they must be memorised. Common patterns: verb + noun (make a decision, do homework), adjective + noun (heavy rain, strong opinion), adverb + adjective (deeply concerned, highly likely).

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### Where grammar and vocabulary appear at the terminal

The MoEHE Grade 12 English terminal tests grammar and vocabulary in context — through reading comprehension, cloze tests, error correction, sentence transformation, and writing tasks. The B2 CEFR exit descriptor expects accurate use of a range of complex structures and a wide vocabulary.

#### Exam technique

- For cloze tests, read the whole sentence (or paragraph) before choosing — context drives the answer.
- For error correction, find the error by reading aloud.
- For sentence transformation, identify what must be preserved (meaning) and what must change (grammar).

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Phrasal verbs can be replaced by single-word synonyms." Often, but not always ("put up with" = tolerate, but "put off" = postpone AND "discourage from doing").
- "Reported speech = past tense." Tense shifts, but the meaning can be present (He said he works in Doha — reported speech with a present-tense reference).
