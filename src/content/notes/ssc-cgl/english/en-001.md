# Error Detection

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Error Detection** (also called *Spotting Errors*) presents a single sentence split into four labelled parts (a), (b), (c), (d). Exactly one part carries a grammatical flaw — the other three are correct — and the candidate must mark that flawed segment. The question tests your eye for agreement, tense, articles, prepositions, pronouns, and parallel structure rather than vocabulary.

| Error Zone | What to Scan For | Typical Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Part (a) — Subject area | True subject after phrases/clauses | Singular subject → singular verb |
| Part (b) — Verb area | Tense, voice, modal+verb pairing | Match tense sequence; drop `-ed` from modal |
| Part (c) — Article/Adj/Adv | `a/an/the`, `-ly` adverbs, degrees | Drop `-ly` after `slowly`, use `the` before superlative |
| Part (d) — Conjunction/Pronoun | `since` vs `for`, `each other` vs `one another` | Point of time → `since`; 2 items → `each other` |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"Subject, Verb, Modifier, Connector"** — read the sentence in that order (SVMC). Find the true subject first, confirm the verb agrees, check the modifier (article/adj/adv), then validate the connector (conjunction/preposition). The error almost always surfaces in this scan order.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Mechanism of the Question

SSC CGL splits a sentence into four segments and asks: *"Which part contains the error?"* Your job is **detection, not correction** — though mentally substituting a fix prevents mis-selection. Always read the full sentence with each proposed fix before marking, because a phrase that *sounds* wrong in isolation can be correct in context.

The standard SSC pattern places the **subject in Part (a)**, the **main verb in Part (b)**, the **object/modifier cluster in Part (c)**, and the **remainder/conjunction in Part (d)**. This convention lets you triage: agreement errors live up front, article/degree errors in the middle, conjunction/pronoun errors at the end.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix

| In many papers Confused | Correct Rule | Wrong Pattern to Reject |
|---|---|---|
| `since` vs `for` | `since` + point of time (since 2010); `for` + duration (for 5 years) | "I have lived here **for** 2010" |
| `each other` vs `one another` | 2 items → `each other`; >2 items → `one another` | "The three friends helped **each other**" |
| Comparative vs Superlative | 2 items → comparative (`better`); >2 items → superlative (`best`) | "He is **the best** of the two brothers" |
| `make` vs `do` | `make` = create/produce (mistake, decision); `do` = perform a task (homework, duty) | "She **made** her homework yesterday" |
| Gerund vs Infinitive after verb | Enjoy/avoid/deny → gerund; want/decide/agree → infinitive | "I enjoy **to read** novels" |
| `none of` + countable/uncountable | Uncountable/mass → singular verb; countable → plural verb is acceptable | "None of the water **are** fit" |

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

#### Question:
*Identify the part of the sentence that contains an error.*

"Neither of the candidates (a) / have submitted (b) / their application forms (c) / before the deadline. (d)"

#### Solution:
Step 1 — Identify the true subject: "**Neither** of the candidates." The subject is the indefinite pronoun `neither`, which is **singular** in formal English.
Step 2 — Check verb agreement: `neither` requires a singular verb (`has`), so "have submitted" is the error.
Step 3 — Cross-check the other segments:
- (a) "Neither of the candidates" — correct prepositional phrase.
- (c) "their application forms" — singular `they`-antecedent is accepted in modern usage; not the tested error.
- (d) "before the deadline" — correct preposition + noun.
Step 4 — Mark **Part (b)** as the answer. Correction: *"has submitted"*.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students in many papers select Part (c) thinking `their` disagrees with `neither`. In contemporary SSC keys, the singular `they` is accepted; the agreement error in (b) is the *only* flagged flaw. Always verify all four parts before locking the answer.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Advanced Traps and Edge Cases

| Trap Type | Sentence Clue | Right Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Plural subject + singular-looking noun | "The news **are** shocking" | `news` is singular → `is` |
| Collective noun disagreement | "The team **are** divided" | British English permits plural; SSC prefers singular for `team`, `committee` |
| Modal + past form | "He **must went**" | Modals take base form → `must go` |
| Type-2 conditional mix | "If I **would have** money, I would travel" | `If` clause takes past simple → "If I **had** money" |
| Parallel structure break | "He likes swimming, to run, and biking" | All gerunds → "swimming, running, biking" |
| Preposition stranded with adjective | "He is good **in** mathematics" | Adjective `good` takes `at`, not `in` |
| Comparative double-degree | "This is **more better**" | Drop `more` before `-er` form → "better" |

#### Pronoun Reference and Voice Nuances

Pronoun errors cluster in Part (d). Watch for **ambiguous antecedents** ("Rohit told Vikram that *he* had won" — who is `he`?). Also track **voice consistency**: if a sentence opens in passive voice, the second clause in most keys stays passive unless an agent is named. Transitive verbs only — `consist of`, `look into`, `depend on` cannot be passivised because the preposition travels with the verb idiom.

#### Spelling and Word-Form Errors

SSC slips in spelling traps (`recommend` → `recommed`, `separate` → `seperate`) and confusable word-forms (`adverse` vs `averse`, `sensible` vs `sensitive`). Treat these as Part (c) errors: the surrounding grammar is fine, only the lexical form breaks.

#### Practice Prompts

1. *"Each of the students (a) / were given (b) / a separate (c) / instruction booklet. (d)"* — Decide which segment breaks agreement, then rewrite using `each other`/`one another` if the subject were pluralised.
2. *"If he would have studied (a) / harder, he (b) / would have cleared (c) / the examination easily. (d)"* — Identify the conditional type violated and restate both clauses in the correct form.
