# Write texts to communicate ideas and information (US32405) — NCEA UE Literacy Notes

Unit Standard 32405 (US32405) is the dedicated co-requisite unit standard for the University Entrance literacy writing requirement. It is internally assessed by the school (or by an approved provider) and carries 5 credits at Level 1 or above. The standard tests the ability to produce written texts that communicate ideas and information effectively for a purpose and audience. Together with US32403 (reading), US32405 satisfies the full 10-credit UE literacy requirement.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **5 credits**, internally assessed, NZQA moderated.
- **Level 1 or above**, can be used for the NCEA Level 1 certificate AND for UE literacy.
- **Writing focus:** produce written texts that communicate ideas and information effectively for a purpose and audience.
- The "Y" column in the UE literacy standards spreadsheet confirms this standard counts toward UE literacy.
- The standard is the dedicated co-requisite writing standard, so it can be relied upon to provide the writing half of the 10-credit UE literacy requirement.

#### Examiner traps

- Treating writing as a "free" task — the marking schedule is rigorous; the standard requires deliberate language choices and audience awareness.
- Skipping the planning stage — well-organised writing starts with a plan, not just an opening sentence.
- Confusing US32405 (writing) with US32403 (reading) — they are separate standards.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### What the standard tests

US32405 tests the ability to:

1. **Plan** a written text for a specific purpose and audience.
2. **Draft** the text, using language features appropriate to the purpose and audience.
3. **Revise** the text for clarity, coherence, and effectiveness.
4. **Produce** a final version that meets the requirements of the task.

The standard is often delivered as a Common Assessment Activity (CAA), which is a national writing task set by NZQA. The school then marks the writing against the published schedule.

#### The marking schedule

US32405 uses a four-grade scale (Not Achieved, Achieved, Merit, Excellence). The grade descriptors typically assess:

| Grade | What it requires |
|---|---|
| Achieved | Produce a text that communicates ideas and information for a purpose and audience. |
| Merit | Produce a text that communicates ideas and information effectively for a purpose and audience. |
| Excellence | Produce a sustained, integrated text that communicates ideas and information effectively and with control for a purpose and audience. |

#### How it fits into UE

US32405 contributes the writing half of the UE literacy requirement. Together with US32403 (reading, 5 credits), it satisfies the full 10-credit requirement.

For students who prefer a subject-based path, achievement standards in English, History, Geography, and similar subjects can also contribute writing credits (e.g., AS 91102, AS 91473, AS 91475).

#### Common text types

The CAA typically asks for one of:

- **Article** for a magazine or newspaper.
- **Essay** for a school publication.
- **Report** for a community group.
- **Speech** for an audience.
- **Blog post** for an online audience.
- **Letter** for a specific recipient (e.g., a letter to the editor).

Each text type has its own conventions (structure, tone, formality) that the student should demonstrate awareness of.

#### Preparation strategy

1. **Practice writing diverse text types** — articles, essays, reports, speeches, blog posts, letters.
2. **Plan before drafting** — outline the main points, the structure, the opening and closing.
3. **Revise for language features** — vary sentence length, use appropriate vocabulary, ensure coherence.
4. **Match tone to audience** — formal for a report, conversational for a blog post, persuasive for a speech.
5. **Proofread** — check spelling, grammar, punctuation, and sentence completeness.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### The Excellence move

The biggest gap between Merit and Excellence on US32405 is the integration of purpose, audience, and language choices throughout the text.

For example, on a task to "write a blog post about screen time for teenagers":

- Merit: A blog post that addresses the topic, with some language features appropriate to a teenage audience.
- Excellence: A blog post that hooks the teenage reader from the opening, sustains an appropriate conversational-but-informed tone throughout, integrates evidence and opinion, and closes with a clear takeaway — all while demonstrating conscious control of language.

The Excellence version shows deliberate choices throughout, not just in selected sections.

#### Connecting to UE readiness

US32405 is one of the two dedicated co-requisite unit standards for UE literacy. Together with US32403, it is the simplest, fastest path to UE literacy. For students aiming for university, achieving both dedicated co-requisites at Level 1 (Year 11) means they have one less thing to worry about in Years 12 and 13.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "US32405 is just an English essay." No — the standard requires deliberate purpose and audience awareness, which varies by text type.
- "Longer is better." No — Excellence is about control, not length.
- "Audience doesn't matter." It does — every text type has an implied audience.
- "The CAA is the same every year." It varies; check the latest NZQA materials before each cycle.
