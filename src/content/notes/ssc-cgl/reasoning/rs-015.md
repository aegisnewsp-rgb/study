# Dice

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

A **standard die** is a cube with faces numbered 1 to 6. The single rule that unlocks 90% of SSC CGL dice questions is the **opposite-faces-sum-to-7** property: pairs are **1↔6, 2↔5, 3↔4**. From any single view, **three faces are visible** and three are hidden, and knowing two **adjacent** faces fully fixes the cube's orientation.

- Total dots on all six faces: 1+2+3+4+5+6 = **21**.
- Opposite face of n = **7 − n**, valid only for n ∈ {1,2,3,4,5,6}.
- Spot patterns: **1** = single dot, **2** = diagonal, **3** = diagonal, **4** = four corners, **5** = four corners + centre, **6** = two columns of three.
- **Clockwise rotation preserves face order**; a mirror (reflection) reverses it — examiners exploit this swap.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** "**1-6, 2-5, 3-4** — Sum Seven, no more." Picture 7 as the boss; opposite faces are employees that always add up to its salary. Rotation = **C**lockwise stays **C**lockwise; reflection flips it.

| Term | Meaning | Quick use |
|---|---|---|
| Standard die (d6) | Cube with 1–6 dots | Only this die is tested |
| Opposite pair | Faces summing to 7 | Find hidden face: 7 − known |
| Adjacent faces | Share an edge | Two adjacent faces = orientation locked |
| Rotation | Cube turned in place | Face order stays clockwise |
| Reflection | Mirror image | Face order reverses |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Geometry of the Die

A standard die has **6 faces, 12 edges, 8 vertices**. From any single viewpoint, exactly three faces meet at the nearest corner — these are the **visible faces**. The other three faces are hidden behind them and form the complementary trio.

If you know **one face** of a die, you know its opposite (7 − n), but rotation around the axis through that pair is still free. Knowing **two adjacent faces** (faces sharing an edge) locks the cube because both their opposites and the remaining pair are fixed simultaneously.

#### Rotation vs Reflection

When a die is **rotated** in place (no flipping across a plane), the **clockwise** order of any four faces around a vertex remains clockwise. A **reflection** (mirror image) reverses that order. SSC CGL options often swap two adjacent faces precisely to catch students who mentally mirror instead of rotate. Always trace one dot pattern across two positions to confirm direction.

#### Two-Dice Problem Mechanic

In two-dice questions you usually see the **top, front, and right** face of each die. For each die separately: top + bottom = 7 and front + back = 7. So visible dots on die 1 + visible dots on die 2 + hidden dots = 2 × 21 = 42. Hidden dots = 42 − visible, and you can find any single hidden face by elimination.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** Two standard dice are placed side by side. The visible faces are: Die A shows 2 (top), 3 (front), 6 (right). Die B shows 5 (top), 4 (front), 1 (right). What number is on the **bottom face of Die B**?

#### Solution:

Step 1 — Apply the opposite-faces-sum-to-7 rule to Die B.
Top of Die B = 5 → Bottom = 7 − 5 = **2**.

Step 2 — Verify with Die A as a consistency check.
Die A top = 2, so Die A bottom = 7 − 2 = 5. Front 3 → back 4. Right 6 → left 1. All sums equal 7. ✓

Step 3 — Sanity-check the full die.
Die B top 5, front 4, right 1. Bottom must be 2, back must be 3, left must be 6. Every pair sums to 7. ✓

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students see "5" on top and "4" on front and panic, trying to derive the bottom by combining three faces. You only need **one** face and the rule **opposite = 7 − known**. Ignore the other visible faces for this question.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Boundary Conditions

- **Non-standard dice are never tested.** If a question states a custom opposite pair (e.g., 1-5), confirm whether it is a standard die; otherwise treat the stated pair as given.
- **Three-face visibility rule:** visible dots + hidden dots = **21** per die, because every face is either seen or hidden from one viewpoint.
- **Two-dice dot sum shortcut:** total visible on both dice + total hidden = 2 × 21 = 42. If asked total dots on a hidden face of Die B, subtract Die A's hidden faces first.
- **Minimum-count four-dice problems:** if four dice show faces a, b, c, d on top, the minimum number showing **at least** X dots is computed by counting how many dice show **more than** X and subtracting from 4. For example, with tops {1, 3, 5, 6}, dice showing more than 2 are {3, 5, 6} = 3, so minimum showing ≥2 = 4 − 3 = **1**.

| Trap | What students do | Correct move |
|---|---|---|
| Confusing rotation with mirror | Swap two faces using reflection | Trace one dot through positions; clockwise stays clockwise |
| Applying 7-rule to a non-standard die | Assume 1-6 opposite | Read question stem; honour stated pair |
| Off-by-one in min-count | Subtract dice showing ≥ X | Subtract dice showing **> X** from total |
| Treating two dice as one | Use one die's hidden pair | Apply sum-7 to **each** die independently |
| Ignoring adjacent requirement | Use opposite faces when faces are adjacent | If faces share an edge, they cannot be opposite |

#### Practice Prompts

1. A die shows 4 on top and 2 on the front. The right face is 6. After a clockwise rotation that brings the right face to the front, what is now on top?
2. Three dice show top faces 6, 5, 4 with front faces 2, 3, 6 respectively. Find the sum of all hidden bottom faces.
