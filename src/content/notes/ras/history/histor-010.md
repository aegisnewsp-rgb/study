# Indian National Movement

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The **Indian National Movement** spans roughly **1857–1947**, beginning with the **Revolt of 1857** (Mangal Pandey, Meerut, Rani Lakshmibai, Tantia Tope) and ending with the **Indian Independence Act, 1947** and Partition.

- **INC** was founded in **1885** by **A.O. Hume** (retired ICS officer), not by an Indian leader.
- Two clear phases before Gandhi: **Moderates (1885–1905)** used prayer, petition, protest — Dadabhai Naoroji, Gokhale, S.N. Banerjee. **Extremists (post-1905)** demanded **Swaraj** — Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal, Lala Lajpat Rai (**Lal-Bal-Pal**).
- Gandhian mass movements: **Non-Cooperation (1920–22)**, **Civil Disobedience (1930–34)** with the **Dandi March on 12 March 1930**, and **Quit India (8 August 1942)** — slogan "Do or Die."
- Final acts: **Cabinet Mission (1946)**, **Mountbatten Plan (3 June 1947)**, **Indian Independence Act (18 July 1947)**, Independence on **15 August 1947**.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Pre-Congress Resistance and 1857

The **Revolt of 1857** was triggered by the greased-cartridge issue (Mangal Pandey, 29 March 1857, Barrackpore) and spread from **Meerut** to Delhi, Kanpur, Lucknow, Jhansi, and Hyderabad. Leaders included **Rani Lakshmibai**, **Tantia Tope**, **Nana Sahib**, and **Begum Hazrat Mahal**. Failure of the revolt shifted political mobilisation from soldiers to educated civilians.

#### Moderate Phase (1885–1905)

**A.O. Hume**, with Allan Octavian's blessing and guidance from Lord Dufferin, founded the **Indian National Congress** in **Bombay in 1885**. Early objectives were modest: political dialogue, demands for Indianisation of services, and representation in legislatures. Methods: **prayer, petition, and constitutional protest** — three "P"s. Dadabhai Naoroji authored *"Poverty and Un-British Rule in India"* (Drain Theory). Gokhale mentored Gandhi in South Africa.

#### Extremist Phase and Swadeshi

**Lord Curzon's Partition of Bengal (1905)** sparked the **Swadeshi–Boycott Movement (1905–08)**. Leaders Tilak, Pal, and Lajpat Rai demanded **Swaraj** as a birthright and promoted **Boycott** of British goods, national education, and **Ganapati/Shivaji festivals** for mass mobilisation. The **Morley–Minto Reforms (1909)** introduced **separate electorates** for Muslims — a decision with long communal consequences. The **Lucknow Pact (1916)** temporarily reunited Moderates and Extremists.

#### Gandhian Mass Movements

Returning from South Africa, Gandhi led **Champaran (1917)**, **Ahmedabad Mill Strike (1918)**, and **Kheda (1918)**. The **Rowlatt Act (1919)** enabled detention without trial, triggering the **Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (13 April 1919)** at Amritsar under General Dyer. Joined with the **Khilafat Movement**, Gandhi launched the **Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–22)**, suspended after the **Chauri Chaura incident (February 1922)**. The **Simon Commission (1928)** was all-British and met with "Simon Go Back" protests. The **Lahore Session (December 1929)** under Jawaharlal Nehru declared **Purna Swaraj** (complete independence); **26 January 1930** was first celebrated as Independence Day.

| Movement | Year | Key Event |
|---|---|---|
| Non-Cooperation | 1920–22 | Chauri Chaura (Feb 1922) |
| Civil Disobedience | 1930–34 | Dandi March, 12 Mar 1930 |
| Round Table | 1930–32 | Poona Pact, 1932 |
| Quit India | 1942 | "Do or Die," 8 Aug 1942 |

> **Trap:** The **Poona Pact (1932)** between Gandhi and **B.R. Ambedkar** **replaced** separate electorates with **reserved seats in a joint electorate** — it did not grant separate electorates.

#### Towards Partition

The **Government of India Act 1935** introduced provincial autonomy. The **Cripps Mission (1942)** was rejected by Congress. The **Cabinet Mission (1946)** proposed a united federal India, which Congress accepted and the League rejected. The **Mountbatten Plan (3 June 1947)** accepted Partition; the **Indian Independence Act** was enacted by the **British Parliament on 18 July 1947**, leading to freedom on **15 August 1947** amid the Partition upheaval.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Ideological Currents

Three ideological streams shaped the movement's end-phase. **Gandhian nationalism** emphasised non-violence, Hindu-Muslim unity, and village self-rule through constructive programme (khadi, harijan uplift). **Subhas Chandra Bose's INA (Azad Hind Fauj, 1943–45)** pursued armed liberation from Singapore, Imphal, and Kohima, and the **Red Fort Trials (November 1945)** of Shah Nawaz, Prem Kumar Sahgal, and Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon electrified public opinion against the British. **V.D. Savarkar's Hindutva** (Essentials of Hindutva, 1923) defined Indian nationhood in cultural-religious terms, while **Jinnah's Two-Nation Theory** (1940) insisted Hindus and Muslims were separate nations, hardening the demand for Pakistan.

#### Constitutional Milestones and Their Logic

The **Indian Councils Acts (1892, 1909)** incrementally widened representation; the **Government of India Act 1919** introduced dyarchy at the provinces; **Act 1935** proposed an All-India Federation (never realised) and provincial autonomy, which Congress rejected as office-grabbing but later contested under the 1937 elections. The **Cabinet Mission (1946)** plan had three tiers — Union, Groups of provinces, Provinces — and an interim government; Congress's acceptance followed by the League's withdrawal and Direct Action Day (16 August 1946, Calcutta) hardened communal lines.

#### Common Mistakes in RPSC Prelims

- Confusing **Cripps (1942)** with **Cabinet Mission (1946)** — Cripps offered dominion status post-war; Cabinet Mission proposed a united India.
- Crediting the **Indian Constituent Assembly** with passing the **Indian Independence Act** — it was enacted by the **British Parliament**.
- Believing **A.O. Hume** was an Indian freedom fighter — he was a **retired ICS officer**; do not confuse him with Dadabhai Naoroji.
- Attributing **separate electorates** to the **Poona Pact** — they were introduced by **Morley–Minto (1909)**; the Pact abolished them.

> **Exam weightage:** History fetches roughly **8–10 questions** in RAS Prelims, and the National Movement contributes about **3%** — typically 1–3 MCQs. High-yield targets: **INC phases, Dandi March date (12 March 1930), Lahore Session 1929, Quit India (8 Aug 1942), Cabinet Mission vs Mountbatten Plan, and INA Red Fort trials**.
