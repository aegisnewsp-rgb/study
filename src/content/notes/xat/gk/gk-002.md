# Indian Polity and Governance

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Indian Polity and Governance** for XAT is the institutional framework of the Indian state — how the **Union**, **State**, and **Local** tiers share power, and how that power is checked. Polity is the structure; governance is the process of using it.

- **Constitutional source:** The Constitution of India (adopted 26 Nov 1949, enacted 26 Jan 1950) is the supreme law; **Parliament** cannot override the **Basic Structure** (Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala, 1973).
- **Rights vs Duties:** **Fundamental Rights (Art. 12–35)** are **justiciable** (enforceable by courts via writs); **Directive Principles (Art. 36–51)** are **non-justiciable** but guide policy and judicial interpretation, especially of **Art. 21** (Right to Life).
- **Money Bill (Art. 109):** Introduced **only in Lok Sabha**; **Rajya Sabha** can only recommend (no veto), with a **14-day** upper limit.
- **Writ jurisdiction:** **Art. 32** lies with the **Supreme Court**; **Art. 226** with **High Courts** (wider scope — covers rights beyond Part III).
- **Statutory watchdogs:** **CAG (Art. 148)** audits public spending; **UPSC (Art. 315)** recruits civil services; **Election Commission (Art. 324)** conducts free elections; **Finance Commission (Art. 280)** recommends Centre–State revenue sharing.

**High-yield pointers for XAT:** (1) Don't rote-learn article numbers — expect *application* MCQs linking a constitutional concept to a current affair. (2) Centre–State friction items (GST Council, Governor's role) recur every year. (3) Ethics-flavored governance questions (Lokpal, RTI) fit XAT's **Decision Making** DNA.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Constitutional Architecture and Federal Balance

India follows a **quasi-federal** system with a **strong unitary bias** — the Constitution favours the Centre during emergencies (Art. 352, 356, 360). Power is divided across the **Seventh Schedule**: **Union List** (defence, atomic energy, foreign affairs), **State List** (police, public order, agriculture), and **Concurrent List** (criminal law, education, forests) where Parliament prevails on conflict.

#### Legislature, Executive, and the Money Bill Rule

**Parliament** = **President** + **Lok Sabha** (directly elected, 5-year term, can be dissolved) + **Rajya Sabha** (indirectly elected, 6-year staggered terms, **not subject to dissolution**, capped at 250). The **Money Bill** asymmetry (Art. 109) is a recurring MCQ trap: introduce in LS, RS has 14 days to return, no amendment power, no rejection.

| Provision | Lok Sabha | Rajya Sabha |
|---|---|---|
| Money Bill introduction | Yes | No |
| Ordinary bill | Yes | Yes |
| Joint sitting (deadlock) | Speaker can summon (Art. 108) | Joint |
| Maximum strength | 552 | 250 |

#### Judiciary and the Basic Structure

The **Supreme Court** (Art. 124) is the apex court; **High Courts** (Art. 214) head state judiciaries. **Judicial Review** lets courts strike down unconstitutional laws. **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)** froze Parliament's amending power — the **Basic Structure** (federalism, secularism, democracy, judicial review, fundamental rights) cannot be erased even by a unanimous constitutional amendment.

#### Justiciable vs Non-Justiciable: A Frequent Trap

> **Trap:** "DPSPs are meaningless because courts cannot enforce them."  
> **Reality:** Courts **routinely** read DPSPs into **Art. 21** expansion (right to livelihood, education, clean environment) and use them to interpret ambiguous statutes.

#### Governance Institutions and Contemporary Reforms

**CAG (Art. 148)** audits Union and State accounts and reports to Parliament — the **PAC** then examines findings. **UPSC (Art. 315)** conducts the Civil Services Examination. **NITI Aayog** replaced the **Planning Commission (2015)** as the advisory policy think-tank. **RTI Act 2005** empowers citizens to demand information; the **Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act 2013** created an independent anti-corruption ombudsman for the Prime Minister's Office and central entities.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Polity vs Governance — Why XAT Cares

For XAT, treat **polity** as the *hardware* (institutions, articles, powers) and **governance** as the *software* (how decisions are made, contested, implemented, and audited). XAT GK questions increasingly test the **interface** — e.g., *"A state Governor refuses to sign a bill into law"* → you must invoke **Art. 200** (assent, withholding, reservation for President's consideration) plus the **S.R. Bommai (1994)** doctrine on the federal limits of **Art. 356** President's Rule.

#### Centre–State Friction in Practice

The **Finance Commission** (quasi-judicial, constituted every 5 years under **Art. 280**) recommends the **vertical** (Centre→States share, currently ~41%) and **horizontal** (among states) revenue split. The **GST Council (Art. 279A)**, chaired by the Union Finance Minister, decides rates by **3/4 weighted majority** — a live tension point because states fear losing fiscal autonomy.

#### Common Mistakes and Exam Traps

| Mistake | Correction |
|---|---|
| Art. 32 and Art. 226 grant same writs | Both grant the **five writs** (habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, certiorari, quo warranto), but Art. 226 also covers non-Fundamental-Right matters |
| Governor holds real executive power | Governor is a **constitutional/nominal head**; **Chief Minister** and Council of Ministers hold real power (Art. 163) |
| Money Bill needs RS concurrence | **No** — RS can only delay 14 days; LS speaker's certification is final |
| DPSPs override Fundamental Rights in court | **No** — Fundamental Rights prevail in direct conflict (Minerva Mills, 1980) |
| XAT GK has a fixed syllabus | **No fixed syllabus** — expect polity + current-affairs blends |

#### Worked Micro-Example

**Question style:** "A parliamentary statute confers absolute discretion on the executive to intercept communications. Which constitutional doctrine is most directly engaged?"

**Reasoning path:** It implicates **Art. 21** (Right to Life and Personal Liberty, including privacy per **K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India, 2017**). The doctrine engaged is the **Basic Structure Doctrine** — privacy and judicial review are part of the unamendable core, so even a parliamentary majority cannot write a statute that destroys them.

#### Practice Prompts

1. Distinguish the **ordinary bill** passage process from the **Money Bill** process, naming each house's powers and the maximum delay each can impose.
2. The **Finance Commission** recommends a higher devolution share to states. Explain how this interacts with **fiscal federalism**, **GST Council** decisions, and the **Basic Structure** doctrine.
