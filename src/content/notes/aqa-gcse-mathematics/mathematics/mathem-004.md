# Geometry and Measures — GCSE Mathematics (AQA 8300) Notes

Geometry and Measures is the third-largest content strand by marks. It spans angle properties, polygons, area and volume, similarity and congruence, Pythagoras, trigonometry, vectors, and transformations. AQA assesses it across all three papers at both tiers, with higher-tier candidates extending into the sine and cosine rules, 3D trigonometry, and transformations of trigonometric graphs.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Angle facts:** angles on a straight line sum to 180°, angles round a point sum to 360°, vertically opposite angles are equal, alternate angles are equal (parallel lines), corresponding angles are equal (parallel lines), co-interior angles sum to 180°.
- **Polygon angles:** exterior angle of a regular n-gon = 360°/n; sum of interior angles of an n-gon = (n − 2) × 180°.
- **Pythagoras:** a² + b² = c², where c is the hypotenuse (the longest side, opposite the right angle).
- **Trigonometry ratios:** sin θ = opposite / hypotenuse; cos θ = adjacent / hypotenuse; tan θ = opposite / adjacent.
- **Area of a circle:** πr². **Circumference:** 2πr.
- **Volume of a prism:** area of cross-section × length.

#### Formulae to remember (higher tier)

- Sine rule: a / sin A = b / sin B = c / sin C
- Cosine rule: a² = b² + c² − 2bc cos A
- Area of a triangle = ½ ab sin C (when two sides and included angle known)

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Angles

**Angle facts** are the bedrock. In any polygon, exterior + interior = 180° at each vertex. For a regular pentagon (n = 5), each exterior = 360°/5 = 72°, each interior = 180° − 72° = 108°, sum = 5 × 108° = 540° — matching (5 − 2) × 180° = 540°.

**Parallel lines.** When a transversal cuts two parallel lines:

- Alternate angles (Z-shape) are equal.
- Corresponding angles (F-shape) are equal.
- Co-interior angles (C-shape) sum to 180°.

#### Triangles, polygons and quadrilaterals

**Triangle types.** Equilateral (all 60°), isosceles (two equal angles), scalene (all different), right-angled (one 90°).

**Quadrilateral types.** Square (4 equal sides, 4 right angles), rectangle (opposite sides equal, 4 right angles), parallelogram (opposite sides parallel, opposite angles equal), rhombus (4 equal sides, opposite angles equal), trapezium (one pair of parallel sides), kite (two pairs of adjacent equal sides).

#### Circles

**Radius** r is the distance from centre to circumference. **Diameter** d = 2r. **Circumference** = 2πr = πd. **Area** = πr².

**Arc length** = (θ/360) × 2πr where θ is the angle at the centre in degrees. **Sector area** = (θ/360) × πr².

#### Area and volume

**Rectangle:** A = lw. **Triangle:** A = ½bh. **Parallelogram:** A = bh. **Trapezium:** A = ½(a + b)h. **Circle:** A = πr².

**Prisms.** Volume = area of cross-section × length. **Cylinder.** Volume = πr²h. **Sphere.** Volume = (4/3)πr³. **Cone.** Volume = (1/3)πr²h. **Pyramid.** Volume = (1/3) × base area × height.

**Surface area** is the sum of all faces. For a cylinder: 2πr² + 2πrh.

#### Similarity and congruence

**Congruent** shapes are identical in shape and size. Four tests: SSS, SAS, ASA, RHS.

**Similar** shapes have the same shape but different size. The ratio of corresponding sides is constant (the scale factor), and the ratio of areas is the scale factor squared. A triangle with sides 3, 4, 5 is similar to one with sides 6, 8, 10 (scale factor 2); the area ratio is 4.

#### Pythagoras

In a right-angled triangle, a² + b² = c² where c is the hypotenuse. To find a side:

- Hypotenuse: c = √(a² + b²)
- A shorter side: a = √(c² − b²)

For 3D problems, apply Pythagoras twice. First find the diagonal of the base, then use that diagonal with the height to find the space diagonal.

#### Trigonometry

For a right-angled triangle with an angle θ at one vertex:

- sin θ = opposite / hypotenuse
- cos θ = adjacent / hypotenuse
- tan θ = opposite / adjacent

To find a side, multiply: opposite = hypotenuse × sin θ. To find an angle, use the inverse function: θ = sin⁻¹(opposite / hypotenuse).

**Exact values** (higher tier):

- sin 30° = 1/2, cos 60° = 1/2, tan 45° = 1
- sin 45° = cos 45° = √2/2
- sin 60° = cos 30° = √3/2

**Sine rule** (for non-right triangles): a / sin A = b / sin B = c / sin C. Use it when you have two angles and one side, or two sides and an angle opposite one of them.

**Cosine rule** (for non-right triangles): a² = b² + c² − 2bc cos A. Use it when you have two sides and the included angle, or all three sides.

#### Vectors

A **vector** has magnitude and direction. It is written as a column (2 4) or as a line (4i + 2j). Two vectors are equal if they have the same magnitude and direction.

**Addition:** (a, b) + (c, d) = (a + c, b + d). **Scalar multiplication:** k(a, b) = (ka, kb). **Magnitude:** |(a, b)| = √(a² + b²).

A vector parallel to another can be written as a scalar multiple of it. The vector (4, 6) is parallel to (2, 3).

#### Transformations

Four transformations map a shape to a new position:

- **Translation:** by a vector.
- **Reflection:** in a line (mirror line).
- **Rotation:** by an angle around a centre.
- **Enlargement:** by a scale factor from a centre.

Higher tier also covers **combinations of transformations** and **negative scale factor enlargements** (the image is on the opposite side of the centre from the original).

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Worked pattern — sine rule with two angles

In triangle ABC, angle A = 65°, angle B = 40°, side a = 12 cm. Find side b.

- Angle C = 180° − 65° − 40° = 75°.
- b / sin B = a / sin A → b = 12 × sin 40° / sin 65° ≈ 12 × 0.6428 / 0.9063 ≈ 8.51 cm.

#### Worked pattern — cosine rule with two sides and included angle

In triangle PQR, p = 7, q = 9, angle P = 60°. Find r.

- r² = p² + q² − 2pq cos P = 49 + 81 − 2(7)(9) cos 60° = 130 − 126 × 0.5 = 130 − 63 = 67.
- r = √67 ≈ 8.19.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Pythagoras works for any triangle." It only works for right-angled triangles. For other triangles use sine rule or cosine rule.
- "sin(2θ) = 2 sin θ." It is not, in general. The correct identity is sin(2θ) = 2 sin θ cos θ.
- "Vectors and scalars are the same." Vectors have direction; scalars do not.
- "An equilateral triangle has all angles 60°." Correct only if it is equilateral AND non-degenerate. A line segment is degenerate and not a triangle.

#### Exam technique

- For Pythagoras and trigonometry problems, always draw and label the triangle first. Identifying the hypotenuse and the opposite/adjacent sides from a diagram prevents sign and ratio errors.
- Show the substitution into the formula before computing. A 3-mark cosine-rule question awards 1 mark for the formula, 1 mark for substitution, 1 mark for the answer.
- Keep π in the answer unless the question asks for a decimal. πr² is exact; 3.14r² is approximate.
