# Indian Banking System and Structure

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The **Reserve Bank of India (RBI)**, constituted under the RBI Act, 1934 and operational from 1 April 1935, sits at the apex of the Indian Banking System and regulates all banks under the **Banking Regulation Act, 1949**. Banks whose names appear in the **Second Schedule of the RBI Act** are *Scheduled Commercial Banks* and must maintain the Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR); they are classified by ownership into **Public Sector Banks (PSBs)**, **Private Sector Banks**, **Foreign Banks**, and **Regional Rural Banks (RRBs)**.

- **PSBs** are banks where the Government of India holds more than 50% equity (e.g., SBI, PNB, Bank of Baroda); 14 were nationalised in 1969 and 6 in 1980.
- **RRBs** were created under the RRB Act, 1976, owned 50% Central Government, 35% Sponsor Bank, 15% State Government.
- **Payment Banks** (deposit cap Rs 2 lakh, no lending) and **Small Finance Banks** (75% priority-sector lending) were introduced on the **Nachiket Mor Committee (2013)** recommendations.

> **High-Yield Tip:** Memorise the RBI founding year (1935), DICGC insurance limit (Rs 5 lakh, revised in Feb 2020), and the priority-sector lending target of **40%** of ANBC.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Apex and Regulatory Architecture

The RBI functions as the central bank, monetary authority, currency issuer, and supervisor of the banking system. Adjacent regulators handle other segments: **SEBI** (1992) for securities, **IRDAI** for insurance, **PFRDA** for pensions, **NABARD** (1982) for agricultural and rural credit, and **SIDBI** (1990) for MSMEs. **DICGC**, an RBI subsidiary established in 1962, insures each depositor up to Rs 5 lakh per bank.

#### Classification of Banks

| Criterion | Categories | Key Distinction |
|---|---|---|
| RBI Schedule status | Scheduled / Non-Scheduled | Scheduled banks maintain CRR with RBI |
| Ownership | PSB / Private Indian / Foreign | PSB = >50% Govt equity |
| Function | Universal / Differentiated (Payment / SFB) | Payment banks cannot lend |
| Geography | Commercial / Regional Rural / Local Area | RRBs operate in notified districts |

#### Selected Prudential Formulas

- **CRR %** = (Cash with RBI / NDTL) × 100
- **SLR %** = (Liquid assets in gold, govt securities, cash / NDTL) × 100
- **Net NPA Ratio %** = (Gross NPA − Provisions) / Net Advances × 100
- **CRAR %** = (Tier 1 + Tier 2 Capital) / Risk-Weighted Assets × 100 (RBI minimum 9% under Basel III)

#### Reforms and NPA Norms

The **Narasimham Committee I (1991)** ended interest-rate controls and opened the sector, while **Narasimham II (1998)** strengthened asset-quality norms. The 2020 RBI circular harmonised the NPA trigger to **180 days past due (DPD)** across all categories, superseding older sectoral timelines.

> **Exam Pointer:** IBPS PO Prelims typically asks 1–3 questions here, often as a one-liner like "Headquarters of NABARD" or "Deposit cap of Payment Banks." Focus on year, headquarters, and cap-based numbers.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Frequently Confused Pairs

Several pairs of concepts are deliberately close, making them examiner favourites. **NBFCs** are registered under the Companies Act, cannot accept *demand deposits*, and do not issue cheques — so they fall outside the Banking Regulation Act and DICGC cover. **Foreign banks** must apply separately for Scheduled status even if they operate a branch in India.

#### Differentiated Banks in Detail

- **Payment Banks** (deposit cap Rs 2 lakh, remittance up to Rs 25,000 per transaction, no CRR exemption, 75% of deposits in government securities) were licensed from 2015 onward via recommendations of the **Nachiket Mor Committee (2013)**.
- **Small Finance Banks** (Khan Committee, 2014) must lend **75% of ANBC to Priority Sectors** and maintain a minimum capital of Rs 200 crore, scaling to Rs 500 crore within five years.

#### Common Mistakes in the Exam Hall

> **Trap 1:** Treating all Scheduled Banks as Public Sector Banks — incorrect, because ICICI, HDFC, and Citibank are Scheduled but privately owned.
> **Trap 2:** Assuming RRBs are Cooperative Banks — RRBs are scheduled commercial banks under the RRB Act, 1976, not under state cooperative legislation.
> **Trap 3:** Confusing DICGC's Rs 5 lakh cover (per depositor per bank) with unlimited insurance; the cover rose from Rs 1 lakh to Rs 5 lakh effective 4 February 2020.

#### Worked Linkage

A single IBPS PO Prelims question can chain three facts: "Which body, established in 1962 under the Companies Act, insures deposits up to Rs 5 lakh per bank?" → Answer: **DICGC**, RBI subsidiary, Rs 5 lakh limit. Note that DICGC is *not* the regulator of the bank itself — that is the RBI's role under Section 35A of the Banking Regulation Act.

#### Practice Prompts

1. A 2014-licensed bank must keep 75% of its ANBC in Priority Sectors and a minimum capital of Rs 200 crore. Identify the bank type and the committee that recommended it.
2. "Banks listed in the Second Schedule of the RBI Act must maintain CRR but cannot be supervised by the RBI." State whether the statement is true or false, and justify with the relevant section of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949.
