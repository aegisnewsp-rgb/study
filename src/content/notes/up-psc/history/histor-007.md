# Medieval India: The Delhi Sultanate (1206–1526)

## UPPSC PCS Examination Blueprint and Historical Scope

In the Uttar Pradesh Public Service Commission (UPPSC PCS) Combined State / Upper Subordinate Services Examination, Medieval Indian History forms an indispensable component of Preliminary General Studies Paper 1 (accounting for 5 to 7 direct questions) and Main Examination General Studies Paper 1. Questions on the Delhi Sultanate (1206–1526 CE) rarely focus on simple battle dates. Instead, UPPSC examiners consistently probe four specific areas:
1. **Administrative Institutions and Terminology**: Departmental portfolios (such as *Diwan-i-Arz*, *Diwan-i-Insha*, and *Diwan-i-Kohi*) and official revenue terms (*Iqta*, *Khalsa*, *Kharaj*, *Charai*, and *Ghari*).
2. **Agrarian and Market Control Regulations**: Particularly the land measurement systems (*Masahat* and *Biswa*) and market surveillance mechanisms (*Shahna-i-Mandi* and *Sarai-Adl*) instituted by Alauddin Khalji.
3. **Theocratic Ideology and Royal Monarchy**: Ghiyas-ud-din Balban's theory of kingship (*Niyabat-i-Khudai* and *Zill-i-Ilahi*), the destruction of the *Turkan-i-Chahalgani* (the Forty), and Persian court etiquette (*Sijda* and *Paibos*).
4. **Primary Historical Chronicles and Foreign Accounts**: Authors, patron rulers, and source texts (including Minhaj-i-Siraj, Ziauddin Barani, Amir Khusrau, and Ibn Battuta's *Rihla*).

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> High-yield facts, chronologies, and administrative terms for rapid pre-exam revision.

In our analysis of past UPPSC question papers, candidates often lose marks by confusing the administrative reforms of Alauddin Khalji with those of Muhammad bin Tughlaq or Firuz Shah Tughlaq. Memorize this chronological dynasty sequence and institutional checklist first.

#### The Five Sultanate Dynasties at a Glance

- **Mamluk / Slave Dynasty (1206–1290 CE)**: Founded by Qutb-ud-din Aibak; consolidated by Iltutmish; absolute monarchy restored by Balban.
- **Khalji Dynasty (1290–1320 CE)**: Founded by Jalal-ud-din Khalji; imperial expansion and radical market controls under Alauddin Khalji.
- **Tughlaq Dynasty (1320–1414 CE)**: Founded by Ghiyas-ud-din Tughlaq; visionary administrative experiments under Muhammad bin Tughlaq; orthodox public works and canal networks under Firuz Shah Tughlaq.
- **Sayyid Dynasty (1414–1451 CE)**: Established by Khizr Khan under the nominal suzerainty of Timur; marked by declining centralized power.
- **Lodi Dynasty (1451–1526 CE)**: First Afghan dynasty in India; founded by Bahlul Lodi; architectural transition and Agra founded by Sikandar Lodi; ended at the First Battle of Panipat (1526).

## Dynastic Evolution and Structural Reforms

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Systematic study of political consolidation, administrative architecture, and socioeconomic developments.

When preparing for the UPPSC Mains and Prelims, candidates should trace how Sultanate governance evolved from an unstable military occupation into a centralized institutional empire.

#### 1. The Mamluk Dynasty (1206–1290 CE)
- **Qutb-ud-din Aibak (1206–1210 CE)**: A Turkish slave and general of Muhammad Ghori, Aibak ruled from Lahore. Known as *Lakh Baksh* (giver of lakhs) for his generosity. He commenced construction of the *Qutub Minar* in Delhi (named after Sufi saint Khwaja Qutbuddin Bakhtiyar Kaki) and built the *Quwwat-ul-Islam* mosque in Delhi and *Adhai Din Ka Jhonpra* in Ajmer. He died from a fall during a game of *Chaugan* (horse polo).
- **Shams-ud-din Iltutmish (1211–1236 CE)**: Regarded by historians as the true consolidator of the Delhi Sultanate. He shifted the capital from Lahore to Delhi, obtained formal investiture from the Abbasid Caliph of Baghdad (in 1229 CE), and introduced the pure Arabic coinage system: the silver *Tanka* (175 grains) and copper *Jital*. He organized the nobility into the *Turkan-i-Chahalgani* (corps of forty Turkish slave-officers) and institutionalized the *Iqta* system (granting revenue assignments to officers in exchange for military service). He saved the nascent Sultanate from Mongol conqueror Genghis Khan in 1221 CE by politely refusing asylum to Jalal-ud-din Mangabarni, the Shah of Khwarizm.
- **Razia Sultan (1236–1240 CE)**: Nominated by Iltutmish over her incapable brothers, Razia was the first and only Muslim woman to occupy the throne of Delhi. She abandoned the purdah, donned the male robe (*Qaba*) and riding hat (*Kulah*), and personally led armies. Her assertiveness antagonized the conservative Turkish *Chahalgani*, who conspired with Altunia (governor of Bhatinda) to unseat and execute her near Kaithal in 1240 CE.
- **Ghiyas-ud-din Balban (1266–1287 CE)**: A prominent member of the *Chahalgani*, Balban rose through merit and usurped the throne following decades of factional anarchy. He introduced his celebrated **Blood and Iron Policy** (*Lauh evam Rakta*) to suppress Mewati bandits in the Doab and eliminate rebellious Turkish aristocrats. Balban formulated a rigorous Persian-inspired Theory of Kingship: the Sultan was God's shadow on earth (*Zill-i-Ilahi*) and the recipient of divine grace (*Niyabat-i-Khudai*). To humble the proud Turkish nobles, he instituted mandatory Persian court etiquette: prostration before the throne (*Sijda*) and kissing the monarch's feet (*Paibos*). He established the first dedicated military intelligence spy service (*Barid-i-Mamalik*).

#### 2. The Khalji Revolution and Imperial Centralization (1290–1320 CE)
The accession of Jalal-ud-din Khalji in 1290 CE ended the Turkish aristocratic monopoly over state offices, opening administrative ranks to non-Turks and Indian converts.

##### The Reforms of Alauddin Khalji (1296–1316 CE)
Alauddin Khalji expanded the empire through aggressive campaigns in Gujarat (1299), Ranthambore (1301), Chittor (1303), and Malwa (1305). His military commander, Malik Kafur (the eunuch general purchased in Gujarat for a thousand dinars, hence called *Hazar-Dinari*), led southern expeditions that subdued the Yadavas of Devagiri, Kakatiyas of Warangal, Hoysalas of Dwarasamudra, and Pandyas of Madurai.

```
Alauddin Khalji's Four Pillars of Governance:
├── Military Efficiency ── Cash salaries (234 tankas/year) + Dag (horse branding) + Huliya (soldier muster roll)
├── Agrarian Control ── Measurement by Masahat + Standard Biswa unit + Direct collection from cultivators
├── Market Regulation ── Shahna-i-Mandi + Fixed commodity prices + Elimination of middlemen hoarders
└── Internal Security ── Total liquor prohibition + Ban on noble gatherings + Omnipresent Barid intelligence
```

#### Agrarian and Market Control Regulations

| Policy Sphere | Key Institution / Term | Functional Operation | UPPSC Examination Relevance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Land Measurement** | *Masahat* & *Biswa* | State measured cultivated acreage directly using standard units | Ended arbitrary tax estimations by local *Muqaddams* |
| **State Revenue Rate** | *Kharaj* at 50% | Fixed state demand at half the gross agricultural produce | Highest standardized tax rate under the Sultanate |
| **House and Pasture Tax** | *Ghari* and *Charai* | Levied on residential dwellings (*Ghari*) and milch animals (*Charai*) | Frequently tested unique revenue sources |
| **Grain Market** | *Mandi* | Controlled grain trade under superintendent *Shahna-i-Mandi* | Guaranteed steady food supply to army in Delhi |
| **Manufactured Goods** | *Sarai-Adl* | Open cloth, sugar, and oil exchange inside the Badaun Gate | Subsidized luxury imports under state licenses |
| **Espionage System** | *Munhiyan* | Secret child and adult spies reporting unauthorized price hikes | Ensured ironclad enforcement of retail price lists |

## The Tughlaqs, Sayyids, and Lodis

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> In-depth administrative experiments, agrarian public works, and late Sultanate fragmentation.

In our analysis of UPPSC Mains GS-1 questions, candidates are frequently asked to assess the character and policy blunders of Muhammad bin Tughlaq versus the constructive public works of Firuz Shah Tughlaq.

#### 1. Muhammad bin Tughlaq's Five Ambitious Experiments (1325–1351 CE)
Muhammad bin Tughlaq was a polymath versed in mathematics, astronomy, medicine, logic, and philosophy. However, his visionary schemes lacked pragmatic execution:
1. **Taxation in the Doab (1326 CE)**: The Sultan increased revenue demands in the fertile Ganga-Yamuna Doab to fund imperial campaigns. Unfortunately, the decree coincided with a devastating regional drought and famine. Peasant uprisings ensued, which the state crushed harshly, leading to widespread abandonment of farmland.
2. **Transfer of Capital to Daulatabad (1327 CE)**: To govern newly conquered Deccan territories and protect the court from recurring Mongol raids, the Sultan ordered the entire imperial court and population of Delhi to relocate to Devagiri (renamed Daulatabad) in Maharashtra. The arduous 1,500 km trek caused immense suffering and loss of life. Recognizing that the northern frontier could not be defended from the Deccan, he reversed the order two years later, marching the survivors back to Delhi.
3. **Introduction of Token Currency (1329–1330 CE)**: Inspired by token paper currency (*Chao*) in Yuan China under Kublai Khan, Muhammad bin Tughlaq issued bronze and copper tokens with the legal parity of silver *Tankas*. However, the state failed to establish a royal mint monopoly or complex mint marks. Every Hindu goldsmith and goldsmith household began minting counterfeit tokens (*Dar-ul-Zarb* in every home). Foreign merchants refused the token coins, crippling commerce. The Sultan honorably recalled the copper coins, redeeming them with authentic gold and silver from the imperial treasury.
4. **The Khurasan Project (1330 CE)**: Aimed at conquering central Asian territories destabilized by Mongol succession wars, the Sultan raised an army of 370,000 men, paying them a full year's cash salary in advance. When geopolitical conditions stabilized, the costly expedition was abandoned without a fight.
5. **The Qarachil Expedition (1333 CE)**: Dispatched into the Kumaon-Garhwal Himalayan region to secure the northern trade route. While initial mountain outposts fell, monsoon rains and guerrilla ambushes decimated the retreating Sultanate forces.

To rehabilitate agriculture after the Doab famine, the Sultan established the **Diwan-i-Amir-Kohi** (department of agriculture). He distributed state agricultural loans called *Sondhar* or *Takkavi* to peasants for purchasing seeds, digging wells, and switching to high-value cash crops.

#### 2. Firuz Shah Tughlaq's Paternalistic Administration (1351–1388 CE)
Unlike his cousin, Firuz Shah sought the reconciliation of the orthodox *Ulema* and noble aristocracy:
- He abolished 24 non-Sharia taxes (*Abwabs*), retaining only four Quranic taxes: *Kharaj* (land tax at 10%), *Jizya* (poll tax on non-Muslims, which he separated from *Kharaj* and imposed on Brahmins for the first time), *Zakat* (2.5% wealth tax on Muslims for charity), and *Khams* (one-fifth of war booty reserved for the state, reversing Alauddin's 80% state retention).
- He pioneered extensive **Canal Irrigation Networks** across northern India: the Yamuna-Hissar canal, the Sutlej-Ghaggar canal, and the Sirhind canal. Beneficiaries paid a water cess (*Haqq-i-Sharb*) amounting to 10% of produce.
- He founded new cities including **Firozabad** (Delhi), **Jaunpur** (named in memory of Muhammad bin Tughlaq, whose real name was Jauna Khan), **Hissar**, and **Fatehabad**.
- He established state welfare institutions: **Dar-ul-Shafa** (free charitable hospital in Delhi), **Diwan-i-Khairat** (financial aid for marriage of poor girls), and **Diwan-i-Bandagan** (maintaining 180,000 royal slaves trained in various crafts).

#### 3. The Sayyids and Lodis (1414–1526 CE)
Following Timur's catastrophic sack of Delhi in 1398 CE, the Sultanate splintered into regional sultanates (Jaunpur, Malwa, Gujarat, Bengal, and Bahmani).
- **Sikandar Lodi (1489–1517 CE)**: The greatest of the Lodi rulers. He shifted his operational capital to **Agra** in 1504 CE to monitor Rajput chiefs. He introduced the standardized land measurement gauge known as **Gaz-i-Sikandari** (32 digits / 30 inches), which remained in use until Akbar's reign. He was a patron of learning and composed Persian verses under the pen name *Gulrukhi*.
- **Ibrahim Lodi (1517–1526 CE)**: An autocratic ruler who alienated his Afghan tribal chiefs (who viewed the Sultan as merely first among equals). Disaffected nobles, notably Daulat Khan Lodi (governor of Punjab) and Alam Khan (Ibrahim's uncle), invited Zahir-ud-din Muhammad Babur, the ruler of Kabul, to invade India. At the **First Battle of Panipat** on 21 April 1526, Babur employed Ottoman wheel tactics (*Tulughma*) and field artillery (*Rumi* cart formation) to crush Ibrahim Lodi, bringing the Delhi Sultanate to an end and inaugurating the Mughal Empire.

## High-Yield UPPSC PCS Previous Year Practice Stems

#### Practice Question 1 (UPPSC Prelims 2019)
**Question:** Which Sultan of Delhi established a separate department for agriculture (*Diwan-i-Amir-Kohi*)?
- (A) Iltutmish
- (B) Balban
- (C) Alauddin Khalji
- (D) Muhammad bin Tughlaq
*Answer:* **(D)**. Muhammad bin Tughlaq established the *Diwan-i-Amir-Kohi* to bring uncultivated land under crops by advancing *Takkavi* loans to cultivators.

#### Practice Question 2 (UPPSC Prelims 2021)
**Question:** Who among the following sultans was the first to levy *Haqq-i-Sharb* or irrigation tax?
- (A) Alauddin Khalji
- (B) Ghiyas-ud-din Tughlaq
- (C) Muhammad bin Tughlaq
- (D) Firuz Shah Tughlaq
*Answer:* **(D)**. Firuz Shah Tughlaq constructed an extensive canal network and charged *Haqq-i-Sharb* (water tax) at 10% of total agricultural yield with the approval of religious jurists.

#### Practice Question 3 (UPPSC Prelims 2023)
**Question:** Consider the following statements regarding the market reforms of Alauddin Khalji:
1. He established a separate market called *Sarai-Adl* for manufactured clothes, sugar, herbs, and oils.
2. The officer in charge of the grain market was known as *Shahna-i-Mandi*.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
- (A) 1 only
- (B) 2 only
- (C) Both 1 and 2
- (D) Neither 1 nor 2
*Answer:* **(C)**. Both statements are accurate historical facts regarding Alauddin Khalji's four market divisions in Delhi.
