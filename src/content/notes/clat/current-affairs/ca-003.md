# Legal News

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Legal News for CLAT covers recent Indian judicial, legislative, and constitutional developments from the **12-month window** before the exam. It sits inside the Current Affairs & GK section and carries roughly **5%** of total marks. The three new criminal codes — **BNS, BNSS, BSA** — replaced the IPC, CrPC and Indian Evidence Act from **1 July 2024**, so section numbers cited in any 2024-25 question are almost always the new ones.

- Read **The Hindu** or **Indian Express** daily, plus one law outlet such as **LiveLaw** or **Bar & Bench**.
- Track **Supreme Court** and **High Court** landmark rulings, **Parliament bills** with presidential assent, and **Article 123 ordinances**.
- For each item, note: **issue, bench strength, provision interpreted, and relief granted** — not the political headline.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### What "Legal News" tests on CLAT

The Consortium of NLUs frames Legal News as part of the static GK + dynamic current affairs blend. About **25 of 120** CLAT questions belong to Current Affairs, of which roughly **6** hinge directly on legal awareness. Three formats recur: factual MCQs (e.g. "Which section of BNS deals with …?"), comprehension passages drawn from a recent judgment, and principle-application sets that drop a new ruling into a hypothetical.

#### Reading the three new criminal codes correctly

The **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS)** is the substantive penal code, the **Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS)** governs procedure and investigation, and the **Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA)** is the law of evidence. All three took effect on **1 July 2024**. Mapping old to new sections is the single highest-yield skill: murder moved from **IPC 302** to **BNS 302**, rape from **IPC 376** to **BNS 63**, and cheating from **IPC 420** to **BNS 318**.

| Old code | New code | Subject | New section |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| IPC | BNS | Murder | 302 |
| IPC | BNS | Rape | 63 |
| IPC | BNS | Cheating | 318 |
| CrPC | BNSS | FIR registration | 154 |
| Indian Evidence Act | BSA | Documentary evidence | 74 |

#### Distinguishing binding law from judicial commentary

A Supreme Court judgment contains **ratio decidendi** — the binding legal principle on the facts — and **obiter dicta**, which are persuasive observations. CLAT answer keys reward the ratio, not the headline. Always note **bench strength** (single judge, division bench, constitution bench) because a ruling by a larger bench overrides a smaller one under **Article 141**.

- **Article 32** — writ jurisdiction of the Supreme Court for fundamental rights.
- **Article 226** — wider writ jurisdiction of High Courts against any authority.
- **Article 142** — complete justice power of the Supreme Court.
- **Article 123** — promulgation of ordinances by the Union when Parliament is not in session.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Mapping news to subject heads

A reliable revision method is to file every legal item under one of ten subject heads: **criminal, constitutional, commercial, environmental, family, labour, tax, cyber, IPR, and ADR**. This mirrors how the Consortium clusters passage-based questions and prevents you from missing recurring themes such as **PMLA** attachments, **PIL** admissions under **Article 32**, or **NALSA**-mediated mediation schemes.

#### Common traps in the exam hall

- Treating a **Law Commission Report** or **Standing Committee recommendation** as enacted law — only a Gazette notification makes a statute operative.
- Reporting interim orders as final judgments. PILs are often *admitted* long before they are *disposed of*.
- Quoting IPC sections after **1 July 2024** when the matter actually travels under the BNS.
- Citing the political slug of a ruling instead of the **legal issue, statutory provision, and relief** granted.

#### Worked micro-example

Suppose a CLAT passage states: "The Supreme Court held that the State's power under Section 9 of the BSA to summon a witness is subject to the right against self-incrimination." Your answer should identify **(i)** the bench strength, **(ii)** the **BSA provision** interpreted, **(iii)** the **Article 20(3)** right engaged, and **(iv)** the **ratio** — not the political reaction. A model 60-word answer names the provision, the article, and the binding principle.

| Revision checklist per item | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Date of judgment | Confirms it falls in the 12-month window |
| Court and bench strength | Decides binding value under Article 141 |
| Statute or article interpreted | Lets you apply it to a new fact pattern |
| Relief granted | Distinguishes interim from final orders |

#### Practice prompts

1. Frame a two-mark factual MCQ on the **BNS section dealing with organised crime** and justify the correct option.
2. Draft a 60-word note on the **abrogation of Article 370** covering the **constitution bench ruling, the reorganization Act, and the ongoing legal status of Article 35A** — without quoting any political slogan.
