# Banking Awareness - RBI and Monetary Policy

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The **Reserve Bank of India (RBI)** is India's central bank, established on 1 April 1935 under the **RBI Act, 1934** and nationalised on 1 January 1949. It manages the **monetary policy** framework under **Flexible Inflation Targeting (FIT)**, aiming at a **CPI inflation target of 4% with a ±2% tolerance band**, set by the Central Government in consultation with RBI under Section 45ZA.

- **MPC composition:** 6 members — 3 from RBI (Governor as Chair, Deputy Governor in charge of monetary policy, one RBI Board nominee) + 3 external experts appointed by the Central Government; meets at least 4 times a year; majority vote with Governor holding the casting vote.
- **LAF corridor (top to bottom):** MSF (ceiling) ≥ Repo Rate (policy rate) ≥ SDF (floor, since April 2022, set 25 bps below repo) → Reverse Repo Rate.
- **Quantitative tools:** Bank Rate, Repo Rate, Reverse Repo, MSF, SDF, CRR, SLR, Open Market Operations (OMO).
- **Qualitative tools:** margin requirements, moral suasion, priority-sector directives, consumer credit control.
- **CRAR (Basel III):** (Tier 1 + Tier 2) ÷ Risk-Weighted Assets × 100; RBI norm = 9% + 2.5% Capital Conservation Buffer = 11.5%.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"MSF on top, SDF at bottom, Repo rules the middle"** — recall the Liquidity Adjustment Facility corridor ceiling-floor-policy ladder. Pair it with **"Banks must Carry Cash Reserves and Stock Liquidity"** to remember CRR (cash with RBI) vs SLR (stocks/securities/gold held by bank).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Institutional Architecture of RBI

RBI operates from its central office in Mumbai with regional offices and 31 issue offices handling currency chests. Its statutory powers flow from three layers: the **RBI Act, 1934** (institution, currency, reserve management), the **Banking Regulation Act, 1949** (bank supervision, licensing, NPA norms), and the **Finance Act, 2016 amendment** which inserted Sections 45ZA–45ZO giving RBI statutory inflation targeting and an explicit MPC.

RBI performs four core public functions:
1. **Monetary Authority** — formulates and implements policy via the MPC.
2. **Issuer of Currency** — issues and destroys banknotes (except ₹1 coin/note, handled by Ministry of Finance); manages the **Subsidiary General Ledger (SGL)** for government securities.
3. **Regulator of the Banking System** — licensing, inspection, prudential norms under BASEL, NPA classification, NBFC supervision.
4. **Manager of Foreign Exchange** — administers FEMA, 1999 and manages the forex reserves.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix

| Concept | Definition | Legal Basis | Key Distinction |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Repo Rate** | Rate at which RBI lends short-term funds to banks against G-Sec collateral | RBI Act + LAF operating procedure | Policy rate; benchmark for external lending |
| **Reverse Repo Rate** | Rate at which RBI absorbs overnight liquidity from banks | LAF corridor floor (until 2022) | Earlier corridor floor; now superseded by SDF |
| **Bank Rate** | Long-term discount/rediscount rate under Section 49 of RBI Act | Section 49, RBI Act, 1934 | Aligned with MSF; used for long-term refinance |
| **MSF** | Penal overnight borrowing window up to 1% of deposits (raised to 2% post-2020) | LAF emergency window | Coridor ceiling; only scheduled commercial banks |
| **SDF** | Uncollateralised overnight deposit facility | Introduced April 2022 | New corridor floor; 25 bps below repo |
| **CRR** | % of NDTL kept as cash balance with RBI in current account | Section 42(1), RBI Act | No interest paid since 2007 |
| **SLR** | % of NDTL held as liquid assets (G-Sec, gold, cash) | Section 24, Banking Regulation Act, 1949 | Maintained daily by bank itself |

#### Monetary Policy Transmission

The MPC's repo decision transmits to the economy through:
- **Call money rate** interbank overnight market, weighted by volume: WACR ≈ Σ(Amountᵢ × Rateᵢ) ÷ Σ Amountᵢ, expressed in % per annum.
- **Money supply aggregates** — M0 (reserve money = CIC + bankers' deposits + other deposits with RBI), M1 (narrow money), M3 (broad money = M1 + time deposits), all in ₹ crore (dimension [M]).
- **Money multiplier** m = (1 + c) ÷ (c + r + e), where c = currency-deposit ratio, r = reserve ratio (CRR+SLR holdings), e = excess reserves ratio; then Money Supply M = m × Monetary Base.
- **Bank lending rates** — since 2019, all new floating-rate personal and MSME loans must link to an external benchmark (Repo, 3M/6M T-Bill, or FBIL benchmark).

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** Under the LAF corridor, if the Repo Rate is 6.50%, MSF is 6.75%, Reverse Repo Rate is 3.35%, and the Standing Deposit Facility rate is 6.25%, identify which rate acts as the **ceiling** and **floor** of the corridor. If inflation is at 5.8% and the nominal policy rate is 6.50%, calculate the **approximate real interest rate** using the Fisher relation.

#### Solution:
- The **ceiling** of the LAF corridor is **MSF = 6.75%** (highest rate, penal emergency window).
- The **floor** of the LAF corridor is **SDF = 6.25%** (lowest rate, introduced in April 2022; below the older Reverse Repo Rate of 3.35%, which is no longer the corridor floor).
- Real Interest Rate ≈ Nominal − Inflation = 6.50% − 5.8% = **0.70 percentage points**.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Many aspirants still pick Reverse Repo Rate (3.35%) as the corridor floor. After April 2022, the **SDF replaced Reverse Repo** as the effective floor of the LAF corridor. Also, the Fisher equation gives an *approximation*; precise real rate = (1 + i) ÷ (1 + π) − 1, but for UPPSC MCQs the linear approximation is expected.

#### Capital Adequacy and PSL

#### Capital to Risk-Weighted Assets Ratio (CRAR):
CRAR = (Tier 1 Capital + Tier 2 Capital) ÷ Risk-Weighted Assets × 100. RBI requires a minimum **9% + 2.5% Capital Conservation Buffer = 11.5%** under Basel III, plus sector-specific buffers (D-SIB, G-SIB framework).

#### Priority Sector Lending (PSL) targets:
- 40% of Adjusted Net Bank Credit (ANBC) or credit-equivalent of off-balance-sheet exposure, whichever is higher.
- Sub-targets: Agriculture, MSME, Education, Housing, Social Infrastructure, Export Credit, Renewable Energy.
- Weaker Sections: 10%; within MSME allocation (7.5% for small/micro enterprises).

**NPA classification (IRAC norms):** Asset becomes NPA when interest/installment is overdue >90 days (180 days for select categories); categorised Sub-standard → Doubtful (D1, D2, D3) → Loss asset.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Flexible Inflation Targeting — Legal Mechanics

The **Finance Act, 2016** amended the RBI Act to insert a statutory inflation target, replacing the multiple-indicator approach that operated between 2008 and 2016. The **Central Government**, in consultation with RBI, sets the target for a period of five years. Once inflation breaches the ±2% band for **three consecutive quarters**, the RBI must:
1. Submit a **Report to the Central Government** under Section 45ZN stating causes, remedial actions, and an estimated time to return.
2. The Central Government may issue a **directions to RBI** if it considers necessary — but this is rare and politically sensitive.

> 📌 **Formula Check:** Inflation Rate (%) = [(CPI_current − CPI_base) ÷ CPI_base] × 100, where the CPI base year is **2011-12 = 100** (revised by MoSPI). Result is dimensionless expressed as %.

#### LAF Corridor Evolution and Liquidity Tools

| Tool | Function | Direction of Liquidity |
|---|---|---|
| **Repo Auction** | RBI lends against G-Sec | Injection |
| **Reverse Repo Auction** | RBI absorbs funds from banks | Absorption |
| **MSF** | Overnight penal borrowing window | Injection (emergency) |
| **SDF** | Overnight uncollateralised deposit | Absorption (since Apr 2022) |
| **Open Market Operations (OMO)** | Outright purchase/sale of G-Sec | Durable injection / absorption |
| **Market Stabilisation Scheme (MSS)** | Issuance of T-Bills/SLR securities to absorb surplus | Sterilisation |
| **Operation Twist** | Simultaneous buy long-dated, sell short-dated G-Sec | Flattens yield curve |
| **Variable Rate Reverse Repo (VRRR)** | Longer-tenure absorption | Structural tightening |

> 💡 **High-Yield Revision Hook:** Distinguish **tactical tools** (daily LAF/MSF/SDF — short-tenor, fine-tuning) from **structural tools** (CRR, SLR, OMO, MSS — longer-tenor, durable impact). UPPSC frequently tests the tactical-structural split.

#### NPA Resolution Framework

1. **IRAC Norms** — Income Recognition and Asset Classification (master circular updated annually).
2. **SARFAESI Act, 2002** — allows secured creditors to seize and sell collateral without court intervention.
3. **IBC, 2016** — Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code provides time-bound resolution through NCLT/NCLAT; CIRP within 180 days (extendable to 330 days).
4. **National Asset Reconstruction Company (NARCL)** — bad bank set up in 2021 to aggregate stressed assets.
5. **5/25 scheme, S4A, JLF (Joint Lenders' Forum)** — older frameworks now largely retired.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** The 90-day NPA norm applies to most advances, but for **agriculture loans** the overdue period is **two crop seasons (short duration) or one crop season (long duration)** — not 90 days. Aspirants who write "90 days for all NPAs" lose marks.

#### Fiscal Deficit Monetisation Prohibition

Under the amended **RBI Act (Section 47, post-1997 amendment)**, RBI cannot directly finance the Centre's fiscal deficit by subscribing to primary issuances of government securities — the so-called **"prohibition on monetary financing of fiscal deficit."** RBI may, however, participate in the **secondary market** for liquidity management.

> 📌 **Formula Check:** **Money Multiplier** m = (1 + c) ÷ (c + r + e). A 1 percentage-point rise in CRR (the 'r' component) compresses the multiplier and reduces broad money M3 for a given reserve base — this is why CRR changes are structural, not tactical.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. **Numerical:** If CRR is 4.5%, SLR is 18%, currency-deposit ratio (c) = 0.4, excess reserves ratio (e) = 0.02, compute the money multiplier and discuss how a 50 basis-point CRR cut alters broad money supply M3 given a reserve base of ₹12 lakh crore. *(Hint: recompute r, then m, then M = m × MB.)*
2. **Conceptual:** RBI raises the repo rate by 25 bps. Trace the **step-by-step transmission** to (a) call money rate, (b) bank MCLR, (c) floating-rate retail loan EMI under the 2019 external benchmark regime, and (d) aggregate demand. Identify the typical lag observed in Indian data (often 3–6 quarters).
