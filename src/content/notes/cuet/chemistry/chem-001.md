# Some Basic Concepts

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Some Basic Concepts of Chemistry** is the CUET UG unit that grounds every later numerical problem in **stoichiometry**, the **mole**, and unit handling. The single most-tested relationship is the mole triangle: `n = given mass (g) / molar mass (g mol⁻¹)`, and `N = n × N_A`, where `N_A = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹`.

- **Molar volume at STP** = 22.4 L per mole of an ideal gas (0 °C, 1 atm).
- **Molarity (M)** = moles solute / volume of solution (L); **Molality (m)** = moles solute / mass of solvent (kg).
- **Molecular formula** = `n × (empirical formula)`, where `n = molecular mass / empirical formula mass`.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Classification of Matter and Dalton's Theory

Matter divides into **pure substances** (elements, compounds) and **mixtures** (homogeneous — uniform composition like salt water; heterogeneous — visibly distinct phases like sand in water). Mixtures are separated by physical methods (filtration, distillation), whereas compounds require chemical breakdown.

Dalton's atomic theory (1808) proposed that atoms are indivisible, identical for a given element, and combine in fixed whole-number ratios. Two modern corrections: atoms are divisible (subatomic particles exist), and isotopes mean atoms of the same element can differ in mass.

#### Atomic, Molecular, and Formula Mass

| Quantity | Definition | Unit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Atomic mass** | Weighted average of isotope masses, relative to C-12 = 12 exactly | amu (u) |
| **Molecular mass** | Sum of atomic masses of atoms in one molecule | amu (u) |
| **Formula unit mass** | Sum for one formula unit of an ionic compound (e.g., NaCl) | amu (u) |
| **Molar mass** | Mass of one mole of substance | g mol⁻¹ |

#### The Mole and Stoichiometry

One **mole** contains exactly `6.02214076 × 10²³` elementary entities. The mole is the bridge between mass, number of particles, and gas volume at STP. In balanced-equation problems, identify the **limiting reagent** by dividing each reactant's moles by its stoichiometric coefficient — the smallest ratio runs out first.

#### Concentration Terms

- **Mass percent (w/w %)** = (mass solute / mass solution) × 100 — used in commercial chemistry.
- **Mole fraction (x_A)** = n_A / (n_A + n_B) — used in gas-law and colligative-property work.
- **Molarity** = moles solute / litres of solution — temperature-dependent.
- **Molality** = moles solute / kilograms of solvent — temperature-independent, preferred in ΔT_f and ΔT_b problems.

> CUET trap: molarity and molality are numerically close only in dilute aqueous solutions; outside that, the density difference between solute and solvent changes the answer.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Worked Example — Empirical to Molecular Formula

A compound contains 40.00 % C, 6.67 % H, 53.33 % O by mass, and its molecular mass is 180 g mol⁻¹.

1. In 100 g: n(C) = 40.00 / 12 = 3.33; n(H) = 6.67 / 1 = 6.67; n(O) = 53.33 / 16 = 3.33.
2. Divide by smallest (3.33): C : H : O = 1 : 2 : 1 → **empirical formula CH₂O** (mass = 30).
3. n = 180 / 30 = 6 → **molecular formula C₆H₁₂O₆** (glucose).

#### Limiting Reagent — Worked Logic

For 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O with 4 mol H₂ and 1 mol O₂: H₂ ratio = 4/2 = 2; O₂ ratio = 1/1 = 1. **O₂ is limiting**; the smaller ratio wins. Yield = 1 × 2 = 2 mol H₂O.

#### Significant Figures and STP Standards

| Rule | Example | Significant figures |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Non-zero digits always count | 245.3 | 4 |
| Leading zeros are not significant | 0.0042 | 2 |
| Zeros between non-zeros count | 5007 | 4 |
| Trailing zeros after decimal count | 2.500 | 4 |

IUPAC's newer STP (0 °C, 1 bar) gives 22.711 L mol⁻¹, but CUET questions still default to 22.4 L mol⁻¹ at 1 atm — read the stem carefully.

#### Common Mistakes

- Adding coefficients instead of dividing moles by coefficients when picking the limiting reagent.
- Forgetting that **molar mass is numerically equal to atomic/molecular mass** but carries units g mol⁻¹, not amu.
- Computing mole fraction with masses instead of moles.
- Ignoring density when converting between molarity and molality: m = (M × 1000) / (1000ρ − M × M_solute).

#### Practice Prompts

1. A 2.5 L flask at STP contains 5.0 g of a gas. Find its molar mass and identify the gas from {He, N₂, CO₂}.
2. 25 mL of 0.10 M H₂SO₄ reacts with 50 mL of 0.10 M NaOH. Which is limiting, and what is the concentration of the excess reactant in the final mixture?
