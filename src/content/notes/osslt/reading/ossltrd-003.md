# Making Connections Between Texts and Personal Knowledge — OSSLT Reading 3

Reading 3 is the third of the three reading skills named in the EQAO Framework. It is the bridge from the selection to the reader: items ask the student to link ideas across selections, compare perspectives, or connect what the selection says to what the reader already knows. Reading 3 is the skill most often paired with the open-response reading item on the OSSLT.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### What Reading 3 actually tests

- **Text-to-text connections.** The current selection reminds the student of something in another selection they have read on the OSSLT. Items ask "which other selection on this test makes a similar point?" or "in what way is selection A like selection B?"
- **Text-to-self connections.** The current selection reminds the student of something in their own life. Items ask "the student would most likely connect this to..." or "this selection would be most useful to a student who..."
- **Text-to-world connections.** The current selection reminds the student of a current event, public figure, or general knowledge. Items ask "the writer's claim is most consistent with..." or "this detail is most similar to..."

#### One-pass drill

1. Read the selection and identify the central idea or claim.
2. For each Reading 3 item, decide whether the item is asking text-to-text, text-to-self, or text-to-world.
3. For text-to-text: think back to the other selections on the OSSLT and ask which one makes a similar point or uses a similar example.
4. For text-to-self: ask "have I or someone I know experienced this?" The right answer is the one that connects cleanly to a real-world situation.
5. For text-to-world: ask "what general knowledge or current event does this match?" The right answer is the one that aligns with broadly accepted information.

#### Examiner traps on Reading 3

- An answer that connects to a *different* aspect of the selection than the item is asking about. Reading 3 items target the central idea or a specific claim, not a peripheral detail.
- An answer that is true but not the *best* connection. The right answer is the most direct and most relevant connection, not just any true statement.
- For text-to-self items, an answer that requires the reader to invent personal experience. The right answer should make sense for *most* Ontario Grade 10 students, not just one specific reader.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Strategy for Reading 3 passages

**Identify the central idea of the selection first.** The central idea is what the writer wants the reader to take away. Reading 3 items almost always connect to the central idea rather than to a peripheral detail.

**For text-to-text items, look for shared theme.** Two OSSLT selections that share a theme (e.g., both about environmental change) are the candidates. The right answer is the one with the closest parallel in *claim*, not just in topic.

**For text-to-self items, anchor to a typical student experience.** The OSSLT is taken by 15-16-year-old students in Ontario schools. The right answer is the connection that is plausible for that age group — going to school, doing homework, working a part-time job, playing on a sports team, looking after a sibling. Connections that require unusual life experience (working as a researcher, running a business) are usually wrong.

**For text-to-world items, use general knowledge only.** The right answer is the one that matches a *broadly known* fact or current event, not a piece of specialist knowledge. If the answer requires domain expertise, it is probably not the OSSLT's intended reading.

#### Practice routine for Reading 3

- Take two OSSLT-style selections on a related topic (e.g., two on technology in the classroom). Read both, then answer the text-to-text items as if you were answering from memory.
- Take one Real-Life Narrative. Read it, then write two sentences: "this reminds me of..." (text-to-self) and "this is similar to..." (text-to-world). Compare your sentences to the items.
- Take one Information Paragraph. Read it, then explain it to a friend in your own words. The points you choose to highlight are usually the central ideas the items will target.
- Take one News Report. Identify the lead and the supporting claims. The supporting claims are what Reading 3 items will connect to other selections and to general knowledge.

#### How Reading 3 differs from Reading 2

- Reading 2 is *inside the selection*. The answer is grounded in what the writer implies.
- Reading 3 is *across the selection and outside knowledge*. The answer requires the reader to bring something else in.
- Reading 2 answer choices are derivable from the selection alone. Reading 3 answer choices require the reader to recognise a parallel in another selection or in their own experience.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How Reading 3 connects to the other OSSLT items

- **Reading 1 and Reading 2** give you the explicit and inferred content of the selection. Reading 3 uses that content as the anchor for the cross-selection or cross-knowledge connection.
- **The open-response reading item** is built on Reading 3. The item asks the student to explain how the selection connects to their own experience or to another text. If Reading 3 is solid, the open-response paragraph is faster to write.
- **The long-writing tasks** depend on Reading 3 because the source material for the writing tasks is interpreted through Reading 3 (connecting the source to the writer's opinion).

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"I need to find the answer in the selection."** Reading 3 items are about the connection, not about the selection itself. The selection is the starting point, not the answer.
- **"Any connection works."** The OSSLT expects a *reasonable* connection that is supported by the selection. A choice that requires stretching the selection is wrong.
- **"I should pick the answer that is true in real life."** For Reading 3, the right answer is the one that connects to the selection's central idea. A true statement that does not connect to the selection is wrong.

#### Exam technique

- For text-to-text items, take a moment to remember the other selections you have read on the test. The right answer is often the most recent selection on the same topic.
- For text-to-self items, ask "would most Grade 10 students in Ontario recognise this experience?" If yes, it is likely the right answer.
- For text-to-world items, use general knowledge that you have known for years, not specialist knowledge you learned recently.
- Use the platform's flag-for-review function. Do not leave blanks on selected-response items.
