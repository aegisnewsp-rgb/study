# Carbohydrate Metabolism

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Carbohydrate metabolism centres on glucose handling through four intersecting pathways: **glycolysis** (cytosolic, 2 ATP + 2 NADH per glucose), **gluconeogenesis** (mitochondrial + cytosolic, liver/kidney only), the **TCA cycle** (3 NADH + 1 FADH2 + 1 GTP per acetyl-CoA), and the **pentose phosphate pathway** (NADPH + ribose-5-phosphate, no ATP). Three rate-limiting enzymes govern flux: **hexokinase/glucokinase**, **PFK-1**, and **pyruvate kinase** — each opposed by a unique gluconeogenic bypass.

- **Hexokinase** (Km ~0.1 mM, ubiquitous, G6P-inhibited) vs **glucokinase** (Km ~10 mM, liver/β-cells, insulin-induced): distinguishes postprandial hepatic glucose storage from peripheral glucose trapping.
- **Aerobic yield** ≈ 30–32 ATP/glucose; **anaerobic** = 2 ATP/glucose with lactate regenerating NAD+.
- **HbA1c ≥ 6.5%** (ADA) diagnoses diabetes; reflects ~120-day mean glycaemia.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Pathway Architecture

Glycolysis converts glucose to two pyruvate molecules across ten cytosolic steps, three of which are physiologically irreversible and serve as the control points. PFK-1 is the **single most important regulatory enzyme** of glycolysis, allosterically activated by **fructose-2,6-bisphosphate** (the PFK-2/FBPase-2 bifunctional enzyme product) and AMP, inhibited by ATP and citrate.

Pyruvate then faces three fates: (1) mitochondrial oxidation by the **pyruvate dehydrogenase complex** (cofactors: thiamine-pyrophosphate, lipoamide, FAD, NAD+, CoA — all B-vitamin derivatives); (2) reduction to **lactate** by LDH under hypoxia, regenerating NAD+; (3) carboxylation to oxaloacetate by **pyruvate carboxylase** (biotin-dependent, acetyl-CoA-activated) for gluconeogenesis.

#### Key Enzyme Comparison

| Enzyme | Location | Km for glucose | Inhibitor | Inducer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hexokinase (I–III) | Most tissues | ~0.1 mM | G6P (feedback) | — |
| Glucokinase (IV) | Liver, β-cells | ~10 mM | None by G6P | Insulin |
| PFK-1 | Cytosol | — | ATP, citrate | F-2,6-BP, AMP |
| Pyruvate kinase | Cytosol | — | ATP, alanine | F-1,6-BP |

#### Reciprocal Regulation

- **Insulin** (high glucose): dephosphorylates glycogen synthase (active), PFK-2 (makes F-2,6-BP), and pyruvate kinase.
- **Glucagon/Epinephrine** (low glucose/stress): cAMP-PKA cascade phosphorylates glycogen phosphorylase → active, and FBPase-2 → lowers F-2,6-BP, slowing glycolysis while accelerating gluconeogenesis.
- The four unique gluconeogenic enzymes — **pyruvate carboxylase, PEPCK, F-1,6-bisphosphatase, G6Pase** — bypass the three irreversible glycolytic steps.

#### Standard exam pointers

- Electron transport: NADH enters at Complex I (P/O ≈ 2.5), FADH2 at Complex II (P/O ≈ 1.5).
- Cytosolic NADH shuttle choice changes the total yield: **malate-aspartate** = 2.5 ATP/NADH (liver/heart); **glycerol-3-phosphate** = 1.5 ATP/NADH (skeletal muscle/brain).
- G6PD deficiency (X-linked) presents with **haemolytic anaemia** after fava beans, sulfa drugs, or infection — NADPH cannot regenerate reduced glutathione.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Clinical Integration

**Glycogen storage diseases** are exam favourites and test recognition of which enzyme is deficient in which tissue:

| Type | Disease | Enzyme defect | Hallmark |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | von Gierke | G6Pase | Severe fasting hypoglycaemia, lactic acidosis, hepatomegaly |
| II | Pompe | Lysosomal α-1,4-glucosidase | Cardiomegaly, early death (lysosomal) |
| V | McArdle | Muscle glycogen phosphorylase | Exercise intolerance, myoglobinuria, "second-wind" |
| VI | Hers | Hepatic phosphorylase | Mild hepatomegaly |

A critical misconception is that **skeletal muscle can release free glucose** — it lacks G6Pase, so muscle glycogen only fuels its own glycolysis, exporting lactate to the liver (Cori cycle) for resynthesis.

#### ATP Accounting Traps

1. Aerobic glucose: 2 (glycolytic substrate-level) + 2 × 2.5 (glycolytic NADH via malate-aspartate) + 2 (GTP from TCA) + 6 × 2.5 (TCA NADH) + 2 × 1.5 (TCA FADH2) ≈ **30–32 ATP**.
2. Anaerobic: **2 ATP** only; lactate formation reoxidises NADH without electron-chain entry.
3. The "3 ATP per cytosolic NADH" figure is outdated — modern P/O ratios yield 2.5.

#### Worked Micro-Example

A patient presents with confusion after prolonged fasting. Lab: glucose 2.2 mmol/L, lactate 6.5 mmol/L, anion gap 18. Consider von Gierke disease: hepatic G6Pase deficiency blocks the final step of gluconeogenesis and glycogenolysis, causing accumulation of G6P that shunts to lactate (high anion-gap metabolic acidosis) while blood glucose falls. Treatment: continuous nocturnal gastric drip or uncooked cornstarch.

#### High-Yield Practice Prompts

1. **MCQ stem**: "Which cofactor of the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex is derived from vitamin B1?" — Answer: **TPP (thiamine pyrophosphate)**. Trap: students confuse with biotin (pyruvate carboxylase).
2. **Clinical vignette**: "A young boy develops haemolysis within 24 hours of sulfa-drug ingestion; Heinz bodies present." — **G6PD deficiency**; the HMP shunt cannot produce NADPH to reduce glutathione.

#### Common Traps Recap

- TCA produces **1 GTP (not ATP)** per turn via succinyl-CoA synthetase.
- PDH is inhibited by its **products** (acetyl-CoA, NADH) and activated by Ca²⁺/ADP.
- The HMP shunt is **anabolic, not ATP-producing** — its currency is NADPH and ribose-5-P.
