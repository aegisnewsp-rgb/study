# Capacitance

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Capacitance measures how much charge a conductor holds per volt. One isolated plate does store charge, but the ratio Q/V stays fixed by the geometry alone, not by how much you push in.

**Memory Hook — "CQA" rule:** Capacitance = Quantity / Voltage, i.e. C = Q/V, with Q in coulombs, V in volts, C in farads (F). For a parallel-plate capacitor, the area grows C, the gap shrinks C — remember "A up, d down."

| Geometry | Capacitance formula | Key variables |
|---|---|---|
| Parallel plate | C = ε₀ A / d | A = plate area (m²), d = plate separation (m), ε₀ = 8.854 × 10⁻¹² F/m |
| Isolated sphere | C = 4π ε₀ r | r = radius of sphere (m) |
| Spherical shell | C = 4π ε₀ r_a r_b / (r_b − r_a) | r_a = inner radius, r_b = outer radius (m) |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** Series capacitance behaves like parallel resistance — both are dominated by the smallest value. Parallel capacitance behaves like series resistance — they simply add.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### The defining relation

C = Q / V is the one equation the examiner will assume you never forget. Q is the magnitude of equal and opposite charges on the two conductors (C), V is the potential difference between them (V), and C carries the SI unit farad, equal to coulomb per volt. The dimensional formula [M⁻¹ L⁻² T⁴ A²] falls out of Q/V once you express charge in base units.

Capacitance is geometric. It cannot change by altering Q or V; only by reshaping the conductors or swapping the dielectric. For a parallel plate filled with dielectric of relative permittivity κ, the capacitance becomes C = κ ε₀ A / d. Filling the gap multiplies C by κ — that single factor is the heart of every dielectric problem.

#### Comparison matrix: series vs parallel

| Feature | Series combination | Parallel combination |
|---|---|---|
| Equivalent rule | 1/C_eq = Σ 1/C_i | C_eq = Σ C_i |
| Common quantity | Same charge Q on each | Same voltage V across each |
| Voltage split | V_i ∝ 1/C_i | V_i identical |
| Charge split | Q_i identical | Q_i ∝ C_i |
| C_eq vs individual | Always < smallest C | Always > largest C |
| Energy stored | U_i = Q² / (2C_i), distributes inversely with C | U_i = ½ C_i V², distributes proportionally to C |

#### Energy stored in three forms

The same energy can be written three ways depending on which variable is held fixed:

- U = Q² / (2C) — useful when Q is given and C is known
- U = ½ C V² — the workhorse for fixed-voltage problems
- U = ½ Q V — convenient when both Q and V appear in the question

Energy density in the field is u = ½ ε₀ E² (J/m³) between the plates, where E = σ/ε₀ = V/d. Multiplying by volume A·d reproduces U = ½ C V² exactly, confirming the field is the storage medium, not the metal.

#### Worked problem

A 10 μF capacitor charged to 200 V is connected across an uncharged 30 μF capacitor through a resistor. Find the common final voltage and the energy lost in the process.

Charge conservation gives the final common voltage: V_f = (C₁V₁ + C₂V₂)/(C₁ + C₂) = (10 × 200 + 30 × 0)/(10 + 30) = 50 V.

Initial energy U_i = ½ C₁ V₁² = ½ × 10 × 10⁻⁶ × (200)² = 0.20 J.
Final energy U_f = ½ (C₁ + C₂) V_f² = ½ × 40 × 10⁻⁶ × (50)² = 0.05 J.
Energy dissipated as heat in the resistor ΔU = U_i − U_f = 0.15 J, which is 75% of the original — a classic JEE trap because students expect "no resistor, no loss" when the switch is ideal.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Even with an ideal switch, charge redistribution through any finite resistance dissipates energy as Joule heat. The fraction lost equals C₂/(C₁ + C₂) when C₂ starts uncharged; here 30/40 = 75%, matching the answer above.

#### RC transient behaviour

When a battery V charges a capacitor C through resistor R, the equations are:

- q(t) = CV (1 − e^(−t/RC))
- i(t) = (V/R) e^(−t/RC)

On discharge from initial charge Q₀:

- q(t) = Q₀ e^(−t/RC)
- i(t) = −(Q₀/RC) e^(−t/RC)

The time constant τ = RC carries the second as its unit, and after one τ the residual charge is about 36.8%, after 5τ the system is effectively settled (>99%).

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

#### Edge cases and Wheatstone-type networks

A symmetric bridge of five equal capacitors C has the middle branch redundant when the bridge balances — simply delete it and compute the remaining diamond. When the network lacks such symmetry, assign node potentials, inject a test charge, and read off C_eq = Q/V as a Kirchhoff-style problem.

For a parallel plate half-filled by a dielectric slab of thickness equal to the gap, the system splits into two capacitors in parallel, each with its own area and permittivity. C_eq = (κ ε₀ A/2d) + (ε₀ A/2d) = ε₀ A(κ + 1)/(2d). A common error treats the slab as altering the field uniformly instead of producing two regions in parallel.

#### Force on a dielectric slab

Pulling a slab of length L and width w into a capacitor of plate separation d at constant voltage V produces an inward force F = ½ (κ − 1) ε₀ w V² / d. At constant charge Q, the inward force becomes F = ½ (κ − 1) Q² / (κ² ε₀ w d), smaller because the battery is no longer supplying energy — the capacitor itself does the pulling.

#### Advanced trap list

| Situation | Wrong assumption students make | Correct treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Series chain with one very large C | Largest C dominates the sum | Smallest C dominates 1/C_eq |
| Dielectric inserted at constant V | Energy stored decreases | Energy stored increases by factor κ; battery does extra work |
| Dielectric inserted at constant Q | Energy stored decreases | Energy stored decreases by factor κ; field drops |
| Force between plates | F = Q E | F = Q² / (2 A ε₀) = ½ ε₀ E² A — the factor of ½ arises because the other plate's field does not act on a plate's own charge |
| Van de Graaff sphere | Charge resides inside the shell | Charge resides on the outer surface; potential builds up to the air-breakdown limit |

#### Connections across the syllabus

Capacitance links electrostatics to current electricity through the RC time constant, to mechanics through the energy method (F = −dU/dx for the dielectric slab), and to modern physics through the parallel-plate geometry used as a model for cavity resonators. JEE Advanced typically sets one numerical on a complex network and one linked question combining capacitor energy with a moving dielectric, so practice the energy-method derivation until it is reflex.

#### Two practice prompts

1. A spherical capacitor has inner radius a, outer radius b, and is half-filled (lower hemisphere) with dielectric of constant κ. Find C in closed form.
2. In the circuit of battery V, resistor R, and capacitor C in series, derive the time at which the power dissipated in R equals the rate of energy storage in C.

> 📌 **Formula Check:** C = Q/V has SI unit F = C/V; energy U = ½ C V² has unit J = F·V²; time constant τ = RC has unit s = Ω·F, confirming dimensional consistency before you submit.
