# Quantitative Reasoning in CARS — MCAT CARS Skill 4

Quantitative Reasoning is CARS Skill 4 in the AAMC CARS framework. It tests reading simple tables, graphs, and numerical claims inside CARS passages. The math is introductory (ratios, percentages, basic inference); the skill is reading numbers as part of an argument, not solving algebra. Roughly one to two CARS items per passage test Quantitative Reasoning.

> Verify the live CARS section length and item distribution on https://students-residents.aamc.org/ before planning around the figures below.

---

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### What Skill 4 tests

- Reading a table or graph and answering a question about the data.
- Comparing two quantities or computing a ratio or percentage.
- Recognising the trend or pattern in a graph.
- Translating between verbal and numerical descriptions of the same information.

#### One-pass drill

1. Read the passage once for the central claim and the context for the data.
2. Read the table or graph carefully. Note the axis labels, the units, and the scale.
3. For each Skill 4 item, identify the specific data point the item targets.
4. Compute or estimate the answer. Compare to the choices.

#### Examiner traps

- Misreading the axis labels. Check the units and the scale before answering.
- Picking a choice that is close to the right answer but off by a decimal place.
- Treating a correlation in the data as a causal claim by the author.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Reading tables

Tables organise data in rows and columns. The MCAT tests:

- Reading a specific cell value: locate the row and column.
- Comparing two values across rows or columns.
- Identifying a trend across rows or columns.
- Computing a simple summary (sum, mean, ratio, percentage).

#### Reading graphs

Graphs plot data visually. The MCAT tests:

- Reading a value at a specific point on the x-axis or y-axis.
- Comparing two points on the graph.
- Identifying a trend (increasing, decreasing, stable, cyclical).
- Recognising the type of graph (bar, line, scatter, pie) and its conventions.

#### Reading numerical claims

Numerical claims appear in the passage text. The MCAT tests:

- Translating a verbal description into a numerical one (or vice versa).
- Recognising a claim that is supported by the data and one that is not.
- Comparing two numerical claims in the passage.

#### Practice routine

- Take one CARS passage with a table or graph. Read the passage in 4 minutes.
- Read the table or graph carefully. Identify the key data points.
- Answer the Quantitative Reasoning items. Time yourself: under 8 minutes per passage.
- Take 5 passages in a row. Repeat the routine.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How Skill 4 connects to the other CARS skills

- **Skill 1 (Foundations of Comprehension)** supports Skill 4 by helping the reader understand what the data represents.
- **Skill 2 (Reasoning Within the Text)** supports Skill 4 by helping the reader evaluate the author's interpretation of the data.
- **Skill 3 (Reasoning Beyond the Text)** supports Skill 4 by helping the reader extend the data to new situations.

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"The graph is exact."** Graphs often show approximate values; read the axis scale and the gridlines carefully.
- **"The table is complete."** Tables may omit some data; check what the table includes and excludes.
- **"The author must agree with the data."** The author may interpret the data in a specific way; the items test the author's interpretation.

#### Exam technique

- For table items, locate the row and column. Read the value carefully.
- For graph items, identify the axis labels and units. Read the value at the specific point.
- For numerical claim items, locate the claim in the passage. Check whether the data supports the claim.

- Use the platform's flag-for-review function for items you cannot answer.
