# Introduction to Economics

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Economics studies how societies allocate **scarce resources** among unlimited wants, producing two branches: **microeconomics** (households and firms) and **macroeconomics** (economy-wide aggregates). Every introductory question in RPSC RAS hinges on three central problems — *what* to produce, *how* to produce, and *for whom* to produce.

#### Must-Know Formulas

| Formula | Expression | Units / Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Price Elasticity of Demand | $E_p = \dfrac{\%\Delta Q}{\%\Delta P} = \dfrac{\Delta Q / Q}{\Delta P / P}$ | Dimensionless; $E_p > 1$ elastic, $= 1$ unitary, $< 1$ inelastic |
| Total Revenue | $TR = P \times Q$ | ₹ (rupees) |
| National Income Identity | $Y = C + I + G + (X - M)$ | All in ₹ crore; $NX = X - M$ |
| Inflation Rate (CPI) | $\pi = \dfrac{CPI_t - CPI_{t-1}}{CPI_{t-1}} \times 100$ | % per annum |
| Fisher's Quantity Theory | $MV = PT$ | $M$ (₹), $V$ (turns/year), $P$ (index), $T$ (units) |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"CHEX"** for the expenditure method — **C**onsumption + **H**ealth-and-investment (I) + Government + (E**X**ports − Imports). Recall it as *"Chex boxes stacked into national income."*

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Vocabulary and the Scarcity–Choice Link

Economics begins with **scarcity** — the gap between limited means and unlimited wants. Scarcity forces **choice**, and every choice carries an **opportunity cost**: the value of the next-best alternative foregone. The four **factors of production** are Land (natural resources), Labour (human effort), Capital (man-made instruments), and Entrepreneurship (risk-bearing coordination).

#### Micro vs Macro — Concept Comparison Matrix

| Dimension | Microeconomics | Macroeconomics |
|---|---|---|
| Unit of analysis | Individual household, firm, market | Whole economy |
| Key variables | Price, quantity, elasticity | GDP, inflation, unemployment |
| Tools used | Demand–supply, utility, cost curves | Fiscal policy, monetary policy, multiplier |
| Example question | "Effect of a price rise on rice demand" | "Effect of repo-rate hike on inflation" |
| Equilibrium condition | $MU_x/P_x = MU_y/P_y$ (consumer); $MC = MR$ (producer) | $Y = C + I + G + NX$ |

#### Demand, Supply and Market Equilibrium

The **Law of Demand** states an inverse price–quantity relation, *ceteris paribus*. The **Law of Supply** gives a direct relation. Equilibrium price $P^*$ and quantity $Q^*$ clear the market where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied. A shift in demand (income, tastes) or supply (input costs, technology) moves this intersection — a distinction RAS examiners test repeatedly.

#### National Income Accounting — Worked Identity

**GDP at market price** via the expenditure route:

$$GDP_{MP} = C + I + G + NX$$

where $C$ = private final consumption expenditure (₹ crore), $I$ = gross domestic capital formation (₹ crore), $G$ = government final consumption expenditure (₹ crore), $NX = X - M$ = net exports (₹ crore).

The bridge to **GDP at factor cost** is:

$$GDP_{MP} = GDP_{FC} + \text{Net Indirect Taxes (NIT)}$$

This identity is the single most-missed linkage in RAS Mains answer sheets.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** In a hypothetical economy, private consumption is ₹400 crore, investment is ₹100 crore, government spending is ₹80 crore, exports are ₹60 crore, and imports are ₹40 crore. Net indirect taxes amount to ₹20 crore. Calculate (a) GDP at market price and (b) GDP at factor cost.

#### Solution:
(a) Apply $GDP_{MP} = C + I + G + (X - M)$:
$GDP_{MP} = 400 + 100 + 80 + (60 - 40) = 400 + 100 + 80 + 20 =$ **₹600 crore**.

(b) Use $GDP_{FC} = GDP_{MP} - NIT$:
$GDP_{FC} = 600 - 20 =$ **₹580 crore**.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Many candidates subtract NIT from the wrong side and write $GDP_{FC} = GDP_{MP} + NIT$. Remember: indirect taxes inflate market price *above* factor cost, so factor cost is the **lower** figure.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Consumer and Producer Equilibrium — Mechanism

A rational consumer equates the **marginal utility per rupee** across goods: $MU_x / P_x = MU_y / P_y$. If $MU_x / P_x > MU_y / P_y$, the consumer shifts spending from $Y$ to $X$ until equality holds. A profit-maximising producer sets **marginal cost equal to marginal revenue** ($MC = MR$). When $MC < MR$, producing one extra unit adds more to revenue than to cost — output must expand. When $MC > MR$, output contracts.

#### Fiscal vs Monetary Policy — Distinguishing the Twins

| Instrument | Fiscal Policy (Government) | Monetary Policy (RBI) |
|---|---|---|
| Primary lever | Taxation, public expenditure, borrowing | Repo rate, CRR, SLR, OMO |
| Affects | Aggregate demand via disposable income | Money supply and credit cost |
| Speed of effect | Slow (budget cycle) | Fast (weekly auctions) |
| Tracked indicator | Fiscal deficit (% of GDP) | Policy repo rate (%), bank credit growth |
| Recent role | Capex push, subsidy rationalisation | Inflation targeting under 4% ± 2% band |

#### Advanced Traps and Edge Cases

1. **Giffen and Veblen paradox** — Law of Demand fails when quantity rises with price (e.g. staple foods for the poorest, status goods).
2. **Multiplier misuse** — $k = \dfrac{1}{1 - MPC}$, not $\dfrac{1}{MPC}$. If $MPC = 0.8$, then $k = 5$, not $1.25$.
3. **Nominal vs Real GDP** — Nominal uses current-year prices; Real uses a fixed base year. India's base year was revised by the MoSPI; the latest published base year must be confirmed from the official press release before quoting.
4. **CRR vs SLR confusion** — CRR is **cash** parked with the RBI (no interest earned), while SLR is gold plus government securities kept **with the bank itself** (interest-bearing). Both are statutory but serve different liquidity purposes.
5. **Fiscal deficit ≠ Budget deficit** — Budget deficit includes borrowings; Fiscal deficit excludes them, isolating the government's true borrowing requirement.

#### Mixed Economy Features (India-specific)

India is classified as a **mixed economy** because the public and private sectors coexist with planning. Key features: (a) coexistence of public and private ownership; (b) indicative planning through Five-Year Plans and now NITI Aayog; (c) regulatory frameworks (Competition Act, SEBI, RBI) guiding private enterprise; (d) social-welfare objectives alongside growth. Sectors divide into Primary (agriculture, mining), Secondary (manufacturing, construction), and Tertiary (services, IT, finance).

#### Practice Prompts for Self-Test

1. Suppose MPC rises from 0.75 to 0.90 in an economy. Compute the change in the investment multiplier and explain why a small rise in MPC sharply amplifies demand.
2. Distinguish between $GDP_{MP}$, $GDP_{FC}$, $GNP$, and $NNP$ using the standard textbook identities, and indicate which measure best reflects domestic production available to residents.

#### Common Mistakes Compendium

1. Writing $TR = P/Q$ instead of $TR = P \times Q$ — Total Revenue rises with quantity, not falls.
2. Treating elasticity as a percentage-point change rather than a percentage change — always divide by the original base.
3. Treating monetary policy as fiscal policy because both "manage the economy" — only RBI's instruments count as monetary.
4. Confusing the **inflation rate formula** with a simple CPI ratio — the denominator is $CPI_{t-1}$, not $CPI_t$.
