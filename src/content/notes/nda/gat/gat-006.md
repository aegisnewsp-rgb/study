# General Science Biology

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Cell biology**: the cell is the structural and functional unit of life; plant cells carry a **cell wall**, **chloroplasts**, and a large central **vacuole**, whereas animal cells lack these. **Photosynthesis** converts light energy into chemical energy using the equation 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ (in chloroplasts). **Cellular respiration** releases this energy: glycolysis (cytoplasm) → Krebs cycle (mitochondrial matrix) → electron transport chain (inner mitochondrial membrane), yielding ~36–38 ATP per glucose. **Human physiology basics**: four-chambered heart drives **double circulation** (pulmonary + systemic); oxygen travels bound to **haemoglobin** in RBCs, while CO₂ is carried mainly as **bicarbonate** (HCO₃⁻) in plasma. **Genetics**: DNA in chromosomes codes for proteins via **transcription** (DNA → mRNA) and **translation** (mRNA → protein). **Mendel's laws** — dominance, segregation, independent assortment — govern inheritance patterns tested via monohybrid/dihybrid crosses. NDA GAT weight is roughly **3%**, with 2–4 MCQs drawn from cell biology, human physiology, nutrition/diseases, and ecology.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Classification and Cell Structure

Living organisms are grouped under the **five-kingdom system** — Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia. Monera are **prokaryotes** (no membrane-bound nucleus, e.g., bacteria); the other four kingdoms are **eukaryotes**. The **cell** membrane (phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins) controls transport; **mitochondria** are the powerhouse (ATP synthesis); **ribosomes** (80S in eukaryotes, 70S in prokaryotes) translate mRNA; the **nucleus** stores DNA wrapped with histones into **chromosomes** (46 in humans = 23 pairs).

#### Nutrition, Respiration, and Digestion

**Autotrophs** make their own food via photosynthesis; **heterotrophs** consume others. Human digestion begins with **salivary amylase** (mouth) acting on starch, continues with **gastric juice** (HCl + pepsin for proteins) in the stomach, and finishes in the **small intestine** with **bile** (emulsifies fats), **pancreatic amylase, trypsin, and lipase**. Absorption occurs mainly through **villi** in the ileum. **Cellular respiration** has three stages: **glycolysis** (glucose → 2 pyruvate + 2 ATP, in cytoplasm), the **Krebs cycle** (in mitochondrial matrix, releases CO₂ and NADH), and the **electron transport chain** (inner mitochondrial membrane, produces the bulk of ATP using O₂ as the final electron acceptor → forms H₂O).

#### Circulation, Respiration, and Excretion

The human heart has **two atria and two ventricles**; the **left ventricle** pumps oxygenated blood into the systemic circuit via the aorta. **Double circulation** keeps oxygenated and deoxygenated blood separate. **RBCs** contain **haemoglobin** which binds O₂ to form **oxyhaemoglobin**; CO₂ is transported as **bicarbonate ions** (via carbonic anhydrase: CO₂ + H₂O ⇌ H₂CO₃ ⇌ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻). Each **nephron** in the kidney filters blood through the **glomerulus**, reabsorbs useful substances, and secretes wastes to form **urine** (chief nitrogenous waste in humans: **urea**).

#### Genetics and Inheritance

**DNA** is a double helix of complementary base pairs (A–T, G–C). It replicates **semi-conservatively** — each daughter molecule retains one parental strand. **Mendel's Law of Segregation** states alleles separate during gamete formation; the **Law of Independent Assortment** applies to genes on different chromosomes. A monohybrid cross of heterozygotes (Tt × Tt) gives the genotypic ratio **1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt** and phenotypic **3 : 1**. Sex determination in humans: females are **XX**, males are **XY**; the father determines the child's sex.

#### Common Exam Question Patterns

NDA GAT Biology questions are typically single-correct MCQs testing (a) identification of cell organelles from function, (b) deficiency diseases of vitamins (e.g., **Vitamin C → scurvy**, **Vitamin D → rickets**, **Vitamin B₁₂ → anaemia**), (c) blood-group inheritance (**ABO** with A, B antigens and antibodies; **Rh** factor causing erythroblastosis foetalis when Rh⁻ mother carries Rh⁺ foetus), and (d) pathogen terminology (**antigen** = foreign molecule, **antibody** = protein made by lymphocytes, **vaccine** = attenuated/killed pathogen that triggers adaptive immunity).

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Endocrine and Reproductive Systems

**Hormones** are chemical messengers released by ductless **endocrine glands**. The **pituitary** is the master gland, secreting GH (growth), TSH (thyroid-stimulating), ACTH (adrenal cortex), FSH and LH (gonads). **Insulin** (from β-cells of the islets of Langerhans) lowers blood glucose by promoting cellular uptake; **glucagon** (α-cells) raises it. **Diabetes mellitus** results from insulin deficiency or resistance. The male reproductive system produces sperm in **seminiferous tubules** of the testes; testosterone is secreted by **Leydig cells**. In females, **oogenesis** in the ovaries releases one ovum per menstrual cycle; the **corpus luteum** secretes progesterone to maintain the uterine lining.

#### Ecology, Evolution, and Disease

Energy flows unidirectionally through a **food chain**; only about **10%** of energy is transferred at each trophic level (Lindeman's 10% law), with the rest lost as heat. **Nutrients**, in contrast, **cycle** (carbon, nitrogen, water cycles). Biodiversity loss destabilises ecosystems. **Communicable diseases** spread via pathogens — examples: tuberculosis (bacteria, airborne), malaria (*Plasmodium*, vector = female *Anopheles* mosquito), AIDS (HIV, body fluids). **Non-communicable diseases** include diabetes, hypertension, and cancers (uncontrolled mitosis). **Vaccination** stimulates adaptive immunity by exposing the body to harmless antigens, producing memory B and T lymphocytes that mount a faster response upon re-exposure. **Antibiotics** kill or inhibit bacteria but are ineffective against viruses — a frequently tested distinction.

#### Common Mistakes

1. Confusing **mitosis** (one division → 2 diploid, genetically identical daughter cells) with **meiosis** (two divisions → 4 haploid, genetically varied gametes).
2. Stating that oxygen is transported in plasma — it is carried by **haemoglobin** in RBCs; only a small fraction dissolves in plasma.
3. Calling **photosynthesis** a cellular respiration step — photosynthesis builds glucose in chloroplasts; respiration breaks it down in mitochondria.
4. Mixing up **arteries** (carry blood away from the heart; most carry oxygenated blood except the pulmonary artery) and **veins** (carry blood toward the heart; most carry deoxygenated blood except the pulmonary vein).
5. Writing that **Rh incompatibility** occurs in the first pregnancy — the first Rh⁺ foetus typically sensitises the mother; the second Rh⁺ foetus is at risk.

#### Practice Prompts

1. Trace one glucose molecule from ingestion through digestion, absorption, glycolysis, the Krebs cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation, naming the enzymes, locations, and ATP yields at each stage.
2. A mother has blood group B (genotype I^B i) and the father has group A (I^A i). List all possible blood groups of their children with expected probabilities, and explain why a child with group O confirms that both parents carry the recessive *i* allele.
