# Tissue Types and Histology

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The human body is built from **four primary tissues**: epithelial, connective, muscular, and nervous. INI CET (AIIMS PG) tests these through slide identification and one-line statements on cell shape, layering, location, and surface specializations.

- **Epithelium** covers surfaces, lines lumina, and forms glands. It is **avascular**, sits on a **basement membrane** (basal lamina of type IV collagen + laminin; reticular lamina of type III collagen), and is classified by **shape** (squamous, cuboidal, columnar, transitional) and **layers** (simple, stratified, pseudostratified).
- **Connective tissue** binds and supports; the dominant component is the **extracellular matrix** (collagen, elastic, reticular fibres in ground substance of GAGs/proteoglycans).
- **Muscle** types differ in striation, nuclear position, and cell shape — skeletal (peripheral nuclei, striated, voluntary), cardiac (central nuclei, intercalated discs, involuntary), smooth (fusiform, no striations).
- **Nervous tissue** = neurons (signalling) + neuroglia (astrocytes, oligodendrocytes, microglia, ependymal, Schwann cells, satellite cells).

| Tissue | Defining feature | Key example |
|---|---|---|
| Simple squamous | Single flat layer, rapid diffusion | Endothelium, alveolus |
| Pseudostratified ciliated columnar | One layer, nuclei at different heights, with cilia | Trachea (respiratory) |
| Transitional | Dome-shaped umbrella cells, distensible | Urinary bladder urothelium |
| Hyaline cartilage | Type II collagen, chondrocytes in lacunae, glassy matrix | Tracheal rings, costal cartilage |
| Skeletal muscle | Multinucleated, peripheral nuclei, sarcomeres | Biceps, tongue |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"E-C-M-N — Every Cell's Matrix Needs Neurons"** classifies the four tissues as **E**pithelium (covering), **C**onnective (matrix-rich), **M**uscle (movement), **N**ervous (signalling). For muscle: **"Skeletal = Striped, Peripheral nuclei; Cardiac = Central, Connected; Smooth = Spindle, no Stripes."**

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Epithelial Tissue Architecture

Epithelium is polarized with an **apical surface** (often bearing microvilli, cilia, or stereocilia), **lateral surfaces** (with junctional complexes — tight, adherens, desmosomes, gap), and a **basal surface** anchored to the basement membrane. It is **avascular** and depends on diffusion from underlying connective tissue (the lamina propria). Glandular epithelium divides into **exocrine** (ducts, e.g., salivary, sweat) and **endocrine** (ductless, hormones into blood, e.g., thyroid follicles, islets of Langerhans).

| Surface modification | Structure | Functional site |
|---|---|---|
| Microvilli | Actin-core finger projections | Enterocyte brush border, proximal renal tubule |
| Cilia | 9+2 microtubule axoneme | Trachea, fallopian tube |
| Stereocilia | Long microvilli (not true cilia) | Epididymis, hair cells of cochlea |
| Glycocalyx | Carbohydrate-rich coat | Protection, cell recognition |

#### Concept Comparison Matrix — Commonly Confused Tissue Features

| Feature | What students think | Correct fact |
|---|---|---|
| Pseudostratified vs stratified | "Both have many layers" | Pseudostratified has **one layer** — every cell touches the basement membrane, but nuclei sit at varying heights (e.g., trachea). True stratified has multiple layers; only basal cells touch the BM (e.g., oesophagus). |
| Transitional vs stratified squamous | "Both line hollow organs" | Transitional (urothelium) has **dome-shaped umbrella cells**, is **non-keratinized**, and stretches with bladder filling. Stratified squamous lines the oesophagus and vagina. |
| Intercalated disc | "Just gap junctions" | Three-junction complex: **fascia adherens** (anchors actin), **desmosomes** (mechanical), **gap junctions** (electrical coupling between cardiomyocytes). |
| Cartilage perichondrium | "Always present" | **Absent on articular hyaline cartilage** — nourished by synovial fluid. Present elsewhere (trachea, ear). |
| Tendon vs cartilage | "Both are dense connective tissue" | Tendon = **dense regular** connective tissue (parallel type I collagen, fibroblasts in rows). Hyaline cartilage = chondrocytes in lacunae, type II collagen, basophilic matrix. |

#### Key Steps — Identifying a Histology Slide

1. **Low power (4×):** assess cellularity vs matrix density. Matrix-heavy → connective tissue; tightly packed sheets → epithelium.
2. **Medium power (10×):** count layers (simple, stratified, pseudostratified) and note surface contour (flat, dome, folded).
3. **High power (40×):** identify cell shape (squamous, cuboidal, columnar), nucleus position, and any striations or branching.
4. **Look for unique markers:** intercalated discs → cardiac; multinucleation with peripheral nuclei + striations → skeletal; lacunae with canaliculi → bone; lacunae without canaliculi → cartilage.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** A histology slide shows a single layer of tall cells with basally located oval nuclei. The apical surface bears motile projections on a 9+2 microtubule arrangement. The cells line the lower respiratory tract. Identify the tissue type and one clinically important cell found on its surface.
**(A)** Simple columnar, brush border — small intestine
**(B)** Pseudostratified ciliated columnar, with goblet cells — trachea
**(C)** Stratified squamous, non-keratinized — oesophagus
**(D)** Transitional epithelium, umbrella cells — urinary bladder

#### Solution:
Step 1 — **Layer count:** Single layer with nuclei at *different heights* despite one tier of cells → **pseudostratified**, not true stratified.
Step 2 — **Shape:** Tall cells → **columnar**. Apical 9+2 microtubule projections → **cilia** (not microvilli, which are actin-based).
Step 3 — **Location:** Lower respiratory tract lining → **trachea/bronchi**.
Step 4 — **Specialized cell:** **Goblet cells** secrete mucus that the cilia propel upward (mucociliary escalator).
#### Correct answer: (B) Pseudostratified ciliated columnar, with goblet cells — trachea.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students pick (A) because they see "tall columnar with apical projections," but microvilli (intestine) lack a 9+2 axoneme and are non-motile. The phrase "lower respiratory tract" and the cilia description force the tracheal answer.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Cartilage and Bone Histology — High-Yield Edge Cases

**Hyaline cartilage** is the most prevalent type (articular surfaces, costal cartilages, tracheal rings, larynx, nasal septum, bronchi). Its matrix stains basophilic due to abundant **type II collagen** and proteoglycans (aggrecan with chondroitin and keratan sulfate). Chondrocytes occupy **lacunae**, often in isogenous groups of 2–4 (cell nests). Nutrition is by diffusion from perichondrium vessels (except articular cartilage). **Elastic cartilage** adds elastic fibres (ear pinna, epiglottis); **fibrocartilage** has parallel type I collagen bundles (intervertebral discs, menisci, pubic symphysis).

**Compact bone** is organized into **osteons (Haversian systems)**: a central Haversian canal carries vessels and nerves, surrounded by concentric **lamellae** of mineralized matrix (hydroxyapatite — Ca₁₀(PO₄)₆(OH)₂). Osteocytes sit in **lacunae** and connect through **canaliculi** via cytoplasmic processes (gap junctions allow nutrient exchange). **Volkmann's canals** run transversely, connecting Haversian canals to the periosteum and to each other.

#### Muscle Tissue — Electron-Microscopic Distinctions

| Feature | Skeletal | Cardiac | Smooth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Striations | Yes (sarcomeres) | Yes (sarcomeres) | No (dense bodies) |
| Nuclei | Multinucleated, peripheral | 1–2, central | 1, central, fusiform |
| Intercalated discs | Absent | Present (fascia adherens + desmosomes + gap junctions) | Absent |
| Control | Voluntary (somatic α-motor neurons at NMJ) | Involuntary (autonomic) | Involuntary (autonomic) |
| Sarcomere proteins | Actin, myosin, troponin C/I/T | Same | Actin, myosin; **no troponin** (calmodulin-based Ca²⁺ regulation) |
| Regeneration | Satellite cells | Minimal (cardiomyocytes are terminally differentiated) | Modest (pericyte-derived) |

#### Nervous Tissue — Glial Cell Functions (IN CET Favourite)

- **Astrocytes** (CNS): form the **glial limitans**, maintain the blood–brain barrier, take up K⁺ and glutamate.
- **Oligodendrocytes** (CNS): myelinate up to ~50 axons simultaneously (one cell, many internodes).
- **Schwann cells** (PNS): one cell myelinates **one internode** of a single axon; unmyelinated fibres are simply embedded in Schwann cell grooves.
- **Microglia** (CNS): bone-marrow-derived macrophages, not of neuroectodermal origin — a common trap.
- **Ependymal cells** (CNS): line ventricles and central canal; produce CSF via choroid plexus modification.
- **Satellite cells** (PNS): surround neuron cell bodies in ganglia.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. A 45-year-old presents with progressively hardening skin (scleroderma). Predict two histological changes in the **dermis** (dense irregular connective tissue) and explain why the disorder disrupts ECM homeostasis. Discuss which cells in the dermis normally produce type I collagen and how myofibroblasts contribute to pathology.
2. Compare **regeneration capacity** across the four tissues after injury (epithelium → excellent; connective tissue → good; muscle → variable; nervous tissue → CNS neurons do not regenerate, PNS axons can regrow at 1–2 mm/day along Schwann cell bands). Explain why astroglial scarring limits CNS axon regrowth.
