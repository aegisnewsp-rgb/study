# Fractions, Decimals and Percentages — PSLE Mathematics Notes

Fractions, Decimals and Percentages is the second sub-strand under "Number and Algebra" in the 2021 MOE Primary Mathematics syllabus and the heaviest paper-1 load at Primary 6. The strand spans equivalent fractions; conversion among fractions, decimals and percentages; the four operations with fractions and mixed numbers; and percentage contexts including percentage change, discount, simple interest and GST (where the syllabus still uses the term). Mastery of this strand is the second-highest return investment for a Primary 6 student after Whole Numbers and Operations.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### The seven facts every Primary 6 student needs

1. **Equivalent fractions**: multiplying or dividing the numerator and denominator of a fraction by the same non-zero number gives an equivalent fraction.
2. **Conversion to decimals**: divide the numerator by the denominator; the long division result is the decimal form.
3. **Conversion to percentages**: multiply the decimal form by 100, or multiply the fraction by 100%.
4. **Conversion back to fractions**: read the place value (tenths = 1/10, hundredths = 1/100, thousandths = 1/1000).
5. **Adding and subtracting fractions**: find a common denominator first, then add or subtract the numerators.
6. **Multiplying fractions**: multiply numerator by numerator, denominator by denominator. Mixed numbers must be converted to improper fractions first.
7. **Dividing fractions**: multiply by the reciprocal of the divisor (KCF — Keep, Change, Flip).

#### Key notations

- 1/2 = 0.5 = 50%
- 1/4 = 0.25 = 25%
- 3/4 = 0.75 = 75%
- 1/3 ≈ 0.333… = 33.3…%
- 1/8 = 0.125 = 12.5%

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Equivalent fractions and conversion

Two fractions are equivalent if they represent the same part of a whole. 2/4 = 1/2 because dividing both 2 and 4 by 2 gives 1/2. To find an equivalent fraction with a specific denominator, divide the target denominator by the original denominator to find the multiplier, then multiply the numerator by the same multiplier.

**Fraction to decimal**: divide the numerator by the denominator. 3/8 = 3 ÷ 8 = 0.375.

**Decimal to fraction**: read the place value. 0.65 = 65/100 = 13/20 (after simplifying by 5).

**Decimal to percentage**: multiply by 100. 0.65 × 100 = 65%. 0.075 × 100 = 7.5%.

**Percentage to decimal**: divide by 100. 65% ÷ 100 = 0.65. 7.5% ÷ 100 = 0.075.

**Percentage to fraction**: write the percentage over 100, then simplify. 65% = 65/100 = 13/20.

#### Adding and subtracting fractions

To add or subtract fractions, the denominators must be the same.

**Same denominator**: 2/7 + 3/7 = 5/7. The denominator does not change.

**Different denominators**: find the LCM of the two denominators, then convert each fraction to an equivalent fraction with the LCM as the new denominator. 1/3 + 1/4 = 4/12 + 3/12 = 7/12. The LCM of 3 and 4 is 12.

**Mixed numbers**: convert to improper fractions first. 1 2/3 = 5/3. 2 1/4 + 1 2/5 = 9/4 + 7/5 = 45/20 + 28/20 = 73/20 = 3 13/20.

#### Multiplying fractions

Multiply the numerators, then multiply the denominators. Simplify before multiplying if possible — this keeps the numbers small. 2/3 × 3/4 = 6/12 = 1/2. With mixed numbers: 1 1/2 × 2/3 = 3/2 × 2/3 = 6/6 = 1.

#### Dividing fractions (KCF)

To divide by a fraction, multiply by its reciprocal. "KCF" stands for Keep the first fraction, Change ÷ to ×, Flip the second fraction. 3/4 ÷ 2/5 = 3/4 × 5/2 = 15/8 = 1 7/8. With mixed numbers: 2 1/2 ÷ 1/1/4 = 5/2 ÷ 5/4 = 5/2 × 4/5 = 20/10 = 2.

#### Percentage contexts

**Finding a percentage of a quantity**: convert the percentage to a fraction or decimal, then multiply. 25% of 80 = 0.25 × 80 = 20.

**Percentage change**: percentage change = (change / original) × 100. If a price goes from $80 to $100, the change is $20, so the percentage change is 20 / 80 × 100 = 25%.

**Discount**: sale price = original price × (1 − discount rate). A 20% discount on $50 = $50 × 0.80 = $40.

**Percentage increase**: new value = original value × (1 + increase rate). A 10% increase on $50 = $50 × 1.10 = $55.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where Fractions, Decimals and Percentages appears across the paper

- **PSLE Mathematics Paper 1**: short-answer questions on conversion, the four operations with fractions and decimals, and percentage of a quantity.
- **PSLE Mathematics Paper 2**: word problems involving percentage change, discount, simple interest, and ratio / proportion contexts that bridge into the next sub-strand.
- **Across both papers**: fluency in conversion between fractions, decimals and percentages is the single biggest lever for a Primary 6 student who can already do the four operations.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "To add fractions, add the denominators too." Adding the denominators gives an answer that is not equivalent to the sum. Find a common denominator first.
- "Multiplying by a fraction makes the number smaller." Multiplying by a fraction less than 1 makes the number smaller; multiplying by a fraction greater than 1 makes the number larger.
- "Dividing by a fraction is the same as dividing by a smaller number." It is not. Dividing by 1/2 is the same as multiplying by 2, so the result is larger.
- "10% off means the new price is 10%." 10% off means subtract 10% of the original from the original; the new price is 90% of the original.
- "A percentage increase and the corresponding percentage decrease are equal." A 20% increase followed by a 20% decrease does not return to the original — the decrease is taken on the larger value.

#### Exam technique

- For conversion questions, write the conversion step on a single line. The marker can award partial credit even if the final simplification slips.
- For fraction-of-a-quantity questions, write "amount = percentage × original" before substituting. The formula line is the method mark.
- For KCF questions, write out the Keep, Change, Flip steps explicitly. The marker can then distinguish a "flipped wrong direction" error from a "computational" error.
- For mixed-number arithmetic, convert to improper fractions first, then simplify before multiplying. Carrying a mixed number through a calculation is a common source of arithmetic slips.
