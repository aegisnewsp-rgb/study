# Legal News

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Legal News in CLAT covers recent judicial pronouncements, statutory amendments, ordinances, and constitutional events reported within the 12-month reference window before the exam. It sits inside the Current Affairs & General Knowledge section, which itself carries roughly 25% of CLAT marks, with legal items contributing an estimated 5%. From 1 July 2024, the IPC, CrPC and Indian Evidence Act were replaced by the **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS)**, **Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS)** and **Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA)** — every criminal-law answer must use the new section numbers.

- Track Supreme Court and High Court **landmark judgments** of the preceding year; note bench strength, judge names, citation, and the exact relief granted.
- Read at least one national daily (The Hindu / Indian Express) and one law outlet (LiveLaw, Bar & Bench, Supreme Court Observer) consistently.
- Map every item to a subject head — criminal, constitutional, commercial, environmental, family, labour, tax, cyber — before memorising it.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### What counts as "Legal News" for CLAT
Anything that changes, clarifies or applies the law in India within the 12-month reference window: Supreme Court and High Court judgments, presidential assent to bills, Central ordinances under Article 123, constitutional amendments, statutory notifications from the Ministry of Law and Justice, Election Commission of India orders, and NALSA directives. Items older than the window or purely political slug ("SC slams government") are out of scope; the legal issue, relief and provisions interpreted are what examiners reward.

#### The four new criminal codes
The criminal-law syllabus shifted completely on 1 July 2024. Candidates quoting the old IPC, CrPC or Evidence Act lose marks because the corresponding sections now sit under the BNS, BNSS and BSA. The mapping below is the single most-tested table in this topic.

| Old provision | New provision (post-1 July 2024) | Subject |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IPC Section 302 (murder) | BNS Section 101 | Punishment |
| IPC Section 376 (rape) | BNS Section 63 | Sexual offences |
| IPC Section 420 (cheating) | BNS Section 318 | Property offences |
| CrPC Section 154 (FIR) | BNSS Section 154 | Procedure |
| Indian Evidence Act Section 27 | BSA Section 27 | Evidence |

#### How to read a reported judgment
Distinguish **ratio decidendi** (the binding legal principle on which the decision rests) from **obiter dicta** (judicial observations that are persuasive, not binding). Reporting obiter as the "law declared" is the most common error examiners exploit in passage-based questions.

- Note Article **32**, **226** and **142** whenever a writ, fundamental-right plea or complete justice order is discussed — these three articles define the court's jurisdiction in news items.
- Cross-check every quote against the official SCI cause list, LiveLaw, or the Bar & Bench daily digest before committing it to revision notes.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge cases and traps
Three traps appear repeatedly in CLAT legal-news items. First, candidates confuse a High Court division-bench ruling with a Supreme Court judgment and cite the wrong court — always verify whether the report ends with "leave granted" or "appeal dismissed." Second, PILs admitted are routinely confused with PILs disposed of; an admission order is procedural, not a ruling on merits. Third, a Law Commission Report or a Standing Committee recommendation is treated as enacted law, whereas only a gazetted Act or a presidential assent carries binding force.

#### Worked micro-example
Consider a news item: "Supreme Court directs States to frame guidelines on Live-in relationships." A weak response names the political reaction; a strong response records the bench (Constitution Bench, 5-Judge), the date, the case title, the specific Article relied on (Article 21, right to dignity and autonomy), the relief (binding guidelines within six weeks), and any obiter on **same-sex marriage** and **PIL** standing. The difference between a topper's note and an average one is exactly this level of structured reporting.

#### Practice prompts
1. Identify the ratio decidendi and any obiter dicta in a 2025 Supreme Court ruling on Article 370 abrogation, and explain how basic-structure doctrine references bind future benches.
2. Recast three recent criminal-law news items using the post-1 July 2024 BNS, BSA and BNSS section numbers instead of the IPC, CrPC and Evidence Act numbers originally cited.
