# Task 2 — Opinion, Discussion and Problem/Solution Essays — IELTS Writing (Qatar)

In the IELTS Academic Writing test, **Task 2** is the **essay task**: you
are given a **point of view, argument, or problem** and you must write an
**essay in 250+ words in approximately 40 minutes**. The essay type is
specified by the prompt wording, and the three most common Task 2 prompt
types are: **Opinion essays** (*To what extent do you agree or
disagree?*), **Discussion essays** (*Discuss both views and give your
opinion*), and **Problem/Solution essays** (*What are the causes of /
solutions to...?*). Each prompt type requires a slightly different
paragraph plan.

This is the **fifth topic** in the ielts-writing subject (weight 4). It
follows ieltsw-004 (Task 2 — Essay Structure and Argument w=5) and is
followed by ieltsw-006 (Task 2 — Cohesion, Lexical Resource and Grammar
w=4). Each prompt type has its own strategy:

- **Opinion essay** — take a clear position (agree or disagree); present
  2-3 reasons for your position; acknowledge the other side.
- **Discussion essay** — present both views; state your opinion; support
  with arguments.
- **Problem/Solution essay** — identify causes; propose solutions;
  explain why the solutions will work.

The examiner scores Task 2 on **task response** (did you address the
prompt type? did you write to the prompt type's strategy?), **coherence
and cohesion** (is your essay well-organized for the prompt type?),
**lexical resource**, and **grammatical range and accuracy**.

This note shows the LCA version: a **Lite** (5-minute) read, a **Standard**
(15-minute) read, and the **Exam Essentials** table a Qatari candidate can
use to revise in the last hour before the test.

## Lite (4-Step Method)

1. **Identify the prompt type from the keywords (1 minute).** Look at the
   prompt wording: *To what extent do you agree or disagree* = opinion
   essay. *Discuss both views and give your opinion* = discussion essay.
   *Causes / Solutions / Problems* = problem/solution essay.
2. **Apply the prompt-type strategy (1 minute).** Opinion = clear position;
   Discussion = both views + your opinion; Problem/Solution = causes +
   solutions.
4. **Write the introduction (3 minutes) with the prompt-type-specific
   thesis.** Paraphrase the prompt + state your thesis (your position for
   opinion essay; both views + your opinion for discussion essay; causes +
   solutions for problem/solution essay).
5. **Write 2 body paragraphs + (counter-argument or extra solution) +
   conclusion (32 minutes).** Each body paragraph: topic sentence +
   explanation + example. The third body paragraph depends on the prompt
   type: counter-argument for opinion; another view for discussion;
   another solution for problem/solution.

**Insight: Task 2 prompts are not interchangeable — each prompt type has
its own paragraph plan.** The examiner scores task response. ALWAYS
identify the prompt type first.

### 6 Examiner Traps (Task 2 Prompt Types)

- **The wrong-essay-type trap** — candidates treat an opinion essay as a
  discussion essay (or vice versa). The examiner scores task response.
  ALWAYS identify the prompt type first.
- **The no-position trap (opinion)** — candidates write both sides without
  taking a position. ALWAYS take a clear position in an opinion essay.
- **The no-your-opinion trap (discussion)** — candidates present both views
  without stating their opinion. ALWAYS state your opinion in a discussion
  essay.
- **The no-causes trap (problem/solution)** — candidates describe the
  problem without identifying causes. ALWAYS identify 1-2 causes in a
  problem/solution essay.
- **The no-solutions trap (problem/solution)** — candidates describe the
  problem without proposing solutions. ALWAYS propose 1-2 solutions in a
  problem/solution essay.
- **The no-counter-argument trap (opinion)** — candidates take a position
  but do not acknowledge the other side. ALWAYS include a counter-argument
  paragraph in an opinion essay.

### Qatari-Context Matches (2025–2026 cohort signal)

In recent IELTS Writing Task 2 tests used in Qatar (and the broader Gulf),
the prompts commonly address:

- **Education (opinion)** — should higher education be free? Should exams
  be the only measure of student ability?
- **The environment (opinion)** — should individuals or governments
  address climate change?
- **Technology (discussion)** — should social media be regulated? Does
  technology reduce or increase face-to-face communication?
- **Work (opinion + discussion)** — should working from home be permanent?
  Should retirement age be increased?
- **Society (opinion + discussion)** — should wealth be distributed more
  equally? Should the voting age be lowered?
- **Problem/Solution** — what are the causes of youth unemployment in the
  Gulf? What are the solutions to traffic congestion in Doha?

The Qatari *Educational Reform* (Ministry of Education and Higher Education)
emphasizes critical thinking and argumentation in Year 12 / Year 13
English classes, which is why argumentative essays are commonly featured
in the Qatari cohort.

## Standard (7-Step Method with Long-Form Guidance)

### Step 1 — Identify the prompt type from the keywords (1 minute)

Before you write anything, identify the prompt type:

- **Opinion essay keywords** — *agree / disagree / opinion / to what
  extent / do you think / is this a positive or negative development*.
- **Discussion essay keywords** — *discuss both views / give your opinion /
  some people think ... others believe*.
- **Problem/Solution essay keywords** — *causes / solutions / problems /
  what can be done / why is this happening*.
- **Two-part question keywords** — *why is this happening? Is this a
  positive or negative development?*

### Step 2 — Apply the prompt-type strategy (1 minute)

Apply the prompt-type strategy:

- **Opinion essay** — take a clear position (agree or disagree); present
  2-3 reasons for your position.
- **Discussion essay** — present both views; state your opinion; support
  with arguments.
- **Problem/Solution essay** — identify 1-2 causes; propose 1-2
  solutions; explain why the solutions will work.

### Step 4 — Write the introduction (3 minutes)

The introduction has 2 parts: **paraphrase the prompt** + **state your
prompt-type-specific thesis**. Do NOT copy the prompt wording.

Examples:

- **Opinion essay** — *"It is often argued that higher education should
  be free for all students. While I disagree with this view because of
  the financial implications, I acknowledge that there are valid reasons
  on both sides."*
- **Discussion essay** — *"Some people believe that social media has had
  a positive impact on society, while others argue that it has caused
  more harm than good. In my opinion, the negative effects outweigh the
  positive ones."*
- **Problem/Solution essay** — *"Youth unemployment in the Gulf region
  is a growing problem that has multiple causes, including the mismatch
  between education and the labour market and the limited availability of
  private-sector jobs. There are several solutions that governments can
  implement to address this issue."*

### Step 5 — Write body paragraphs (12 minutes each)

Body paragraph strategy depends on the prompt type:

- **Opinion essay** — 2 body paragraphs: each presents 1 reason for
  your position. Counter-argument paragraph: acknowledge + refute.
- **Discussion essay** — 2 body paragraphs: each presents 1 view (View 1
  in body 1, View 2 in body 2). Counter-argument paragraph: your
  opinion with 1 argument.
- **Problem/Solution essay** — 2 body paragraphs: body 1 presents
  causes; body 2 presents solutions.

Each body paragraph: topic sentence + explanation + example. Each body
paragraph is typically 80–100 words.

### Step 6 — Write counter-argument / extra paragraph (8 minutes)

- **Opinion essay** — counter-argument paragraph: acknowledge the other
  side + refute.
- **Discussion essay** — third paragraph: your opinion with 1
  argument.
- **Problem/Solution essay** — third paragraph: another solution +
  explanation.

### Step 7 — Write conclusion (3 minutes)

The conclusion restates the thesis + summarizes the main points. Do NOT
introduce new ideas in the conclusion.

## Previous Year Patterns (IELTS 2018–2025)

IELTS Writing Task 2 has used the same format consistently since 2018:
essay prompt + 250+ words in ~40 minutes. Over 2018–2025:

- 2018–2020: Task 2 commonly used opinion essays (50%+ of tests).
- 2021–2023: Task 2 diversified to include discussion and problem-
  solution essays (~25% of tests each).
- 2024–2025: Task 2 frequently uses discussion essays with two-part
  questions (e.g. "Why is this happening? Is this positive or
  negative?").

The pattern: IELTS Writing Task 2 essay types have diversified since
2018, with discussion essays becoming more frequent. Candidates who
practice all essay types outperform those who focus on opinion essays.
