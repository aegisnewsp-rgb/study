# Biomolecules

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

> Start here if your exam is tomorrow and Biomolecules is one chapter you keep postponing.

Biomolecules are organic compounds built mainly from **C, H, O, N**, with traces of **P** and **S**, that living cells synthesise and use. The single most testable split is into **micromolecules** (M < 1000 Da — amino acids, monosaccharides, nucleotides, fatty acids) and **macromolecules** (M > 1000 Da — proteins, polysaccharides, nucleic acids, lipids), the latter made by joining the former through **condensation** reactions.

| Building block (monomer) | Polymer formed | Bond joining units | Average residue mass |
|---|---|---|---|
| α-amino acid (H₂N–CHR–COOH) | Protein / polypeptide | Peptide bond (–CO–NH–) | ~110–120 Da |
| Monosaccharide (e.g. C₆H₁₂O₆) | Polysaccharide | Glycosidic bond | 162 Da per hexose unit |
| Nucleotide (base + sugar + PO₄) | Nucleic acid (DNA/RNA) | 3′–5′ phosphodiester | ~330 Da |
| Fatty acid + glycerol | Lipid (triglyceride) | Ester bond | ~800–900 Da per triacylglycerol |

> 💡 **Memory Hook:** "**P**roteins use **P**eptide bonds; **N**ucleic acids use **N**o-peptide (phosphodiester) bonds; **S**tarch uses **S**ugar (glycosidic) bonds." Peptide = drop of water (18 g mol⁻¹) released per bond.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

> Read this tier if you want the working understanding NEET actually tests.

#### Primary vs secondary metabolites

NEET frames the classification as a function question, not a memory question. **Primary metabolites** (amino acids, sugars, nucleotides, organic acids) participate directly in **growth, development and reproduction** — without them the cell stops functioning. **Secondary metabolites** (alkaloids, flavonoids, terpenes, rubber, antibiotics, essential oils) are by-products of pathways that fork off primary metabolism; they often serve ecological roles — defence against herbivores, UV protection, attraction of pollinators — and are not required for the cell's day-to-day survival.

#### Peptide bond formation

The general amino acid structure is **H₂N–CHR–COOH**, where **R** is a variable side chain. The 20 standard amino acids differ only in R (glycine has R = H). When two amino acids join:

H₂N–CHR₁–COOH + H₂N–CHR₂–COOH → H₂N–CHR₁–CO–NH–CHR₂–COOH + H₂O

The **–CO–NH–** linkage is the peptide bond, formed by condensation with release of **18 g mol⁻¹** of water per bond. A dipeptide has one peptide bond, a tripeptide two, and so on; an **n-residue polypeptide has (n − 1) peptide bonds**.

#### Starch vs cellulose — the same monomer, different geometry

Both are homopolymers of **glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆, M = 180 g mol⁻¹)**, but their glycosidic linkages differ:

| Polymer | Monomer | Linkage | Digestible by humans? | Function |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Starch (amylose) | α-D-glucose | α-1,4 | Yes | Energy store in plants |
| Glycogen | α-D-glucose | α-1,4 + α-1,6 branches | Yes (own metabolism) | Animal energy reserve |
| Cellulose | β-D-glucose | β-1,4 | **No** (no cellulase) | Plant cell wall |

#### Identification tests worth memorising

| Test | Reagent / principle | Detects |
|---|---|---|
| Biuret | Cu²⁺ in alkaline medium | Peptide bonds (→ protein) |
| Folin–Lowry / Bradford | Colour reagents | Total protein |
| Anthrone | Anthrone in H₂SO₄ | Carbohydrates (green) |
| Orcinol | Orcinol + FeCl₃ | RNA (pentose) |
| Dische (DPA) | Diphenylamine | DNA (deoxyribose) |
| Benedict's / Fehling's | Cu²⁺ reducing test | Reducing sugars |

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** A tripeptide is formed from three different α-amino acids (A, B, C). How many peptide bonds are present, how many water molecules are released during its synthesis, and what is its approximate molecular mass if each amino acid residue averages 110 Da? (NEET-style assertion-reason)

#### Step-by-step solution:

1. **Bonds:** Three residues linked linearly carry **n − 1 = 2 peptide bonds**.
2. **Water released:** Each peptide bond removes one H₂O; therefore **2 × 18 = 36 g mol⁻¹** of water is released during synthesis.
3. **Approximate mass:** Sum of three free amino acids = 3 × (110 + 18) = 3 × 128 = 384 Da, then subtract 2 × 18 = 36 Da of lost water → **≈ 348 Da**. Using the residue average of 110 Da directly: **3 × 110 = 330 Da** (this is the working shortcut examiners accept).
4. **Unit check:** Da = g mol⁻¹ (numerically equivalent for mass); the reaction stoichiometry is dimensionless per mole.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students double-count water loss, writing **3 H₂O released for 3 amino acids** instead of 2. Remember: *n* amino acids give **n − 1** bonds, not *n*.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

> Push past NCERT lines — these are the points where NEET's assertion-reason and statement-based questions come from.

#### Nucleic-acid backbone chemistry

Each nucleotide carries a nitrogenous base (purine — A, G; pyrimidine — C, T, U), a pentose sugar (deoxyribose in DNA, ribose in RNA), and one or more phosphate groups. Adjacent nucleotides join through a **3′–5′ phosphodiester bond**: the **3′–OH** of one sugar attacks the α-phosphate of the next, displacing a pyrophosphate. The sugar-phosphate chain therefore runs **5′ → 3′**, with nitrogenous bases projecting inward to pair across the helix. ATP, the energy currency, is a single nucleotide with three phosphates; hydrolytic cleavage of its terminal phosphoanhydride bond yields **ΔG°′ ≈ −30.5 kJ mol⁻¹** — the figure NEET expects, not a per-gram value.

#### Are lipids really polymers?

Strictly, no. A triglyceride is glycerol esterified with three fatty acids — three ester bonds, not a long repeating chain. NEET accepts the NCERT line that lipids are macromolecules because their molecular mass exceeds 1000 Da, but the *polymer* terminology is loose here. Wax, sterol and phospholipid subclasses each break this generalisation in their own way, which is why the safest answer to "is a lipid a polymer?" is **"it depends on the definition used"**.

#### Atomic composition — the 50-60-20-10-5 rule

By mass, a typical biomolecule is roughly **C 50–60 %, O 20–30 %, N 10–15 %, H 5–10 %**, with P and S together usually under 3 %. This is why **CHON** estimation (Liebig's method) and **Kjeldahl's nitrogen method** work as quantitative tools in biochemistry — the percentages are predictable enough to back-calculate protein content (N × 6.25 is the conventional factor).

#### Living-tissue composition

Fresh living tissue is ~**60–65 % water**, ~**20–25 % proteins**, ~**10–15 % carbohydrates**, ~**2–3 % lipids**, ~**1 % nucleic acids**, with the remainder inorganic salts. NEET has asked the water-percentage value directly in past papers, so treat it as recall-grade fact.

#### Advanced traps and edge cases

1. **Glucose in solution stays glucose.** A common mis-statement: "glucose exists as C₁₂H₂₄O₁₂ in water." It does not — the monomer formula (180 g mol⁻¹) is preserved; only ring–chain tautomerism occurs between α and β forms.
2. **ATP energy is per mole, not per gram.** Roughly 7 kcal mol⁻¹ of free energy is released on hydrolysis; per gram this is far less than fats (~9 kcal g⁻¹). Confusing the two is a frequent assertion-reason distractor.
3. **Reducing ends.** Only the free anomeric carbon reduces Benedict's reagent; in a polysaccharide the internal glycosidic carbons do not. Sucrose, with both anomeric carbons tied up, is non-reducing.
4. **20 standard amino acids — but >500 natural ones.** NEET asks only about the 20 encoded ones; selenocysteine and pyrrolysine are university-level exceptions.
5. **Primary metabolites can become secondary.** Phenylalanine (primary) is the precursor of alkaloids, flavonoids and lignin (secondary); the boundary is pathway-defined, not molecule-defined.

#### Practice prompts

1. Why does substitution of α-glucose by β-glucose in cellulose make it indigestible to humans even though the molecular formula remains (C₆H₁₀O₅)ₙ? (Hint: linkage geometry vs enzyme stereospecificity.)
2. If a protein contains 145 peptide bonds and each amino acid residue averages 110 Da, estimate the protein's molar mass and explain why the value is slightly lower than the simple sum 146 × 110 Da would suggest.
