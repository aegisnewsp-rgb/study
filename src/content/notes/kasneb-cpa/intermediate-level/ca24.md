# CA24: Auditing and Assurance

CA24 is one of the six Intermediate Level papers of the KASNEB CPA qualification. KASNEB states that examinations are held three times each year — April, August and December — and that CPA papers run for three hours, with morning sessions beginning at 9:00 a.m. and afternoon sessions at 2:00 p.m.

Auditing is the paper where structure beats volume. Almost every mark in it comes from knowing what a step is for and what would make it fail, rather than from memorising standard wording.

## The audit is a risk-driven argument

The chain that runs through the whole paper is: identify risk, assess risk, respond to risk, conclude. If a question gives you a scenario, that chain is usually the answer.

Risk assessment is at the assertion level, and you should know the assertions cold because they map onto the procedures. Existence — does the asset exist? Completeness — is everything recorded? Valuation — is it at the right amount? Rights and obligations — does the entity own it and owe it? Cut-off — is it in the right period? Presentation and disclosure — is it properly described?

An item that gives you "the client did not count inventory at year end" and asks which assertion is at risk is testing this mapping directly. Excessive inventory means existence; an unrecorded liability means completeness.

## Evidence, materiality and sampling

Audit evidence must be sufficient and appropriate. Sufficient is a quantity judgement — more when the risk is higher. Appropriate is a quality judgement — the evidence must be relevant and reliable, and reliability depends on its source and form.

External evidence is generally more reliable than internal, and evidence obtained directly by the auditor is more reliable than evidence obtained orally or through the client. But relevance still governs: a document that is highly reliable and proves nothing about the assertion is worth very little.

Materiality has both a quantitative benchmark and a qualitative dimension. An item below materiality can still be material if it changes a loss, affects a covenant, or concerns a related party. Items that are qualitatively material are the ones most often missed.

## Internal controls

A control is effective only if it prevents or detects misstatement promptly and at a cost reasonable relative to the benefit. Controls can be preventive or detective, manual or automated, and a control operated by the same person who benefits from the error is rarely a control at all.

Where controls are strong, the auditor may rely on them and reduce substantive testing. Where they are weak, substantive work rises and control testing is largely wasted.

## The auditor's report

Unmodified versus modified is the core distinction, and it turns on materiality and pervasiveness. A misstatement is material but not pervasive and you can qualify; a misstatement is both material and pervasive and you disclaim.

Going concern is separate from the opinion and requires its own treatment. Scope limitation caused by the client versus by the circumstances produces different conclusions, and that distinction is examined repeatedly because candidates flatten it.

The report also has to be independent of the entity — management prepares the statements, the auditor opines on them — and this separation is examined in its own right, particularly on ethical questions such as fee dependence and non-audit services.

## Common mistakes

Conflating materiality with significance. An item can be numerically small and qualitatively decisive.

Asserting that stronger controls always reduce substantive testing. They reduce it only if the controls are operating effectively and are relevant to the assertion.

Assuming a qualified opinion means the accounts are false. It means the auditor cannot obtain sufficient appropriate evidence, or disagrees with a material figure.

Missing the pervasiveness test when deciding between qualification and disclaimer.

Ignoring ethical independence. Fees and non-audit services are assessed as threats, and a candidate who never considers them loses a recurring cluster of marks.
