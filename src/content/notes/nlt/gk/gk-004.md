# Pakistan's Economy

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Pakistan runs a **mixed, semi-industrialized developing economy** organized under a federal structure, with fiscal authority vested in the **Ministry of Finance**, monetary policy executed by the **State Bank of Pakistan (SBP)**, and national accounts compiled by the **Pakistan Bureau of Statistics (PBS)**. Output is measured through **Gross Domestic Product (GDP)** using the expenditure identity **GDP = C + I + G + (X − M)**, where C is consumption, I is investment, G is government spending, and (X − M) is net exports. **GNP = GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA)**, so a country with large outflows of profit and remittance outflows can have a GNP lower than its GDP. The services sector dominates at roughly **53–55% of GDP**, with agriculture at **22–24%** and industry at **20–22%**. For NLT Pakistan, expect MCQs on sectoral shares, top exports (textiles, rice), and what the SBP actually controls — **the policy rate and money supply**, not the federal budget.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Sectoral composition of GDP
Pakistan's GDP is dominated by the **services sector** (wholesale and retail trade, transport, finance, telecommunications, public administration), contributing roughly **53–55%**. **Industry** (manufacturing, construction, mining, energy) accounts for about **20–22%**, and **agriculture** (crops, livestock, forestry, fisheries) — despite employing the largest share of the labour force — contributes only around **22–24%**. The NLT trap is to assume agriculture is the largest sector; by share of output it is not.

#### National income identities
- **GDP (market price)** sums the value of all final goods and services produced within Pakistan in a year.
- **GNP = GDP + NFIA**, where NFIA is the income earned by Pakistani factors abroad minus income earned by foreign factors in Pakistan. Because Pakistan is a net remittance recipient but a net profit repatriation payer, the two figures are not identical.
- **Real GDP = (Nominal GDP / GDP Deflator) × 100** removes the effect of price changes, so real growth reflects actual output change rather than inflation.
- **Per Capita Income = National Income / Total Population** is the standard living-standard proxy cited in PBS yearbooks.

#### Trade structure
**Major exports** are dominated by **textiles and apparel** (over 60% of merchandise exports), followed by rice (especially Basmati), leather goods, surgical instruments, and IT services. **Major imports** are **petroleum and petroleum products**, machinery, chemicals, fertilizers, and edible oils — the import bill is heavily exposed to global oil prices, which directly drives the **current account deficit** (the balance of goods, services, and primary/secondary income).

#### Fiscal side and monetary authority
The federal **budget** sets revenue (taxes, non-tax) and expenditure, and any shortfall is the **fiscal deficit = Total Expenditure − Total Revenue**, usually financed through domestic borrowing or external loans. The **State Bank of Pakistan** conducts **monetary policy** through the **policy (interest) rate**, open market operations, and reserve requirements, targeting inflation rather than fiscal balance. **Remittances** from overseas Pakistanis are recorded as **secondary income** in the current account, not as exports of goods.

#### Typical NLT question patterns
1. Identify which sector contributes the largest share of GDP.
2. Distinguish GDP from GNP, or nominal from real GDP.
3. Match an institution (SBP, PBS, Ministry of Finance) to its function.
4. Identify an item as an export, import, remittance, or FDI component.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Inflation measurement and policy levers
Pakistan reports inflation through the **Consumer Price Index (CPI)** compiled by PBS. The headline **Inflation Rate = ((Current Year Index − Base Year Index) / Base Year Index) × 100**. The SBP cannot control imported oil prices directly, but raises the **policy rate** to anchor inflation expectations and stabilise the rupee. Fiscal slippages (lower tax collection, higher subsidies) and currency depreciation pass through to CPI within 1–3 months, which is why inflation in Pakistan has historically spiked after IMF programme negotiations or fuel-price adjustments.

#### External sector and debt
**Foreign exchange reserves** are held by the SBP and used to defend the rupee and service external debt. Chronic **current account deficits** combined with **fiscal deficits** (the so-called **twin deficits**) force Pakistan into repeated **IMF Extended Fund Facility** programmes, often conditional on energy subsidy reform, tax-base expansion, and exchange-rate flexibility. **External debt servicing** crowds out **Public Sector Development Programme (PSDP)** allocations, slowing infrastructure spending.

#### Common mistakes to avoid
- **GDP vs GNP**: GNP includes net factor income from abroad; do not treat them as interchangeable.
- **Fiscal vs current account deficit**: the former is government revenue minus expenditure; the latter is the balance of goods, services, and income flows with the rest of the world.
- **Nominal vs real growth**: a 20% nominal GDP rise with 18% inflation means only ~2% real growth.
- **Remittances are not exports** — they enter the current account as secondary income, which is why they ease the current account deficit without changing the trade balance.
- **Agriculture is not the largest GDP sector**; services are, even though agriculture still employs the most people.
