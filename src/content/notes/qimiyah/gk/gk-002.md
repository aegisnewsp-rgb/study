# History of the Saudi Kingdom and the Arabian Peninsula

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The modern **Kingdom of Saudi Arabia** was proclaimed on **23 September 1932** by **King Abdulaziz ibn Abdul Rahman Al Saud (Ibn Saud)** after unifying the regions of Najd, Hejaz, Asir, and Al-Hasa. The Saudi statehood story spans three polities: the **First Saudi State** (Emirate of Diriyah, 1727–1818), the **Second Saudi State** (Emirate of Riyadh, 1824–1891), and the Third Saudi State founded in 1932.

Key dates to memorise:

| Year | Event |
|------|-------|
| 570 CE | Birth of Prophet Muhammad in Mecca |
| 622 CE | Hijrah — Year 1 of the Islamic calendar |
| 1727 | First Saudi State established at Diriyah |
| 1902 | Recapture of Riyadh by Ibn Saud |
| 1932 | Kingdom of Saudi Arabia declared |

Remember the caliphate order — **Rashidun (Medina) → Umayyad (Damascus) → Abbasid (Baghdad)** — and that oil was struck in **1938 at Dammam No. 7, Dhahran**.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Origins of the Saudi State

The **House of Saud** (Al Saud) and the **Al ash-Sheikh** clerical family formed an alliance in 1744 rooted in **Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab's** reformist teachings, later called **Wahhabism**. This pact gave the Saudi emirs religious legitimacy while the ulema gained political protection. The **First Saudi State** under Muhammad ibn Saud expanded from Diriyah to control most of the Najd, attacking Ottoman caravans and reaching Ottoman Iraq. Its destruction came in 1818 at the hands of Ottoman-Egyptian forces led by Ibrahim Pasha, ending the Emirate of Diriyah.

#### Second State and the Path to Unification

The **Second Saudi State** rose in 1824 under Turki ibn Abdullah, with Riyadh as its capital, but lost control of the Hejaz to the Sharif of Mecca and was finally extinguished in 1891 by the rival **Al Rashid** dynasty. In **1902**, young Abdulaziz recaptured Riyadh in a daring night assault — the event that became the foundation myth of the modern Kingdom. Over the next three decades he consolidated Najd (1904–1912), absorbed Al-Hasa from the Ottomans in 1913, took the **Asir** highlands (1920s), and finally conquered the **Hejaz** with the surrender of Taif (1924) and Jeddah (1925). The Kingdom was formally declared on **23 September 1932**, with the Hejaz's **Kingdom of Hejaz** and the **Sultanate of Najd** merged into a single state.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Pre-Islamic and Islamic-Era Foundations

Before Islam, the Peninsula hosted **tribal confederations** such as the Quraysh in Mecca, **incense trade routes** linking southern Arabia to the Levant, and regional powers including the **Kingdom of Himyar** in Yemen and the **Lakhmid** and **Ghassanid** client states on the desert fringes. The Prophet Muhammad was born in Mecca around **570 CE**; the **Hijrah** to Medina in **622 CE** initiated the Islamic calendar and the political community (ummah) that soon controlled the entire Peninsula within a generation.

#### Caliphate Succession and Ottoman Overrule

After the Prophet's death (632), the **Rashidun Caliphs** ruled from Medina (632–661), followed by the **Umayyad Caliphate** (661–750) seated in **Damascus**, then the **Abbasid Caliphate** (750–1258) based in **Baghdad**. The Ottoman conquest of the Hejaz in **1517** placed Mecca and Medina under Istanbul's suzerainty for four centuries. The **Sharif Hussein revolt (1916)**, allied with Britain via the **McMahon Correspondence**, briefly produced the **Kingdom of Hejaz** before Ibn Saud absorbed it in 1924–1925.

#### Modern Transformation

Oil was struck on **3 March 1938** at the **Dammam No. 7 well** in Dhahran by Texaco geologists, transforming Saudi finances within a decade. Subsequent milestones include **abolition of slavery (1962)**, the **Basic Law of Governance (1992)** which codified the state as a monarchy ruled by Sharia and the Qur'an, female entry into the Consultative Council, and the launch of **Vision 2030** under Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, aiming to diversify the economy beyond hydrocarbons.

#### Common Mistakes and Practice Prompts

Candidates often confuse the founding dates of the three Saudi states or the capitals of the caliphates. Watch for trick options swapping Damascus and Baghdad, or placing Riyadh's capture in 1919 instead of 1902.

2. Trace the unification timeline from 1902 to 23 September 1932, naming each region absorbed.
