# II. Force and Motion — HKDSE Physics Notes

This topic covers Topic II of the Compulsory Part of HKDSE Physics. It addresses position and movement (displacement, velocity, acceleration, motion graphs); force and motion (Newton's three laws, free-body diagrams, friction); projectile motion (horizontal and angled launch, trajectory equations); work, energy and power (kinetic and potential energy, conservation of energy); momentum (impulse, conservation of momentum, collisions); uniform circular motion (centripetal force and acceleration); and gravitation (Newton's law of gravitation, gravitational field strength, g).

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Scalar**: a quantity with magnitude only (e.g. mass, time, speed, energy).
- **Vector**: a quantity with magnitude and direction (e.g. displacement, velocity, acceleration, force, momentum).
- **Displacement**: change in position (vector). Distance: total path length (scalar).
- **Speed**: distance/time (scalar). Velocity: displacement/time (vector). Acceleration: change in velocity/time (vector).
- **Equations of motion (uniform acceleration)**: v = u + at; s = ut + ½at²; v² = u² + 2as; s = ½(u + v)t.
- **Newton's three laws**: 1. An object continues at rest or in uniform motion unless acted on by a net force. 2. F = ma (net force equals mass times acceleration). 3. For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.
- **Free-body diagram**: shows all forces on an object as vectors.
- **Friction**: opposes motion; proportional to normal force for dry friction.
- **Projectile motion**: horizontal and vertical motions are independent. Horizontal velocity is constant; vertical motion has constant acceleration g.
- **Work**: W = F·s·cos θ (force times displacement times angle between them). Units: J.
- **Kinetic energy**: Ek = ½mv². Gravitational potential energy: Ep = mgh (near Earth's surface).
- **Power**: P = W/t (rate of doing work). P = Fv (force times velocity for constant motion).
- **Conservation of energy**: total energy in a closed system is constant.
- **Momentum**: p = mv. Conservation of momentum: in a closed system, total momentum before = total momentum after.
- **Impulse**: F·Δt = Δp.
- **Collision**: elastic (kinetic energy conserved) or inelastic (kinetic energy not conserved).
- **Uniform circular motion**: centripetal acceleration a = v²/r; centripetal force F = mv²/r.
- **Gravitation**: F = Gm₁m₂/r² (Newton's law of gravitation); G is the gravitational constant; g is the gravitational field strength (acceleration due to gravity).

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing mass and weight — mass is constant; weight is the force of gravity on an object.
- Treating momentum and kinetic energy as the same — they are different; momentum is a vector (mv), kinetic energy is a scalar (½mv²).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Kinematics

**Uniform acceleration equations** (for constant a):

- v = u + at
- s = ut + ½at²
- v² = u² + 2as
- s = ½(u + v)t

**Graphs**:

- Position-time graph: slope is velocity; curvature indicates acceleration.
- Velocity-time graph: slope is acceleration; area under curve is displacement.

For non-uniform acceleration, calculus is used (v = ds/dt, a = dv/dt).

#### Forces and Newton's laws

**Newton's first law (inertia)**: an object continues at rest or in uniform motion in a straight line unless acted on by a net external force.

**Newton's second law**: F_net = ma (vector equation). The net force is the vector sum of all forces on the object.

**Newton's third law**: for every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction. The two forces act on different objects.

**Common forces**: weight (mg, downward), normal force (perpendicular to surface), tension (along string/cable), friction (opposes motion), air resistance (opposes motion through air).

**Free-body diagrams**: draw all forces on the object as arrows starting from the centre of the object. The arrows should be labelled with the type of force and the magnitude.

#### Projectile motion

A projectile launched with initial velocity u at angle θ above horizontal:

- Horizontal velocity: u·cos θ (constant).
- Vertical velocity: u·sin θ - gt.
- Horizontal range: u²·sin 2θ/g.
- Maximum height: u²·sin²θ/(2g).
- Time of flight: 2u·sin θ/g.

The trajectory is a parabola. Horizontal and vertical motions are independent.

#### Work, energy and power

**Work W = F·d·cos θ** (force times displacement times cosine of angle).

**Kinetic energy Ek = ½mv²**.

**Gravitational potential energy (near Earth) Ep = mgh**.

**Conservation of energy**: in a closed system, total energy is conserved. KE + PE + heat + work = constant.

**Power**: P = W/t = Fv (for constant velocity). Units: W (watts) = J/s.

#### Momentum and collisions

**Momentum p = mv**. Units: kg·m/s. Vector.

**Conservation of momentum**: in a closed system, total momentum is conserved in any collision or explosion.

**Impulse J = F·Δt = Δp**. The area under a force-time graph.

**Collisions**:

- **Elastic**: KE conserved (e.g. billiard balls).
- **Inelastic**: KE not conserved (e.g. most car crashes).
- **Perfectly inelastic**: objects stick together after collision (e.g. ballistic pendulum).

#### Uniform circular motion

An object moving in a circle at constant speed has:

- **Centripetal acceleration a = v²/r**, directed toward the centre of the circle.
- **Centripetal force F = ma = mv²/r**, directed toward the centre.

Sources of centripetal force: gravity (for satellites, planets), tension (for objects on strings), friction (for cars turning), normal force (for banked turns).

#### Gravitation

**Newton's law of universal gravitation**: F = Gm₁m₂/r². The gravitational force between two point masses m₁ and m₂ is attractive, with magnitude proportional to the product of the masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.

**Gravitational field strength g**: the gravitational force per unit mass at a point. At Earth's surface, g ≈ 9.81 m/s². g varies with distance from Earth (g ∝ 1/r²).

**Gravitational potential energy (general)**: Ep = -Gm₁m₂/r (negative; convention).

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-topic links

- **I. Heat and Gases**: kinetic theory relates microscopic motion to temperature.
- **III. Wave Motion**: SHM links to circular motion.
- **V. Radioactivity and Nuclear Energy**: nuclear reactions conserve momentum.

#### Exam technique

- **For kinematics questions**: identify the type of motion (uniform, non-uniform, projectile); choose the appropriate equation or graph.
- **For force questions**: draw a free-body diagram; apply Newton's second law.
- **For momentum questions**: identify the system; check whether the net external force is zero (then momentum is conserved).
- **For circular motion questions**: identify the source of centripetal force; use a = v²/r.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Air resistance is always negligible." At low speeds it is negligible; at high speeds it dominates.
- "An object in circular motion has no net force." Wrong — it has a net centripetal force toward the centre.
- "Weight and mass are the same." Mass is the amount of matter; weight is the gravitational force on that matter (W = mg).
