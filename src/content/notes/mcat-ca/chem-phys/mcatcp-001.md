# Translational Motion, Forces and Work — MCAT Chem/Phys

Translational motion, forces and work form AAMC Foundational Concept 4 of the Chem/Phys section. The MCAT tests these first-year physics concepts in the context of biological systems — body movement, blood flow, muscle contraction, and lab techniques. Most items pair a short physics passage with a multiple-choice item that requires applying the formulas to a new situation.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### Core formulas

- **Velocity:** v = Δx / Δt (units: m/s).
- **Acceleration:** a = Δv / Δt (units: m/s²).
- **Newton's second law:** F = m × a (force in newtons, mass in kg, acceleration in m/s²).
- **Work:** W = F × d × cos(θ) (work in joules, force in N, distance in m, θ the angle between F and d).
- **Kinetic energy:** KE = (1/2) × m × v² (energy in joules).
- **Potential energy (gravitational):** PE = m × g × h (g ≈ 9.8 m/s² near Earth's surface).
- **Power:** P = W / t (power in watts, work in joules, time in s).

#### One-pass drill

1. Identify the quantity asked for: velocity, acceleration, force, work, energy, or power.
2. List the known quantities from the passage.
3. Pick the formula that connects knowns to the unknown.
4. Solve algebraically first; plug in numbers last.
5. Check the units of the answer against the expected units.

#### Examiner traps

- Mixing up distance and displacement. Distance is a scalar; displacement is a vector with direction.
- Forgetting to convert units (cm to m, g to kg, min to s). The MCAT uses SI units.
- Treating weight and mass as the same. Weight is a force (N); mass is a quantity of matter (kg).

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Translational kinematics

The MCAT tests the kinematic equations in one dimension with constant acceleration:

- v = v₀ + a × t
- x = x₀ + v₀ × t + (1/2) × a × t²
- v² = v₀² + 2 × a × Δx

For projectile motion in two dimensions, treat horizontal and vertical motion independently. Horizontal velocity is constant (ignoring air resistance); vertical motion is constant-acceleration under gravity.

#### Newton's laws in biological contexts

- **First law (inertia):** A body at rest stays at rest; a body in motion stays in motion at constant velocity unless acted on by a net force.
- **Second law:** F = m × a. The acceleration of an object is proportional to the net force and inversely proportional to the mass.
- **Third law:** For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.

The MCAT often frames Newton's laws in biological terms: the force a muscle applies, the force of blood against a vessel wall, the force needed to lift a limb.

#### Work, energy and power

**Work** is energy transferred by a force acting through a distance. The MCAT formula is W = F × d × cos(θ); θ is the angle between the force and the displacement.

**Energy** is conserved. The work-energy theorem: W = ΔKE. The total mechanical energy (KE + PE) is conserved in the absence of non-conservative forces (friction, drag).

**Power** is the rate at which work is done: P = W / t. The MCAT often asks for power in watts (1 W = 1 J/s).

#### Practice routine

- Take 10 kinematic-equation items. Solve each algebraically before plugging in numbers.
- Take 5 work-energy items. Identify the forces, the displacements, and the angles before computing.
- Take 5 power items. Power is rate; check the time unit.
- Pair each set with an AAMC-style passage.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How this topic connects to the other Chem/Phys topics

- **Equilibrium, torque and circuits** extend the force concept to static situations and to electric forces.
- **Kinetics** uses the work-energy theorem implicitly: activation energy is the work needed to reach the transition state.
- **Acid-base and electrochemistry** rely on the same energy concepts (Gibbs free energy, electrochemical work).

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"Velocity is always positive."** Velocity is a vector. Negative velocity means motion in the opposite direction.
- **"Work is always positive."** Work is positive when the force and displacement are in the same direction; negative when they are opposite; zero when perpendicular.
- **"Power is the same as energy."** Power is the rate at which energy is transferred or used. A high-power device can do a small amount of work in a short time.

#### Exam technique

- Draw a free-body diagram for every force problem. Mark the direction of every force.
- List knowns and unknowns. Pick the formula that connects them.
- Solve algebraically before plugging in numbers.
- Check units. The MCAT uses SI units; convert before computing.
- For projectile motion, separate horizontal and vertical motion.
- Use the platform's calculator for arithmetic.
