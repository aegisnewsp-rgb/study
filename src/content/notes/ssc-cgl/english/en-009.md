# Sentence Improvement

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Sentence Improvement is a four-option MCQ in SSC CGL English where one underlined part of a sentence (occasionally the whole sentence) must be replaced with the most accurate, idiomatic, and grammatically correct alternative. The test checks your ear for **Subject–Verb Agreement**, **tense concordance with time-markers**, **voice (Active/Passive)**, **direct–indirect speech shifts**, **parallel structure**, and **fixed idioms/phrases**.

| Key Term | What to Verify |
|---|---|
| Subject–Verb Agreement | Verb number must match the **real** subject, ignoring phrases between them |
| Tense Consistency | Verb tense must agree with the time-marker (yesterday, since, for, ago) |
| Active–Passive | Only transitive verbs convert; object becomes new subject |
| Direct–Indirect | Pronoun shift, tense back-shift, de-quotation |
| Idiom/Phrase | Must use the standard, fixed preposition or particle |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"A-T-V-D-I-P"** — **A**greement, **T**ense, **V**oice, **D**irect–Indirect, **I**diom, **P**reposition. Scan the underlined portion in that order; it catches 90% of SSC CGL items in under 30 seconds.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Grammar Checks
SSC CGL frames Sentence Improvement around six recurring grammar pillars. Master each before attempting mocks.

1. **Subject–Verb Agreement:** Identify the true head noun. Phrases like *with, along with, together with, as well as, besides, including* do **not** pluralise the verb. Collective nouns (*committee, team, jury*) take singular when acting as a unit.
2. **Tense Concord:** A single time-marker governs the clause. *Since + point in time → Present Perfect*; *for + duration → Perfect Continuous*; *ago/last/yesterday → Simple Past*.
3. **Voice (Active/Passive):** Only **transitive** verbs (those taking a direct object) admit passive conversion. The original object becomes the new subject; the agent is introduced with *by*.
4. **Direct–Indirect Speech:** Reporting verb in past (*said, told*) triggers: tense back-shift, pronoun shift (1st→3rd, 2nd→1st if addressee), place/time shift (*here→there, now→then, today→that day*), and removal of inverted commas.
5. **Modals & Semi-Modals:** *Should, would, can, can, can* back-shift to *should→would, can→can, can→can* when the reporting verb is past.
6. **Idioms & Prepositions:** Learn the verb's fixed partner. *Discuss, lack, enter, reach, comprise, emphasise, request* are **transitive** — never take *about / into / to / of / for*.

#### Comparison Matrix: Easily Confused Items

| Category | Wrong Form (Trap) | Correct Form | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tense | *He has went home.* | *He has gone home.* | *go* → past participle *gone*, not *went* |
| Voice | *The book is being wrote by her.* | *The book is being written by her.* | *write* → past participle *written* |
| Direct–Indirect | *He said, "I am tired."* → *He said that he is tired.* | *He said that he was tired.* | Present → past back-shift |
| Idiom | *We should *discuss about* the issue.* | *We should discuss the issue.* | *Discuss* is transitive |
| Parallelism | *She likes reading *and to swim*.* | *She likes reading and swimming.* | -ing/-ing; *to*/*to*; noun/noun — never mixed |
| Agreement | *Each of the boys *have* arrived.* | *Each of the boys has arrived.* | *Each* is singular |
| Comparison | *He is *more better* than me.* | *He is better than me.* | *Better* is already comparative |
| Preposition | *She is *lacking in* courage.* | *She lacks courage.* OR *She is lacking courage.* | *Lack* = transitive |

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** *Choose the most appropriate alternative for the underlined part.*
"The team of engineers **have submitted** their report yesterday, and the manager has **accepted** it without any changes."
(a) has submitted … accepted
(b) have submitted … has accepted
(c) has submitted … accepted
(d) had submitted … accepted

#### Solution:

- **Agreement check:** *The team* is a collective noun acting as a unit → singular verb. Phrase *of engineers* is a prepositional modifier, not the subject. So **"have" → "has"**.
- **Tense check:** Time-marker *yesterday* → Simple Past needed for both verbs. *Has accepted* is present perfect, which conflicts with *yesterday*.
- Apply both fixes: **"has submitted … accepted"**.
- Option **(c)** matches both corrections.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students in many papers jump to (d) *had submitted … accepted*, assuming the past perfect is "more past." But there is **no earlier past action** to compare against; the simple past alone satisfies *yesterday*. Past perfect requires the context *by/before/already/when another past event happened*. Choosing (d) introduces an unnecessary tense shift.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Advanced Traps and Edge Cases

| Trap Type | Hidden Cue in Sentence | Correct Response |
|---|---|---|
| Intervening phrase | *The box of chocolates **are** on the table.* | Singular verb — *box* is the head noun, not *chores/chocolates* |
| Pronoun shift | *Neither of the applicants **has** submitted **their** resume.* | **His or her** (formal) — *neither* is singular |
| Comparative doubling | *This book is **more superior** than that one.* | **Superior** already encodes comparison — drop *more* |
| Time-marker mismatch | *I **am working** here since 2019.* | **Have been working** — *since* demands present perfect continuous |
| Passive with intransitive | *The accident **was happened** near the bridge.* | **Happened** — *happen* is intransitive; no passive form |
| Redundant idiom | *He **returned back** to his hometown.* | **Returned** — *back* is pleonastic |
| Article omission | *He is **M.A.** in English.* | **An M.A.** — vowel *sound* (em) needs *an* |
| Modal sequence | *He **should must** complete the task.* | **Must** — double modal is ungrammatical |

#### Micro-Worked Example: Direct–Indirect Conversion

**Original:** Sita said to me, "I will help you **now** because I am free **here**."
#### Converted (with reporting verb in past):
Sita told me that she would help me **then** because she was free **there**.

| Element | Original | After Conversion |
|---|---|---|
| Reporting verb | *said to* | *told* |
| First pronoun (speaker) | *I* | *she* |
| Second pronoun (listener) | *you* | *me* |
| Modal *will* | *will* | *would* |
| Verb *am* | *am* | *was* |
| Time word | *now* | *then* |
| Place word | *here* | *there* |
| Inverted commas | " … " | removed |

#### Exam Strategy for SSC CGL

- Tier-I in standard papers carries **2–3 Sentence Improvement items** out of 25 English questions; Tier-II Paper-II adds another **2–3**.
- Allocate **45 seconds per item**: 10 s to identify the grammar pillar, 20 s to test each option against that pillar, 15 s to re-read the full sentence for fluency.
- When two options both fix the visible error, prefer the one that also resolves a *secondary* issue (agreement + tense in one go).

#### Two Practice Prompts

1. *"The list of items **were** placed on the table, and each one **represent** a different category."* — Identify and correct both agreement errors without altering word count beyond standard substitutions.
2. *"He said, 'I **have bought** this book **yesterday**.'"* — Convert to indirect speech and justify each pronoun, tense, and time-marker shift.
