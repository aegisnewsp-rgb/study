# IJMB Registration, Examination Process and University Admission Strategy

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

IJMB (Interim Joint Matriculation Board) is the NUC-approved one-year A'Level programme that grants Direct Entry (DE) admission into 200-level of Nigerian universities. The candidate must hold five O'Level credit passes (WAEC/NECO/NABTEB) including English Language and Mathematics, register on the IJMB portal with a scratch card PIN, pick three subjects aligned with the target faculty, write the IJMB examination at a designated centre, and then separately sit JAMB DE before any university can offer admission.

- Five O'Level credits (English + Maths mandatory), max two sittings.
- Pick three IJMB subjects that match the intended degree course.
- IJMB alone does not admit — JAMB DE form is mandatory for admission.
- Grading scale: A=9, B=8, C=6, D=4, E=2; target A's in principal subjects.

| Stage | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Eligibility | 5 O'Level credits incl. English & Maths |
| Registration | IJMB portal → scratch card → affiliated centre |
| Examination | Written papers at IJMB centres (≈Mar/Apr) |
| Admission | IJMB result + JAMB DE form → DE admission |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Eligibility and O'Level baseline

Before paying any IJMB fee, the candidate must already hold at least five credit-level passes in O'Level subjects (WAEC, NECO, or NABTEB) including English Language and Mathematics, obtained across no more than two sittings. Universities screen DE applicants on this baseline first; a candidate missing English or Maths is screened out even with strong IJMB grades. NABTEB is permissible only when paired with WAEC or NECO in the same sitting rule.

#### Registration procedure

Registration runs through the official IJMB portal (`ijmb.com.ng`). The candidate purchases a scratch card PIN, fills in bio-data, O'Level details, and chooses three IJMB subjects, uploads a passport photograph, prints the registration slip, and presents it physically at an affiliated IJMB study centre. Only centres officially accredited by the IJMB office issue examination numbers — unofficial agents cannot register candidates for the national examination.

#### Subject combination strategy

Subject choice is the single most consequential decision in the programme. The three IJMB subjects must map directly onto the target faculty's prerequisites, for example:

- Medicine / Pharmacy / Nursing → Physics, Chemistry, Biology.
- Engineering → Physics, Chemistry, Mathematics.
- Law / Political Science → Economics, Government, History or Literature in English.
- Accounting / Business → Economics, Mathematics, Government or Geography.

Mismatched combinations invalidate DE eligibility regardless of the grade scored.

#### Examination and grading

Papers are written at designated IJMB centres, typically after the academic session closes (March/April window). Each subject is graded on the NUC IJMB scale A=9, B=8, C=6, D=4, E=2, with anything below E scored 0. Universities typically compute the aggregate as (IJMB Score × 0.60) + (JAMB DE Score × 0.40).

| Grade | Points | Typical DE meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | 9 | Excellent — competitive for top courses |
| B | 8 | Very good — meets most DE cut-offs |
| C | 6 | Upper credit — meets many DE cut-offs |
| D | 4 | Lower credit — limited course access |
| E | 2 | Minimum pass — rarely sufficient alone |

#### Linking IJMB to admission

A candidate who scores well but skips the JAMB DE form cannot be offered admission by any Nigerian university. After IJMB releases results, the candidate purchases the JAMB DE form, lists IJMB as the A'Level qualification, chooses universities on CAPS, and arranges transcript procurement from the affiliated IJMB centre. Federal, state, and most private universities accept IJMB; some private, military, and professional programmes do not — verification on the institution's DE page is mandatory before choosing institutions.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge cases and the re-sit framework

A failed IJMB attempt is not final. Per IJMB regulations, candidates may re-sit at the next session, but the number of re-sit attempts is capped, and only the best grade counts toward the final transcript. A candidate with an O'Level deficiency discovered mid-programme cannot remediate the IJMB pathway until the missing credit is cleared, because universities verify O'Level before IJMB. NABTEB-only candidates must confirm that the target institution treats NABTEB as equivalent to WAEC/NECO for DE purposes.

#### Aggregate calculation worked example

Suppose a Medicine candidate scores A in Physics (9), A in Chemistry (9), and B in Biology (8). Grade points = 26; maximum = 27; IJMB Score = (26 ÷ 27) × 100 ≈ 96.3%. If the same candidate scores 70 on the JAMB DE (rescaled to /100 = 17.5), the aggregate becomes (96.3 × 0.60) + (17.5 × 0.40) = 57.78 + 7.00 = 64.78. Most public universities publish DE cut-offs in the 55–70 band for competitive courses, so the bottleneck in this scenario is the JAMB DE component — not the IJMB score.

#### Common mistakes that void DE eligibility

- Choosing IJMB subjects that do not match the target faculty's DE requirements.
- Registering through unaccredited agents who issue fake examination numbers.
- Omitting English Language or Mathematics from the five O'Level credits.
- Treating IJMB as a substitute for JAMB DE rather than a complement.
- Applying through CAPS without procuring the IJMB transcript from the study centre.
- Confusing IJMB with JUPEB or Cambridge A'Level — each has its own portal, syllabus, and grading.
