# Environment

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

- **Environment** = the sum total of **physical, chemical, biological and socio-cultural surroundings** of an organism, covering both natural ecosystems and human-modified systems that govern resource flows and life processes.
- The **10% law of energy flow** (Raymond Lindeman) states that only about 10% of energy transfers from one trophic level to the next; the rest is lost as heat.
- **Four biodiversity hotspots of India**: Western Ghats, Himalaya, Indo-Burma, Sundaland — together hosting ~4% of global flora/fauna.
- **BOD** (Biochemical Oxygen Demand) = DO_initial − DO_final (mg/L); high BOD ⇒ water is heavily polluted.
- **Doubling Time** T_d = 70 / r (Rule of 70) — quick way to estimate population doubling from percentage growth.
- UPSC loves: matching conventions (Ramsar, CITES, CBD, UNFCCC, Kyoto, Paris), India-specific Acts (1972, 1980, 1986, 2002), and 10% energy-flow numericals.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Ecosystem Structure and Function
An **ecosystem** is a functional unit of **biotic components** (producers, consumers, decomposers) interacting with **abiotic components** (light, temperature, water, soil, nutrients). Energy flow is **unidirectional** and obeys Lindeman's **10% law**: of the energy at trophic level *n*, only ~10% reaches trophic level *n+1*. In contrast, **nutrient cycling** (carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus, hydrological) is cyclic. Key ecosystem types include **biomes** (tundra, taiga, tropical rainforest, desert, grassland) and the **biosphere** (zone of life on Earth).

#### Biodiversity
**Biodiversity** has three levels — **genetic** (variation within species), **species** (variety among species) and **ecosystem** (variety of habitats). Latitudinal gradient shows richness highest near the equator and lowest at poles. India hosts **4 of 34 global biodiversity hotspots**. Biodiversity value is classified as **direct** (food, medicine), **indirect** (ecosystem services), **option** (future use) and **existence** (moral/aesthetic).

#### Pollution and Pollutants
Major pollution types: **air** (PM2.5, SO₂, NOₓ, CO, VOCs), **water** (high **BOD**, heavy metals, pathogens), **soil** (pesticides, e-waste) and **noise**. **Eutrophication** occurs when nutrient overloading (nitrates/phosphates) triggers algal blooms, depleting dissolved oxygen. **Biomagnification** = increasing toxin concentration up the food chain (e.g., DDT, mercury).

#### Climate Change and Ozone
**Greenhouse effect** is natural; **enhanced greenhouse effect** is driven by anthropogenic CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, CFCs. **GWP (Global Warming Potential)** compares per-molecule warming of a gas vs CO₂ over 100 years. India's **NAPCC (2008)** has **8 national missions** (Solar, Enhanced Energy Efficiency, Sustainable Habitat, Water, Himalayan, Green India, Sustainable Agriculture, Strategic Knowledge). **Ozone depletion** is caused by **CFCs/halons**; addressed by the **1987 Montreal Protocol**.

#### Indian Environmental Legislation
| Year | Act | Focus |
|------|-----|-------|
| 1972 | Wildlife Protection Act | Schedules I–VI for species protection |
| 1974 | Water (Prevention & Control of Pollution) Act | Water pollution |
| 1980 | Forest Conservation Act | Restricts forest diversion |
| 1981 | Air (Prevention & Control of Pollution) Act | Air pollution |
| 1986 | Environment Protection Act | Umbrella Act post-Bhopal |
| 2002 | Biological Diversity Act | Access & benefit sharing |

**Conservation** has two routes: **in-situ** (National Parks, Wildlife Sanctuaries, Biosphere Reserves, Tiger Reserves, Community Reserves) and **ex-situ** (zoos, botanical gardens, **seed banks**, gene banks, cryopreservation).

#### Sustainable Development
Defined by the **Brundtland Report (1987)** as development that meets present needs without compromising future generations. Linked to **SDGs 13 (Climate Action), 14 (Life Below Water), 15 (Life on Land)**. **Carrying capacity** is the maximum population an ecosystem can sustain; **ecological footprint** measures human demand against biocapacity (global hectares).

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Mechanism Depth
- **Ecological succession**: primary (on bare rock, e.g., volcanic island) vs secondary (on disturbed soil). Climax community is the relatively stable end-stage; modern ecology accepts **multiple stable states** rather than a single climax.
- **Carbon sequestration** natural pathways (oceans ~25%, forests ~30% of anthropogenic CO₂) versus engineered pathways (CCS, DACCS, afforestation under REDD+). Distinguish **gross** vs **net** sequestration — soil carbon can re-emit under warming.
- **Coral bleaching** is not death but **expulsion of zooxanthellae** under heat stress (>1–2°C above summer maximum for 4+ weeks); reefs can recover if stress is brief.
- **Mangroves** act as **carbon sinks** ("blue carbon", 3–5× higher per-hectare sequestration than tropical forests) and as bioshields against tsunamis/cyclones.
- **Ramsar sites** in India (currently 80+): only wetlands of international importance under the **1971 Ramsar Convention**. UPSC often asks the *largest* (Loktak) or *criteria-based* examples.

#### Common Mistakes and Traps
- Confusing **Biodiversity hotspots** (Myers 1988/2000) with **Biosphere Reserves** (UNESCO MAB programme). Hotspots = conservation priority based on endemism + habitat loss; Biosphere Reserves = learning sites with **core, buffer, transition** zones.
- **National Park** vs **Wildlife Sanctuary**: in a national park, no human activity is permitted; sanctuaries allow rights of tribals and regulated grazing.
- **Kyoto Protocol (1997)** had binding targets for Annex-I (developed) countries only; **Paris Agreement (2015)** uses **NDCs (Nationally Determined Contributions)** and applies to all parties — a frequent MCQ trap.
- **CITES** bans trade, not use; **CBD** governs access to genetic resources; **UNFCCC** is the parent framework treaty. Memorising one as a synonym for another loses marks.

#### Worked Example
Suppose a lake's dissolved oxygen (DO) at 6 am = 8 mg/L, and after 5 days of incubation at 20°C, DO = 2 mg/L. Then **BOD₅ = 8 − 2 = 6 mg/L**, indicating **highly polluted** water (Indian CPCB standard for bathing: ≤3 mg/L; for drinking source: ≤2 mg/L).

#### Practice Prompts
1. "The 10% energy law limits the number of trophic levels in a food chain." Discuss with examples and compute how 10,000 units of producer energy translate across four trophic levels.
2. Compare in-situ and ex-situ conservation with two India-specific examples each, and evaluate which is more effective for protecting endemic, range-restricted species like the *Nilgiri tahr*.
