# Nutrition and Health

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your NEET PG exam.

## Nutrition and Health — Key Facts

### Macronutrients
- **Carbohydrates**: 4 kcal/g — Main energy source
- **Proteins**: 4 kcal/g — Body building
- **Fats**: 9 kcal/g — Dense energy, essential fatty acids

### Protein-Energy Malnutrition (PEM)

**Marasmus**: Calories AND protein deficiency
- Severe wasting, "old man" face, **NO edema**
- MUAC <115 mm (severe wasting); the 115–125 mm band flags moderate wasting

**Kwashiorkar**: Protein deficiency (calories adequate)
- **Bilateral pitting edema**, fatty liver, peeling skin
- Hair changes (flag sign)

**Marasmic Kwashiorkar**: Both — wasting + edema

### Fat-Soluble Vitamins

| Vitamin | Deficiency | Functions |
|---------|-----------|-----------|
| **A** | Night blindness → Xerophthalmia → Keratomalacia | Vision, epithelium |
| **D** | Rickets (children), Osteomalacia (adults) | Calcium absorption |
| **E** | Hemolytic anemia, neurological | Antioxidant |
| **K** | Bleeding disorders (PT prolonged) | Clotting factors |

⚡ **NEET PG High-Yield**: Marasmus vs Kwashiorkar (edema vs no edema) is a very common question.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

## Chapter: Nutrition and Health

### 1.1 Nutrients and Energy

#### Energy
- **Carbohydrates**: 4 kcal/g
- **Proteins**: 4 kcal/g
- **Fats**: 9 kcal/g

#### ICMR Recommended Dietary Allowances (Adult Male)
- Energy: 2,320 kcal (sedentary)
- Protein: 60g/day
- Fat: 20-30g/day
- Iron: 17mg

### 1.2 Protein-Energy Malnutrition (PEM)

#### Marasmus
**Definition**: Severe deficiency of both calories AND protein

**Clinical Features**:
- Severe muscle wasting
- No subcutaneous fat
- "Old man's face" (wrinkled)
- **No edema**
- MUAC <115 mm
- Severe growth retardation

#### Kwashiorkar
**Definition**: Protein deficiency with adequate calories

**Clinical Features**:
- **Bilateral pitting edema** (hallmark)
- Fatty liver (fat deposition)
- Dermatosis (peeling skin)
- Hair changes: Sparse, thin, easily pluckable, **flag sign** (alternating bands)
- Subcutaneous fat preserved
- Apathy, irritability

#### Marasmic Kwashiorkar
Both wasting + edema → Most severe form

#### Classification (WHO)
**SAM (Severe Acute Malnutrition)**:
- MUAC <115 mm OR
- Weight-for-height < -3 SD OR
- Bilateral pitting edema

### 1.3 Vitamins — Fat Soluble

#### Vitamin A (Retinol) — *Very Important*

**Functions**:
- Visual pigments (rhodopsin)
- Epithelial cell maintenance
- Immune function

**Deficiency (Xerophthalmia Sequence)**:
1. **XN**: Night blindness
2. **X1A**: Conjunctival xerosis (dry, non-wettable)
3. **X1B**: Bitot's spots (foamy, triangular, temporal conjunctiva)
4. **X2**: Corneal xerosis (dry, opaque cornea)
5. **X3**: Corneal ulcer/keratomalacia
6. **XS**: Scarring (permanent)

**Treatment**: Oral Vitamin A 200,000 IU (immediately, next day, 2 weeks)

**Prevention**: Supplementation 100,000 IU (6-11 mo), 200,000 IU (12-59 mo) every 6 months

#### Vitamin D (Cholecalciferol) — *Important*

**Functions**: Calcium and phosphorus absorption, bone mineralization

**Deficiency — Rickets** (children):
- Craniotabes (soft skull)
- Frontal bossing
- **Rachitic rosary** (enlarged costochondral junctions)
- Bow legs (genu varum) / Knock knees (genu valgum)
- Delayed fontanelle closure
- X-ray: Cupping and fraying of metaphyses

**Deficiency — Osteomalacia** (adults):
- Bone pain, muscle weakness
- Waddling gait
- Pseudofractures (Looser's zones)

#### Vitamin K (Phylloquinone)
- **Functions**: Synthesis of clotting factors II, VII, IX, X
- **Deficiency**: Bleeding disorders, **PT prolonged** (Factor VII shortest half-life)
- Newborns receive Vitamin K injection at birth

#### Vitamin E (Tocopherol)
- **Functions**: Antioxidant, protects RBC membranes
- **Deficiency**: Hemolytic anemia (premature infants)

### 1.4 Vitamins — Water Soluble

#### B-Complex

| Vitamin | Deficiency | Functions |
|---------|-----------|-----------|
| B1 (Thiamine) | Beriberi (wet: cardiac; dry: neurological) | Energy metabolism |
| B2 (Riboflavin) | Angular stomatitis, cheilosis | FAD in ETC |
| B3 (Niacin) | Pellagra (3 Ds + Death) | NAD in redox |
| B6 (Pyridoxine) | Peripheral neuropathy | Amino acid metabolism |
| B12 (Cobalamin) | Pernicious anemia, SCD | DNA synthesis |
| Folate | Megaloblastic anemia, NTDs | DNA synthesis |
| C (Ascorbic acid) | Scurvy (bleeding gums, petechiae) | Collagen synthesis |

#### Pellagra (Niacin Deficiency) — 4 Ds
1. **Dermatitis**: Casal's necklace (around neck)
2. **Diarrhea**: Mouth ulcers, enteritis
3. **Dementia**: Neurological symptoms
4. **Death**: If untreated

#### Scurvy (Vitamin C Deficiency)
- Gum bleeding, swollen gums
- Petechiae, ecchymoses
- Perifollicular hemorrhages
- Poor wound healing
- **Treatment**: Vitamin C 500 mg/day

#### Folate Deficiency
- Megaloblastic anemia
- Neural tube defects (spina bifida) in fetus
- **Prevention**: Folic acid in pregnancy (400 μg/day)

#### B12 Deficiency
- Pernicious anemia (autoimmune against parietal cells)
- **Subacute Combined Degeneration**: Posterior columns + lateral corticospinal tracts
- Treatment: Hydroxocobalamin injection

### 1.5 Minerals

#### Iron
- **RDA**: Male 17mg, Female 21mg
- **Functions**: Hemoglobin (oxygen transport)
- **Deficiency**: Microcytic hypochromic anemia
- **India**: 50% women have anemia (NFHS-5)
- **Prevention**: IFA tablets (Ferrous sulfate + Folic acid)

#### Iodine
- **RDA**: 150 μg/day
- **Functions**: Thyroid hormone synthesis (T3, T4)
- **Deficiency**: Goiter, Hypothyroidism, Cretinism
- **Prevention**: Iodized salt (USI — Universal Salt Iodization)

#### Calcium
- **RDA**: 600 mg/day (adult)
- **Functions**: Bones, teeth, muscle, nerve
- **Deficiency**: Osteoporosis, tetany, rickets

#### Fluoride
- **Optimal**: 0.7-1.2 ppm in water
- **Deficiency**: Dental caries
- **Excess**: Fluorosis (dental: white spots; skeletal: osteosclerosis)

### 1.6 Obesity

**Definition**: BMI ≥ 25 kg/m² (Asian criteria)
- Overweight: 23-24.9
- Obese I: 25-29.9
- Obese II: ≥30

**Comorbidities**:
- Type 2 diabetes
- Hypertension, dyslipidemia
- CAD, stroke
- Sleep apnea, osteoarthritis

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

#### Marasmus versus kwashiorkor — decide on the missing variable

The two are separated by one question: **is protein missing alone, or protein missing with carbohydrate?**

| Feature | Marasmus | Kwashiorkor |
|---|---|---|
| Deficiency | Total calorie and protein deficit | Protein deficit with adequate carbohydrate |
| Child | Older than 2 years, weaned late | Younger, commonly after a second birth |
| Weight | Severely underweight, "wasted" | May be near normal, "swollen" |
| Oedema | Absent | Pitting, generalised, often the first sign |
| Muscle | Wasted, prominent ribs | Preserved relative to the oedema |
| Liver | Fatty infiltration possible | Grossly fatty, enlarged |
| Skin | Loose, dry, wrinkled | Dermatosis with flaky-paint appearance |
| Hair | Thin, sparse, pulled out easily | Flag sign — alternating light and dark bands |
| Mental state | Irritable but alert | Apathetic, irritable, withdrawn |
| Mortality | Lower | Markedly higher |

**Why the oedema fools the examiner.** Kwashiorkor children often weigh *more* than marasmus children on a scale, so a question that routes by weight alone is testing whether you know weight is not the discriminator. Oedema masks the loss of subcutaneous fat, and the pitting test — firm pressure on the dorsum of the foot for three seconds — is the bedside step that separates them.

**The flag sign** is melanin banding in hair that alternates with periods of adequate nutrition. Each pale band marks an episode; the bands are not the illness, they are the record of it.

#### The third form, and why WHO classification matters

Marasmic kwashiorkor is the overlap: wasting *and* oedema, with the marasmus component dominant on anthropometry. It is not a fourth entity, and a question presenting four separate PEM types is signalling a distractor — the WHO list has marasmus, kwashiorkor, and their combined form, plus the underweight category in infants.

The underweight category (weight-for-age below −2 SD) ignores height entirely and so cannot separate wasting from stunting. Exam questions use it as a trap: a child can be underweight purely because they are short.

#### Worked problem — the infant who is not gaining

> A 14-month-old is brought in for poor growth. Weight-for-age is −3 SD, weight-for-height is −0.5 SD. Skinfolds are within normal limits. The mother reports the child eats rice porridge with almost no protein, six times a day.

Read the anthropometry before the history.

1. Weight-for-age −3 SD but weight-for-height −0.5 SD says the child is underweight **but not wasted**.
2. Normal skinfold thickness confirms there is no loss of subcutaneous fat, so this is not marasmus and not kwashiorkor.
3. The child is **stunted** — short for age — which points to chronic deprivation of the total diet rather than an acute protein crisis.
4. The intervention is therefore the whole diet, not a protein supplement alone. Supplementing protein to a stunted child without correcting total energy does not produce catch-up growth.

The lesson generalises: in anthropometry, weight-for-height isolates *wasting*, height-for-age isolates *stunting*, and weight-for-age confuses the two.

#### Vitamin A — why night blindness and immunity fail together

Retinol has three functions that the exam links: vision, epithelial integrity, and immune competence. Deficiency therefore produces a characteristic sequence.

1. Dark adaptation slows — the earliest symptom is night blindness
2. Conjunctival and corneal xerosis — Bitot's spots are foamy white conjunctival plaques, not corneal ones
3. Keratomalacia — ulceration and blindness
4. Impaired immunity — raised child mortality, which is why supplementation is one of the highest-impact child-survival interventions available

Bitot's spots are the classic one-mark item: conjunctival, foamy, triangular patches. Corneal xerosis is a later and more dangerous stage, and confusing the two is the standard distractor.

#### The fat-soluble rule, and its two traps

A, D, K and E are stored in the liver and in adipose tissue, so deficiency takes months to appear; water-soluble B and C are stored minimally and turn over fast. The traps:

- **K**, despite being fat-soluble, is synthesised by gut bacteria, so its deficiency shows up first in someone with prolonged broad-spectrum antibiotics or with biliary obstruction — where fat absorption itself fails.
- **E** deficiency is rare and almost always secondary — severe fat malabsorption, prolonged cholestasis, or the low-bile-acid states that follow it — not in ordinary undernutrition. It does not follow high-dose tocopherol, which treats rather than causes it.

Deficiency can therefore be caused either by too little intake or by failure to *absorb* — and cholestatic jaundice, coeliac disease and giardiasis all produce the fat-soluble tetrad A, D, K, E deficient at once. Recognising the tetrad as a malabsorption signature is worth more marks than memorising individual symptoms.

#### Vitamin D — calcium homeostasis in three steps

1. Skin synthesises cholecalciferol under **UVB**
2. Liver hydroxylates it to 25-hydroxycholecalciferol (calcidiol) — the storage and **status** form
3. Kidney hydroxylates to 1,25-dihydroxycholecalciferol (calcitriol) — the active form

Rickets is a failure of **mineralisation** of growing bone, osteomalacia is the same failure in **mature** bone. Children with rickets show craniotabes, rachitic rosary at the costochondral junction, Harrison's sulcus and bow legs. In adults the equivalent is bone pain, Looser zones (pseudofractures) and diffuse osteoporosis.

Because the kidney step is under parathyroid control, chronic kidney disease produces an active-vitamin D deficiency despite abundant stores — which is why calcitriol rather than cholecalciferol is the preparation used there.

#### Pellagra — the 4 Ds and what they are made of

Niacin (B3) is required for NAD and NADP. Deficiency gives dermatitis, diarrhoea, dementia and death. The classic descriptor is **3 Ds** (dermatitis, diarrhoea, dementia), with the fourth D — death — reserved for untreated severe cases.

The photosensitive distribution is the discriminator: the rash is symmetrical and affects sun-exposed surfaces — dorsum of hands, neck (Casal's necklace), elbows, knees. It looks like a burn that has healed and recurred with each sun exposure.

**Why maize is the risk food.** Maize is low in available niacin and, more importantly, its niacin is bound to a protein as **niacytin**, which is not released unless the grain is first treated with alkali — the lime treatment of nixtamalisation in Mesoamerican cooking. Where maize is eaten without that step, pellagra clusters in maize-dependent populations. This is why the deficiency has a dietary signature rather than being random.

#### Iron — the four stages of depletion

Stores fall before serum iron does, which is why early iron deficiency is easy to miss.

| Stage | Stores | Serum iron | Ferritin | TIBC | Appears as |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Depletion | Low | Normal | Low | High | None clinically |
| 2 Iron-deficient erythropoiesis | Low | Low | Low | High | None clinically |
| 3 Iron-deficiency anaemia | Low | Low | Low | Normal | Microcytic, hypochromic |
| 4 Anaemia with tissue change | Exhausted | Low | Very low | Low | Symptoms, koilonychia, pica |

Stage 1 is the one that matters clinically: a pregnant woman can be iron-deficient with a normal haemoglobin, and it still affects fetal neurodevelopment. Screening on haemoglobin alone misses it — ferritin is the earlier marker.

**The microcytic anaemia differential.** Iron deficiency has low TIBC; thalassaemia trait has normal or raised TIBC with a high red cell count and target cells. Both are microcytic, so TIBC and RDW separate them, and an MCQ that offers only "microcytic hypochromic" has not yet given you enough to answer — look for the distinguishing feature.

#### Examiner trap sheet

- Anthropometry: **weight-for-height = wasting**, **height-for-age = stunting**, **weight-for-age = underweight** (and therefore ambiguous). Never use weight-for-age alone to name the PEM.
- Oedema in a malnourished child points to **kwashiorkor** regardless of the child's apparent weight.
- Pellagra has **four** Ds, and Casal's necklace is part of the dermatitis finding.
- Carotene, not retinol, gives the yellow-orange colour in carotenaemia, and it does not raise serum retinol. A jaundice question built on yellow palms with normal retinol is carotenaemia.
- Vitamin K deficiency is suspected in a neonate on day 3–7 with bleeding at the venepuncture or circumcision site, and prolonged PT is the first abnormal test — K acts at factors II, VII, IX and X.
