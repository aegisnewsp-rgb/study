# Plants: Structure and Functions

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Plants are made up of two main systems: the **shoot system** (stem, leaves, flowers, fruits) above ground and the **root system** below ground. **Roots** anchor the plant, absorb water and dissolved minerals from the soil, and in some species (carrot, cassava) store food. The **stem** holds the plant upright, conducts substances between roots and leaves, and may store food (sugarcane, ginger). **Leaves** are the main site of **photosynthesis**, the process by which plants make glucose using sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide; gas exchange and water loss (transpiration) happen through tiny pores called **stomata**. **Flowers** are the reproductive organs, producing pollen and ovules that lead to **seeds** and **fruits**. A seed contains an embryo, a food store (endosperm), and a protective coat, and needs water, oxygen, and warmth to germinate. Water moves up through **xylem**; food moves through **phloem**.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Plant Organ Systems
A typical flowering plant has two systems. The **root system** (radicle-derived) lies below the soil and the **shoot system** (stem, leaves, flowers, fruits, seeds) lies above. Two root types exist: a **taproot** (one main root with side branches, common in dicots like beans) and **fibrous roots** (many thin roots of similar size, common in monocots like maize and grass).

#### Functions of Major Organs

| Organ | Main Functions | Example/Note |
|-------|----------------|--------------|
| Root | Anchorage, absorption of water + minerals, food storage | Carrot, cassava store starch |
| Stem | Support, conduction, storage, sometimes photosynthesis (cladodes) | Sugarcane stores sucrose |
| Leaf | Photosynthesis, transpiration, gas exchange | Flat blade maximises light capture |
| Flower | Reproduction (produces pollen and ovules) | Has sepals, petals, stamens (♂), pistil (♀) |
| Fruit | Protects seed, aids dispersal | Develops from fertilised ovary |
| Seed | Contains embryo + endosperm; dormancy + dispersal | Germinates under suitable conditions |

#### Vascular Tissues
Two conducting tissues run through roots, stems, and leaves. **Xylem** carries water and mineral salts absorbed by roots upward to the stem and leaves; it is composed of dead, hollow tubes. **Phloem** carries dissolved sugars (food) made in the leaves to all other parts of the plant for use or storage; it is composed of living cells.

#### Photosynthesis and Transpiration
**Photosynthesis** can be summarised as: carbon dioxide + water → (in light, with chlorophyll) → glucose + oxygen. The green pigment **chlorophyll** in leaf **mesophyll** cells traps light energy. **Stomata** (usually on the underside of leaves) let CO₂ in and O₂ and water vapour out. Loss of water vapour through stomata is **transpiration**, which also helps pull water up the xylem.

#### Reproduction and Seeds
**Pollination** is the transfer of pollen from anther to stigma. **Fertilisation** is the fusion of male and female gametes inside the ovule, which then develops into a seed and the ovary into a fruit. For **germination**, a seed needs water (to activate enzymes), oxygen (for respiration of the embryo), and a suitable warm temperature.

#### Exam Patterns
NCEE objective questions commonly ask: matching organ to function, identifying parts of a flower from a diagram, naming conditions for photosynthesis or germination, and choosing the correct direction of flow in xylem/phloem.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Plant Groups
NCEE distinguishes **flowering plants (angiosperms)** from **non-flowering plants** such as mosses (bryophytes) and ferns (pteridophytes). Flowering plants produce seeds enclosed in fruits; non-flowering plants reproduce by spores and lack true vascular tissue (mosses) or have it but no seeds (ferns).

#### Mechanism Spotlight — Photosynthesis in Detail
Light is absorbed by chlorophyll in the **palisade mesophyll** (tightly packed cells just under the upper epidermis). CO₂ enters through stomata and diffuses to these cells; water reaches them via xylem. Inside chloroplasts, light energy splits water molecules and the resulting hydrogen combines with CO₂ to form glucose. Oxygen is released as a by-product through the stomata. Stomatal opening is controlled by **guard cells**, which swell with water to open the pore and shrink to close it — this balances the plant's need for CO₂ against water loss.

#### Mechanism Spotlight — Transpiration Pull
Water evaporates from mesophyll cell walls into air spaces and exits via stomata. This creates a suction ("transpiration pull") that draws water up the continuous xylem column from roots to leaves. Factors that increase transpiration: bright light, high temperature, low humidity, and wind.

#### Common Traps in NCEE Questions
- Confusing **xylem (water ↑)** with **phloem (food bidirectional)**.
- Stating that "plants get food from the soil" — soil supplies raw materials (water, minerals); food (glucose) is *made* in leaves.
- Mixing up **pollination** (pollen transfer) with **fertilisation** (gamete fusion).
- Saying "the leaf photosynthesises" without naming **chlorophyll** and **sunlight**.
- Calling the stem "the food-making organ" — only leaves (with chlorophyll) do that.

#### Worked Example
**Q:** Name the part of the leaf that controls gas exchange and state the gas that enters during photosynthesis.
**A:** **Stomata** (singular: stoma), and the gas that enters is **carbon dioxide (CO₂)**.

#### Practice Prompts
1. List three conditions required for the germination of a seed and explain the role of each.
2. Describe two ways the root system is suited to its function of absorbing water from the soil.
