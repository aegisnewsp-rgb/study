# F. Measurement of Economic Performance — HKDSE Economics Notes

This topic covers Topic F of the Compulsory Part of HKDSE Economics. It addresses national income accounting (GDP at market prices, GNP, NDP, NN/NP; the income, output and expenditure approaches); real versus nominal GDP; the limitations of GDP as a measure of welfare; and the standard of living and economic growth.

> Verify the live HKDSE Economics Assessment Framework on https://www.hkeaa.edu.hk/ before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Gross Domestic Product (GDP)**: the total value of all final goods and services produced within a country in a given period.
- **Gross National Product (GNP)**: GDP + net primary income from abroad (income earned by residents abroad minus income earned by foreigners domestically).
- **Net Domestic Product (NDP)**: GDP - depreciation (capital consumption allowance).
- **Net National Product (NNP)**: GNP - depreciation.
- **Three approaches to GDP**:
  - **Output (production) approach**: sum of value added at each stage of production.
  - **Income approach**: sum of all factor incomes (wages, rent, interest, profit).
  - **Expenditure approach**: C + I + G + (X - M).
- **C**: personal consumption expenditure.
- **I**: gross private domestic investment.
- **G**: government purchases.
- **X - M**: net exports (exports minus imports).
- **Nominal GDP**: measured at current prices; includes the effect of inflation.
- **Real GDP**: measured at constant (base-year) prices; removes the effect of inflation. Real GDP growth measures actual output growth.
- **GDP deflator**: (Nominal GDP / Real GDP) × 100. A measure of the overall price level.
- **Limitations of GDP as a measure of welfare**: ignores distribution; ignores non-market activities (housework, volunteering); ignores underground economy; ignores environmental degradation; ignores leisure.
- **Economic growth**: sustained increase in real GDP per capita. Source: capital accumulation, labour force growth, productivity growth.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing GDP with GNP — GDP is domestic; GNP includes net primary income from abroad.
- Treating GDP growth as welfare growth — GDP measures output, not welfare.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Three approaches to GDP

**Output approach**: GDP = Σ (value added) at each stage of production. Value added = revenue - intermediate inputs.

Example: a baker buys flour for $1, sells bread for $3. Value added = $3 - $1 = $2.

**Income approach**: GDP = compensation of employees + rental income + corporate profits + proprietors' income + indirect taxes - subsidies + depreciation.

**Expenditure approach**: GDP = C + I + G + (X - M). The most commonly used.

#### Real vs nominal GDP

**Nominal GDP**: measured at current year prices. Increases with both output and prices.

**Real GDP**: measured at base-year prices. Increases only with output.

Real GDP = (Nominal GDP / Price deflator) × 100.

The GDP deflator is the broadest price index (covers all goods and services produced domestically).

**Real GDP growth rate** = ((Real GDP_t - Real GDP_(t-1)) / Real GDP_(t-1)) × 100%.

#### GNP, NDP, NNP

**GNP = GDP + Net Primary Income from Abroad (NPIA)**

NPIA = income earned by residents abroad - income earned by foreigners domestically.

**NDP = GDP - Depreciation**

Depreciation is the capital consumption allowance (the value of capital used up in production).

**NNP = GNP - Depreciation**

NNP is sometimes used as a measure of national income available for consumption or saving.

#### Limitations of GDP

GDP doesn't measure welfare. Limitations:

- **Non-market activities**: housework, childcare, volunteer work — all produce value but are not counted.
- **Underground economy**: undeclared work — not counted in official GDP.
- **Income distribution**: GDP says nothing about who benefits from the growth.
- **Environmental degradation**: pollution and resource depletion are not deducted from GDP.
- **Quality vs quantity**: GDP counts the number of cars, not their quality or environmental impact.
- **Defensive expenditures**: spending on pollution control, security, etc. increase GDP but do not improve welfare.

**Alternative measures**:

- **Genuine Progress Indicator (GPI)**: adjusts GDP for environmental, social and inequality factors.
- **Human Development Index (HDI)**: combines GDP per capita, life expectancy and education indicators.
- **Better Life Index (OECD)**: covers 11 topics of well-being.

#### Economic growth

**Sources of growth**:

- **Capital accumulation**: investment in physical capital (machinery, infrastructure).
- **Labour force growth**: increase in the number of workers.
- **Productivity growth**: improvements in the efficiency of production (technology, organisation, skills).

**Growth accounting**: GDP growth = capital contribution + labour contribution + total factor productivity (TFP) growth.

**Policies to promote growth**:

- **Investment in infrastructure**: roads, ports, telecommunications.
- **Investment in education and health**: human capital.
- **R&D and innovation**: technology adoption.
- **Trade openness**: access to larger markets and foreign technology.
- **Macroeconomic stability**: low inflation, sustainable debt.

#### Exam technique

- **For GDP calculation questions**: identify the correct approach; for expenditure approach, identify C, I, G, X, M.
- **For real GDP questions**: deflate using the price index.
- **For limitations questions**: cite specific examples (housework, environmental degradation, etc.).

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Higher GDP means better welfare." Not necessarily — GDP does not capture distribution or non-market activities.
- "GDP measures production." GDP measures the value of final goods and services produced, not just production (services are counted too).
- "Real GDP and nominal GDP differ by the inflation rate." They differ by the GDP deflator, which is the broadest price index.
