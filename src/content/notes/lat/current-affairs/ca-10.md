# Human Rights Issues

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> One-sentence anchor before you scan: rights protection in Pakistan lives at the intersection of *international instruments* and *Part II of the 1973 Constitution* — LAT tests that overlap.

Human rights are entitlements inherent to every person, recognised first in the UN Charter (1945) and codified in the **Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR, 10 December 1948)** adopted by the UN General Assembly under Resolution 217 A. Two binding covenants carry the UDHR's political side and economic side forward: the **ICCPR (1966)** and the **ICESCR (1966)**. Pakistan mirrors these obligations in **Articles 8–28** of its Constitution.

| Instrument / Body | Year | Binding? | Pakistan's status |
|---|---|---|---|
| UN Charter (first to use "human rights") | 1945 | Yes (treaty) | Founding member |
| UDHR (General Assembly Resolution 217 A) | 1948 | No (declaration) | Endorsed |
| ICCPR & ICESCR | 1966 | Yes (treaties) | Party, with reservations |
| UN Human Rights Council (succeeded Commission on HR) | 2006 | — | Member, elected |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook (Memory Hook):** "**CPES**" — Civil-Political (ICCPR), Economic-Social-Cultural (ICESCR). Think *CP* = *Cold* political liberty, *ES* = *Welfare* state. Pair it with the date tag **"48-66-06"** — UDHR 1948, Covenants 1966, UNHRC 2006.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Aim this pass at students 2 days to 2 months out — mechanisms, traps, and a worked MCQ follow below.

#### The Three Generations of Rights

Karl Vasak's 1979 classification, routinely asked, sorts rights by the value they pursue:

| Generation | Value | Examples | Where it lives in law |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st — Civil & Political | Liberty | Right to life, free speech, fair trial | ICCPR; Constitution Articles 9, 10, 14, 19, 25 |
| 2nd — Economic, Social & Cultural | Equality | Education, health, labour rights | ICESCR; Articles 25-A, 38 |
| 3rd — Solidarity / Collective | Fraternity | Self-determination, environment, peace | UN General Assembly resolutions; Articles 4 & 25 of UDHR |

#### Pakistan's Constitutional Map (Part II, 1973)

The Constitution protects rights through **Fundamental Rights (Chapter 1, enforceable under Article 199)** and **Directive Principles (Article 38, non-justiciable)** — and examiners love the contrast.

| Article | Right protected | LAT-tested? |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Inalienable right to life, liberty, property | High |
| 8 | Laws inconsistent with Fundamental Rights void | High |
| 9 | Security of person — anti-arbitrary detention | High |
| 10 | Safeguards on arrest and detention | Medium |
| 14 | Inviolability of dignity of man | High |
| 25 | Equality before law, equal protection | High |
| 25-A | Free and compulsory education 5–16 years | Medium |
| 38 | Promotion of social/economic well-being (non-justiciable) | High (trap) |

#### State Obligations — the Triad

Every rights-holder can demand three things from the state: **respect** (don't violate), **protect** (stop third-party violations), **fulfil** (take positive steps). LAT questions often convert this into a one-mark "which duty is missing" MCQ.

#### Worked Practice Question

> Q. The Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR) was adopted by:
> (A) The UN Security Council under Resolution 217 A in 1948
> (B) The UN General Assembly under Resolution 217 A in 1948
> (C) The UN Commission on Human Rights in 1946
> (D) The International Court of Justice in 1949

Resolution. Walk through the elimination: (A) fails because the Security Council does not adopt declaratory human-rights instruments — Resolution 217 A is a General Assembly number, and 1948 is correct. (B) names the correct body and correct resolution — the Eleanor Roosevelt-led drafting Commission's text was tabled by the General Assembly third committee on 10 December 1948. (C) misstates both the year and the body; the Commission on HR drafted, it did not adopt. (D) misnames the court and the year. Correct: **(B)**.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Option (A) is built to catch students who mix up the General Assembly with the Security Council. Watch for any MCQ that swaps the two — the GA adopts declarations; the SC adopts resolutions on peace/security. The same trap recurs on the ICCPR/ICESCR, both 1966.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Read this only if LAT is more than three months out or you're sitting a follow-up CSS-style paper.

#### Hierarchy: *Jus Cogens* Beats Treaty

Some norms — prohibition of genocide, slavery, torture, racial discrimination — are *jus cogens*. A treaty clause that contradicts them is void under Article 53 of the Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties (1969). Pakistan has not signed VCLT, but the principle is repeatedly cited by the Supreme Court of Pakistan in *Jamat-e-Islami* line of cases.

#### Bodies and Their Confusing Names

| Body | Years active | Function | Easy confusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| UN Commission on Human Rights | 1946–2006 | Drafted UDHR, ICCPR | Often swapped with UNHRC |
| UN Human Rights Council (UNHRC) | 2006–present | 47 states, Universal Periodic Review | Seat elected by GA |
| International Court of Justice (ICJ) | 1946–present | State-to-state disputes | Not a criminal court |
| International Criminal Court (ICC) | 2002–present | Prosecutes individuals | Pakistan is **not** party to Rome Statute |
| Pakistan Human Rights Commission (HRCP) | 1986–present | Independent civil-society watchdog | Not a statutory body |
| National Commission for Human Rights (NCHR) | 2012–present (NCHR Act 2012) | Statutory national institution | Quasi-judicial powers |

#### Edge Cases and Common Traps

1. **Derogation under ICCPR Article 4** is allowed *only* during a *formally proclaimed* public emergency. Pakistan's Article 233 emergency provisions do not automatically align — courts read them strictly.
2. **Pakistan's reservations** to the ICCPR (notably on religious law under Articles 3 and 25) remain on record with the UN treaty database; LAT has asked which reservations Pakistan entered.
3. **UPR (Universal Periodic Review)** — every UN member is reviewed every 4.5 years; Pakistan's most recent cycle is checked on the OHCHR portal.
4. **Blasphemy law (PPC 295-B, 295-C)** is not a human-rights instrument but is regularly tested *as a current-affairs tension* with ICCPR Article 18 (freedom of religion).
5. **Bonded labour** sits at the intersection of ICESCR Article 6 and the Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act 1992 — examiners often link the two.

#### Two Advanced Practice Prompts

1. *Assertion-Reason:* "The UDHR is binding on Pakistan because Pakistan voted for Resolution 217 A." Is this correct? Reason why the UDHR is a declaration, not a treaty, and why GA votes do not create treaty obligations absent ratification.
2. *Numerical match:* Match (i) Eleanor Roosevelt, (ii) René Cassin, (iii) René Maheu, (iv) Charles Malik — to their roles in the UDHR drafting process. Cassin drafted the French text; Roosevelt chaired the Third Committee effort.
