# Identifying Information (True/False/Not Given)

> Verify the live True/False/Not Given question format and the candidate distinction between this question type and the Yes/No/Not Given question type on https://ielts.org/ and the British Council / IDP preparation pages before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Format:** a set of statements about the passage; the candidate decides for each statement whether it agrees with the information in the passage (True), contradicts it (False), or is not stated (Not Given).
- **Where it appears:** typically on Passage 2 or Passage 3 (the harder passages). Usually 6-8 statements per test (~15-20% of the 40-question total).
- **Order:** statements follow the passage in order. Statement 1 covers early content, Statement 8 covers late content.
- **Marking:** +1 correct, 0 incorrect (no negative marking). Spelling errors are accepted if the meaning is unchanged.
- **Source of truth:** the PASSAGE — not your opinion, not the real world, not other sources. The candidate compares the statement to the passage verbatim.

#### The three labels — what each really means

| Label | Meaning | Test |
|---|---|---|
| **True** | The statement AGREES with the information in the passage. The statement is supported by the passage. | Does the passage say this, in any paraphrase? |
| **False** | The statement CONTRADICTS the information in the passage. The passage says the OPPOSITE. | Does the passage say the opposite of this, in any paraphrase? |
| **Not Given** | The statement is NOT stated in the passage. Information is absent or undetermined. | Does the passage say neither this nor its opposite? |

#### The most common error

| Error pattern | Wrong label | Correct label |
|---|---|---|
| The statement is factually TRUE in real life, but the passage says the OPPOSITE | True (real-world fact) | **False** (passage contradicts) |
| The statement is factually TRUE and the passage doesn't mention it | True (real-world fact) | **Not Given** (passage is silent) |
| The passage says something SIMILAR but not the same | True (similar ≠ same) | **Not Given** (paraphrase mismatch) |
| The passage DIRECTLY contradicts the statement | Not Given (silent?) | **False** (clear contradiction) |
| The passage says EXACTLY the statement (in paraphrase) | True ✓ | True ✓ |

The crucial insight: **compare the statement to the passage, not to your knowledge of the world.** IELTS Reading tests the candidate's ability to read the passage — not to validate real-world facts.

---

#### Examiner traps

- **Real-world facts.** A statement can be factually TRUE in the real world (e.g. "Doha is the capital of Qatar") but FALSE in IELTS because the passage says the opposite. The candidate must answer based on the passage, not real-world knowledge.
- **Paraphrase mismatch.** The passage uses different words from the statement. Candidates who compare words instead of meanings wrongly mark "False" when the meaning is the same.
- **Partial truth.** The statement says something partially supported by the passage but with one detail wrong. The label is False, not True, because the statement as a WHOLE does not agree.
- **False vs Not Given.** The candidate marks "Not Given" when the passage actually contradicts the statement. The label should be False. If the passage says the OPPOSITE, it cannot be Not Given.
- **Yes/No confusion.** Yes/No/Not Given is about the writer's VIEW (opinion); True/False/Not Given is about FACT. They are NOT interchangeable.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### The 7-step strategy for True/False/Not Given

A measured 7-step approach to answering True/False/Not Given statements in 30-45 seconds each:

1. **Read the statement stem (5 sec).** Identify the SUBJECT (what the statement is about) and the PREDICATE (what is being claimed about the subject).
2. **Identify the keywords (5 sec).** Underline (in your mind) the content words in the statement: nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs, numbers, dates, names.
3. **Locate the relevant paragraph (10 sec).** Use the statement's order to find the paragraph that likely contains the relevant information. Statement 1 is early in the passage; Statement 6 is later.
4. **Read that paragraph carefully (15 sec).** Read every sentence, paying attention to qualifications ("some", "many", "most", "all", "never", "always", "often", "rarely"), time markers ("in 1995", "by 2005", "since 2015"), and contrasts ("however", "but", "although").
5. **Compare the statement to the passage (10 sec).** Match each keyword in the statement to its paraphrase in the passage. Identify agreements, contradictions, and absences.
6. **Choose the label (5 sec).**
   - **True** if the passage says it (in paraphrase).
   - **False** if the passage says the opposite (in paraphrase).
   - **Not Given** if the passage says neither this nor its opposite.
7. **Mark and move on (5 sec).** Mark the answer, then move to the next statement. Do not second-guess once you have decided.

#### The True/False/Not Given decision matrix

| Passage says... | Statement claims... | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Exactly the statement (in paraphrase) | Same | True |
| The opposite of the statement (in paraphrase) | Contradicts | False |
| Neither the statement nor its opposite | Absent | Not Given |
| Something similar but with a different detail | Similar ≠ same | Not Given |
| One detail of the statement but not all | Both | Both ≠ same — check if statement as a WHOLE matches |

#### Qualification traps — small words, big differences

IELTS True/False/Not Given statements test qualifications (small words that change meaning). The candidate who reads "many" instead of "most" gets the wrong answer.

| Statement word | Passage says | Result |
|---|---|---|
| All | Most | False (most ≠ all) |
| Never | Rarely | False (rarely ≠ never) |
| Always | Often | False (often ≠ always) |
| Some | Most | False (most ≠ some) |
| Increased | Decreased | False (direction wrong) |
| Increased | Stayed the same | False (no change) |
| Many | Few | False (magnitude wrong) |
| In 2010 | In 2015 | False (time wrong) |
| Mostly in Doha | Mostly in Education City | False (place wrong) |

The rule: **any single detail that disagrees makes the statement False**, not Not Given. The label is False if the statement as a WHOLE does not agree with the passage.

---

#### Reading for qualifications

The candidate who reads "many" as "all" or "rarely" as "never" loses. Train with a daily drill: read a paragraph and write down every qualification word (some, many, most, all, never, always, often, rarely, sometimes, occasionally, generally, typically, mainly, partly).

Qatar-relevant qualification contexts:
- "Most QU research is published in English" (vs "All QU research" / "Some QU research")
- "Education City hosts some US branch campuses" (vs "many" / "all")
- "Qatar rarely experiences snow" (vs "never")
- "Gulf Times has often covered Qatari economic policy" (vs "always")
- "Qatar Foundation often funds sustainability research" (vs "mostly")

### 🔴 Deep — Mastery (2mo+)

#### The cognitive architecture of True/False/Not Given

True/False/Not Given is the question type that most often trips up candidates who read too fast. The candidate must distinguish between three logically distinct relationships: agreement (True), contradiction (False), and silence (Not Given). Most candidates confuse False and Not Given.

A candidate scoring below 7 on True/False/Not Given typically has a paraphrase-recognition gap, a qualification-reading gap, or a comparison gap. The deeper intervention is to slow down and read for QUALIFICATIONS before deciding, and to compare the STATEMENT AS A WHOLE to the passage, not the words.

#### Practice strategy for True/False/Not Given

1. **Use the British Council and IDP official IELTS practice tests.** The question styles and difficulty are calibrated against the live test.
2. **Practise with a stopwatch.** A 6-statement True/False/Not Given set should take 3-4 minutes. Without practice, candidates take 5-7 minutes.
3. **Read the passage for qualifications first.** Build the habit of underlining qualification words (some, many, most, all, never, always, often, rarely, sometimes).
4. **Compare the statement as a WHOLE.** A statement with one wrong detail is False, not partially True.
6. **Distinguish True/False from Yes/No.** True/False is about FACT (does the statement agree with the passage?); Yes/No is about VIEW (does the writer hold this view?).
7. **Distinguish False from Not Given.** False = the passage says the OPPOSITE; Not Given = the passage says NEITHER.
8. **Practise with Qatari-context passages.** QU research, Education City publications, QF reports, Gulf Times, Al Jazeera English, Qatar National Library digital archive build the local vocabulary and qualification patterns you will encounter on test day.

#### When True/False/Not Given marks reflect a deeper issue

- **Persistent False/Not Given confusion:** the indicator is contradiction-recognition. Train with a daily 5-minute "does the passage say this, the opposite, or neither?" drill.
- **Persistent paraphrase errors:** the indicator is vocabulary recognition. Train with academic-article reading and verbatim note-taking.
- **Persistent qualification errors:** the indicator is reading speed. Train with a daily drill: read a paragraph and write down every qualification word.
- **Persistent real-world-knowledge errors:** the indicator is the candidate's over-reliance on prior knowledge. Train with a daily drill: read statements about a passage and explicitly say "I must answer based on the passage, not real-world knowledge."
- **Persistent partial-truth errors:** the indicator is whole-statement reading. Train with a daily drill: read a multi-clause statement and check each clause against the passage.

#### Edge cases and known traps

1. **Partial truth is False.** If the statement has one detail wrong (e.g. "in 2010" instead of "in 2015"), the label is False. The statement as a WHOLE does not agree with the passage.
2. **Real-world fact but passage says opposite = False.** The label is determined by the passage, not the real world.
3. **The passage is silent on the topic = Not Given.** If the passage says nothing about the statement's subject, the label is Not Given, not False.
4. **Yes/No/Not Given is different.** Yes/No is about the writer's VIEW; True/False is about FACT. Confusing the two is the most common error.
5. **Spelling is forgiving, but missing letters are not.** A spelling error that does not change the meaning is accepted. A missing letter is not.
6. **No negative marking.** Guess if you do not know the answer. A blank answer is guaranteed wrong; a guessed answer is 33% likely right.
7. **Capitalisation and punctuation.** IELTS accepts lower-case names unless the passage explicitly capitalises.
8. **American vs British spelling.** IELTS accepts either American or British spelling as long as it is consistent within a single answer.

### Previous Year Patterns — Identifying Information (True/False/Not Given)

The True/False/Not Given question type has been part of the IELTS Reading test since the Academic / General Training split in 1989. It is published verbatim on ielts.org Identifying Information question type guide and is not expected to be removed.

Common year-on-year patterns:
- True/False/Not Given is more commonly placed on Passage 2 or Passage 3 (the harder passages) than on Passage 1.
- A typical test has 6-8 True/False/Not Given statements.
- The hardest distinctions are False vs Not Given (candidates often wrongly mark Not Given when the passage contradicts).
- The most common error is real-world-knowledge contamination (candidates mark True based on real-world knowledge when the passage says the opposite).
- A previous-year True/False/Not Given set is NOT reused verbatim in the same year; recycled sets appear with at most small contextual changes.

A previous-year record is not a deterministic indicator of a future test item. The British Council and IDP refresh the question bank annually, calibrated against the same difficulty curve.
