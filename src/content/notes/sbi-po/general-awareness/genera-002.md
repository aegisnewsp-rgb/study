# RBI's Monetary Policy Framework

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The RBI's Monetary Policy Framework is the structured architecture the central bank uses to control money supply, credit and inflation. Since February 2016, it operates under **Flexible Inflation Targeting (FIT)**, anchored by a six-member **Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)** that votes on the **policy repo rate** to keep **CPI inflation at 4% with a ±2% tolerance band (2%–6%)**.

- **Repo rate** — rate at which RBI lends short-term funds to commercial banks against government securities (the benchmark).
- **Reverse Repo rate** — rate at which RBI borrows from banks.
- **Marginal Standing Facility (MSF)** — overnight emergency window, set at **Repo + 2%**.
- **Bank Rate** — since 2022, aligned with the MSF rate; applies to long-term RBI lending.
- **CRR** — cash reserves banks park with RBI (% of NDTL, currently 4%, zero interest).
- **SLR** — gold + government securities + cash (% of NDTL, currently 18%).

The MPC meets **bi-monthly** (six meetings a year). A rate hike signals a **hawkish/withdrawal** stance; a cut signals **accommodative**; a hold is **neutral**. Transmission to retail loan rates typically takes **3–6 months**.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Legal basis and the MPC
Section 45ZA of the RBI Act, 1934 (amended through the Finance Act, 2016) gave FIT statutory backing. The MPC consists of **three RBI officials** (Governor as Chair, Deputy Chair in charge of monetary policy, and one nominee of the RBI Board) plus **three external members** appointed by the Central Government. Decisions are by majority; on a tie, the **Governor holds the casting vote**. The committee has six scheduled meetings annually, with minutes published within two weeks.

#### Operating targets and the corridor
The immediate operating target is the **weighted average overnight call money rate**, which RBI keeps near the repo rate using the **Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF)**. The corridor spans **Reverse Repo (floor)** and **MSF (ceiling)**, giving banks an incentive to transact with RBI rather than in the interbank market when rates stray.

#### Reserve requirements and liquidity tools

| Instrument | Base | Current | Key feature |
|---|---|---|---|
| CRR | NDTL | 4% | Cash with RBI, **zero interest** |
| SLR | NDTL | 18% | Gold + G-Secs + cash |
| Repo | – | Benchmark | Short-term lending against G-Secs |
| MSF | – | Repo + 2% | Overnight penal window |
| Bank Rate | – | = MSF (since 2022) | Long-term/penal lending |
| OMO | – | Discretionary | G-Sec buy/sell, MSS, forex swaps |

#### Multiple Indicator Approach
Although FIT now anchors policy, the RBI still tracks **GDP growth, money supply, credit growth, exchange rate, capacity utilisation, and inflation expectations** to gauge the broader economy.

> **Exam pointer:** A 2–6% band breach forces the RBI to issue a written explanation to the Central Government; it does not automatically trigger a rate change.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### How a policy decision travels to your EMI
1. MPC votes on repo rate.
2. Interbank call rate tracks repo within the corridor.
3. Banks re-price MCLR/EBR-linked loans and term deposit rates.
4. **Transmission lag to retail lending: roughly 3–6 months**, longer for fixed-rate segments.

#### Reading the stance correctly
- **Accommodative** → repo cuts or guidance to support growth.
- **Neutral** → repo unchanged, neither easing nor tightening.
- **Withdrawal of accommodation / Hawkish** → repo hikes aimed at cooling inflation.

#### Common traps in the SBI PO GA paper
- Treating Bank Rate and Repo Rate as interchangeable — they differ in tenor and purpose.
- Forgetting CRR is calculated on **NDTL, not total deposits**.
- Believing the inflation target is "2–6%" rather than 4% ± 2%.
- Stating MPC composition as 4 external + 2 RBI; the actual current balance is **3 + 3**.
- Assuming OMO refers only to G-Sec trading; it also covers **Market Stabilisation Scheme (MSS)** and USD/INR sell–buy swaps.
- Saying the MPC meets monthly — it is **bi-monthly**.

#### Adjacent topics worth pairing
Repo decisions influence **G-Sec yields**, the **rupee exchange rate**, and **bank NIMs**. Revising **Fiscal Policy**, **types of banks in India**, and the **current account deficit** together gives a complete macroeconomic picture often tested in Banking Awareness.

#### Practice prompts
1. If CPI inflation stays above 6% for three consecutive quarters, what are the RBI's obligations under FIT, and does a band breach automatically raise the repo rate?
2. A bank holds ₹100 crore NDTL. How much must it maintain as CRR and SLR today, and why is CRR described as a "costly" reserve?
