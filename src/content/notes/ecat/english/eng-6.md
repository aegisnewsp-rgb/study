# Active and Passive Voice

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Active voice** places the doer (agent) in the grammatical **subject** position before the main verb. **Passive voice** shifts the **object** into the subject slot, follows it with an appropriate form of the verb *to be*, and attaches the **past participle (V3)** of the main verb. ECAT tests this topic across all twelve tenses, modals, imperatives, and infinitives, so the auxiliary must always agree with the new subject and carry the tense of the original active verb.

| Voice | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Active | Subject + V1/V2 + Object | The **engineer** designed the bridge. |
| Passive | Object + be + V3 + by + Agent | The bridge **was designed** by the engineer. |
| Negative Passive | Object + be + not + V3 + by + Agent | The bridge was **not designed** by the engineer. |
| Interrogative Passive | Be + Object + V3 + by + Agent? | Was the bridge designed by the engineer? |

- Only **transitive verbs** (those taking a direct object) convert to passive. *Sleep, arrive, happen* are intransitive and reject passive transformation.
- The past participle never carries tense on its own; the auxiliary *be* does.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"STAB"** — **S**ubject becomes **T**he receiver, **A**uxiliary *be* agrees with new subject, **B**e + **V3** lands at the end. Spot a verb with no object? Skip the change — it cannot be passivised.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Conversion Rule
Take the active triad **Subject → Verb → Object** and remap it as **Object + be + V3 + by + Subject**. The auxiliary *be* must match the new subject in number and person, and it must carry the exact tense, aspect, and modality of the original active verb. The meaning stays identical; only the focus shifts from doer to receiver.

#### Tense-by-Tense Conversion Map

| Active Tense | Active Form | Passive Form | Example (Active → Passive) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simple Present | V1 / V1+s | is / are + V3 | She **writes** a letter → A letter **is written** by her. |
| Simple Past | V2 | was / were + V3 | He **wrote** a letter → A letter **was written** by him. |
| Present Continuous | is / are + V1+ing | is / are + being + V3 | She **is writing** a letter → A letter **is being written** by her. |
| Past Continuous | was / were + V1+ing | was / were + being + V3 | He **was writing** a letter → A letter **was being written** by him. |
| Present Perfect | has / have + V3 | has / have + been + V3 | She **has written** a letter → A letter **has been written** by her. |
| Past Perfect | had + V3 | had + been + V3 | He **had written** a letter → A letter **had been written** by him. |
| Future (will) | will + V1 | will + be + V3 | She **will write** a letter → A letter **will be written** by her. |
| Future (going to) | is / are + going to + V1 | is / are + going to + be + V3 | He **is going to write** → A letter **is going to be written** by him. |
| Modal | modal + V1 | modal + be + V3 | You **should respect** elders → Elders **should be respected**. |

#### Concept Comparison Matrix

| Feature | Active Voice | Passive Voice |
|---|---|---|
| Sentence focus | The doer (agent) | The action or the receiver |
| Typical use | Clear, direct, academic default | When the agent is unknown, obvious, or unimportant |
| Verb structure | Main verb alone (V1/V2/V3 as needed) | Auxiliary *be* + past participle (V3) |
| Typical ECAT MCQ clue | No auxiliary *be* before V3 | Form of *be* followed by V3 |
| Transitivity requirement | Works for any verb | Only transitive verbs qualify |

#### Special Constructions
- **Imperatives:** "Open the door" → **"Let the door be opened."** Negative: "Let the door not be opened."
- **Infinitives:** "to + V1" → **"to + be + V3"** (e.g., *to help → to be helped*).
- **Gerunds:** "V1 + ing" → **"being + V3"** (e.g., *I hate people interrupting me → I hate being interrupted*).
- **Yes/No questions:** invert the auxiliary and place the object first. *"Did she write the report?"* → *"Was the report written by her?"*
- **Wh- questions:** keep the wh-word in front. *"Who wrote the report?"* → *"By whom was the report written?"* or *"Who was the report written by?"*
- **Omitting the agent:** drop *by + noun* when the doer is unknown, obvious, or irrelevant. *"The window was broken"* (no need to say *by someone*).

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem
**Question:** Choose the correct passive form of: *"The committee has approved the proposal."*

(a) The proposal has approved by the committee.
(b) The proposal has been approved by the committee.
(c) The proposal is approved by the committee.
(d) The proposal was approved by the committee.

#### Solution:
1. Identify the active triad: **Subject** = *The committee*, **Verb** = *has approved* (present perfect tense), **Object** = *the proposal*.
2. Apply the present perfect rule from the conversion map: *has/have + been + V3*.
3. Promote the object *the proposal* to subject position; the auxiliary *has* stays attached to *been* before V3 *approved*.
4. Add the agent *by the committee* (optional but standard in MCQ contexts).
5. Final answer: **The proposal has been approved by the committee.** → Option **(b)**.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Option (a) drops the *been* auxiliary. Option (c) loses the perfect aspect by switching to simple present. Option (d) shifts the tense into simple past. Only option (b) preserves the present perfect aspect of the original active sentence.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Advanced Traps

| Trap | Wrong Conversion | Correct Conversion | Why It Fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intransitive verbs | *The accident was happened here.* | *The accident happened here.* | *Happen* takes no object, so no passive exists. |
| V2 confused with V3 | *A letter is wrote by her.* | *A letter is written by her.* | *Wrote* is simple past; the participle is *written*. |
| Modal passive | *Elders should be respect.* | *Elders should be respected.* | The bare infinitive *respect* must become the past participle *respected* after *be*. |
| Imperative passive | *Open the door* → *The door is opened.* | *Let the door be opened.* | Imperatives need the *Let + subject + be + V3* frame. |
| Two-object verbs | *He gave me a book* → *A book was given by him.* | *A book was given to me by him.* / *I was given a book by him.* | Indirect object may be retained or promoted; the direct object becomes the new subject. |
| Stative verbs | *The house is belonged to him.* | *The house belongs to him.* | *Belong* is stative and resists passive even though transitive in meaning. |

#### Common Mistakes in ECAT MCQs
1. **Dropping the auxiliary *be*.** Students write *"The work has completed"* instead of *"The work has been completed."* Always double-check for the *been* in perfect passive.
2. **Misplacing *by*.** Options such as *"By the committee the proposal has approved"* appear as distractors. The agent phrase must sit at the end.
3. **Over-passivising intransitives.** *"He is slept"* or *"The accident was occurred"* appear because students memorise the rule mechanically without checking transitivity.
4. **Breaking tense consistency.** Converting *"She writes"* into *"A letter was written"* changes tense from present to past. Match the auxiliary *is/are* to a present-tense source verb.
5. **Forgetting subject–verb agreement.** *"The proposals was approved"* fails agreement after the object becomes the new subject.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts
1. Convert this multi-clause sentence into passive while preserving every tense marker: *"While the manager was reviewing the file, the assistant had already submitted the report, and the client will sign the contract tomorrow."*
2. Identify and correct the error: *"The new policy is being agreed by most employees, but the budget was not yet being approved by the board when the meeting happened."* Justify each fix using transitivity, aspect, and tense rules.

It also intersects with **Subject–Verb Agreement**, since the auxiliary agrees with the promoted object.
