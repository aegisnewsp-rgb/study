# Legal Maxims

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Legal maxims are compact Latin or English phrases that state a rule of law — they are the shorthand every CLAT Legal Reasoning passage expects you to recognise. Around 4–6 MCQs per paper test maxims through meaning-recall, fact-pattern matching, or consequence-inference. Memorising 25–30 high-frequency maxims with their English equivalents covers most marks here.

#### Must-know maxims:

- *Actus Non Facit Reum Nisi Mens Sit Rea* — an act alone does not make one guilty unless the mind is also guilty.
- *Ignorantia Juris Non Excusat* — ignorance of the law is no excuse.
- *Audi Alteram Partem* — hear the other side (rule of natural justice).
- *Nemo Judex In Causa Sua* — no one should be a judge in their own cause.
- *Volenti Non Fit Injuria* — to a willing person, no injury is done.

#### Exam pointers:

- Read the **legal effect**, not just the Latin translation — CLAT tests application.
- Anchor each maxim to its **statutory home**: Section 6 IPC (Mens Rea), Section 10 Indian Contract Act (free consent).
- Eliminate distractors by checking whether the maxim governs **substance, evidence, or procedure**.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### What Legal Maxims Are

A legal maxim is a concise, established phrase — usually Latin — that crystallises a doctrine, principle, or rule of law. Common-law systems inherited them from Roman and English jurisprudence; Indian courts still cite them alongside statutes. In CLAT, maxims appear inside passage-based fact patterns and as stand-alone MCQs.

#### Three Functional Families

| Family | Governing Domain | Sample Maxims |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Substantive law | Defines rights, duties, liability | Mens Rea, Actus Reus, Volenti Non Fit Injuria, Caveat Emptor, Pacta Sunt Servanda |
| Evidence | Shifts the burden of proof | Res Ipsa Loquitur, Prima Facie, Ejusdem Generis |
| Procedure | Governs hearings, judges, remedies | Audi Alteram Partem, Nemo Judex In Causa Sua, Ubi Jus Ibi Remedium, Habeas Corpus |

#### Application Method

When a CLAT stem describes a fact pattern, follow this sequence:

1. Identify the **legal domain** the facts touch — criminal, contract, tort, procedure, evidence.
2. Recall the maxim that **governs that domain** in this exact situation.
3. Match the maxim's **legal effect** to the question asked — liability, defence, burden, or remedy.

#### Indian Statutory Anchors

- **Section 6 IPC** codifies *Mens Rea* (mental element) for criminal liability.
- **Section 10 Indian Contract Act, 1872** grounds *free consent* — defeating voluntariness defeats the contract.
- **Section 14 Sale of Goods Act, 1930** implied conditions narrow the scope of *Caveat Emptor*.

#### Frequent Trap

Students often translate *Audi Alteram Partem* as "judge in one's own cause" — that is *Nemo Judex In Causa Sua*. The first is a hearing rule; the second is a bias rule.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Modern Limits

Several maxims survive on paper but are heavily qualified by statute. *Caveat Emptor* (let the buyer beware) yields wherever the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 imposes a duty on sellers, or where implied conditions under Section 14 of the Sale of Goods Act apply. Similarly, *Volenti Non Fit Injuria* fails when consent was obtained by fraud, duress, or when the conduct amounts to negligence so gross it transcends the risk consented to.

#### Worked Micro-Example

Fact: A enters B's pharmacy, asks for a sleeping tablet, and is given a mislabelled bottle of rat poison. B did not know. A dies.

Reasoning: *Res Ipsa Loquitur* (the thing speaks for itself) applies — a properly handled sleeping tablet would not cause death unless someone was negligent. The doctrine shifts the **burden of proof** onto B to explain how the harm occurred without his fault. Notice that *Mens Rea* is irrelevant here because the question is civil negligence, not criminal intent.

#### Connections to Adjacent Topics

1. **Principles of Natural Justice** — *Audi Alteram Partem* and *Nemo Judex In Causa Sua* together form the two pillars.
2. **Interpretation of Statutes** — *Ejusdem Generis*, *Noscitur a Sociis*, and *Expressio Unius Est Exclusio Alterius* govern how courts read lists.
3. **Tort Liability** — *Volenti* and *Res Ipsa Loquitur* are recurring defences and presumptions.

#### Practice Prompts

1. A customs officer seizes goods without giving the importer a chance to be heard. Which maxim was violated, and which Indian statute or constitutional article reinforces it?
2. A signs a contract under threat of physical harm. Identify the maxim governing the defect in consent and the contract's legal status.
