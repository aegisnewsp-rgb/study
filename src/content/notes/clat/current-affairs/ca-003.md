# Legal News

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Legal News is the slice of the CLAT Current Affairs section that tests your awareness of recent Indian judicial and legislative developments. The Consortium of NLUs draws questions from the 12-month window preceding the exam, so anything older than a year is fair game to skip. You are tested on Supreme Court and High Court judgments, parliamentary statutes, presidential ordinances under Article 123, and amendments to the three new criminal codes that replaced the IPC, CrPC and Indian Evidence Act on 1 July 2024.

- **Read at least one national daily daily** (The Hindu or Indian Express) and one legal outlet (LiveLaw, Bar & Bench, Supreme Court Observer).
- **Always note** the bench strength, judge names, citation and date of judgment when summarising a case.
- **Map every item** to a subject head — criminal, constitutional, commercial, environmental, family, labour, tax, cyber.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### What "Legal News" covers in CLAT

Legal News sits inside the Current Affairs & General Knowledge section, which itself carries roughly 25% weightage in the 150-question paper. Of that, about 5% is drawn directly from legal developments reported in national dailies and law journals within the trailing 12 months. Questions appear as factual MCQs, comprehension passages or principle-application sets — they are never abstract jurisprudence.

#### Must-know terminology

Before you read any news item, lock down the vocabulary below. Examiners frequently drop one of these terms into a passage and test whether you caught the legal nuance.

| Term | Working meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Ratio Decidendi | The binding principle on which a decision rests |
| Obiter Dicta | Remarks by the court that are persuasive, not binding |
| PIL | Public Interest Litigation filed under Article 32 or 226 |
| Ordinance | A law made by the President under Article 123 when Parliament is not in session |
| Article 142 | Power of the Supreme Court to pass orders necessary for complete justice |
| BNS / BNSS / BSA | Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, Sakshya Adhiniyam — the three codes replacing IPC, CrPC, Evidence Act from 1 July 2024 |

#### How to read a judgment report

A reliable summary identifies the court, the bench (constitution/division/full), the parties, the statutory provisions interpreted, the issue, the holding and the relief. Skip the editorial framing — "SC slams government" tells you nothing about the legal principle. Instead, note whether the court read down a statute, struck it down, issued a mandamus, or referred the matter to a larger bench.

#### Where CLAT picks its questions from

- Supreme Court and High Court landmark judgments of the preceding year.
- Bills passed by Parliament and presidential assent timelines.
- Central Government ordinances promulgated under Article 123.
- Notifications from the Ministry of Law and Justice, the Election Commission of India, and NALSA.
- Significant amendments to the BNS, BNSS and BSA.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Mapping the 12-month window

CLAT's official syllabus fixes a 12-month reference window so the paper stays current. Build a month-by-month folder dated from the exam backwards. For each item log: date, court, bench, judges, citation, statutory provisions, holding, and the subject head (criminal, constitutional, commercial, environmental, family, labour, tax, cyber). This single habit eliminates the most common mistake students make — recalling the political slug but losing the legal core.

#### Edge cases examiners exploit

- **The criminal-code transition.** From 1 July 2024, the IPC, CrPC and Indian Evidence Act ceased to operate and were replaced by the BNS, BNSS and BSA. Many old section numbers still exist (e.g. murder is BNS Section 100, the equivalent of IPC 300; the new Section 63 BNS replaces IPC 376 for rape), but several punishments and procedures changed. Quoting the old IPC number for a 2025 fact is an instant trap.
- **PIL status confusion.** A PIL "admitted" is not the same as a PIL "disposed of". Interim orders do not bind lower courts permanently — note when the Supreme Court records "interim protection" rather than a final ruling.
- **Ordinance vs Law Commission Report.** An Ordinance is law the moment the President promulgates it; a Law Commission Report or Standing Committee recommendation has zero statutory force. Examiners regularly conflate the two to test attention.
- **Obiter as ratio.** Treat persuasive remarks as binding law and you have already lost the question. Always separate the holding from the commentary.

#### Practice prompts

1. List the three new criminal codes that replaced the IPC, CrPC and Indian Evidence Act, name the date they came into force, and cite the section of BNS that corresponds to murder.
2. Explain the difference between ratio decidendi and obiter dicta, then describe how a CLAT comprehension passage might test your ability to separate them in a recent Supreme Court ruling.
