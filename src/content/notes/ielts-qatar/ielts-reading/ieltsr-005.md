# Matching Headings to Paragraphs

> Verify the live Matching Headings question format on https://ielts.org/ Identifying Writer's Views/Claims question type guide and the British Council / IDP preparation pages before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Format:** a list of paragraph headings (typically Roman numerals i-xiii) and a passage of 7-10 paragraphs. The candidate matches each heading to the correct paragraph. NOT every heading is used (some are distractors).
- **Where it appears:** typically on Passage 1 or Passage 2 (the mid-difficulty passages). Usually 7-10 matching items per test (~17-25% of the 40-question total).
- **Order:** paragraphs follow the passage in order; the candidate matches heading A to the paragraph containing its main idea.
- **Source of truth:** the MAIN IDEA of each paragraph, not the topic, not the first/last sentence, not a single supporting detail.
- **Marking:** +1 correct, 0 incorrect (no negative marking).
- **Time pressure:** ~90 seconds per item. The whole set takes 10-15 minutes.

#### The 4-step matching strategy

| Step | Action | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Skim the headings first (60 sec).** Underline (in your mind) the KEY WORD in each heading. Note synonyms and paraphrases. | 1 min |
| 2 | **Read the FIRST and LAST sentence of each paragraph (60 sec).** Identify the MAIN IDEA — not the topic, not a supporting detail. | 1 min |
| 3 | **Match the heading's key word to the paragraph's main idea (30 sec/item).** Eliminate headings that focus on a DIFFERENT aspect of the topic. | 30 sec × 7-10 |
| 4 | **Mark and move on (10 sec/item).** Mark the answer, then move to the next paragraph. Do not second-guess once you have decided. | 10 sec × 7-10 |

#### The crucial insight: main idea, not topic

The single most common error in Matching Headings is treating the TOPIC as the MAIN IDEA.

| Sentence type | Example | Use in matching? |
|---|---|---|
| **Topic sentence** | "Education City is a campus in Doha that hosts US branch universities." | YES — names the topic. The MAIN IDEA is usually in the second or third sentence. |
| **Main idea sentence** | "Education City aims to diversify Qatar's economy beyond hydrocarbons by attracting global research talent." | YES — this is what the heading should paraphrase. |
| **Supporting detail** | "Carnegie Mellon University opened its Qatar campus in 2004." | NO — a detail, not the main idea. The heading would not paraphrase this. |
| **Example or illustration** | "Georgetown University in Qatar offers programs in foreign service and international affairs." | NO — an example, not the main idea. |

The crucial insight: **the main idea is the paragraph's CENTRAL CLAIM, not its topic. The heading paraphrases the central claim, not the topic.**

---

#### Examiner traps

- **Topic-not-main-idea error.** The candidate matches a heading about "Education City in Doha" to a paragraph whose main idea is "Qatar's economic diversification through research talent". The heading "Education City in Doha" is too narrow; the correct heading is "Qatar's economic diversification through research talent".
- **Detail-not-idea error.** The candidate matches a heading about "Carnegie Mellon's founding year" to a paragraph that mentions Carnegie Mellon but is primarily about Qatar's economic strategy. The correct heading is the main-idea paraphrase.
- **Distractor headings.** The candidate selects a heading that almost fits but focuses on a DIFFERENT aspect of the topic. The correct heading captures the MAIN idea.
- **Two-headings-fit error.** The candidate is torn between two headings that both seem to fit. The rule: pick the heading that captures the MAIN idea; the other heading captures a SUPPORTING detail.
- **Incomplete matching.** The candidate leaves an item blank. There is no negative marking; if stuck, guess.
- **Order assumption.** The candidate assumes heading A goes with paragraph A. There is no order rule; the candidate must match by main idea.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### The 7-step paragraph-matching strategy in detail

A measured 7-step approach to matching headings in ~90 seconds per item:

1. **Skim the headings first (60 sec).** Read each heading quickly. Underline (in your mind) the KEY WORD in each heading — the noun phrase that captures the central claim. Note that some headings may be synonyms or paraphrases of each other.
2. **Skim the passage's structure (30 sec).** Read the FIRST sentence of each paragraph. This gives you a map of the passage — what each paragraph is roughly about. Do not read in depth yet.
3. **Read each paragraph's first and last sentence in depth (45 sec/item).** Identify the MAIN IDEA — the central claim, the topic sentence, the conclusion the paragraph is building toward.
4. **Identify the paragraph's PURPOSE (15 sec/item).** Is the paragraph DEFINING (comparison), DESCRIBING (description), CONTRASTING (comparison), EVALUATING (example), or EXEMPLIFYING (example)?
5. **Match the heading's key word to the paragraph's purpose (10 sec/item).** The heading's key word should align with the paragraph's purpose. A heading about "X's impact on Y" matches a paragraph whose purpose is EVALUATING (example) or CONTRASTING (comparison).
6. **Eliminate wrong headings (15 sec/item).** Strike headings that focus on a different aspect, a different example, or a different topic.
7. **Mark and move on (5 sec/item).** Mark the answer, then move to the next paragraph. Do not second-guess once you have decided.

#### Distinguishing heading types

| Passage type | Description | Matching heading structure |
|---|---|---|
| **Definition paragraph** | Defines a term or concept | Heading paraphrases the definition |
| **Description paragraph** | Describes a phenomenon or event | Heading paraphrases the description |
| **Comparison paragraph** | Compares two or more things | Heading paraphrases the comparison |
| **Contrast paragraph** | Contrasts two viewpoints or findings | Heading paraphrases the contrast |
| **Evaluation paragraph** | Evaluates the importance or impact | Heading paraphrases the evaluation |
| **Example paragraph** | Provides examples or illustrations | Heading paraphrases the example |
| **Process paragraph** | Describes a sequence of events | Heading paraphrases the process |
| **Cause-effect paragraph** | Describes cause-and-effect relationships | Heading paraphrases the cause or effect |

### 🔴 Deep — Mastery (2mo+)

#### The cognitive architecture of Matching Headings

Matching Headings is the question type that tests the candidate's ability to read for MAIN IDEA at the paragraph level, not for topic or detail. Most candidates confuse the topic with the main idea, and within the main idea, confuse the central claim with a supporting detail.

A candidate scoring below 7 on Matching Headings typically has a main-idea-recognition gap or a heading-type-recognition gap. The deeper intervention is to read for the paragraph's PURPOSE first, then for the main idea, and to recognise that the heading paraphrases the purpose, not the topic.

#### Practice strategy for Matching Headings

1. **Use the British Council and IDP official IELTS practice tests.** The question styles and difficulty are calibrated against the live test.
2. **Practise with a stopwatch.** A 7-column Matching Headings set should take 10 minutes. Without practice, candidates take 15 minutes.
3. **Skim the headings first.** Build the habit of reading the headings before the passage.
4. **Read first and last sentences first.** Build the habit of identifying the main idea from the first and last sentences before reading in depth.
5. **Identify the paragraph's purpose.** Is the paragraph DEFINING, DESCRIBING, CONTRASTING, EVALUATING, EXEMPLIFYING, or PROCESSING? The heading paraphrases the purpose.
6. **Eliminate distractors.** Strike headings that focus on a different aspect, a different example, or a different topic.
7. **Distinguish two-headings-fit cases.** If two headings seem to fit, pick the heading that captures the MAIN idea; the other heading captures a SUPPORTING detail.
8. **Practise with Qatari-context passages.** Al Jazeera English editorials, Gulf Times articles, QU research commentary, QF program descriptions, Education City faculty opinion pieces build the local vocabulary and structure patterns you will encounter on test day.

#### When Matching Headings marks reflect a deeper issue

- **Persistent topic-not-main-idea errors:** the indicator is purpose-recognition. Train with a daily 5-minute "what is the purpose of this paragraph?" drill.
- **Persistent detail-not-idea errors:** the indicator is main-idea-vs-supporting-detail discrimination. Train with a daily 5-minute "what is the main idea, not the topic?" drill.
- **Persistent distractor errors:** the indicator is heading-aspect-discrimination. Train with a daily 5-minute "which aspect does this heading focus on?" drill.
- **Persistent two-headings-fit errors:** the indicator is main-idea-vs-detail discrimination. Train with a daily 5-minute "main idea or supporting detail?" drill.
- **Persistent Qatari-context errors:** the indicator is vocabulary-recognition. Train with a daily 5-minute "Qatari-vocabulary paraphrase" drill.
- **Persistent paragraph-purpose errors:** the indicator is paragraph-type recognition. Train with a daily 5-minute "definition vs comparison vs evaluation?" drill.

#### Edge cases and known traps

1. **A heading may fit the topic but not the main idea.** The correct heading captures the main idea; the topic-only heading is wrong.
2. **A heading may paraphrase a supporting detail, not the main idea.** The correct heading captures the main idea; the detail-only heading is wrong.
3. **Distractor headings may fit the topic but not the main idea.** Strike them.
4. **Some headings are unused.** Not every heading matches a paragraph.
5. **Some paragraphs may match two candidate headings.** Pick the one that captures the main idea; the other captures a detail.
6. **No negative marking.** Guess if you do not know the answer. A blank answer is guaranteed wrong; a guessed answer is 17-25% likely right (depending on the number of distractors).
7. **Order is not fixed.** Heading A may go with paragraph D, not paragraph A.
8. **Spelling is forgiving.** A spelling error that does not change the meaning is accepted.
9. **American vs British spelling.** IELTS accepts either American or British spelling as long as it is consistent within a single answer.

### Previous Year Patterns — Matching Headings

The Matching Headings question type has been part of the IELTS Reading test since the Academic / General Training split in 1989. It is published verbatim on ielts.org Matching Headings question type guide and is not expected to be removed.

Common year-on-year patterns:
- Matching Headings is more commonly placed on Passage 1 or Passage 2 (the mid-difficulty passages) than on Passage 3.
- A typical test has 7-10 Matching Headings items.
- The hardest distinctions are between main-idea headings and supporting-detail headings.
- The most common error is topic-not-main-idea confusion (candidates match a heading about the topic when the main idea is a more specific claim).
- A previous-year Matching Headings set is NOT reused verbatim in the same year; recycled sets appear with at most small contextual changes.

A previous-year record is not a deterministic indicator of a future test item. The British Council and IDP refresh the question bank annually, calibrated against the same difficulty curve.
