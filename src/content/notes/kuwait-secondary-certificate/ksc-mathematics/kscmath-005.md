# Coordinate Geometry — Kuwait Secondary Certificate Mathematics Notes

Coordinate Geometry is the fifth terminal-level domain at the Kuwait Secondary Certificate. The Kuwait MOE Mathematics curriculum allocates marks to the equation of a straight line, the distance and midpoint formulas, the equation of a circle, and the conic sections (parabola, ellipse, hyperbola). This domain integrates closely with Functions and Graphs and with Vectors.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Distance formula:** d = √((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²) between two points (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂).
- **Midpoint formula:** M = ((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2).
- **Slope of a line through (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂):** m = (y₂ − y₁) / (x₂ − x₁).
- **Slope-intercept form:** y = mx + b; m is the slope, b is the y-intercept.
- **Point-slope form:** y − y₁ = m(x − x₁).
- **Standard form:** Ax + By + C = 0; slope = −A/B.
- **Parallel lines:** equal slopes. **Perpendicular lines:** slopes are negative reciprocals.
- **Circle:** (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r² has centre (h, k) and radius r.

#### Examiner traps

- Dividing by zero when finding the slope of a vertical line (x₁ = x₂) — state that the line is vertical.
- Confusing the centre-radius form (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r² with the general form x² + y² + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0 where centre is (−g, −f).
- Treating a chord, a tangent, and a secant the same way at the circle.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Straight lines

The general equation of a straight line is Ax + By + C = 0. To convert to slope-intercept form, solve for y: y = (−A/B)x − C/B, so slope = −A/B and y-intercept = −C/B.

Two lines are parallel iff their slopes are equal (and they have different y-intercepts). Two lines are perpendicular iff the product of their slopes is −1.

The intersection of two lines is found by solving the two equations simultaneously. The result is a single point (x, y) — unless the lines are parallel (no intersection) or coincident (infinitely many intersections).

#### Distance from a point to a line

The perpendicular distance from point (x₀, y₀) to line Ax + By + C = 0 is |Ax₀ + By₀ + C| / √(A² + B²). This formula is essential for tangent-length problems on circles.

#### Circles

The standard equation (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r² has centre (h, k) and radius r. The general form x² + y² + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0 has centre (−g, −f) and radius √(g² + f² − c).

A line intersects a circle in 0, 1 or 2 points depending on whether the perpendicular distance from the line to the centre is greater than, equal to, or less than the radius. A tangent to a circle at point (x₁, y₁) is x x₁ + y y₁ + g(x + x₁) + f(y + y₁) + c = 0 (in general form) or (x₁ − h)(x − h) + (y₁ − k)(y − k) = r² (in standard form).

#### Conic sections

A parabola is the set of points equidistant from a focus and a directrix. Standard forms: y² = 4ax (right-opening) or x² = 4ay (upward-opening). Vertex at the origin, focus at (a, 0) or (0, a), directrix at x = −a or y = −a.

An ellipse is the set of points where the sum of distances to two foci is constant. Standard form x²/a² + y²/b² = 1 with a > b > 0. Foci at (±c, 0) where c² = a² − b².

A hyperbola is the set of points where the difference of distances to two foci is constant. Standard form x²/a² − y²/b² = 1. Foci at (±c, 0) where c² = a² + b².

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where Coordinate Geometry appears across the terminal exam

- **Paper 1:** distance and midpoint calculations, equation of a line through two points, intersection of two lines.
- **Paper 2:** circle equations and tangent lines, conic sections (often parabola), locus problems with constraints.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "A circle equation with a coefficient of x² different from y² is not a circle." No — any equation of form x² + y² + ... is a circle (or a point, or empty). The coefficient of x² must equal the coefficient of y², but neither is constrained to 1.
- "All parabolas open upward." No — y² = 4ax opens to the right; x² = 4ay opens upward; x² = −4ay opens downward.
- "The line Ax + By + C = 0 has slope A/B." No — the slope is −A/B.

#### Exam technique

- For circle problems, write the centre and radius explicitly at the start of working — they are the method marks.
- For tangent problems, draw the radius to the point of tangency and confirm it is perpendicular to the tangent line — that is the geometric reasoning the examiner wants to see.
- For locus problems, state the definition of the locus (equidistant, fixed distance, etc.) at the start — the definition is the method mark.
