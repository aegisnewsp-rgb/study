# Part 3 — Academic Discussion — IELTS Listening Notes

Part 3 of the IELTS Listening test is a conversation between two main speakers in an educational or training context. Typical contexts: two university students discussing an assignment with a tutor, two students planning a research project, two researchers discussing methodology. The recording runs about 5-7 minutes and has 10 questions. Part 3 is the third section and a strong band discriminator — most candidates drop marks here.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Format:** 10 questions. Test types: multiple choice, matching, flow-chart completion, short-answer questions.
- **Transcript style:** two main speakers (often students) with a third speaker occasionally (tutor). Discussion is argumentative or analytical.
- **Speed:** standard academic pace with academic vocabulary.
- **Order of answers:** questions follow the recording in order.
- **Predictable content:** opinions, reasons, methods, comparisons, decisions.
- **Common distractor:** speakers may change their minds, agree then disagree, or qualify their views. The answer is the final position.

#### Examiner traps

- Writing the first opinion before the speaker changes their mind.
- Missing the answer because the relevant phrase is brief and quickly followed by the next point.
- Forgetting to write plurals (e.g. "advantage" vs "advantages") on summary items.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Question types in Part 3

- **Multiple choice:** choose one of three or four options. Common in Part 3 because the discussion offers multiple viewpoints.
- **Matching:** match speakers to opinions, or items to categories. Each speaker may have multiple opinions.
- **Flow-chart completion:** follow a process described in the discussion. Information flows in order.
- **Short-answer questions:** open-ended questions answered in 1-3 words.

#### Active listening strategy

1. **Track the speakers:** note the names and roles of the speakers (e.g. Student A, Student B, Tutor).
2. **Note opinions:** each speaker has distinct views. Match the question to the right speaker.
3. **Follow the argument:** the discussion is structured — position 1, position 2, agreement/disagreement, resolution.
4. **Watch for qualifiers:** "actually," "in fact," "well, I think" often signal the speaker's real opinion.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where Part 3 appears across IELTS Listening

Part 3 is always the third section, accounting for 10 of the 40 questions. It is the section most candidates lose marks on because the academic vocabulary is dense and the speakers' positions change.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Both speakers agree at the end." Often they don't — Part 3 sometimes leaves disagreement unresolved.
- "The tutor gives the answers." No — the tutor facilitates; the students do most of the talking.
- "I can guess the answer without listening." Multiple-choice options are plausible; only listening carefully gets the right one.

#### Exam technique

- For matching items, identify which speaker has which opinion before the recording plays; then listen for confirmation.
- For flow-chart items, the answer is the next step in the process — listen for transitions ("first," "then," "next").
- For short-answer items, write exactly what you hear — paraphrase is usually wrong.
