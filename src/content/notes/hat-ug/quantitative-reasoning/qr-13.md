# Trigonometry Basics

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Trigonometry** rests on six ratios of a right-angled triangle, defined against an acute angle θ:

- **sin θ = Opposite / Hypotenuse**
- **cos θ = Adjacent / Hypotenuse**
- **tan θ = Opposite / Adjacent = sin θ / cos θ**
- **cosec θ = 1/sin θ, sec θ = 1/cos θ, cot θ = 1/tan θ**

Three **Pythagorean identities** convert any ratio to another: **sin²θ + cos²θ = 1**, **1 + tan²θ = sec²θ**, **1 + cot²θ = cosec²θ**. **Complementary angles** obey **sin(90° − θ) = cos θ**, **cos(90° − θ) = sin θ**, **tan(90° − θ) = cot θ**. Apply the **ASTC rule** for obtuse and reflex angles: in Quadrant II only sin is positive, Quadrant III only tan, Quadrant IV only cos. Memorize exact values at **0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 90°** — they appear in roughly 1 of every 5 HAT-UG Quantitative Reasoning MCQs.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### The Six Ratios in a Right Triangle
Pick the acute angle θ at one corner of a right triangle. The side facing θ is **Opposite**, the side next to θ (other than the longest) is **Adjacent**, and the longest side (opposite the 90° angle) is the **Hypotenuse**. The six ratios are pure numbers — no units — and every angle has a unique triple (sin θ, cos θ, tan θ) in the range 0 < θ < 90°.

#### Pythagorean Identities
The Pythagoras relationship sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 follows directly from the triangle itself (divide the sides² by Hypotenuse²). Dividing by cos²θ gives 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ; dividing by sin²θ gives 1 + cot²θ = cosec²θ. These identities let you find **sec θ from tan θ** (sec θ = √(1 + tan²θ)) without ever knowing the triangle's sides.

#### ASTC (Sign Convention)
On the unit circle, angles sweep from 0° to 360°. **All** six ratios are positive in Quadrant I (0°–90°). In Quadrant II (90°–180°), only **sin** and **cosec** are positive. In Quadrant III (180°–270°), only **tan** and **cot** are positive. In Quadrant IV (270°–360°), only **cos** and **sec** are positive. This is the **ASTC rule**.

#### Complementary Angles
For acute θ, the angle (90° − θ) swaps the role of opposite and adjacent, so each ratio becomes its co-ratio: **sin ↔ cos, tan ↔ cot, sec ↔ cosec**. This conversion is the engine behind most "find x without a calculator" HAT-UG items.

#### Standard Values Table

| θ (deg) | sin θ | cos θ | tan θ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0° | 0 | 1 | 0 |
| 30° | 1/2 | √3/2 | 1/√3 |
| 45° | 1/√2 | 1/√2 | 1 |
| 60° | √3/2 | 1/2 | √3 |
| 90° | 1 | 0 | undefined |

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Radian Measure and the Unit Circle
Angles are measured in **radians** (2π rad = 360°). On the unit circle (radius 1), a point on the rim has coordinates **(cos θ, sin θ)**, which is why tan θ is read as the slope of the radius line. Memorize the radian equivalents **0, π/6, π/4, π/3, π/2, π, 3π/2, 2π** — HAT-UG frequently mixes degrees and radians in the same question stem.

#### Heights and Distances
Two angle types model real-world sightlines from horizontal: the **angle of elevation** (looking up at an object) and the **angle of depression** (looking down). In a typical problem — tower of height h, distance d from base — **tan θ = h/d** gives h = d·tan θ. These word-problems usually chain two right triangles sharing a common side.

#### Edge Cases and Traps
- **tan 90° is undefined**, not infinity; likewise cot 0° is undefined.
- **sin θ and cos θ** are bounded in [−1, 1]; **tan θ and cot θ** are unbounded.
- An equation like **sin θ = 2** has **no real solution** — HAT-UG uses this to test whether you checked the range.
- When the angle is obtuse, write **θ = 180° − α** where α is acute, then use **ASTC** to fix the sign (e.g., sin 120° = sin 60° = √3/2, positive in Quadrant II).

#### Common Mistakes
- Marking **tan θ = 1/tan θ** instead of **cot θ = 1/tan θ**.
- Using **sin(90° − θ) = sin θ** (wrong) instead of **cos θ**.
- Forgetting that **sec θ = 1/cos θ**, not cos θ itself.
- Mixing degrees and radians on the calculator.

#### Practice Prompts
1. If sin θ = 3/5 and θ lies in Quadrant II, find cos θ, tan θ, and sec θ using only the Pythagorean identity and ASTC.
2. From a point 40 m from the foot of a tower, the angle of elevation of the top is 30°. Find the tower's height (use √3 ≈ 1.732).
