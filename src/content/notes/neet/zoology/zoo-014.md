# Health Disease

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> The fastest fact worth memorising first: a *sign* is what the doctor measures, a *symptom* is what the patient feels.

- **Health (WHO, 1948)** — complete physical, mental and social well-being, not just the absence of disease.
- **Disease** — a disorder of structure or function producing specific signs and symptoms.
- **Etiology** answers *what causes* a disease; **pathogenesis** answers *how* it develops; **pathology** is the scientific study of disease as a whole.
- **Innate immunity** — non-specific barriers (skin, mucous membranes, phagocytes, lysozyme in tears/saliva). **Adaptive immunity** — specific, with **B-cells (humoral, antibodies)** and **T-cells (cell-mediated)**.
- **Active vs Passive immunity** — body makes its own antibodies (long-lasting) versus ready antibodies are given (short-lived).

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook — "FEPS-VAC":** **F**emale Anopheles (malaria), **E**ntamoeba (amoebiasis), **P**lasmodium, **S**almonella typhi (typhoid); **V**ector-borne, **A**ir/water-borne, **C**ontact-borne — pair the pathogen with its route in the same breath.

| Disease | Pathogen | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Malaria | *Plasmodium vivax / falciparum / malariae* | Protozoan |
| Filariasis | *Wuchereria bancrofti* | Helminth |
| Ascariasis | *Ascaris lumbricoides* | Helminth |
| Typhoid | *Salmonella typhi* | Bacterium |
| Pneumonia | *Streptococcus pneumoniae* | Bacterium |
| TB | *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* | Bacterium |
| Ringworm | *Microsporum / Trichophyton / Epidermophyton* | Fungi |
| Common cold | Rhino virus | Virus |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Concepts That Look Similar But Are Not

NEET routinely frames options that swap one technical term for another. Lock these distinctions early.

| Term | What it really means | Easy confusion to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Sign | Objective, measurable (fever on thermometer, rash, swelling) | Not the same as symptom |
| Symptom | Subjective, felt by patient (pain, nausea, fatigue) | Not the same as sign |
| Disease | Objective disorder with identifiable pathology | Distinct from illness |
| Illness | Subjective feeling of being unwell | Can exist without diagnosed disease |
| Syndrome | Group of signs/symptoms occurring together | Not a single disease |
| Infection | Pathogen present and multiplying in the host | Not the same as disease (subclinical cases exist) |
| Contagion | Infectious agent that spreads between hosts | Not every infection is contagious |
| Endemic | Constant low-level presence in an area | Not the same as epidemic |
| Epidemic | Sudden spike above normal expectancy | Not pandemic (which is across countries) |
| Pandemic | Epidemic spread across continents | COVID-19, 1918 influenza |

#### Classification of Diseases

Diseases sort into overlapping categories that examiners mix and match in MCQs.

1. **Communicable (infectious)** — caused by pathogens and transmissible: bacterial (TB, typhoid, pneumonia), viral (common cold, polio), protozoan (malaria, amoebiasis), helminthic (filariasis, ascariasis), fungal (ringworm).
2. **Non-communicable (non-infectious)** — cancer, diabetes, hypertension, arthritis; cannot pass person-to-person under normal conditions.
3. **Congenital** — present from birth (e.g. sickle cell anaemia, haemophilia).
4. **Acquired** — develops after birth from environment, lifestyle, or infection.
5. **Acute vs chronic** — sudden/short course (pneumonia, common cold) versus long-drawn (TB, arthritis).
6. **Deficiency** — caused by lack of nutrients (scurvy from vitamin C, rickets from vitamin D, anaemia from iron).
7. **Degenerative** — progressive wear of tissues (osteoarthritis, osteoporosis).
8. **Neoplastic** — uncontrolled cell proliferation (benign vs malignant tumours).

#### How Diseases Travel

- **Direct contact** — skin-to-skin (ringworm spreads via shared towels, soil contact).
- **Droplet** — expelled during coughing/sneezing within ~1 m (tuberculosis, pneumonia).
- **Air-borne** — suspended particles or droplet nuclei travelling further (TB, measles).
- **Fecal-oral (water-borne)** — contaminated drinking water and food (typhoid, amoebiasis, ascariasis).
- **Vector-borne** — through a living carrier, almost always the **female mosquito** needing a blood meal for egg production: *Anopheles* → malaria; *Culex* → filariasis; *Aedes* → dengue.
- **Sexual contact / blood transfusion** — HIV, hepatitis B.

#### Worked Problem

A patient presents with fever recurring every third day, chills, and sweating. Peripheral blood smear shows a protozoan parasite inside RBCs. Identify the disease, the pathogen, and the vector.

#### Resolution

1. The third-day cyclic fever with chills and sweating is the textbook description of **benign tertian malaria**, caused by **Plasmodium vivax**. *P. falciparum* gives malignant tertian (irregular / 36–48 h cycles with severe complications); *P. malariae* gives quartan fever on the fourth day. Cycle length matches *P. vivax*.
2. Pathogen: *Plasmodium vivax*, an **intracellular protozoan parasite** with a digenetic life cycle — asexual schizogony in human liver and RBCs, sexual sporogony in the mosquito.
3. Vector: **female *Anopheles* mosquito**. Males feed on plant nectar and do not bite humans. The female requires a vertebrate blood meal to mature her eggs; *Plasmodium* completes its sexual phase inside her gut.
4. Prevention: use insecticide-treated bed nets, eliminate stagnant water, oil spraying of breeding sites, prophylactic chloroquine in endemic zones.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** options usually include *Culex* (which transmits filariasis, not malaria), *Aedes* (dengue), and *male Anopheles*. Female *Anopheles* is the only correct answer. Also, students often type *Plasmodium falciparum* for cyclic third-day fever; that species is malignant tertian, not benign tertian.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

#### Where the Margins Get Thin

1. **Antigen vs antibody.** Antigen is the foreign molecule (often surface protein of a pathogen) that provokes an immune response. Antibody (immunoglobulin) is the protein synthesised by **plasma cells** (differentiated B-lymphocytes) against a specific antigen. Antigens do not fight; antibodies do. NEET reverses these terms in distractors.
2. **Active vs passive vs herd immunity.** Active = host manufactures antibodies after antigen exposure (natural infection or vaccination), immunological memory formed, long-lasting. Passive = pre-formed antibodies transferred in (maternal IgG via placenta, IgA via colostrum, anti-tetanus serum, anti-rabies immunoglobulin), short-lived, no memory. Herd immunity = a sufficient fraction of a population is immune that transmission chains break — protects even non-immune individuals.
3. **Vaccine types that confuse candidates.** OPV (oral polio vaccine) = **live attenuated** (Sabin). IPV (injectable polio) = **killed/inactivated** (Salk). BCG for TB = live attenuated *Mycobacterium bovis*. Typhoid Ty21a = live attenuated; Vi polysaccharide = subunit. Toxoids (tetanus, diphtheria) = inactivated exotoxins, not killed organisms.
4. **Helminths vs protozoans.** Wuchereria is a filarial nematode transmitted by *Culex*, lives in lymphatics, causes elephantiasis. Ascaris is an intestinal roundworm transmitted by **ingesting embryonated eggs from contaminated soil/food** — it is NOT vector-borne. Mixing these two is one of the highest-yield errors on this topic.
5. **Mycobacterium vs Streptococcus in the lungs.** Both cause respiratory disease, but *M. tuberculosis* is an acid-fast, slow-growing, intracellular bacillus (caseating granulomas, Mantoux screening); *S. pneumoniae* is a Gram-positive diplococcus causing lobar pneumonia with rust-coloured sputum.
6. **Pathogenesis of malaria.** Sporozoites from mosquito saliva → liver hepatocytes (exo-erythrocytic schizogony) → merozoites released → invade RBCs (erythrocytic schizogony) → haemozoin (malarial pigment) from haemoglobin digestion causes cyclic fever. *P. vivax* has a dormant hypnozoite stage in liver — this is why primaquine is needed for radical cure and relapse is possible.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. A 4-year-old is given an injection of anti-rabies immunoglobulin after a dog bite. The same child previously completed the full post-exposure vaccine course a year ago. What type of immunity does the immunoglobulin provide, and is it faster or slower-acting than the vaccine-induced response? Justify with respect to memory cells.
2. In a district reporting >50 malaria cases per 1000 population for three consecutive years with no seasonal fall, classify the outbreak as endemic, epidemic, or pandemic and design a control plan that addresses the vector, the parasite reservoir, and human susceptibility simultaneously.
