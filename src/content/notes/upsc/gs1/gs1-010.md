# Post-Independence

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Post-Independence India spans 15 August 1947 (Independence under the **Indian Independence Act 1947**) through the present, anchored by the Constitution adopted 26 November 1949 and enforced 26 January 1950. UPSC Prelims repeatedly tests these two distinct dates and the framing bodies behind them — the **Constituent Assembly** (President Rajendra Prasad; Drafting Committee Chair B.R. Ambedkar) drafted the text, while the British Parliament enacted the Independence Act.

The **1991 LPG reforms** under PM P.V. Narasimha Rao and Finance Minister Manmohan Singh dismantled the **Licence Raj**, cut tariffs from ~350% to ~30%, and opened the **mixed economy** to foreign investment. Earlier, the **Nehruvian model** (Planning Commission 1950, Five Year Plans, Panchsheel 1954, NAM 1961) shaped non-alignment and state-led industrialisation until the 1967 and 1971 political-economic turning points.

- **Three high-yield Prelims traps**: adoption vs commencement date of the Constitution; Pokhran-I (1974) vs Pokhran-II (1998); Operation Flood's three phases (1970–80, 1981–85, 1985–96).
- **Article 370** abrogated 5 August 2019; **Article 352** declared during the 1975–77 Emergency.
- **Planning Commission** replaced by **NITI Aayog** on 1 January 2015.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Constitutional foundation and integration

The Constituent Assembly held 166 sittings over roughly three years, producing a text adopted 26 November 1949 and enforced 26 January 1950 — the latter chosen to commemorate the 1930 Purna Swaraj declaration. Simultaneously, **Sardar Patel** and **V.P. Menon** integrated 562+ princely states via Instruments of Accession; Hyderabad (police action 1948), Junagadh, and Kashmir (accession October 1947, leading to Article 370) were the most contested.

#### Nehruvian nation-building (1947–64)

Nehru set up the Planning Commission (1950), Atomic Energy Commission (1948), IITs, and ISRO's predecessor. The **First Plan (1951–56)** used the Harrod–Domar growth model; the **Second Plan (1956–61)** adopted the **Mahalanobis model** prioritising heavy industry. Foreign policy rested on **Panchsheel (1954)** and the **Non-Aligned Movement** (Bandung 1955, Belgrade 1961). Linguistic states were reorganised under the **States Reorganisation Act 1956**, beginning with Andhra (1953).

#### Crises and turning points (1962–1989)

The **1962 Sino-Indian War** ended Nehru's non-alignment optimism. **Indo-Pak wars of 1965 and 1971** culminated in Bangladesh's liberation and the **Shimla Agreement (1972)**. The **1975–77 Emergency** suspended Fundamental Rights under Article 352; the **42nd Amendment (1976)** later entrenched Parliament's supremacy. **Pokhran-I (1974)** was India's first peaceful nuclear test. The 1989 **Mandal–Mandir phase** reshaped coalition politics.

| Era | Anchor policy | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1950–69 | Mixed economy, Five Year Plans | State-led industrialisation |
| 1975–77 | Emergency (Art. 352) | Suspended Fundamental Rights |
| 1991–99 | LPG reforms | Tariffs cut, FDI ceilings raised |
| 2014–19 | NITI Aayog (2015), GST (2017) | Cooperative federalism |

#### The 1991 LPG reforms

Triggered by a balance-of-payments crisis, the reforms abolished industrial licensing except in 18 sectors, dismantled public-sector monopoly in many areas, established **SEBI (1992)**, and introduced rupee convertibility on the trade account.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge cases and adjacent links

The **Green Revolution** (HYV seeds, M.S. Swaminathan, 1960s–70s) solved Punjab/Haryana's food deficit but widened regional inequality — a recurring GS1 Mains theme on **continuity vs change** from colonial agrarian structures. **Operation Flood** (White Revolution, Verghese Kurien) ran in three distinct phases: **Phase I (1970–80)** linked 18 milk sheds with Delhi; **Phase II (1981–85)** expanded to 136 milk sheds; **Phase III (1985–96)** consolidated India's status as the world's largest milk producer. Mains often expects you to contrast these phases.

Constitutional continuity is tested through amendments: **1st (1951)** added the Ninth Schedule; **42nd (1976)** is the "mini-Constitution" (added words Socialist, Secular, Integrity to the Preamble); **44th (1978)** reversed much of 42nd; **73rd and 74th (1992)** devolved power to Panchayats and Municipalities. **Article 370's abrogation (5 Aug 2019)** reorganised Jammu & Kashmir into two Union Territories.

#### Common mistakes to avoid

- **Conflating adoption and commencement** dates (26 Nov 1949 vs 26 Jan 1950).
- **Attributing the Independence Act** to the Constituent Assembly — it was a British Parliamentary statute.
- **Citing the Planning Commission** as the current apex policy body — NITI Aayog replaced it on 1 January 2015.
- **Calling 1991 reforms "privatisation only"** — LPG stands for Liberalisation, Privatisation, **and** Globalisation.
- **Mislocating Pokhran tests**: Pokhran-I (1974, Indira Gandhi, "Smiling Buddha") vs Pokhran-II (1998, Vajpayee).

#### Worked Mains prompt

> "Examine how the integration of princely states shaped India's federal character." (Hint: Instruments of Accession, States Reorganisation Act 1956, Article 370 as a unique autonomy arrangement later abrogated.)
