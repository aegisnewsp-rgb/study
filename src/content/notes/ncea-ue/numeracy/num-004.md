# NCEA Level 1 Mathematics: Demonstrate mathematical reasoning (91947) — NCEA UE Numeracy Notes

Achievement Standard 91947 is the second of two NCEA Level 1 Mathematics external standards, alongside 91946. It is assessed by an end-of-year NZQA examination and carries 5 credits. The standard contributes to UE numeracy when used in conjunction with the dedicated co-requisite numeracy standard US32406, in the same way as 91946.

> Verify the live UE numeracy standards spreadsheet on https://www2.nzqa.govt.nz/about-us/publications/newsletters-circulars/assessment-matters/a2023-17/ before planning revision around the figures below.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **5 credits**, external NZQA examination at the end of Year 11.
- Tests mathematical reasoning: solving problems with relational and extended abstract thinking; justifying reasoning.
- Counts toward UE numeracy when used in conjunction with US32406.
- Sister standard to 91946 (Interpret and apply in context); both contribute equally to UE numeracy.

#### Examiner traps

- Writing only the final answer — NZQA markers need to see the reasoning that connects the start to the end.
- Skipping the justification of method choice.
- Confusing 91947 (reasoning) with 91946 (interpret and apply in context) — they are separate standards with separate exams.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### How 91947 contributes to UE numeracy

The 2024 UE numeracy rule is 10 credits at Level 1 or above. US32406 (5 credits) provides the dedicated co-requisite numeracy half. The other 5 credits can come from:

- US32412 (Te Pāngarau, 5 credits).
- A numeracy-rich achievement standard (downloadable spreadsheet).
- A NCEA Level 1 Mathematics external standard (91946 or 91947, 5 credits each).

So a student who achieves both US32406 and 91947 satisfies the full 10-credit UE numeracy requirement.

#### What the standard tests

91947 tests mathematical reasoning with explicit justification:

- Solve problems using number, algebra, geometry, measurement, statistics, or probability.
- Show the working.
- Justify the method (Merit and Excellence grades).
- Reflect on why the result holds (Excellence grade).

The exam is reasoning-led: each question asks the student to set up an argument, carry out the derivation, and justify the conclusion.

#### Preparing for UE numeracy through 91947

Same as 91946: take Level 1 Mathematics, achieve the standard, and combine with US32406 to satisfy UE numeracy.

Strategy:

1. Take Level 1 Mathematics (which includes 91947 as one of the externals).
2. Achieve the standard.
3. Also achieve US32406.
4. Combine the credits to satisfy UE numeracy.

#### Why choose 91947 over 91946 (or both)

A student typically takes BOTH 91946 and 91947, since they are both required for the Level 1 Mathematics programme. The choice is not between them but between US32406 + (91946 or 91947 or both) for UE numeracy.

A student who achieves both 91946 and 91947 plus US32406 has 15 credits of numeracy standards — well above the 10-credit UE requirement.

#### Combined pathway

The most common UE numeracy pathway through NCEA is:

- US32406 (5 credits, dedicated co-requisite).
- 91946 OR 91947 (5 credits, NCEA Level 1 Mathematics external).
- Total: 10 credits, satisfying UE numeracy.

This pathway is recommended because it combines applied numeracy (US32406) with formal mathematics (91946/91947), giving a strong foundation for university study.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Excellence in 91947

The biggest gap between Merit and Excellence on 91947 is the depth of justification.

For example, on a question about solving a quadratic equation by completing the square:

- Merit: Solve x² + 6x + 5 = 0 by completing the square, getting x = −1 or x = −5. Verify by substitution.
- Excellence: Solve the equation by completing the square, verify by substitution, AND connect the vertex of the parabola to the minimum value of the quadratic, AND explain why completing the square gives the vertex directly.

The Excellence response reflects on the method, not just applies it.

#### Transferable skills

The mathematical reasoning skills in 91947 are foundational for university-level thinking:

- Logical argument (every step justified).
- Method selection (choosing the right approach).
- Self-reflection (checking that the answer makes sense).

These skills are valuable across all university courses, not just mathematics.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "91947 alone satisfies UE numeracy." No — it provides 5 credits; the dedicated co-requisite US32406 is also needed.
- "Reasoning is the same as calculation." No — reasoning requires visible justification, not just a correct answer.
- "The exam is the same as 91946." It is sister but distinct — 91947 emphasises reasoning, 91946 emphasises interpretation and application in context.
- "UE numeracy is optional." No — it is required for University Entrance.
