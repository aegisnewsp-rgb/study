# Geography and Location

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

> **The single fact that wins you points:** Saudi Arabia sits in the southwest of Asia, occupying roughly four-fifths of the Arabian Peninsula, and is anchored between two seas — the Red Sea to the west and the Arabian Gulf to the east.

The Kingdom covers about **2,330,000 km²** of land, stretching between latitudes **16°N and 33°N** and longitudes **35°E and 56°E**. The capital, **Riyadh**, sits near the centre of the Najd plateau. Land borders run with Jordan and Iraq in the north, Kuwait in the northeast, Qatar, Bahrain and the UAE across the Gulf to the east, Oman in the southeast, and Yemen in the south.

Skip Iraq only if forced: the full ring is *Jordan–Iraq–Kuwait–Qatar–Bahrain–UAE–Oman–Yemen*.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Astronomical and relative location

Astronomical (الموقع الفلكي) location answers *where on the globe* — Saudi Arabia lies between latitudes **16°N** (near Jizan) and **33°N** (near the Northern Borders), and between longitudes **35°E** (Red Sea coast near Duba) and **56°E** (the empty eastern desert near the UAE frontier). Relative (الموقع الجغرافي) location answers *what lies next to it* — the Kingdom dominates the Arabian Peninsula, the largest peninsula in the world, sharing land frontiers with **eight neighbours** and sea frontiers across two gulfs.

The two seas are not interchangeable on the exam. The **Red Sea (البحر الأحمر)** is a young rift basin separating Arabia from Africa; the **Arabian Gulf (الخليج العربي)** — never "Persian" in Saudi curricula — is a shallow shelf sea bordered by the Kingdom's Eastern Province. The Gulf coast is shorter (~800 km) than the Red Sea coast (~1,800 km) because the Arabian Shield pushes the western shoreline inland.

#### The six topographical sections

Saudi terrain is conventionally grouped into **six physical units**:

1. **Hejaz mountains (حجاز)** — a north-south chain parallel to the Red Sea.
2. **Najd plateau (نجد)** — the central limestone plateau, where Riyadh sits.
3. **Asir highlands (عسير)** — the highest block in the southwest, with peaks above 3,000 m near Abha.
4. **Al-Hasa plain (الإحساء)** — the low coastal shelf facing the Arabian Gulf.
5. **Rub' al-Khali (الربع الخالي, "Empty Quarter")** — the world's largest continuous sand sea, dominating the southeast.
6. **An-Nafud (نفود الكبير)** — the great northern sand sea; **distinct from Ad-Dahna (الدهناء)**, the curved dune belt linking An-Nafud to Rub' al-Khali.

#### Climate, regions, and major cities

Climate follows the **BWh (hot desert)** classification under the Köppen system: summer daytime readings regularly exceed **45°C** in the interior, while the Asir highlands stay noticeably cooler. Rainfall is sparse and irregular. Administratively, the Kingdom is divided into **13 regions (مناطق إدارية)** after the recent reorganisations — the older "13 emirates" count is outdated.

#### Common concept confusions

| Confused pair | Correct distinction |
|---|---|
| Arabian Gulf vs "Persian Gulf" | Saudi textbooks use **الخليج العربي (Arabian Gulf)**; "Persian" is rejected in KSA. |
| An-Nafud vs Ad-Dahna | **Nafud** is the northern sand sea; **Dahna** is the curved dune arc joining Nafud to Rub' al-Khali. |
| Jordan's direction | Jordan is **northern**, not western; Yemen is the southern neighbour. |
| Iran as a neighbour | Iran is **not** a land neighbour — the Arabian Gulf separates the two. |
| Hejaz vs Asir | Hejaz runs north of Asir along the Red Sea; Asir is the southern, higher block. |

#### Worked question

**Item stem:** "The Empty Quarter (Rub' al-Khali) is one of the largest sand deserts in the world. In which part of Saudi Arabia is it located, and which neighbouring country lies closest to it on its southern flank?"

**Resolution:** Rub' al-Khali covers roughly **650,000 km²** in the **southeastern** quarter of the Kingdom, spilling across the border into **Yemen (and marginally into Oman and the UAE)**. On a map it fills the triangle bounded by the Tuwayq escarpment (west), the Hadhramaut (south), and the Gulf coast near Abu Dhabi (east). The southern flank is therefore Yemen, not Oman.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students frequently pick "Oman" because Rub' al-Khali extends near the Omani border, but the **southern** boundary is shared with Yemen. Direction matters more than "looks close on the atlas."

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

#### Latitudinal implications a sharp candidate should know

Because the Kingdom straddles the **Tropic of Cancer (23.5°N)** — the line passes through the Eastern Province and central Najd — much of the country lies within the **horse latitudes** belt of descending dry air. That single geographic fact explains three downstream exam points: persistent high pressure, minimal convective rainfall, and the dominance of mechanical (wind-driven) weathering over chemical weathering in exposed rock. Latitude also fixes daylight length: at 33°N the solsticial swing is roughly **10 hours in winter / nearly 14 hours in summer**, a contrast students sometimes confuse with equatorial regions.

The longitudinal range of **21°** (35°E–56°E) produces a **1 hour 24 minute** difference between the easternmost and westernmost points if you used local solar time, which is why a **single official time zone (UTC+3, Arabia Standard Time)** is enforced nationwide — a frequent assertion-reason question.

#### Edge cases and exception handling

- **The "13 regions" trap.** Always verify the version referenced in the question paper. The pre-2022 enumeration listed 13 provinces but split Eastern Province differently; the post-reorganisation count is **13 regions**, with NEOM, Al-Ula and other giga-projects sitting inside Tabuk and Al-Madinah rather than forming their own regions.
- **Coastline split.** About **1,800 km Red Sea + 800 km Arabian Gulf = ~2,600 km total coast**. If the question asks for the longer coast, the answer is the Red Sea, even though the Gulf gets more geopolitical attention.
- **Hejaz vs Tihama.** Tihama (تهامة) is the **coastal plain** along the Red Sea; Hejaz is the **mountain range** immediately inland. They are not synonyms.
- **Highest point.** Jabal Sawda near Abha, at roughly **3,015 m**, is the highest peak in the Kingdom — useful when an item offers Asir and Hejaz peaks as options.
- **The Rub' al-Khali's "active" dunes vs "stable" dunes.** Active dunes migrate northeast under the Shamal winds; the stable interdune flats host ancient lake beds (paleolakes) from wetter Pleistocene phases — a clue when questions invoke climate history.

#### Advanced practice prompts

1. An assertion-reason item states: *"Because Saudi Arabia lies between 16°N and 33°N, it experiences four distinct seasons."* Identify the false premise, name the climatic classification that governs the Kingdom (BWh), and explain in two sentences why latitudinal position alone does not produce seasonal contrasts here.
2. A map-based item shows the Arabian Peninsula with a shaded country along the **southeastern** coast of the Red Sea and asks for its capital. Decide whether the shading matches Saudi Arabia's western flank, name the principal Red Sea Saudi cities in north-to-south order (**Tabuk region → Duba → Jeddah → Makkah corridor → Jizan**), and list the two Saudi regions whose territory touches the Red Sea south of Jeddah.
