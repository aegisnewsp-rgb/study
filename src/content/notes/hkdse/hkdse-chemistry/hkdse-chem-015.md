# XV. Analytical Chemistry (Elective) — HKDSE Chemistry Notes

This topic covers Elective Topic XV of the HKDSE Chemistry Elective Part. Candidates sit any two of the three electives on Paper 2. The topic addresses detection of species (qualitative analysis of cations, anions and gases); separation methods (filtration, distillation, solvent extraction, chromatography); volumetric analysis and titration; and instrumental methods (colorimetry, pH meters, conductivity meters, mass spectrometry basics).

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Qualitative analysis**: identifies the species present (cations, anions, gases). Uses flame tests, precipitation reactions, and specific reagents.
- **Cations**: NH₄⁺ (ammonia released with NaOH, detected by smell or litmus), Cu²⁺ (blue solution, deep blue with NH₃, brown with KI), Fe³⁺ (yellow-brown solution, blue-black with K₄[Fe(CN)₆] or blood-red with KSCN), Fe²⁺ (green solution, blue with K₃[Fe(CN)₆]), Al³⁺ (white precipitate with NaOH, dissolves in excess), Zn²⁺ (white precipitate with NaOH, dissolves in excess).
- **Anions**: Cl⁻ (white precipitate with AgNO₃, insoluble in dilute NH₃), Br⁻ (cream precipitate with AgNO₃), I⁻ (yellow precipitate with AgNO₃), SO₄²⁻ (white precipitate with BaCl₂, insoluble in dilute HCl), CO₃²⁻ (effervescence with dilute HCl, gas turns limewater milky), NO₃⁻ (brown ring test), OH⁻ (turns litmus blue).
- **Gases**: H₂ (burns with a pop), O₂ (relights a glowing splint), Cl₂ (yellow-green, bleaches damp litmus), CO₂ (turns limewater milky), NH₃ (turns damp red litmus blue), SO₂ (turns damp blue litmus red, decolourises acidified KMnO₄).
- **Separation methods**: filtration (insoluble solid from liquid), centrifugation (small particles from liquid), evaporation/crystallisation (dissolved solid from solution), distillation (liquid from solution by boiling), fractional distillation (liquids with different boiling points), solvent extraction (solute from one solvent to another), chromatography (components of a mixture by differential partition between stationary and mobile phases).
- **Chromatography**: paper chromatography, thin-layer chromatography (TLC), column chromatography, gas chromatography (GC), high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC). The retention factor Rf = distance travelled by spot / distance travelled by solvent.
- **Volumetric analysis**: titration — acid-base, redox, complexometric, precipitation. The endpoint is detected by indicator, colour change, or instrumental signal.
- **Instrumental methods**: colorimetry (measures absorbance at a specific wavelength; A = εcl, Beer-Lambert law); pH meter (measures [H⁺]); conductivity meter (measures ion concentration); mass spectrometry (separates ions by mass-to-charge ratio).

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing flame test colours (Li red, Na yellow, K lilac, Ca brick-red, Cu blue-green, Ba green).
- Forgetting to acidify before the SO₄²⁻ test (to remove carbonate interference).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Qualitative analysis of cations

**Flame tests**: a clean wire loop dipped in the sample is held in a Bunsen flame. Each element emits a characteristic colour.

- Li⁺ — crimson red
- Na⁺ — yellow
- K⁺ — lilac (view through blue cobalt glass)
- Ca²⁺ — brick-red
- Sr²⁺ — crimson
- Ba²⁺ — apple green
- Cu²⁺ — blue-green (with HCl, blue)

**Aqueous cation tests**:

- **NH₄⁺**: add NaOH; warm gently; release of NH₃ (turns damp red litmus blue, distinctive smell).
- **Cu²⁺**: blue solution (Cu²⁺·6H₂O); deep blue [Cu(NH₃)₄]²⁺ with excess NH₃; brown CuI precipitate with KI (with release of I₂).
- **Fe²⁺**: pale green solution; Prussian blue (KFe[Fe(CN)₆]) with K₃[Fe(CN)₆].
- **Fe³⁺**: yellow-brown solution; Prussian blue (KFe[Fe(CN)₆]) with K₄[Fe(CN)₆]; blood-red Fe(SCN)₃ with KSCN.
- **Al³⁺**: white Al(OH)₃ precipitate with NaOH; dissolves in excess NaOH (amphoteric) or excess NH₃ (no, only Al(OH)₃ dissolves in NaOH, not NH₃).
- **Zn²⁺**: white Zn(OH)₂ precipitate with NaOH; dissolves in excess NaOH (amphoteric).

#### Qualitative analysis of anions

- **Cl⁻**: white AgCl precipitate with AgNO₃; insoluble in dilute NH₃.
- **Br⁻**: cream AgBr precipitate with AgNO₃; partially soluble in dilute NH₃.
- **I⁻**: yellow AgI precipitate with AgNO₃; insoluble in dilute NH₃.
- **SO₄²⁻**: white BaSO₄ precipitate with BaCl₂; insoluble in dilute HCl (this distinguishes from SO₃²⁻, which gives white BaSO₃ soluble in HCl).
- **SO₃²⁻**: white BaSO₃ precipitate with BaCl₂; soluble in dilute HCl; SO₂ released on acidification.
- **CO₃²⁻**: effervescence of CO₂ with dilute HCl; gas turns limewater milky.
- **NO₃⁻**: brown ring test — carefully add FeSO₄ solution to acidified sample, then carefully add concentrated H₂SO₄ down the side; brown ring at the interface ([Fe(H₂O)₅NO]²⁺).

#### Separation methods

- **Filtration**: separates an insoluble solid from a liquid (e.g. sand from water).
- **Centrifugation**: for small particles; the mixture is spun at high speed, denser particles settle to the bottom.
- **Crystallisation**: a solution is evaporated to crystallise the dissolved solid; cooling may also be used.
- **Simple distillation**: separates a liquid from a dissolved solid (e.g. water from salt water); the liquid is boiled, the vapour is condensed, and the condensate is collected.
- **Fractional distillation**: separates liquids with different boiling points (e.g. petroleum fractions); uses a fractionating column.
- **Solvent extraction**: a solute is extracted from one solvent into another (immiscible) solvent in which it is more soluble.
- **Chromatography**: separates a mixture based on differential partition between a stationary phase and a mobile phase. In TLC, the Rf value identifies components. In GC and HPLC, the retention time identifies components.

#### Volumetric analysis

- **Acid-base titration**: standard acid vs. unknown base (or vice versa); phenolphthalein or methyl orange as indicator.
- **Redox titration**: standard oxidising agent (KMnO₄, K₂Cr₂O₇) vs. unknown reducing agent (or vice versa); KMnO₄ is self-indicating (pink endpoint), starch indicator for iodine titrations.
- **Complexometric titration**: EDTA (ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid) titrates metal ions; Eriochrome Black T indicator; used for water hardness.
- **Precipitation titration**: standard solution of one ion precipitates the unknown ion; Mohr method uses AgNO₃ + K₂CrO₄ indicator.

#### Instrumental methods

- **Colorimetry**: a coloured solution absorbs light; absorbance is proportional to concentration (Beer-Lambert law: A = εcl). Used to determine the concentration of a coloured species (or a coloured complex with a colourless species).
- **pH meter**: measures the [H⁺] using a glass electrode; accurate to ±0.01 pH units.
- **Conductivity meter**: measures the electrical conductivity of a solution; proportional to ion concentration.
- **Mass spectrometry**: ionises a sample and separates the ions by mass-to-charge ratio (m/z). Used to determine molecular mass and structure. The mass spectrum shows peaks at different m/z values with relative intensities.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-topic links

- **IV. Acids and Bases**: pH measurement and acid-base titration.
- **VI. Microscopic World II**: concentration and dilution.
- **VII. Redox reactions, chemical cells and electrolysis**: redox titration.

#### Exam technique

- **For cation/anion identification**: state the test, the observation, and the conclusion.
- **For separation questions**: identify the mixture type (solid-liquid, liquid-liquid, dissolved solutes) and the appropriate method.
- **For instrumental questions**: state the principle, the application, and the relevant equation (e.g. Beer-Lambert law for colorimetry).

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Sodium gives a yellow flame because of the element's colour." The flame test is for the metal cation, not the atom. Different metals produce different colours.
- "All solutions conduct electricity equally." Conductivity depends on ion concentration and mobility; pure water has very low conductivity.
- "Mass spectrometry can determine the structure of any molecule." Mass spectrometry is one tool; full structure determination usually requires NMR, IR and UV-Vis as well.
