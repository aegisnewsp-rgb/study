# Identifying Information (True/False/Not Given) — IELTS Reading Notes

True/False/Not Given is the question type that most candidates lose marks on. The task is to decide whether each statement agrees with the information in the passage (True), contradicts it (False), or is not stated (Not Given). It tests careful reading and the ability to distinguish what is said from what is implied.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **True:** the statement agrees with the passage (it is a correct paraphrase or summary of explicit information).
- **False:** the statement contradicts the passage (it is the opposite of what the passage says).
- **Not Given:** the statement is neither confirmed nor contradicted — the passage is silent on it.
- **Common confusion:** candidates often confuse Not Given with False. The difference is that False contradicts; Not Given is silent.
- **Order of answers:** questions follow the passage in order.

#### Examiner traps

- Choosing False when the statement is simply not addressed.
- Choosing True when the statement adds information not in the passage (it is partly true but includes a false detail).
- Misreading the statement to match the passage.

---

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Strategy

1. Read the statement carefully — identify the main claim and any details.
2. Scan the passage for the relevant section.
3. Compare the statement to the passage:
   - Does it agree? → True.
   - Does it contradict? → False.
   - Is it silent? → Not Given.

#### Distinguishing True from Not Given

If the statement is a strict paraphrase of the passage (same meaning, different words), it is True. If the passage is silent on the statement, it is Not Given. The test is whether the passage is silent — even if the statement seems plausible, if the passage does not address it, it is Not Given.

#### Distinguishing False from Not Given

If the statement is the opposite of what the passage says, it is False. If the passage is silent on the statement, it is Not Given. Test: if you can find a sentence in the passage that says the opposite, it is False. If you cannot find any sentence that addresses the statement, it is Not Given.

#### Paraphrase detection

The IELTS Reading test is built on paraphrases — the statements use different words from the passage but the same meaning. Practice identifying paraphrases:

- "The number of students increased" ↔ "There was a rise in student numbers"
- "Researchers found that..." ↔ "According to the study,..."
- "The experiment was successful" ↔ "The trial produced the desired outcome"

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where True/False/Not Given appears across IELTS Reading

True/False/Not Given is one of the most common question types in IELTS Academic Reading. It typically appears in one of the three passages, often with 4-6 questions.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Not Given means false." No — Not Given means silent. The passage does not say it is true or false.
- "If the statement is plausible, it is True." No — plausibility is irrelevant; only the passage's content counts.
- "True and Yes are the same." In Identifying Information (True/False/Not Given), the answers are True/False/Not Given. In Identifying Writer's Views (Yes/No/Not Given), the answers are Yes/No/Not Given. They are different question types.

#### Exam technique

- For each statement, locate the relevant passage section first.
- Read the statement carefully and identify the main claim.
- Compare word-by-word against the passage.
- If the passage is silent on the statement, choose Not Given, even if it seems plausible.
