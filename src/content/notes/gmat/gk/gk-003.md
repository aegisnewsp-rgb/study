# Ghana Political System and Governance

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Ghana runs a **unitary presidential constitutional republic** under the **1992 Fourth Republican Constitution**. The President is elected by absolute majority for a **four-year term**, renewable once. Legislative power sits with a **unicameral Parliament of 275 MPs**, and the judiciary is led by the **Supreme Court** as the final appellate authority.

- Head of State & Government: President (directly elected, 4-year term, one re-election)
- Legislature: Unicameral Parliament, 275 single-member constituencies, first-past-the-post
- Judiciary: Lower Courts → High Court → Court of Appeal → Supreme Court
- Local Government: 16 Regions, 261 MMDAs, District Chief Executive (President-appointed, Assembly-confirmed)
- Advisory body: Council of State (no executive or legislative power)

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Three Branches of Government

The 1992 Constitution distributes state power across three arms with explicit checks and balances. The President appoints ministers from Parliament, but each minister must secure parliamentary approval. Parliament can pass a vote of censure with a two-thirds majority, and the Supreme Court strikes down legislation that violates the Constitution.

| Branch | Head | Key Function | Term / Composition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Executive | President | Head of State & Government, appoints ministers | 4 years, one re-election |
| Legislature | Speaker of Parliament | Law-making, oversight, approval of ministers | 275 MPs, unicameral |
| Judiciary | Chief Justice | Interpretation, final appellate review | Lower → High → Appeal → Supreme |

#### Decentralized Local Government

Local governance is operationalised through the **Local Government Act, 1993 (Act 462)**. Ghana is divided into **16 Regions**, which are subdivided into **261 Metropolitan, Municipal and District Assemblies (MMDAs)**. Each assembly has elected members plus 30% appointed by the President. A **District Chief Executive (DCE)** is nominated by the President and must be approved by **two-thirds of the assembly**.

Common exam traps:

- Ghana is **unitary**, not federal — there is one sovereign central government.
- Parliament is **unicameral** (one chamber), not bicameral like the United States.
- The DCE is **not directly elected**; the public votes for assembly members only.
- The presidential limit is **two terms** (one re-election), not unlimited.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### The Council of State and Constitutional Bodies

The **Council of State** is a purely advisory body nominated by the President and confirmed by Parliament. It advises on national policy, statutory appointments, and the exercise of the prerogative of mercy (pardoning). Critically, it holds **no executive or legislative power** — a frequent multiple-choice trap where distractors imply it can veto Bills.

Ghana's constitutional architecture also embeds independent commissions: the **Electoral Commission of Ghana (EC)** conducts elections and referenda; the **Commission on Human Rights and Administrative Justice (CHRAJ)** investigates abuse; and the **National Media Commission** oversees broadcasting standards. These sit outside the three branches and report directly to Parliament or the President on specific mandates.

#### Exam Strategy for GAT

On the Ghana GAT, Governance questions are typically **1–3 mark MCQs** testing institutional facts. The highest-yield items ask about the number of MPs (275), parliamentary structure (unicameral), and presidential term limits (4 years, two terms max). Expect distractor answers that swap term lengths with the U.S. system or misname the constitution as the "First Republican" instead of the Fourth.

| Frequently Tested Fact | Correct Answer | Common Wrong Answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Parliamentary size | 275 MPs | 200, 250, 300 |
| Constitution in force | 1992 Fourth Republican | 1960 First Republican |
| Number of Regions | 16 | 10, 13 |
| DCE appointment | President nominates, Assembly confirms | Directly elected by voters |
