# Practice Test and Test-Taking Strategies

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The NMAT (Philippines) Verbal subtest is a fixed-format, multiple-choice section administered by the **Center for Educational Measurement (CEM)**. It draws four item types from a standardized Item Bank: **word analogy**, **reading comprehension**, **sentence completion**, and **error analysis**. Scoring uses **no-penalty marking**, so every blank answer is equivalent to a wrong one.

- **Pacing formula:** total section seconds ÷ number of items = per-item ceiling (e.g., 40 minutes ÷ 60 items ≈ 40 sec/item).
- **Process of Elimination (POE):** strike options that contradict keywords, contain absolutes, or break grammar before guessing.
- **Active reading:** read the question stem first, then scan for keyword matches instead of reading every word.
- **No-penalty rule:** always attempt the final 5–8 items even if you must guess; expected value is non-negative.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### NMAT Verbal Item Distribution
Practice tests must mirror the Item Bank’s four item families so your pacing calibration transfers to test day. Skewed drills (e.g., 90% RC) leave you under-trained on analogy or error analysis.

#### Pacing and the Flag-and-Return Rule
Compute your **per-item ceiling** before the timer starts. When a single item consumes more than 1.5× that ceiling, mark it, move on, and return only if 20–30 seconds remain. On the NMAT Verbal subtest, the last items are no harder than the first, so forfeiting them to early dwelling destroys your percentile band.

#### Process of Elimination (POE) Mechanics
Apply POE as a four-step filter:
1. Eliminate options that contradict a question stem keyword (e.g., “*except*,” “*weakening*”).
2. Cross out choices with absolute qualifiers (*always*, *never*, *completely*) when a moderate option exists.
3. Discard options that violate subject–verb agreement or coreference rules on error-analysis items.
4. Between the two survivors, pick the one preserving the passage’s **register and tone**.

#### Active Reading for Reading Comprehension
Read the **stem first**, circle the operative verb (infer, imply, weaken, strengthen), then scan the passage for the keyword cluster. This converts a 300-word passage into a 30-second lookup task and prevents the classic trap of selecting an answer that is true in real life but unsupported by the text.

#### Common Strategy Errors

| Mistake | Why It Hurts | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Leaving items blank | No-penalty scoring means 0 = wrong, so blanks forfeit EV | Attempt every item |
| Bringing outside knowledge into RC | Rewards answers unsupported by passage | Match text, not memory |
| Choosing the “sophisticated” answer | Tests syntactic/collocation fit, not vocabulary flair | Test each option against the stem |
| Dwelling on one RC passage | Steals time from later items | Flag-and-return at 1.5× ceiling |

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### No-Penalty Scoring: The Expected-Value Argument
Because the NMAT (Philippines) does not subtract marks for incorrect responses, the expected gain of a random guess on a four-option item is +0.25 of a correct answer’s value. This makes **blanking strictly dominated** by guessing. The corollary: when POE leaves two options, your hit probability rises to ~0.50 — at that point, guessing is a high-leverage move, not a last resort.

#### Educated Guessing Beyond POE
When POE stalls, test each surviving option against three diagnostic prompts:
- Does it **change the meaning** of the stem? (Eliminate.)
- Does it **shift the register** (formal↔informal, literal↔figurative) relative to the passage? (Eliminate.)
- Does it introduce an **unsupported extreme** where a qualified alternative exists? (Eliminate.)

The option that survives all three is your best guess.

#### Error Categorization for Post-Test Review
Tag every wrong answer as **Content Gap**, **Careless Slip**, **Misread Stem**, or **Time Pressure**. A review log showing 60% careless slips signals a pacing problem; 60% content gaps signals a curriculum gap. This single habit converts practice tests from score-checks into diagnostic instruments.

#### Worked Micro-Example: Sentence Completion
*Stem:* “The professor’s lecture was so __________ that several students dozed off.”  
Options: (A) invigorating, (B) soporific, (C) concise, (D) contentious.  
Apply POE: (A) contradicts *dozed off* → eliminate. (C) is neutral, not causally linked to sleep → eliminate. (D) implies debate, not drowsiness → eliminate. Survivor: **(B) soporific**, confirmed by collocation with *dozed off*.

#### Practice Prompts
1. Take one timed 40-item Verbal mini-section. Score it, then classify every error using the four-tag system above; plan the next week around the largest category.
2. On your next RC set, read stems first for 100% of passages and log your average seconds per item to verify the per-item ceiling.
