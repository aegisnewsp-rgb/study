# Passport to Advanced Math — Exponential, Radical and Rational Expressions — SAT Math Notes

Exponential, radical and rational expressions are part of the SAT Math Passport to Advanced Math content domain. The SAT tests equivalent expressions with exponents, radicals and rational denominators; growth and decay; function transformation; and inverse functions.

> Verify the live SAT specifications on https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/ before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Exponent rules:** aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ; aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ; (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ; a⁰ = 1; a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ.
- **Radical rules:** √(ab) = √a × √b; √(a/b) = √a / √b.
- **Rationalising the denominator:** multiply top and bottom by the conjugate (a + b) → (a - b)(a + b) = a² - b².
- **Exponential growth:** y = a × bᵗ where b > 1.
- **Exponential decay:** y = a × bᵗ where 0 < b < 1.
- **Function transformations:** y = f(x - h) shifts right by h; y = f(x) + k shifts up by k; y = a × f(x) stretches vertically by a.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing exponential growth with linear growth.
- Treating exponents of zero as producing zero (a⁰ = 1, not 0).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Simplifying exponential expressions

Apply the exponent rules:

- aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ
- aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ
- (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ
- a⁰ = 1 (a ≠ 0)
- a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ

#### Radical expressions

- √a × √b = √(ab)
- √a / √b = √(a/b)
- √(a²) = |a|

#### Rationalising the denominator

To rationalise 1/(a + b), multiply top and bottom by the conjugate (a - b):

1/(a + b) = (a - b) / ((a + b)(a - b)) = (a - b) / (a² - b²).

#### Exponential growth and decay

For y = a × bᵗ:

- If b > 1, the function grows exponentially.
- If 0 < b < 1, the function decays exponentially.
- If b = 1, the function is constant.
- If b < 0, the function oscillates.

#### Function transformations

The graph of y = f(x - h) is the graph of y = f(x) shifted h units to the right. The graph of y = f(x) + k is shifted k units up. The graph of y = a × f(x) is stretched vertically by a.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where Exponential, Radical and Rational Expressions appears across the SAT Math section

Exponential, radical and rational expressions items appear throughout the SAT Math section. They test the candidate's ability to manipulate and simplify complex expressions.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Exponential growth is the same as linear growth." No — exponential growth accelerates; linear growth is constant.
- "a⁰ = 0." No — a⁰ = 1.

#### Exam technique

- For exponential expressions, apply the exponent rules systematically.
- For radical expressions, use the radical rules to simplify.
- For rationalising, identify the conjugate and multiply.
- For transformations, identify the shift direction.

#### Step-by-step worked College Board test example

**Problem**:
A water filtration facility operates pumps that filter 450 liters of water every 18 minutes. At this constant rate, how many hours will it take the facility to filter 7,500 liters of water?

**Solution**:
1. Calculate the flow rate in liters per minute:
   Rate = 450 liters / 18 minutes = 25 liters per minute.
2. Determine total minutes required to filter 7,500 liters:
   Total time in minutes = 7,500 liters / 25 liters per minute = 300 minutes.
3. Convert minutes to hours:
   Hours = 300 minutes / 60 minutes per hour = 5.0 hours.
4. Always verify unit conversion: the problem requested the final duration in hours, not minutes.
