# XI. Chemistry of Carbon Compounds — HKDSE Chemistry Notes

This topic covers Topic XI of the Compulsory Part of HKDSE Chemistry. It addresses functional groups and homologous series (alcohols, carboxylic acids, esters, amides, amines, carbonyl compounds); isomerism (structural and stereoisomerism); important reactions (substitution, addition, esterification, hydrolysis, polymerisation); and synthetic polymers.

> Verify the live HKDSE Chemistry Assessment Framework on https://www.hkeaa.edu.hk/ before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Functional groups**: alkane (none, only C-C/C-H), alkene (C=C), alkyne (C≡C), alcohol (-OH on saturated C), ether (-O-), aldehyde (-CHO), ketone (-CO-), carboxylic acid (-COOH), ester (-COO-), amide (-CONH₂), amine (-NH₂), nitrile (-C≡N), halide (-X where X = F/Cl/Br/I).
- **Homologous series**: alkanes (CₙH₂ₙ₊₂), alkenes (CₙH₂ₙ), alkynes (CₙH₂ₙ₋₂), alkanols (CₙH₂ₙ₊₂O), alkanals (CₙH₂ₙO), alkanones (CₙH₂ₙO), alkanoic acids (CₙH₂ₙO₂), esters (CₙH₂ₙO₂), amines (CₙH₂ₙ₊₃N), amides (CₙH₂ₙ₊₁NO).
- **Isomerism**: structural (chain, position, functional group) and stereoisomerism (geometric E/Z, optical enantiomers).
- **Key reactions**: substitution (alkanes with halogens); addition (alkenes with H₂, HX, X₂, H₂O); esterification (acid + alcohol → ester + water); hydrolysis (ester → acid + alcohol; amide → acid + amine); polymerisation (addition and condensation).
- **Synthetic polymers**: addition polymers (e.g. poly(ethene), poly(propene), poly(vinyl chloride), poly(styrene)); condensation polymers (e.g. polyamides/nylon, polyesters/Terylene); biopolymers (proteins, nucleic acids, polysaccharides).

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing E/Z isomerism with cis-trans — E/Z is the IUPAC nomenclature for geometric isomerism.
- Treating "polyester" as a single structure — there are many polyesters with different monomer units.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Functional groups and reactions

**Alkanes**: combustion, substitution with halogens (free-radical mechanism).

**Alkenes**: addition reactions (electrophilic addition). H₂ (catalyst Pt/Pd), HX (Markovnikov's rule), X₂ (Br₂, Cl₂), H₂O (acid catalyst).

**Alkanols**: combustion, substitution (-OH → -X with HX), oxidation (primary alkanol → alkanal → alkanoic acid; secondary alkanol → alkanone; tertiary alkanol → no reaction with mild oxidising agents), dehydration (alkanol → alkene + H₂O with concentrated H₂SO₄).

**Alkanoic acids**: as weak acids (acid + alkali, acid + carbonate, acid + active metal), esterification (alkanoic acid + alkanol → ester + H₂O with concentrated H₂SO₄ catalyst).

**Esters**: hydrolysis (acid hydrolysis or base hydrolysis; base hydrolysis is saponification if the ester is a fat/oil).

**Amides**: hydrolysis (amide + acid or base → carboxylic acid + amine).

**Amines**: as bases (amine + acid → salt + H₂O).

#### Isomerism

**Structural isomers**: same molecular formula, different connectivity.

- **Chain isomers**: differ in the carbon skeleton (e.g. n-butane vs. isobutane).
- **Position isomers**: differ in the position of a functional group (e.g. propan-1-ol vs. propan-2-ol).
- **Functional group isomers**: differ in the functional group (e.g. ethanol vs. dimethyl ether).

**Stereoisomers**: same connectivity, different spatial arrangement.

- **Geometric (cis-trans, E/Z) isomers**: differ in the arrangement around a C=C double bond or a ring. E (entgegen) — opposite; Z (zusammen) — together.
- **Optical isomers (enantiomers)**: non-superimposable mirror images. Require a chiral centre (a carbon with four different groups).

#### Polymerisation

**Addition polymerisation**: monomers with C=C double bonds add together to form a polymer without loss of any atom. Examples: poly(ethene), poly(propene), poly(vinyl chloride), poly(styrene), PTFE (Teflon).

**Condensation polymerisation**: monomers with two functional groups react, eliminating a small molecule (often water) each time a bond forms. Examples: polyamides (nylon 6,6 from hexanedioic acid and 1,6-diaminohexane), polyesters (Terylene/Dacron from benzene-1,4-dicarboxylic acid and ethane-1,2-diol), proteins (amino acids), polysaccharides (glucose), nucleic acids (nucleotides).

**Polymer properties**:

- Thermoplastic polymers (e.g. poly(ethene), PVC) soften on heating and can be remoulded.
- Thermosetting polymers (e.g. Bakelite, melamine) harden permanently on heating.
- Elastomers (e.g. rubber) can be stretched and return to shape.
- Biodegradable polymers (e.g. PLA, PHB) break down under environmental conditions.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-topic links

- **V. Fossil Fuels and Carbon Compounds**: builds on the basics of alkanes, alkenes and combustion.
- **VIII. Chemical Reactions and Energy**: combustion is the main energy source from hydrocarbons.
- **XIII. Industrial Chemistry (Elective)**: petrochemicals and polymers are major industrial products.

#### Exam technique

- **For naming questions**: identify the longest chain, the principal functional group, and the substituents.
- **For isomerism questions**: distinguish structural isomers from stereoisomers; for stereoisomers, identify E/Z or enantiomers.
- **For polymerisation questions**: identify the monomers, the type of polymerisation (addition or condensation), and the small molecule eliminated (if condensation).

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Optical isomers are the same as geometric isomers." Optical isomers are mirror images; geometric isomers differ in arrangement around a double bond.
- "All plastics are the same." Plastics vary widely in properties depending on the polymer structure and additives.
- "Polymerisation produces no waste." Condensation polymerisation releases water (or other small molecules); addition polymerisation does not.
