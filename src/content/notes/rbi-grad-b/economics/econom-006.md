# Cost Theory

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Cost theory** maps how a firm's spending moves with output, separating **explicit costs** (cash outflows like wages, rent, raw material) from **implicit costs** (the owner's foregone salary, the opportunity cost of own capital). Summing both yields **economic cost**; only explicit payments form **accounting cost**. In the **short run** at least one input (typically capital K) is fixed, so total cost splits into **FC + VC**. The five must-know identities are **TC = FC + VC**, **AFC = FC/Q**, **AVC = VC/Q**, **ATC = TC/Q**, and **MC = ΔTC/ΔQ**. In the **short run**, the **MC curve cuts ATC and AVC at their respective minimums**, and **AFC falls continuously** but never touches the axis. In the **long run** all inputs vary, the **LRAC** is the envelope of SRACs, and a firm chooses plant size where **P = SRMC = LRAC minimum**. Quick exam cues: **break-even is P = ATC**, **shut-down is P = min AVC**.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Cost taxonomy

Start every cost-theory answer by classifying costs along two axes. By **traceability**: *explicit* (contractual, monetary — wages, interest, raw material) vs *implicit* (foregone next-best earnings — owner's time, own capital's normal profit). **Economic cost = Explicit + Implicit**; **accounting cost = Explicit only**, so economic profit = accounting profit − implicit cost. By **variability with output**: *fixed cost* (FC) paid even at Q = 0, *variable cost* (VC) that rises with Q, *total cost* TC = FC + VC.

#### Short-run per-unit curves

Define **AFC = FC/Q**, **AVC = VC/Q**, **ATC = TC/Q = AFC + AVC**. Because FC is constant in the numerator and Q grows in the denominator, **AFC is a downward rectangular hyperbola** approaching but never touching the Q-axis. **AVC and ATC are U-shaped** — falling when MC < AC, rising when MC > AC, reaching minimum exactly where **MC = AC**. **MC = dTC/dQ** measures the cost of producing one more unit; geometrically it is the slope of the TC curve.

#### Long-run cost envelope

In the **long run** all factors are variable, so the firm can pick any plant size. Each plant gives a different SRAC; the **LRAC is the lower envelope** of all SRACs. As scale expands, **LRAC falls** because of **internal economies of scale** — *technical* (indivisibilities, longer runs), *managerial* (specialisation), *financial* (cheaper credit), *marketing* (bulk advertising), *risk-bearing* (portfolio effect) — and *external economies* like localisation. Beyond the **minimum efficient scale**, **diseconomies of scale** (coordination failure, principal–agent problems) push LRAC upward.

In the RBI Grade B Phase-II Economics paper, questions arrive as MCQs on (a) the MC–AC relationship, (b) shut-down vs break-even point under perfect competition, and (c) reasons for U-shaped LRAC. Use the table below to fix the algebra.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Mechanism behind the U-shape

The short-run U is not an assumption — it is **derived from the Law of Variable Proportions**. With fixed K and rising labour L, the **marginal product of labour (MPL)** first rises (stage I: increasing returns from specialisation), peaks, then falls (stage II: diminishing returns), and finally turns negative (stage III). Because **MC = w/MPL**, MC first falls (MPL rising), hits a minimum where MPL is maximum, then rises as MPL declines. Since AVC falls while MC < AVC and rises while MC > AVC, AVC's minimum coincides with MC's minimum, and similarly for ATC — **MC leads AC by one period of analysis**.

#### LRAC envelope and MES

The LRAC is constructed by drawing the lowest SRAC attainable at every Q. The point where LRAC first flattens is the **minimum efficient scale (MES)** — the smallest output at which long-run average cost is minimised. Industries with low MES (e.g. retail) host many firms; those with high MES (e.g. airlines, telecom) trend toward oligopoly. Note that the **envelope may have flat regions** where two SRACs are tangent — here the firm is **indifferent between two plant sizes**, a nuance the basic "lowest envelope" diagram hides.

#### Policy and RBI context

For macroeconomic applications, an **economy-wide short-run aggregate supply curve** is built by aggregating firm-level MC curves using product prices as weights, so understanding cost theory underpins inflation analysis in the RBI Grade B Monetary Policy paper. The **shut-down rule P = min AVC** and **break-even rule P = ATC** appear in the Market Structure and Pricing chapter, and links to **contestable markets** (Baumol–Panzar–Willing): even monopoly LRAC must equal competitive LRAC at minimum for hit-and-run entry to discipline pricing.

#### Common mistakes

- Saying MC always equals AC. It equals AC **only at AC's minimum**.
- Treating accounting profit and economic profit as the same — the gap is implicit cost including **normal profit**.
- Drawing AFC touching the Q-axis; AFC approaches zero asymptotically but never reaches it within the relevant range.
- Confusing **break-even** (P = ATC, firm earns normal profit) with **shut-down** (P = min AVC, firm covers only variable cost).
