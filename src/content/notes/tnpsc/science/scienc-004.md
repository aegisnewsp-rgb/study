# Human Body Systems

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid review of cardiovascular, nervous, respiratory, and endocrine physiological systems for TNPSC Group 1 preliminary examination.

**Physiological Systems — High-Yield Essentials**

1. **Cardiovascular System:**
   - **Circulation Architecture:** Human heart possesses four chambers (2 atria, 2 ventricles). Double circulation encompasses pulmonary circulation (right ventricle → pulmonary artery → lungs → pulmonary veins → left atrium) and systemic circulation (left ventricle → aorta → tissues → vena cava → right atrium).
   - **Cardiac Pacemaker:** Sinoatrial (SA) node located in right atrium generates spontaneous action potentials (70–72 beats/min) and is known as the biological pacemaker.
   - **Blood Composition:** Normal blood volume is ~5 liters. Formed elements comprise Erythrocytes (RBCs, lifespan 120 days, non-nucleated), Leucocytes (WBCs: granulocytes and agranulocytes), and Thrombocytes (platelets, lifespan 8–10 days, clot initiation).
2. **Nervous System Coordination:**
   - Central Nervous System (CNS: brain and spinal cord) and Peripheral Nervous System (PNS: cranial nerves 12 pairs, spinal nerves 31 pairs).
   - Autonomic Nervous System: Sympathetic (fight-or-flight: adrenergic, dilates pupil, elevates heart rate) and Parasympathetic (rest-and-digest: cholinergic, constricts pupil, slows heart rate via vagus nerve).
3. **Endocrine Glands and Master Regulators:**
   - **Pituitary Gland (Hypophysis):** Master endocrine gland seated in the sella turcica of the sphenoid bone. Anterior pituitary (adenohypophysis) secretes GH, TSH, ACTH, FSH, LH, and PRL. Posterior pituitary (neurohypophysis) releases oxytocin and vasopressin (ADH), synthesized in hypothalamus.
   - **Thyroid Gland:** Largest endocrine gland, producing T3 (triiodothyronine) and T4 (tetraiodothyronine / thyroxine), controlling basal metabolic rate (BMR).
   - **Adrenal Gland:** Adrenal cortex produces glucocorticoids (cortisol), mineralocorticoids (aldosterone), and sex steroids. Adrenal medulla secretes catecholamines (adrenaline / epinephrine and noradrenaline).
   - **Pancreas (Islets of Langerhans):** Alpha cells secrete glucagon (hyperglycemic); Beta cells secrete insulin (hypoglycemic); Delta cells secrete somatostatin.

⚡ **TNPSC Group 1 Exam Trap:** Candidates often confuse the universal blood donor and universal recipient groups under the ABO-Rh system. Blood group **O-negative (O-)** is the true universal donor (possesses no A, B, or Rh surface antigens). Blood group **AB-positive (AB+)** is the universal recipient (possesses no anti-A, anti-B, or anti-Rh serum antibodies).

## 1. Excretory System and Nephron Architecture

The human kidney filters approximately 180 liters of glomerular filtrate daily to excrete ~1.5 liters of concentrated urine:

| Segment of Nephron | Transport Mechanism | Permeability Characteristics | Primary Filtrate Modifications |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bowman Capsule & Glomerulus** | Ultrafiltration under hydrostatic pressure | Permeable to water and solutes < 68 kDa | Blood cells and large plasma proteins retained; protein-free filtrate formed |
| **Proximal Convoluted Tubule (PCT)** | Obligate active and passive reabsorption | Highly permeable, lined by brush border cuboidal epithelium | Reabsorbs 70–80% of electrolytes and water; 100% of glucose and amino acids |
| **Descending Limb of Loop of Henle** | Passive osmosis | Permeable to water, impermeable to electrolytes | Concentrates medullary filtrate progressively toward hairpin bend |
| **Ascending Limb of Loop of Henle** | Active transport of Na+, K+, 2Cl- | Impermeable to water, permeable to electrolytes | Dilutes tubular fluid; creates hyperosmotic renal medullary gradient |
| **Distal Convoluted Tubule (DCT)** | Facultative reabsorption | Governed by aldosterone (Na+ reabsorption) and parathyroid hormone (Ca2+) | Regulates final ionic balance and acid-base equilibrium |
| **Collecting Duct** | Regulated water reabsorption | Governed by Antidiuretic Hormone (ADH / Vasopressin) | Aquaporin insertion enables water conservation; hypertonic urine production |

## 2. Respiratory Mechanics and Gas Transport

Breathing operates via atmospheric and intrapulmonary pressure gradients created by the contraction and relaxation of the **diaphragm** and **external intercostal muscles**.
- **Inspiration:** Diaphragm contracts (flattens) and external intercostals pull ribs upward and outward, expanding thoracic volume and lowering intrapleural pressure below atmospheric pressure.
- **Gas Transport Chemistry:**
  - Oxygen is transported primarily as **Oxyhaemoglobin** (97%); 3% dissolves in plasma. Each haemoglobin molecule binds up to four O2 molecules cooperatively.
  - Carbon dioxide is transported in three modalities: as **Bicarbonate ions** (70%, mediated by erythrocyte carbonic anhydrase), as **Carbaminohaemoglobin** (23%), and dissolved in plasma (7%).
  - **Bohr Effect:** Increased pCO2, elevated temperature, and decreased pH (acidosis) shift the oxygen-haemoglobin dissociation curve to the **right**, facilitating oxygen delivery to metabolically active peripheral tissues.

## 3. High-Yield Diagnostic Clinical Indices

#### Diagnostic Comparison: Diabetes Mellitus vs Diabetes Insipidus
Civil service examinations test candidate ability to differentiate endocrine pathologies presenting with overlapping polyuric manifestations:
- **Diabetes Mellitus (Type 1 & Type 2):** Caused by absolute insulin deficiency (autoimmune destruction of beta cells) or peripheral insulin resistance. Hallmarks include persistent hyperglycemia, glucosuria (glucose spilling into urine when blood glucose exceeds the renal threshold of ~180 mg/dL), polyuria, polydipsia, and polyphagia.
- **Diabetes Insipidus:** Caused by impaired synthesis or release of vasopressin (ADH) from the hypothalamus/posterior pituitary (central) or renal unresponsiveness to ADH (nephrogenic). Characterized by massive excretion of dilute urine (up to 15–20 L/day) and intense thirst, but **urine contains zero glucose** and blood glucose levels remain entirely normal.

## 4. TNPSC Group 1 Previous Year Questions (PYQs)

**Question 1 (TNPSC Group 1 Prelims):** Which cranial nerve is responsible for the sensory perception of smell?  
*(A) Cranial Nerve I (Olfactory) (B) Cranial Nerve II (Optic) (C) Cranial Nerve V (Trigeminal) (D) Cranial Nerve VIII (Vestibulocochlear)*  
**Answer:** (A) Cranial Nerve I (Olfactory) (Explanation: The olfactory nerve is the first cranial nerve, transmitting sensory sensory signals from the nasal olfactory neuroepithelium to the olfactory bulb).

**Question 2 (TNPSC Group 1 Prelims):** The hormone calcitonin, which lowers blood calcium levels, is secreted by:  
*(A) Parathyroid chief cells (B) Thyroid parafollicular C-cells (C) Adrenal cortex (D) Thymus*  
**Answer:** (B) Thyroid parafollicular C-cells (Explanation: Calcitonin is a peptide hormone produced by parafollicular or C-cells of the thyroid gland. It promotes calcium deposition in bone tissue, antagonizing parathyroid hormone (PTH)).

**Question 3 (TNPSC Group 1 Prelims):** The site of erythropoiesis in the mature adult human body is:  
*(A) Spleen (B) Red bone marrow (C) Liver (D) Yellow bone marrow*  
**Answer:** (B) Red bone marrow (Explanation: In early embryonic life, erythropoiesis occurs in the yolk sac, followed by the liver and spleen in mid-gestation. In adult humans, red blood cell synthesis is localized to the red bone marrow of flat and cancellous bones).
