# Biostatistics, Demography, and Research Methodology

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your FMGE attempt.

**Biostatistics** applies statistical reasoning to health data; **demography** quantifies population change; **research methodology** structures valid clinical and epidemiological inquiry. For FMGE, three formula clusters dominate the MCQs.

- **Vital statistics (per 1000 mid-year population):** CBR = (live births / mid-year pop) × 1000; CDR = (deaths / mid-year pop) × 1000; IMR = (deaths <1 yr / live births) × 1000.
- **Statistical inference:** SD measures spread in the sample; SEM = SD/√n measures precision of the mean; **p < 0.05** rejects the null hypothesis at 95% confidence.
- **Study-design cues:** RR comes from cohort/RCT; OR comes from case-control; chi-square handles categorical data, t-test handles two means.

> **Exam tip:** FMGE loves "denominator trap" MCQs — IMR uses live births, MMR uses live births, but CDR uses mid-year population.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months of preparation.

#### Measures of Central Tendency and Dispersion

**Mean** suits symmetric data; **median** with interquartile range (IQR) suits skewed data. **Standard deviation (SD)** is the square root of the average squared deviation from the mean (using n−1 for an unbiased sample estimate). **Standard error of the mean (SEM = SD/√n)** shrinks as sample size grows, reflecting how precisely the sample mean estimates the true population mean.

#### Sampling and Hypothesis Testing

Probability sampling — **simple random, systematic, stratified, cluster, multistage** — underpins generalisability. In hypothesis testing, the **null hypothesis (H₀)** states no effect; the **alternative (H₁)** states an effect exists. Setting **α = 0.05** means a 5% chance of a Type I error (false positive). The **p-value** is the probability of observing the data — or more extreme — assuming H₀ is true; it is *not* the probability that H₀ is true.

#### Choosing a Statistical Test

| Data type | Two groups | More than two groups |
|---|---|---|
| Categorical | Chi-square (Fisher if n<5) | Chi-square / Cochran's Q |
| Continuous, normal | Student's t-test (paired/unpaired) | ANOVA |
| Continuous, skewed | Mann-Whitney / Wilcoxon | Kruskal-Wallis |

#### Demographic Indicators

| Indicator | Numerator | Denominator | Multiplier |
|---|---|---|---|
| CBR | Live births in a year | Mid-year population | × 1000 |
| GFR | Live births | Women aged 15–49 | × 1000 |
| TFR | Sum of ASFRs across 5-year age bands | — | — |
| IMR | Deaths < 1 year | Live births in same year | × 1000 |
| MMR (current WHO) | Maternal deaths | Live births | × 100 000 |

**TFR ≈ 2.1** indicates replacement-level fertility; **NRR = 1** indicates each generation exactly replaces itself. India's current IMR hovers near 28 per 1000 live births (SRS 2022), and MMR near 97 per 100 000 (SRS 2022).

#### Research Designs

Observational designs include **cross-sectional (snapshot, gives prevalence), case-control (OR, retrospective), and cohort (RR, prospective)**. The randomised controlled trial (RCT) is the experimental gold standard. **Bias** — selection, information, or confounding — distorts the observed association.

> **Common trap:** "Prevalence" describes existing disease at a point in time; "incidence" counts *new* cases developing in a population at risk over time.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Screening Test Mathematics

**Sensitivity** = TP / (TP + FN) — the test's ability to detect disease among the truly diseased. **Specificity** = TN / (TN + FP) — its ability to clear the truly healthy. **PPV** rises with rising prevalence; **NPV** rises with falling prevalence. **Likelihood ratios** combine sensitivity and specificity into a single number usable at the bedside via Bayes' theorem.

#### Bias, Confounding, and Error

**Selection bias** distorts who enters the study (Berkson, healthy-worker effects). **Information/measurement bias** distorts how variables are recorded (recall bias in case-control studies). A **confounder** is associated with both exposure and outcome but lies on neither causal pathway — adjusted for by stratification, matching, or multivariable regression. **Random error** shrinks with larger samples; **systematic error** does not.

#### Population Dynamics

The **demographic cycle** (Stage 1 high birth–high death → Stage 4 low birth–low death) frames Malthusian concerns of population explosion. **Life expectancy** at birth is derived from a period life table using age-specific mortality rates. **Net migration rate** = (immigrants − emigrants) / mid-year population × 1000.

#### Sample Size for a Proportion

For estimating a single proportion: **n = Z²α · p(1−p) / d²**, where Z = 1.96 at 95% confidence, p is the expected prevalence, and d is the absolute precision. Halving d quadruples n. For comparing two means, the formula uses pooled variance and the standardised difference.

> **Extended trap:** Using the *population* denominator to compute IMR is the single most-tested FMGE-PSM error. IMR strictly uses *live births* in the denominator because the "population at risk of dying in infancy" is the birth cohort, not the general population.

#### Worked Micro-Example

A district records **1200 live births** and **38 infant deaths** in a calendar year. IMR = (38 / 1200) × 1000 = **31.7 per 1000 live births**. If mean birth weight = 2.8 kg with SD = 0.4 kg across 100 neonates, then SEM = 0.4 / √100 = **0.04 kg** — the 95% CI for the mean birth weight is 2.8 ± 1.96 × 0.04 ≈ 2.72 to 2.88 kg.

#### Practice Prompts

1. A case-control study reports an OR of 2.4 for smoking and oral cancer with 95% CI 1.3–4.5. Interpret the CI and the statistical significance.
2. A new screening test has sensitivity 95% and specificity 80%. In a population with 1% prevalence, will PPV exceed 5%? Compute and explain.
