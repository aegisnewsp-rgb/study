# Pakistan's Culture, Heritage and Society

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your NLT Pakistan test.

Pakistan's cultural identity rests on five pillars: **provincial diversity**, **archaeological heritage**, **Sufi traditions**, **national symbols**, and the **1973 Constitution**. The country has **6 UNESCO World Heritage Sites** and roughly **240 million people**, making it the world's 5th most populous nation. Muslims form ~96% of the population; Urdu is the national language while Punjabi is the most spoken mother tongue.

- **Six UNESCO Sites (memorise order):** Taxila, Mohenjo-daro, Lahore Fort, Makli Hill, Rohtas Fort, Takht-i-Bahi.
- **Two landmark dates:** Lahore Resolution — **23 March 1940**; Independence — **14 August 1947**.
- **Four provinces + two regions + ICT:** Punjab, Sindh, KP, Balochistan; AJK, Gilgit-Baltistan; Islamabad Capital Territory.
- **National motto:** "Iman, Ittihad, Nazm" (Faith, Unity, Discipline).

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** "**T-M-L-M-R-T**" = **T**axila, **M**ohenjo-daro, **L**ahore Fort, **M**akli, **R**ohtas, **T**akht-i-Bahi — the six UNESCO sites in alphabetical order. Pair with "**23-3-40 → 14-8-47**" for the two founding dates.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Heritage Foundations

Pakistan inherits three distinct civilisational layers: the **Indus Valley Civilization** (~2500 BCE, evidenced at Mohenjo-daro in Sindh), the **Gandhara Buddhist period** (1st–7th century CE, centred at Taxila in Punjab), and the **Mughal era** (16th–17th century, represented by Lahore Fort, Shalimar Gardens, and Badshahi Mosque). Each layer contributed motifs, architecture, and script traditions still visible in modern Pakistani design.

The Lahore Resolution of 23 March 1940, passed at Minto Park under Allama Iqbal's intellectual guidance, formalised the Two-Nation Theory articulated by Quaid-e-Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah. Partition followed on 14 August 1947, after which the Constitution of Pakistan 1973 codified Islam as the state religion, Urdu and English as official languages, and Articles 8–28 as fundamental rights.

| Heritage Layer | Period | Key Site | Region | Patron/Culture |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Indus Valley | ~2500 BCE | Mohenjo-daro | Sindh | Pre-Aryan, planned city |
| Gandhara Buddhist | 1st–7th c. CE | Taxila | Punjab | Greco-Buddhist art |
| Mughal | 16th–17th c. | Lahore Fort | Punjab | Akbar → Aurangzeb |
| Suri Afghan | 1541 CE | Rohtas Fort | Punjab | Sher Shah Suri |
| Sindhi Islamic | 14th–18th c. | Makli Hill | Sindh | Samma, Arghun dynasties |

#### Cultural Markers by Region

Provincial identity is read through dress, language, and festivals. The **ajrak** (block-printed shawl) and **Jora cap** belong to Sindh; the **Peshawari topi** marks Pashtun identity; **shalwar kameez** is pan-Pakistani; **bhangra** originates in Punjab. **Basant**, the kite festival, was historically a Punjabi spring celebration before regulatory bans. Sufi **urs** festivals at **Data Darbar** (Lahore) and **Sehwan Sharif** (Lal Shahbaz Qalandar) draw millions of devotees and embody the syncretic folk Islam that defines rural Pakistan.

#### National Symbols

- **Flag:** Green field with white crescent and five-pointed star; adopted 11 August 1947.
- **Anthem:** "Qaumi Taranah," lyrics by **Hafeez Jalandhari**, music composed by **A. R. Ghani**.
- **State Emblem:** Four crescents, one star, floral wreath, motto "Iman, Ittihad, Nazm" in Urdu.
- **Mausoleum:** Mazar-e-Quaid in Karachi houses Jinnah's tomb.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** Which of the following is **not** inscribed on UNESCO's World Heritage List for Pakistan?
(a) Taxila
(b) Mohenjo-daro
(c) Rohtas Fort
(d) Badshahi Mosque

#### Solution:
Step 1 — Recall the six inscribed sites: Taxila (1980), Mohenjo-daro (1980), Lahore Fort (1981), Makli Hill (1981), Rohtas Fort (1997), Takht-i-Bahi (1980).
Step 2 — Cross-check each option: Taxila ✓, Mohenjo-daro ✓, Rohtas Fort ✓, Badshahi Mosque ✗ (it is on Pakistan's tentative list, not inscribed).
Step 3 — Therefore, **the correct answer is (d) Badshahi Mosque**.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Candidates confuse "tentative list" with "inscribed list." Badshahi Mosque and Ranigat are sometimes tested as red herrings — only six are formally inscribed, and Ranigat is grouped under the Takht-i-Bahi extension, not listed separately.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Demographic and Linguistic Nuance

Pakistan's 240 million population breaks down across four major ethno-linguistic groups: **Punjabi** (~38%), **Pashto** (~18%), **Sindhi** (~14%), and **Balochi** (~3.6%), with **Urdu** speakers at roughly 7.5% as mother tongue but used as lingua franca by an estimated 75% of urban households. Saraiki, Hindko, and Brahui hold regional official status. Religious minorities — Hindus (~1.7%, concentrated in Sindh), Christians, Sikhs, Parsis, Ahmadis, Buddhists, and the Kalash of Chitral — retain constitutional protection under Articles 20–28 despite sectarian policy tensions.

#### Legal Architecture for Heritage

Two statutes govern cultural preservation:

1. **Antiquities Act 1975** — covers archaeological excavation, declaration, and protection of monuments older than 75 years.
2. **Cultural Heritage Act 1995** — broader scope covering movable and intangible heritage, museums, and the National Heritage Council.

| Law | Year | Scope | Key Provision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Antiquities Act | 1975 | Tangible antiquities | Declares Mohenjo-daro, Taxila as protected |
| Cultural Heritage Act | 1995 | Heritage broadly | Establishes NHC, inventory duties |

#### Advanced Traps and Edge Cases

1. **Date confusion:** 23 March is observed as **Pakistan Day** (the 1940 Resolution) — not Independence Day. 14 August is the actual independence date; 27 Ramadan (11 August 1947) marks Jinnah's Constituent Assembly address.
2. **Site-period mismatch:** Mohenjo-daro is *not* Gandhara; Taxila is *not* Mughal. Examiners pair wrong period names to wrong sites.
3. **Language status:** Urdu is the **national** language; English remains the **official** language alongside it — students often swap these labels.
4. **Dress attribution:** Ajrak is Sindhi, topi is Pashtun, chador is Baloch, phiran is Kashmiri — avoid attributing all to Punjab.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. List the six UNESCO World Heritage Sites in chronological order of inscription year and identify which dynasty or culture patronised each. (Expected order: Taxila 1980 → Mohenjo-daro 1980 → Takht-i-Bahi 1980 → Lahore Fort 1981 → Makli Hill 1981 → Rohtas Fort 1997.)
2. Differentiate the legal scope of the Antiquities Act 1975 from the Cultural Heritage Act 1995, and explain which statute would apply to (a) excavating a Buddhist stupa, (b) registering a Sufi music tradition, and (c) declaring a Mughal mosque protected.
