# H. Money and Banking — HKDSE Economics Notes

This topic covers Topic H of the Compulsory Part of HKDSE Economics. It addresses the functions of money; the banking system (commercial banks, fractional reserve banking, the money multiplier); the central bank (Hong Kong's three-issuing-bank system, the role of the Hong Kong Monetary Authority); and monetary policy tools and transmission mechanisms.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Functions of money**:
  - **Medium of exchange**: money is accepted in payment for goods and services.
  - **Unit of account**: money provides a common measure of value.
  - **Store of value**: money can be saved and used in the future.
  - **Standard of deferred payment**: money is used for loans and future payments.
- **Types of money**:
  - **Commodity money**: has intrinsic value (e.g. gold, silver).
  - **Fiat money**: has no intrinsic value but is declared legal tender by the government.
  - **Bank deposits**: most money today is bank deposits (digital).
- **The banking system**: accept deposits, make loans, provide payment services, create money through lending.
- **Fractional reserve banking**: banks hold only a fraction of deposits as reserves; the rest is loaned out. This creates money.
- **Money multiplier**: 1 / reserve ratio. A $1 deposit creates $1/(reserve ratio) of total deposits.
- **Central bank**: the bank that issues the currency, regulates banks, and conducts monetary policy. In Hong Kong, the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) oversees monetary policy and banking regulation, but currency notes are issued by three commercial banks: HSBC, Standard Chartered, and Bank of China (Hong Kong).
- **Monetary policy tools**:
  - **Interest rate policy**: setting the policy interest rate.
  - **Open market operations**: buying or selling government securities.
  - **Reserve requirements**: specifying the minimum reserves banks must hold (Hong Kong does not have formal reserve requirements).
  - **Discount window**: lending to banks at the discount rate.
- **Transmission mechanism**: monetary policy → interest rates → investment and consumption → aggregate demand → output and inflation.
- **Hong Kong's Linked Exchange Rate system**: HKD pegged to USD at HK$7.80 = USD 1. The HKMA operates the link through a Convertibility Undertaking.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing M1, M2, M3 — different monetary aggregates include different components.
- Treating fractional reserve banking as "creating money from nothing" — banks create deposits, but this is regulated and constrained by reserves and capital requirements.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Functions of money

Money has four functions:

1. **Medium of exchange**: money is widely accepted in exchange for goods and services. Eliminates the need for barter (double coincidence of wants).
2. **Unit of account**: money provides a common measure of value. Prices are quoted in money terms.
3. **Store of value**: money can be saved and used in the future. (Inflation reduces the purchasing power over time.)
4. **Standard of deferred payment**: money is used for loans and future payments.

The three properties of a good money: durability, portability, divisibility.

#### The banking system

**Commercial banks**: accept deposits, make loans, provide payment services. Banks are profitable because they pay lower interest on deposits than they charge on loans.

**Fractional reserve banking**: banks hold only a fraction of deposits as reserves (cash or deposits at the central bank). The rest is loaned out. A $1 deposit at a bank with 10% reserve ratio creates up to $10 of total deposits in the banking system.

**Money multiplier**: 1 / r, where r is the reserve ratio. (More precisely, includes the currency drain ratio.)

Example: reserve ratio 10% → money multiplier 10. $1 initial deposit → $10 total deposits in the banking system.

#### Central banks and monetary policy

**Hong Kong's central banking arrangement**:

- **HKMA**: the Hong Kong Monetary Authority oversees monetary policy and banking regulation. It is not a traditional central bank (it does not issue currency).
- **Three issuing banks**: HSBC, Standard Chartered, and Bank of China (Hong Kong) issue Hong Kong banknotes under a note-issuing arrangement.
- **Linked Exchange Rate system**: the HKD is pegged to the USD at HK$7.80 = USD 1. The HKMA operates the link through Convertibility Undertakings: the HKMA undertakes to buy USD from licensed banks at HK$7.75 (strong-side CU) and sell USD at HK$7.85 (weak-side CU).

**Monetary policy tools**:

- **Interest rate policy**: setting the policy interest rate (base rate in Hong Kong). Hong Kong has no independent interest rate policy because of the Linked Exchange Rate; interest rates follow US rates.
- **Open market operations**: buying/selling government securities.
- **Reserve requirements**: banks must hold a minimum fraction of deposits as reserves. (Not used in Hong Kong.)
- **Discount window**: lending to banks at the discount rate (in Hong Kong, the discount window is effectively the weak-side CU).

#### Monetary policy in Hong Kong: the Linked Exchange Rate

The Linked Exchange Rate system has been in place since 1983. Key features:

- HKD is pegged to USD at HK$7.80 = USD 1.
- The HKMA's Convertibility Undertakings: weak-side CU at HK$7.85 (sell USD), strong-side CU at HK$7.75 (buy USD). The intraday range is HK$7.75-HK$7.85.
- Interest rates in Hong Kong follow US interest rates closely because of the peg. This limits the HKMA's ability to set independent monetary policy.
- The peg has been tested by various crises (1997 Asian financial crisis, 2019 social unrest) but has held.

#### Transmission mechanism

Monetary policy works through several channels:

1. **Interest rate channel**: monetary policy changes the policy rate → market interest rates change → investment and interest-sensitive consumption change → AD shifts.
2. **Credit channel**: monetary policy affects the supply of credit → bank lending changes → investment and consumption change.
3. **Exchange rate channel**: monetary policy affects the exchange rate → net exports change → AD shifts (less relevant under the Linked Exchange Rate in Hong Kong).
4. **Asset price channel**: monetary policy affects asset prices (stocks, bonds, real estate) → wealth and investment change.

#### Exam technique

- **For money multiplier questions**: identify the reserve ratio; apply the formula.
- **For monetary policy questions**: identify the channel; trace the transmission mechanism.
- **For Hong Kong Linked Exchange Rate questions**: identify the CUs; explain the constraint on independent monetary policy.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Banks create money from nothing." Banks create deposits through lending, but the amount is constrained by reserves, capital requirements, and demand for loans.
- "The Hong Kong Monetary Authority is the central bank." The HKMA is the de facto central bank, but the note-issuing function is split among three commercial banks.
- "The Hong Kong dollar is freely floating." The HKD is pegged to the USD under the Linked Exchange Rate system.
