# Sentence Improvement

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your GATE Verbal Ability section.

Sentence Improvement items give you a sentence with an underlined or boxed segment and four replacement options. Your job is to pick the option that makes the sentence grammatically correct, idiomatic, and logically consistent. In GATE, this appears in the Verbal Ability section (about 2% weight), usually as 2–3 single-correct MCQs out of the 10 VA questions.

- **Spot the error first.** Decide whether the fault is grammatical (agreement, tense, voice), idiomatic (wrong preposition, wrong collocation), logical (broken parallelism, comparison), or stylistic (wordiness, redundancy).
- **Apply the no-change rule.** If the original is already correct, the answer is the option that reproduces it verbatim; many distractors introduce fresh errors.
- **Read the full sentence with the option plugged in** before locking your answer; do not judge the option in isolation.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### What the Examiner Tests
The question type checks your command of standard English usage rather than obscure rules. Each item contains one identifiable error in the highlighted span, and four options offer alternate phrasing. Only one option resolves the error without creating a new one. The remaining three are crafted to look plausible: they often fix the surface-level grammar but break idiom, parallelism, or modifier placement.

#### Error Categories You Must Recognise

| Category | Typical Cue | Example Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Subject–Verb Agreement | Subject separated from verb by a phrase | "The list of items **is** ready" (not "are") |
| Tense Consistency | Time adverbial conflicts with verb form | "She has lived here since 2019" (not "lived") |
| Parallelism | Coordinated list or comparison pair | "He likes reading, writing, **and to edit**" → "**and editing**" |
| Modifier Placement | Adverb/phrase far from the word it modifies | "Running fast, the bus **was missed** by him" |
| Idiomatic Preposition | Wrong collocation after verb/adjective | "interested **in**", "good **at**", "prefer A **to** B" |

#### The Substitution Check
Before finalising an answer, mentally splice the option back into the original sentence. Then read aloud and verify three things: grammar still holds, meaning is preserved, and idiom is natural. This single habit eliminates most of the wrong-answer traps.

> **Tip:** The longest-sounding option is rarely correct. Examiners pad distractor options with formal-sounding but grammatically broken phrases.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases That Appear in Higher-Difficulty Sets
- **No-change options:** Several items have no grammatical error at all; option (A) repeats the original. Selecting any other option introduces a new fault.
- **Voice shifts:** Switching active to passive can break tense or alter agent clarity. Verify that the doer remains explicit when the action matters.
- **Article traps:** "a" vs "an" depends on the *sound* that follows, not the letter ("an honest man", "a university"). Countable vs uncountable nouns decide "a/an" vs "the" vs zero article.
- **Comparison pairs:** "not only … but also" and "either … or" require parallel structures on both sides; breaking symmetry is a frequent distractor move.

#### Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Picking an option that reads fluently alone but breaks parallelism when inserted.
2. Over-correcting a sentence that was already correct, losing a mark you would have gained.
3. Ignoring agreement when a long phrase separates subject from verb.
4. Using "discuss about", "since long", or "comprise of" — these are non-standard.
5. Switching tense mid-sentence without a time-shift marker such as "by then" or "previously".

#### Micro-Worked Example
Original: *The committee have submitted their report yesterday.*
Option (A) No change — (B) *has submitted its report yesterday* — (C) *submitted their report yesterday* — (D) *have submitted its report yesterday*.

**Diagnosis:** "Committee" is a collective noun treated as singular → "has", "its". The adverb "yesterday" demands simple past → "submitted". Therefore (C) is correct: subject–verb agreement fixed and tense aligned with the time marker.

#### GATE-Specific Strategy
Sentence Improvement carries roughly 2% weight across most streams. Expect 2–3 such items mixed with sentence completion, odd-one-out, and analogy. Budget about 45–60 seconds per question. Skip-and-return works well: if no error is visible within 30 seconds, the no-change option is often the answer.

#### Practice Prompts
1. Take any 10-item set and categorise each error before reading the options; this trains the spot-first reflex.
2. After solving, rewrite each corrected sentence in the opposite voice to confirm you understand the tense and agent structure.
