# Geometry and Trigonometry — Junior Cycle Mathematics Notes

The Geometry and Trigonometry strand spans constructions, transformations (reflections, rotations, translations, enlargements), Pythagoras' theorem, congruence and similarity, areas and volumes of 2D and 3D shapes, circle theorems (angle at centre, angle in semicircle, angles in the same segment, cyclic quadrilateral, tangent-radius), coordinate geometry of the line, and right-angled trigonometry with sine, cosine and tangent. At Higher level, it extends to sine and cosine rules for non-right-angled triangles. It is one of the highest-weights strands in the topic catalogue, and the mark scheme rewards correct diagrams with full marks, while penalising mislabelled ones disproportionately. Candidates who under-prepare this strand tend to lose marks in clusters - one error in a labelled diagram cascades through the rest of the working.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Weight & Frequency**: Rated 4/5 - appears on every paper with a sustained run of geometry questions in the second half. Confirm the live mark allocation on the latest SEC specimen paper.
- **Timing Goal**: Pythagoras and right-angled trig should average 60 to 90 seconds per question; circle theorems and similarity proofs should sit in the 2 to 4 minute band.
- **Core Principle**: Master five diagram habits: (1) draw the shape accurately even if the question does not say "show diagram", (2) label every length and angle mentioned, (3) mark right angles with a small square, (4) identify the hypotenuse on trig questions before choosing sin/cos/tan, (5) state the theorem being used.
- **Pythagoras**: *a^2 + b^2 = c^2* where *c* is the hypotenuse (longest side, opposite the right angle). To find a shorter side, rearrange: *a = √(c^2 - b^2)*.
- **Trig ratios (right-angled)**: sin θ = opposite / hypotenuse, cos θ = adjacent / hypotenuse, tan θ = opposite / adjacent. Use SOH-CAH-TOA to choose. The angle, not the side, dictates the ratio.
- **Circle theorems**: The angle at the centre is twice the angle in the same segment at the circumference. The angle in a semicircle is 90°. The tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact.
- **Area & volume**: Area of triangle = ½ × base × height. Volume of prism = area of cross-section × length. Surface area = sum of all face areas.
- **Answer Verification**: For trig, check whether the answer is plausible (sin of 30° must be 0.5; tan of 45° must be 1). For Pythagoras, check that the hypotenuse is longer than either leg.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing opposite and adjacent sides on a trig question - the labelling, not the position on the page, dictates which is which.
- Forgetting to convert an angle from degrees to radians or vice versa when the calculator is in the wrong mode.
- Treating "perimeter" and "area" as interchangeable on word problems - they have different units (cm vs cm²).
- Solving a geometry problem without stating the theorem used - the method mark is forfeit on proof questions.
- Drawing a transformation on the wrong side of the line of reflection or in the wrong direction for a rotation - the answer is full marks or zero, no partial.
- Leaving π in the answer when the question asks for a decimal - and vice versa, writing 3.14 when the question expects an exact answer with π.

#### 20-minute triage checklist

- [ ] Can you draw and label any right-angled triangle given two sides or one side and one angle, before touching the calculator?
- [ ] Do you know the five core circle theorems and how to spot which one applies to a given diagram?
- [ ] Can you compute the area of a triangle, trapezium, and circle without mixing up the formulas?
- [ ] Have you practiced reflection, rotation and enlargement transformations on coordinate grids?

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Conceptual framework and solving methodology

Geometry and Trigonometry is the strand where the diagram IS the working. Apply this scaffold to every question:

1. **Draw first, then read the question a second time**:
   Even if the question supplies a diagram, redraw it larger with all lengths and angles labelled. The act of redrawing surfaces which information is given, which is to be found, and which angles are equal by construction. A correctly drawn diagram is half the marks on most extended-response geometry items.

2. **Identify the relevant theorem or formula**:
   State the theorem or rule you intend to use in a single short sentence: "Using Pythagoras' theorem", "By the angle at the centre is twice the angle in the same segment", "Since triangles ABC and DEF are similar (AAA)". This sentence is a method mark.

3. **Substitute and solve, with units**:
   For trig, identify the angle (the given or to-be-found quantity), identify the sides (opposite, adjacent, hypotenuse), then choose the ratio that matches. For Pythagoras, identify the hypotenuse first; if the unknown is a leg, the hypotenuse appears on the right of the rearranged equation.

4. **Verify by dimensional check and reasonableness**:
   The longest side of a triangle must be opposite the largest angle. The hypotenuse must be longer than either leg. An angle in a right-angled triangle must be less than 90°. A reflection produces a congruent shape, not a similar one. A 90° rotation maps a point at (x, y) to (-y, x) (about the origin).

#### Detailed worked example

**Problem Context**:
A combined Pythagoras-and-trig question typical of the Higher level paper.

**Item Prompt**:
A ladder of length 4.2 m leans against a vertical wall. The foot of the ladder is 1.4 m from the base of the wall. **(a)** Find the height up the wall at which the ladder touches, correct to two decimal places. **(b)** Find the angle the ladder makes with the ground, correct to the nearest degree. **(c)** If the ladder slips so that the foot moves a further 0.5 m away from the wall, by how much does the height drop?

**Step-by-Step Solution**:
- **Step 1 (Decode)**: (a) is Pythagoras - hypotenuse and one leg given, find the other leg. (b) is right-angled trig - have the ladder length (hypotenuse) and the base distance (adjacent to the angle with the ground), find the angle. (c) is a second Pythagoras calculation then a subtraction.
- **Step 2 (Represent)**: Sketch the wall as a vertical line, the ground as a horizontal line, the ladder as the hypotenuse. Mark the right angle at the base of the wall. Label the ladder 4.2 m, the foot distance 1.4 m, the unknown height *h*. For (b), the angle at the foot of the ladder (between ladder and ground) is the angle we want.
- **Step 3 (Execute)**:
  - (a) *h = √(4.2² - 1.4²) = √(17.64 - 1.96) = √15.68 ≈ 3.96 m*.
  - (b) cos θ = adjacent/hypotenuse = 1.4/4.2 = 1/3. θ = cos⁻¹(1/3) ≈ 70.53°, so 71° to the nearest degree. (Check: tan θ = 3.96/1.4 ≈ 2.83, θ = tan⁻¹(2.83) ≈ 70.5°. Match.)
  - (c) New base distance = 1.4 + 0.5 = 1.9 m. New height = √(4.2² - 1.9²) = √(17.64 - 3.61) = √14.03 ≈ 3.746 m. Drop = 3.96 - 3.746 ≈ 0.21 m, or about 21 cm.
- **Step 4 (Verify)**: (a) The hypotenuse (4.2) is longer than the legs (1.4 and 3.96), consistent. (b) The angle with the ground is acute, around 70°, consistent with the steep lean of a 4.2 m ladder against a wall at only 1.4 m out. (c) The drop should be small for a 0.5 m slip; 21 cm is plausible.
- **Conclusion**: Three marks from one question - one Pythagoras, one trig, and one Pythagoras-comparison. State the unit (m or cm) explicitly throughout.

### 🔴 Deep Dive — Mastery & Edge Cases (3mo–2yr)

#### Advanced variations and high-difficulty edge cases

At Higher level, Geometry and Trigonometry extends to non-right-angled triangles. Practice the four highest-leverage patterns:

- **Sine rule for unknown angles**: *a / sin A = b / sin B = c / sin C*. Use the sine rule when given two angles and one side, or two sides and a non-included angle. The trap is the ambiguous case (SSA), which can produce zero, one, or two triangles - check the height of the unknown side against the given side.
- **Cosine rule for unknown sides**: *c² = a² + b² - 2ab cos C*. Use when given three sides, or two sides and the included angle. The trap is sign error on the cos term.
- **Area of a triangle using two sides and the included angle**: Area = ½ × *a* × *b* × sin C. Useful for bearings questions and for any triangle drawn from a surveyor's measurement.
- **3D geometry extensions**: A diagonal across a rectangular box of dimensions *a*, *b*, *c* has length √(*a² + b² + c²*). The angle between a diagonal and a face uses the cosine rule on the diagonal and the diagonal of the face.

#### Systematic drill schedule

A four-week Geometry and Trigonometry pass before mocks:

- **Week 1**: Pythagoras and right-angled trig - 12 short questions per day, mixing given-hypotenuse and given-leg variants. Force the labelling step before any calculation.
- **Week 2**: Circle theorems - identify the theorem in 10 diagrams per day, then prove the angle on a fresh diagram. State the theorem name in the proof line.
- **Week 3**: Sine and cosine rules - 8 questions per day, varying between unknown-angle and unknown-side. Track which rule is required by the given information.
- **Week 4**: Area, volume, and 3D problems - 6 extended-response items per sitting, with explicit unit labelling and a one-sentence sanity check at the end.
