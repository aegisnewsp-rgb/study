# Direction

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

- **Direction sense** tracks the final **facing** and **position** of a person after a sequence of moves + turns.
- The **8 principal bearings** are N, NE, E, SE, S, SW, W, NW; the full circle has **360°**, so each step on a clock face equals **30°**.
- **Right turn = +90° (clockwise)**, **Left turn = −90° (anticlockwise)**, **U-turn = 180°**.
- Final bearing formula: **θ_final = (θ_start + Σ turns) mod 360°**.
- Displacement: **North component = Σ d·cos θ**, **East component = Σ d·sin θ**.
- Common trap: applying turns from a *fixed* north reference rather than the *cumulative* heading after each prior turn.

---

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core framework

Every direction-sense problem has two independent variables: **heading** (which way you face) and **position** (where you stand). Each "move 5 km North" instruction updates position; each "turn right" instruction updates heading. The two must be tracked **cumulatively** — never reset.

#### The 8-point compass and clock mapping

The clock dial is the fastest mental model. **12 o'clock = North, 3 = East, 6 = South, 9 = West.** Each hour mark is 30° apart, so a 3-hour shift on the dial equals a 90° turn.

| Clock position | Compass | Bearing |
|---|---|---|
| 12 | N | 0° |
| 3 | E | 90° |
| 6 | S | 180° |
| 9 | W | 270° |
| 1–2 | NE | 30°, 60° |
| 4–5 | SE | 120°, 150° |
| 7–8 | SW | 210°, 240° |
| 10–11 | NW | 300°, 330° |

#### Worked relationship

If Ravi starts facing NE, turns right, walks 4 km, turns left, walks 3 km — compute heading first:
- Start: 45° (NE). Right turn → 45° + 90° = 135° (SE). Left turn → 135° − 90° = 45° (NE again).
- Displacement: 4 km at 135° adds (4·cos135°, 4·sin135°) = (−2.83, +2.83); then 3 km at 45° adds (+2.12, +2.12). Net = (−0.71, +4.95), so Ravi finishes roughly **NNW** of the start.

#### Typical CAT question patterns

1. **Standalone MCQ** (1 mark): "After walking X, turning Y, what is the final direction from start?"
2. **Shadow/sun puzzles**: a person's shadow at 9 AM points West (sun is East) → deduce starting direction.
3. **Set-based sub-question** inside a DILR game involving circular seating + movement.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge cases and shadow problems

A **shadow always points opposite to the sun**. The sun moves from East (6 AM) → South (12 noon in India) → West (6 PM), so a shadow at 3 PM points **East**. Multi-person shadow problems (e.g., "A's 9 AM shadow falls on B, B's 12 noon shadow falls on C") require reconstructing each person's line of stance at two different sun angles — a classic CAT trap worth 1–2 marks.

#### Cumulative-heading vs. origin-reset error

The single highest-frequency mistake: after a turn, students re-evaluate "left/right from North" instead of "left/right from current facing." Example — facing East, "turn left" gives **North**, not West. After two consecutive turns you may even face the opposite of what intuition suggests. Always draw an arrow and rotate it.

#### Displacement-as-vector mechanics

For long paths, resolve each segment into (ΔN, ΔE) components using **d·cos θ** and **d·sin θ** where θ is measured clockwise from North. Sum components, then compute **final bearing = atan2(ΣΔE, ΣΔN)** in degrees, mapped to 0°–360°. This decouples heading from position and handles 20-step paths cleanly.

#### Connections to adjacent DILR topics

Direction problems overlap with **circular arrangements** (clockwise = right turn convention), **blood relations** (left/right of whom?), and **coding-decoding** (NESW letter shifts). Mastering cumulative-heading here pays off in seating-arrangement sets worth 6–8 marks.

#### Common mistakes catalogue

- Mixing **shadow direction** with **sun direction** → 180° error.
- Treating a "U-turn" as 90° because the person "looks back" → always **180°**.
- Forgetting that **"turn right, then turn right"** from facing N lands you facing S, not E.

#### Practice prompts

1. Priya walks 6 km South, turns right, walks 8 km, turns left, walks 5 km. What direction and approximate distance from start?
2. At 4:30 PM, Ravi's shadow points exactly NW. In which direction was he facing?
