# Legal News

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Legal News in the CLAT Current Affairs section tests your awareness of Indian judicial, legislative, and constitutional developments reported in the **12-month window** before the exam. Roughly 5% of CLAT marks, drawn from the 50-question Current Affairs & GK block (about 25% of the paper).

- The three **new criminal codes — BNS, BNSS, BSA** — replaced the IPC, CrPC, and Indian Evidence Act from **1 July 2024**. Every "old IPC section" question now needs the BNS equivalent.
- Supreme Court and High Court **landmark judgments** from the reference year form the bulk of factual MCQs.
- Read **The Hindu** or **Indian Express** plus one law outlet such as **LiveLaw** or **Bar & Bench** for coverage.

| Source | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Supreme Court of India (sci.gov.in) | Authoritative text of every reported judgment |
| The Hindu / Indian Express | Daily legislative and executive news |
| LiveLaw / Bar & Bench | Verdict summaries with bench and citation details |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### What CLAT tests under Legal News
Legal News questions are drawn from the **Current Affairs & General Knowledge** section of CLAT, with an estimated **5% weightage** out of the 50 questions in that block. The Consortium of NLUs specifies a **12-month reference window** — anything older than that is outside the syllabus.

Items are tested as: (a) factual MCQs (e.g., "Which Article was invoked in case X?"), (b) passage-based comprehension of a news report, and (c) principle-application sets where you must apply a recent ruling to a fresh fact pattern.

#### Core categories to track

- **Supreme Court and High Court judgments** — focus on constitutional, criminal, environmental, and family law benches.
- **Parliamentary bills and presidential assent** — track timeline from introduction to Gazette notification.
- **Ordinances under Article 123** — note when promulgated, when lapsed, and the subject matter.
- **Constitutional amendments and Article 370 abrogation follow-ups**.
- **PILs admitted in public-interest matters** — distinguish admission orders from final rulings.
- **Notifications** from the Ministry of Law and Justice, ECI, and NALSA.

#### Distinguishing the binding part of a judgment
Knowing the difference between **ratio decidendi** (the binding legal principle) and **obiter dicta** (persuasive observations) is essential. CLAT often asks which statement reflects the "law declared" — only the ratio qualifies.

| Term | Meaning | CLAT relevance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ratio Decidendi | Binding principle on which the decision rests | Often the answer to "law declared" questions |
| Obiter Dicta | Judge's incidental remark, not binding | A common distractor in MCQs |
| PIL | Public Interest Litigation under Article 32/226 | Track admission vs. disposal |
| Ordinance | Executive legislation under Article 123 | Lapses in 6 weeks unless re-passed |

- **Daily reading habit**: minimum one national daily and one law outlet.
- **Note the metadata**: bench strength, judge name, citation, date of judgment.
- **Map each item** to a subject head (criminal, constitutional, commercial, environmental, family, labour, tax, cyber) for faster retrieval on revision day.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### The 12-month window — how to operationalise it
CLAT 2026 draws legal news from roughly **December 2024 to November 2025** (or the equivalent window the Consortium announces). Build a rolling monthly log: for each verdict, capture case name, court, bench, date, issue, holding, and the **statutory provision interpreted**. Do not copy the political slug ("SC slams Centre"); record the legal issue and relief granted.

#### Post-July 2024: mapping old sections to the new codes
Since **1 July 2024**, the **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS)**, **Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS)**, and **Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA)** replaced the IPC, CrPC, and Indian Evidence Act. Any question framed as "Section 302 IPC" in a pre-2024 case now has a BNS counterpart under section 302 (murder), 63 BNS (rape — formerly Section 376 IPC), 318 BNS (cheating — formerly Section 420 IPC). Carry this mapping in your notes.

#### Common traps examiners set

- Quoting repealed IPC sections as still in force.
- Confusing a **High Court division bench** ruling with a Supreme Court judgment.
- Treating a **Law Commission Report** or Standing Committee recommendation as enacted law.
- Inflating the importance of **interim orders** versus final rulings.
- Citing judgments outside the 12-month reference window.

#### Worked micro-example
Suppose CLAT asks: *"In the 2025 ruling concerning sub-classification within Scheduled Castes, the Supreme Court invoked which Article to read down the State OBC list?"* The answer is **Article 14 / 15 / 21** jurisprudence, but the student who only read headlines will miss the **bench strength (5-Judge Constitution Bench)** and the **neutral citation (e.g., 2025 INSC 234)**. Capture these in your notes.

- **Practice prompt 1**: Summarise one 2025 Supreme Court judgment in 100 words — issue, holding, ratio, citation.
- **Practice prompt 2**: Take any three news items from this week and rewrite each as a CLAT-style MCQ with one correct answer and three plausible distractors.

#### Exam strategy
Legal News items are dispersed across the 50 Current Affairs questions, so budget about **6–8 minutes** for the entire block. Mark and move; do not over-reason any single item. Reading **LiveLaw** daily is the single highest-yield habit, followed by **The Hindu's Law page** on Sundays.
