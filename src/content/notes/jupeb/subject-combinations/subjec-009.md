# A JUPEB Candidate Registers for Exactly Three Subjects

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Subject Combination** in JUPEB is the grouping of three approved subjects into a set that satisfies a target Nigerian university's direct-entry admission requirement for a specific faculty. Each combination carries an **SC code** (e.g., SC-008, SC-029, SC-030) and is faculty-driven, not student-driven. The subject that matches the proposed course is treated as the **principal subject**, while the other two serve as **subsidiaries**.

- ABE = Accounts, Business Studies, Economics
- GEM = Government, Economics, Mathematics
- MPC = Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry (the classic Science trio)
- BCP = Biology, Chemistry, Physics (medicine-bound route)
- Five O'Level credits (WAEC/NECO/NABTEB) are mandatory alongside JUPEB results for direct entry.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### How combinations are built

A JUPEB candidate registers for exactly three subjects at an affiliated study centre. Universities publish **relevant-subject lists** per faculty: Economics Education, for instance, accepts Government/Economics/Mathematics or History/Economics/Mathematics. The three subjects must overlap with that faculty's list; otherwise the candidate is screened out at direct-entry stage.

#### Principal vs Subsidiary subjects

The subject that bears the closest name-match to the intended course (e.g., Economics for Economics Education) is the **principal subject** whose grade largely determines offer chances. The other two are **subsidiaries** that pad the score aggregation. Choosing two narrow subjects without a quantitative third (such as pairing Economics with Business Studies alone) shrinks eligible degree options.

#### Common combinations at a glance

| Combination | Subjects | Typical Faculty Route |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MPC | Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry | Engineering, Computer Science, Physics |
| BCP / MCB | Biology, Chemistry, Physics | Medicine, Pharmacy, Nursing, Biology |
| ABE | Accounts, Business Studies, Economics | Accounting, Business Admin, Banking & Finance |
| GEM | Government, Economics, Mathematics | Economics, Public Admin, Sociology |
| HEM | History, Economics, Mathematics | History, Economics, Humanities |

#### Registration mechanics

Each registered set is tagged with an SC code recorded on the JUPEB result slip. Candidates must verify the code matches their intended faculty before sitting the exam, because changing a combination after the host university's deadline usually requires a fresh application.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Faculty-based grouping logic

Every Nigerian university that runs JUPEB publishes a relevant-subjects table on its foundation programme portal. Universities such as UNILAG and UNN list SC-001 to SC-030+ groupings tied to faculties like Engineering, Law, Education, and Environmental Sciences. A candidate studying **Law**, for example, typically registers Government/Literature/CRS or Government/Literature/History — Literature-in-English is rarely a standalone principal subject for non-Arts faculties, so omitting it can close off Law, English, and Theatre Arts simultaneously.

#### Edge cases and traps

1. **Trade subjects rule:** subjects like Data Processing, Financial Accounting, or Civic Education are accepted by some faculties but rejected by others; always cross-check the SC code.
2. **O'Level equivalency window:** the five credits must include English Language and Mathematics (plus three relevant subjects) obtained in not more than two sittings.
3. **Post-registration change deadline:** most study centres close combination edits within the first four to six weeks of lectures.

#### High-yield mistakes examiners exploit

| Mistake | Consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Picking subjects by interest, not faculty list | Disqualified at direct entry |
| Two narrow subjects, no Mathematics | Fewer degree options |
| Ignoring the principal subject's grade | Out-ranked by higher-scoring candidates |
| Wrong SC code on registration form | Mis-routed result processing |

#### Practice prompts

2. A candidate registered ABE (SC-008) but later wishes to switch to Banking and Finance. State one condition that must be satisfied and one O'Level credit that becomes mandatory.
