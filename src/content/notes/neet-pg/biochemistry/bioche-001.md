# Biomolecules and Enzymes

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Biomolecules** are organic compounds built mainly from C, H, O, N, S and P, grouped as carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, nucleic acids, vitamins and water. **Enzymes** are globular, mostly proteinaceous biocatalysts that accelerate reactions by lowering **activation energy (Ea)** without altering ΔG or the equilibrium constant. A small set of catalytic RNAs (**ribozymes**, e.g. peptidyl transferase) also qualify as enzymes.

| Term | One-line meaning |
|---|---|
| Apoenzyme | Protein part alone, catalytically **inactive** |
| Holoenzyme | Apoenzyme **+ cofactor**, catalytically **active** |
| Coenzyme | Loosely bound organic cofactor (NAD⁺, CoA, TPP) |
| Prosthetic group | Tightly bound cofactor (FAD, heme) |
| Km | Substrate concentration at **½ Vmax** |

- **Michaelis–Menten:** v = Vmax·[S] / (Km + [S])
- **Competitive inhibition** ↑ apparent Km, **Vmax unchanged**; **non-competitive** ↓ Vmax, **Km unchanged**.
- High-yield NEET PG: EC class (1–6), B-vitamin coenzymes, CK-MB/LDH1 in MI, zymogens (trypsinogen → trypsin by **enteropeptidase**).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Classification and Cofactors
The IUBMB **EC system** splits enzymes into six classes: **oxidoreductases (EC 1)**, **transferases (EC 2)**, **hydrolases (EC 3)**, **lyases (EC 4)**, **isomerases (EC 5)** and **ligases/synthetases (EC 6)**. Each EC number (e.g. EC 3.4.21.4 for trypsin) is a four-level code reflecting reaction type, sub-class, sub-sub-class and serial. Catalytic activity resides in the **active site**, whose 3-D geometry is shaped by tertiary folding of the polypeptide chain.

> **Tip:** Mnemonic **"Over The HILL"** — Oxidoreductases, Transferases, Hydrolases, Isomerases, Lyases, Ligases.

Cofactors fall into three functional groups. **Metal ions** (Zn²⁺ in carbonic anhydrase, Fe in catalase), **prosthetic groups** (heme, FAD) tightly bound, and **coenzymes** (NAD⁺/NADP⁺ from niacin, FAD from riboflavin, TPP from thiamine, PLP from pyridoxine, CoA from pantothen ate, FH₄ from folate) loosely bound and derived from **B-vitamins**.

#### Kinetics and the Lineweaver–Burk Plot
Under steady-state, the **Michaelis–Menten equation** v = Vmax·[S]/(Km+[S]) describes a rectangular hyperbola. The **double-reciprocal plot** linearises it as **1/v = (Km/Vmax)·(1/[S]) + 1/Vmax**, giving a Y-intercept of 1/Vmax and X-intercept of –1/Km. The **turnover number kcat = Vmax/[Et]** gives catalytic cycles per second; **kcat/Km** is the catalytic efficiency at low [S].

| Inhibition | Vmax | Km | Lineweaver–Burk lines |
|---|---|---|---|
| Competitive | Unchanged | ↑ | Lines intersect on Y-axis |
| Non-competitive | ↓ | Unchanged | Lines intersect on X-axis |
| Uncompetitive | ↓ | ↓ | Parallel lines |

#### Regulation and Clinical Markers
Enzymes are regulated by **allosteric effectors** (sigmoidal kinetics, e.g. aspartate transcarbamoylase), **covalent modification** (phosphorylation/dephosphorylation of glycogen phosphorylase) and **zymogen activation** (chymotrypsinogen → π-chymotrypsin → α-chymotrypsin). Diagnostic enzymes include **AST/ALT** (liver), **CK-MB** (myocardium), **amylase and lipase** (pancreas), **GGT** (cholestasis), **ALP** (bone/liver) and **LDH isoenzymes** (LDH1 cardiac, LDH5 hepatic).

> **Mnemonic for MI timeline:** "**C**ASTLE" — **C**K-MB (4–24 h), **AS**T (peaks 24 h), **S**T elevation persists; **L**DH1 (peaks 72 h), **E**CG.

NEET PG repeatedly tests EC numbering, coenzyme-to-vitamin mapping, double-reciprocal plot interpretation and zymogen cascades. Aim for one solved Lineweaver–Burk question per sitting.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Mechanism and Thermodynamics
Enzymes accelerate reactions by stabilising the **transition state** through acid/base catalysis, covalent intermediate formation, metal-ion facilitation, electrostatic steering and substrate proximity/orientation effects. Rate enhancements of **10⁶–10¹² fold** are common (e.g. orotidine-5′-phosphate decarboxylase: ~10¹⁷). Because catalysis lowers **Ea** but not **ΔG**, the equilibrium constant **Keq = [P]/[S] at ΔG = 0** remains unchanged — a frequent MCQ trap.

#### Edge Cases and Non-Protein Catalysts
Ribozymes catalyse phosphodiester cleavage (Group I introns, RNase P) and peptide bond formation (**peptidyl transferase**, a ribozyme on the 50S subunit). **Abzymes** (catalytic antibodies) are engineered immunoglobulins raised against transition-state analogues.

#### Inhibition Subtleties
- **Mixed inhibition:** both Km and Vmax change; lines intersect left of the Y-axis.
- **Suicide (mechanism-based) inhibition:** the inhibitor is activated by the enzyme into a reactive species that covalently modifies the active site (e.g. allopurinol → alloxanthine on xanthine oxidase).
- **Km ≈ Kd** only when k₂ ≪ k₋₁; for many physiological enzymes Km is set by kcat, so Km is best read as the **operational [S] at half-Vmax**, not as a binding affinity.

#### Worked Micro-Example
For an enzyme at [Et] = 1×10⁻⁸ M giving Vmax = 1×10⁻⁴ M s⁻¹ and Km = 2×10⁻⁴ M:
- **kcat** = Vmax/[Et] = 10⁴ s⁻¹ (≈10 000 turnovers s⁻¹)
- **Catalytic efficiency** = kcat/Km = 10⁴ / (2×10⁻⁴) = **5×10⁷ M⁻¹ s⁻¹**, approaching the diffusion limit (~10⁸–10⁹ M⁻¹ s⁻¹).
- At [S] = Km = 2×10⁻⁴ M, **v = Vmax/2 = 5×10⁻⁵ M s⁻¹**.

#### Practice Prompts
1. A double-reciprocal plot of a competitive inhibitor shows the X-intercept shifted toward the origin and an unchanged Y-intercept — derive the new apparent Km and explain why Vmax is preserved.
2. Match each zymogen with its activator and final enzyme: **pepsinogen, trypsinogen, chymotrypsinogen, proelastase** — and state the clinical consequence of premature intra-pancreatic activation (acute pancreatitis, raised **serum amylase/lipase**).
