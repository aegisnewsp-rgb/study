# Prepositions and Conjunctions

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

A **preposition** is a word placed before a noun or pronoun (its **object**) to show how that noun/pronoun relates to another word in the sentence — relationships of time, place, direction, manner, or possession. Common single-word prepositions include *in, on, at, by, for, from, with, to, of, between, through*. A **prepositional phrase** = preposition + object (+ modifiers), e.g. *under the big tree*, *during the exam*.

A **conjunction** is a joining word. **Coordinating conjunctions** (the FANBOYS set: **F**or, **A**nd, **N**or, **B**ut, **O**r, **Y**et, **S**o) link equal elements. **Subordinating conjunctions** (*because, although, when, if, since, while*) attach a dependent clause to an independent clause. **Correlative conjunctions** come in pairs (*both…and, either…or, neither…nor*).

WAEC quick hits: (1) *since* = point of time (*since 2020*); *for* = duration (*for five years*). (2) *between* = two items; *among* = more than two. (3) Never pair *although/though* with *but* in the same clause.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Prepositions by Function
- **Time:** *at* (clock times, *at noon*), *on* (days/dates, *on Monday*), *in* (months, years, seasons, *in July*), *by* (deadline, *by Friday*), *since* (point in past, *since 2019*), *for* (duration, *for two hours*), *during, before, after*.
- **Place:** *in* (enclosed space), *on* (surface), *at* (specific point), *between* (two), *among* (more than two), *above, below, under, over, beside, near*.
- **Direction/Movement:** *to, from, into, onto, towards, across, through, along, around*.
- **Manner/Instrument:** *with* (instrument, *cut with a knife*), *by* (means/agent, *written by Ama*), *in* (manner, *in ink*).
- **Relationship:** *of, for, with, to, about, concerning*.

#### Types of Conjunctions
**Coordinating (FANBOYS)** join words, phrases, or independent clauses of equal rank: *Ama and Kojo went home. He studied hard, so he passed.* When joining two independent clauses, place a comma before *for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so*.

**Subordinating** introduce dependent (subordinate) clauses:
- *Time:* when, while, after, before, until, since, as soon as
- *Cause:* because, since, as, now that
- *Condition:* if, unless, provided that, as long as
- *Contrast:* although, though, even though, whereas, while
- *Purpose:* so that, in order that
- *Result:* so…that, such…that

**Correlative** appear in matched pairs and must keep parallel structure: *Both Kwame and Ama passed. Either you apologise or you leave. Neither the teacher nor the students were informed.*

#### Common WAEC Traps
- *Since* (point of time, with present perfect) vs *for* (duration): *I have lived here **since** 2020* (correct) vs *for five years* (correct).
- *Between* two; *among* more than two: *The money is shared between Kofi and Ama*; *shared among the students*.
- *Into* = motion from outside to inside; *to* = direction only: *She walked **into** the room*; *She walked **to** the room*.
- *By* = agent in passive (*written **by** Sika*); *with* = instrument (*cut **with** a knife*).
- Double conjunction error: ~~*since because*~~ → use one (*because, since*).

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Exam-Specific Patterns
**Complex (multi-word) prepositions** act as a single preposition and frequently appear in WAEC comprehension and summary passages: *in spite of, with regard to, on behalf of, in front of, in addition to, because of, apart from*. Test them by substituting a single preposition: *in spite of* ≈ *despite*; *because of* ≈ *due to*.

**Conjunctive adverbs** (therefore, however, moreover, nevertheless, otherwise, furthermore) are NOT conjunctions. They join independent clauses but require semicolons or conjunctions, not commas alone: *It rained; therefore, the match was postponed.* WAEC error-spotting questions regularly flag *however* misused as a coordinating conjunction.

**Tense interaction with subordinators**: *since* + present perfect in main clause = cause starting in the past (*Since I have arrived, I can help*). *Since* + simple past = time point. *Before/after* follow normal tense rules: *Before he left, he ate.*

**Punctuation with FANBOYS**: When FANBOYS join two independent clauses, a comma precedes them. When they join two words or phrases, no comma: *I bought rice and beans* (no comma) vs *I bought rice, and I cooked beans* (comma).

#### Worked Micro-Example
Rewrite using correct prepositions: *Ama has been studying **since** (point) **for** three hours.* Correct: *Ama has been studying for three hours since 9 a.m.* Identify errors in: *The prize was shared between all the six contestants.* Correct: *among all the six contestants* (more than two items).

#### Common Mistakes
1. *On/in time* — *on time* = punctual; *in time* = before it is too late.
2. *To/at/in* with places — *to* (destination motion), *at* (specific point), *in* (enclosed).
3. Dropping a relative pronoun after a preposition: *the man **with whom** I spoke* (not *with who*).
4. Mixing *so…that* with *so that*: *so…that* = result; *so that* = purpose.

#### Practice Prompts
1. Fill in the blanks: *The meeting will hold _____ (in/on/at) Tuesday _____ (in/on/at) the conference room.*
2. Correct and explain: *Although he was tired, but he continued working.*
