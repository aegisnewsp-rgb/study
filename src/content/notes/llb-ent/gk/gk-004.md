# The Republic of South Africa

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

> Read this block before walking into the test hall; the nine provinces and the 1996 Constitution carry more marks than any other detail.

South Africa became a republic on **31 May 1961**, but the legal order tested in LLB admission questions is the **Constitution of the Republic of South Africa, 1996**, which took effect on **4 February 1997**. That text is the supreme law under Section 2 — every other statute, common-law rule, and customary practice that conflicts with it is invalid.

- **Head of state = President**, who is also head of government and is elected by the **National Assembly** (not by voters directly).
- **Parliament is bicameral**: National Assembly (400 members, proportional representation) + National Council of Provinces (90 delegates, 10 per province).
- **Three spheres of government**: national, provincial, local — bound by *co-operative government* (Chapter 3).
- **Highest court on constitutional matters**: Constitutional Court (11 judges). The Supreme Court of Appeal is highest on non-constitutional matters.
- **Nine provinces**: Eastern Cape, Free State, Gauteng, KwaZulu-Natal, Limpopo, Mpumalanga, Northern Cape, North West, Western Cape.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook — "RAM-9-97":** **R**epublic (1961), **A**ssembly elects President, **M**ulti-party democracy, **9** provinces, **'97** took effect. Walk through RAM-9-97 in your head and you've covered the four facts examiners love to flip into MCQs.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

> Work through the Constitution's structure once; the rest of the topic falls into place around it.

#### Constitutional foundation

The 1996 Constitution replaced the interim 1993 Constitution on **4 February 1997** and binds every organ of state. Chapter 1 fixes the founding values (human dignity, equality, non-racialism, non-sexism, the rule of law, universal adult suffrage), Chapter 2 contains the **Bill of Rights**, and Chapter 3 establishes co-operative government across the three spheres. Section 2 — the supremacy clause — means a court will strike down any inconsistent act of Parliament, provincial legislation, or customary rule.

#### Branches and their separation

| Branch | Head | Key institution | Source in Constitution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Executive | President (head of state **and** government) | Cabinet, line-function ministries | Chapter 5 |
| Legislature | Speaker / Chairperson of NCOP | National Assembly (400) + NCOP (90) | Chapter 4 |
| Judiciary | Chief Justice + 10 others | Constitutional Court (11) | Chapter 8 |

#### Parliamentary mechanics

- The **National Assembly** is elected by proportional representation; it may have no more than 400 members, with at least half elected from a national list and the remainder from regional lists.
- The **National Council of Provinces** represents the nine provinces, each sending a delegation of 10 (six special delegates plus four permanent). It must approve ordinary legislation affecting provinces under Section 75/76.
- The **President** is elected by the Assembly from among its members after a general election, appoints the Cabinet, and may serve a maximum of two terms (Section 86).

#### Bill of Rights and Constitutional Court

The Bill of Rights binds the legislature, the executive, the judiciary, and all organs of state. When a constitutional question reaches the apex of the system, the **Constitutional Court** (11 judges, the Chief Justice plus ten) gives the final word. The **Supreme Court of Appeal** remains the highest court on non-constitutional matters.

#### Commonwealth and citizenship

South Africa left the Commonwealth in 1961, retained observer status, rejoined in 1994, and hosts the Secretariat. The **South African Citizenship Act 88 of 1995** governs citizenship; **dual citizenship is permitted**.

#### Practice prompt resolved

A multiple-choice stem offers: *"The highest court in South Africa on **all** matters is the Constitutional Court."* Most candidates tick "True" and lose a mark. Read Section 167 carefully — the Constitutional Court is highest on **constitutional matters only**. On non-constitutional matters, the Supreme Court of Appeal has the final say. This is the single most-tested distinction in this topic.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap — "highest court on all matters":** the stem adds "all". Drop one qualifier and the answer flips. Always re-read the question's qualifying words before ticking.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

> Push beyond the MCQ layer; the harder prompts test chronology and the boundary between spheres.

#### Dates that examiners swap

| Year | Event | Distractor to watch |
|---|---|---|
| 1961 (31 May) | Republic declared, leaves Commonwealth | Confused with 1994 or 1997 |
| 1993 | Interim Constitution adopted | Confused with the supreme 1996 text |
| 1994 | First democratic election; rejoins Commonwealth | Confused with the start of the constitutional order |
| 1997 (4 Feb) | 1996 Constitution takes effect | Confused with the adoption date (10 December 1996) |
| 1995 | Citizenship Act 88 enacted | Confused with effective dates |

#### Boundary cases worth memorising

1. **Republic ≠ current Constitution.** Republic status dates to 1961; the current constitutional order dates to 1997. A stem phrased as "the Constitution that established the Republic" is wrong — no Constitution did; a statute did.
2. **Speaker vs Chairperson.** The Speaker presides over the National Assembly; the Chairperson of the NCOP presides over the Council of Provinces. Conflating the two is a recurring trap.
3. **Section 2 supremacy vs parliamentary sovereignty.** South Africa is **not** a parliamentary-sovereignty system. Parliament legislates **under** the Constitution; the Constitutional Court may strike down an Act of Parliament.
4. **National Assembly delegates ≠ voters.** The President is elected by the Assembly from its members, not by voters in a direct presidential ballot. Stems that describe a direct election are wrong.
5. **Co-operative government is binding, not advisory.** Chapter 3 obliges all three spheres to co-operate; failure to do so can trigger constitutional dispute resolution in the Constitutional Court.
6. **Dual citizenship since 1995.** The 1995 Act reversed earlier restrictions; a stem stating South Africa forbids dual citizenship fails on its face.

#### Worked scenario — what court answers?

A provincial premier enacts a regulation that restricts assembly on private property. A civil-rights group challenges it on the ground that it violates the Bill of Rights. The High Court hears the matter and reaches a constitutional finding.

Resolution pathway: any court may decide a constitutional issue, but **only the Constitutional Court** is the final arbiter on constitutional matters. If the matter is appealed, it must be lodged directly with the Constitutional Court (Section 167(3) — the direct-access route for constitutional issues). The Supreme Court of Appeal has no jurisdiction to overturn a constitutional finding.

#### Advanced practice prompts

1. *"The 1993 Constitution remains the supreme law until replaced by ordinary legislation."* Identify two errors and rewrite the statement correctly. *(Answer: there is no 1993 Constitution in force, and no ordinary Act can override a Constitution.)*
2. *"The President appoints the Cabinet but is accountable to the electorate through direct election."* Identify the error and cite the correct constitutional provision. *(Answer: Section 86 — election is by the National Assembly, not voters.)*

#### Common mistakes to drop before the test

- Treating "republic" and "Constitution" as synonymous.
- Counting provinces by memory (the Northern Cape and North West are the ones students drop or merge).
- Forgetting that the President is both head of state **and** head of government — unlike the United Kingdom, where these roles are split.
