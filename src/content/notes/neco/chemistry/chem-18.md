# Environmental Chemistry and Green Chemistry

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Environmental Chemistry** studies chemical species in air, water, and soil — their sources, reactions, transport, effects, and fate. **Green Chemistry** is the design of chemical products and processes that reduce or eliminate hazardous substances, guided by 12 principles developed by Anastas and Warner.

#### Atom economy formula:
$$\text{Atom Economy (\%)} = \frac{\text{Molar mass of desired product (g/mol)}}{\text{Total molar mass of all products (g/mol)}} \times 100$$

Normal rain has a pH of about **5.6** because dissolved CO₂ forms weak carbonic acid (H₂CO₃). Acid rain falls below pH 5.6 due to SO₂ and NOₓ emissions.

#### High-yield pointers for NECO SSCE:
- Acid rain is caused mainly by **SO₂ and NOₓ**, not CO₂ alone.
- Ozone depletion is caused by **CFCs**, not greenhouse gases like CO₂ or CH₄.
- BOD₅ measures biodegradable organic pollution in water over 5 days at 20 °C.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Air Pollution and Its Effects

Major air pollutants include CO, CO₂, SO₂, NO₂, NOₓ, hydrocarbons, suspended particulates, and CFCs. Combustion of fossil fuels in vehicles and power stations releases SO₂ and NOₓ, which combine with atmospheric water to form sulfuric acid (H₂SO₄) and nitric acid (HNO₃) — the cause of acid rain. Greenhouse gases (CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, CFCs) trap infrared radiation in the troposphere and drive global warming. In the stratosphere, CFCs release chlorine atoms that catalytically destroy ozone (O₃), thinning the protective layer.

#### Water Pollution Parameters

Water quality is measured by **BOD**, **COD**, **dissolved oxygen (DO)**, **pH**, and **turbidity**. BOD₅ in mg/L = (Initial DO − DO after 5 days at 20 °C). A high BOD means the water contains lots of biodegradable organic waste, which depletes oxygen and kills aquatic life. Sewage treatment follows a sequence: sedimentation → filtration → chlorination, sometimes with pH adjustment.

#### Green Chemistry Principles (selected)

- **Prevention** — prevent waste rather than treat it after formation.
- **Atom economy** — maximise incorporation of reactant atoms into the final product.
- **Less hazardous synthesis** — use and generate substances with little toxicity.
- **Safer solvents and auxiliaries** — avoid volatile organic solvents where possible.
- **Catalysis** — catalytic reagents are superior to stoichiometric ones.
- **Design for degradation** — products should break down into harmless substances after use.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Distinguishing the Two Branches

Environmental Chemistry describes what pollution does once it is released; Green Chemistry prevents pollution at the design stage. A student can lose marks by treating the two as synonyms. Real-world success stories illustrate the difference: replacing CFCs with HFCs and HCFCs protects the ozone layer (environmental response), while redesigning plasticisers and paints to be biodegradable reduces future landfill leaching (green chemistry design).

#### Common Examiner Traps

- Confusing **atom economy** with **percentage yield**. Atom economy is theoretical, calculated from the balanced equation; percentage yield is experimental.
- Listing only CO₂ as a greenhouse gas. CH₄, N₂O, and CFCs also contribute.
- Believing chlorination alone purifies water. Chlorination only disinfects; particulates and heavy metals need sedimentation and filtration first.
- Thinking biodegradable means any natural substance. A "natural" compound can still be non-biodegradable if microbes cannot break it down.

#### Worked Mini-Example

A reaction produces 4.0 g of useful product and 6.0 g of by-product from 10.0 g of reactants. Atom economy = (4.0 / 10.0) × 100 = **40 %**. The remaining 60 % of atoms are wasted as by-products — a clear target for greener redesign.

#### Practice Prompts

1. State three principles of Green Chemistry and explain how each reduces environmental harm.
2. A water sample has initial DO of 8.0 mg/L and DO after 5 days of 3.0 mg/L. Calculate the BOD₅ and comment on the pollution level.
