# International News

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**International News** for CLAT covers cross-border political, economic, security, and diplomatic events that affect India's interests or the global order. The section is tested inside Current Affairs & GK and contributes ~4% of the paper, with 6–8 direct questions typically drawn from the preceding 12–18 months.

#### Must-Know Anchors

- **UN Security Council (UNSC):** 15 members, 5 permanent (P5) with veto — **USA, UK, France, Russia, China**. India is not permanent but was a non-permanent member in 2021–22.
- **G20 Presidency 2023:** India hosted in New Delhi; theme **"Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam — One Earth, One Family, One Future"**.
- **BRICS expansion:** Effective 1 January 2024, adding Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, UAE, Saudi Arabia, Argentina to the original five.
- **Indus Waters Treaty (1960):** World Bank-brokered, allocates eastern rivers to India, western rivers to Pakistan.

#### High-Yield Exam Pointers

- **Paris Agreement** goal: limit warming to **well below 2°C, pursue 1.5°C** — not "2°C only".
- **IAEA** (atomic verification) ≠ **CERN** (particle physics, Switzerland) ≠ **ITER** (fusion, France).
- UNGA resolutions are **non-binding**; UNSC resolutions under Article 25 are **binding**.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

International news in CLAT spans seven recurring sub-domains: global institutions, conflict zones, treaties/climate accords, India's bilateral and multilateral engagements, persons-in-news, and annual summits. Mastering the institutional architecture is the highest ROI area because static GK questions on UN bodies and Bretton Woods twins appear every year.

#### The UN System and Bretton Woods Architecture

The United Nations has six principal organs, of which the **General Assembly** (all 193 members, one state one vote) and the **Security Council** (15 members, 5 permanent) matter most for CLAT. Resolutions of the GA are recommendatory; UNSC decisions are binding on all members. Specialized agencies — **WHO, UNESCO, UNICEF, FAO, ILO, IAEA, WIPO** — operate under UN coordination but with their own memberships.

The post-1945 economic order rests on the **Bretton Woods institutions**: the **IMF** (surveillance, SDRs, balance-of-payments support, Article IV consultations) and the **World Bank Group** (IBRD + IDA, project lending). The **WTO**, established in 1995, governs trade via TRIPS, GATS, and the dispute settlement mechanism; the long-stalled **Doha Round** remains a staple factual question.

#### India on the Global Stage

India's diplomatic footprint maps onto a tight set of groupings worth memorising in one table.

#### Multilateral and Bilateral Engagements

| Forum | Members / Partner | Exam-Relevant Anchor |
|---|---|---|
| **QUAD** | India, USA, Japan, Australia | Indo-Pacific security dialogue |
| **BRICS** | Original 5 + 6 from 1 Jan 2024 | New Development Bank headquartered in Shanghai |
| **SCO** | India, China, Russia, Central Asian states | Founded 2001, expanded 2017 |
| **G20** | 19 countries + EU + AU | India held presidency 2023 |

> Trap: **SAARC** has been effectively dormant since the 2016 Islamabad Summit — do not describe it as a currently active platform in options.

#### Conflict and Crisis Hotspots

- **Russia–Ukraine:** War since 24 February 2022; multiple UNGA votes condemning Russia.
- **Israel–Palestine:** Gaza conflict from October 2023; ongoing ICJ South Africa vs. Israel case (Jan 2024).
- **India–China LAC:** Standoff at Galwan (2020) and Tawang (Dec 2022).
- **Red Sea / Houthi attacks** disrupted global shipping through 2024.

#### Persons in News

- **UN Secretary-General:** António Guterres (term to end-2026).
- **WTO DG:** Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala.
- **World Bank President:** Ajay Banga (since June 2023).
- **IAEA DG:** Rafael Grossi.

#### Typical Question Patterns

Match-the-following (organisation to HQ/head), "which of the following statements is correct" on summits, and assertion-reason items on treaty provisions. The treaty detail most often misremembered is the **Paris Agreement's 1.5°C aspiration** versus the Kyoto Protocol's binding targets for Annex-I countries.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Conceptual Distinctions

Several closely-named institutions trip students on match-the-column sets. **IAEA** is a verification body for civil nuclear programmes under the NPT regime; **CERN** is a European particle-physics laboratory not affiliated with the UN; **ITER** is a fusion megaproject under construction in France. Similarly, the **Indus Waters Treaty (1960)** is distinct from the **Permanent Indus Commission**, which is merely the dispute-resolution mechanism created under the treaty. India issued a notice seeking to modify the treaty in 2023, citing cross-border terrorism — a nuance examiners now use in current-affairs assertion-reason items.

Another recurring trap is the **Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations (1961)**, which codifies ambassadorial immunity (Article 29 — inviolability of the diplomatic agent; Article 31 — jurisdictional immunity). Pair this with the **Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties (1969)** for the rule that a material breach justifies suspension.

#### Climate and Trade Mechanisms

The **Paris Agreement** uses **Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs)**, refreshed every five years, with a global stocktake. The **Montreal Protocol (1987)** for ozone-depleting substances is the only UN treaty to have achieved universal ratification. In trade, the **TRIPS waiver** debate during COVID-19 and the **WTO Appellate Body impasse** since 2019 are high-yield current items.

#### India-Specific Strategic Corridors

- **IMEC (India–Middle East–Europe Economic Corridor):** Announced at the G20 New Delhi Summit, 2023; links Indian ports to the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Israel, Greece, and the EU.
- **INSTC:** International North–South Transport Corridor connecting Mumbai to St. Petersburg via Iran and Azerbaijan.
- **Chabahar Port (Iran):** Gives India a foothold bypassing Pakistan.

#### Adjacent Topic Links

- **Constitutional Law** — Article 51 (international peace and law) anchors India's treaty-making.
- **Economics** — Bretton Woods institutions tie to balance-of-payments and SDR pricing.
- **Environment** — COP processes connect to domestic climate law and net-zero 2070 target.

#### Common Mistakes

- Treating all UN votes as "binding"; UNGA resolutions are recommendatory.
- Citing the G20 2023 theme as "One World, One Family" — the actual phrase is **Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam**.
- Forgetting that **Argentina declined BRICS membership** after the initial 2024 invitation, so the actual expansion added five, not six, members in practice.
- Confusing **QUAD** (security dialogue, no treaty) with **AUKUS** (Australia–UK–US security pact, 2021).

> Strategy: 4% weightage × ~120 questions ≈ 4–5 questions. Spend ≤40 minutes here; memorise one table of organisations and one of summits rather than reading long analyses.

#### Practice Prompts

1. **Assertion-Reason:** "The Paris Agreement sets a binding 2°C warming limit" — *because* "all UN climate decisions are legally binding on parties." Identify the flaw.
2. **Match the Following:** Pair IAEA, CERN, ITER, OPCW to their correct mandate (atomic verification / particle physics / fusion / chemical weapons) and headquarters.
