# General Science

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Read this block once the night before Tier-I. Six bullets carry 80% of what SSC actually asks.

General Science for SSC CGL means NCERT Class 10 recall across Physics, Chemistry, and Biology — no derivations, no engineering depth. Roughly 7–9 questions appear per shift, making it the densest non-current section of the 25-question General Awareness paper. Every question is one-line factual recall, so speed matters more than solving skill.

- **Force, work, power:** F = m·a (N), W = m·g·h (J), P = W/t = F·v (W). g on Earth ≈ 9.8 m/s².
- **Ohm's law:** V = IR. SI units — V (volt), I (ampere), R (ohm). Do not confuse with Joule's heating H = I²Rt.
- **pH scale:** pH = −log₁₀[H⁺], dimensionless. pH < 7 is acid, pH > 7 is base, pH = 7 is neutral at 25 °C.
- **Blood groups:** O is universal **donor**, AB is universal **recipient**. This single swap costs lakhs of candidates a mark yearly.
- **Vitamins:** A, D, E, K are fat-soluble; B-complex and C are water-soluble.
- **Wave rule:** v = f·λ. Higher frequency = shorter wavelength at constant speed.

> 💡 **Memory Hook — "ROYGBIV is fat, B and C flush":** Vitamins A, D, E, K (fat-soluble, stored in liver) — keep the vowels **A D E K**; everything else (B, C) dissolves in water and excess leaves through urine.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### What SSC actually tests
The General Awareness paper has 25 questions × 2 marks = 50 marks, and General Science contributes the highest non-current count per shift. The examiner stays at NCERT Class 10 level: definitions, one-word SI units, named laws, everyday phenomena. Negative marking of 0.50 means accuracy beats attempts — leave a question blank rather than guess.

#### Physics — laws you must know cold
Newton's three laws, the difference between mass and weight, and the conservation of momentum (m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂) form the spine of physics questions. For electricity, memorise V = IR, P = VI = I²R = V²/R, and the household definitions — 1 unit of electricity = 1 kWh = 3.6 MJ. Light questions usually hit reflection, refraction, the visible spectrum order, and the mirror formula 1/v + 1/u = 1/f (sign convention: focal length of concave mirror is negative). Sound questions test the speed in air ≈ 343 m/s at 20 °C, the human hearing range (20 Hz – 20 kHz), and the fact that sound cannot travel through vacuum.

#### Chemistry — reactions and the periodic table
Acids release H⁺ in water; bases release OH⁻. Litmus turns **red in acid**, **blue in base**. The pH scale runs 0–14; each unit is a tenfold change in [H⁺]. Metals are electropositive, malleable, ductile, and good conductors; non-metals are the opposite. Carbon compounds (alkanes single bond, alkenes double, alkynes triple) and the distinction between saturated and unsaturated fats come up almost every shift.

#### Biology — the living system
The cell is the basic unit. Plant cells have a cell wall, large vacuole, and chloroplasts; animal cells lack these. In human physiology, examiners ask about heart chambers (four), blood groups with the donor-recipient rule, the function of RBCs (oxygen via haemoglobin) and WBCs (immunity), and the excretory system where nephrons filter blood in kidneys. Deficiency diseases pair up neatly: Vitamin A → night blindness, B1 → beriberi, C → scurvy, D → rickets, K → slow clotting; iron → anaemia, iodine → goitre.

#### Comparison matrix — the five swaps SSC loves
| Topic | Correct fact | Common wrong answer |
|---|---|---|
| Universal donor / recipient | O donates, AB receives | O receives, AB donates |
| Ohm's law vs Joule's law | V = IR / H = I²Rt | V = I²R (mixing them) |
| Fat- vs water-soluble vitamins | A D E K fat / B C water | "All vitamins are fat-soluble" |
| Concave vs convex mirror | Concave converges, convex diverges | Reversed roles |
| Acid-base litmus | Red in acid, blue in base | Blue in acid, red in base |

#### Worked exam-level problem
When a 4 Ω resistor and a 6 Ω resistor are connected in series across a 12 V battery, the current through the circuit is **A**. Two: the same current flows at every point in a series loop. V = IR gives I = 12 / (4 + 6) = 12 / 10 = **1.2 A**.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students read "6 Ω" and divide 12 by 6 to get 2 A. That treats only the second resistor. Total resistance in series is the **sum**, not the largest value.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

#### Where students lose marks they did not expect
The single costliest trap is the SI-unit swap. Examiners quote a temperature in °C and ask for the SI unit — the answer is **kelvin (K)**, not Celsius. Same pattern with work (joule, not newton), pressure (pascal, not bar), and force (newton, not kgf). Always convert the *unit name* in your head before ticking.

Heat transfer mechanisms confuse test-takers because three different carriers get tangled. Conduction needs a medium and transfers through solids (metals conduct well via free electrons); convection needs a fluid in motion; radiation needs no medium and travels through vacuum (this is how the Sun's heat reaches Earth). Misnaming these is a guaranteed wrong answer.

In the periodic table, examiners test *trends*, not values. Atomic radius **decreases** across a period (more nuclear pull on the same shell) and **increases** down a group (new shell added). Ionisation energy and electronegativity **increase** across a period and **decrease** down a group. Metallic character does the opposite of ionisation energy.

#### Biology edge cases that appear in shifted papers
- The largest cell in the human body is the **ovum**; the longest is the **motor neuron**.
- Mitochondria generate ATP; ribosomes synthesise protein; the nucleus stores DNA.
- Vaccines contain **killed or weakened pathogens**, not dead cells of the host.
- Scurvy affects collagen synthesis, beriberi affects the nervous system (dry) and heart (wet).
- AIDS is caused by HIV, which attacks **CD4+ T helper cells**, not RBCs.

#### Advanced traps and exceptions

1. **Zero work, but a force acts.** Holding a stationary suitcase does no work because displacement is zero, even though force is non-zero. Examiners test this every few shifts.
2. **Resistance is not linear at extremes.** Ohm's law V = IR holds for ohmic conductors at constant temperature; filament bulbs and semiconductors deviate. SSC asks this as a "true / false" MCQ.
3. **Speed of sound depends on the medium.** Sound travels fastest in **solids**, slower in liquids, slowest in gases — students usually invert this.
4. **Light year is distance, not time.** It is the distance light travels in one Julian year (≈ 9.46 × 10¹⁵ m). Mixing units of distance and time is a classic trap.
5. **All bases are not alkalis.** Bases are broad; alkalis are water-soluble bases (NaOH, KOH). CuO is a base but not an alkali.
6. **Frequency and pitch are linked, not equal to loudness.** Pitch depends on frequency; loudness depends on amplitude.

#### Cross-topic linkage to exploit in revision
A sound question often hides a wave formula: speed of sound in air ≈ 343 m/s, so a 1000 Hz tuning fork has wavelength λ = v / f ≈ 0.34 m. Same formula structure applies to light (≈ 3 × 10⁸ m/s) and radio waves. Once the relation v = f·λ is solid, all wave questions collapse to one calculation.

#### Practice prompts before you close the chapter
- A copper wire of length 2 m, area of cross-section 1 mm², carries 0.5 A. Given resistivity of copper, find the potential drop across the wire — apply ρL/A for R, then V = IR.
- A solution has [H⁺] = 10⁻³ M. Is it acidic, basic, or neutral? Justify using the pH definition. (pH = 3, so acidic.)
- Two mirrors are placed at 60° to each other. How many images of a candle placed between them are formed? Use n = (360°/θ) − 1 when 360°/θ is even.
