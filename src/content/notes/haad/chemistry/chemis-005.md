# Aromatic Chemistry: Benzene and Its Derivatives

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Benzene (C₆H₆)** is a cyclic, planar, fully conjugated ring of six sp²-hybridised carbons sharing 6 π electrons. It obeys **Hückel's rule** (4n + 2 π electrons; n = 1) and resists addition, preferring **electrophilic aromatic substitution (EAS)**.

| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Molecular formula / molar mass | C₆H₆, 78.11 g mol⁻¹ |
| Degree of unsaturation | 4 (1 ring + 3 π bonds) |
| C–C bond length / order | ≈140 pm, bond order 1.5 |
| Resonance (delocalisation) energy | ≈150 kJ mol⁻¹ |

> **HAAD tip:** Memorise ortho/para directors (–OH, –NH₂, –OR, –CH₃, halogens) and meta directors (–NO₂, –COOH, –CN, –SO₃H, –CHO). This single distinction decides most MCQ answers.

Phenol, aniline, and toluene are **activated**; nitrobenzene and benzoic acid are **deactivated**.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Aromaticity Criteria
A compound is aromatic only when **cyclic, planar, fully conjugated** (every ring atom sp²) and possesses **4n + 2 π electrons** (Hückel: n = 0, 1, 2…). Benzene (6 π e⁻, n = 1) is the textbook case. Cyclooctatetraene is *non-aromatic* — it puckers into a tub to avoid the 4n (antiaromatic) state, so it behaves as a normal polyene.

#### Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution (EAS)
The five canonical EAS reactions are **nitration, sulphonation, halogenation, Friedel–Crafts alkylation, and Friedel–Crafts acylation**. The shared three-step mechanism is:

1. Generation of a strong electrophile (e.g. NO₂⁺ from HNO₃ + H₂SO₄; R⁺ or RCO⁺ from RCl/AlCl₃).
2. Attack on the π cloud, forming a resonance-stabilised **arenium ion (Wheland σ-complex / carbocation intermediate)**.
3. Loss of H⁺ to restore aromaticity — the driving force.

#### Directing Effects of Common Substituents

| Group | Electronic effect | Direction | Ring reactivity |
|---|---|---|---|
| –OH, –NH₂, –OR | +M (donate by resonance) | ortho/para | Strongly activated |
| –CH₃, alkyl | +I | ortho/para | Activated |
| –X (F, Cl, Br, I) | –I, +M | ortho/para | Deactivated overall |
| –NO₂, –CN, –COOH, –SO₃H, –CHO | –M, –I | meta | Deactivated |

> **Trap:** Halogens are the only group that is **ortho/para-directing yet deactivating** — exam MCQs exploit this asymmetry.

#### Side-Chain Oxidation
Hot **KMnO₄/H⁺** (or K₂Cr₂O₇/H⁺) cleaves any alkyl side chain with at least one **benzylic hydrogen** down to **–COOH**. Toluene → benzoic acid; ethylbenzene → benzoic acid. *tert*-Butylbenzene (no benzylic H) is **not** oxidised.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Reactivity Ranking and Clinical Relevance
Order toward EAS: **phenol > aniline > toluene > benzene > chlorobenzene > nitrobenzene > benzoic acid.** This ranking explains why **paracetamol (acetaminophen)**, **aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid)**, and **salicylic acid** undergo nitration/sulphonation far more readily than benzene itself — and why nitration of phenol gives *o*- and *p*-nitrophenol almost exclusively.

#### Friedel–Crafts Caveats
Three limitations appear repeatedly in HAAD/MOH-style questions:

- **Deactivated rings fail** — nitrobenzene cannot be alkylated or acylated because the ring is too electron-poor, and its nitro group coordinates to AlCl₃.
- **Polyalkylation** is common because the first alkyl group activates the ring for the next; the acylation variant avoids this because –COR is deactivating.
- **Carbocation rearrangement** during alkylation; acylium ions (RCO⁺) do not rearrange, giving cleaner products.
- **Aniline fails** because the –NH₂ lone pair ties up AlCl₃ and is protonated by HCl by-product; protect it as **acetanilide**, then deprotect after the EAS step.

#### Aromaticity Edge Cases
Cyclopentadienyl **anion** (6 π e⁻) is aromatic; **cyclopropenyl cation** (2 π e⁻) is aromatic — both charged species obey Hückel when planar and conjugated. Pyridine (6 π e⁻, lone pair in sp² orbital, not in the ring) is aromatic, but its nitrogen lone pair is *not* part of the π count.

#### Common Mistakes
- Drawing **Kekulé alternating bonds** and assuming localised reactivity; benzene never adds Br₂ in water at room temperature.
- Treating –OH and –NH₂ as electron-withdrawing; they donate by **resonance (+M)**.
- Writing nitration with NO₂⁻; the true electrophile is **NO₂⁺ (nitronium)**.
- Predicting oxidation of *tert*-alkyl side chains — needs a benzylic H to proceed.

#### Practice Prompts
1. Rank nitration rates of benzene, toluene, chlorobenzene, and nitrobenzene. Justify using +M, +I, and –M effects.
2. Propose a synthesis of *p*-nitrotoluene from benzene that avoids *o*-nitrotoluene as the major isomer, and explain why a single Friedel–Crafts step would not work.
