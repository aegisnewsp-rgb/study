# A. Basic Economic Concepts — HKDSE Economics Notes

This topic covers Topic A of the Compulsory Part of HKDSE Economics. It addresses scarcity and choice; opportunity cost; the production possibilities frontier; demand and supply (market demand, individual demand, market supply, individual supply, equilibrium, shifts of demand and supply curves); and elasticity of demand and supply (price elasticity, income elasticity, cross elasticity).

> Verify the live HKDSE Economics Assessment Framework on https://www.hkeaa.edu.hk/ before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Scarcity**: unlimited wants vs. limited resources; the fundamental economic problem.
- **Choice**: choosing between alternatives given scarce resources.
- **Opportunity cost**: the next-best alternative foregone when a choice is made.
- **Production possibilities frontier (PPF)**: a curve showing the maximum combinations of two goods that can be produced with the available resources and technology. Points on the curve are efficient; points outside are unattainable; points inside are inefficient.
- **Demand**: the quantity of a good that consumers are willing and able to buy at each price, ceteris paribus.
- **Law of demand**: as price increases, quantity demanded decreases (and vice versa), ceteris paribus.
- **Supply**: the quantity of a good that producers are willing and able to sell at each price, ceteris paribus.
- **Law of supply**: as price increases, quantity supplied increases (and vice versa), ceteris paribus.
- **Equilibrium**: the price at which quantity demanded equals quantity supplied.
- **Shifts vs movements**: a shift of the demand or supply curve is caused by a non-price determinant; a movement along the curve is caused by a change in the price of the good itself.
- **Price elasticity of demand (PED)**: % change in Qd / % change in P. |PED| > 1 elastic, |PED| = 1 unit elastic, |PED| < 1 inelastic.
- **Income elasticity of demand (YED)**: % change in Qd / % change in income. YED > 1 luxury, YED > 0 normal good, YED < 0 inferior good.
- **Cross elasticity of demand (XED)**: % change in Qd of good A / % change in P of good B. XED > 0 substitute, XED < 0 complement.
- **Price elasticity of supply (PES)**: % change in Qs / % change in P. PES > 1 elastic, PES < 1 inelastic.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing a shift of the demand curve (caused by a non-price factor) with a movement along the curve (caused by a price change).
- Forgetting to use absolute values when reporting PED.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Scarcity, choice and opportunity cost

**Scarcity**: unlimited wants + limited resources = choice required.

**Opportunity cost**: the value of the next-best alternative foregone. Example: if a student spends Saturday studying for economics, the opportunity cost is the value of the other activity (e.g. a part-time job) that they could have done instead.

**PPF**: a graphical representation of the trade-off between two goods. The PPF is concave to the origin (because of increasing opportunity cost — as more of one good is produced, more of the other must be sacrificed).

PPF movements:

- **Outward shift**: economic growth (more resources, better technology).
- **Inward shift**: economic decline (resource depletion, disaster).

PPF points:

- **On the curve**: efficient (all resources fully employed).
- **Inside the curve**: inefficient (unemployment or underutilisation).
- **Outside the curve**: unattainable with current resources.

#### Demand and supply

**Demand**: the relationship between the price of a good and the quantity demanded, ceteris paribus (other things equal).

**Non-price determinants of demand** (cause the demand curve to shift):

- Income (most goods: normal; inferior: inverse).
- Prices of related goods (substitutes, complements).
- Tastes and preferences.
- Expectations about future prices or income.
- Number of buyers.

**Law of demand**: as price increases, quantity demanded decreases (and vice versa). The demand curve slopes downward.

**Supply**: the relationship between the price of a good and the quantity supplied, ceteris paribus.

**Non-price determinants of supply** (cause the supply curve to shift):

- Input prices.
- Technology.
- Expectations about future prices.
- Number of sellers.
- Taxes and subsidies.
- Prices of related goods in production (substitutes in production, complements in production).

**Law of supply**: as price increases, quantity supplied increases (and vice versa). The supply curve slopes upward.

**Equilibrium**: the price at which Qd = Qs. The market clears; no shortage or surplus.

**Disequilibrium**:

- **Price above equilibrium**: surplus (Qs > Qd). Price falls toward equilibrium.
- **Price below equilibrium**: shortage (Qd > Qs). Price rises toward equilibrium.

#### Elasticity

**Price elasticity of demand (PED)**: |PED| = %ΔQd / %ΔP. Measures the responsiveness of quantity demanded to a change in price.

Determinants of PED: availability of substitutes (more substitutes → more elastic), necessity vs luxury (necessities → less elastic), share of income (larger share → more elastic), time horizon (longer → more elastic), definition of the market (narrower → more elastic).

Total revenue test: if demand is elastic, a price decrease raises total revenue; if demand is inelastic, a price decrease lowers total revenue; if demand is unit elastic, total revenue is unchanged.

**Income elasticity of demand (YED)**: YED = %ΔQd / %ΔY. YED > 1 luxury; 0 < YED < 1 normal necessity; YED < 0 inferior.

**Cross elasticity of demand (XED)**: XED = %ΔQd(A) / %ΔP(B). XED > 0 substitute; XED < 0 complement.

**Price elasticity of supply (PES)**: PES = %ΔQs / %ΔP. Determinants: availability of inputs, spare capacity, time horizon (longer → more elastic), mobility of factors, number of producers, storage possibilities.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-topic links

- **B. Firms and Production**: theory of the firm.
- **C. Market and Price**: market structures.
- **J. International Trade and Finance**: exchange rates and trade policy.

#### Exam technique

- **For PPF questions**: identify the trade-off; explain the slope; state what shifts cause inward or outward shifts.
- **For demand/supply questions**: identify whether the change is a shift or a movement; state the non-price determinant causing the shift.
- **For elasticity questions**: calculate using the formula; interpret the result (elastic, inelastic, unit elastic).

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "A change in demand is a movement along the curve." A change in demand is a SHIFT of the curve; a change in quantity demanded is a MOVEMENT along the curve.
- "Higher price means higher demand." Higher price means LOWER quantity demanded.
- "PED is always negative." PED is typically reported as an absolute value; the sign is implied.
