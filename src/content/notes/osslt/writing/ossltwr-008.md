# Selected-Response Writing Items — OSSLT Writing

The OSSLT selected-response Writing items test editing and revising skills: identifying the most effective sentence, the best word choice, the correct punctuation, and the most coherent paragraph order. These items sit alongside the long-writing tasks and short-writing tasks. The EQAO Framework describes them as multiple-select items that test the same conventions and language skills as the open-response writing scoring.

> Verify the live item count, item types, and any in-year changes on https://www.eqao.com/the-assessments/osslt/ before planning around the figures below.

---

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### What selected-response Writing items test

- **Effective sentences.** Identifying the most clear, concise, or grammatically correct sentence from a set of choices.
- **Word choice.** Identifying the best word for a specific context — usually a precise verb or noun replacing a vague one.
- **Punctuation.** Identifying the correct punctuation — comma, apostrophe, quotation mark, end punctuation.
- **Paragraph order.** Identifying the most coherent order of sentences in a paragraph.
- **Conventions.** Identifying the correct spelling, grammar, or punctuation for a specific context.

#### One-pass strategy for selected-response Writing items

1. Read the item stem carefully. Note what is being tested — sentence effectiveness, word choice, punctuation, paragraph order, or conventions.
2. If the item asks about sentence effectiveness, read each choice aloud. The most clear and concise choice is usually right.
3. If the item asks about word choice, substitute each choice in place of the original word. The choice that fits the context is right.
4. If the item asks about punctuation, recall the rule. The choice that follows the rule is right.
5. If the item asks about paragraph order, identify the topic sentence first. Then arrange the supporting sentences in logical order.

#### Examiner traps

- A choice that is grammatical but adds no information. The OSSLT scores effective sentences on clarity and conciseness, not just on grammar.
- A choice that changes the meaning. Word choice items ask for the best fit, not the most dramatic change.
- A punctuation choice that follows the rule but breaks the sentence's flow. Recount the rule; the choice that follows the rule AND keeps the sentence reading smoothly is right.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Strategy for sentence-effectiveness items

**Read each choice aloud.** The most clear and concise sentence is usually right. A choice that is wordy or vague is usually wrong.

**Look for the choice that fixes a specific problem.** If the original sentence has a run-on, the right choice is the one that splits the run-on into two sentences or joins the clauses with a semicolon.

**Look for the choice that uses a precise verb.** Vague verbs (walked, went, did) are weaker than precise verbs (sprinted, entered, accomplished). The choice with the precise verb is usually right.

#### Strategy for word-choice items

**Substitute each choice in place of the original word.** The choice that fits the context — the meaning of the sentence, the tone of the passage — is right.

**Consider connotation.** Two words can be similar in dictionary meaning but different in connotation (the feelings they evoke). The choice with the right connotation for the context is right.

#### Strategy for punctuation items

**Recall the rule.** Capital letter at the start of a sentence. Period at the end. Comma in a list. Comma after an introductory clause. Comma before a coordinating conjunction in a compound sentence. Apostrophe for possession.

**Apply the rule to each choice.** The choice that follows the rule is right.

#### Strategy for paragraph-order items

**Identify the topic sentence first.** The topic sentence states the paragraph's main idea. It is usually the first sentence in the right order.

**Arrange the supporting sentences in logical order.** The supporting sentences should flow from the topic sentence in a logical sequence — most important first, then supporting details, then wrap-up.

**Check the transitions.** The paragraph should have transitions between sentences ("for example," "in addition," "however"). The right order has the transitions in the right places.

#### Practice routine for selected-response Writing items

- Take one EQAO sample selected-response Writing item set. Time yourself on the items alone.
- Keep a personal "missed items" list. Note the item type (sentence effectiveness, word choice, punctuation, paragraph order) and the rule that was tested.
- Re-take the missed items the next day. Repeat until the items are consistently correct.
- Pair this work with practice on the long-writing tasks. The same conventions rules are tested in both.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How selected-response Writing items connect to the other OSSLT items

- **The conventions scoring** on the long-writing tasks uses the same rules tested in the selected-response Writing items. Practising selected-response items reinforces the conventions pass on long-writing tasks.
- **The language and vocabulary scoring** on the long-writing tasks uses the same word-choice and sentence-effectiveness skills tested in the selected-response items.

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"All choices are grammatical."** Sentence-effectiveness items ask for the most clear and concise, not just the most grammatical. The choice with the best word choice AND grammar is right.
- **"The longest choice is the most thorough."** Length without clarity scores lower than concise clarity. The shortest choice that is clear and complete is usually right.
- **"Punctuation is optional."** Punctuation is required. The choice that adds the required punctuation is right.

#### Exam technique

- Read the item stem carefully. Note what is being tested.
- Apply the rule or strategy for that item type.
- Eliminate choices that break the rule or do not fit the context.
- If two choices remain, read the original sentence with each choice substituted. Pick the choice that reads more smoothly.
- Use the platform's flag-for-review function. Do not leave blanks on selected-response items.
