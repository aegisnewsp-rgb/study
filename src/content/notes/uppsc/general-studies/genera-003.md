# Topic 3 — Indian Polity and Governance

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before the UPPSC RO/ARO exam.

Indian Polity in the UPPSC RO/ARO General Studies paper tests the **Constitution of India**: its Preamble, Parts, Schedules, Articles, amendments, and the institutional architecture of **Parliamentary Federalism**. The Preamble declares India a **Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic** committed to Justice, Liberty, Equality, and Fraternity — the words "Socialist" and "Integrity" were added by the **42nd Amendment (1976)**.

| Must-Remember Item | Article / Source |
|---|---|
| Fundamental Rights | Articles 12–35 |
| Directive Principles | Articles 36–51 (non-justiciable) |
| Fundamental Duties | Article 51A (42nd Amendment) |
| Writ jurisdiction | SC: Art. 32; HC: Art. 226 (wider) |
| Basic Structure Doctrine | Kesavananda Bharati, 1973 |

**High-yield pointers:** remember the **Seventh Schedule** (Union, State, Concurrent Lists), the **Tenth Schedule** (anti-defection, 52nd Amendment, 1985), and the **Ninth Schedule** (land reforms). Centre–state legislative relations are governed by **Articles 245–263**.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Constitutional Framework and Preamble
The **Constitution of India** came into force on **26 January 1950**. The **Preamble** is not enforceable in court (Berubari Union case, 1960) but is used to interpret ambiguities (Kesavananda Bharati, 1973). It states the source of authority ("We, the People of India"), the type of government, and the objectives.

#### Fundamental Rights, Duties, and Directive Principles
**Fundamental Rights (Part III, Articles 12–35)** are justiciable. The **Right to Property** was removed by the **44th Amendment (1978)** and made a legal right under Article 300A. **Directive Principles (Part IV, Articles 36–51)** are non-justiciable under **Article 37** but are "fundamental in governance." **Fundamental Duties** were added by the **Swaran Singh Committee** recommendation via the **42nd Amendment (1976)** under Article 51A — ten duties for citizens.

> **Trap:** Directive Principles are NOT enforceable by any court. They guide policy, while Fundamental Rights are legally enforceable.

#### Writ Jurisdiction Distinction
The **Supreme Court (Article 32)** can issue five writs — habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, certiorari, quo warranto — but only for Fundamental Rights. The **High Court (Article 226)** has wider scope: it can issue writs for Fundamental Rights **and** any other legal right, and extends to the entire territorial jurisdiction of the state.

#### Federal Structure and Centre–State Relations
India is a **quasi-federal** state with a strong unitary bias. **Articles 245–263** govern legislative relations through three lists in the **Seventh Schedule**:

| List | Subject Examples | Legislative Competence |
|---|---|---|
| **Union List** (100 subjects) | Defence, Atomic Energy, Foreign Affairs | Parliament only |
| **State List** (61 subjects) | Police, Public Order, Agriculture | State Legislature only |
| **Concurrent List** (52 subjects) | Criminal Law, Marriage, Education | Both; Centre prevails in conflict |

**Administrative relations** are guided by Articles 256–263, while **financial relations** rest on Articles 268–293, including the **Finance Commission (Article 280)**.

#### Parliamentary System and Constitutional Bodies
India follows the **Westminster model**: Prime Minister is the head of government, President is the constitutional head. The **Council of Ministers** is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha (Article 75). Key constitutional bodies include the **Election Commission (Art. 324)**, **CAG (Art. 148)**, **UPSC (Art. 315)**, and **Finance Commission (Art. 280)**.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Constitutional Amendment Procedure
**Article 368** prescribes three methods: by a simple majority (not covered under 368, e.g., creation of new states), by a special majority of Parliament (most amendments), and by a special majority ratified by at least half the state legislatures (federal amendments). The **basic structure doctrine** in *Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973)* held that Parliament cannot amend the "basic structure" of the Constitution, even using constituent power. Later rulings (*Minerva Mills, 1980*) reinforced the harmony between Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles.

#### Schedules of the Constitution — A Closer Look
The Constitution has **12 Schedules**. Frequently tested ones:

| Schedule | Subject | Added By |
|---|---|---|
| First | List of States and UTs with territories | Original |
| Second | Salaries, allowances, privileges of President, Governors, Speaker, Judges | Original |
| Seventh | Union, State, Concurrent Lists | Original |
| Eighth | Languages (originally 14, now 22 by 92nd Amendment) | Original / 92nd Amendment |
| Ninth | Acts protected from judicial review (land reforms) | 1st Amendment, 1951 |
| Tenth | Anti-defection law | 52nd Amendment, 1985 |
| Twelfth | Panchayat and Municipality provisions | 73rd & 74th Amendments |

#### Centre–State Edge Cases
The **President's Rule (Article 356)** is imposed on grounds of "breakdown of constitutional machinery," but after **S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994)**, it is subject to judicial review. The **Governor** acts as a constitutional link between Centre and State and has discretionary powers in a few specific areas, notably during hung assemblies.

> **Common mistake:** Conflating the Governor's "discretion" with a general power to act independently — discretionary powers are limited and exceptional, not routine.

#### Adjacent Topics to Revise

- **Writs:** Connect to **Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act (2013)**.
- **Federalism:** Link to the **Punchhi Commission (2010)** on Centre–State relations.

#### Practice Prompts
1. **Assertion–Reason:** "Directive Principles can override Fundamental Rights." — Critically evaluate with reference to *Minerva Mills (1980)* and Article 37.
2. **Identification:** Which Schedule contains the anti-defection law, and which constitutional amendment inserted it? Compare it with the Ninth Schedule's purpose.
