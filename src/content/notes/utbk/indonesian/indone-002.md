# Topic 2

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Topic 2 of Literasi Bahasa Indonesia (UTBK/SNPMTN)** measures *membaca dan menalar* — your ability to read passages and reason about them, not recall grammar rules. The passage types you'll meet are **narasi, eksposisi, deskripsi, argumentasi, prosedur,** and **persuasi**, each governed by a different **tujuan komunikatif** and **kaidah kebahasaan** (tense markers, pronomina, konjungsi, pilihan kata). The high-yield operations tested are: locating the **ide pokok** (kalimat utama in a **deduktif, induktif,** or **campuran** paragraph), distinguishing **fakta vs opini**, performing **inferensi** (makna tersirat), evaluating **koherensi** and **koesi**, and mapping information between **teks ↔ tabel/grafik/ringkasan**. Two traps to flag now: inductive paragraphs put the main idea *at the end*, and inference answers must go *beyond* the printed words.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Operations Tested

UTBK/SNPMTN passages for Topic 2 always arrive as a stimulus — typically 150–350 words — followed by 1–3 questions. The seven operations you must internalise:

1. **Locating the main idea.** Identify the *kalimat utama*. In a **deduktif** paragraph it sits in the opening sentence; in **induktif**, the closing sentence carries it; in **campuran**, both ends matter and the middle amplifies. Stripping examples, enumerations, and elaborations usually isolates the core claim.
2. **Structure and purpose of text type.** Each genre has a signature: **narasi** → orientasi–komplikasi–resolusi (uses past tense and temporal conjunctions *kemudian, akhirnya*); **eksposisi** → tesis–argumentasi–penegasan ulang; **deskripsi** → detail sensoris about object/subject; **argumentasi** → klaim–bukti–sanggahan; **prosedur** → tujuan–langkah–penutup (imperative verb forms); **persuasi** → ajakan with emotive diction.
3. **Fact vs opinion vs assumption.** A *fakta* is verifiable; an *opini* uses value/quality adjectives (baik, terbaik, harus); an *asumsi* is a taken-for-granted premise the author never proves.
4. **Inference.** The answer must be *implied* — neither stated verbatim nor contradicted. The correct choice is usually the one that requires a logical step beyond the literal text.
5. **Argument evaluation.** Check (a) evidence relevance, (b) internal consistency between paragraphs, (c) counter-argument handling, and (d) logical connectors (*karena, namun, oleh karena itu, dengan demikian*).
6. **Cross-text / cross-format translation.** Map prose to a **tabel**, **grafik batang**, or **ringkasan satu kalimat** — these appear every year.
7. **Diksi, majas, idiom, context clues.** Interpret figurative meaning (e.g., *kambing hitam*, *tulang punggung*) using surrounding context; pick **sinonim/antonim** by nuance, not surface word overlap.

#### Typical Question Stems

- *"Ide pokok paragraf ke-3 adalah…"* → main idea extraction.
- *"Penulis teks tersebut bertujuan untuk…"* → communicative purpose.
- *"Kata pada paragraf X yang bermakna paling dekat dengan…"* → synonym by context.
- *"Pendapat yang tidak didukung data pada teks adalah…"* → opinion spotting.
- *"Simpulan yang paling tepat tentang isi teks adalah…"* → inference.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Reasoning Mechanisms

**Mixed-genre passages.** UTBK frequently blends eksposisi with argumentasi, or prosedur embedded in deskripsi. Treat the *dominant* communicative purpose as the answer key for "tujuan penulis" questions; secondary features (e.g., emotive adjectives in a procedure) are stylistic, not structural.

**Implicit causality.** When a passage uses *akibatnya* or *hal ini menyebabkan*, the *cause* must precede or be inferable from the preceding clause. If a question asks the *cause* of an event mentioned only by consequence, reconstruct the antecedent by backward reference (pronoun resolution: *ia, mereka, hal tersebut*).

**Nuance in synonym items.** UTBK avoids dictionary synonyms; it tests *register* and *connotation*. Example: *giat* ≈ *rajin* (positive connotation), but *giat* ≠ *hebat* (different semantic field). When two options share a root word, the one matching the passage's *sense relation* (cause-effect, part-whole, means-purpose) is correct.

#### Common Mistakes That Cost Points

- Choosing the **first sentence** of an inductive paragraph as the main idea.
- Selecting an inference answer that merely **paraphrases** an explicit sentence.
- Matching by **keyword** alone ("energi" appears → pick the energy option) without checking logical fit.
- Confusing **opini** with **fakta** because the opinion *sounds* authoritative.

#### Connections to Other UTBK Areas

Topic 2 overlaps with **Penalaran Umum** (logical connective reasoning) and **Penalaran Matematika** when tables and graphs are translated from prose. Strong Topic 2 readers develop the same skills tested in **English-language reading comprehension** on the same exam — inferencing, stance detection, and paraphrase matching — so cross-domain practice compounds gains.

#### Practice Prompts

1. A 200-word eksposisi on renewable energy in Indonesia presents three arguments, then a closing paragraph containing *fakta* and *opini*. Write the **ide pokok** of the closing paragraph and label each sentence as F (fakta) or O (opini).
2. Given a procedure text with steps written out of order and 4 reorder options (A–D), identify the **konjungsi** signals of sequence and justify the correct order using *kaidah kebahasaan* of the prosedur genre.

#### Exam Strategy

Allocate ~90 seconds per Topic 2 item. Skim the *first and last* sentence of each paragraph first to lock in the **main-idea map** before reading details; this also pre-loads inference cues for later questions.
