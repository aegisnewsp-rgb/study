# Health Promotion and Patient Education

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Health promotion enables individuals and communities to act on determinants of health, while patient education is the bedside process of teaching skills, knowledge and behaviours needed for self-care. The DOH exam tests both within Medical-Knowledge (~3% of the paper).

- **Three levels of prevention**: primary stops disease before it starts (vaccination, smoking cessation); secondary detects disease early (mammography, HbA1c screening); tertiary limits complications in established disease (diabetic foot care, post-MI rehab).
- **Bloom's three domains** a patient-education plan must cover: cognitive (knowledge), affective (attitudes), psychomotor (skill) — teach-back verifies each.
- **Stages of Change** (Prochaska): precontemplation → contemplation → preparation → action → maintenance — match your counselling strategy to the patient's current stage.
- **OARS** (Open questions, Affirmations, Reflections, Summaries) is the working toolkit of motivational interviewing.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Definitions and Scope
**Health education** transfers information to a patient (leaflet, verbal advice). **Health promotion** is broader: it reshapes environments, policies and social conditions so healthy choices become easier, anchored by the WHO **Ottawa Charter (1986)**. Both are DOH clinical-governance expectations, not optional courtesies.

#### Prevention Framework
Apply the prevention level first, then choose the activity. A vaccine is primary; a screening test is secondary; an insulin-foot-care programme in a diabetic with neuropathy is tertiary. Misclassifying levels is a recurring MCQ trap.

#### Patient-Education Process
1. Assess learning needs, health literacy and language preference.
2. Set SMART objectives spanning all three Bloom domains.
3. Plan method (verbal, written, demonstration, pictogram, video).
4. Deliver using plain language, avoiding jargon.
5. Evaluate with the **teach-back method** — ask the patient to restate instructions in their own words.

#### Behaviour-Change Counselling
Match the intervention to the **Transtheoretical (Stages of Change) model**. In *precontemplation* use OARS to build discrepancy; in *preparation* co-create an action plan; in *maintenance* reinforce relapse prevention. **Motivational interviewing** with OARS resolves ambivalence without confrontation.

#### UAE-Specific Priorities
DOH/MOHAP campaigns (Weqaya, Fit-for-Life) emphasise diabetes prevention, obesity, tobacco cessation, mental-health awareness, maternal-child health and vaccine uptake. Materials must be culturally adapted for Arabic, English, Hindi, Urdu, Tagalog and Malayalam speakers.

| Concept | Key point |
| --- | --- |
| Primary prevention | Vaccination, smoking cessation, seatbelt counselling |
| Secondary prevention | HbA1c, mammography, FIT stool test, BP screening |
| Tertiary prevention | Foot care in diabetic neuropathy, post-MI cardiac rehab |
| Bloom — cognitive | Patient states the action of metformin |
| Bloom — psychomotor | Patient demonstrates insulin injection correctly |
| Bloom — affective | Patient accepts long-term dietary change |

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Common Traps
- **Education ≠ promotion.** A dietitian giving a carb-counting lecture is health *education*; a school removing sugar drinks is health *promotion*. MCQs test this distinction.
- **Teach-back is mandatory**, not optional — DOH documentation must show comprehension was verified, not just that information was given.
- **Health literacy** is the strongest modifier of outcome; low-literacy patients need pictograms, short sentences, and a family member present. A discharge summary in English-only is non-compliant for many UAE residents.
- **Domain mismatch**: writing a cognitive objective ("patient understands inhaler steps") when the clinical need is psychomotor ("patient demonstrates correct inhaler technique"). Always pick the domain that matches the goal.
- **Shared decision-making** sits between education and consent — explore values, present options, confirm preference, then document.

#### Worked Example (DOH-style MCQ)
A 54-year-old Emirati male with newly diagnosed Type 2 diabetes (HbA1c 8.9%) is referred for education. Best initial patient-education strategy?

1. Provide an English leaflet on carbohydrate counting. *(Wrong — ignores literacy/language and is passive.)*
2. Schedule a motivational-interviewing session to explore readiness before dietary change. *(Correct — matches stages-of-change, OARS-based.)*
3. Prescribe insulin and demonstrate injection technique on day one. *(Wrong — premature; education should precede escalation.)*
4. Tell him to lose 10 kg before review. *(Wrong — directive, no SMART objective, no teach-back.)*

The correct option addresses ambivalence first, then layers cognitive and psychomotor teaching.

#### Exam Strategy
Health-Promotion questions appear as short MCQs and integrated OSCE stations. Expect ~1–2 questions per DOH paper. Memorise the three prevention levels with one named example each, the five Ottawa Charter action areas, and the OARS acronym.

#### Practice Prompts
1. Classify each intervention — vaccination, colonoscopy, post-stroke physiotherapy — and justify the prevention level in one sentence.
2. Design a 10-minute teach-back session for a newly prescribed metered-dose inhaler, listing Bloom domains covered.
