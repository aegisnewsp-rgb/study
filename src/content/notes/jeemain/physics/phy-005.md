# Work Energy Power

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

- **Work** is the scalar product of force and displacement: **W = F·d·cosθ**, where θ is the angle between **F** and **d**. Unit: joule (J).
- **Work–Energy Theorem:** net work done on a body equals its change in kinetic energy, **W_net = ΔK = ½mv_f² − ½mv_i²**. It uses the *net* force, not individual forces.
- **Power** is the time-rate of doing work: **P = dW/dt = F·v·cosθ**. SI unit: watt (W = J/s); 1 hp ≈ 746 W.
- **Potential energy** is defined only for **conservative forces** (gravity, spring, electrostatic): U_grav = mgh, U_spring = ½kx² (x measured from natural length).
- Work done by the **centripetal force is always zero** because **F** ⊥ **v**.
- JEE Main: expect 1–2 questions/year, usually a numerical on W = ΔK, energy conservation, or spring problems.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Work and the Cosine Factor
Work is energy transferred by a force through a displacement. The sign of W is decided by cosθ: positive (0° ≤ θ < 90°), zero (θ = 90°), negative (90° < θ ≤ 180°). For a **variable force along a straight line**, W = ∫F·dx — geometrically, the signed area under the F–x graph. For a spring, integrating Hooke's law gives W_spring = ½k(x_i² − x_f²), so compressing a relaxed spring from 0 to x stores ½kx² of potential energy.

#### Work–Energy Theorem
For any particle, **W_net = ΔK**, where W_net is the work of the *resultant* force summed over the path. This is path-independent in its conclusion: it does not matter whether the forces are conservative or not, as long as the net work is computed correctly. A common JEE trap is to add W from each force and then forget the kinetic-energy term already includes them — pick one method and stick to it.

#### Conservative vs Non-Conservative Forces
A force is **conservative** if ∮F·dr = 0 around any closed loop, equivalently if W depends only on endpoints. Gravity and the spring force are conservative; kinetic friction and air drag are not. **Mechanical energy E = K + U is conserved** iff no non-conservative force does net work: ΔE = W_nc.

#### Power
**Average power** P_avg = W/Δt; **instantaneous power** P_inst = dW/dt = F·v·cosθ. For a vehicle climbing at constant speed, P = F_engine·v, so doubling speed doubles the power demand for the same driving force.

#### Quick Comparison

| Quantity | Formula | Path dependent? | Scalar/Vector |
|---|---|---|---|
| Work | F·d·cosθ / ∫F·dx | Yes (in general) | Scalar |
| Kinetic energy K | ½mv² | No | Scalar |
| Potential energy U | −∫F·dr (conservative) | No | Scalar |
| Power | dW/dt = F·v | — | Scalar |

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Subtle Points
- **Spring with a hanging mass:** the *natural-length* reference gives U = ½kx², but the *equilibrium-under-load* reference shifts zero by mg/k. JEE answers always use the natural-length reference unless told otherwise.
- **Work by gravity on inclined planes:** W = −mgh regardless of path length; only the vertical drop h matters. This is why a smooth curved ramp and a straight slide give the same K at the bottom.
- **Variable mass systems (rockets):** the work–energy theorem in its basic form assumes constant mass. For a rocket ejecting fuel at relative speed u, use the **variable-mass energy equation**, not plain ½mv².
- **Power delivered vs power dissipated:** for a car at constant v on a level road, engine power equals P = (F_drag)·v. If drag scales as v², then P ∝ v³ — a non-intuitive result examiners love.

#### Connections to Adjacent Topics
- **Circular motion:** centripetal force does zero work, so kinetic energy at constant speed is unchanged; only tangential forces change K.
- **Projectile motion:** at the same height, speed is identical on the way up and down because gravity is conservative.
- **Thermodynamics link:** W_nc = ΔE_mech is the mechanical-energy balance; in first-law form it becomes ΔU_int = Q − W (sign convention dependent).

#### Common Mistakes (exam traps)
- Writing W = F·d when **F** and **d** are not collinear — must include cosθ.
- Using W = ½mv² with v² as a vector or averaging speeds.
- Forgetting that P = F·v requires v along F; otherwise insert cosθ.
- Sign error on spring PE during *decompression* — released spring does +½kx² of work on the block.

#### Worked Micro-Example
A 2 kg block is pushed 5 m up a 30° incline (μ = 0.2) by a horizontal force F = 30 N starting from rest.
- Component of F along incline: 30·cos30° ≈ 25.98 N (up the plane).
- Weight component along incline: 2·9.8·sin30° = 9.8 N (down).
- Friction: μ·N = 0.2·(2·9.8·cos30° + 30·sin30°) ≈ 0.2·(16.97 + 15) = 6.39 N.
- Net force along plane: 25.98 − 9.8 − 6.39 ≈ 9.79 N.
- W_net = 9.79 × 5 ≈ 48.95 J ⇒ v_f = √(2·48.95/2) ≈ 6.99 m/s.

#### Practice Prompts
1. A spring (k = 200 N/m) compressed 10 cm releases a 0.5 kg block on a frictionless surface — find v at natural length.
2. An engine delivers 5 kW to keep a 1000 kg car at 20 m/s on a level road — find the total resistive force.
