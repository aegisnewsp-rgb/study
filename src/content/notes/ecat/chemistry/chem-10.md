# Periodic Properties and s-Block Elements

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

- **Effective nuclear charge (Z_eff)** is the net positive charge felt by a valence electron after inner-shell shielding; it rises **left → right** across a period and stays almost constant **top → bottom** in a group.
- **Atomic radius** decreases across a period and increases down a group. **Ionic radius**: cations < parent atom < anions; among **isoelectronic** species (e.g. Na⁺, Mg²⁺, Al³⁺), size shrinks as nuclear charge rises.
- **First ionization energy (IE₁)** increases across a period (anomalies at Be→B and N→O) and decreases down a group; successive IEs jump sharply once a noble-gas core is reached.
- **s-Block** = **Group 1 (alkali metals, ns¹)** and **Group 2 (alkaline earth metals, ns²)**. They are highly electropositive, form **+1 / +2** cations, and give **ionic** compounds.
- ECAT Chemistry carries ~3 % weight on this cluster; expect 1–2 direct MCQs on trends and flame-test/solubility reasoning.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Periodic Trends — the Underlying Drivers
Across a period, protons are added to the same shell, so shielding by inner electrons changes little while **Z_eff rises**, pulling electrons closer. Down a group, an extra shell is added each row, increasing the principal quantum number *n* and the **distance** of valence electrons from the nucleus, which dominates over the rise in nuclear charge.

- **Atomic / ionic radius**: decreases L→R, increases top→bottom. For an isoelectronic series (e.g. N³⁻, O²⁻, F⁻, Na⁺, Mg²⁺, Al³⁺), radius ∝ 1/Z (more protons pulling the same electron count → smaller ion).
- **Ionization energy (IE)**: IE ∝ Z_eff / r. Rises L→R with two breaks — **Be→B** (B's 2p electron is higher in energy and shielded by 2s²) and **N→O** (paired electron in 2p³ vs 2p⁴ repulsion). Decreases down the group.
- **Electron affinity (EA)**: becomes more negative L→R; less negative down the group. Breaks at filled (Be, Mg) and half-filled (N, P) subshells.
- **Electronegativity (Pauling)**: χ ∝ (IE + EA)/2 — increases L→R, decreases down the group.

#### s-Block Chemistry at a Glance
**Group 1 (Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs)** and **Group 2 (Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba)** are soft, low-melting metals with low density (Li, Na, K even float on water). Their single/double valence s-electrons are loosely held, giving:

| Property | Group 1 | Group 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Oxidation state | +1 | +2 |
| Hydration of cation | High (small Li⁺, Be²⁺ strongest) | High (Mg²⁺ notable) |
| Oxide / hydroxide basicity | Strongly basic | Basic, less so for Be |
| Flame colour | Li crimson, Na yellow, K lilac, Rb/Cs bluish | Ca brick-red, Sr crimson, Ba apple-green |

#### Solubility — Lattice vs Hydration
Salt solubility is decided by **U** (lattice energy) versus **ΔH_hyd** (hydration enthalpy), via the Born–Haber cycle. **U ∝ (Z⁺·Z⁻)/(r⁺ + r⁻)** and **ΔH_hyd ∝ −(Z⁺)²/r⁺**. A compound is soluble when the hydration release overcomes the lattice cost. That's why **LiF is sparingly soluble** (very high U from small ions) while **LiClO₄ is soluble** (large anion, lower U).

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Anomalies That Examiners Love
- **IE₂ of Li is very high** because removing the second electron breaks a noble-gas core — this is why Li never forms Li²⁺. Same logic explains why **Be shows fixed +2** (no Be⁺ chemistry in solution) and why Group 2 has **no variable valency** in contrast to d-block metals.
- **Diagonal relationship**: **Li ↔ Mg** and **Be ↔ Al** behave alike because of similar charge/size ratio (charge density). Examples: LiCl and MgCl₂ are both covalent/soluble in organic solvents; Be(OH)₂ and Al(OH)₃ are **amphoteric**, unlike the other Group 2 hydroxides.
- **Hydration enthalpy of Li⁺ (≈ −519 kJ mol⁻¹)** exceeds that of Na⁺ (≈ −406), which is why Li salts of small anions (F⁻, OH⁻) and large anions alike can be soluble or not — track the **charge density** of the ion, not the metal alone.

#### Flame Test Logic
The alkali/alkaline-earth cations are heated in a Bunsen flame; their loosely held valence electrons are excited to a higher level and emit light of characteristic wavelength on return (ΔE = hν). This is a qualitative test, not quantitative — examiners often ask: *"Which metal ion gives a brick-red flame?"* → **Ca²⁺**.

#### Worked Micro-Example
Compare **solubility of MgSO₄ vs BaSO₄**. Both are Group 2 sulphates, but BaSO₄ is *insoluble* while MgSO₄ is *soluble*. Reason: Ba²⁺ is much larger than Mg²⁺, so the lattice energy of BaSO₄ (with a large anion) drops faster than its hydration enthalpy, making ΔH_sol positive (K_sp of BaSO₄ ≈ 1.1 × 10⁻¹⁰).

#### ECAT Strategy
- 1–2 MCQs from this topic per paper; pair with **Chemical Bonding** and **p-Block** MCQs.
- Memorise **direction of trend** (L→R / top→bottom) for radius, IE, EA, EN — examiners word reverses ("which has the *lowest* IE in Period 2") to trap rote learners.
- For s-block MCQs, learn the **cation charge** (+1 / +2) and the **flame colour** table above; one direct question is almost guaranteed.

#### Common Mistakes
- Saying "ionization energy increases down the group" — it **decreases**.
- Confusing **electron gain enthalpy sign convention** (more negative = more energy released = greater EA).
- Assuming all Group 2 hydroxides are strongly basic — **Be(OH)₂ is amphoteric** and dissolves in NaOH.
- Forgetting that **Na₂O₂ is a peroxide**, not a normal oxide; it gives H₂O₂ with water.

#### Practice Prompts
1. Arrange Na⁺, Mg²⁺, Al³⁺, Si⁴⁺ in order of increasing ionic radius and justify using Z_eff.
2. Explain, with a Born–Haber argument, why **BeCO₃ decomposes more easily than BaCO₃**.
