# Cognition, Memory and Language — MCAT Psych/Soc

Cognition, memory, and language form the largest cluster of AAMC Foundational Concept 6 in the Psych/Soc section. The MCAT tests information processing, encoding, storage and retrieval, working memory versus long-term memory, forgetting, language production and comprehension, problem solving, decision making, and judgement under uncertainty. These topics underpin nearly every cognitive and clinical passage on the exam.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### Core concepts

- **Information processing:** input (sensation), processing (perception, attention, memory), output (response).
- **Encoding:** converting sensory input into a memory. Types: acoustic, visual, semantic.
- **Storage:** maintaining encoded information. Three stores: sensory memory, short-term/working memory, long-term memory.
- **Retrieval:** accessing stored information. Recall (no cues) versus recognition (with cues).
- **Forgetting:** decay (time), interference (proactive, retroactive), motivated forgetting, retrieval failure.
- **Working memory:** limited capacity (~7 items); actively manipulates information. Baddeley's model includes phonological loop, visuospatial sketchpad, central executive.
- **Long-term memory:** explicit (declarative: episodic, semantic) and implicit (procedural, classical conditioning).
- **Language:** production (Broca's area) and comprehension (Wernicke's area) in the brain.

#### One-pass drill

1. Identify the topic: encoding, storage, retrieval, or language.
2. Recall the relevant principle or structure.
3. Apply to the specific scenario.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing recall and recognition. Recall has no cues; recognition has cues.
- Treating all long-term memory as declarative. Procedural memory (skills) is implicit.
- Forgetting that interference is a common cause of forgetting.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Memory in detail

**Encoding:** the first step in forming a memory. Levels of processing (shallow versus deep) affect retention. Elaborative rehearsal (linking new info to existing knowledge) is more effective than maintenance rehearsal (repetition).

**Storage:** three stores differ in capacity and duration:
- Sensory memory: very brief (~250 ms for visual icons; ~2 s for auditory echoes).
- Short-term/working memory: ~7 items; ~15-30 s without rehearsal.
- Long-term memory: essentially unlimited capacity and duration.

**Retrieval:**
- Recall: retrieving information without cues (e.g., essay exam).
- Recognition: identifying information from a list (e.g., multiple-choice exam).
- Retrieval cues improve recall (encoding specificity principle).

**Forgetting:**
- Decay: memory fades with time.
- Interference: proactive (old info interferes with new) and retroactive (new info interferes with old).
- Motivated forgetting: repressing unwanted memories (controversial).
- Retrieval failure: information is stored but not accessible without the right cues.

**Memory errors:** misinformation effect, source amnesia, confabulation, false memories. Eyewitness testimony is particularly vulnerable to memory errors.

#### Language in detail

**Broca's area:** left frontal lobe. Involved in language production. Damage causes Broca's aphasia (non-fluent, effortful speech).

**Wernicke's area:** left temporal lobe. Involved in language comprehension. Damage causes Wernicke's aphasia (fluent but meaningless speech).

**Language development:** stages (cooing, babbling, one-word, two-word, telegraphic, full sentences). Critical period for language acquisition.

#### Problem solving and decision making

**Problem solving:** algorithms (step-by-step, guarantee a solution) versus heuristics (shortcuts, may fail). Common heuristics: availability (judging by ease of recall), representativeness (judging by similarity to a prototype), anchoring (relying on the first piece of information).

**Decision making:** expected value (rational); prospect theory (people avoid losses more than they seek gains). Framing effects (the same choice presented differently leads to different decisions).

**Judgement under uncertainty:** overconfidence, confirmation bias, hindsight bias.

#### Practice routine

- Take 5 encoding items. Identify the level of processing and the rehearsal type.
- Take 5 memory items. Identify the store, the retrieval type, and the cause of forgetting.
- Take 5 language items. Identify the brain area (Broca's, Wernicke's) and the aphasia type.
- Take 5 decision items. Identify the heuristic or bias.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How this topic connects to the other Psych/Soc topics

- **Learning** depends on memory (encoding and retrieval of learned associations).
- **Development** shapes cognitive abilities (Piaget's stages).
- **Biology of behaviour** provides the neural basis of memory and language.

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"Working memory and short-term memory are different."** Modern usage treats them as the same; Baddeley's working memory model is the standard.
- **"Repression is a well-established cause of forgetting."** Motivated forgetting (repression) is controversial; interference and decay are better-established.
- **"Heuristics are always wrong."** Heuristics are useful shortcuts but can lead to systematic biases.

#### Exam technique

- For memory items, identify the store (sensory, short-term, long-term) and the process (encoding, storage, retrieval).
- For language items, identify the brain area and the aphasia symptoms.
- For decision items, identify the heuristic or bias.
