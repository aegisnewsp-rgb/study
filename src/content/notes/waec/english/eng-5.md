# Active and Passive Voice

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Active voice places the **doer (subject)** before the **action (verb)**: *The chef (S) cooked (V) the meal (O)*. Passive voice reverses this focus, placing the **receiver** before the verb and using a form of **be** + **past participle (V3)**: *The meal was cooked by the chef*. Only **transitive verbs** (verbs that take a direct object) can be passivised; intransitive verbs like *arrive*, *happen*, and *sleep* have no passive form.

| Tense in Active | Active Form | Passive Form |
|---|---|---|
| Present simple | cooks | is/am/are cooked |
| Past simple | cooked | was/were cooked |
| Present perfect | has cooked | has/have been cooked |
| Future simple | will cook | will be cooked |
| Modal | can cook | can be cooked |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"SUBJECT-VERB-OBJECT → BE+V3+BY"** — think **SVO → B3B**. The Subject of the active becomes the agent after *by*, the Object becomes the new Subject, and the Verb becomes *be + past participle*. Also remember **"I-T cannot pass"**: Intransitive verbs Take no passive form.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Transformation Rule

To convert an active sentence into a passive one, follow this ordered procedure:
1. Identify the **transitive verb** and the **direct object**; intransitive verbs stop the process.
2. Move the **object** to the **subject position**.
3. Insert the correct form of **be** that matches the original tense.
4. Change the main verb to its **past participle (V3)**.
5. Add the original subject as the agent with **by**, or omit *by-phrase* when the agent is unknown, obvious, or unimportant.

Example: *The students (S) are writing (V) the letters (O).* → *The letters are being written by the students.* Notice how the auxiliary *are* is followed by *being* + *V3* to preserve the **present continuous** sense.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix

| Feature | Active Voice | Passive Voice |
|---|---|---|
| Word order | Subject → Verb → Object | Subject → be + V3 → by + Agent |
| Emphasis | On the doer (agent) | On the action or receiver |
| Length | Usually shorter | Usually longer |
| Verb requirement | Any verb (transitive or intransitive) | Only transitive verbs |
| Typical use | Narrative, conversation, instructions | Reports, scientific writing, formal notices |
| Agent mention | Mandatory (implied subject) | Optional via by-phrase |

#### Reporting Passive Structures

Reporting verbs (*say, believe, think, know, report, allege, consider*) take a special passive pattern:

- *People say that he is rich.* → *It is said that he is rich.* (introductory *it*)
- *People say that he is rich.* → *He is said to be rich.* (infinitive form)
- *People believe she won the election.* → *She is believed to have won the election.* (perfect infinitive for past reporting)

> 📌 **Formula Check:** Reporting passive uses **subject + reporting verb in passive + to-infinitive**. Tense of the infinitive reflects when the reported action occurred: *to be* (present), *to have been* (past).

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** Rewrite the following sentences in the passive voice, keeping the tense unchanged:
(a) *The gardener is watering the flowers.*
(b) *They have completed the bridge.*
(c) *People believe that the witness saw the thief.*

#### Solution:
(a) Identify SVO: *The gardener (S) is watering (V) the flowers (O)*. The auxiliary *is* signals present continuous, so the passive needs *is being + V3*: **The flowers are being watered by the gardener.**
(b) Identify SVO: *They (S) have completed (V) the bridge (O)*. *Have completed* is present perfect active, so passive is *have/has been + V3*: **The bridge has been completed (by them).** *By them* is awkward, so omit it.
(c) Reporting passive: subject *the witness*, reporting verb *saw*, so move to *to have seen*: **The witness is believed to have seen the thief.**

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Candidates commonly write *"The flowers is being watered"* (subject-verb agreement failure) and *"The witness is believed to saw the thief"* (using a bare past tense instead of the perfect infinitive *to have seen*). Always match *be* to the new subject, and shift the reported verb to a *to-infinitive*.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Special Constructions

| Construction | Active Form | Passive Form | Key Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Imperative | *Open the door.* | *Let the door be opened.* | Use *let + object + be + V3* |
| Modal passive | *You must submit the form.* | *The form must be submitted.* | *modal + be + V3* |
| Causative | *She cut her hair.* | *She had her hair cut.* | *have/get + object + V3* |
| Gerundive passive | *We watched him interview the star.* | *We watched his being interviewed by the star.* | *possessive + being + V3* |
| Stative passive | *The window is broken.* | (no agent) | Describes a state, not an action |

#### Common Advanced Traps

1. **Intransitive verbs disguised as transitive:** *The accident happened* cannot become *The accident was happened*. Verbs of occurrence (*happen, occur, take place, erupt*) never passivise.
2. **Verb agreement after tense change:** When the active subject is plural and the new passive subject is singular, the *be* auxiliary must agree — *The jury gave the verdict* → *The verdict was given by the jury* (not *were*).
3. **Causative vs perfect confusion:** *I had eaten the food* (perfect tense, I ate it myself) is structurally different from *I had the food eaten* (causative, someone else ate it for me).
4. **Double passive error:** Avoid stacking two passive auxiliaries — write *The work ought to be finished*, not ~~*The work ought to be being finished*.~~

#### Connections to Adjacent Topics

Active and Passive Voice intersects with **Tense Consistency** (Paper 2 oral items), **Sentence Transformation** (guided rewrite questions), and **Essay Style** (Paper 3), where passive constructions lend objectivity to formal and report-style writing. Mastering reporting passives also feeds directly into **Reported Speech** questions, because both manipulate verb complementation.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. Convert the following paragraph into passive voice, retaining tense: *The company will announce the results tomorrow. The board has approved the proposal. Investors are expecting good news.*
2. Identify and correct the error in: *"The children were played in the park all afternoon."*

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** WAEC markers penalise sentences where the **be auxiliary** fails to agree with the new subject — *The man were arrested* (should be *was*). In a 40-objective Paper 2 set, two to three items per year test subject-verb agreement inside passive transformations.
