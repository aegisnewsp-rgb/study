# Sensation, Perception and Attention — MCAT Psych/Soc

Sensation, perception, and attention form part of AAMC Foundational Concept 6 of the Psych/Soc section. The MCAT tests sensory transduction, thresholds, signal detection theory, the visual and auditory systems, perceptual organisation, attention, and consciousness. These topics connect psychology to neurobiology and underpin many passages on sensory processing and disorders.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### Core concepts

- **Sensation:** detection of physical stimuli (light, sound, pressure, chemicals) by sensory receptors.
- **Transduction:** conversion of a physical stimulus into a neural signal.
- **Absolute threshold:** the minimum stimulus intensity needed to detect a stimulus 50% of the time.
- **Difference threshold (just noticeable difference, JND):** the minimum change in stimulus intensity needed to detect a difference 50% of the time. Weber's law: JND = k × I (the JND is proportional to the stimulus intensity).
- **Signal detection theory:** detection depends on signal strength, background noise, and the observer's criterion (response bias).
- **Visual system:** cornea, lens, retina (rods for dim light; cones for colour). Optic nerve; lateral geniculate nucleus; visual cortex.
- **Auditory system:** outer ear, middle ear, cochlea (organ of Corti, hair cells), auditory nerve.
- **Attention:** selective (focus on one stimulus), divided (focus on multiple), sustained (maintain focus over time).

#### One-pass drill

1. Identify the topic: sensation, perception, or attention.
2. Recall the relevant principle or structure.
3. Apply to the specific scenario.

#### Examiner traps

- Treating sensation and perception as the same. Sensation is the detection of stimuli; perception is the interpretation.
- Confusing rods and cones. Rods detect dim light (no colour); cones detect colour (bright light).
- Treating Weber's law as universal. Weber's law applies to many but not all sensory dimensions.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Sensation in detail

**Visual:** light passes through the cornea and lens, focuses on the retina. Photoreceptors (rods and cones) transduce light into neural signals. Bipolar cells transmit to ganglion cells; ganglion cell axons form the optic nerve. The fovea has the highest density of cones (sharp central vision). The blind spot is where the optic nerve exits the retina (no photoreceptors).

**Auditory:** sound waves enter the outer ear, vibrate the tympanic membrane, are amplified by the ossicles (malleus, incus, stapes) in the middle ear, and travel through the oval window into the cochlea. The organ of Corti contains hair cells that transduce sound vibrations into neural signals.

**Other senses:** somatosensation (touch, pressure, temperature, pain via mechanoreceptors, thermoreceptors, nociceptors); gustation (taste buds on the tongue, five basic tastes); olfaction (olfactory receptors in the nasal epithelium).

#### Perception in detail

**Perceptual organisation:** Gestalt principles (proximity, similarity, closure, continuity, figure-ground). The brain organises sensory input into meaningful wholes.

**Depth perception:** binocular cues (retinal disparity, convergence) and monocular cues (linear perspective, interposition, texture gradient, motion parallax, relative size).

**Perceptual constancy:** size constancy (objects appear the same size despite distance); shape constancy; brightness constancy.

#### Attention in detail

**Selective attention:** focusing on one stimulus while ignoring others. The cocktail party effect is the ability to attend to one conversation in a noisy room.

**Divided attention:** focusing on multiple stimuli at once. Performance decreases as task complexity increases.

**Sustained attention:** maintaining focus over time. Vigilance tasks test sustained attention.

#### Practice routine

- Take 5 sensation items. Identify the sensory receptor and the transduction pathway.
- Take 5 perception items. Identify the Gestalt principle or the depth cue.
- Take 5 attention items. Distinguish selective, divided, and sustained attention.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How this topic connects to the other Psych/Soc topics

- **Cognition, memory and language** depend on attention (selective attention filters information entering memory).
- **Biology of behaviour** provides the neural basis of sensation and perception.
- **Psychopathology** includes sensory and perceptual disorders (e.g., schizophrenia, autism spectrum disorder).

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"The eye detects colour with rods."** Rods detect dim light (no colour); cones detect colour in bright light.
- **"Selective attention means ignoring everything else."** Selective attention focuses on one stimulus but does not fully suppress others (e.g., hearing your name in a noisy room).
- **"Weber's law is exact for all senses."** Weber's law is approximate; the constant k varies by sensory dimension.

#### Exam technique

- For sensation items, identify the receptor type and the pathway.
- For perception items, identify the Gestalt principle or the depth cue.
- For attention items, identify the type of attention (selective, divided, sustained).
- Use the platform's calculator for any arithmetic.
