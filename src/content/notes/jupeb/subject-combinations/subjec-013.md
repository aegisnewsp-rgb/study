# Arts Subject Combinations for JUPEB

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Three subjects. One must match the course. The other two fill the rule.

JUPEB (Joint Universities Preliminary Examinations Board) awards an Advanced-Level certificate equivalent to A-Level. Every candidate sits exactly three (3) subjects over one academic year, scored on an A–F scale where grades carry point values. For Arts applicants, those three subjects come from a published pool of Arts and approved Social Science disciplines.

| Approved Arts subjects | Typical point values |
|---|---|
| Literature-in-English, Government, History, CRS, IRS, Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa, Music, Fine and Applied Arts / Visual Arts, French, Arabic | A1=5, B2=4, B3=3, C4=2, C5=1 (varies by affiliate) |
| Allowed Social Science substitutes | Economics, Geography, Commerce, Accounting, Business Studies (where the affiliate permits) |

The Direct Entry (DE) screening point is at least **16 total points**, with no D (or lower) grade in any of the three subjects. Without meeting both thresholds, the certificate is unusable for admission.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** "**One-Course, Two-Support, Three-Total**" — one subject must be course-mandatory, two are fillers from the approved Arts/Social Science list, and the total sitting is exactly three.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> The combination is locked at registration. A wrong choice is not corrected by a high score.

#### How the combination rule works

JUPEB does not let a candidate "pick any three." The combination is governed by three nested filters. First, all three subjects must sit inside the approved Arts/Social Science pool listed in the JUPEB examination-subjects register. Second, at least one of the three must be the **principal (course-mandatory) subject** for the intended degree (e.g., Literature-in-English for Mass Communication, History for History/International Studies, CRS for Christian Religious Studies). Third, the receiving affiliate university must accept the resulting combination against its own DE brochure — JUPEB centrally does not endorse a combination, the university does.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix

| Combination style | Example trio | Where it works | Where it fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pure Arts (classic) | Government, Literature-in-English, CRS | Law at most affiliates, English, Linguistics | Mass Communication (needs Lit-in-Eng), History (needs History) |
| Pure Arts (linguistic) | Yoruba, Government, CRS | B.A. Yoruba, B.A. Linguistics (where accepted) | Mass Communication, Theatre Arts, History |
| Arts + Social Science | Economics, Government, Literature-in-English | Public Administration, Economics-adjacent Arts, Political Science | Pure Humanities (English, History) if Social Science is rejected |
| Language-led | French, Government, History | B.A. French, International Relations | Communication degrees that require Lit-in-Eng |

#### What "course-mandatory" means in practice

- **Mass Communication** → Literature-in-English is compulsory; the other two come from Government, CRS, IRS, History, Economics, Yoruba/Igbo/Hausa (per affiliate).
- **Law** → Government + any two from Literature-in-English, CRS, IRS, History, Economics (university-specific).
- **History / International Studies** → History is compulsory.
- **Theatre Arts / Music / Fine and Applied Arts** → the matching arts subject is compulsory (Theatre typically Lit-in-Eng + two Arts; Music → Music + two Arts; Fine Art → Fine and Applied Arts + two Arts).
- **CRS / IRS / Religious Studies** → the matching religious studies subject is compulsory.

#### Worked scenario

A candidate wants Mass Communication at a UNILAG-style affiliate. They register **Government, CRS, History** — three clean Arts subjects, all approved. At DE screening, the panel flags the combination: Mass Communication at this affiliate requires Literature-in-English as a principal subject. The candidate's certificate is valid (≥16 points, no D grade), but the combination is **ineligible** for the chosen course. The fix is to swap History for Literature-in-English, giving **Government, CRS, Literature-in-English**, which satisfies the principal-subject rule while staying inside the approved pool.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Screening officers reject a valid certificate when the combination does not list the course-mandatory subject — the score is irrelevant once the trio is misaligned. Always cross-check the affiliate's JUPEB brochure before paying acceptance fees.

#### Point thresholds in plain English

| Component | Minimum | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Total points across 3 subjects | 16 | Below this, no DE offer is issued, regardless of combination |
| Grade per subject | At least C (pass) | A D-grade in any one subject disqualifies the certificate |
| Subjects sat | Exactly 3 | Registering 2 or 4 is an administrative error and voids the sitting |

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

#### Edge cases that derail otherwise strong candidates

Language subjects — Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa, French, Arabic — sit inside the approved Arts pool, but they only function as the **principal** subject for a B.A. in that specific language. A candidate adding Hausa as a "balanced" third subject for Mass Communication is screened out, because Hausa is not on the affiliate's Mass Communication principal list and does not substitute for Literature-in-English.

Social Science substitutes behave asymmetrically. Economics and Geography are widely accepted as a third subject for Political Science, Public Administration, and some Law combinations, but Mathematics is rejected by most Arts-degree programmes despite sitting inside the broader JUPEB pool. Commerce, Accounting, and Business Studies are accepted only by a handful of affiliates — treating them as universal substitutes is the second-most common cause of DE rejection.

University autonomy is the deepest trap. JUPEB publishes the approved subject pool, but each affiliate publishes its own course-combination list. A combination valid at UNIJOS may be rejected at UNILAG for the same course. Candidates who register without consulting the receiving university's JUPEB brochure take the largest avoidable risk in the entire application cycle.

#### Advanced traps and exceptions

1. **Five-vs-three confusion.** WASSCE requires 5–9 subjects including English and Mathematics. JUPEB requires exactly 3. Candidates who treat JUPEB as another WASSCE register fewer subjects than they need elsewhere or carry WASSCE habits into JUPEB registration.
2. **Visual Arts vs Fine and Applied Arts.** The official JUPEB title is "Fine and Applied Arts / Visual Arts." Writing only "Visual Arts" on the form can mismatch the affiliate's database column and delay processing.
3. **CRS/IRS separation.** A CRS candidate cannot use IRS to satisfy the compulsory subject for a CRS degree, and vice versa. The religious-studies subject must match the degree's own prefix.
4. **Re-sit invalidation.** Sitting a fourth subject later to "upgrade" is not how JUPEB works; the original trio on the registration slip is the trio that is scored for the certificate.
5. **Score-blind rejection.** A candidate scoring 19 points with the wrong combination still loses the admission. Combinations are screened before points are read.
6. **Late brochure change.** Affiliates occasionally revise combinations mid-cycle. The brochure version stamped at registration is the version that governs the screening.

#### Connections to adjacent topics

- **Direct Entry (DE) admission rules** — JUPEB feeds directly into DE; the combination determines whether the candidate qualifies for DE at all.
- **JAMB subject combinations** — DE universities apply both JAMB and JUPEB rules; mismatches between the two are a separate but related screening layer.
- **A-Level equivalence** — JUPEB sits alongside Cambridge A-Level, IJMB, and NABTEB A-Level as a recognised A-Level route; combinations differ across these boards.

#### Two advanced prompts for self-testing

1. A candidate wants to study Christian Religious Studies at UNN and registers Government, CRS, Economics. The score is A1, B2, B3 (total 12 points — below the 16-point floor). Identify **two distinct failures** in this application: one in the combination, one in the scoring.
2. Compare two applicants both targeting Law at the same affiliate. Applicant A picks Government, Literature-in-English, CRS. Applicant B picks Government, Economics, History. Both score 18 points with no D grade. Explain why the affiliate's screening panel may treat them differently, even though both trios sit inside the approved Arts/Social Science pool.
