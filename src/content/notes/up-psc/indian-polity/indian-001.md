# Preamble and Salient Features of Constitution

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The **Preamble** is the introductory statement of the Constitution of India outlining its aims, philosophy, and the source of its authority. Drafted from **Nehru's Objectives Resolution** of 13 December 1946, it was adopted on **26 November 1949** and came into force on **26 January 1950**.

- **Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic** — India declares itself all five.
- **Justice** (social, economic, political), **Liberty**, **Equality**, **Fraternity** — the four guarantees to every citizen.
- 42nd Amendment (1976) added **Socialist**, **Secular**, and **Integrity**; confirmed as part of the Constitution in **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)**.

| --- | --- |
| Moved by | Jawaharlal Nehru |
| Adopted | 26 November 1949 |
| Commencement | 26 January 1950 |
| Amended by | 42nd Amendment, 1976 |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Meaning and Status of the Preamble

The Preamble declares India as a **Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic**. **Sovereignty** means supreme internal and external authority; **Socialist** and **Secular** were inserted by the 42nd Amendment, 1976. **Democratic** indicates rule by the people through adult suffrage; **Republic** means the **President** is elected and not a hereditary monarch.

#### Justice, Liberty, Equality, Fraternity

The Preamble secures to every citizen **Justice** in social, economic, and political dimensions, **Liberty** of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship, **Equality** of status and opportunity, and **Fraternity** assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the Nation.

#### Judicial Position

In the **Berubari Union case (1960)**, the Supreme Court held the Preamble is **not a part of the Constitution** and is therefore not enforceable. In **Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973)**, the bench overruled Berubari and declared the Preamble **is a part of the Constitution** and an interpretive key. **LIC of India v. Consumer Education and Research Centre (1995)** reaffirmed this position.

| Case | Holding |
| --- | --- |
| Berubari Union (1960) | Preamble is not a part of the Constitution |
| Kesavananda Bharati (1973) | Preamble is part of the Constitution and the basic structure |
| LIC v. CERC (1995) | Preamble guides interpretation of statutes |

#### Amendability

The Preamble can be amended under **Article 368**, but any amendment must not destroy the **basic structure**. So far only the 42nd Amendment (1976) has altered its text.

#### High-Yield Pointers

- Preamble is the **source of constitutional authority**, not a source of legislative power.
- Original authorship traces to the **Objectives Resolution** moved by Nehru.
- Fraternity clause uniquely blends individual dignity with national unity.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Sources of the Preamble and Constitution

The substantive text rests on **Nehru's Objectives Resolution of 13 December 1946**, later refined by the **Drafting Committee's Sub-Committee on Fundamental Rights and Minorities**. Many constitutional scholars treat the American Declaration of Independence, the Irish Constitution's Directive Principles, and the Australian Constitution's referendum clauses as structural influences on the Preamble's wording.

#### Salient Features of the Constitution

The framers drew provisions from multiple jurisdictions, producing a **synthesis of rigidity and flexibility** with three amendment methods: by simple majority, by special majority, and by special majority with ratification by half the state legislatures. India is a **federal system with a unitary bias** — emergency provisions, a single integrated judiciary, and a common All-India Services tilt the balance toward the Union.

| Feature | Source / Mechanism |
| --- | --- |
| Parliamentary form | British Constitution |
| Federal scheme, emergency, public service commissions | Government of India Act, 1935 |
| Fundamental Rights, judicial review, impeachment | US Constitution |
| Directive Principles of State Policy | Irish Constitution |
| Federation with strong centre, residuary powers | Canadian Constitution |
| Concurrent List, freedom of trade | Australian Constitution |
| Suspension of Fundamental Rights during emergency | German (Weimar) Constitution |
| Procedure for constitutional amendment | South African Constitution |

#### Edge Cases and Common Mistakes

Candidates frequently confuse the date of **adoption** (26 November 1949) with the date of **commencement** (26 January 1950). The 1949 date is when the Constituent Assembly signed the document; the 1950 date is when Articles 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 60, 324, 366, 367, and 379 came into force.

- Misattributing the Preamble to **B.R. Ambedkar** — Ambedkar chaired the Drafting Committee, but the Preamble flows from Nehru's resolution.
- Treating **Equality** as a single concept — the Preamble covers legal, political, economic, and social equality.
- Assuming the Preamble grants **direct enforceability** — courts invoke it only as an interpretive aid.
