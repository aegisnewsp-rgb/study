# Introduction to Complex Numbers — H2 Mathematics (9758) Notes

Introduction to Complex Numbers is the fourth content strand in the H2 Mathematics 9758 syllabus. It is a strand unique to H2 Mathematics — neither H1 Mathematics 8865 nor the O-Level Mathematics 4052 covers complex numbers. The strand covers the Argand diagram, modulus and argument of a complex number, polar form, De Moivre's theorem, the nth roots of a complex number, and loci in the complex plane. A weak grasp of complex numbers is the most common reason an otherwise-strong JC2 student misses marks on the pure-mathematics items of H2 Paper 1.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### The seven facts every JC2 student needs

1. **Complex number**: a number of the form z = x + iy, where x and y are real and i is the imaginary unit with i² = −1.
2. **Real part Re(z)**: x. **Imaginary part Im(z)**: y.
3. **Modulus |z|**: the distance from the origin to z in the Argand diagram. |z| = √(x² + y²).
4. **Argument arg(z)**: the angle that z makes with the positive real axis, measured counter-clockwise. arg(z) = arctan(y/x) (in the appropriate quadrant).
5. **Polar form**: z = r (cos θ + i sin θ) where r = |z| and θ = arg(z).
6. **De Moivre's theorem**: (cos θ + i sin θ)^n = cos nθ + i sin nθ. Extends to (r cis θ)^n = r^n cis nθ.
7. **Conjugate**: z̄ = x − iy. Used to simplify z + z̄ = 2 Re(z) and z z̄ = |z|².

#### Key notations

- i — the imaginary unit, with i² = −1
- Re(z) — the real part of z
- Im(z) — the imaginary part of z
- |z| — the modulus of z
- arg(z) — the argument of z
- z̄ — the complex conjugate of z
- cis θ — shorthand for cos θ + i sin θ

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### The Argand diagram

The Argand diagram represents complex numbers as points in the plane: the real part x is plotted along the horizontal axis and the imaginary part y along the vertical axis. The point (x, y) represents the complex number z = x + iy.

#### Modulus and argument

The **modulus** |z| is the distance from the origin to z. For z = x + iy, |z| = √(x² + y²). The modulus is always non-negative.

The **argument** arg(z) is the angle that z makes with the positive real axis, measured counter-clockwise. The principal argument is usually taken in the interval (−π, π] (or [0, 2π)). For z = x + iy with x > 0, arg(z) = arctan(y/x); for z in other quadrants, adjust by adding or subtracting π.

A complex number with x = 0 is purely imaginary (arg = ±π/2); a complex number with y = 0 is purely real (arg = 0 if x > 0, arg = π if x < 0).

#### Polar form

A complex number z = x + iy can be rewritten in polar form z = r (cos θ + i sin θ) where r = |z| and θ = arg(z). The shorthand cis θ = cos θ + i sin θ gives z = r cis θ.

To convert from x + iy to r cis θ: compute r = √(x² + y²), then compute θ from the quadrant.

To convert from r cis θ to x + iy: x = r cos θ, y = r sin θ.

#### Multiplication and division in polar form

Multiplying two complex numbers in polar form: r₁ cis θ₁ × r₂ cis θ₂ = (r₁ r₂) cis (θ₁ + θ₂). The moduli multiply and the arguments add.

Dividing: (r₁ cis θ₁) / (r₂ cis θ₂) = (r₁ / r₂) cis (θ₁ − θ₂). The moduli divide and the arguments subtract.

#### De Moivre's theorem

De Moivre's theorem: for any real θ and any positive integer n, (cos θ + i sin θ)^n = cos nθ + i sin nθ.

Equivalently, (r cis θ)^n = r^n cis nθ.

De Moivre's theorem extends the product rule for cis. It is the foundation for many of the proofs in the H2 complex-numbers strand.

#### Roots of a complex number

The nth roots of a complex number z = r cis θ are r^(1/n) cis [(θ + 2kπ) / n] for k = 0, 1, 2, …, n − 1.

These n roots are equally spaced on a circle of radius r^(1/n) in the Argand diagram; consecutive roots differ in argument by 2π/n.

#### Locus problems

A **locus** in the Argand diagram is the set of points z that satisfy a given condition.

- |z| = r: the circle of radius r centred at the origin.
- |z − a| = r: the circle of radius r centred at the point a in the Argand diagram.
- arg(z) = θ: the half-line from the origin at angle θ.
- arg(z − a) = θ: the half-line from a at angle θ.
- |z − a| = |z − b|: the perpendicular bisector of the segment from a to b.
- |z − a| = k |z − b|: the Apollonius circle (a circle passing through a and b if k = 1, or one of two circles otherwise).

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where Complex Numbers appears across the H2 Mathematics papers

- **H2 Mathematics 9758 Paper 1**: short to medium structured items on Argand diagram, polar form, and De Moivre's theorem. Locus items often appear as a 5- to 8-mark problem.
- **H2 Mathematics 9758 Paper 3**: complex-numbers items can appear alongside the HL-extension content. Sum-of-series and roots-of-unity problems are common Paper 3 items.
- **Across the papers**: drawing the Argand diagram with the relevant points and the angle marked is the highest-leverage exam technique for complex-numbers items.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "i = √−1, so i is a real number." i is the imaginary unit; i² = −1 by definition. i is not a real number.
- "|z| is always positive." |z| ≥ 0; |z| = 0 only when z = 0.
- "arg(z) is unique." The argument has infinitely many values differing by multiples of 2π. The principal argument is usually taken in (−π, π] or [0, 2π).
- "(cos θ + i sin θ)^n = cos θ^n + i sin θ^n." No — De Moivre's theorem says (cos θ + i sin θ)^n = cos nθ + i sin nθ.
- "A complex equation has at most two solutions." A complex polynomial equation of degree n has exactly n roots (counted with multiplicity), which may be real, purely imaginary or genuinely complex.

#### Exam technique

- For Argand-diagram questions, draw the diagram with the relevant points and the angle marked. The diagram is the method mark.
- For polar-form conversion questions, state the quadrant before computing the argument. The quadrant statement is the method mark.
- For De Moivre's theorem questions, write the theorem explicitly before applying it. The theorem statement is the method mark.
- For locus questions, identify the geometric interpretation of the condition (circle, half-line, perpendicular bisector). The interpretation is the method mark.
