# Apply graphical methods in solving problems (91257) — NCEA Level 2 Mathematics Notes

Achievement Standard 91257 is the largest of the NCEA Level 2 internal mathematics standards, carrying 4 credits. It tests graphical methods across linear, quadratic, cubic, exponential, reciprocal and trigonometric functions, plus transformations and the use of the derivative at Level 2. Most schools schedule 91257 in Term 1 because it sits at the foundation of the Level 2 calculus work in 91262.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **4 credits**, internally assessed by the school, NZQA moderated.
- Topics: graphs of linear, quadratic, cubic, exponential (a^x), reciprocal (1/x) and trigonometric (sin, cos, tan) functions; transformations (translations, reflections, stretches); solving equations graphically; the derivative as the gradient of the tangent to a curve.
- **Merit** requires relational thinking — using the shape of a graph to choose the right method.
- **Excellence** requires extended abstract thinking — connecting the algebraic form to the graphical features, generalising the transformation rules.

#### Examiner traps

- Mixing up translations — adding to x moves the graph horizontally; adding to y (or to the function value) moves it vertically.
- Reflecting in the wrong axis — f(−x) reflects in the y-axis; −f(x) reflects in the x-axis.
- Confusing the period of sin (360°) and tan (180°).
- Trying to find an exact solution from a graph when the question asks for an approximate one (or vice versa).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Family of functions and their key features

**Linear:** y = mx + c. Straight line. Gradient m, y-intercept c. One root.

**Quadratic:** y = ax² + bx + c. Parabola opening up if a > 0, down if a < 0. Vertex at x = −b/(2a). Roots (where y = 0) found by factorising or using the quadratic formula.

**Cubic:** y = ax³ + bx² + cx + d. S-shape for positive leading coefficient; reverse S for negative. Up to three real roots.

**Exponential:** y = a^x where a > 0, a ≠ 1. Passes through (0, 1). Increasing if a > 1, decreasing if 0 < a < 1. Always positive. Never crosses the x-axis.

**Reciprocal:** y = 1/x. Hyperbola with two branches, one in the first quadrant, one in the third. Asymptotes at x = 0 (vertical) and y = 0 (horizontal).

**Trigonometric:** y = sin x, y = cos x, y = tan x. Sin and cos have amplitude 1 and period 360°. Tan has period 180° and vertical asymptotes where cos x = 0.

#### Transformations

For y = f(x):

- **y = f(x) + k** — translate up by k.
- **y = f(x + h)** — translate left by h.
- **y = −f(x)** — reflect in the x-axis.
- **y = f(−x)** — reflect in the y-axis.
- **y = a·f(x)** where a > 1 — stretch vertically by factor a. If 0 < a < 1, compress vertically.

The order of transformations matters: apply the inside (horizontal) transformations first, then the outside (vertical) ones.

#### Solving equations graphically

To solve f(x) = g(x) graphically, plot both y = f(x) and y = g(x) on the same axes and read off the x-coordinates of the intersections. This gives approximate solutions; algebraic methods are needed for exact solutions.

To solve f(x) = k graphically, plot y = f(x) and the horizontal line y = k and read off the intersections.

#### The derivative as gradient of a tangent

At Level 2, the derivative of y = f(x) at x = a is defined as the gradient of the tangent to the curve at the point (a, f(a)). Finding it algebraically (using f'(x) rules) is part of 91262; finding it graphically (drawing the tangent and measuring its gradient) is part of 91257.

To find the gradient of a tangent at a point:

1. Plot the curve.
2. Draw a tangent at the point, making sure it touches the curve at exactly that point.
3. Choose two points on the tangent line, well separated.
4. Calculate (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁).

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Connecting algebraic form to graphical features

The single most powerful move in 91257 is reading the algebraic form and predicting the graph:

- y = 2(x − 3)² + 5 is a parabola opening up, vertex at (3, 5), y-intercept at 2(9) + 5 = 23.
- y = sin(2x) has period 180° (not 360°), so it oscillates twice as fast as y = sin x.
- y = 3·2^x passes through (0, 3), increasing, asymptotic to the x-axis.
- y = 1/(x − 2) is the reciprocal graph shifted right by 2, with vertical asymptote at x = 2.

Excellence-grade work on 91257 includes sketching a function from its algebraic form without plotting points, then verifying with a calculator sketch.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Vertical translation changes the gradient." No — vertical translation changes the y-intercept but not the gradient.
- "y = sin(x + 30°) is shifted right by 30°." No — it is shifted left by 30°.
- "f(−x) and −f(x) are the same thing." No — f(−x) reflects in the y-axis; −f(x) reflects in the x-axis.
- "The derivative is just the slope." More than that — the derivative is a function in its own right, defined at every point on the curve.
