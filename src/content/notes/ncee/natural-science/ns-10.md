# The Solar System and Earth

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**The Solar System** is the Sun together with the eight planets, dwarf planets, moons, asteroids, comets and other bodies held by the Sun's gravity. **Earth** is the **third planet** from the Sun and the only known planet supporting life.

Key facts to memorise:
- **Eight planets in order:** Mercury, Venus, **Earth**, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune.
- **Inner 4** = terrestrial (rocky); **outer 4** = gas giants (Jovian). **Pluto** is a **dwarf planet**, not the 9th planet.
- **Earth rotates** on its axis in **24 hours** (causing day and night) and **revolves** around the Sun in **365¼ days** (causing one year).
- Earth's **axis is tilted at 23.5°**, which produces the **four seasons**.
- Distance Earth–Sun ≈ **150 million km (1 AU)**; Earth–Moon ≈ **384,400 km**.
- **Solar eclipse** = Moon between Sun and Earth; **lunar eclipse** = Earth between Sun and Moon.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Members of the Solar System
The Solar System is centred on the **Sun**, a star at the centre. Bound to it by **gravity** are eight **planets**, dwarf planets (including **Pluto**, reclassified in 2006 by the IAU), **moons (satellites)**, **asteroids** (rocky bodies, mostly between Mars and Jupiter in the asteroid belt), **comets** (icy bodies that develop a tail near the Sun), and **meteoroids**.

The **eight planets in order from the Sun** are: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune. A useful mnemonic is **"My Very Educated Mother Just Served Us Noodles."**

#### Two Groups of Planets

| Group | Planets | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **Terrestrial (inner)** | Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars | Small, rocky, dense, few/no moons |
| **Jovian / Gas giants (outer)** | Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune | Large, gaseous, ring systems, many moons |

#### Movement of the Earth
- **Rotation** = Earth spinning on its tilted axis from **west to east** in **24 hours** → causes **day and night**.
- **Revolution** = Earth orbiting the Sun in an **elliptical path** in **365¼ days** → causes one **year**; the extra ¼ day is corrected every four years in a **leap year**.

#### Why We Have Seasons
Earth's axis is tilted at **23.5°** relative to its orbital plane. As Earth revolves, different hemispheres tilt toward or away from the Sun:
- **Summer**: hemisphere tilted **toward** the Sun (more direct light, longer days).
- **Winter**: hemisphere tilted **away** from the Sun.
- **Equinoxes** (≈21 March, 23 September): Sun directly over the equator; day and night nearly equal.
- **Solstices** (≈21 June, 22 December): maximum tilt toward or away from the Sun.

#### Eclipses
- **Solar eclipse**: New Moon position — **Moon** is between the Sun and Earth; blocks sunlight.
- **Lunar eclipse**: Full Moon position — **Earth** is between the Sun and Moon; Earth's shadow falls on the Moon.

#### NCEE-style question patterns
Expect MCQs such as: *"How long does Earth take to revolve around the Sun?"*, *"What causes day and night?"*, *"Which planet is the third from the Sun?"* and *"What is the correct order of planets?"* — always read the option list carefully, as distractors swap rotation/revolution or seasons/distance.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### The Moon, Tides and Phases
The **Moon** is Earth's only natural **satellite**, orbiting Earth in about **27.3 days** (revolution) and rotating on its axis in the same time (hence we always see the same side). It does **not** produce its own light; we see it because it **reflects sunlight**. As the Moon orbits, the visible illuminated portion changes — producing **phases**: New Moon, Waxing Crescent, First Quarter, Waxing Gibbous, Full Moon, Waning Gibbous, Last Quarter, Waning Crescent.

The Moon's and Sun's gravitational pull on Earth's oceans produces **tides**. When Sun, Earth and Moon are aligned (new or full moon), **spring tides** (highest high tides) occur; when the Moon is at right angles to the Sun–Earth line (first/last quarter), **neap tides** (smallest range) occur.

#### Earth's Shape, Size and Atmosphere
Earth is an **oblate spheroid** — nearly spherical but slightly flattened at the poles. Approximate measurements: **diameter ≈ 12,742 km**, **circumference ≈ 40,075 km** (equatorial). Earth's **atmosphere** is roughly **78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen** with traces of carbon dioxide, argon and water vapour — this composition supports respiration, photosynthesis, and weather.

#### Beyond the Planets: Stars and Galaxies
The **Sun is a star**, an enormous ball of hydrogen and helium undergoing nuclear fusion. Stars are grouped into **constellations** (apparent patterns) and vast **galaxies**; our galaxy is the **Milky Way**. Stars appear to move across the sky at night because **Earth rotates**, not because the stars themselves move around us.

#### Common Mistakes to Avoid
1. **Rotation vs Revolution** — rotation = spin on axis (24 h); revolution = orbit around Sun (365¼ d). Exams swap these.
2. **Why seasons occur** — caused by **axial tilt (23.5°)**, not by Earth being closer to the Sun in summer (Earth is actually slightly closer in January).
3. **Eclipses** — solar eclipse happens at **new moon**; lunar eclipse at **full moon**. A common error is reversing the alignment.
4. **Pluto** — reclassified as a **dwarf planet** in 2006; the Solar System has **eight**, not nine, planets.
5. **Order of planets** — watch for missing Mercury or Neptune in options; recite the full sequence.

#### Worked Example
**Q:** "Earth takes 24 hours to _________ on its axis and 365¼ days to _________ the Sun."
**A:** **rotate**; **revolve around**.

#### Practice Prompts
1. Explain two effects of Earth's **23.5° axial tilt**.
2. Describe the arrangement of Sun, Earth and Moon during a **lunar eclipse** and state the Moon's phase at that time.
