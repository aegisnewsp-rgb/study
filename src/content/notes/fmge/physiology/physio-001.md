# Cell Physiology and Membrane Transport

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

- **Plasma membrane**: phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins; **fluid mosaic model** explains lateral mobility of lipids and proteins.
- **Selective permeability** lets small non-polar molecules cross freely; ions and polar solutes need protein channels or carriers.
- **Transport types**: passive (simple diffusion, facilitated diffusion, osmosis) vs. active (primary ATP-driven, secondary gradient-driven) vs. vesicular (endo/exocytosis).
- **Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase**: electrogenic pump exporting 3 Na⁺ out, importing 2 K⁺ in per ATP; sets the resting membrane potential near **−70 mV**.
- **Nernst at 37 °C**: E (mV) ≈ **61.5 / z × log₁₀([ion]ₒᵤₜ / [ion]ᵢₙ)** — used to calculate equilibrium potential for any single ion.
- **Tonicity** (not osmolarity) drives osmosis: isotonic = no cell change, hypotonic = RBC lysis, hypertonic = RBC crenation.
- **High-yield traps**: Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase is 3:2 (not 1:1); facilitated diffusion is saturable but passive; endocytosis types are not interchangeable.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Membrane Structure and Permeability
The plasma membrane is a **phospholipid bilayer** (~5 nm thick) with embedded integral proteins and a peripheral coat of glycoproteins/glycolipids. Cholesterol inserts between phospholipids to modulate fluidity. According to the **fluid mosaic model**, lipids move laterally (~2 µm/s) while proteins drift within the plane. **Selective permeability** arises because the hydrophobic core blocks ions and large polar molecules, permitting free passage of O₂, CO₂, steroid hormones, and small lipid-soluble drugs.

#### Passive Transport Mechanisms
**Simple diffusion** follows Fick's law: J = −D·(dC/dx), where J is net flux, D the diffusion coefficient, and dC/dx the concentration gradient. **Facilitated diffusion** uses carrier proteins (e.g., GLUT1–4 for glucose) or channels; it is saturable, shows Michaelis–Menten kinetics (V_max, K_m), and is inhibited non-competitively — yet still requires no ATP. **Osmosis** is the net water movement across a semipermeable membrane, quantified by van't Hoff's law: π = i·C·R·T.

#### Active Transport
**Primary active transport** hydrolyses ATP directly; the prototype is **Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase**, which exchanges 3 Na⁺ out for 2 K⁺ in, contributing −5 to −10 mV to the resting potential. **Secondary active transport** harnesses the Na⁺ gradient set up by the pump: **symporters** (e.g., SGLT co-transporting Na⁺ and glucose) and **antiporters** (e.g., Na⁺/Ca²⁺ exchanger, NCX) move solutes against their gradients without direct ATP use.

| Transport Type | ATP Required | Gradient Direction | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simple diffusion | No | Down | O₂, CO₂ |
| Facilitated diffusion | No | Down | GLUT, ion channels |
| Primary active | Yes | Up | Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase, Ca²⁺ ATPase |
| Secondary active (symport) | Indirect | Up | SGLT (Na⁺-glucose) |
| Secondary active (antiport) | Indirect | Up | Na⁺/Ca²⁺ exchanger |

#### Resting Membrane Potential
The **resting membrane potential** (~−70 mV in neurons, ~−90 mV in skeletal muscle) arises from the K⁺ gradient maintained by Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase and the high K⁺ permeability at rest. The **Nernst equation** gives the equilibrium potential for a single ion; the **Goldman–Hodgkin–Katz (GHK) voltage equation** combines permeabilities of K⁺, Na⁺, and Cl⁻ to predict V_m. The simplified form at body temperature: E (mV) ≈ 61.5/z · log₁₀([ion]ₒᵤₜ/[ion]ᵢₙ).

#### FMGE Question Patterns
- Calculate E_K or E_Na from given intracellular/extracellular concentrations.
- Identify transport mechanism from a vignette (e.g., glucose uptake in intestinal epithelium → SGLT1 symport).
- Predict RBC fate when placed in 0.9%, 0.45%, or 5% saline.

> **Trap to avoid**: Tonicity considers only **non-penetrating** solutes. Urea is osmotically active but freely crosses membranes, so a 300 mOsm urea solution behaves as **functionally hypotonic**, not isotonic.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Channel Versus Carrier Kinetics
**Channel proteins** form a continuous aqueous pore and allow ~10⁶–10⁸ ions/s — among the fastest biological catalysts known. They may be **leak (always open), voltage-gated (e.g., Naᵥ, Kᵥ, Caᵥ), ligand-gated (nicotinic ACh receptor, NMDA), or mechanically gated (hair cells)**. **Carrier proteins** undergo cyclic conformational change, transport 10²–10⁴ molecules/s, and exhibit **saturation kinetics**. This kinetic asymmetry explains why channelopathies (long QT, cystic fibrosis) disrupt excitability rapidly, while carrier defects (GLUT1 deficiency) cause graded metabolic phenotypes.

#### Vesicular and Bulk Transport
**Exocytosis** requires SNARE-mediated vesicle docking and is triggered by Ca²⁺-calmodulin in neurons. **Endocytosis** has three mechanistically distinct forms: **phagocytosis** (large particles, actin-driven, used by macrophages), **pinocytosis** (non-specific fluid uptake), and **receptor-mediated endocytosis** (highly specific; clathrin-coated pits internalise ligands like LDL, transferrin, and vitamin B₁₂). Defective LDL receptors cause **familial hypercholesterolemia** — a frequently tested FMGE correlation.

#### Clinical Correlations
- **Cystic fibrosis**: ΔF508 mutation in **CFTR** (a cAMP-activated Cl⁻ channel) causes defective Cl⁻ and water secretion → thick mucus.
- **Cholera**: cholera toxin permanently activates Gs → ↑cAMP → sustained CFTR opening → massive Cl⁻ and water efflux into the gut lumen → secretory diarrhoea.
- **Bartter and Gitelman syndromes**: defects in Na⁺-K⁺-2Cl⁻ (NKCC2) and Na⁺-Cl⁻ (NCC) cotransporters mimic loop and thiazide diuretic effects, respectively.
- **Digitalis**: inhibits Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase → raises intracellular Na⁺ → reduces NCX-driven Ca²⁺ extrusion → increased cardiac contractility.

#### Common Mistakes
1. Confusing **osmolarity** (total solute particle count, e.g., includes urea) with **tonicity** (effective osmotic drive; only non-penetrating solutes).
2. Stating Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase is electroneutral — it moves 3 positive charges out for 2 in, so it is **electrogenic** (~−5 mV direct contribution).
3. Believing secondary active transport does not require energy — it uses the **pre-established Na⁺ gradient**; if Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase is blocked, secondary transport halts.
4. Misclassifying phagocytosis and receptor-mediated endocytosis as the same process.

#### Practice Prompts
1. Given [K⁺]ᵢₙ = 140 mM and [K⁺]ₒᵤₜ = 5 mM at 37 °C, calculate the K⁺ equilibrium potential and explain why the resting membrane potential is less negative than E_K. *(Answer: E_K ≈ −90 mV; V_m is closer to −70 mV because Na⁺ permeability, though small, is not zero, pulling V_m toward E_Na ≈ +60 mV.)*
2. A patient with cholera toxin-induced diarrhoea loses isotonic fluid. Predict the intracellular volume of his erythrocytes and the driving ion. *(RBCs shrink slightly due to activation of NKCC and CFTR in intestinal epithelium, but the systemic effect is iso-osmotic volume contraction; the gut lumen gains Cl⁻, Na⁺, and water.)*
