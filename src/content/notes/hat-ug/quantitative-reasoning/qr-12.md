# Area and Perimeter

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Area** measures the plane region enclosed by a 2D figure (square units like cm² or m²). **Perimeter** measures the total boundary length (linear units like cm or m). For HAT-UG, six shapes dominate: triangle, rectangle, square, parallelogram, trapezium, and circle.

- **Rectangle:** A = l × w, P = 2(l + w)
- **Square:** A = a², P = 4a
- **Triangle:** A = ½ × b × h, P = sum of three sides
- **Circle:** A = πr², C = 2πr (use π = 22/7 or 3.1416)
- **Scaling law:** multiply every side by k → perimeter × k, area × k²
- **Unit trap:** 1 m² = 10,000 cm²; never mix cm and m before squaring

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core formulas by shape

Every HAT-UG MCQ reduces to one of these formulas. Variables use metres; answers carry m or m².

| Shape | Area formula | Perimeter formula |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rectangle | l × w | 2(l + w) |
| Square | a² | 4a |
| Triangle | ½ × b × h | a + b + c |
| Parallelogram | b × h | 2(b + s) |
| Trapezium | ½ × (a + b) × h | a + b + c + d |
| Circle | πr² | 2πr |

#### When Heron's formula is needed

For a triangle where the perpendicular height is unknown, use A = √[s(s−a)(s−b)(s−c)], where s = (a+b+c)/2. This appears when only the three side lengths are given.

#### Common HAT-UG traps

- **Slant vs perpendicular height:** triangles and parallelograms need the height that is perpendicular to the base, not the slanted side.
- **Rhombus:** area = ½ × d₁ × d₂ (diagonals), not b × h unless height is given.
- **π choice:** use 22/7 when radius is a multiple of 7; use 3.1416 otherwise.
- **Sector of a circle:** area = (θ/360) × πr², arc length = (θ/360) × 2πr.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Scaling and percentage change

Linear dimensions and area behave differently under multiplication. If every side is doubled (k = 2), perimeter becomes 2× and area becomes 4×. For a percentage change p% in side length, the new area = old area × (1 + p/100)². A 10% increase in side length produces a 21% increase in area, not 10%.

#### Composite figures

HAT-UG occasionally presents a shape made of two standard figures. Break it into parts, compute each area, then add (overlap shape = sum) or subtract (cut-out shape = difference). Perimeter follows only the exposed outer edges — internal seams are excluded.

#### Worked example

A rectangular lawn is 50 m long and 35 m wide. A 5 m wide path runs inside along the perimeter. Find the lawn's remaining area.

- Outer area = 50 × 35 = 1750 m²
- Inner rectangle = (50 − 10) × (35 − 10) = 40 × 25 = 1000 m²
- Path area = 1750 − 1000 = 750 m²
- Remaining lawn area = 1000 m²

#### Practice prompts

1. A circle has circumference 44 m. Find its area using π = 22/7.
2. The sides of a square are increased by 20%. By what percent does the area change?

#### HAT-UG strategy

This topic carries 3% of the Quantitative Reasoning section. Expect 1–2 MCQs per paper, usually a single-formula calculation or a unit conversion. Budget 60–90 seconds per question: identify the shape, write the formula, plug in values with units, and double-check whether the answer is linear or square.
