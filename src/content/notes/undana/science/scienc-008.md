# Biology Fundamentals

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> The cell is the smallest unit that satisfies every property of life. Treat that sentence as the seed; everything else in UNDANA biology grows from it.

Biology Fundamentals for UNDANA asks you to reason across six blocks: the cell, classification, ecology, plant and animal physiology, Mendelian genetics, and evolution. Around 3 to 5 questions appear, each carrying equal marks under the penalised SNBT scoring, so every wrong answer actively subtracts from raw score.

| Block | Must-know concept | One-line anchor |
|---|---|---|
| Cell | Cell theory (Schleiden, Schwann, Virchow) | All life = cells, all cells from cells |
| Classification | Whittaker's 5 kingdoms | Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia |
| Ecology | Energy flows, matter cycles | Unidirectional arrows in food chains |
| Genetics | Monohybrid cross ratios | Genotypic 1:2:1, Phenotypic 3:1 |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Cell biology, genetics, and ecology together account for most of the 3–5 questions you will face. Read this tier once and the rest becomes recall, not learning.

#### Cell theory and the two cell types

The cell doctrine states three claims: every organism is built of cells, the cell is the basic living unit, and every cell descends from a pre-existing cell. Prokaryotes (Monera — bacteria, archaea) carry no membrane-bound nucleus; eukaryotes (Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia) keep DNA inside a nuclear envelope and divide by mitosis. UNDANA frequently frames this as a comparison: *"Which feature distinguishes Monera from Protista?"* — the answer is the absence of a true nucleus, not size or motility.

#### Photosynthesis versus cellular respiration

These reactions are stoichiometric inverses. The light-dependent and Calvin-cycle steps in the chloroplast produce glucose and O₂; the mitochondrion oxidises that glucose back to CO₂ and H₂O, capturing roughly 38 ATP per glucose. Memorise the reciprocal: whatever photosynthesis consumes, respiration produces, and vice versa.

| Feature | Photosynthesis | Cellular respiration |
|---|---|---|
| Location | Chloroplast (thylakoid, stroma) | Mitochondrion (matrix, inner membrane) |
| Reactants | 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + light | C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂ |
| Products | C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂ | 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + ~38 ATP |
| Energy flow | Stores in glucose | Releases as ATP |
| Time of day | Light-dependent phase only | Continuous |

#### Mendelian genetics and the ratios that trap candidates

A monohybrid cross of two heterozygotes (Aa × Aa) yields F₂ genotypes in the ratio **1 AA : 2 Aa : 1 aa**, but phenotypes in the ratio **3 : 1** because the dominant allele masks the recessive one in heterozygotes. A dihybrid cross yields the classic **9 : 3 : 3 : 1** phenotypic ratio only when the two genes assort independently and both alleles are fully dominant. Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium, p² + 2pq + q² = 1, describes allele frequencies in an **ideal** population (no mutation, no migration, random mating, no selection, large size) — most exam distractors assume equilibrium where it fails.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix

| Concept pair | The confusion | How to separate them |
|---|---|---|
| Dominant vs most common allele | "Dominant" sounds like "frequent" | Dominance describes a heterozygote phenotype, not a population count |
| Genotypic 1:2:1 vs phenotypic 3:1 | Same cross, different ratio | Count alleles for genotype, count visible traits for phenotype |
| Food chain vs food web | Chain = one path, web = many | A web shows redundancy; energy loss at every trophic step (~90%) is identical |
| Autotroph vs heterotroph | Both eat something | Autotrophs fix carbon from CO₂; heterotrophs must consume organic carbon |
| Population density vs frequency | Density = count per area, frequency = share | D = N/A (ind m⁻²); fᵢ = nᵢ/N_total (dimensionless 0–1) |

#### Worked example: Hardy–Weinberg in a UNDANA-style question

Suppose a population of 10 000 individuals contains 640 homozygous recessive (aa) individuals for a Mendelian trait. Find the recessive allele frequency *q*, the dominant allele frequency *p*, and the expected number of heterozygous carriers (2pq).

**Step 1 —** Identify q²: q² = 640 / 10 000 = 0.064 (dimensionless).

**Step 2 —** Take the square root: q = √0.064 ≈ 0.253.

**Step 3 —** Use p + q = 1: p = 1 − 0.253 = 0.747.

**Step 4 —** Compute 2pq: 2 × 0.747 × 0.253 ≈ 0.378.

**Step 5 —** Convert to carrier count: 0.378 × 10 000 ≈ 3 780 heterozygotes.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students often square q² instead of taking its square root, then call the result "allele frequency" — the answer then reads ~6.4% allele, which is wrong by an order of magnitude. Always check whether the given count is **genotype frequency** (q²) or **allele frequency** (q).

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Edge-case genetics, ecological limiting factors, and physiology cross-overs are where the 3% slice pays back the longest study hours.

#### Edge cases that surface in SNMPB-style papers

1. **Codominance and incomplete dominance** break the 3 : 1 phenotypic rule — ABO blood groups and snapdragon colour give 1 : 2 : 1 phenotypes instead.
2. **Linked genes** violate independent assortment: if two loci sit on the same chromosome close together, the 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 collapses toward 3 : 1 (parental types dominate).
3. **Polygenic inheritance** (skin colour, height) produces continuous variation; no single Mendelian ratio applies.
4. **Limiting factors** in ecosystems — Liebig's Law of the Minimum says the scarcest abiotic resource (light, water, nitrogen, pH) sets the population ceiling, not total resource sum.
5. **Nitrogen cycle** detail — nitrifying bacteria (*Nitrosomonas*, *Nitrobacter*) convert NH₃ → NO₂⁻ → NO₃⁻; UNDANA has asked which organism fixes atmospheric N₂ (answer: *Rhizobium* in legume root nodules, not free soil bacteria).

#### Two advanced practice prompts

1. **Cross a heterozygous tall pea plant (Tt) with a homozygous recessive (tt).** Predict the genotypic and phenotypic ratios of the offspring, then state whether this is a test cross and what its diagnostic value is.
2. **A forest loses 80% of its primary producers to drought.** Predict the cascading effect on (a) primary consumer biomass, (b) decomposer activity, and (c) soil nitrogen concentration over the next two growing seasons. Identify which abiotic factor Liebig's Law flags as the new constraint.

> 📌 **Pitfall to bank:** Treating "dominant" as a population-level statement. Mendelian dominance describes what a heterozygote *looks like*, not how often the allele *appears*.
