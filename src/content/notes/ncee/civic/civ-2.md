# Our National Symbols

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

National Symbols are the official emblems, colours, songs and pledges a country adopts to show its identity, sovereignty, history and unity. For Nigeria, the core symbols you must recognise are the **National Flag**, the **Coat of Arms**, the **National Anthem**, the **National Pledge** and the **National Motto**. The flag carries three equal vertical stripes of **green-white-green**, where green stands for agriculture/vegetation and white stands for peace and unity.

The Coat of Arms has a **black shield** marked with a **wavy white Y-shape** (the two rivers Niger and Benue), two white **horses** on either side, and an **eagle** perched on top. The motto reads **"Unity and Faith, Peace and Progress"**. The National Anthem is *"Arise O Compatriots, Nigeria's Call Obey"*.

- Flag stripes: green = agriculture, white = peace and unity.
- Motto: Unity and Faith, Peace and Progress.
- Shield Y-shape = Niger and Benue rivers (NOT the horses).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### What makes a symbol "national"
A symbol becomes national when the state officially adopts it to represent sovereignty, history and shared values. In Nigeria these symbols are protected by law and must be treated with respect — abused flags, distorted anthems or defaced coats of arms can attract penalties under the **National Flag and Coat of Arms Act**.

#### The Nigerian National Flag
Adopted in 1960 at independence, the flag is a horizontal rectangle divided into three equal vertical bands of **green, white and green**. The green bands symbolise the country's **agricultural wealth and rich vegetation**, while the central white band symbolises **peace and unity** among Nigeria's diverse peoples.

| Element of flag | Colour | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Left band | Green | Agriculture and natural vegetation |
| Centre band | White | Peace and unity |
| Right band | Green | Agriculture and natural vegetation |

#### The Nigerian Coat of Arms
Granted in 1960 and revised in 1971, the Coat of Arms is a black shield charged with a **wavy white pall (Y-shape)** representing the rivers **Niger and Benue**. It is supported by two **white horses** (dignity), surmounted by a red **eagle** (strength), and stands on a green field with a yellow **wreath** of *Costus spectabilis* (the national flower). The ribbon beneath carries the motto **"Unity and Faith, Peace and Progress"**.

#### Symbols of identity: Anthem, Pledge and Motto

- **National Anthem** — *"Arise O Compatriots, Nigeria's Call Obey"*, adopted 1978, reaffirms loyalty and service.
- **National Pledge** — recited alongside the Anthem, affirming allegiance to the nation.
- **National Motto** — *Unity and Faith, Peace and Progress*, the binding creed printed on the Coat of Arms.

#### Why they matter in Civic Education
National symbols encourage **patriotism**, foster a sense of belonging across Nigeria's ethnic groups, and remind citizens of the sacrifices made for independence. NCEE questions usually test **colour symbolism**, the **motto**, and the correct **identification** of flag stripes versus shield markings.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge cases and frequent examiner traps
NCEE setters deliberately mix up flag and coat-of-arms details. Watch for these classic traps:

| Common mistake | Correct answer |
| --- | --- |
| Saying the two white horses represent the Niger and Benue rivers | The horses represent **dignity**; the **Y-shape** on the shield represents the rivers |
| Writing the motto as "Unity and Faith" only | Full motto is **"Unity and Faith, Peace and Progress"** |
| Calling the bird on the Coat of Arms a hawk | It is an **eagle**, symbolising strength |
| Saying the white band on the flag represents purity only | White officially means **peace and unity** |
| Treating the National Pledge as optional | It is one of Nigeria's official national symbols alongside Anthem, Flag, Coat of Arms and Motto |

#### Connections to adjacent Civic Education topics
National Symbols link directly to **Citizenship**, **Nationalism**, **Rights and Duties of a Citizen**, and **Independence/History of Nigeria** (1960). Symbols gain meaning only when citizens understand the values they encode; studying them alongside civic duties explains why abusing the flag or refusing to recite the pledge is treated as anti-national conduct.

#### Exam strategy for the 3% weight
With Civic Education contributing roughly 3% to the NCEE paper, expect **one to two questions** drawn from this topic — usually a multiple-choice on colour symbolism and a short-answer asking you to state the motto or identify a coat-of-arms component. Spend under 10 minutes revising it: memorise the flag colours, the full motto, and the meaning of the Y-shape, eagle and horses.

#### Practice prompts
1. List the five core national symbols of Nigeria and state what each stripe of the flag represents.
2. Draw or describe the Coat of Arms, then explain the symbolism of the Y-shape, the eagle, the two horses and the ribbon.
