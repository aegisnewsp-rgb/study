# Intelligence and Creativity

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your UPTET CDP paper.

Intelligence is the global capacity to **act purposefully, think rationally, and deal effectively with the environment** (Wechsler). Creativity is the ability to produce work that is both **novel (original) and useful (appropriate)**, classically measured through four divergent-thinking traits: fluency, flexibility, originality, and elaboration (Guilford; operationalised by Torrance). For UPTET, the two are distinct but moderately correlated (~r ≈ +0.3 to +0.5): a high IQ does not guarantee high creativity once IQ crosses roughly **120** — the **threshold hypothesis**.

| Term | One-line meaning |
|---|---|
| IQ | Ratio score; **IQ = (MA / CA) × 100** (children only) |
| Deviation IQ | Wechsler scale: **Mean = 100, SD = 15** (used for adults) |
| Multiple Intelligences | Gardner's 8 (later 9) distinct ability domains |
| Triarchic Theory | Sternberg's 3 parts: analytical, creative, practical |
| Divergent Thinking | Producing many varied ideas; core of creativity |
| Emotional Intelligence | Salovey & Mayer: perceive–use–understand–manage emotions |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"Wechsler's MAP"** — *Mental Age ÷ Chronological Age × 100* = IQ. For adults, forget MA/CA and remember **"Deviation IQ = 100 ± 15"** (M, σ). Gardner's 8 intelligences → **"L²S M B I² N"** = Linguistic, Logical-mathematical, Spatial, Musical, Bodily-kinesthetic, Interpersonal, Intrapersonal, Naturalistic.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Working understanding of the theories, formulas, and standard question types tested in UPTET Paper-I/II.

#### Core Definitions and IQ Formula
Wechsler defined intelligence as the **aggregate or global capacity** of an individual to act purposefully, to think rationally, and to deal effectively with the environment. The ratio IQ used by **Stanford-Binet** is:

$$\text{IQ} = \frac{\text{MA}}{\text{CA}} \times 100$$

where **MA** = Mental Age (years), **CA** = Chronological Age (years). The result is dimensionless (a ratio scaled by 100). This formula works only for children because adult mental age plateaus.

For older learners, **Wechsler's deviation IQ** is used. Scores are normally distributed with **M = 100** and **SD = 15**. Approximately 68% of people score between 85 and 115, and about 95% between 70 and 130 — these bands are repeated almost verbatim in UPTET MCQs.

#### Major Theories of Intelligence

| Theorist | Theory | Key idea |
|---|---|---|
| Spearman | Two-factor | Performance = **g** (general) + **s** (specific) |
| Thurstone | Primary Mental Abilities | Verbal, numerical, perceptual, memory, reasoning, spatial, word fluency |
| Cattell | Gf–Gc | Fluid (novel reasoning) vs Crystallised (accumulated knowledge) |
| Gardner | Multiple Intelligences | 8 distinct domains (linguistic, logical-mathematical, spatial, musical, bodily-kinesthetic, interpersonal, intrapersonal, naturalistic) |
| Sternberg | Triarchic / WICS | Analytical + Creative + Practical intelligence |
| Salovey & Mayer | Emotional Intelligence | Perceive, use, understand, manage emotions |

#### Creativity: Components and Stages
Torrance's tests score four traits:

$$C = \alpha F + \beta F_x + \gamma O + \delta E$$

where **F** = Fluency (number of ideas), **F_x** = Flexibility (number of categories), **O** = Originality (novelty), **E** = Elaboration (detail), and the Greek letters are weights. Wallas described the **creative process** in four stages: **Preparation → Incubation → Illumination → Verification** — a UPTET-favourite sequencing question.

Sternberg's **investment theory** says creative people *"buy low and sell high"* — they champion unfamiliar ideas until society accepts them.

#### Intelligence vs Creativity — Comparison Matrix

| Feature | Intelligence | Creativity |
|---|---|---|
| Thinking style | Convergent (one right answer) | Divergent (many possibilities) |
| Measurement | IQ tests (Stanford-Binet, Wechsler, Raven's) | Torrance Tests of Creative Thinking (TTCT) |
| Correlation with each other | r ≈ +0.3 to +0.5; weakens above IQ 120 | Same |
| Heritability | High (~50–80%) | Moderate; shaped by motivation + environment |
| Key theorists | Wechsler, Spearman, Gardner | Guilford, Torrance, Sternberg |

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem
**Question:** A child has a Mental Age of 10 years and a Chronological Age of 8 years. What is her IQ and how should it be interpreted on the Wechsler scale?
#### (A) 80 — below average
#### (B) 100 — average
#### (C) 125 — above average
#### (D) 140 — gifted

#### Solution:
Step 1 — Apply the ratio IQ formula:
IQ = (MA / CA) × 100 = (10 / 8) × 100 = **125**.

Step 2 — Compare with Wechsler's M = 100, SD = 15:
125 = M + 1.67 SD → falls above the +1 SD band (85–115) and well inside the +2 SD cap (70–130).

Step 3 — Interpretation: an IQ of 125 is **above average / high average / near-gifted** (gifted is conventionally ≥ 130).

Step 4 — Match option: **(C) 125 — above average**.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students divide the wrong way (CA/MA), getting 80 instead of 125. Also, do **not** apply the MA/CA formula to adults — for adults the score must be read against the Wechsler deviation scale (M = 100, SD = 15), not computed.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Edge cases, threshold effects, classroom application, and traps specific to UPTET CDP.

#### Threshold, Investment, and Heritability
Guilford's **threshold hypothesis** states that below IQ ≈ 120, intelligence and creativity rise together, but above 120 the correlation collapses — a very high IQ does not predict a very high creativity score. UPTET has asked this as a direct TRUE/FALSE: *"Above IQ 120, creativity and intelligence become independent"* — answer **TRUE**.

Heritability of IQ is estimated at **50–80%** (polygenic), but the environment — nutrition, language exposure, preschool stimulation, socio-economic status — explains the remaining variance. UPTET answer key pattern: questions on "intelligence is determined by" expect **both heredity and environment**, not one alone.

#### Classroom Strategies for Creativity (Rogers & Sternberg)
1. Encourage **divergent questioning** ("How many uses can you think of for a brick?").
2. Use **brainstorming** with deferred judgement to protect fluency.
3. Build **intrinsic motivation** through choice and autonomy-supportive feedback.
4. Tolerate ambiguity and risk-taking; **avoid premature evaluation**.
5. Teach **metacognitive reflection** after a task to support the Verification stage.

#### Common Traps and Edge Cases

| Trap | Why students fall for it | Correct handling |
|---|---|---|
| Gardner = "exactly 7" intelligences | Textbook oversimplification | Gardner proposed **8** (added naturalistic); a possible 9th (existential) is discussed |
| EQ measured by Stanford-Binet | Conflating IQ and EQ tests | EQ → Bar-On EQ-i / MSCEIT; Stanford-Binet measures IQ |
| Divergent = Creative (synonyms) | Loose usage | Divergent thinking is a **process underlying** creativity; creativity also needs originality + value + motivation |
| Wallas vs Sternberg confusion | Both are "creative theories" | Wallas = **stages** of the act; Sternberg's investment = **personality + strategy** |
| MA/CA formula for adults | Memorising one formula | Ratio IQ valid only for children; adults use **deviation IQ (M=100, σ=15)** |

#### Advanced Practice Prompts
1. A UPTET Paper-II MCQ states: *"Which intelligence helps a classroom teacher resolve peer conflicts sensitively?"* — answer by naming Gardner's **interpersonal** intelligence and Salovey–Mayer's EQ component (**empathy / managing relationships**).
2. *"A 9-year-old scores M=120 on Wechsler. The teacher assumes she will also score high on TTCT."* Critique the assumption using Guilford's threshold hypothesis and discuss two classroom strategies the teacher should adopt to nurture creativity irrespective of IQ.
