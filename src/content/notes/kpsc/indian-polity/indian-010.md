# Federalism and Centre-State Relations

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

India's Constitution creates a **quasi-federal** structure — K.C. Wheare's term — distributing power between the Union and States through **three lists** in the Seventh Schedule, with a deliberate tilt toward the Centre in normal times.

- **Union List (List I):** Defence, Foreign Affairs, Atomic Energy, Railways, Banking, Posts and Telegraphs.
- **State List (List II, pre-42nd CAA):** Police, Public Order, State Courts, Prisons, Local Government, Public Health.
- **Concurrent List (List III):** Criminal Law, Criminal Procedure, Education (post-1976), Forests, Marriage, Contracts.
- **Residuary Powers:** Parliament, under Article 248.
- **Supremacy clause:** Article 254 — Central law prevails over State law on a Concurrent subject in case of repugnancy.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"USC-R"** — Union, State, Concurrent, Residuary. Remember the count shifts after the **42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976**, which moved five State-List subjects (education, forests, weights & measures, wild animals & birds, administration of justice) to the Concurrent List.

| List | Article | Items (approx.) | Conflict rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Union List | 246(1) | 100 (post-42nd CAA) | State cannot legislate |
| State List | 246(3) | 66 (original) | Union cannot ordinarily legislate |
| Concurrent List | 246(2) | 47 | Article 254 — Central prevails |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Constitutional Basis and Three Lists
Article 246 divides legislative competence between Parliament and State Legislatures using the **Seventh Schedule**. The **42nd CAA, 1976** reshaped the lists: education, forests, weights and measures, protection of wild animals and birds, and administration of justice shifted from the State List to the Concurrent List, lifting the Union item count from 97 to 100.

#### Parliamentary Supremacy Tools
Articles **2, 3, 249, 252, 356, 357** empower Parliament to override State autonomy:
- **Article 2:** Admission or establishment of new States.
- **Article 3:** Formation of new States, boundary changes, name changes — without State consent.
- **Article 249:** Rajya Sabha **special majority** to move a State subject to the Union List for **one year** (extendable).
- **Article 252:** Two or more State Legislatures pass a resolution authorising Parliament to legislate on a State-List subject.
- **Articles 356–357:** President's Rule — State Legislature is suspended, and Parliament assumes State powers.

#### Cooperative vs Competitive Federalism

| Feature | Cooperative Federalism | Competitive Federalism |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | Inter-State Council (Art 263), NITI Aayog | States competing for FDI, investment |
| Finance | Finance Commission (Art 280) | Tax incentives, ease-of-doing-business rankings |
| Examples | GST Council (Art 279A), Zonal Councils | Bengaluru vs Hyderabad for tech investment |

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem
**Question:** The **42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976** transferred five subjects from the State List to the Concurrent List. Which of the following was **not** among them?

(a) Education  
(b) Forests  
(c) Atomic Energy  
(d) Administration of Justice

#### Solution:
The 42nd CAA, 1976 moved (a) education, (b) forests, weights and measures, protection of wild animals and birds, and (d) administration of justice from State to Concurrent List. **Atomic Energy** remained on the Union List and was never on the State List. The correct answer is **(c) Atomic Energy**.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Don't pick education or forests by instinct — many students assume the question lists only "social sector" items. Administration of Justice is the easy-to-miss fifth subject because it was an unusual State-List entry pre-1976.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Commissions and Reports
The **Sarkaria Commission (1983–88)** delivered 247 recommendations; it opposed frequent invocation of Article 356 and pushed for the **Inter-State Council**, which was finally set up in **1990** under Article 263. The **Punchhi Commission (2007–10)** went further — it proposed a **sunset clause for Article 356**, made the Inter-State Council a permanent statutory body, and codified the conventions governing the Governor's appointment (the Governor remains a constitutional irritant in Centre-State friction).

#### Emergency Provisions and Federal Distortion
- **Article 352 (National Emergency):** Converts the Concurrent List into an effective Union List for six months.
- **Article 356 (State Emergency / President's Rule):** Suspends the State Legislature; the Governor rules on behalf of the President.
- **Article 360 (Financial Emergency):** Centre can direct State financial policy.

**S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994)** is the binding precedent: the Supreme Court held that Article 356 is subject to judicial review and that **secularism and federalism are part of the basic structure** (per **Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala, 1973**).

#### Advanced Traps and Edge Cases

1. **Article 371J (Karnataka):** Added by the **98th CAA, 2012**; provides reservation in educational institutions and government jobs for the Hyderabad-Karnataka region — KAS-specific.
2. **GST Council misconception:** Article 279A(4) makes Council recommendations **advisory, not binding**; both Parliament and State Legislatures must separately pass the law.
3. **Puducherry anomaly:** Article 239A grants a legislature, unlike most UTs; Delhi has its own variant under Article 239AA (NCT Act, 1991).
4. **Section 3 vs 4:** Article 3 governs formation/boundary change; Article 4 complements it by excluding boundary changes from being deemed State-internal reorganisations under Article 3.
5. **Residuary Powers:** Article 248 places them with Parliament, not States — a frequent MCQ trap.

#### KAS-Specific Hooks
Expect questions on **Cauvery / Mahadayi** water disputes, the **Kalasa-Banduri Nala** project, the role of the **Karnataka State Finance Commission**, and the Governor's discretionary powers under Article 163. Article 371J becomes a frequent Mains topic because it directly shapes Hyderabad-Karnataka reservation policy.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts
1. "Article 356 is the constitutional Achilles' heel of Indian federalism." Discuss with reference to S.R. Bommai (1994).
2. Examine whether the 101st Constitutional Amendment (GST) has strengthened cooperative federalism or eroded State fiscal autonomy.
