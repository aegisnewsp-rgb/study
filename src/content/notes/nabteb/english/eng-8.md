# Letter Writing

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Letter Writing in NABTEB English Language is the structured composition of a written message to a named recipient for a defined purpose, drawn from **Formal**, **Semi-formal**, or **Informal** registers. NABTEB awards roughly **3–5% of total marks** (about 10 marks) for one letter of **150–250 words** chosen from four prompts. Markers split marks **40% format**, **40% content**, **20% expression**, so mastering the layout yields the fastest score gain.

#### Format Anchors at a Glance

| Block | Formal | Semi-formal | Informal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Writer's address | Top right | Top right | Top right (optional) |
| Date | Right, below address | Right, below address | Left, below salutation line |
| Recipient's address | Left, below date | Left, below date | Omitted (use salutation name) |
| Subject line | Required (underlined) | Sometimes used | Never used |
| Salutation | Dear Sir/Madam or Dear Mr/Mrs/Miss + Surname | Dear Mr/Mrs/Miss + Surname or Dear Uncle/Aunty + first name | Dear + first name (e.g. Dear Chika) |
| Complementary close | Yours faithfully (unknown) / Yours sincerely (known) | Yours sincerely / Yours respectfully | Yours lovingly / Yours affectionately |
| Signature | Signature + printed name | Signature + printed name | First name only (signature suffices) |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"Faithful for the Faceless, Sincere for the Seen"** — use *Yours faithfully* when the recipient's name is unknown (Dear Sir/Madam) and *Yours sincerely* when the name is known (Dear Mr Adeyemi). One slip here is an automatic mark loss.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Conventions NABTEB Tests

NABTEB expects three full blocks in every letter: the **heading block** (writer's address + date + recipient's address for formal/semi-formal), the **opening block** (salutation + optional subject line), and the **closing block** (subscription phrase + signature + printed name). The body itself always sits in **three paragraphs**: paragraph 1 states the purpose, paragraph 2 carries the details or reasons, and paragraph 3 requests action or closes politely. Skipping the third paragraph is a recurring marker complaint.

#### Tone and Register Matrix

| Feature | Formal | Semi-formal | Informal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Person | Third person / impersonal | Mixed | First and second person |
| Contractions (don't, I'm, can't) | Forbidden | Avoid | Acceptable |
| Slang / idioms | Forbidden | Sparingly | Welcome |
| Diction | Polite, precise | Polite, warmer | Conversational |
| Sample close | "Thank you for your prompt attention." | "I shall be grateful if…" | "Give my love to Mama and Sola." |

#### Salutation-to-Close Rule (Exam-Critical)

1. Identify whether the recipient's name appears in the prompt.
2. If **no name is given** (e.g. "The Manager", "The Editor") → salute **Dear Sir/Madam** → close **Yours faithfully**.
3. If a **named person** is given (Mr, Mrs, Miss, Dr + surname) → salute **Dear Mr/Mrs/Miss/Dr + Surname** → close **Yours sincerely**.
4. For relatives or elders without a professional title, write **Dear Uncle/Aunty + first name** → close **Yours sincerely** or **Yours respectfully**.
5. To friends and family, use **Dear + first name** → close **Yours lovingly**, **Yours affectionately**, or **Yours ever**.

#### Common NABTEB Prompt Types

1. **Letter to a friend or family member** recounting an experience (e.g. a holiday, an exam, an illness).
2. **Letter of complaint** to a company, hospital, or government office about a defective product, poor service, or disruption.
3. **Letter of application** for a job, admission, or appointment, often requiring a CV reference.
4. **Letter to the editor** of a national newspaper expressing an opinion on a public issue.
5. **Letter of invitation, apology, sympathy, or congratulations** for personal occasions.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix — Easily Confused Elements

| Element | Correct Position / Form | Common NABTEB Mistake |
|---|---|---|
| Date in formal letter | Right, beneath the writer's address | Placed on the left under the recipient's address |
| Subject line | Underlined, in capital or title case, below salutation in formal letters only | Omitted in formal letters or included in informal letters |
| Recipient's address | Left margin, after date, before salutation | Placed before writer's address (order reversed) |
| Printed name | Directly below signature (e.g. "Adebayo Johnson") | Omitted, leaving only a handwritten signature |
| Enclosure (if any) | Bottom left, beneath printed name (e.g. "Enc: Photocopy of receipt") | Forgotten, or placed in the heading block |
| Reference number | Top right of formal business letter, above writer's address | Confused with the recipient's reference line |

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

#### Question:
*You recently purchased a brand-new refrigerator from CoolMart Stores, Lagos, for ₦185,000. After two weeks, the freezer stopped cooling and the store has ignored your complaints. Write a letter to the Manager of CoolMart Stores, 14 Marina Road, Lagos, complaining about the faulty refrigerator and requesting either a replacement or a full refund within 14 days. Sign as Chinedu Okafor of 7 Bisi Close, Abuja.*

#### Solution:

- **Writer's address:** 7 Bisi Close, Abuja. (Top right)
- **Date:** 15th April, 2026. (Right, below address)
- **Recipient's address:** The Manager, CoolMart Stores, 14 Marina Road, Lagos. (Left)
- **Subject line:** COMPLAINT ABOUT A FAULTY REFRIGERATOR (underlined)
- **Salutation:** Dear Sir/Madam,
- **Body paragraph 1:** State purchase — "I write to draw your attention to a refrigerator, model CM-200, bearing serial number 04521, purchased from your Marina Road branch on 1st April, 2026 for ₦185,000."
- **Body paragraph 2:** Give details — "After only two weeks of use, the freezer compartment stopped cooling. Our several visits to your service centre and telephone calls have produced no response."
- **Body paragraph 3:** Request action — "I therefore request a replacement of the same model or a full refund of ₦185,000 within 14 days of this letter, failing which I shall approach the Consumer Protection Council."
- **Closing:** "Thank you for your prompt attention to this matter."
- **Subscription:** Yours faithfully,
- **Signature:** *(handwritten)* Chinedu Okafor (printed below).

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students write **"Yours sincerely"** because they know the manager exists, but the prompt never names the recipient — so **Dear Sir/Madam + Yours faithfully** is the only correct pairing. Marks are also lost when the writer's home address (Abuja) is written where the recipient's address (Lagos) belongs.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Advanced Traps and Edge Cases

1. **Letters to the editor** carry no recipient address, but the editor's name is often supplied (e.g. "The Editor, The Guardian"). If named, use **Dear Sir/Madam** + **Yours faithfully**; if titled **Dear Mr + surname**, switch to **Yours sincerely**.
2. **Letters of application** frequently demand a reference to an advert. Quote the advert's date, page, and position title in the opening paragraph — NABTEB markers treat this as evidence the student has read the prompt.
3. **Semi-formal letters to teachers or principals** must avoid slang but may carry a warm touch. Use **Dear Sir/Madam** or **Dear Mr/Mrs + Surname**; close with **Yours sincerely** or **Yours respectfully**.
4. **Letters of sympathy** should never lead with the writer's news. Open by acknowledging the recipient's loss, share a brief warm memory of the departed, then offer concrete help.
5. **Letters with enclosures** (CVs, receipts, photocopies) require **Enc:** or **Encl:** notation in the bottom left, beneath the printed name. Forgetting it costs format marks.
6. **Block format indentation** is required by NABTEB: each new paragraph begins flush left, not indented. Some students import indented British style and lose format marks.
7. **Postscripts (P.S.)** are forbidden in formal and semi-formal letters but acceptable in informal ones for an afterthought.
8. **Title spacing** in salutations: write **"Mr"**, **"Mrs"**, **"Miss"**, **"Dr"** without a following full stop in modern NABTEB marking (e.g. *Dear Mr Adeyemi*, not *Dear Mr. Adeyemi*).

#### Connection to Adjacent Topics

Letter Writing in NABTEB shares scoring DNA with **Essay Writing** (Section C alternatives) and **Comprehension** (Section A). The 20% expression mark is graded using the same criteria as essays — correct spelling, subject–verb agreement, appropriate punctuation, and lexical variety. Practising one feeds the other. Letter layouts also reinforce **address conventions** tested in **Report Writing** and **Memo Writing**, where NABTEB occasionally asks students to convert a letter into a memo to test register awareness.

#### Common Mistakes That Cost Marks

1. Writing **Yours faithfully** beneath **Dear Mr Adeyemi** (or vice versa) — the single most penalised error.
2. Placing the date on the left in a formal letter, copying informal letter conventions by mistake.
3. Skipping the **printed name** beneath the signature, which NABTEB marking scheme explicitly requires.
4. Using **"I want to"** or **"I need to"** in formal letters instead of **"I wish to"**, **"I would like to"**, or **"I shall be grateful if…"**.
5. Exceeding 250 words — examiners note that over-long letters contain more language errors, dragging down the 20% expression score.
6. Forgetting the **reference number or subject line** in a job application letter, which signals to the marker that the student cannot distinguish formats.
7. Using **contractions or texting abbreviations** ("u", "pls", "thx") in any formal register; NABTEB's marking scheme is strict on register.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. **Formal:** Write a letter to the Director-General of the National Drug Law Enforcement Agency, Abuja, requesting a public enlightenment campaign against drug abuse in secondary schools in your state. Sign as the Senior Prefect of your school, with a full school address.
2. **Informal:** Write a letter to your cousin abroad describing a recent cultural festival you attended at home and inviting him to visit during the next Christmas break.
