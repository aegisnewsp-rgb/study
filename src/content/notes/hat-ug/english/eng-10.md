# Tenses and Grammar

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your HAT-UG English section.

**Tenses and Grammar** for HAT-UG tests your command of twelve English tenses formed by combining a **time marker** (present, past, future) with an **aspect** (simple, continuous, perfect, perfect continuous). The aspect uses either **have + past participle** (perfect) or **be + present participle** (continuous). HAT-UG presents these rules through **sentence correction**, **error-spotting**, and **fill-in-the-blank** MCQs worth roughly 4% of the total score.

- Master the four conditional types: Type-0 (truth), Type-1 (real future), Type-2 (unreal present), Type-3 (unreal past).
- Memorise the **passive formula**: object + *be* (conjugated) + past participle (+ by-agent).
- Apply the **backshift rule** when the reporting verb is past: present → past, will → would, can → can.

| Time | Aspect marker |
| --- | --- |
| Past / Present / Future | simple (V) / continuous (be + V-ing) / perfect (have + V3) / perfect continuous (have + been + V-ing) |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months before the test.

#### The Twelve-Tense Framework
Every English verb can flex into twelve tenses built from two axes: **time** (past, present, future) and **aspect** (simple, continuous, perfect, perfect continuous). The **simple aspect** carries the bare verb form (*She writes*), **continuous** uses *be + present participle* (*She is writing*), **perfect** uses *have + past participle* (*She has written*), and **perfect continuous** fuses both (*She has been writing*). Continuous tenses stress an action in progress, while perfect tenses link that action to an earlier reference point.

#### Sequence of Tenses and Reported Speech
When the reporting verb sits in the **past tense**, the reported clause must backshift one step. The shift covers tense markers (present → past, present continuous → past continuous, present perfect → past perfect, will → would, can → can), pronouns (I → he/she, my → his/her), and time-place markers (today → that day, here → there, tomorrow → the next day, yesterday → the day before).

#### Voice Transformation and Agreement
Passive formation takes the **object**, the appropriate form of *be* matching the original tense, and the **past participle**. The original object becomes the subject, and the original subject moves into an optional *by*-phrase.

| Original tense | Passive structure |
| --- | --- |
| Simple Present | am/is/are + past participle |
| Simple Past | was/were + past participle |
| Present Perfect | has/have been + past participle |
| Future (will) | will be + past participle |

- A singular subject always takes a **singular verb**; subjects joined by *and* take plural.
- *Either…or* and *neither…nor* make the verb agree with the **nearest subject**.
- Indefinite pronouns (*each, every, someone, everyone, anybody*) demand singular verbs.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students targeting near-perfect accuracy on grammar items.

#### Conditionals and Modals in Depth
Conditional mastery is decisive on HAT-UG. **Type-1** uses *if + simple present* in the condition and *will + base verb* in the result, expressing a real future possibility. **Type-2** swaps to *if + simple past* paired with *would + base verb*, depicting an unreal or unlikely present. **Type-3** employs *if + past perfect* alongside *would have + past participle*, referring to an unreal past. Mixing *will* into the if-clause of any conditional is a classic HAT-UG trap. Modal auxiliaries (*can, can, can, can, must, should*) modify the base verb and observe their own backshift rules: *can → can, will → would, can → can*.

#### Common Examiner Traps
Six errors appear again and again in HAT-UG English items. Watch for them carefully.

1. Confusing **present perfect** (*has gone*) with **simple past** (*went*) when a current-time signal (*just, already, yet, since, for*) is present.
2. Inserting *since* with simple past instead of present perfect — *I know him since 2019* must become *I have known him*.
3. Forgetting to backshift when the reporting clause is past, or backshifting unnecessarily when it is present.
4. Subject-verb disagreement with phrases like *each of the students* or *the team with the coach*.
5. Dropping the past participle in passive sentences (*was wrote* instead of *was written*).
6. Using *will* or *would* inside the if-clause of Type-1 or Type-2 conditionals.

#### Practice Prompts
- Convert: *“The committee will announce the results tomorrow,” the spokesperson said* → Indirect form.
- Correct: *If it will rain tomorrow, we will cancel the match.*
- Transform: *Someone has stolen my wallet* → Passive voice with appropriate tense.
