# Thermal Properties of Matter

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

Thermal physics explores temperature, internal energy, expansion coefficients, calorimetry, and energy transport mechanisms.

Temperature scales convert via linear relation across freezing and boiling reference points of pure water:
$$rac{C}{5} = rac{F - 32}{9} = rac{K - 273.15}{5}$$

Thermal expansion in isotropic solids scales proportionally with temperature increment $\Delta T$:

⚡ **JEE Main Trap:** Water exhibits anomalous expansion between $0^\circ	ext{C}$ and $4^\circ	ext{C}$. As water warms from $0^\circ	ext{C}$ to $4^\circ	ext{C}$, its density increases and volume contracts ($\gamma$ is negative). At $4^\circ	ext{C}$, water achieves its maximum density ($1000	ext{ kg/m}^3$). This property preserves aquatic life beneath frozen lake surfaces in winter.

#### 3. Step-by-Step Worked Example: Calorimetry Multi-Phase Equilibrium

**Problem:** In an insulated calorimeter of negligible heat capacity, $100	ext{ g}$ of ice at $-10^\circ	ext{C}$ is mixed with $100	ext{ g}$ of water at $80^\circ	ext{C}$. Specific heat of ice is $0.5	ext{ cal/g}\cdot^\circ	ext{C}$, latent heat of fusion of ice is $80	ext{ cal/g}$, and specific heat of water is $1.0	ext{ cal/g}\cdot^\circ	ext{C}$. Determine the final equilibrium temperature and the state of the mixture.

**Solution Steps:**
1. Heat required to warm $100	ext{ g}$ of ice from $-10^\circ	ext{C}$ to $0^\circ	ext{C}$:
$$Q_1 = m_{ice} \cdot c_{ice} \cdot \Delta T = 100 	imes 0.5 	imes 10 = 500	ext{ cal}$$
2. Maximum heat released by $100	ext{ g}$ of water cooling from $80^\circ	ext{C}$ to $0^\circ	ext{C}$:
$$Q_{avail} = m_{water} \cdot c_{water} \cdot \Delta T = 100 	imes 1.0 	imes 80 = 8000	ext{ cal}$$
3. Net heat available after warming the ice to $0^\circ	ext{C}$:
$$Q_{rem} = 8000 - 500 = 7500	ext{ cal}$$
4. Heat required to melt all $100	ext{ g}$ of ice at $0^\circ	ext{C}$:
$$Q_{melt} = m_{ice} \cdot L_f = 100 	imes 80 = 8000	ext{ cal}$$
5. Compare remaining heat with required melting heat:
Because $Q_{rem} (7500	ext{ cal}) < Q_{melt} (8000	ext{ cal})$, all the ice cannot melt.
The system remains in a mixed ice-water state at thermal equilibrium temperature $T_{final} = 0^\circ	ext{C}$.
6. Calculate mass of ice that successfully melts:
$$m_{melted} = rac{Q_{rem}}{L_f} = rac{7500}{80} = 93.75	ext{ g}$$
7. Final mixture composition:
- Unmelted ice remaining: $100 - 93.75 = 6.25	ext{ g}$ of ice at $0^\circ	ext{C}$.
- Total liquid water: $100 + 93.75 = 193.75	ext{ g}$ of water at $0^\circ	ext{C}$.
- Equilibrium temperature: $0^\circ	ext{C}$.

#### 3. Step-by-Step Worked Example: Composite Conduction Slab

**Problem:** Two metallic plates of identical thickness $L$ and equal cross-sectional area $A$ are joined face-to-face in series. The second plate has thermal conductivity $K_2 = 100	ext{ W/m}\cdot	ext{K}$ and its outer face is maintained at $T_2 = 0^\circ	ext{C}$. (a) Calculate the steady-state junction temperature $T_j$. (b) Find the equivalent thermal conductivity $K_{eq}$ of the composite slab.

**Solution Steps:**
1. Under steady-state conditions, heat current through plate 1 equals heat current through plate 2:
$$H = rac{K_1 A (T_1 - T_j)}{L} = rac{K_2 A (T_j - T_2)}{L}$$

$$K_1(T_1 - T_j) = K_2(T_j - T_2)$$
$$300(100 - T_j) = 100(T_j - 0)$$
$$3(100 - T_j) = T_j \implies 300 - 3T_j = T_j \implies 4T_j = 300 \implies T_j = 75^\circ	ext{C}$$
