# Static GK — Indian Geography & Physical Features

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your IBPS PO Prelims.

India occupies ~32.87 lakh km² between 8°4′N–37°6′N and 68°7′E–97°25′E, with a ~7,516.6 km coastline. The terrain divides into six physiographic units: **Northern Mountains**, **Northern Plains**, **Peninsular Plateau**, **Indian Desert**, **Coastal Plains**, and **Islands**. **K2 (8,611 m, PoK)** is the highest peak in Indian territory; **Kanchenjunga (8,586 m, Sikkim)** is the highest wholly within India; **Anai Mudi (2,695 m)** tops South India.

- **Narmada** and **Tapi** are the only major **west-flowing** peninsular rivers; they form **estuaries**, not deltas.
- **Tropic of Cancer (23°30′N)** cuts through **8 Indian states**.
- **SW monsoon** (Jun–Sep) drives India's rainfall; **NE monsoon** (Oct–Nov) waters **Tamil Nadu**.
- **Black soil (regur)** suits cotton; **Alluvial** dominates the Indo-Gangetic belt.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content covering mechanisms, classifications, and standard MCQ patterns.

#### Physiographic Divisions

India's landmass is conventionally split into six units, each with distinct rock type, relief, and drainage behaviour.

| Division | Key Feature | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Northern & NE Mountains | Young fold mountains (Himalayas), three parallel ranges | Greater, Lesser, Shivaliks |
| Northern Plains | Alluvial, fertile, formed by Indus–Ganga–Brahmaputra | Uttar Pradesh, Bihar |
| Peninsular Plateau | Ancient shield, igneous + metamorphic | Deccan, Chhota Nagpur |
| Indian Desert | Arid, sand dunes, low rainfall | Thar (Rajasthan) |
| Coastal Plains | Narrow, fertile, deltaic + estuarine | Konkan, Malabar, Coromandel |
| Islands | Coral (Arabian Sea), volcanic (Bay) | Lakshadweep, Andaman & Nicobar |

#### River Direction & Drainage

Most peninsular rivers flow **east** into the Bay of Bengal and build **deltas** (Godavari, Krishna, Cauvery, Mahanadi). **Narmada** and **Tapi** flow **west** through **rift valleys** and form **estuaries**. Himalayan rivers are **perennial**; peninsular rivers are largely **rainfed**.

#### Soil Classification (ICAR)

- **Black/Regur** — basaltic, self-ploughing, cotton-growing; Deccan trap.
- **Red** — iron-rich, crystalline parent rock; Eastern Deccan.
- **Laterite** — heavily leached, aluminium + iron; Western Ghats, NE.
- **Alluvial** — most widespread; Indo-Gangetic, Brahmaputra valley.

#### Monsoon Mechanism

The **SW monsoon** splits at the Kerala coast into the **Arabian Sea branch** (heavy rain on Western Ghats, NE India) and the **Bay of Bengal branch** (Ganga plains, NE states). The **retreating NE monsoon** delivers Oct–Nov rain to the **Tamil Nadu coast**.

#### Common Examiner Traps

- Treating the **Western Ghats** as discontinuous — they are **continuous** and higher than the Eastern Ghats.
- Calling **Sundarbans** a freshwater swamp — it is the world's largest **mangrove** ecosystem and a UNESCO site.
- Saying the **Deccan Plateau** is young — it is an **ancient Precambrian shield**.
- Mixing up **SW monsoon** (all-India) with **NE monsoon** (Tamil Nadu, Oct–Dec).

#### Lake & Wetland Quick-Reference

- **Wular Lake** — largest freshwater lake (J&K).
- **Chilika Lake** — largest brackish lagoon (Odisha).
- **Sambhar Lake** — largest inland saline lake (Rajasthan).

#### Worked Example (IBPS-style MCQ)

> **Q:** Which Indian river flows westward and forms an estuary?
> **A:** Narmada (or Tapi). Both flow through rift valleys into the Arabian Sea; the absence of a delta distinguishes them from east-flowing peninsular rivers like Godavari.

#### Practice Prompts

1. Match each soil type to its parent rock and a representative crop.
2. List the **8 states** through which the **Tropic of Cancer** passes, in west-to-east order.
