# Sentence Variety and Clarity — OSSLT Writing

Sentence variety and clarity affect the "language and vocabulary" dimension of the EQAO OSSLT rubric. A response that mixes simple, compound, and complex sentences — and that uses precise verbs and nouns — scores higher than a response that relies on short, choppy sentences or on long, vague sentences. This skill is central to the long-writing tasks and to the conventions scoring on sentence completeness.

> Verify the live rubric and any in-year task changes on https://www.eqao.com/the-assessments/osslt/ before planning around the figures below.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### What sentence variety means on the OSSLT

- **Simple sentences.** One independent clause. "The school adopted the new program." Clear, direct, but monotonous if overused.
- **Compound sentences.** Two independent clauses joined by a coordinating conjunction (and, but, or, nor, for, yet, so) or by a semicolon. "The school adopted the new program, and attendance improved within a month."
- **Complex sentences.** One independent clause joined to one or more dependent clauses. "After the school adopted the new program, attendance improved."
- **Varied openings.** Sentences that begin with the subject are clear but monotonous. Vary the openings with adverbial phrases, prepositional phrases, or dependent clauses.

#### What sentence clarity means on the OSSLT

- **Precise verbs.** "She walked quickly" is less precise than "She sprinted" or "She darted." Specific verbs replace adverb-heavy phrases.
- **Precise nouns.** "The thing" is vague; "the policy," "the program," "the rule" are specific.
- **No ambiguity.** The reader should not have to re-read the sentence to understand it. Place modifiers next to the words they modify.
- **No wordiness.** Cut filler words ("really," "very," "in order to," "the fact that"). Each cut word tightens the sentence.

#### Examiner traps

- A sentence that is long but vague. Length without clarity scores lower than a shorter, clearer sentence.
- A sentence with a misplaced modifier. "Running quickly, the finish line was crossed by the runner" puts the runner's action on the finish line. The modifier should be next to the word it modifies.
- A sentence that starts the same way as the previous sentence. Variety of openings is part of the rubric.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Strategy for sentence variety

**Mix sentence types.** A response that uses only simple sentences feels choppy. A response that uses only complex sentences feels dense. Mix simple, compound, and complex sentences throughout.

**Vary sentence openings.** Not every sentence should start with the subject. Open some sentences with adverbial phrases ("In the meantime," "For these reasons"), prepositional phrases ("In the schoolyard," "With the new policy"), or dependent clauses ("Although the program was new..."). Aim for at least three different opening patterns per response.

**Use precise verbs.** Replace "walked quickly" with "sprinted," "raced," "hurried." Replace "said loudly" with "shouted," "declared," "announced." Each precise verb replaces an adverb-verb pair with a single word.

**Cut filler words.** "Really," "very," "actually," "in order to," "the fact that" — these words usually add no meaning. Cut them. "She ran very quickly" becomes "She sprinted."

#### Strategy for sentence clarity

**Place modifiers next to the words they modify.** "She almost drove the car into the lake" means something different from "She drove the car almost into the lake." Modifiers should be unambiguous.

**Use active voice where possible.** "The ball was kicked by the boy" is passive and wordy. "The boy kicked the ball" is active and clear.

**Choose specific nouns.** "The thing," "the stuff," "the idea" are vague. "The program," "the policy," "the proposal" are specific.

#### Practice routine for sentence variety and clarity

- Take one EQAO sample long-writing task. Write the response.
- Re-read the response. Count the simple, compound, and complex sentences. Aim for a mix: roughly one-third simple, one-third compound, one-third complex.
- Re-read the response. Count the sentences that start with the subject. Aim for variety: at least half the sentences should start with something other than the subject.
- Cut filler words. Replace vague verbs with precise verbs.
- Time yourself: under 20 minutes for the response, plus 5 minutes for the sentence-variety pass.

#### How this skill connects to the OSSLT reading tasks

Sentence variety and clarity are part of what makes a reading selection readable. The OSSLT reading selections on Information Paragraph and News Report use varied sentence structure to convey the writer's purpose. Recognising sentence variety in reading supports sentence variety in writing.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How sentence variety and clarity connect to the other OSSLT items

- **The Series-of-Paragraphs task** scores language and vocabulary partly on sentence variety. A response with varied sentences and precise word choice scores higher than a response with choppy sentences and vague words.
- **The conventions scoring** on sentence completeness (no run-ons, no fragments) is the floor under sentence variety. A response with varied sentences but frequent run-ons scores lower on conventions.

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"Long sentences are sophisticated."** Length without clarity scores lower than shorter, clearer sentences. Aim for sentences that the reader can understand on first read.
- **"Vague words are safer."** Vague words are not safer; they score lower on language and vocabulary. Specific verbs and nouns are the safer choice.
- **"I should avoid compound sentences."** Compound sentences are useful for showing logical connections. Use them where appropriate.

#### Exam technique

- Write the response. Set the ideas aside.
- Re-read the response for sentence variety. Count the simple, compound, and complex sentences.
- Re-read the response for sentence clarity. Cut filler words. Replace vague verbs with precise verbs.
- Re-read the response for sentence completeness. Catch any run-on sentences or fragments.
- Use the platform's spell-check.
