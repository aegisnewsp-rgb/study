# Standard English Conventions — Grammar and Punctuation — SAT R&W Notes

Standard English Conventions is one of the four SAT R&W content domains. It tests the candidate's command of grammar, punctuation, and usage, with a particular focus on subject-verb agreement, verb tense, pronoun-antecedent agreement, modifier placement, parallel structure, and the rules for commas, semicolons, colons, dashes, and apostrophes.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Subject-verb agreement:** singular subjects take singular verbs; plural subjects take plural verbs.
- **Verb tense:** use the tense that matches the timeline of the action.
- **Pronoun-antecedent agreement:** singular antecedents take singular pronouns; plural antecedents take plural pronouns.
- **Modifier placement:** place modifiers next to what they modify.
- **Parallel structure:** items in a list should be in the same grammatical form.
- **Punctuation:** commas, semicolons, colons, dashes, apostrophes, parentheses, quotation marks.

#### Examiner traps

- Placing a modifier far from what it modifies (creating a dangling modifier).
- Using different grammatical forms in a parallel list.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Subject-verb agreement

- "The list of items is on the table." (singular "list," singular verb "is")
- "The items on the list are missing." (plural "items," plural verb "are")
- "Neither he nor she is coming." (verb agrees with the nearer subject)

#### Verb tense

Use the tense that matches the timeline:

- "Yesterday I walked to the store." (past)
- "Today I am walking to the store." (present continuous)
- "Tomorrow I will walk to the store." (future)

#### Pronoun-antecedent agreement

- "Each student must bring his or her textbook." (singular "each" → singular "his or her")
- "The students must bring their textbooks." (plural "students" → plural "their")

#### Modifier placement

Modifiers should be placed next to the word they modify.

- "Running quickly, the finish line came into view." (wrong — the finish line isn't running)
- "Running quickly, the runner saw the finish line come into view." (correct)

#### Parallel structure

Items in a list should be in the same grammatical form.

- "She likes reading, writing, and to swim." (wrong — mix of gerunds and infinitive)
- "She likes reading, writing, and swimming." (correct — all gerunds)

#### Punctuation rules

- **Comma:** separates items in a list, joins two independent clauses with a conjunction, sets off non-essential clauses.
- **Semicolon:** joins two independent clauses without a conjunction.
- **Colon:** introduces a list, explanation, or quotation.
- **Dash:** sets off an appositive or aside.
- **Apostrophe:** indicates possession or contraction.
- **Parentheses:** contain asides.
- **Quotation marks:** enclose direct speech or titles of short works.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where Standard English Conventions appears across the SAT R&W section

Standard English Conventions items appear across the SAT Reading and Writing section, with grammar and punctuation tested at a level higher than recall. Strong performance here is the single highest-leverage preparation for the SAT R&W section.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Modifiers can be placed anywhere." No — they should be next to what they modify.
- "Lists can mix grammatical forms." No — they should be parallel.
- "Commas and semicolons are interchangeable." No — semicolons join independent clauses; commas do not.

#### Exam technique

- For subject-verb agreement, identify the subject and its number.
- For verb tense, identify the timeline of the action.
- For modifier placement, locate the modifier and what it modifies.
- For parallel structure, identify the items in the list and ensure they match.
