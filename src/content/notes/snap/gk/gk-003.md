# Geography of India & World

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your SNAP exam.

Geography in the SNAP General Knowledge section evaluates physical, agricultural, mineral, and geopolitical geography across India and international trade arteries. Indian territory spans $3,287,263 \text{ sq km}$, stretching $3,214 \text{ km}$ from Indira Col in Jammu & Kashmir / Ladakh to Kanyakumari in Tamil Nadu, and $2,933 \text{ km}$ from Ghuar Mota in Gujarat to Kibithu in Arunachal Pradesh. The standard meridian ($82^\circ 30\text{E}$) passes through Mirzapur near Prayagraj, establishing Indian Standard Time (IST, $\text{UTC} + 5:30$).

| Geographical Feature | Canonical Indian Extreme / Benchmark | Key Geographic Fact | High-Frequency SNAP Trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Northernmost Point** | Indira Col (Siachen Glacier, Ladakh) | $37^\circ 6\text{N}$ latitude | Confusing Jammu & Kashmir UT with Ladakh UT |
| **Southernmost Point** | Indira Point (Great Nicobar Island) | $6^\circ 45\text{N}$ latitude (Submerged partially in 2004 tsunami) | Choosing Kanyakumari ($8^\circ 4\text{N}$, which is mainland southernmost point) |
| **Westernmost Point** | Ghuar Mota / Sir Creek (Kutch, Gujarat) | $68^\circ 7\text{E}$ longitude | Misidentifying Dwarka as the westernmost extreme |
| **Easternmost Point** | Kibithu (Anjaw District, Arunachal Pradesh) | $97^\circ 25\text{E}$ longitude | Confusing Assam or Nagaland with Arunachal Pradesh |
| **Highest Peak in India** | Kangchenjunga ($8,586 \text{ m}$, Sikkim) | Located on India-Nepal border in Sikkim Himalayas | Choosing K2 ($8,611 \text{ m}$), which lies in Pakistan-administered Gilgit-Baltistan |
| **Coastline Total Length** | $7,516.6 \text{ km}$ ($5,422.6 \text{ km}$ mainland) | Gujarat has the longest coastline ($1,214.7 \text{ km}$) | Assuming Andhra Pradesh or Tamil Nadu has longer coastline than Gujarat |

#### 1. Major Indian Soil Classifications (ICAR Classification)

The Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR) classifies Indian soils into eight categories. The four major soils listed below account for over $85\%$ of India's agricultural land area.

| Soil Group | Parent Rock & Origin | Subcontinent Land Coverage | Chemical Balance | Dominant Crops Supported |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Alluvial Soil** | Silt deposited by Indo-Gangetic-Brahmaputra rivers | Approximately $40\%$ ($1.5 \text{ million sq km}$) | Rich in potash and lime; deficient in nitrogen and phosphorus | Wheat, rice, sugarcane, jute, pulses |
| **Black Soil (*Regur*)** | Weathering of Deccan basaltic lava flows | Approximately $16\%$ (Deccan Trap: MH, MP, GJ, KA) | Rich in iron, lime, calcium, magnesium; deficient in nitrogen | Cotton, soybean, groundnut, jowar, tobacco |
| **Red & Yellow Soil** | Crystalline igneous rocks (Granite, Gneiss) | Approximately $18\%$ (Eastern & Southern Deccan) | Ferric oxide gives red tint; deficient in nitrogen, phosphorus, humus | Millets, ragi, pulses, oilseeds, groundnut |
| **Laterite Soil** | Intense leaching under high tropical heat and rainfall | Approximately $3.7\%$ (Western Ghats summits, Meghalaya) | Rich in iron oxide and bauxite; severely leached of silica and lime | Cashew nuts, tea, coffee, rubber, coconut |

#### 2. River Systems: Himalayan vs. Peninsular Drainage

Indian river networks partition into two contrasting hydrologic regimes based on geological age, gradient, and perennial volume.

| Hydrologic Characteristic | Himalayan River Systems (Ganga, Indus, Brahmaputra) | Peninsular River Systems (Godavari, Krishna, Narmada, Tapi) |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary Origin** | Glaciers in Greater Himalayas, Karakoram, and Tibetan plateau | Peninsular plateau ridges, Western Ghats, and Vindhya-Satpura |
| **Nature of Water Flow** | Perennial: Fed by both glacial snowmelt and summer monsoon rain | Seasonal: Reliant exclusively on south-west and north-east monsoon rains |
| **Geological Age & Valley Form** | Geologically youthful; steep gradients, deep V-shaped valleys and gorges | Geologically mature; graded shallow valleys with low longitudinal gradients |
| **Drainage Features** | High silt load; meandering plains, oxbow lakes, extensive deltaic mouths | Non-meandering straight channels; rapids, waterfalls, hard rock beds |
| **Terminal Discharge Forms** | Submerged deltas (Sundarbans formed by Ganga-Brahmaputra) | East-flowing rivers form deltas; west-flowing (Narmada, Tapi) form **estuaries** |
| **Longest Basin Length** | Ganga ($2,525 \text{ km}$ within India) | Godavari ($1,465 \text{ km}$, termed *Dakshin Ganga*) |

|---|---|---|---|

#### International Straits, Canals, and Maritime Choke Points

SNAP frequently features international trade routes and naval choke points controlling global hydrocarbons and shipping transit.

| Maritime Choke Point | Water Bodies Interconnected | Flanking Nations / Landmasses | Strategic & Commercial Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Strait of Malacca** | Andaman Sea (Indian Ocean) and South China Sea (Pacific) | Malaysia, Singapore, and Indonesia (Sumatra) | Main maritime corridor connecting East Asia to South Asia and Europe |
| **Strait of Hormuz** | Persian Gulf and Gulf of Oman (Arabian Sea) | Iran (north) and Oman / UAE (south) | World's premier oil transit choke point; carries $20\%$ of global petroleum |
| **Bab-el-Mandeb** | Red Sea and Gulf of Aden (Indian Ocean) | Yemen (Arabian Peninsula) and Djibouti / Eritrea (Horn of Africa) | Western gateway into the Red Sea and approach to the Suez Canal |
| **Suez Canal** | Mediterranean Sea and Red Sea | Egypt (Sinai Peninsula to the east, African Egypt to the west) | Man-made sea-level canal (opened 1869); bypasses Cape of Good Hope |
| **Panama Canal** | Atlantic Ocean (Caribbean Sea) and Pacific Ocean | Panama (Isthmus of Panama) | Lock-based canal system (opened 1914); connects Atlantic and Pacific |
| **Strait of Gibraltar** | Atlantic Ocean and Mediterranean Sea | Spain / Gibraltar (Europe) and Morocco (Africa) | Sole natural outlet linking the Mediterranean directly to the Atlantic |
| **Bosphorus & Dardanelles** | Black Sea to Sea of Marmara to Aegean Sea | Turkey (Istanbul bisected by Bosphorus) | Regulated by Montreux Convention (1936); critical for Black Sea states |

#### Indian Agricultural Cropping Seasons

SNAP frequently tests the exact calendar boundaries, seasonal temperatures, and crop groupings across India's agricultural cycles:

1. **Kharif Season (Monsoon Crops)**:
   - Sowing: June–July (onset of South-West Monsoon).
   - Harvesting: September–October.
   - Primary Crops: Rice (Paddy), Maize, Jowar, Bajra, Cotton, Jute, Groundnut, Soybean.
   - Climatic Demand: High temperature, heavy rainfall, high humidity.
2. **Rabi Season (Winter Crops)**:
   - Sowing: October–November (retreating monsoon).
   - Harvesting: March–April (onset of summer).
   - Primary Crops: Wheat, Barley, Mustard, Gram, Peas, Linseed.
   - Climatic Demand: Mild winter temperatures for vegetative growth; warm sunny weather for grain maturation.
3. **Zaid Season (Summer Short Crops)**:
   - Sowing & Harvesting: March to June (between Rabi harvest and Kharif sowing).
   - Primary Crops: Watermelon, Muskmelon, Cucumber, Vegetables, Fodder crops.

#### High-Yield SNAP Practice Questions

**Question 1 (Physiographic Extremes):**  
*Prompt:* Which of the following Indian states shares international terrestrial borders with three distinct foreign sovereign nations?  
- (A) Arunachal Pradesh  
- (B) Nagaland  
- (C) Manipur  
- (D) Tripura  

*Detailed Solution:*  
- **Arunachal Pradesh** shares borders with Bhutan (west), China/Tibet (north and north-east), and Myanmar (east). Therefore, it borders three sovereign nations.
- Nagaland borders only Myanmar. Manipur borders only Myanmar. Tripura is surrounded on three sides by Bangladesh alone.
- Other Indian states/UTs bordering three nations are Sikkim (Nepal, Bhutan, China), West Bengal (Bangladesh, Nepal, Bhutan), and Ladakh (Pakistan, China, Afghanistan).  
*Correct Answer:* **(A) Arunachal Pradesh**.

**Question 2 (Maritime Transit):**  
*Prompt:* A commercial container cargo vessel sails from the port of Rotterdam in Europe to Shanghai in China via the shortest standard maritime route. In which sequence does it pass through the following choke points?  
1. Strait of Malacca  
2. Strait of Gibraltar  
3. Bab-el-Mandeb  
4. Suez Canal  

*Detailed Solution:*  
- Leaving Rotterdam in the North Atlantic, the vessel enters the Mediterranean Sea via the **Strait of Gibraltar (2)**.
- It traverses the Mediterranean and enters the Red Sea through the **Suez Canal (4)**.
- Exiting the Red Sea into the Gulf of Aden, it passes through **Bab-el-Mandeb (3)**.
- Sailing across the Indian Ocean toward East Asia, it enters the South China Sea via the **Strait of Malacca (1)**.  
*Sequence:* **2 $\rightarrow$ 4 $\rightarrow$ 3 $\rightarrow$ 1**.

#### Common Pitfalls in SNAP Geography

- **Union Territory Counts**: India comprises **28 States and 8 Union Territories**. On 26 January 2020, Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu were merged into a single UT, reducing the total count from 9 to 8.
- **Tropic of Cancer ($23^\circ 30\text{N}$)**: Passes through **eight Indian states** from west to east: Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura, and Mizoram. (Mahi River crosses the Tropic of Cancer twice).
- **Gulf of Mannar vs. Palk Strait**: Palk Strait connects Palk Bay with the Bay of Bengal; Gulf of Mannar lies south-west of Palk Strait between south-eastern India and western Sri Lanka.
