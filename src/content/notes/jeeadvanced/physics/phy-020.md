# Electromagnetic Induction

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

Magnetic flux $\Phi_B$ measures the total magnetic field passing through an oriented surface area:
1163787\Phi_B = \iint \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{A} = BA\cos\theta1163787

Faraday's law states that an electromotive force (EMF) appears in any closed boundary whenever the magnetic flux linking that boundary changes over time:
1163787\mathcal{E} = -\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}1163787

The negative sign embodies Lenz's law. The induced current flows in a direction whose magnetic field opposes the original change in magnetic flux.

If the external flux increases, the induced field points opposite to the external field. If the external flux decreases, the induced field points along the external field to sustain it.

A straight conductor of length $\ell$ translating with velocity $\vec{v}$ through a uniform magnetic field $\vec{B}$ generates a motional EMF:
1163787\mathcal{E} = \int (\vec{v} \times \vec{B}) \cdot d\vec{\ell}1163787

For mutually perpendicular velocity, length, and field vectors, this simplifies directly to $\mathcal{E} = B\ell v$.

Self-inductance $ relates the back EMF of a single circuit to its own rate of current change:
1163787\mathcal{E}_L = -L\frac{dI}{dt}1163787

The magnetic energy stored inside an inductor carrying steady current $ equals:
1163787U_B = \frac{1}{2}LI^21163787

⚡ **JEE Advanced Trap:** Voltmeter readings in a region with time-varying magnetic fields depend entirely on the path of the voltmeter leads. Because the induced electric field is non-conservative ($\oint \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{\ell} \neq 0$), electric potential is not a single-valued scalar function.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

Electromagnetic induction links mechanics, vector calculus, and circuit dynamics in JEE Advanced. You must distinguish between motional EMF (driven by the magnetic Lorentz force on moving charges) and transformer EMF (driven by an induced non-conservative electric field).

#### 1. Motional EMF in Rigid Conductors

When a rigid conductor sweeps through a static magnetic field, free conduction electrons experience the Lorentz force $\vec{F} = q(\vec{v} \times \vec{B})$. Electrons migrate until an internal electrostatic charge separation balances the magnetic force:
1163787\vec{E}_{int} = -(\vec{v} \times \vec{B})1163787

For a rod of length $ rotating about one end with constant angular velocity $\omega$ perpendicular to uniform field $:
1163787\mathcal{E} = \int_0^L (\omega r B) dr = \frac{1}{2}B\omega L^21163787

#### 2. Induced Non-Conservative Electric Fields

A stationary loop enclosing a time-varying magnetic field experiences an induced electric field $\vec{E}_{ind}$. Faraday's law in differential and integral forms:
1163787\nabla \times \vec{E}_{ind} = -\frac{\partial \vec{B}}{\partial t} \iff \oint \vec{E}_{ind} \cdot d\vec{\ell} = -\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}1163787

Inside a cylindrical region of radius $ where magnetic field varies as (t)$:
- For  < R$: {ind}(2\pi r) = -\pi r^2 \frac{dB}{dt} \implies E_{ind} = -\frac{r}{2}\frac{dB}{dt}$
- For  \ge R$: {ind}(2\pi r) = -\pi R^2 \frac{dB}{dt} \implies E_{ind} = -\frac{R^2}{2r}\frac{dB}{dt}$

#### 3. Core Mechanisms Comparison

| Feature | Motional EMF | Induced Electric Field (Transformer EMF) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Originating Agent** | Lorentz force $\vec{F}_m = q(\vec{v} \times \vec{B})$ on moving charges | Time-dependent magnetic field $\frac{\partial \vec{B}}{\partial t}$ |
| **Electric Field Character** | Purely electrostatic from accumulated boundary charges | Non-conservative, closed field lines with zero divergence |
| **Line Integral $\oint \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{\ell}* | Zero around any stationary closed path | hBc\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt} \ne 0$ around path enclosing varying flux |
| **Governing Force Law** | $\vec{F} = q(\vec{E} + \vec{v} \times \vec{B})$ | $\vec{F} = q\vec{E}_{ind}$ |
| **Typical Problem Type** | Sliding rails, rotating discs, rolling cylinders | Solenoid with varying current, cylindrical field regions |

#### 4. Step-by-Step Worked Example: Rail with Capacitor

**Problem:** A conducting rod of mass $, length $\ell$, and zero electrical resistance rests on two parallel, horizontal frictionless rails separated by distance $\ell$. A uniform magnetic field $ points vertically upward. A capacitor of capacitance $ connects the ends of the rails. At  = 0$, a constant horizontal force $ pulls the rod perpendicular to its length. Determine the acceleration of the rod and the charge on the capacitor as a function of time.

**Solution Steps:**
1. Let the rod have velocity (t)$ directed along the pull force. The induced motional EMF across the rod is:
1163787\mathcal{E}(t) = B\ell v(t)1163787
2. Because rail and rod resistance is zero, the voltage across the capacitor instantaneously equals this motional EMF:
1163787q(t) = C\mathcal{E}(t) = CB\ell v(t)1163787
3. Differentiating charge with respect to time gives the instantaneous current flowing onto the plates:
1163787I(t) = \frac{dq}{dt} = CB\ell \frac{dv}{dt} = CB\ell a(t)1163787
4. The current passing through the rod experiences a magnetic braking force directed opposite to $:
1163787F_{mag} = I(t)\ell B = (CB\ell a)\ell B = CB^2\ell^2 a(t)1163787
5. Apply Newton's second law to the translating rod:
1163787F_0 - CB^2\ell^2 a(t) = m a(t)1163787
1163787F_0 = (m + CB^2\ell^2) a(t)1163787
1163787a(t) = \frac{F_0}{m + CB^2\ell^2} = \text{constant}1163787

The capacitor acts as an apparent additional mechanical inertia equal to  B^2 \ell^2$. The velocity grows linearly as (t) = \frac{F_0 t}{m + CB^2\ell^2}$, and the capacitor charge accumulates according to (t) = \frac{CB\ell F_0 t}{m + CB^2\ell^2}$.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

Deep mastery for top rankers in JEE Advanced requires analyzing self and mutual inductance coupling, transient RL circuits, and eddy current dissipation under mechanical feedback.

#### 1. Inductance Coefficients and Mutual Coupling

Self-inductance relates flux linkage to current: \Phi_B = L I$. For a tightly wound long solenoid with $ turns per meter, cross-sectional area $, and length $\ell$:
1163787L = \mu_0 n^2 A \ell1163787

When two circuits share magnetic flux, the mutual inductance $ obeys reciprocity:
1163787M_{12} = M_{21} = M = k\sqrt{L_1 L_2}1163787
where $ is the coupling coefficient (bash \le k \le 1$).

| Coupling Arrangement | Schematic Description | Equivalent Inductance ({eq}$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Series Aiding** | Flux from coil 1 reinforces flux from coil 2 | {eq} = L_1 + L_2 + 2M$ |
| **Series Opposing** | Flux from coil 1 opposes flux from coil 2 | {eq} = L_1 + L_2 - 2M$ |
| **Parallel Aiding** | Both coils wound in phase across same potential | {eq} = \frac{L_1 L_2 - M^2}{L_1 + L_2 - 2M}$ |
| **Parallel Opposing** | Coils wound in opposite phase across potential | {eq} = \frac{L_1 L_2 - M^2}{L_1 + L_2 + 2M}$ |

#### 2. Transient Response in RL and LC Systems

In a series circuit with battery $\mathcal{E}$, switch, resistor $, and inductor $, closing the switch initiates current growth governed by:
1163787\mathcal{E} - L\frac{dI}{dt} - IR = 0 \implies I(t) = \frac{\mathcal{E}}{R}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau_L}\right)1163787
where inductive relaxation time $\tau_L = \frac{L}{R}$.

At  = 0^+$, the unenergized inductor acts as an open circuit ( = 0$). At  \to \infty$, the inductor acts as a zero-resistance ideal conductor with steady current {max} = \frac{\mathcal{E}}{R}$.

When suddenly breaking an inductor circuit with a switch, current cannot change discontinuously because infinite voltage would arise across the gap. Energy converts rapidly into an arc or charges stray inter-turn capacitance.

In an ideal LC circuit, energy trades between electric field in capacitor and magnetic field in inductor at natural angular frequency:
1163787\omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}1163787
Total energy remains conserved: {tot} = \frac{q(t)^2}{2C} + \frac{1}{2}L I(t)^2 = \frac{Q_0^2}{2C}$.

#### 3. Advanced Problem: Damped Conducting Loop on Inclined Plane

**Problem:** A square loop of wire with side $, mass $, and electrical resistance $ is released from rest on a plane inclined at angle $\theta$ to the horizontal. A uniform vertical magnetic field $ exists in space. Find the terminal velocity $ of the loop as it slides down the frictionless incline.

**Analysis:**
1. The area vector $\vec{A}$ points perpendicular to the inclined plane: magnitude  = a^2$.
2. The angle between vertical magnetic field $\vec{B}$ and normal to incline is $\theta$.
3. When the loop moves with velocity $ along the incline, the flux through the loop is constant if the field is uniform everywhere.
4. However, if the loop is entering or leaving a field boundary, only the front or rear segment cuts flux:
1163787\mathcal{E} = B a v \cos\theta1163787
5. Induced current:  = \frac{B a v \cos\theta}{R}$.
6. The horizontal magnetic force on the horizontal edge has magnitude  = I a B = \frac{B^2 a^2 v \cos\theta}{R}$.
7. The component of this magnetic braking force opposing motion along the incline is:
1163787F_{retard} = F_B \cos\theta = \frac{B^2 a^2 v \cos^2\theta}{R}1163787
8. At terminal velocity $, gravitational pull downslope balances electromagnetic drag:
1163787mg\sin\theta = \frac{B^2 a^2 v_t \cos^2\theta}{R} \implies v_t = \frac{mg R \sin\theta}{B^2 a^2 \cos^2\theta}1163787
