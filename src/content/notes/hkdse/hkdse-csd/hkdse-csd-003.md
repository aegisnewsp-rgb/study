# Theme 2 — Our Country since Reform and Opening-up — HKDSE CSD Notes

This topic covers Topics 1 and 2 of Theme 2 of the HKDSE Citizenship and Social Development curriculum. Topic 1 addresses the development of China's reform and opening-up, the change in people's life and the enhancement of overall national strength. Topic 2 addresses the development plans and policies related to Hong Kong, including the Five-Year Plans, the Greater Bay Area, and the Mainland and Hong Kong Closer Economic Partnership Arrangement (CEPA).

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Topic 1 — change in people's life and national strength**: stages of reform and opening-up (1978 onwards); the key strategies (Special Economic Zones, the household responsibility system, the open-door policy); changes and enhancement of people's quality of life (income, consumption, education, medical care, life expectancy, poverty eradication); enhancement of overall national strength (economy, culture, natural resources, education, technology, national defence).
- **Topic 2 — development and Hong Kong's integration**: the Five-Year Plans (currently the 14th, covering 2021-2025; the 15th covers 2026-2030); development plans related to Hong Kong (the Greater Bay Area, CEPA, support for Hong Kong as an international financial centre); the relationship with the promotion of the development of Hong Kong.
- **Assessment**: data-response and short-essay items on the 2-hour CSD paper.

#### Examiner traps

- Treating "reform and opening-up" as a single event — it is a process spanning from 1978 to the present, with multiple phases.
- Confusing the Greater Bay Area (a regional integration plan covering 9+2 cities) with the Yangtze River Delta or the Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei integration plans.
- Conflating the Five-Year Plans (national economic and social development plans) with the country's long-term development goals.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Reform and opening-up — stages and strategies

**1978-1992: the early phase.** The Third Plenary Session of the 11th Central Committee of the CPC in December 1978 is the conventional start of reform and opening-up. The household responsibility system replaced collective agriculture; Special Economic Zones (Shenzhen, Zhuhai, Shantou, Xiamen) opened to foreign investment; the Open Door policy attracted technology and capital. The 14th Party Congress in 1992 endorsed the establishment of a socialist market economy.

**1992-2001: expansion.** Reforms extended from agriculture to industry and services. State-owned enterprises were restructured. China joined the World Trade Organization in 2001.

**2001-2012: integration with the global economy.** Rapid industrialisation and urbanisation. China became the world's second-largest economy in 2010.

**2012-present: the "New Era".** The Belt and Road Initiative (2013), Made in China 2025, the Healthy China 2030 plan, the dual carbon goals (carbon peaking by 2030, carbon neutrality by 2060), and the emphasis on high-quality development and common prosperity.

#### Changes in people's life

Indicators to cite:

- **Income**: per-capita disposable income has grown from a few hundred RMB in 1978 to over 36,000 RMB in 2023 (national figure).
- **Consumption**: shift from basic necessities to durables, services, and experiences.
- **Education**: nine-year compulsory education universalised; gross enrolment in higher education exceeded 60% by 2023.
- **Medical care**: life expectancy at birth has risen from around 66 years in 1980 to over 78 years in 2023; the National Healthcare Security Administration covers over 95% of the population.
- **Poverty eradication**: declared in February 2021 — the elimination of absolute poverty in rural China under the current poverty line.

#### Overall national strength

Indicators:

- **Economy**: GDP grew from USD 150 billion in 1978 to over USD 17 trillion in 2023 (second-largest in the world).
- **Technology**: 5G networks, BeiDou navigation, the Tiangong space station, AI and quantum computing initiatives.
- **Culture**: UNESCO World Heritage sites, Confucius Institutes, the international reach of Chinese cinema and literature.
- **National defence**: modernisation of the People's Liberation Army, including the development of advanced fighter aircraft, naval vessels and the nuclear deterrent.

#### The Five-Year Plans

The Five-Year Plans (五年规划/计划) are the country's national economic and social development plans. Currently:

- **14th Five-Year Plan (2021-2025)**: focuses on high-quality development, technological self-reliance, the dual carbon goals, common prosperity, and the Greater Bay Area development.
- **15th Five-Year Plan (2026-2030)**: under preparation during 2024-2025; will set the agenda for the next five years.

#### Hong Kong's integration with national development

**The Greater Bay Area**: a regional integration plan covering the Special Administrative Regions of Hong Kong and Macao plus nine Mainland cities in Guangdong (Guangzhou, Shenzhen, Zhuhai, Foshan, Huizhou, Dongguan, Zhongshan, Jiangmen, Zhaoqing). Launched 2017; aims to build an internationally competitive bay-area cluster.

**CEPA — Closer Economic Partnership Arrangement**: a free-trade agreement between Mainland China and Hong Kong, progressively liberalising trade in goods and services. CEPA has been expanded through multiple supplementary agreements since 2003.

**Other integration mechanisms**: Shanghai-Hong Kong Stock Connect, Shenzhen-Hong Kong Stock Connect, Bond Connect, Wealth Management Connect, the Northbound and Southbound trading channels that link Hong Kong and Mainland financial markets.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-topic links

- **Theme 1 (Hong Kong under One Country Two Systems)**: the integration plans connect with the HKSAR's participation in national affairs (Topic 2 of Theme 1).
- **Theme 3 (Interconnected Contemporary World)**: the development plans set the foundation for Hong Kong's role in economic globalisation (Topic 1 of Theme 3) and the Belt and Road Initiative (Topic 3 of Theme 2).
- **National Security Education**: economic security is one of the 11 traditional and non-traditional security domains; the development plans tie into economic security.

#### Exam technique

- **For short-essay items**: cite specific examples with years (e.g. "1992 — 14th Party Congress endorsed the socialist market economy", "2021 — poverty eradication declared"). Examiners reward specific citation.
- **For data-response items**: distinguish the stated policy from the implementation challenges. The CSD paper often presents a real-world scenario; structure your analysis using the policy framework.
- **For "compare and contrast" items**: use a table or two-column structure to lay out the differences clearly.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Reform and opening-up means copying the West." Reform and opening-up adapts market mechanisms while maintaining the socialist system; the official phrase is "socialism with Chinese characteristics".
- "The Greater Bay Area is part of CEPA." They are distinct mechanisms. The Greater Bay Area is a regional integration plan; CEPA is a free-trade agreement.
- "Hong Kong's integration with the Mainland means assimilation." The principle is "one country, two systems" — Hong Kong maintains its separate customs territory, separate currency (HKD), and separate legal system (common law).
