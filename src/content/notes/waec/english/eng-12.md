# Question Tags and Responses

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

A **question tag** is a short question fragment tacked onto the end of a statement to invite confirmation or keep conversation going. It follows a strict polarity rule: a **positive statement** takes a **negative tag**, and a **negative statement** takes a **positive tag**. Structure: comma + auxiliary/modal verb + pronoun — e.g. *She is tired, isn't she?* The tag's **auxiliary must match** the main verb (use *do/does/did* if the main clause has no auxiliary: *He sings, doesn't he?*). The **response is governed by reality**, not by the tag — *You passed, didn't you?* → "Yes, I did." Three exam traps: matching the tag pronoun to the object instead of the subject, using the wrong auxiliary, and contradicting reality when answering. WAEC questions usually appear in Paper 2 as gap-fill or multiple-choice items testing tag formation or the correct response.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Tag Construction Rules
A question tag has three parts: an **auxiliary (or modal) verb**, a **pronoun** referring back to the main-clause subject, and a question mark. It is set off by a **comma**. The polarity is opposite to that of the main clause.

| Main clause | Tag |
|---|---|
| He is a teacher | isn't he? |
| She didn't call | did she? |
| They have left | haven't they? |
| You can swim | can't you? |
| We must go | mustn't we? |

If the main clause contains only a lexical verb (no *be, have, do* or modal), supply *do/does/did* — *She plays tennis, doesn't she?* The pronoun always echoes the subject: *The boys won the match, didn't they?* (never *didn't them?*).

#### Special Forms
- **Aren't I?** replaces the ungrammatical *amn't I?*: *I am late, aren't I?*
- **Imperatives** use *will you?* for commands (*Open the window, will you?*) and *won't you?* for invitations (*Have a seat, won't you?*).
- **Let's** takes *shall we?*: *Let's begin, shall we?*
- **Negative contractions** are standard in tags: *isn't, aren't, wasn't, weren't, didn't, doesn't, haven't, hasn't, wouldn't, shouldn't, can't, mustn't.*

#### The Response Rule
The reply reflects **truth**, not the tag's polarity. If the statement is true, answer *Yes*; if false, answer *No* — regardless of whether the tag is negative or positive. *You aren't hungry, are you?* → if hungry: "Yes, I am"; if not: "No, I am not."

#### Typical WAEC Items
Paper 2 tests tag formation (choose the correct tag), tag-to-statement matching, and short dialogue completion with the right *Yes/No* response. Intonation is not marked in writing, but falling tone signals confirmation-seeking while rising tone signals a real question.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Echo Tags
**Echo tags** repeat or rephrase part of the previous speaker's statement to express surprise, interest, or reaction — *I lost my wallet on the bus.* — *You lost it on the bus, did you?* Here the polarity rule bends: an echo tag may match or restate the speaker's exact words.

**Rhetorical tags** (e.g. *…, right?*) are common in informal speech but should not appear in WAEC formal answers. Likewise, avoid the dialectal *innit?* — the standard form is *isn't it?*

**Tense matching** is strict: if the main verb is past, the tag's auxiliary must also be past — *She wrote the letter, didn't she?* (not *doesn't she?*). With *used to*, use *didn't … use to?* — never *usedn't she?*

**Modal tags** keep the same modal: *He could help, couldn't he?*; *They might come, mightn't they?*; *You will attend, won't you?*

#### Common Mistakes
- Wrong auxiliary: *She can dance, doesn't she?* → must be *can't she?*
- Object pronoun: *I like oranges, don't I?* — correct; but *The boy hurt his leg, didn't he?* — not *didn't him?*
- Double negative in answers: *Yes, I didn't.* — incorrect; proper form is *No, I didn't.*
- Tagging a *wh-* question: never add a tag to *Who came?*

#### Worked Example
*Choose the correct tag: The students have finished the exam, ______?*
Solution: *haven't they?* — auxiliary *have* matches *have finished*; pronoun *they* matches the subject *students*; polarity is negative because the main clause is positive.

#### Practice Prompts
1. Supply the correct tag: *Nobody called while I was out, ______?*
2. Provide the appropriate response: *"You haven't seen my keys, have you?"* (You have not seen them.)
