# Sindh Province and Its Administration

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Sindh** is the southeastern province of Pakistan, created on **14 August 1947** (formally acceded 14 October 1947) after separating from Bombay Presidency. It ranks **3rd by area (~140,914 km²)** and **2nd by population (~47.9 million, Census 2017)**.

- **Capital:** Karachi — simultaneously the **national capital** of Pakistan, the only province with this dual status.
- **Governor** (appointed by the President, ceremonial) vs **Chief Minister** (elected, executive head of cabinet).
- **Legislature:** unicameral **Provincial Assembly of Sindh** with 168 seats (general + reserved).
- **Administration:** **6 Divisions**, **30+ Districts**, sub-divided into Tehsils → Union Councils → Dehs.
- **High Court:** Sindh High Court (SHC), Karachi (principal seat) with benches at Sukkur and Larkana.

> **Exam pointer:** The Governor–CM distinction, Karachi's dual-capital status, and the count of divisions are the three highest-yield PPSC MCQ targets.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Constitutional Basis

Sindh operates under the **1973 Constitution of Pakistan**. Article 1 names it as a federating unit; **Article 197** governs the **appointment of the Chief Secretary**; **Articles 106–113** define the Provincial Assembly's composition, duration, and legislative powers.

#### Dual Polity: Governor and Chief Minister

The **Governor of Sindh** is appointed by the President of Pakistan and acts on the aid-and-advice of the **Chief Minister** (Article 105). The CM, leader of the majority party in the Assembly, heads the **provincial cabinet**, controls the chief secretary-led bureaucracy, and oversees the **Provincial Legislative List** (Federal Legislative List Part-II, Schedule IV).

| Office | Selection | Role | Constitutional Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Governor | Appointed by President | Ceremonial head, assents to bills | Art. 101 |
| Chief Minister | Elected (leader of majority) | Executive head of province | Art. 130 (analogy) |
| Chief Secretary | Appointed by CM via CM Secretariat | Heads civil bureaucracy (PAS) | Art. 197 |
| Speaker | Elected by Assembly | Presides over Assembly | Art. 53 analogy |

#### Administrative Hierarchy

The province cascades as **Divisions → Districts → Sub-divisions (Tehsils) → Union Councils → Dehs** (revenue villages). **Divisional Commissioners** supervise clusters of districts; **Deputy Commissioners (DC)** head individual districts as chief revenue and executive officers. Current count stands at **6 Divisions** (Karachi, Hyderabad, Sukkur, Larkana, Mirpurkhas, Shaheed Benazirabad).

> **Trap:** Pre-2022 reference books list 5 divisions. After 2022 reorganisations (Keamari, South Karachi, Malir restructurings), answer 6.

#### Judiciary and Language

The **Sindh High Court**, established 1906 as the Chief Court of Sindh, sits in Karachi with benches at **Sukkur** and a circuit bench at **Larkana**. **Sindhi** is the official provincial language under provincial statute, with **Urdu** as the national language under Article 251.

> **Exam pointer:** PPSC frequently asks: "Karachi is the capital of ____" — fill-in blanks test whether candidates know it serves both provinces.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Historical Formation

Sindh was separated from the **Bombay Presidency** as a **Chief Commissioner's Province** in **1936** under the Government of India Act 1935 — *not* 1947. It acceded to Pakistan on **14 October 1947**. The first Chief Minister under the 1970–71 elections was **Mumtaz Ali Bhutto** (PPP). The PPP has remained the historically dominant party in Sindh politics since.

#### Administrative Edge Cases

The **Local Government Act 2013 (amended 2022)** restructured local tiers: District Councils, Municipal Corporations (Karachi, Hyderabad, Sukkur), Tehsil Councils, and Union Committees now operate under elected local governments alongside the provincial bureaucracy. District status has been revised — the count exceeds 30 after 2022 bifurcations.

#### Worked Example — Identifying the Correct Official

**Question:** Who heads the provincial executive of Sindh?
- (a) Governor of Sindh (b) Chief Minister of Sindh (c) Chief Secretary (d) Speaker

**Solution:** (b). The Governor is constitutional head but acts on aid-and-advice of the CM (Art. 105). The Chief Secretary heads the bureaucracy, not the executive. Speakers preside over the Assembly, not the executive.

#### Common Mistakes Table

| Mistake | Correction |
|---|---|
| Sindh is 2nd by area | 3rd (after Balochistan, Punjab); 2nd by population |
| Karachi is only Sindh's capital | Karachi is also the **national capital** of Pakistan |
| Provincial Assembly is bicameral | Unicameral — only one house (168 seats) |
| Sindh has 5 divisions | 6 divisions since 2022 reorganisations |
| SHC sits only in Karachi | Principal seat Karachi; benches at Sukkur and Larkana |

#### Practice Prompts

2. Trace Sindh's administrative evolution from a 1936 Chief Commissioner's Province to a 2022 reorganised structure with 6 divisions and 30+ districts.
