# Economics & Business

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Economics & Business** in MAT General Knowledge tests commercial awareness across micro and macro concepts, Indian banking, taxation, and capital markets. The most-tested items are **GDP, GNP, NDP, NNP** national income identities and the **fiscal deficit** family of budget terms. Three formulas cover roughly 60% of calculation questions:

- **GDP** = C + I + G + (X − M), where C, I, G, X, M are private consumption, investment, government spending, exports, and imports (all in INR).
- **Inflation Rate** = [(CPI_t − CPI_(t−1)) / CPI_(t−1)] × 100, where CPI is the Consumer Price Index value.
- **Money Multiplier** = 1 / CRR, where CRR is expressed as a decimal.

**High-yield pointers for MAT:** RBI uses **repo rate** to lend to banks and **reverse repo rate** to borrow from them; CRR is cash parked with RBI (no interest), while SLR is gold and government securities held with banks (interest-earning). BSE **Sensex** tracks 30 stocks, NSE **Nifty 50** tracks 50, both headquartered in Mumbai. India follows an April–March fiscal year.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### National Income Accounting

Indian national income follows a layered structure. **GDP** measures the market value of all final goods produced within domestic territory. **GNP** = GDP + NFIA (Net Factor Income from Abroad), capturing income earned by residents overseas minus income earned by foreigners domestically. **NDP** subtracts depreciation (capital consumption allowance), and **NNP** at market price is the conventional "National Income" figure. Nominal GDP is converted to real GDP using the GDP deflator: Real GDP = (Nominal GDP / Deflator) × 100.

#### Fiscal Terminology Hierarchy

The three deficit concepts nest inside each other. **Revenue Deficit** = Revenue Expenditure − Revenue Receipts, indicating how much current spending is funded by borrowing. **Fiscal Deficit** = Total Expenditure − Total Receipts (excluding borrowings), the headline borrowing number. **Primary Deficit** = Fiscal Deficit − Interest Payments, showing the borrowing needed for non-interest outlays.

> **MAT trap:** Revenue Deficit is a subset of Fiscal Deficit, not a separate parallel measure.

#### Banking and Monetary Tools

| Tool | Function | Who earns |
|---|---|---|
| Repo Rate | RBI lends to banks | Banks pay interest |
| Reverse Repo Rate | RBI borrows from banks | RBI pays interest |
| CRR | Cash banks park with RBI | No interest |
| SLR | Gold + govt securities with bank | Interest earned |

#### Business Organisation Forms

Sole proprietorship has a single owner with unlimited liability. Partnership requires 2–50 partners under the Partnership Act 1932. **Private limited company** needs minimum 2 members and cannot invite the public. **Public limited company** needs minimum 7 members and can raise capital from the public through an **IPO** on either NSE or BSE.

#### LPG Reforms of 1991

**Liberalisation** reduced industrial licensing and import controls. **Privatisation** shifted ownership from public to private hands (and today includes disinvestment of PSU equity). **Globalisation** integrated India with world markets through FDI and trade. NITI Aayog replaced the Planning Commission in January 2015.

> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Worked Numeric Example

A firm's annual turnover is ₹50,00,000 with cost of goods sold at ₹40,00,000 and operating expenses of ₹5,00,000. Selling Price = ₹50,00,000; Cost Price = ₹40,00,000 + ₹5,00,000 = ₹45,00,000.

Profit % = [(50,00,000 − 45,00,000) / 45,00,000] × 100 = **11.11%**.

#### Adjacent Topic Connections

- **Capital vs Money Market:** Capital market handles long-tenure securities (shares, debentures) on NSE/BSE; money market handles short-tenure instruments (treasury bills, commercial paper, certificates of deposit) with maturity under one year.
- **FII vs FDI:** FII is portfolio investment that can exit quickly (under 10% stake); FDI is long-term direct investment typically above 10% stake and brings management control.
- **WPI vs CPI:** WPI tracks wholesale/bulk prices and is the producer's index; CPI tracks retail consumer prices and is the household's index. India shifted headline inflation reporting to CPI combined in 2014.

#### Common Mistakes in MAT

> **Most-marked error:** Treating Sensex as a 500-stock index. BSE Sensex = 30 stocks; broader BSE 500 does exist but is not the headline index.

Other frequent slips: confusing WTO (Geneva, trade) with WHO (Geneva, health); treating CRR and SLR as interchangeable; calling privatisation and globalisation synonyms; confusing IMF (BoP surveillance, headquartered Washington DC) with the World Bank (development lending, also Washington DC but distinct institution); misidentifying shares as debt instruments rather than ownership claims.

#### Practice Prompts

1. If CRR is 4%, what is the money multiplier? (Answer: 1 / 0.04 = **25**)
2. GNP of a country is ₹200 lakh crore and NFIA is ₹10 lakh crore. What is GDP? (Answer: 200 − 10 = **₹190 lakh crore**)
