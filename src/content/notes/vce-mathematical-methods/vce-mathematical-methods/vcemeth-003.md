# Calculus — VCE Mathematical Methods (VCAA 2023) Notes

Calculus is one of the four Areas of Study in VCE Mathematical Methods Units 3 and 4. The 2023 Study Design covers rates of change and the derivative (Unit 3), anti-derivatives and the definite integral (Unit 3-4), then differentiation and integration of combined functions, the chain rule, the product rule, differential equations, and applications of calculus. Calculus appears in every Methods examination paper.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Differentiation rules.** Power rule d/dx (x^n) = n x^(n−1). Product rule d/dx (uv) = u'v + uv'. Quotient rule d/dx (u/v) = (u'v − uv')/v². Chain rule d/dx f(g(x)) = f'(g(x))g'(x).
- **Derivatives of standard functions.** d/dx (e^x) = e^x. d/dx (e^(kx)) = k e^(kx). d/dx (ln x) = 1/x. d/dx (sin x) = cos x. d/dx (cos x) = −sin x.
- **Integration rules.** ∫ x^n dx = x^(n+1)/(n+1) + C for n ≠ −1. ∫ e^x dx = e^x + C. ∫ 1/x dx = ln|x| + C. ∫ sin x dx = −cos x + C. ∫ cos x dx = sin x + C.
- **Fundamental theorem of calculus.** ∫_a^b f'(x) dx = f(b) − f(a).
- **Applications.** Stationary points (where f'(x) = 0); rates of change; optimisation; areas under curves.

#### Examiner traps

- Forgetting the + C on indefinite integrals.
- Mixing up the chain rule: d/dx sin(x²) = cos(x²) × 2x (not just cos(x²)).
- Confusing "rate of change" with "average rate" — rate of change at a point requires the derivative.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Differentiation

Power rule: d/dx (x^n) = n x^(n−1). Sum rule: d/dx (f + g) = f' + g'. Constant multiple: d/dx (c f) = c f'. Product rule: d/dx (uv) = u'v + uv'. Quotient rule: d/dx (u/v) = (u'v − uv')/v². Chain rule: d/dx f(g(x)) = f'(g(x))g'(x).

Examples:
- d/dx (x³ + 5x) = 3x² + 5.
- d/dx (x² sin x) = 2x sin x + x² cos x.
- d/dx (sin(3x)) = cos(3x) × 3 = 3 cos(3x).
- d/dx (e^(x²)) = e^(x²) × 2x = 2x e^(x²).

#### Anti-differentiation (integration)

∫ x^n dx = x^(n+1)/(n+1) + C for n ≠ −1. ∫ e^x dx = e^x + C. ∫ 1/x dx = ln|x| + C. ∫ sin x dx = −cos x + C. ∫ cos x dx = sin x + C. ∫ f'(x) f(x)^n dx pattern: let u = f(x), du = f'(x) dx.

#### Definite integrals

∫_a^b f(x) dx = F(b) − F(a) where F is any antiderivative of f. Areas under curves: positive if the curve is above the x-axis; signed otherwise.

#### Applications of calculus

Rates of change: dy/dx is the instantaneous rate of change of y with respect to x.
Curve sketching: stationary points at f'(x) = 0; sign of f'(x) tells increasing/decreasing; sign of f''(x) tells concave up/down.
Optimisation: solve f'(x) = 0, verify with second derivative test.
Areas between curves: ∫_a^b |f(x) − g(x)| dx.

#### Differential equations

A differential equation is an equation involving a function and its derivatives. First-order separable: dy/dx = f(x)g(y) → dy/g(y) = f(x) dx → integrate both sides. The constant of integration becomes the constant of the family of solutions.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Why Calculus is the largest Methods content strand

- **Calculus is the language of rates and accumulation.** Applied Methods questions — optimisation, kinematics, areas — ultimately reduce to "find the rate of change" or "find the accumulated total".
- **Calculus is a prerequisite for Specialist Mathematics.** Specialist extends to integration by parts, differential equations and vector calculus.

#### Exam technique

- Show every step in differentiation and integration. Method marks are awarded even when the final answer is wrong.
- For optimisation, verify the stationary point. Second derivative test: f''(x) > 0 means minimum, f''(x) < 0 means maximum.
- For definite integrals, evaluate the antiderivative at both endpoints and subtract. Keep careful track of signs.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Differentiation and integration always commute." No — these operations do not commute in general.
- "∫ 1/x dx = ln x." ∫ 1/x dx = ln|x| + C. The absolute value matters for x < 0.
- "f'(x) = 0 means f is constant." No — f'(x) = 0 means f is locally stationary, not constant.
