# Active Passive Voice

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Active voice** uses the SVO pattern: the subject performs the verb's action ("Ravi writes a letter"). **Passive voice** rearranges this so the original object becomes the subject, paired with a form of *be* + past participle (V3): "A letter is written by Ravi." The tense of the passive sentence is fixed by the form of *be*, not by adding "by" alone.

CUET UG English tests this as a sentence-transformation question worth 1 mark each, usually bundled in sets where 4–5 sentences need conversion in under 4 minutes.

- **Active formula:** Subject + verb + object
- **Passive formula:** Object + be + V3 (+ by + agent)
- **Two essentials to memorise:** (1) the *be*-form must match the original tense, and (2) intransitive verbs (sleep, happen, arrive) **cannot** be passivised.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Conversion Rule
The passive construction always uses an appropriate form of the auxiliary *be* followed by the V3 (past participle) of the main verb. The form of *be* mirrors the original tense: *is/am/are* for Simple Present, *was/were* for Simple Past, *has/have been* for Present Perfect, and *will be* for Simple Future. Modals slot in as *modal + be + V3* (e.g., "can be done," "must be taken").

#### Tense-by-Tense Conversion Table
| Active Tense | Active Example | Passive Form of *be* | Passive Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Simple Present | She writes a poem. | is/am/are + V3 | A poem is written by her. |
| Simple Past | He broke the glass. | was/were + V3 | The glass was broken by him. |
| Present Perfect | They have finished the work. | has/have been + V3 | The work has been finished by them. |
| Simple Future | I will help you. | will be + V3 | You will be helped by me. |
| Modal | She can solve this. | modal + be + V3 | This can be solved by her. |

#### Sentence-Type Conversions
- **Imperatives:** "Open the door." → "Let the door be opened."
- **Yes/No questions:** "Did you write the letter?" → "Was the letter written by you?"
- **Wh-questions:** "Who taught you French?" → "By whom were you taught French?"

#### Ditransitive Sentences
With two objects (e.g., "He gave me a book"), two passive versions exist. The preferred passive keeps the **indirect object** as the new subject: "I was given a book." The alternative moves the direct object and requires the preposition *to*: "A book was given to me." Dropping *to* is the most common CUET trap here.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Restrictions
Five constructions **cannot** be converted to passive voice and examiners exploit this:
1. **Intransitive verbs** with no object (*sleep, happen, arrive, die, resemble*).
2. **Present perfect continuous** and **future continuous** tenses — the continuous aspect has no clean passive equivalent.
3. Sentences where the verb is followed by a **bare infinitive or gerund complement** without a direct object (*enjoy swimming, avoid saying*).
4. **Stative verbs** expressing possession or state (*have, own, belong*).
5. **Reflexive pronouns** as objects (*He hurt himself*).

#### Agent Omission Rules
The phrase "by + agent" is dropped when the doer is unknown, obvious, or unimportant ("My bag was stolen," not "by someone"). It is also dropped when the active subject is a generic pronoun like *one, people,* or *they* (e.g., "People speak English here" → "English is spoken here").

#### Common Mistakes Table
| Mistake | Wrong | Correct |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Using V2 instead of V3 | The ball was kicked → "was kick" | The ball was kicked (V3) |
| Wrong *be*-form for tense | The letter is written yesterday. | The letter **was** written yesterday. |
| Passivising intransitive | "The accident was happened." | The accident **happened**. |
| Ditransitive preposition drop | "I was given a book." (from "He gave me a book") | "A book was given **to** me." |

#### Worked Example
**Active:** "The gardener is watering the plants."
**Step 1:** Identify object → *the plants*.
**Step 2:** Choose V3 → *watered*.
**Step 3:** Match *be* to tense (Present Continuous → *is being*).
**Passive:** "The plants are being watered by the gardener."

#### Practice Prompts
1. Convert: "Have they completed the project?" — Write the full passive form and label every auxiliary used.
2. Convert: "Someone must inform the principal immediately." — Decide whether to retain or omit the agent and justify in one sentence.
