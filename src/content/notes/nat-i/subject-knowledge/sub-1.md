# Physics: Mechanics

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Mechanics is the physics of **motion** and the **forces** that cause it. For NAT-I (NTS), the entire sub-section maps onto three layers: **kinematics** (motion described without its causes), **dynamics** (motion caused by forces via **Newton's laws**), and **energy-momentum** conservation. The equations you must recall instantly are **v = u + at**, **s = ut + ½at²**, **v² = u² + 2as**, **F = ma**, **W = Fd cosθ**, **KE = ½mv²**, and **p = mv**. High-yield exam pointers: (1) projectile range is maximum at **θ = 45°**, (2) momentum is conserved only when **net external force = 0**, and (3) weight **W = mg** is a force in newtons, never a mass in kilograms.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Newton's Three Laws
**First law (inertia):** a body at rest stays at rest, and a body in uniform motion stays in uniform motion, unless acted on by a net external force. **Second law:** the net force equals mass times acceleration, **F = ma**, with force in newtons, mass in kg, acceleration in m/s². **Third law:** every action has an equal and opposite reaction — note that action and reaction act on *different* bodies, so they never cancel each other.

#### Kinematics in One Dimension
For constant acceleration **a**, starting velocity **u**, final velocity **v**, displacement **s**, and time **t**:

- **v = u + at**
- **s = ut + ½at²**
- **v² = u² + 2as**

Take **a = −g = −9.8 m/s²** (≈ −10 m/s² in NAT-I numericals) for vertical throws. Sign convention: upward positive, downward negative.

#### Projectile Motion
For a projectile launched with speed **u** at angle **θ** to horizontal:

- **Time of flight:** T = 2u sinθ / g
- **Maximum height:** H = u² sin²θ / (2g)
- **Range:** R = u² sin 2θ / g — maximum when θ = 45°.

Horizontal and vertical motions are independent; horizontal velocity stays constant (ignoring air drag), while vertical motion is uniformly accelerated.

#### Work, Energy, Power
**Work:** W = Fd cosθ, in joules. **Kinetic energy:** KE = ½mv². **Potential energy (gravity):** PE = mgh. **Work–energy theorem:** net work done = ΔKE. **Power:** P = W/t = Fv cosθ, in watts.

#### Momentum and Impulse
**Linear momentum:** p = mv. **Impulse:** J = FΔt = Δp (vector change in momentum). In a collision with no external force, **m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂**.

#### Circular Motion and Gravitation
**Centripetal acceleration:** a_c = v²/r = ω²r. **Centripetal force:** F_c = mv²/r, always directed toward the centre. **Newton's gravitation:** F = Gm₁m₂/r², with G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg². Surface gravity varies with height as **g_h = g(R/(R+h))²**.

#### Typical NAT-I Patterns
Expect 2–3 MCQs from Mechanics in the Subject Knowledge section. Common formats: identify the correct kinematic equation, compute range or time of flight for a given u and θ, apply conservation of momentum in a 1-D collision, or select the correct unit (joule, newton, watt). Numericals are short and use g = 10 m/s².

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Torque and Rotational Equilibrium
**Torque** about a pivot: **τ = rF sinθ**, where θ is the angle between the position vector **r** (from pivot to point of application) and the force **F**. SI unit: newton-metre. A body is in **rotational equilibrium** when **Στ = 0** about any pivot — this is how lever and see-saw problems are solved. The force's line of action must pass through the pivot for its torque to vanish; many NAT-I distractors test exactly this.

#### Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM)
SHM is motion where acceleration is proportional and opposite to displacement: **a = −ω²x**. Standard forms: **x = A sin(ωt)**, **v = Aω cos(ωt)**, **a = −Aω² sin(ωt)**. Period of a **mass–spring system:** T = 2π√(m/k). Period of a **simple pendulum (small oscillations):** T = 2π√(L/g). Use **ω = 2π/T** and **f = 1/T** (Hz); do not confuse angular frequency ω (rad/s) with frequency f.

#### Friction
**Static friction** adjusts up to a maximum: f_s ≤ μ_s N. **Kinetic friction:** f_k = μ_k N, with μ_k < μ_s typically. **Angle of friction** λ satisfies tanλ = μ. On an inclined plane, a block slides when tanθ > μ_s.

#### Common Mistakes to Avoid
- Mixing **mass (kg)** with **weight (N)**; weight is a force.
- Sign errors in **v² = u² + 2as** when deceleration is treated as a positive a.
- Using F = ma for circular motion without recognizing the **centripetal direction** — the net radial force equals mv²/r, not zero.
- Applying momentum conservation when an external impulse (e.g., the ground pushing up) is significant.
- Substituting degrees in SHM/trig formulas; convert to **radians**.
- For projectile range, the formula assumes **launch and landing at the same height**; if heights differ, work component-wise.

#### Adjacent Connections
Mechanics underpins **fluid mechanics**, **wave motion**, and **thermal physics** (kinetic theory). SHM reappears in **sound waves** and **AC circuits** (analogous equations). Gravitation links to **orbital mechanics** via v_orbital = √(GM/r).

#### Practice Prompts
1. A ball is thrown at 20 m/s at 30° above horizontal. Compute (a) maximum height, (b) time of flight, (c) range. (Use g = 10 m/s². Answers: H = 5 m, T = 2 s, R = 20√3 ≈ 34.6 m.)
2. A 2 kg block moving at 3 m/s collides head-on with a 1 kg block at rest. They stick together. Find the common velocity and the kinetic energy lost. (Answers: v = 2 m/s; ΔKE = 3 J.)

Allocate roughly 4 minutes per Subject Knowledge MCQ. If a mechanics question involves many unknowns, scan answer choices first — they often differ by a factor of 2 or involve squaring, hinting at a KE or v² step. Mark kinematics questions requiring g = 9.8 vs g = 10 m/s² from the option magnitudes before computing.
