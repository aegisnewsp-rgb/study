# Task 2 — Cohesion, Lexical Resource and Grammar — IELTS Writing (Qatar)

In the IELTS Academic Writing test, **Task 2** is the **essay task**: an
essay of **250+ words in approximately 40 minutes**. The second
sub-skill is **Cohesion, Lexical Resource and Grammar** — using
**cohesive devices without overusing them**, **paragraphing**, **accurate
subject-verb agreement**, **range of complex sentence structures**,
**academic vocabulary**, and the **four marking criteria** (task
response, coherence and cohesion, lexical resource, grammatical range
and accuracy).

This is the **sixth topic** in the ielts-writing subject (weight 4). It
follows ieltsw-005 (Task 2 — Opinion, Discussion and Problem/Solution
Essays w=4) and is followed by ieltsw-007 (Assessment Criteria w=3).
The four marking criteria are scored **equal-weighted** (each contributes
25% of the band score for Writing). Lexical resource and grammatical
range together account for 50% of the Writing band score.

The examiner scores Task 2 on **task response** (did you address the
prompt? did you present a clear position?), **coherence and cohesion**
(did you use cohesive devices without overusing them? is your essay well-
paragraphed?), **lexical resource** (do you have varied vocabulary? do
you use academic vocabulary? do you use collocations?), and **grammatical
range and accuracy** (do you have a mix of simple and complex sentences?
are your complex sentences accurate? do you have accurate subject-verb
agreement?).

This note shows the LCA version: a **Lite** (5-minute) read, a **Standard**
(15-minute) read, and the **Exam Essentials** table a Qatari candidate can
use to revise in the last hour before the test.

## Lite (4-Step Method)

1. **Plan cohesive devices (2-3 per paragraph, NOT in every sentence).**
   Use *however, moreover, in contrast, on the other hand, for example,
   as a result, consequently, furthermore* sparingly — overuse of
   cohesive devices signals band 6; appropriate use signals band 7+.
2. **Vary sentence structures.** Mix simple sentences (*"Education is
   important."*), compound sentences (*"Education is important, and it
   shapes the future."*), and complex sentences (*"Because education is
   important, it shapes the future of individuals and societies."*).
3. **Use academic vocabulary.** Use *significant, considerable,
   substantial, fundamental, essential* (NOT *big, important, very*).
   Use *demonstrate, indicate, suggest, propose* (NOT *show, say,
   think*).
4. **Check subject-verb agreement and tense consistency.** *"Education
   **is** important"* (NOT *"Education **are** important"*). Past
   tense: *"The government **implemented** the policy last year"*.

**Insight: Cohesion is scored on APPROPRIATE use, NOT on volume.** A
Task 2 that uses a cohesive device in every sentence signals band 6; a
Task 2 that uses 2-3 cohesive devices per paragraph appropriately
signals band 7+. The examiner scores whether cohesive devices are used
**at natural transition points**, not in every sentence.

### 6 Examiner Traps (Cohesion, Lexical Resource and Grammar)

- **The overuse-of-cohesive-devices trap** — candidates use a cohesive
  device in every sentence (*"Moreover, ... Furthermore, ... In
  addition, ..."*). The examiner scores coherence and cohesion. ALWAYS
  use 2-3 cohesive devices per paragraph, NOT in every sentence.
- **The basic-vocabulary trap** — candidates use *very, really, big,
  important, good, bad* without academic alternatives. ALWAYS use
  academic vocabulary (*significant, considerable, substantial,
  fundamental, essential*).
- **The subject-verb-agreement trap** — candidates write *"Education
  are important"* (singular subject + plural verb). ALWAYS check
  subject-verb agreement.
- **The tense-consistency trap** — candidates mix past tense, present
  tense, and present perfect within one paragraph. ALWAYS use one
  consistent tense within a paragraph; switch only for clear
  time-reference reasons.
- **The simple-sentences-only trap** — candidates write all simple
  sentences. ALWAYS mix simple, compound, and complex sentences.
- **The no-paragraphing trap** — candidates write the essay as one
  paragraph. ALWAYS use 4 paragraphs (introduction + 2 body
  paragraphs + conclusion).

## Standard (7-Step Method with Long-Form Guidance)

### Step 1 — Plan cohesive devices (2-3 per paragraph)

Before you write anything, plan:

- **Cohesive devices to use** — *however, moreover, in contrast, on the
  other hand, for example, as a result, consequently, furthermore, in
  addition, similarly, likewise, on the contrary*.
- **Frequency** — 2-3 per paragraph, NOT in every sentence.
- **Where to use** — at natural transition points (between ideas,
  between paragraphs).

This planning takes 1-2 minutes but saves 5-10 minutes of uncertain
writing later.

### Step 2 — Plan sentence structure variation

Plan a mix of:

- **Simple sentences** — *"Education is important."*
- **Compound sentences** — *"Education is important, and it shapes the
  future."*
- **Complex sentences** — *"Because education is important, it shapes
  the future of individuals and societies."*

Aim for 60% complex sentences, 25% compound sentences, 15% simple
sentences.

### Step 3 — Plan academic vocabulary

Plan to use:

- **Adjectives** — *significant, considerable, substantial, fundamental,
  essential, crucial, vital, major, minor*.
- **Verbs** — *demonstrate, indicate, suggest, propose, argue, claim,
  contend, maintain, assert*.
- **Nouns** — *significance, consideration, proposal, argument, claim,
  contention*.
- **Adverbs** — *significantly, considerably, substantially,
  fundamentally, essentially, crucially*.

### Step 4 — Write the introduction (3 minutes)

The introduction has 2 parts: **paraphrase the prompt** + **state your
thesis**. Use academic vocabulary and complex sentences.

Example:

> *"It is often argued that higher education should be free for all
> students. While this proposal has considerable merit, I contend that
> the financial implications render it impractical in most
> contemporary settings."*

### Step 5 — Write body paragraphs (12 minutes each)

Each body paragraph has 3 parts: **topic sentence** + **explanation** +
**example**. Use cohesive devices at transition points and academic
vocabulary throughout.

Body paragraph 1:

> *"First, the financial implications of free higher education are
> substantial. Government funding for free higher education would
> require significant budget reallocation, potentially reducing funding
> for essential services such as healthcare and infrastructure.
> Consequently, this proposal may create more problems than it solves."*

### Step 6 — Write counter-argument paragraph (8 minutes)

The counter-argument paragraph acknowledges the other side + refutes.
Use cohesive devices at the transition.

> *"Admittedly, free higher education would significantly increase
> access for low-income students. However, this benefit must be weighed
> against the considerable costs to government budgets and the
> potential reduction in funding for other crucial services."*

### Step 7 — Write conclusion (3 minutes)

The conclusion restates the thesis + summarizes the main points. Use
academic vocabulary and cohesive devices at transition points.

> *"In summary, while free higher education has some merit, the
> substantial financial implications make it impractical in most
> settings. A more balanced approach would involve targeted subsidies
> for low-income students, combined with income-contingent loans for
> others."*

### 8 Cohesive Devices by Function (Table)

| Function                    | Cohesive devices                                | Use case                                      |
|-----------------------------|--------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|
| Addition                    | *moreover, furthermore, in addition, additionally* | Adding a supporting point                    |
| Contrast                    | *however, nevertheless, on the contrary, in contrast* | Showing a contrasting point                |
| Cause / effect             | *as a result, consequently, therefore, thus*    | Showing the result of a previous point       |
| Illustration               | *for example, for instance, such as, namely*    | Providing an example                          |
| Sequence                   | *first, second, finally, subsequently*          | Showing the order of points                  |
| Conclusion                 | *in summary, to summarize, in the final paragraph*       | Summarizing the main points                   |
| Concession                 | *admittedly, certainly, of course, granted*      | Acknowledging the other side                  |
| Similarity                 | *similarly, likewise, in the same way*          | Showing similarity to a previous point       |

## High-Yield Cohesion, Lexical and Grammar Examples (7 rows)

These are the kinds of cohesion, lexical, and grammar examples IELTS

   moreover, in contrast, on the other hand, for example, as a result,
   consequently, furthermore*.
2. **Lexical resource** — use *significant, considerable, substantial,

3. **Grammar - simple** — *"Education is important."*

## Previous Year Patterns (IELTS 2018–2025)

IELTS Writing Task 2 cohesion, lexical resource, and grammar patterns
have been consistent since 2018. Over 2018–2025:

- 2018–2020: Overuse of *moreover, furthermore, in addition* was the
  most common error. Subject-verb agreement errors (*Education are...*)
  were common.
- 2021–2023: Use of academic vocabulary increased. Tense mixing was
  common.
- 2024–2025: Cohesion patterns stabilized; lexical resource and
  grammatical range became more discriminating.

The pattern: IELTS Writing Task 2 cohesion, lexical, and grammar
expectations have become more discriminating since 2018. Candidates
who use academic vocabulary and varied sentence structures outperform
those who use basic vocabulary and only simple sentences.

## Pro Tips (10 enumeration)

1. **Plan cohesive devices (2-3 per paragraph).** Use *however, moreover,
   in contrast, on the other hand, for example, as a result*.
2. **Plan sentence variation (60% complex, 25% compound, 15% simple).**
   Mix simple, compound, and complex sentences.

   fundamental, essential, demonstrate, indicate, suggest, propose*.
4. **Check subject-verb agreement.** Singular subject + singular verb.
5. **Use consistent tense within paragraphs.** One consistent tense.
6. **Write 4 paragraphs (introduction + 2 body + conclusion).** Do not
   write one big paragraph.
7. **Use cohesive devices at transitions.** Between ideas, between
   paragraphs.
8. **Aim for 280-320 words.** Not less than 250 words.
