# Foundation Level Maths and Points

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your CAO application.

**Foundation Level Maths and Points** — Quick Facts for Irish Leaving Certificate students

**What is Foundation Maths in CAO?**
Foundation Level Mathematics is a Leaving Certificate subject available to students who would find Ordinary Level Maths too challenging. It is recorded as **F1, F2, or F3** on your transcript. **Foundation Maths does NOT contribute any points to your CAO aggregate.** Grade is recorded; points are zero.

**At a glance:**
- Foundation Level Maths grade recorded on transcript
- BUT contributes **0 points** to your CAO aggregate
- Subject count check: counts as one of your Leaving Certificate subjects (min 6)
- BUT does NOT count as one of your six best subjects
- Course-specific check: many Level 8 STEM courses require **O6/H7 in OL/HL Maths** OR **a pass in Foundation Maths** as a separate matriculation requirement

**Why it matters for offers:**
Many Level 8 STEM programmes (engineering, science, computing, nursing, some health-science courses) set a **separate matriculation minimum in Mathematics**: usually **O6/H7 in OL/HL Maths** OR **a pass in Foundation Maths (F6 or above)**. The course requirement is independent of your six-best-subjects aggregate — even with 625 points, you can be ineligible for an engineering course if you lack the maths matriculation.

⚡ **Quick rule:** If you are sitting Foundation Maths, check the **matriculation minimum** for your specific course. The aggregate does not move on Foundation Maths grade, but the eligibility gate often does.

**Foundation Level Maths and Points** — CAO Points Study Guide

**What is the Leaving Certificate Foundation Maths pathway?**
Foundation Level Mathematics is part of a three-tier Leaving Certificate Maths offer:

| Tier | Grades recorded | Points contribution |
|---|---|---|
| Higher Level Maths (HL) | H1-H8 | Yes — full CPS values (H1 = 100 ... H8 = 0) |
| Ordinary Level Maths (OL) | O1-O8 | Yes — full CPS values (O1 = 56 ... O8 = 0) |
| Foundation Level Maths (FL) | F1, F2, F3 | **No** — 0 points regardless of grade |

The Foundation Maths pathway is intended for students who would otherwise struggle with the OL Maths syllabus. Schools offer Foundation Maths to students who are unlikely to achieve O6 in OL Maths.

**How Foundation Maths interacts with the six-best-subjects rule:**
The six-best-subjects rule selects the six subjects with the **highest CPS values** for your aggregate. Foundation Maths contributes a CPS value of **0**, regardless of whether you achieve F1, F2, or F3. Therefore:

- Foundation Maths is NOT one of your six best subjects under the standard scoring
- Your aggregate is calculated from your six highest CPS subjects, excluding Foundation Maths (unless ALL of your scoring subjects are below F-grade level, which is not possible under the normal scoring pattern)

The practical implication is that you should NOT count Foundation Maths as a scoring subject for the six-best calculation. If you take Foundation Maths, you need five other scoring subjects at minimum to reach the six-best total (your Foundation Maths + 5 others = 6 total subjects but only 5 contribute to points).

**Worked example:** Subjects and grades: English H4 (66), Maths F2 (0), Irish H5 (56), Biology H5 (56), Chemistry H5 (56), Physics H6 (40), Music H5 (56). CPS values: [66, 56, 56, 56, 56, 56, 40, 0]. Six-best CPS sum (excluding Foundation Maths): 66 + 56 + 56 + 56 + 56 + 56 = 346. No HL Maths H6+ bonus applies (student took Foundation Maths, not HL). Aggregate = 346 (no bonus).

If this student had taken OL Maths instead and achieved an O2 (46), the six-best CPS would be 66 + 56 + 56 + 56 + 56 + 56 = 346 (same aggregate because OL Maths O2 = 46 would displace Music H5 = 56 if O2 was better — actually O2 < H5 so no displacement; the aggregate would be 66 + 56 + 56 + 56 + 56 + 46 = 336). In short: taking OL Maths at a typical grade gives fewer points than taking Foundation Maths (because Foundation gives 0) — so the choice between OL and Foundation is a trade-off between potential points and realistic grade potential.

**Course-specific Foundation Maths matriculation:**
Many Level 8 STEM courses (especially engineering, science, computing, pharmacy, nursing, and some health-science programmes) publish a separate **matriculation minimum in Mathematics**. The typical matriculation minimum is one of:

- O6/H7 in Ordinary Level Mathematics (some courses specify HL)
- F6 or above in Foundation Mathematics (a "pass" in Foundation Maths)
- A pass in Foundation Maths (some courses use a broader "pass" definition)

The matriculation minimum is **independent** of your six-best-subjects aggregate. CAO applies BOTH the aggregate cut-off AND the matriculation minimum when making offers. For example, an engineering programme with a 2024 course cut-off of 450 points and a maths matriculation of O6/H7 OR Foundation Maths pass will:

- Offer places to candidates whose aggregate is ≥ 450 points AND
- Either O6/H7 in OL/HL Maths OR a pass in Foundation Maths

Even if you have 600+ points, you will NOT be eligible if your maths grade is below the matriculation minimum (in either OL or Foundation). Check the specific course handbook for your course's maths matriculation requirement.

**Which courses accept Foundation Maths?**
Courses that explicitly list "Foundation Maths pass" as a matriculation option in their course entry requirements typically include:

- Many Level 7 programmes (ordinary bachelor degrees)
- Some Level 8 programmes in arts, social science, business, humanities, and education
- Some health-science programmes (nursing, midwifery, and allied health, depending on the institution)
- Some engineering and science programmes (less common — usually require OL Maths or HL Maths)

Courses that typically do NOT accept Foundation Maths for the maths matriculation:

- Engineering programmes (most require O6/H7 or HL Maths)
- Medicine, dentistry, veterinary medicine (typically require HL Maths or higher in specific subjects)
- Pharmacy programmes (usually require HL Maths)
- Some science programmes (often require O6/H7 OL Maths)

Always check the specific course handbook for your course — the requirements vary by institution and year.

**Can you upgrade from Foundation to OL Maths?**
**Yes, you can.** Many schools allow students to transfer from Foundation Maths to Ordinary Level Maths in the early part of 5th year or 6th year. The transfer is subject to school policy and student capability. If you take Foundation Maths in 5th year but achieve strong results, you can move to OL Maths in 6th year and aim for the O6/H7 threshold.

If you start with Foundation Maths and achieve F1 (the highest Foundation grade), many schools will allow you to retake Maths at OL in 6th year. Some schools will let you attempt BOTH Foundation and OL Maths in 6th year, with the higher grade contributing to your transcript. Check with your school's maths department.

**The implications for university planning:**
Foundation Maths students often face a more constrained set of university options because the Foundation Maths pass may not satisfy the maths matriculation for many Level 8 STEM programmes. The typical planning approach is:

- Identify 2-3 university courses that explicitly accept Foundation Maths as a matriculation option
- Identify 2-3 alternative courses with broader maths matriculation (e.g., "O6/H7 OR Foundation Maths pass")
- Apply through CAO AND through alternative entry pathways (QQI FET, mature student entry, HEAR, DARE) to maximise your options

See cao-005 for the full matriculation minimum requirements and cao-008 for the order of course choices strategy.

**Difference between F1, F2, F3:**
The grades F1, F2, F3 represent achievement levels in Foundation Maths. F1 is the highest, F3 is the lowest. All three grades contribute **0 points** to your CAO aggregate. The grades are recorded on your transcript. Some courses may have a "pass" threshold (typically F6 or above, which is not always applicable because Foundation Maths grades are F1-F3 only) — always check the specific course's maths matriculation requirement.

**Important: Foundation Maths is NOT an alternative to passing Maths entirely**
Foundation Maths is recorded on your transcript as a pass-grade subject. It counts toward the **six-subject minimum** for the Leaving Certificate (most schools require six subjects, including English and Maths at some level). It does NOT exempt you from sitting Maths entirely; you must take Maths at HL, OL, or Foundation Level for the Leaving Certificate.

**Common myth: "Foundation Maths is for less able students"**
Foundation Maths is a legitimate academic pathway for students who would otherwise not engage with secondary-school mathematics. The level of mathematics in Foundation Maths is designed to be achievable by students who find OL Maths too challenging. Foundation Maths students often progress to further education through PLC (Post-Leaving Certificate) courses, apprenticeships, or specific university programmes that accept Foundation Maths.

Many successful professionals began with Foundation Maths and used alternative entry pathways to higher education. The choice of HL/OL/Foundation Maths is a personal academic decision based on your strengths and goals.

**Comparison with prior to 2017:**
Before the 2017 Common Points Scale reform, Foundation Maths (then called "Foundation Level Maths") had a smaller points contribution (1-5 points depending on grade) and a different role in the aggregate. Since 2017, Foundation Maths contributes **0 points** to the aggregate, and the maths matriculation requirement is a separate course-specific check. The 2017 reform simplified the scoring but tightened the course eligibility gate for some courses.
