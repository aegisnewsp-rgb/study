# Laws of Motion

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your BUET Admission Physics paper.

Newton's **three laws of motion** govern how forces change the motion of a body. **Inertia** (1st law) keeps a body in its state of rest or uniform motion unless a net external force acts. **Newton's second law** defines force quantitatively as $\vec{F} = m\vec{a}$, where $m$ is mass in kg and $\vec{a}$ is acceleration in m·s⁻². The **third law** states every action has an equal and opposite reaction acting on a *different* body.

Key quantities every BUET question uses:

| Quantity | Formula | SI Unit | Vector/Scalar |
|---|---|---|---|
| Force | $F = ma$ | N (kg·m·s⁻²) | Vector |
| Linear momentum | $p = mv$ | kg·m·s⁻¹ | Vector |
| Impulse | $J = F\Delta t = \Delta p$ | N·s | Vector |
| Centripetal force | $F_c = mv^2/r$ | N | Vector (inward) |
| Friction (kinetic) | $f_k = \mu_k N$ | N | Vector (opposes motion) |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"I-F-A"** — **I**nertia, **F**=ma, **A**ction-Reaction (on different bodies). For friction recall **"μN, μN — static bigger, kinetic smaller"** ($\mu_s > \mu_k$). For incline verge-of-sliding, the shortcut is **tan θ = μ**.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months before BUET Admission.

#### Core Framework
Newton's second law in its most general form is $\vec{F} = d\vec{p}/dt$. For constant mass it reduces to $\vec{F} = m\vec{a}$. The **law of conservation of linear momentum** follows directly: if the net external force on a system is zero, the total momentum $\vec{p}_{total}$ remains constant. This principle governs collisions, explosions, and recoil.

A **free body diagram (FBD)** must be drawn *before* writing any equation. All forces — weight $mg$, normal $N$, tension $T$, friction $f$, applied pull $P$ — are marked, then resolved along chosen $x$ and $y$ axes. Sign discipline (taking up-right as positive) prevents the most common BUET mistakes.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix

| Feature | Static Friction | Kinetic Friction |
|---|---|---|
| Formula | $f_s \le \mu_s N$ (self-adjusting) | $f_k = \mu_k N$ (fixed magnitude) |
| Range | $0$ to $\mu_s N$ | Constant at $\mu_k N$ once sliding starts |
| Coefficient | $\mu_s$ (larger) | $\mu_k$ (smaller) |
| Direction | Opposes *impending* motion | Opposes *actual* relative motion |

| Feature | Real Inertial Force | Pseudo (Fictitious) Force |
|---|---|---|
| Frame | Inertial (no acceleration) | Non-inertial (accelerating lift, car) |
| Source | Physical contact/gravity | Frame's acceleration $\vec{a}$ |
| Magnitude | Depends on interaction | $m\vec{a}$, opposite to $\vec{a}$ of frame |
| Example | Tension in string | $m\vec{a}$ backward in accelerating car |

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** A 4 kg block rests on a horizontal surface with $\mu_s = 0.5$ and $\mu_k = 0.3$. A horizontal force of 22 N is applied. Find (a) the friction force on the block, (b) the block's acceleration. Take $g = 9.8$ m·s⁻².

#### Solution:
1. Normal reaction: $N = mg = 4 \times 9.8 = 39.2$ N.
2. Maximum static friction: $f_{s,max} = \mu_s N = 0.5 \times 39.2 = 19.6$ N.
3. Since applied force $F = 22$ N $> f_{s,max} = 19.6$ N, the block slides.
4. Kinetic friction now acts: $f_k = \mu_k N = 0.3 \times 39.2 = 11.76$ N.
5. Net force: $F_{net} = 22 - 11.76 = 10.24$ N.
6. Acceleration: $a = F_{net}/m = 10.24/4 = 2.56$ m·s⁻².

**Answers:** (a) friction = 11.76 N (kinetic), (b) $a = 2.56$ m·s⁻².

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students plug $F = 22$ N directly into $a = F/m$ and report $a = 5.5$ m·s⁻², **forgetting to subtract kinetic friction**. Always check whether the block actually moves by comparing applied force to $\mu_s N$ first — if it does not exceed $f_{s,max}$, the friction is *self-adjusting* up to that limit and acceleration is zero.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Boundary Conditions
**Pseudo force in a lift:** Apparent weight $W' = m(g + a)$ when the lift accelerates upward and $W' = m(g - a)$ when it accelerates downward. If the cable snaps and $a = g$, $W' = 0$ — the occupants feel weightless, identical to free fall.

**Atwood machine with frictionless pulley:** Two masses $m_1 > m_2$ connected by an inextensible string. The acceleration is $a = (m_1 - m_2)g/(m_1 + m_2)$ and the tension $T = 2m_1 m_2 g/(m_1 + m_2)$. Sign discipline: take the direction of motion of $m_1$ as positive for that mass and negative for $m_2$.

**Elastic 1-D collision formulas** (memorise for BUET numericals):

$$v_1' = \frac{m_1 - m_2}{m_1 + m_2}v_1 + \frac{2m_2}{m_1 + m_2}v_2, \quad v_2' = \frac{m_2 - m_1}{m_1 + m_2}v_2 + \frac{2m_1}{m_1 + m_2}v_1$$

Special cases: equal masses swap velocities ($v_1' = v_2$, $v_2' = v_1$); a very heavy target ($m_2 \gg m_1$) leaves $v_1' \approx -v_1$ (reversal); a very heavy projectile ($m_1 \gg m_2$) gives $v_2' \approx 2v_1$ and $v_1' \approx v_1$.

#### Advanced Traps Table

| Trap | Why students fall for it | Correct approach |
|---|---|---|
| Action-reaction cancellation | Both forces look "equal" | They act on *different* bodies — never sum to zero |
| Incline friction $= \mu g \sin \theta$ | Confusing sine/tangent | Driving force is $mg\sin\theta$, friction is $\mu mg\cos\theta$ |
| Conservation of momentum with friction present | External horizontal force exists | Momentum is conserved only along the direction with $\sum F_{ext} = 0$ |
| Centripetal force added as a separate outward force | Misidentifying it as a new force | It is the *net inward* force from tension, gravity, or normal |

#### Worked Micro-Example
A 0.2 kg ball moves at 6 m·s⁻¹ and is struck by a bat, reversing its velocity to −4 m·s⁻² in 0.02 s. Find the impulse and average force.
$\Delta p = m(v_f - v_i) = 0.2(-4 - 6) = -2$ kg·m·s⁻¹. Impulse $J = -2$ N·s. Average force $F_{avg} = J/\Delta t = -2/0.02 = -100$ N (negative sign indicates opposite to original direction).

#### Practice Prompts
1. Two blocks of 5 kg and 3 kg connected by a string over a frictionless pulley — find $a$ and $T$ when released from rest.
2. A car rounds a flat curve of radius 50 m at 20 m·s⁻¹. What is the minimum $\mu_s$ so it does not skid?
