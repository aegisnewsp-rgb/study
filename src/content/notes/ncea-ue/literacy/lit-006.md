# Geography and other social science literacy standards (Level 2 and Level 3) — NCEA UE Literacy Notes

Beyond English and History, several other Level 2 and Level 3 subjects contribute to the University Entrance literacy requirement. These include Geography, Media Studies, Classical Studies, Religious Studies, Art History, and other social science and humanities subjects. A student taking any combination of these subjects can earn UE literacy credits.

> Verify the live UE literacy standards spreadsheet on https://www2.nzqa.govt.nz/ncea/understanding-secondary-quals/university-entrance/literacy-for-ue/ before planning revision around the figures below.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Reading and writing credits** available through a range of social science and humanities subjects at Level 2 and Level 3.
- Common contributing subjects: Geography, Media Studies, Classical Studies, Religious Studies, Art History, and others.
- Each contributing standard has its UE literacy contribution listed on the NZQA spreadsheet.
- Many students satisfy UE literacy entirely through English and one or two other subjects, without needing the dedicated co-requisite unit standards.

#### Examiner traps

- Assuming "Geography" automatically contributes — only specific Geography standards are on the UE literacy list; check the spreadsheet.
- Confusing credits across subjects — a Geography essay and a History essay both contribute, but they are awarded by different standards.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Geography standards contributing to UE literacy

Common contributing Geography standards include:

- AS 91240 (Demonstrate geographic understanding of a large natural environment): typically reading and writing credits (essay-based).
- AS 91241 (Demonstrate geographic understanding of differences in development): typically reading and writing credits.
- AS 91430 (Conduct geographic research): typically writing credits.
- Other standards may contribute; check the UE literacy spreadsheet.

#### Media Studies standards contributing to UE literacy

Media Studies standards test critical engagement with media texts:

- AS 91249 (Demonstrate understanding of the meaning of a media text): reading credits.
- AS 91250 (Demonstrate understanding of the media's role in society): reading and writing credits.
- AS 91251 (Demonstrate understanding of a media genre): reading credits.
- AS 91253 (Produce a media product): writing credits.

#### Classical Studies, Religious Studies, Art History

These subjects contribute through standards that test reading and writing about texts, ideas, and images:

- Classical Studies standards (e.g., AS 91257 about a classical text): reading credits.
- Religious Studies standards (e.g., AS 90821 about a religious tradition): reading and writing credits.
- Art History standards (e.g., AS 91482 about a work of art): reading and writing credits.

#### Subject combinations and UE planning

A typical UE literacy strategy might be:

- English (Level 2 or 3): 6-10 credits toward UE.
- History or Geography (Level 2 or 3): 4-6 credits toward UE.
- Total: 10-16 credits, easily satisfying UE.

This is often easier than relying solely on the dedicated co-requisite unit standards.

#### Preparation strategy

For each subject:

1. **Read the standard** — know what is being assessed.
2. **Practice the genre** — essays, research reports, source analyses.
3. **Use exemplars** — NZQA publishes student work at each grade level.
4. **Get feedback** — from teachers and peers.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Excellence in social science writing

The biggest gap between Merit and Excellence in social science writing is the depth of analysis and the use of evidence.

For example, in a Geography essay on differences in development:

- Merit: Describes indicators of development (GDP, HDI, life expectancy), notes that some countries have higher development than others.
- Excellence: Describes indicators, AND analyses the structural causes of differences (colonialism, geography, institutions), AND evaluates the indicators themselves (e.g., HDI is more nuanced than GDP), AND acknowledges competing perspectives.

The Excellence answer engages with the contested nature of "development" as a concept.

#### Transferable skills

UE literacy credits from social science subjects reflect skills that are directly transferable to university study:

- Critical reading of complex texts.
- Argument construction and evaluation.
- Source evaluation.
- Clear written communication.

These skills are valuable across the humanities, social sciences, and many professional courses.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Only English counts for UE literacy." No — many subjects contribute; check the spreadsheet.
- "Geography is just maps." No — Geography standards at Level 2 and 3 require extended written analysis.
- "Social science essays are easier than English essays." Not necessarily — the standards are equally rigorous.
- "I don't need to check the spreadsheet." You do — standards can change year to year, and not all standards in a subject contribute.

#### Specification reference

The relevant achievement standards are internally and externally assessed by the school. The UE literacy contribution for each standard is published on the NZQA UE literacy spreadsheet. Re-check the live spreadsheet on nzqa.govt.nz before final revision.
