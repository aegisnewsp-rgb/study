# Average

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The **arithmetic mean** of n observations x₁, x₂, …, xₙ equals their sum divided by the count: **A = (x₁ + x₂ + … + xₙ) / n**. It is the single value around which the data set balances — total deviation above equals total deviation below.

For SSC CGL, three shortcut formulas cover 90% of questions:

- **Combined average** of two groups: (n₁·A₁ + n₂·A₂) / (n₁ + n₂)
- **Alligation ratio**: (A₂ − Aₘ) : (Aₘ − A₁), read off as the ratio in which the two group means are mixed to give Aₘ
- **Replacement change**: New average = Old average ± (difference of old and new item) / n

Average of the first n natural numbers is (n+1)/2 — a direct shortcut for "consecutive integers" sets. Watch the **average speed trap**: average speed is total distance ÷ total time, never the simple mean of two speeds unless the time intervals are equal.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Definition and Core Formula

The arithmetic mean A of n observations is A = Σxᵢ / n, where Σxᵢ is the sum of all values and n is the count. Every observation carries the same weight 1/n, so A behaves like the fulcrum of a seesaw: the sum of deviations above A equals the sum of deviations below it, ignoring signs. This balance property is the engine behind most SSC CGL problems disguised as "find the missing value".

#### Assumed-Mean and Deviation Method

When the numbers are large (e.g. marks out of 500, incomes in lakhs), choose a convenient **assumed mean** Aₘ near the centre of the data, compute each deviation dᵢ = xᵢ − Aₘ, and recover the true mean from A = Aₘ + (Σdᵢ) / n. This avoids bulky additions and is faster than computing Σxᵢ directly.

#### Combined and Weighted Averages

When two groups of sizes n₁ and n₂ have averages A₁ and A₂, their combined average is (n₁·A₁ + n₂·A₂) / (n₁ + n₂). This is a **weighted average** — group size acts as the weight. A naïve average of averages, (A₁+A₂)/2, is correct only when n₁ = n₂.

| Situation | Formula | Key Variable |
|---|---|---|
| Two groups combined | (n₁·A₁ + n₂·A₂) / (n₁ + n₂) | n = group size, A = group mean |
| Alligation mix | (A₂ − Aₘ) : (Aₘ − A₁) | ratio of quantities, not values |
| Assumed mean | Aₘ + (Σdᵢ) / n | dᵢ = xᵢ − Aₘ |

> **Scoring tip**: In alligation, the ratio reads **right-to-left of the mean on top, left-to-right of the mean on bottom** — i.e., the cheaper (or lower) average's quantity appears opposite to its deviation.

#### Standard Problem Types in SSC CGL

- Direct mean of 5 or 10 numbers given as a list.
- Finding the new average after one value is added, removed, or replaced.
- Combined mean of two classes, sections, or cricket innings.
- Age-based averages using **Sum = Average × Number of persons**, then equating present and past sums.
- Alligation to find the ratio in which two qualities (or two batches) are mixed.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Replacement and Change-of-Average Mechanics

When one item in a set of n items is replaced, the new average shifts by exactly (new value − old value) / n. The direction depends on whether the new item is heavier or lighter than the old one. This holds even when the old item is *removed entirely* (treat "removed" as replaced by zero) or when a fresh item is *added* (treat as replacing one dummy slot). The general form: **New Average = Old Average ± Δ/n**, where Δ is the net shift introduced.

#### Worked Example

A class of 40 students has an average score of 55. The topper who scored 95 leaves and a new student scoring 65 joins. Find the new average.

- Old sum = 40 × 55 = 2200
- Net change = 65 − 95 = −30
- New sum = 2200 − 30 = 2170
- New average = 2170 / 40 = **54.25**

> **Trap to avoid**: Dividing −30 by the old 40 is correct only because one person left and one joined, keeping n = 40 constant. If only an addition occurs, n becomes 41; if only a removal occurs, n becomes 39.

#### Alligation Edge Cases

Alligation gives the ratio of **quantities**, not values. When the question asks "in what ratio must two types of tea at ₹180/kg and ₹240/kg be mixed to sell at ₹200/kg?", the answer is (240 − 200) : (200 − 180) = 40 : 20 = **2 : 1**. Verify: weighted average = (2·180 + 1·240)/(2+1) = 600/3 = 200 ✓.

#### Average of First n Natural Numbers

The shortcut (n+1)/2 covers any set of consecutive integers 1 to n. For consecutive odd numbers 1, 3, 5, … (2n−1), the average is simply n — the middle term. For an arithmetic progression with first term a and common difference d, the average of n terms equals the **average of first and last terms**: (a + (a + (n−1)d)) / 2.

#### Common Mistakes in SSC CGL

- Dividing by the wrong n after replacement — always count the new total.
- Computing average speed as (v₁+v₂)/2 when the distances (not times) are equal.
- Mixing ages in years and months without converting both to the same unit before averaging.
- Reading the alligation ratio in the wrong order, producing the reciprocal of the correct answer.

#### Practice Prompts

1. The average weight of 25 students is 40 kg. If a new student of weight 48 kg joins, what is the new average?
2. Two varieties of rice costing ₹36/kg and ₹44/kg are mixed in the ratio 3 : 5. Find the average cost per kg.

#### Exam Strategy

In Tier-I, average problems usually appear as 1–2 direct questions solvable in under 60 seconds each. In Tier-II, they merge into **Mixture & Alligation** and **Data Interpretation** sets. Keep the assumed-mean method and the alligation cross ready as reflex tools.
