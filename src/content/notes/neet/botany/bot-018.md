# Reproduction Sexual

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Fastest recall path: flower whorls → gamete formation → double fertilization → seed.

Sexual reproduction in flowering plants fuses two haploid gametes produced by **meiosis** inside the anther and ovule, yielding a diploid zygote plus a triploid **endosperm** that nourishes the embryo.

| Event | Site | Ploidy outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Microsporogenesis | Microsporangium (anther) | MMC (2n) → 4 microspores (n) → pollen grain |
| Megasporogenesis | Ovule (nucellus) | MMC (2n) → linear tetrad → 1 functional megaspore (n) → embryo sac |
| Syngamy | Embryo sac | Sperm (n) + Egg (n) → **Zygote (2n)** |
| Triple fusion | Embryo sac | Sperm (n) + 2 polar nuclei (n+n) → **PEN (3n)** |

- Pollen tube in most keys enters through the **micropyle** (porogamy); two non-motile male gametes are delivered.
- Standard embryo sac is **Polygonum type**: 7 cells, 8 nuclei.
- Endosperm in most angiosperms is **3n (triploid)**, not diploid.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook — "SPE": Syngamy + PEN = Endosperm 3n. Read it as "SP-E" — two Sperms, one PEN, Endosperm triploid.** Pollen grains shed at the **2-celled** stage in many dicots; the generative cell divides inside the pollen tube for grasses (3-celled at shedding).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Use this tier to lock the sequence, the ploidy arithmetic, and the cross/self-pollination distinctions.

#### Microsporogenesis and the Pollen Grain

Sporogenous tissue in each anther lobe differentiates into **microspore mother cells (MMC, 2n)**. Meiosis I and II produce a **microspore tetrad**, in standard papers tetrahedral or decussate. Released microspores mature into **pollen grains** with a two-layered wall: **exine** (sporopollenin, highly resistant) and **intine** (pectin–cellulose). At shedding, most pollen is **2-celled** (vegetative + generative); grasses and a few others are **3-celled** because the generative cell has already divided.

#### Megasporogenesis and the Embryo Sac

A single **MMC** in the nucellus undergoes meiosis to form a linear tetrad of megaspores. Three micropylar megaspores degenerate; the **chalazal megaspore** survives and develops into the **Polygonum-type embryo sac** through three free-nuclear mitotic divisions.

| Mature embryo sac component | Position | Number | Ploidy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Egg apparatus (egg + 2 synergids) | Micropylar end | 3 cells | n each |
| Central cell (2 polar nuclei) | Centre | 1 cell, 2 nuclei | n each |
| Antipodal cells | Chalazal end | 3 cells | n each |

#### Pollination and Outbreeding Devices

- **Autogamy** — pollen lands on the stigma of the same flower (closed corolla, e.g., wheat, rice, pea).
- **Geitonogamy** — pollen moves between flowers of the same plant; genetically selfing but ecologically cross, requires a vector.
- **Xenogamy (allogamy)** — pollen moves between flowers of different plants; only true cross-pollination.

Outbreeding devices enforce xenogamy: **dicliny** (papaya, mulberry — unisexual flowers), **dichogamy** (protandry in sunflower; protogyny in *Mirabilis*), **hercogamy** (e.g., *Calotropis*), **heterostyly** (*Primula* dimorphic with pin and thrum; *Oxalis* trimorphic), and **self-incompatibility** mediated by the multi-allelic S-locus (e.g., mustard, *Petunia*).

#### Pollen–Pistil Interaction and Double Fertilization

Pollen lands on the stigma, hydrates, and germinates; S-allele based recognition rejects incompatible pollen. The pollen tube enters the ovule most in many papers **porogamously**. Two male gametes are discharged into the embryo sac: one fuses with the egg (**syngamy**, 2n zygote) and the other fuses with the two polar nuclei (**triple fusion**, 3n PEN). Together these two fusions are **double fertilization** — Nawaschin, 1898. The PEN develops into endosperm, **free-nuclear** in maize, coconut water, and most species, or **cellular** in *Petunia* and *Balsam*.

#### Embryogeny and Special Modes

The dicot embryo (*Capsella*) progresses through **globular → heart → torpedo → mature** stages with two cotyledons, plumule, radicle, hypocotyl and epicotyl. The monocot embryo (maize) shows a single **scutellum** cotyledon, **coleoptile** covering the plumule, and **coleorhiza** covering the radicle.

- **Apomixis** — asexual seed formation without fertilization; includes diplospory, apospory, and adventitious embryony.
- **Polyembryony** — more than one embryo per seed; in *Citrus* and mango, nucellar embryos make polyembryony an apomictic phenomenon.

#### A Standard Identification Drill

> A typical NEET item shows a labelled embryo sac and asks for the ploidy of cell X after fertilization. Two cells, eight nuclei; central cell carries two polar nuclei — once you see "central cell" in the question, the answer is built from **n + n + n = 3n** for the PEN.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap — Polar nuclei vs antipodals.** Options swap the chalazal end (antipodals) with the central cell (polar nuclei). The central cell is *central* — its two polar nuclei fuse with the second sperm to give the 3n endosperm. Antipodals sit at the chalazal pole and in most keys degenerate.

#### Worked Problem (Standard pattern)

A plant sheds pollen at the **2-celled** stage; 30 minutes later the pollen tube reaches the embryo sac. State the **ploidy of the zygote** and the **ploidy of the primary endosperm nucleus (PEN)** formed at that moment, and name the embryo sac type.

1. Zygote: sperm (n) + egg (n) → **2n**.
2. PEN: sperm (n) + polar nucleus (n) + polar nucleus (n) → **3n**.
3. Embryo sac: the description fits **Polygonum type** (7 cells, 8 nuclei, monosporic).

Answer: zygote 2n; PEN 3n; **Polygonum-type embryo sac**.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap — "Diploid endosperm".** Endosperm is 3n, never 2n. Two polar nuclei plus one sperm give a triploid. Treating the central cell as a single diploid cell is the fastest route to a wrong option.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Most NEET losses here come from boundary cases, not the central definitions.

#### Edge Cases and Confusable Boundaries

| Pair | Key distinction | Why it trips students |
|---|---|---|
| Autogamy vs Geitonogamy | Autogamy = same flower; Geitonogamy = same plant, different flower | Geitonogamy needs a pollinator and is genetically selfing |
| Geitonogamy vs Xenogamy | Same genotype involved vs different plants | Genetically self vs cross; ecologically cross in both |
| Self-incompatibility vs Dichogamy | Rejection at S-allele vs timing separation | Both promote outbreeding but act by different mechanisms |
| Free-nuclear vs Cellular endosperm | Nuclear divisions without walls vs with walls from the start | Coconut water is free-nuclear syncytium; *Petunia* is cellular |
| Diplospory vs Apospory | MMC itself develops without meiosis vs nucellar cell replaces MMC | Both bypass meiosis but originate from different cells |
| Adventitious embryony vs Nucellar polyembryony | In many papers overlapping; Citrus nucellar embryos are apomictic | Polyembryony can or can not be apomictic depending on origin |
| Hypohydrophily vs Epihydrophily | Pollen released underwater vs on the water surface | *Vallisneria* is epihydrophilous; *Ceratophyllum* and *Hydrilla* are hypohydrophilous |
| Protandry vs Protogyny | Androecium matures first vs gynoecium matures first | Sunflower protandrous; *Mirabilis jalapa* protogynous |
| Polygonum type vs Oenothera type | Monosporic 8-nucleate vs monosporic 4-nucleate | Most NEET items assume Polygonum unless explicitly told otherwise |

#### Quantitative and Numerical Anchors

- Pollen production per microsporangium: **~1,000–5,000** grains, depending on species.
- Pollen viability window: rice **~30 minutes**; cereals in general short-lived; mango and coconut pollen viable for **weeks**.
- One ovule per ovary in wheat, paddy, mango; **hundreds of ovules** in *Papaver* and watermelon.
- Embryo sac volume increase during maturation involves **three successive free-nuclear mitotic divisions** of the functional megaspore — no meiotic steps after the tetrad.

#### Connections to Adjacent Topics

- **Meiosis and genetic variation:** crossing over during microsporogenesis and megasporogenesis generates the allelic differences that make xenogamy evolutionarily valuable.
- **Plant breeding:** self-incompatibility is used to force hybrid production (e.g., brassicas); male-sterile lines exploit dicliny to reduce emasculation effort.
- **Embryology to seed:** apomixis produces clonal seeds, which matters for hybrid vigour fixation in *Citrus*, *Mango*, and several forage grasses.
- **Pollen allergy and forensics:** sporopollenin's chemical resistance makes pollen a useful index fossil and allergen marker.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. In *Primula*, the pin flower has a long style and short stamens; the thrum flower has a short style and long stamens. Predict the **fertility outcome of pin × pin** and **pin × thrum** crosses, naming the genetic system involved and the underlying cell–cell recognition step that enforces it.
2. A coconut you open shows clear liquid endosperm with no wall partitions, while a *Petunia* seed has walled endosperm at the free-nuclear stage. Explain the **developmental sequence** in each case and justify why coconut water is consumed as a liquid while *Petunia* endosperm is solid tissue.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap — "Nymphaea is hydrophilous."** *Nymphaea* and *Lotus* are entomophilous despite being aquatic. Only *Vallisneria* (epihydrophily), *Hydrilla*, and *Ceratophyllum* (hypohydrophily) are true hydrophiles. Mixing these in an MCQ option is one of the easiest marks to lose.

#### High-Yield Revision Checklist

- Polygonum-type embryo sac: **7 cells, 8 nuclei, monosporic, chalazal functional megaspore**.
- Double fertilization = **syngamy (2n zygote) + triple fusion (3n PEN)**.
- Outbreeding devices: **dicliny, dichogamy, hercogamy, heterostyly, self-incompatibility**.
- Apomixis produces seeds without fertilization; polyembryony = multiple embryos per seed.
- Monocot embryo parts: **scutellum (cotyledon), coleoptile, coleorhiza, plumule, radicle**.
