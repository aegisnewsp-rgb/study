# Short-Writing Tasks (Summary and Brief Response) — OSSLT Writing

Short-writing tasks on the OSSLT ask the student to write a single focused paragraph in response to a specific prompt — usually a summary of a supplied source, a short response to a specific question, or a brief opinion paragraph. The EQAO Framework describes these as single-paragraph tasks under a tight word limit. Practising short-writing tasks builds the foundation for the two long-writing tasks.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### What short-writing tasks ask for

- A single focused paragraph, usually a tight word limit (often a few sentences).
- A clear focus: the prompt states what the paragraph is about.
- Content drawn from the supplied source (for summary tasks) or from the student's view (for opinion tasks).
- Accurate conventions — spelling, grammar, punctuation.

#### Common short-writing task types

- **Summary.** Read a short source. Write a single paragraph that captures the main idea and the key supporting details.
- **Brief response.** Read a short prompt. Write a single paragraph that answers the prompt directly.
- **Opinion paragraph.** Read a debatable prompt. Write a single paragraph that states an opinion and supports it with one or two reasons.

#### One-pass structure for short-writing tasks

1. Read the prompt and the source (if supplied).
2. Identify the focus: what is the paragraph about?
3. Plan the paragraph in one sentence — the topic sentence.
4. Write the topic sentence. Then write two or three supporting sentences.
5. Close with a sentence that ties back to the focus.

#### Examiner traps

- A short-writing paragraph that tries to do too much. The rubric scores focus — a paragraph that stays on one idea scores higher than a paragraph that drifts.
- A summary that includes the student's opinion. A summary is a restatement of the source, not a response to it.
- A brief response that does not answer the prompt. Read the prompt twice and make sure every sentence in the paragraph addresses it.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Strategy for short-writing tasks

**Read the prompt twice.** The prompt states what the paragraph is about. Read it twice before writing — the second read catches details the first read missed.

**Identify the focus in one sentence.** Before writing, state the focus in your own words. Example: "The focus is to summarise the source's main point about school libraries." This sentence becomes the basis of the topic sentence.

**Stay within the word limit.** Short-writing tasks have a tight word limit. If the limit is, say, a few sentences, do not write a page. Padding scores lower on focus.

**Use one source fact per supporting sentence.** Each supporting sentence should develop one fact from the source. Do not stack multiple facts in one sentence.

**Close with a sentence that ties back to the focus.** The closing sentence restates the focus in light of the supporting sentences. Do not introduce a new idea in the closing.

#### Practice routine for short-writing tasks

- Take one EQAO sample summary task. Read the source. Plan the topic sentence in one sentence.
- Write the paragraph. Aim for a tight word count within the limit.
- Time yourself: under 5 minutes for the paragraph.
- After writing, re-read for conventions. Catch spelling, grammar, and punctuation errors.
- Take one EQAO sample brief-response task. Repeat the routine.
- Take one EQAO sample opinion-paragraph task. Repeat the routine.

#### How short-writing tasks connect to the long-writing tasks

Short-writing tasks are the foundation for the long-writing tasks. The Series-of-Paragraphs long-writing task is essentially a short-writing opinion paragraph plus one or two additional body paragraphs. Practising short-writing tasks makes the long-writing tasks easier.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How short-writing tasks connect to the other OSSLT items

- **Reading 1** (explicit information) is needed to locate the supporting details in the source for summary tasks.
- **Reading 2** (inference) is needed to interpret the source's main point for summary tasks.
- **The conventions scoring** on short-writing tasks is the same as for long-writing tasks. Practising conventions on short tasks reinforces conventions on long tasks.

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"I should write as much as I can."** Short-writing tasks have word limits. Padding scores lower on focus.
- **"A summary can include my opinion."** A summary is a restatement of the source. Personal opinion does not belong in a summary.
- **"I can skip the closing sentence."** The closing sentence ties the paragraph together. Without it, the paragraph feels unfinished.

#### Exam technique

- Read the prompt twice. Identify the focus in one sentence.
- Write the topic sentence. Then write two or three supporting sentences.
- Close with a sentence that ties back to the focus.
- Re-read the paragraph for conventions. Catch spelling, grammar, and punctuation errors.
- Use the platform's spell-check if available.
- Check the word count or length indicator. Stay within the limit.
