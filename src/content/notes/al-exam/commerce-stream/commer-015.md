# Human Resource Management

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> One-page summary to lock the core HRM framework in memory.

**Human Resource Management** is the strategic process of planning, recruiting, developing, motivating and retaining an organisation's workforce so that people decisions line up with business goals. In the A/L Commerce paper it sits inside Business Studies and contributes roughly **3% of the total marks**, almost always through short definitions plus one 10–15 mark essay.

- **Managerial functions**: planning → organising → directing → controlling of human resources.
- **Operative functions**: procurement, development, compensation, integration, maintenance.
- **Recruitment ≠ Selection**: recruitment is **positive** (builds the pool); selection is **negative** (rejects unsuitable).
- **HRM vs HRD**: HRM is a managerial function; HRD is a sub-system focused on training and development only.
- **Wage plans you must memorise**: Halsey (50% of time saved) and Rowan (proportionate share of time saved).

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook — "POD-IM"** for the six operative functions: **P**rocurement, **O**perational training, **D**evelopment, **I**ntegration, **M**aintenance/compensation. Walk through them in this order for any essay opener.

| Quick formula | Expression | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| Turnover Rate | (Leavers ÷ Average Employees) × 100 | % |
| Absenteeism Rate | (Days Absent ÷ Available Working Days) × 100 | % |
| Cost per Hire | Total Recruitment Cost ÷ Successful Hires | LKR |
| Labour Productivity | Output ÷ Labour Input | units/man-hour |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Working level for a fortnight of structured study before exams.

#### Functions and process map

HRM divides into **managerial** functions (planning, organising, directing, controlling the people side of the business) and **operative** functions performed by the HR department itself. The operative chain runs: procure staff → develop them → compensate them → integrate them socially → maintain them through welfare and grievance handling.

#### Human Resource Planning steps

1. Analyse organisational objectives.
2. Forecast **demand** for labour.
3. Forecast **supply** (internal + external).
4. Conduct gap analysis — surplus or deficit.
5. Prepare action plan (recruit, retrench, retrain).

#### Recruitment sources at a glance

| Source type | Examples | Speed | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Internal | Promotion, transfer, re-employment | Fast | Low |
| External | Advertisements, campus drives, walk-ins, e-recruitment, employment exchanges | Slow | Higher |

#### Selection process sequence

Application screening → written test → preliminary interview → selection tests → final interview → medical check → reference check → appointment letter. Each stage is a **filter**, which is why the **yield ratio** formula matters: (number reaching stage ÷ number at previous stage) × 100.

#### Motivation theories to compare

| Theory | Core idea | Workplace application |
|---|---|---|
| Maslow | 5 needs: physiological → safety → social → esteem → self-actualisation | Tailor rewards to the level an employee currently seeks |
| Herzberg | Hygiene factors (prevent dissatisfaction) + Motivators (drive satisfaction) | Pay alone is hygiene; recognition and growth motivate |
| McGregor | Theory X (workers dislike work) vs Theory Y (workers self-direct) | Choose management style based on assumed worker nature |

#### Wage incentive plans — the exact distinction

- **Halsey Plan**: Earnings = Time Rate × Time Taken + (½ × Time Rate × Time Saved).
- **Rowan Plan**: Earnings = Time Rate × Time Taken + [(Time Saved ÷ Standard Time) × Time Rate × Time Taken].
- **Taylor's Differential Piece-Rate**: higher rate for output below standard, lower rate above standard — used to penalise slow workers.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students frequently swap the Halsey and Rowan formulas. Halsey always pays exactly **half** the saved-time bonus; Rowan scales it in proportion to the time taken, so a faster worker gets a larger share only when time saved is large relative to standard time.

#### Sri Lankan statutory context

The Department of Labour operates under acts you can cite directly: **Shop and Office Employees Act** (working hours, holidays), **Industrial Disputes Act** (strike/lockout procedures), **Employees' Provident Fund Act** and **Employees' Trust Fund Act** (retirement contributions), and the **Termination of Employment of Workmen (Special Provisions) Act** (retrenchment benefits).

#### Resolving a typical A/L short question

A common 10-mark question asks: *"Explain the steps in the selection process and state why it differs from recruitment."* The answer should: (i) define recruitment as building a candidate pool and selection as rejecting unsuitable candidates, (ii) list the eight selection steps in order, (iii) justify why each step is necessary (screening saves cost, medical ensures fitness, reference prevents negligent hiring), and (iv) close with one sentence linking the process to the organisational objective of hiring the right person for the right job.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> For students targeting distinctions or sitting re-attempts.

#### Misconceptions that cost marks

- **HRM = HRD**: HRM is a line-and-staff function covering the entire employee lifecycle. HRD is a sub-system focused only on training, education and development. Examiners accept HRD as *part* of HRM, never as a synonym.
- **Performance Appraisal = promotion decision only**: it also identifies training needs, justifies salary revisions, motivates staff, validates selection decisions and documents HR planning data.
- **Industrial Relations = dispute handling only**: the modern reading is proactive harmony-building, with collective bargaining, joint consultation and grievance prevention preceding any conciliation, mediation or arbitration.

#### Wage-plan mathematical limits

When **time saved = 0**, both Halsey and Rowan reduce to plain time-rate wages. When time saved equals standard time, Rowan's bonus equals full time-rate pay (100% bonus), whereas Halsey's bonus remains capped at 50% of standard time. Knowing these boundary values helps when an examiner asks you to *show* which plan motivates a faster worker more.

#### Yield ratio — diagnostic reading

A **high** yield ratio at an early stage signals either generous shortlisting or weak initial sourcing. A **low** yield ratio at the final interview stage signals either poor screening earlier or mismatched job specifications. Sri Lankan campus recruitment drives, as documented by NIBM, frequently show yield ratios below 5% by the final interview — a figure worth remembering for applied essay questions.

#### Advanced practice prompts

1. A factory's HR department reported 240 leavers in a year against an average headcount of 1,200. Compute the turnover rate and recommend two retention strategies grounded in Herzberg's motivators.
2. Compare the Halsey and Rowan plans for a worker who saves 4 hours out of a standard 40-hour week at a time rate of LKR 200/hour, and decide which plan the firm should adopt if it wishes to maximise motivation while controlling payroll.

#### Cross-topic links

HRM intersects with **Organisational Behaviour** (motivation theories), **Business Law** (Sri Lankan labour statutes), **Production Management** (productivity ratios) and **Strategic Management** (Strategic HRM alignment). Revision order: lock the operative functions, then the selection process, then motivation theories, then wage plans — this is the sequence examiners reward in marking schemes.
