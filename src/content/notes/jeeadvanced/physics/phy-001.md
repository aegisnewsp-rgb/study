# Units & Measurement

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Every physical quantity is expressed as **Q = n × u**, where *n* is the numerical value and *u* is the unit. **SI** uses 7 base units — metre (m), kilogram (kg), second (s), ampere (A), kelvin (K), mole (mol), candela (cd) — plus 2 supplementary: radian and steradian.

#### Must-know formulas

| Quantity | Dimensional formula | SI unit |
|---|---|---|
| Force | [M L T⁻²] | N |
| Work / Energy | [M L² T⁻²] | J |
| Power | [M L² T⁻³] | W |
| Pressure | [M L⁻¹ T⁻²] | Pa |
| Planck's constant *h* | [M L² T⁻¹] | J·s |

**Error rule:** for Z = Aᵖ Bᵍ / Cʳ, percentage error in Z = p·(%err A) + q·(%err B) + r·(%err C). For sum/difference, **absolute errors add**.

> **Exam tip:** Significant figures in the final answer match the input with the **fewest** significant figures. Never round mid-calculation.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Dimensional analysis mechanics

Write any derived quantity as **[Mᵃ Lᵇ Tᶜ]**, where exponents are dimensionless integers. A valid physical equation obeys **dimensional homogeneity**: every term, including constants added to variables, must carry identical dimensions. This lets you (a) check an equation's correctness, and (b) find how a quantity depends on given parameters — provided the proportionality constant is **dimensionless**.

> **Trap:** Constants like ½, π, e, sin θ, eˣ, ln x are dimensionless, so dimensional analysis cannot fix them.

#### Significant figures

Count all **certain digits plus the first doubtful digit**. The doubtful digit's place is fixed by the instrument's **least count** — e.g. a vernier caliper reading 3.42 cm has 3 significant figures when least count = 0.01 cm.

| Instrument | Least count | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| Metre scale | 1 mm = 0.1 cm | Lengths ≥ 1 mm |
| Vernier calipers | 0.01 cm | Internal/external diameter |
| Screw gauge | 0.01 mm (pitch/n) | Thin wires, sheet thickness |
| Stopwatch | 0.1 s or 0.01 s | Pendulum timing |

#### Error propagation

For independent measurements, **absolute errors add in sums/differences**, while **relative (or %) errors add in products/quotients and multiply by the power** in exponentials. JEE Advanced frequently tests Young's modulus Y = 4FL / (πd²ℓ), demanding the simultaneous application of vernier and screw-gauge least counts with a timing error.

> **Common slip:** Writing %err(Y) = 2·%err(d) + %err(ℓ) + … but forgetting the **4, π, π/4** are dimensionless and error-free.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Supplementary units and edge cases

The **radian (rad)** and **steradian (sr)** are dimensionless (ratios of arc to radius, area to radius²), yet kept supplementary by SI convention. Angles in radians are used inside trigonometric functions only when arguments are dimensionless — a hidden constraint in oscillations, SHM phase, and diffraction derivations.

#### Quantity categories examiners probe

| Category | Dimensions | Dimensional analysis yields? |
|---|---|---|
| Dimensionless (π, e, refractive index) | [M⁰ L⁰ T⁰] | No |
| Contains trigonometric/exponential term | argument must be [M⁰ L⁰ T⁰] | Constrains form |
| Depends on gravitational *g*, speed *v*, time *t* | e.g. T = k · vᵃ gᵇ → [T] = [Lᵃ T⁻ᵃ][Lᵇ T⁻²ᵇ] | Solves for a, b, k if dimensions balance |

#### Accuracy vs precision

**Accuracy** = closeness to the true value (low absolute error). **Precision** = repeatability of measurements (low spread). An instrument can be precise yet inaccurate if a **zero error** is unaccounted for — always subtract zero error before recording readings.

#### Worked micro-example

A sphere's diameter measured 5 times: 2.04, 2.06, 2.02, 2.04, 2.08 cm. Mean = **2.048 cm**. Absolute errors: 0.008, 0.012, 0.028, 0.008, 0.032 cm → mean absolute error ≈ **0.0176 cm → 0.02 cm**. Reported value: 2.05 ± 0.02 cm (two significant figures in error dictate precision). Relative error = 0.02/2.05 ≈ 0.98%.

#### Practice prompts

1. The period of a simple pendulum depends on *l*, *m*, *g*. Use dimensional analysis to find the dependence, and explain why the mass *m* drops out.
2. For Y = 4FL/(πd²ℓ) with F ±1%, L ±0.5%, d ±0.01 mm (d ≈ 2 mm), ℓ ±1%, compute the maximum % error in Y.
