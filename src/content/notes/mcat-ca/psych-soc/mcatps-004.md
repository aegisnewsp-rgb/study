# Development, Personality and Psychopathology — MCAT Psych/Soc

Development, personality, and psychopathology span AAMC Foundational Concepts 6 and 7 of the Psych/Soc section. The MCAT tests Piaget's cognitive development stages, attachment theory, Erikson's psychosocial stages, Freud and the psychodynamic tradition, trait theories (including the five-factor model), and major psychological disorders (anxiety, mood, schizophrenia, personality disorders). These topics underpin developmental and clinical passages.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### Core concepts

- **Piaget's cognitive development:** sensorimotor (0-2), preoperational (2-7), concrete operational (7-11), formal operational (11+).
- **Attachment (Bowlby, Ainsworth):** secure, avoidant, ambivalent (anxious-resistant), disorganised. Formed in infancy; affects later relationships.
- **Erikson's psychosocial stages:** trust vs mistrust, autonomy vs shame/doubt, initiative vs guilt, industry vs inferiority, identity vs role confusion, intimacy vs isolation, generativity vs stagnation, integrity vs despair.
- **Freud's psychosexual stages:** oral, anal, phallic, latency, genital. The unconscious; id, ego, superego.
- **Trait theories:** Eysenck's PEN (psychoticism, extraversion, neuroticism); the five-factor model (OCEAN: openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness, neuroticism).
- **Anxiety disorders:** generalised anxiety disorder, panic disorder, phobias, social anxiety disorder, OCD, PTSD.
- **Mood disorders:** major depressive disorder, bipolar disorder.
- **Schizophrenia:** positive symptoms (hallucinations, delusions), negative symptoms (flat affect, alogia), cognitive symptoms.
- **Personality disorders:** Cluster A (paranoid, schizoid, schizotypal), Cluster B (antisocial, borderline, histrionic, narcissistic), Cluster C (avoidant, dependent, obsessive-compulsive).

#### One-pass drill

1. Identify the topic: development, personality, or psychopathology.
2. Recall the relevant theory or disorder.
3. Apply to the specific scenario.

#### Examiner traps

- Treating Piaget's stages as fixed. Children progress at different rates.
- Confusing the DSM clusters. Cluster A (odd/eccentric), Cluster B (dramatic/erratic), Cluster C (anxious/fearful).
- Treating all anxiety as the same. Different anxiety disorders have different features.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Cognitive development (Piaget)

- **Sensorimotor (0-2):** learns through sensory and motor activity. Develops object permanence.
- **Preoperational (2-7):** uses symbols and language; egocentric; lacks conservation.
- **Concrete operational (7-11):** thinks logically about concrete events; understands conservation.
- **Formal operational (11+):** thinks abstractly and hypothetically.

#### Psychosocial development (Erikson)

Eight stages across the lifespan, each with a conflict that must be resolved for healthy development. The first five are most tested:

- **Trust vs mistrust (0-1):** consistent care builds trust.
- **Autonomy vs shame/doubt (1-3):** encouragement builds autonomy.
- **Initiative vs guilt (3-6):** freedom to explore builds initiative.
- **Industry vs inferiority (6-12):** success builds competence.
- **Identity vs role confusion (12-18):** exploration builds identity.

#### Attachment (Bowlby, Ainsworth)

- **Secure:** distressed when caregiver leaves; happy when returns.
- **Avoidant:** not distressed when caregiver leaves; avoids caregiver on return.
- **Ambivalent (anxious-resistant):** distressed when caregiver leaves; resists comfort on return.
- **Disorganised:** contradictory behaviours; often associated with abuse.

#### Personality theories

**Freud:** the unconscious; id (pleasure principle), ego (reality principle), superego (morality principle). Psychosexual stages (oral, anal, phallic, latency, genital). Defence mechanisms: repression, projection, denial, rationalisation, reaction formation, displacement, sublimation.

**Trait theories:** the five-factor model (OCEAN) is the most-tested. Each factor is a continuum.

#### Psychopathology

**Anxiety disorders:** excessive anxiety that impairs functioning. Treatments: cognitive-behavioural therapy (CBT), SSRIs, benzodiazepines.

**Mood disorders:** depression (low mood, anhedonia, sleep disturbance) and bipolar (alternating depression and mania). Treatments: SSRIs, mood stabilisers, CBT.

**Schizophrenia:** chronic; positive symptoms (hallucinations, delusions), negative symptoms (flat affect, avolition), cognitive symptoms (disorganised thinking). Treatments: antipsychotics (dopamine antagonists).

**Personality disorders:** inflexible, maladaptive patterns. Cluster A (odd), Cluster B (dramatic), Cluster C (anxious).

#### Practice routine

- Take 5 development items. Identify Piaget's stage or Erikson's conflict.
- Take 5 attachment items. Identify the attachment style.
- Take 5 personality items. Identify the theory (Freud, five-factor) or defence mechanism.
- Take 5 psychopathology items. Identify the disorder and the symptoms.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How this topic connects to the other Psych/Soc topics

- **Cognition** underlies Piaget's stages.
- **Social interaction** affects attachment and identity development.
- **Biology of behaviour** provides the neural basis of many disorders (dopamine in schizophrenia; serotonin in depression).

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"Piaget's stages are age-fixed."** Stages are approximate; children progress at different rates.
- **"Cluster B is the odd cluster."** Cluster A is odd/eccentric; Cluster B is dramatic/erratic; Cluster C is anxious/fearful.
- **"Antipsychotics cure schizophrenia."** Antipsychotics manage symptoms but do not cure the disorder.

#### Exam technique

- For development items, identify the stage by the age and the cognitive milestone.
- For attachment items, identify the style by the behaviour on reunion.
- For personality items, identify the theory or defence mechanism.
- For psychopathology items, identify the disorder by the symptoms.
- Use the platform's calculator for any arithmetic.
