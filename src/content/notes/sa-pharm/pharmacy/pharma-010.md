# Clinical Pharmacy and Pharmaceutical Care

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Clinical pharmacy** is the health-sciences discipline in which pharmacists directly optimise therapeutic outcomes for individual patients through rational, safe, and cost-effective use of medicines. **Pharmaceutical care** is the patient-oriented practice in which the pharmacist accepts personal responsibility for a patient's drug-related needs and outcomes.

- **Pharmaceutical care (Hepler & Strand)** = patient-centred, outcome-oriented responsibility for drug therapy.
- **Clinical pharmacy** = pharmacist's direct, individualised involvement in patient care as part of the multidisciplinary team (distinct from hospital pharmacy under SAPC).
- **Drug-related problems (DRPs)** = the target of intervention; SAPC recognises **7 categories**.
- **Process:** assess → identify DRPs → set goals → design plan → monitor outcomes.
- **Legal anchor:** Pharmacy Act 53 of 1974, Medicines Act 101 of 1965, SAPC Rules (GPP).

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"UP-NEEDS-2-CARE"** for the 7 DRPs — **U**nnecessary drug therapy, need for additional drug therapy, **N**oncompliance, **I**neffective drug, **D**osage too low, dosage too **H**igh, **A**dverse drug **R**eaction.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Definitions

Clinical pharmacy and pharmaceutical care are not interchangeable in SAPC terminology. **Clinical pharmacy** describes the *discipline* — pharmacists applying pharmacological and clinical sciences directly at the patient bedside. **Pharmaceutical care**, as defined by Hepler and Strand (1990), is the *practice philosophy* in which the pharmacist accepts personal responsibility for a patient's drug-related needs and is held accountable for therapeutic outcomes, not merely for accurate dispensing.

SAPC explicitly distinguishes **clinical pharmacy** from **hospital pharmacy**: hospital pharmacy is logistics- and supply-led; clinical pharmacy requires direct, individualised patient care within the multidisciplinary team (doctor, nurse, pharmacist).

#### The Seven Categories of Drug-Related Problems

| # | DRP Category | Trigger Example | Pharmacist Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Unnecessary drug therapy** | Duplicated class, no indication | Discontinue or deprescribe |
| 2 | **Need for additional therapy** | Untreated indication | Recommend initiating drug |
| 3 | **Ineffective drug** | Wrong drug for condition | Substitute alternative |
| 4 | **Dosage too low** | Sub-therapeutic dose | Titrate upward |
| 5 | **Dosage too high** | Toxic serum level | Reduce dose; TDM |
| 6 | **Adverse drug reaction** | Harmful response | Stop, substitute, report ADR |
| 7 | **Noncompliance** | Patient cannot afford or use drug | Counsel; adherence plan |

> 📌 **Formula Check:** The pharmaceutical care process has **5 sequential steps**: assessment → identification of DRPs → establishment of therapeutic goals → design of pharmaceutical care plan → monitoring and review of outcomes. Stopping at step 4 is incomplete care.

#### Comparison Matrix: Often-Confused Pairs

| Concept | Distinguishing Feature | Common Confusion |
|---|---|---|
| Clinical pharmacy vs hospital pharmacy | Direct individual patient care | "All hospital pharmacists are clinical pharmacists" — false; SAPC separates the roles |
| Pharmaceutical care vs dispensing | Outcome accountability | Reducing it to "dispensing + counselling" misses the responsibility component |
| ADR vs side effect | ADR = noxious, unintended, causally linked at normal doses | Side effects can be expected; ADRs must be reported |
| Pharmacovigilance vs TDM | Reporting population-level harm vs individualising dosing | Treating them as the same activity |
| GPP vs Minimum Standard of Pharmaceutical Care | GPP = SAPC rules framework; Min Standard = measurable care criteria | Mixing the two rule sets |

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** A 67-year-old admitted for community-acquired pneumonia is on enoxaparin 40 mg daily. The patient has a CrCl of 22 mL/min. On day 3, haemoglobin dropped from 12.5 g/dL to 9.1 g/dL with no obvious bleeding source. Identify the most likely category of drug-related problem and the immediate pharmacist intervention.

#### Solution:
1. Recognise enoxaparin accumulation in severe renal impairment (CrCl < 30 mL/min) — risk factor for haemorrhage.
2. Falling haemoglobin by ≥ 2 g/dL with no external bleed suggests occult bleeding — a probable **ADR (category 6)**, possibly exacerbated by **dosage too high (category 5)** in renal failure.
3. **Intervention:** Hold enoxaparin, notify prescriber, suggest switch to unfractionated heparin infusion (renal-safe), submit ADR report to the pharmacovigilance centre, document in patient medication record.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students frequently label the fall in haemoglobin as **noncompliance** or simply "need for additional therapy" (e.g. requesting a transfusion). The correct framing is that the enoxaparin regimen itself is the DRP — categorise the *cause*, not the *symptom*. Always look at the drug's appropriateness for renal function before recommending add-ons.

#### Legal and Ethical Framework

The clinical pharmacist in South Africa practises within three interlocking instruments:

- **Pharmacy Act 53 of 1974** — defines the scope of practice of a pharmacist, intern, and pharmacy support personnel.
- **Medicines and Related Substances Act 101 of 1965** — schedules medicines and governs prescribing/dispensing authority.
- **SAPC Rules Relating to Good Pharmacy Practice (GPP)** — operational standards, including the **Minimum Standard of Pharmaceutical Care** (Board Notice 50 of 2008, updated).

Failing to document a clinical intervention or ADR report is a GPP breach — even a correct clinical decision can be deemed professionally negligent without contemporaneous record-keeping.

#### Edge Cases and Advanced Traps

| Scenario | Advanced Trap | Correct Approach |
|---|---|---|
| Patient refuses life-saving therapy | Misclassifying as **noncompliance** | Refusal ≠ noncompliance; assess capacity, counsel, respect autonomy — DRP only if capacity is intact and barriers are unaddressed |
| Polypharmacy ≥ 10 drugs in elderly | Listing every drug as a separate DRP | Cluster using **Beers/STOPP/START**; identify prescribing cascades |
| Therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM) of vancomycin | Reporting trough without adjusting dose | TDM without dose individualisation does not constitute pharmaceutical care |
| ADR causality | Defaulting to "probable" | Use **Naranjo algorithm** to score; document causality objectively |
| Off-label use | Treating as unlicensed | Document rationale, consent, and evidence level; does not bypass GPP duties |
| Continuity of care on discharge | Issuing script without follow-up | Pharmaceutical care extends to discharge counselling and handover to community pharmacist |

#### Common Mistakes (Exam-Focused)

1. Listing only **5 or 6** DRP categories — SAPC uses the full **7**.
2. Describing pharmaceutical care as a single visit — the process is **continuous and documented**.
3. Equating "medicines information" with patient counselling — they are distinct activities under GPP.
4. Ignoring scope-of-practice: pharmacy support personnel cannot perform clinical assessments.
5. Omitting **monitoring and review** when reciting the process steps.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. A patient on warfarin with INR 6.8 has no bleeding — classify the DRP, design the pharmaceutical care plan (including vitamin K decision), and state the monitoring endpoint.
2. A 4-drug regimen in a 78-year-old with recurrent falls — apply STOPP/START, propose deprescribing steps, and outline the multidisciplinary handover to the GP.
