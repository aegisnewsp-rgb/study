# Digestive and Excretory Systems — MCAT Bio/Biochem

The digestive and excretory systems form part of AAMC Foundational Concept 4 of the Bio/Biochem section. The MCAT tests digestive enzymes and absorption, the hepatic portal system, kidney structure, glomerular filtration, tubular reabsorption and secretion, and acid-base balance. These topics connect biochemistry to organ-level physiology.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### Core concepts

- **Digestive enzymes:** salivary amylase (starch), pepsin (protein in the stomach), trypsin and chymotrypsin (protein in the small intestine), pancreatic lipase (lipids), pancreatic amylase (starch), disaccharidases (maltase, sucrase, lactase).
- **Absorption:** small intestine (most nutrients); large intestine (water and electrolytes).
- **Hepatic portal system:** blood from the digestive tract drains into the portal vein, then the liver, before returning to the heart. Allows the liver to process absorbed nutrients.
- **Kidney structure:** nephron (functional unit); glomerulus; Bowman's capsule; proximal convoluted tubule (PCT); loop of Henle; distal convoluted tubule (DCT); collecting duct.
- **Glomerular filtration:** blood is filtered at the glomerulus. Filtrate contains water, ions, glucose, amino acids, urea.
- **Tubular reabsorption:** PCT reabsorbs most of the filtrate (glucose, amino acids, Na⁺, water). Loop of Henle establishes the medullary concentration gradient.
- **Tubular secretion:** DCT and collecting duct secrete H⁺, K⁺, NH₃, drugs.
- **Acid-base balance:** kidneys regulate bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻) and hydrogen ion (H⁺) levels; lungs regulate CO₂.

#### One-pass drill

1. Identify the topic: digestion, absorption, filtration, reabsorption, or secretion.
2. Recall the relevant structure and the molecules involved.
3. Apply to the specific scenario.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing filtration and reabsorption. Filtration happens at the glomerulus; reabsorption happens along the tubule.
- Treating urea as a waste that is not reabsorbed. Urea is partially reabsorbed in the collecting duct.
- Forgetting that the loop of Henle establishes the medullary gradient (countercurrent multiplier).

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Digestive system

The MCAT tests:

- **Salivary glands:** salivary amylase (starts starch digestion); lysozyme (antibacterial); bicarbonate (buffers).
- **Stomach:** HCl (denatures proteins, activates pepsin); pepsin (protein digestion); intrinsic factor (B₁₂ absorption).
- **Small intestine:** pancreatic enzymes (trypsin, chymotrypsin, pancreatic amylase, pancreatic lipase); bile from the liver and gallbladder (emulsifies fats); disaccharidases on the brush border.
- **Large intestine:** water and electrolyte absorption; bacterial fermentation; vitamin K and B vitamin production.

#### Hepatic portal system

Blood from the stomach, intestines, spleen, and pancreas drains into the hepatic portal vein, which carries blood to the liver. The liver processes absorbed nutrients (glycogen storage, gluconeogenesis, detoxification) before the blood returns to the heart.

#### Kidney structure and function

**Nephron:** the functional unit. About 1 million per kidney.

**Glomerular filtration:** blood pressure forces fluid and small solutes through the glomerular filter. Filtrate is similar to plasma but lacks proteins and cells.

**Tubular reabsorption:** essential molecules are reabsorbed:
- PCT: glucose, amino acids, Na⁺, water, K⁺, Cl⁻, HCO₃⁻.
- Loop of Henle: Na⁺, Cl⁻, K⁺, water (descending limb permeable to water; ascending limb impermeable to water, pumps out Na⁺/Cl⁻/K⁺).
- DCT: Na⁺, Cl⁻, water (regulated by aldosterone and ADH).

**Tubular secretion:** H⁺, K⁺, NH₃, drugs (in DCT and collecting duct).

**Hormonal regulation:**
- Aldosterone: increases Na⁺ reabsorption and K⁺ secretion (in DCT).
- ADH (vasopressin): increases water reabsorption (in collecting duct).
- ANP: increases Na⁺ excretion and water excretion.

#### Acid-base balance

The kidneys regulate blood pH by:
- Reabsorbing bicarbonate in the PCT.
- Generating new bicarbonate in the DCT.
- Secreting H⁺ in the DCT and collecting duct.
- Producing ammonia (NH₃) to buffer H⁺.

The lungs regulate blood pH by controlling CO₂ exhalation (carbonic acid-bicarbonate buffer system).

#### Practice routine

- Take 5 digestion items. Identify the enzyme and the substrate.
- Take 5 absorption items. Identify the location and the molecules absorbed.
- Take 5 kidney items. Identify the structure and the process.
- Take 5 acid-base items. Distinguish respiratory and metabolic disturbances.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How this topic connects to the other Bio/Biochem topics

- **Metabolism** is regulated by hormones (insulin, glucagon) that respond to nutrient absorption.
- **Cell signalling** underlies hormone action (aldosterone, ADH, ANP).
- **Acid-base balance** depends on buffers (bicarbonate, phosphate, protein) and on respiration.

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"Filtration and reabsorption are the same."** Filtration happens at the glomerulus; reabsorption happens along the tubule. Filtration is non-selective; reabsorption is selective.
- **"The loop of Henle reabsorbs water."** The descending limb is permeable to water; the ascending limb is impermeable to water but pumps out ions.
- **"Kidneys regulate pH only by secreting H⁺."** Kidneys also regenerate bicarbonate, which is the main buffer in blood.

#### Exam technique

- For digestion items, trace the molecule from mouth to absorption.
- For kidney items, identify the nephron segment and the process.
- For acid-base items, distinguish respiratory (CO₂-driven) from metabolic (HCO₃⁻-driven) disturbances.
- Use the platform's calculator.
