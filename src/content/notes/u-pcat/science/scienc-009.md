# Mendelian Foundations

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

> One fact to lock in: Mendel's three laws predict **ratios**, not guarantees, and those ratios break down on linked genes.

Gregor Mendel published his laws of inheritance in 1865 after eight years of pea-plant crosses. A UPCAT Science item almost always tests one of three ratios:

| Cross type | Parental genotype | F2 phenotypic ratio | F2 genotypic ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monohybrid | Aa × Aa | **3 : 1** | 1 : 2 : 1 |
| Dihybrid | AaBb × AaBb | **9 : 3 : 3 : 1** | many |
| Test cross | Aa × aa | **1 : 1** | 1 : 1 |

> 💡 **Memory Hook — "S.D.I."** for the three laws: **S**egregation, **D**ominance, **I**ndependent assortment. S comes first because gametes get only one allele per gene.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### The three laws in working order

1. **Law of Segregation** — a diploid carries two alleles per gene; meiosis splits them so each gamete gets one. Alleles reunite at fertilization.
2. **Law of Dominance** — in a heterozygote, the dominant allele masks the recessive one; the recessive phenotype appears only when both alleles are recessive (homozygous recessive).
3. **Law of Independent Assortment** — genes on different chromosomes sort into gametes independently, giving 4 gamete types from a double heterozygote (AaBb → AB, Ab, aB, ab each at 25%).

#### Confused concepts at a glance

| Term | What it actually means | Common slip |
|---|---|---|
| Genotype | The allele combination (AA, Aa, aa) | Confusing it with the visible trait |
| Phenotype | The observable trait (tall, short) | Treating phenotype letters as if they were alleles |
| Homozygous | Two **identical** alleles (AA or aa) | Calling Aa homozygous |
| Heterozygous | Two **different** alleles (Aa) | Calling AA heterozygous |
| Dominant | Expressed even with one copy | Assuming dominant = more common in population |
| Recessive | Hidden in heterozygote, shown only when homozygous | Forgetting it can skip a generation |
| Test cross | Unknown dominant phenotype × homozygous recessive | Using a heterozygous parent instead |

#### A standard UPCAT-style item worked out

A pure-breeding tall pea plant (TT) is crossed with a pure-breeding short one (tt). The F1 are all tall. Two F1 plants are then crossed. A student predicts 3 tall : 1 short in F2.

#### Working it through:
- P: TT × tt → F1 genotype all Tt, phenotype all tall.
- F1 × F1: Tt × Tt. Punnett yields **TT : Tt : tt = 1 : 2 : 1**; phenotypes **tall : short = 3 : 1**.
- The short phenotype reappears at 25%, even though P and F1 showed only tall.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap — "Recessive = lost forever."** Recessive alleles are not destroyed by being masked; they segregate back out in F2. UPCAT items in many papers show a 3 : 1 F2 ratio and ask why the "missing" trait returned. Answer: because each F1 still carried one recessive allele.

#### Quick rules for dihybrid crosses

- Always list all **four** gamete types (AB, Ab, aB, ab). Cutting this to two types is the single most common dihybrid error.
- The 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 ratio is for **two genes on different chromosomes** (or very far apart). Linked genes violate it.
- Phenotype ratio collapses when one or both genes show dominance quirks: e.g., epistasis gives 9 : 3 : 4 or 9 : 7 instead.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

#### Where Mendel's ratios break

Mendel's laws are statistical, not absolute. A litter of four kittens is not obligated to give exactly three of one coat color and one of another — the 3 : 1 ratio emerges over large samples. UPCAT can show a small-family Punnett and ask whether the ratio is "wrong"; the answer is that the **expected** ratio still holds, the sample is just too small.

Linked genes — those sitting close on the same chromosome — travel together more in many papers than Mendel's independent-assortment rule predicts. Parental gamete types appear far above 25% each, and the 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 ratio distorts into something like 7 : 1 : 1 : 7 or stricter, depending on recombination frequency.

#### Non-Mendelian patterns UPCAT can list

1. **Incomplete dominance** — heterozygote shows a blended phenotype; crossing two F1 gives 1 : 2 : 1 phenotype (not 3 : 1).
2. **Codominance** — both alleles show in heterozygote (e.g., ABO blood type AB). The 1 : 2 : 1 phenotypic ratio still applies, but the heterozygote class is its own distinct phenotype.
3. **Sex-linked traits** — X-linked recessive appears far more in many papers in males (XY) because a single recessive allele on X is unopposed.
4. **Multiple alleles** — a gene can have more than two allele versions in the population (ABO has three: I^A, I^B, i), but any one diploid still carries only two.

#### Advanced traps to watch

| Situation | Wrong assumption | Correct reading |
|---|---|---|
| Pedigree with skipping generations | Dominant inheritance | Recessive — the trait hid in heterozygous carriers |
| Two affected parents → unaffected child | Impossible / data error | Both parents heterozygous recessive; child is aa-free (homozygous dominant) |
| Dihybrid ratio 3 : 1 instead of 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 | Calculator error | Genes are linked, not independent |
| F1 all phenotypically intermediate | Law of Dominance violated | Incomplete dominance — dominance simply doesn't apply |
| 1 : 1 ratio from a cross | Monohybrid didn't work | Either a test cross, or a backcross to a recessive parent |

#### Two extended practice prompts

1. In garden peas, round (R) is dominant over wrinkled (r), and yellow (Y) is dominant over green (y). A plant with round, yellow seeds is crossed with a wrinkled, green plant, producing ¼ round-yellow, ¼ round-green, ¼ wrinkled-yellow, ¼ wrinkled-green offspring. What is the genotype of the round-yellow parent, and which law does the 1 : 1 : 1 : 1 outcome hint at?
2. A color-blind man (X-linked recessive) has children with a non-carrier woman. Among their daughters, none are color-blind, but all are carriers. Among their sons, none are color-blind and none carry the allele. Explain in one sentence why the daughters are obligate carriers, and state which Mendelian law is **not** sufficient to predict this pattern on its own.
