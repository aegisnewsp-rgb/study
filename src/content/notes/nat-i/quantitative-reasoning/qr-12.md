# Circles and Mensuration

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Circles and mensuration** tests your ability to compute lengths, areas, and volumes of standard 2D shapes and 3D solids. NAT-I expects fast formula recall — three to five seconds per substitution. A circle's perimeter is **C = 2πr** and its area is **A = πr²**, with π taken as 22/7 when r is a multiple of 7, otherwise 3.14159.

- **Sector** = pie-slice region bounded by two radii and an arc; area = (θ/360) × πr².
- **Segment** = region cut off between a chord and its arc; area = sector − triangle.
- **3D solids** to memorise: cube (6a², a³), cuboid (2(lb + bh + hl), lbh), cylinder (2πr(r+h), πr²h), cone (πr(r + l), ⅓πr²h), sphere (4πr², 4/3 πr³).

Watch the trap: substituting **diameter where radius is needed** inflates areas by 4× and volumes by 8×.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core circle relations

A **chord** is any line segment joining two points on a circle; the longest chord passes through the centre and equals the **diameter D = 2r**. A **tangent** touches the circle at exactly one point and is perpendicular to the radius at that point. The **arc** is the curved portion of the circumference subtended by a central angle θ (in degrees), so its length scales linearly: **L = (θ/360) × 2πr**.

#### Distinguishing sector from segment

- **Sector**: the entire pie-slice region between two radii and the arc — area = (θ/360) × πr².
- **Segment**: the smaller region bounded by a chord and its arc — area = sector area − triangle area = (θ/360)πr² − ½r²sin θ.

#### Unit discipline

Always convert all linear dimensions to a **single unit** (metres or centimetres) before substituting. Mixing cm and m in the same formula is the single most common source of wrong answers.

| Quantity | Formula | Units |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Circumference | 2πr | m |
| Area of circle | πr² | m² |
| Arc length | (θ/360) · 2πr | m |
| Sector area | (θ/360) · πr² | m² |

#### Triangle area without height

When only the three sides a, b, c are known, apply **Heron's formula**: s = (a+b+c)/2, then A = √[s(s−a)(s−b)(s−c)]. Useful when a problem gives side lengths but no altitude.

- Always check if r cancels out across a ratio problem before computing numerically.
- Keep π symbolic until the final step to avoid rounding error cascading through the answer.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Lateral vs total surface area

For a cylinder, **lateral (curved) surface area = 2πrh** covers the side wall only, while **total surface area = 2πr(r + h)** adds both circular ends. Cone similarly splits into lateral πrl (where l = slant height = √(r² + h²)) and total πr(r + l). NAT-I MCQs sometimes ask for "cost of painting the curved surface" — lateral only — so read the wording carefully.

#### Worked micro-example

A cylinder has r = 7 cm and h = 10 cm. Find (i) total surface area and (ii) volume.

- TSA = 2 × (22/7) × 7 × (7 + 10) = 2 × 22 × 17 = **748 cm²**.
- V = (22/7) × 49 × 10 = 22 × 70 = **1540 cm³**.

Notice how using 22/7 with r = 7 makes π cancel cleanly — a deliberate NTS pattern.

#### Common mistakes and traps

| Mistake | Consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Using D instead of r in area formula | Answer is 4× too large |
| Forgetting to cube linear dimensions in volume | Answer is off by orders of magnitude |
| Treating segment as sector | Adds or drops the triangular correction term |
| Premature π rounding | Final digit mismatch with answer key |

1. If a question gives circumference and asks for area, first solve r = C/(2π), then substitute.
2. Heron's formula is faster than coordinate geometry only when s, s−a, s−b, s−c are all positive.
3. Memorise **l = √(r² + h²)** for cones — slant height is the most-skipped sub-formula.
