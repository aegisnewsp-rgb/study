# Sindh: Geography, History, and Economy

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Fast scan for the night before — Karachi-as-financial-hub, Sukkur Barrage, and Muhammad bin Qasim's 711 CE landing are the three names examiners rotate around.

Sindh sits on the lower Indus plain, drained by the Indus River and its tributaries that meet via the Panjnad downstream in Punjab before entering Sindh through barrages. The province is bounded by the Kirthar Range on the west, the Thar Desert on the east, the Arabian Sea on the south, and Punjab plus Balochistan on the north.

| Aspect | One-line answer |
|---|---|
| Capital | Hyderabad (Karachi is largest city, not capital) |
| Conqueror 711 CE | Muhammad bin Qasim, landed at Debal |
| Oldest barrage | Sukkur / Lloyd Barrage, 1932 |
| Financial hub | Karachi — port, banking, industry |
| Signature crop | Cotton; rice (Irri-6); Sindhri mango |
| Language act | Sindhi dominant; Urdu official (1990 Act) |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"S-M-K-H-T"** for dynasties in order — **S**amma → **M**ughal → **K**alhora (1701) → **T**alpur (1783) → British (1843). Mnemonic sounds like "Smoke Heat" — burn each dynasty into the next.

Population density $D = P / A$, where $P$ = persons, $A$ = km², $D$ in persons/km². Cropping intensity $CI = (\text{Total cropped area} / \text{Net sown area}) \times 100$, in %.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> The working understanding — barrages, dynasties, and the cropping formula are where most marks come from.

#### Rivers and the Barrage System

The Indus is Sindh's only river system locally; Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas and Sutlej merge into the Panjnad, then join the Indus. Sindh captures the water through three barrages, each with a defined command area.

| Barrage | Year | Serves | Notable canals |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sukkur (Lloyd) | 1932 | Upper Sindh | N.West, Rice, Dadu, Begari, Sukkur, Khairpur, Eastern Nara |
| Guddu | 1962 | Upper–Lower Sindh | Desert Pat Feeder, Ghotki Feeder |
| Kotri | 1955 | Lower Sindh | Phuleli, Pinyari, Lined Channel |

Water duty (Indus basin) $W_d = V / A_{irr}$, where $V$ = volume of water delivered (m³) and $A_{irr}$ = irrigated area (ha), giving m³/ha; one cusec ≈ 0.0283 m³/s.

> 📌 **Formula Check:** Irrigation intensity $II = (\text{Canal + well irrigated area} / \text{Net sown area}) \times 100$. Sindh's II routinely runs above 130 % because canal supplies let farmers grow a second (rabi) crop on the same field.

#### Historical Timeline and Traps

The political sequence runs from the Bronze Age through Arab conquest to British annexation. Each transition is an MCQ anchor.

| Period | Key fact | Examiner magnet |
|---|---|---|
| ~2500 BCE | Mohenjo-daro (Sindh) — distinct from Harappa (Punjab) | Site-to-province mapping |
| 711 CE | Muhammad bin Qasim lands at Debal/Nirun | NOT Mahmud of Ghazni (1027, Somnath) |
| 1701–1783 | Kalhora dynasty | Built many irrigation works |
| 1783–1843 | Talpur Mirs (Hyderabad, Mirpurkhas, Khairpur branches) | Only Khairpur acceded to Pakistan |
| 1843 | Charles Napier annexes Sindh — "Peccavi" legend | "I have sinned" pun |
| 1947 | Karachi = Pakistan's first capital (until 1959) | Karachi ≠ Sindh capital |

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** A question may offer "Mahmud of Ghazni conquered Sindh in 711 CE" — correct answer rejects this; the 711 conquest was Muhammad bin Qasim's, and Mahmud's raids were on Hindu temples further south.

#### Economy — Crops and Industry

Sindh's economy is agro-industrial. Cotton, rice, wheat and sugarcane dominate the kharif and rabi cycles; Karachi adds services, port handling and ship-breaking at Gadani.

| Crop / sector | Heartland district | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| Cotton | Sanghar, Mirpurkhas, Sukkur | Major share of national output |
| Rice (Irri-6) | Larkana, Jacobabad, Dadu | Leading export variety |
| Wheat | Upper Sindh irrigated zone | Rabi staple |
| Mango (Sindhri) | Mirpurkhas | Premium export variety |
| Dates | Khairpur | Largest national cluster |
| Coal | Lakhra, Sonda (Larkana, Thatta) | Lignite grade |
| Natural gas | Khairpur, Kandkot, Mazarani fields | Sui itself is in Balochistan |

#### Worked Exam Question

**Question.** Sukkur Barrage was commissioned in 1932 and irrigates Upper Sindh through seven canals. If the net sown area of its command is 2.5 million hectares and the area actually irrigated (canals + wells) is 3.4 million hectares in a reference year, calculate (a) cropping intensity assuming total cropped area of 3.0 million hectares, and (b) irrigation intensity.

#### Solution.
- (a) $CI = (3.0 / 2.5) \times 100 = 120\%$.
- (b) $II = (3.4 / 2.5) \times 100 = 136\%$.

Both exceed 100 %, signalling double-cropping and heavy groundwater-lift irrigation on top of canal supplies — typical of Upper Sindh's alluvial aquifer.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students confuse $CI$ and $II$. Cropping intensity uses *cropped* area in the numerator; irrigation intensity uses *irrigated* area. They are not interchangeable.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Mastery layer — boundary cases, dynastic sub-branches, and the Karachi-vs-Hyderabad capital trap surface here.

#### Edge Cases and Lesser-Tested Facts

1. **Sui gas field location.** Sui lies in Dera Bugti, Balochistan, not Sindh. Sindh's gas fields cluster at Khairpur, Kandkot and Mazarani — the confusion arises because the Sui Southern Gas Company operates pipelines across both provinces.
2. **Capital switches.** Karachi served as Pakistan's capital (1947–1959) and remains Sindh's largest city, yet Hyderabad is the provincial capital — a deliberate post-1959 downgrade that papers test as one-liners.
3. **Talpur sub-branches.** Hyderabad Talpurs held the central tract, Mirpurkhas Talpurs the eastern zone, and Khairpur Talpurs the northern tract. Only the Khairpur princely state signed an instrument of accession with Pakistan in 1955; Hyderabad Mirs were ousted in 1843 by Napier.
4. **One-Unit period (1955–1970).** Sindh was merged with other West Pakistan provinces into a single administrative unit; the 1970 Legal Framework Order restored provincial status.
5. **Rohri Hills and limestone.** Cement plants at Rohri and Thatta source limestone from the Kirthar foothills; this is the geographic reason cement hubs cluster in Upper Sindh, not Lower Sindh.
6. **Gadani ship-breaking.** Gadani beach, between Karachi and Lasbela, handled peak tonnage in the 1980s–2000s; its ranking has slipped but it still appears in SPSC descriptive questions.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. **Chronology chain (5 marks).** Arrange Samma, Mughal, Kalhora, Talpur and British rule chronologically and state the year each phase ended. Why does the "Smoke Heat" mnemonic fail to capture the Samma–Mughal transition cleanly, and which date would you insert to fix it?
2. **Resource geography (10 marks).** Map the four mineral resources (coal, natural gas, limestone, rock salt) onto their districts and explain why the cement industry clusters at Rohri while the gas-processing industry clusters at Khairpur.

#### Common Exam Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---|---|
| Calling Sindh's desert the Sahara or Cholistan | Thar (extends into Indian Rajasthan) |
| Saying Karachi is Sindh's capital | Hyderabad is; Karachi is the largest city |
| Locating Sui gas field in Sindh | Sui is in Balochistan's Dera Bugti |
| Treating Harappa as a Sindh site | Harappa is in Punjab |
| Citing 1027 CE for the Arab conquest | Correct year is 711 CE under Muhammad bin Qasim |

Per-capita income $PCI = \text{Provincial GDP} / \text{Total population}$, in PKR per person; population growth rate $r = ((P_2 - P_1)/P_1) \times (1/n) \times 100$, in % per year where $n$ = years between the two counts. These two formulas appear in the economics sub-section of the General Knowledge paper and should be practiced with the most recent Pakistan Bureau of Statistics district tables.
