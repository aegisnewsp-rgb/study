# Part 2 — Individual Long Turn — IELTS Speaking Notes

IELTS Speaking Part 2 is the individual long turn. The candidate receives a task card with a topic and bullet prompts, has 1 minute to prepare, then speaks for 1-2 minutes on the topic. Part 2 lasts about 3-4 minutes including the prep time.

> Verify the live Speaking format on https://ielts.org/ before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Format:** task card with topic + bullet prompts, 1 minute prep, 1-2 minutes speaking.
- **Time:** 3-4 minutes including prep.
- **Task card:** describes a topic with 3-4 bullet points to address.
- **Preparation:** use the 1 minute to plan a structure, not to write a script.
- **Speaking:** continuous monologue, the examiner does not interrupt.
- **Common distractor:** candidates often run out of things to say after 1 minute.

#### Examiner traps

- Reading a memorised script — examiners recognise this and the band for lexical resource drops.
- Speaking for less than 1 minute — the topic is not fully addressed.
- Ignoring one of the bullet points — all bullet points should be addressed.

---

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Using the 1-minute preparation

Use the preparation time to plan:

1. **Read the task card carefully.**
2. **Decide what to say for each bullet point.**
3. **Note key vocabulary** (paraphrase of the prompt).
4. **Plan the structure:** introduction, body, conclusion.

#### Speaking structure

1. **Introduction:** state what you will talk about (paraphrase of the prompt).
2. **Body:** address each bullet point with a specific detail or example.
3. **Conclusion:** summarise or round off.

#### Common topic cards

Practice these common topic cards:

- Describe a person you admire.
- Describe a place you would like to visit.
- Describe an object that is important to you.
- Describe an event that changed your life.
- Describe a hobby you enjoy.
- Describe a book you have read recently.

#### Strategies for running out of things to say

- Add a reason or example.
- Compare to something else.
- Describe a memory related to the topic.
- Speculate about the future.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where Part 2 appears across IELTS Speaking

Part 2 is the most heavily weighted section of IELTS Speaking, accounting for about a third of the band but the longest in duration. Strong Part 2 performance is the single highest-leverage preparation for the Speaking test.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Memorised scripts score high." Examiners recognise them; the band for lexical resource drops.
- "Speaking fast scores higher." No — clear pronunciation and appropriate pace score higher.
- "Bullet points are optional." No — all bullet points should be addressed.

#### Exam technique

- Use the 1-minute prep to plan structure and note vocabulary.
- Paraphrase the prompt in the introduction.
- Address all bullet points with specific details.
- Speak for the full 1-2 minutes — do not stop early.
