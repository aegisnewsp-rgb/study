# Vocabulary and Usage

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Vocabulary and Usage** in the HAT-UG English section measures a candidate's control over word meaning, contextual choice, idiomatic phrases, and accurate grammatical deployment of words in sentences. Items appear as MCQs testing synonyms, antonyms, idioms, phrasal verbs, one-word substitutions, analogies, and confusable pairs. The fastest scoring gains come from mastering the five recurring item types listed below.

- **Context clues**: signal meaning via surrounding words (definition, contrast, example).
- **Idioms and phrasal verbs**: fixed expressions whose meaning is non-literal (e.g., *break the ice*, *put off*).
- **One-word substitutions**: precise single words replacing whole clauses (e.g., *polyglot*, *bibliophile*).
- **Analogy mapping**: identify the relation (synonym, antonym, cause-effect, function, degree) between a given pair and apply it to a new pair.
- **Confusables**: distinguish near-twins such as *affect*/*effect*, *principal*/*principle*, *stationary*/*stationery*.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Item types you will face

The English section of HAT-UG dedicates roughly 4% of the test to Vocabulary and Usage. Five item formats repeat across recent test cycles, and each rewards a slightly different strategy. Analogy items require relation identification before substitution; context-fit items demand connotation awareness, not surface synonym matching.

| Item format | What it tests | Quick strategy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Synonym / Antonym | Meaning equivalence or opposition | Check register and connotation, not just dictionary gloss |
| Idiom / Phrasal verb | Non-literal fixed expression | Memorise the whole phrase, never translate word-by-word |
| One-word substitution | Precise lexical compression | Link the suffix/prefix to a known root to decode the answer |
| Analogy | Relational pattern transfer | Label the relation (cause, function, degree), then mirror it |
| Confusables / Usage | Grammatical and semantic discrimination | Match part of speech demanded by the blank |

#### Confusable pairs that cost marks

HAT-UG items routinely exploit pairs whose spellings or pronunciations overlap. Committing the distinction between these pairs eliminates at least two careless errors per mock test.

- **Affect** (verb, to influence) vs **Effect** (noun, a result).
- **Principal** (head, main sum) vs **Principle** (a rule or doctrine).
- **Stationary** (not moving) vs **Stationery** (writing materials).
- **Fewer** (countable items) vs **Less** (uncountable quantity).
- **Elicit** (draw out) vs **Illicit** (illegal).

#### Word-formation shortcuts

A working knowledge of common prefixes and suffixes lets you decode unfamiliar items without memorising every dictionary entry. The table below lists roots that appear with high frequency in HAT-UG passage-based and standalone vocabulary items.

| Root | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| bene- | good | benevolent, benefactor |
| mal- | bad | malevolent, malfeasance |
| -phobia | fear | claustrophobia, xenophobia |
| -philia | love | bibliophilia, Francophilia |
| mono- / poly- | one / many | monologue, polyglot |

#### Typical traps and how to dodge them

1. **Surface-synonym trap**: *cheap* and *inexpensive* are dictionary synonyms, yet in formal register only the latter fits. Always read the surrounding clause for register cues.
2. **Literal-idiom trap**: *kick the bucket* refers to dying, not physical motion. Treat every idiom as an opaque chunk during revision.
3. **Wrong-antonym trap**: the true opposite of *hot* is *cold*, not *warm*. Partial opposites lose marks; select the strongest contrast.
4. **Part-of-speech trap**: a blank demanding an adverb (e.g., *performed ___*) cannot be filled by an adjective, even if the adjective carries the right connotation.
5. **Analogy-direction trap**: A:B :: C:? requires preserving the exact relational direction; reverse it and the answer inverts.

#### Practice prompts

1. Pick any HAT-UG past paper and isolate every confusable pair in the English section; rewrite each sentence using the other member of the pair to test grammatical fit.
2. From a single 500-word passage, list every idiom or phrasal verb and replace each with a literal paraphrase to confirm you understand the non-literal sense.
