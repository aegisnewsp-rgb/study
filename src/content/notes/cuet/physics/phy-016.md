# Capacitance

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

A **capacitor** stores electric charge on two separated conductors and is rated by its **capacitance** C, defined as charge per unit voltage: **C = Q / V**. The SI unit is the **farad (F)**, equal to one coulomb per volt. A parallel-plate capacitor of plate area A and plate separation d has capacitance **C = ε₀A / d** in vacuum, where ε₀ = 8.854 × 10⁻¹² F/m.

Inserting a **dielectric** of relative permittivity k multiplies capacitance by k: C = kε₀A/d. Energy stored in the field is **U = ½CV²**, and during RC charging the characteristic **time constant** is τ = RC.

- For **series** capacitors, 1/C_total = Σ(1/Cᵢ); net value is smaller than the smallest individual C.
- For **parallel** capacitors, C_total = Σ Cᵢ; net value is the sum.
- CUET UG Physics contributes ~4% of the total score; expect 1 MCQ on equivalent capacitance, energy, or dielectric effect at NCERT difficulty.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Definition and Units

Capacitance measures how much charge a conductor pair can hold at a given potential. From C = Q/V, one farad corresponds to storing one coulomb at one volt — an impractically large value, so real capacitors use **microfarads (μF, 10⁻⁶ F)**, **nanofarads (nF)**, or **picofarads (pF, 10⁻¹² F)**.

#### Parallel-Plate Geometry

For two identical metal plates of area A separated by distance d, the field between them is uniform and equal to E = σ/ε₀, giving V = Ed = Qd/(ε₀A). Substituting into C = Q/V yields **C = ε₀A / d**. Doubling A doubles C; doubling d halves C.

#### Effect of a Dielectric

A dielectric slab of constant k polarises in the external field, producing bound surface charges that weaken the net field. The voltage falls by factor k, so capacitance rises by factor k.

#### Combinations and Energy

| Combination | Formula | Key property |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Series (n capacitors) | 1/C = Σ 1/Cᵢ | Same charge Q on each; voltages add |
| Parallel (n capacitors) | C = Σ Cᵢ | Same voltage V across each; charges add |

Energy stored in the field: U = ½CV² = Q²/(2C) = ½QV. Energy density in vacuum: u = ½ε₀E² (J/m³).

#### RC Time Constant

A resistor-capacitor circuit charges as Q(t) = Q₀(1 − e^(−t/RC)) and discharges as Q(t) = Q₀e^(−t/RC). The quantity **τ = RC** (in seconds) is the time to reach ~63% of final charge during charging or to decay to ~37% during discharging.

- Series equivalent C is **always less than** the smallest individual capacitor.
- Inserting a dielectric with the **battery connected** raises Q (V fixed); with the **battery disconnected** it raises V (Q fixed).
- A common CUET trap asks whether U increases when a dielectric is slid in — U rises only if the battery stays connected.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Worked Example: Mixed Network

Consider three capacitors: C₁ = 6 μF, C₂ = 3 μF in series, and C₃ = 4 μF in parallel with the C₁–C₂ branch. Series branch: 1/Cs = 1/6 + 1/3 = 1/2, so Cs = 2 μF. Total: C_total = 2 + 4 = **6 μF**. If the network is connected to a 12 V battery, the series branch holds Q = CsV = 24 μC; voltage across C₁ is 24/6 = 4 V and across C₂ is 24/3 = 8 V. Energy stored in C₃ alone is ½(4 × 10⁻⁶)(12)² = 288 × 10⁻⁶ J.

#### Dielectric Edge Case

When a dielectric slab of thickness t < d is inserted between plates (battery disconnected), the system behaves like two capacitors in series: an air gap of thickness (d − t) with capacitance ε₀A/(d − t) and a dielectric layer with capacitance kε₀A/t. Net capacitance rises by less than factor k, an NCERT exemplar problem.

#### Common Mistakes

1. Applying the parallel formula 1/C = Σ1/Cᵢ and getting the reciprocal wrong.
2. Forgetting to convert μF to F before plugging into C = ε₀A/d.
3. Assuming voltage divides equally in series; it divides **inversely** with C.
