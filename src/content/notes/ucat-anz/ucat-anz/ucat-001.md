# Verbal Reasoning — UCAT ANZ Notes

Verbal Reasoning is the first subtest of the UCAT ANZ. It assesses the candidate's ability to critically evaluate information presented in a written form. Reading comprehension passages are followed by True/False/Can't Tell items targeting the ability to find, interpret and weigh information in dense text.

> Re-check the live test format and any updates on https://www.ucat.edu.au/about-ucat-anz/test-format/ before planning revision around these figures.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Subtest structure.** Passages followed by statements. For each statement, decide if it is True, False or Can't Tell based on the passage.
- **Time pressure.** Approximately 28 seconds per item in the standard test.
- **Reading strategy.** Read the question stem first, then scan the passage for relevant information.
- **Three answer choices.** True (statement is supported by the passage), False (statement contradicts or is not supported), Can't Tell (insufficient information).

#### Examiner traps

- Inferring beyond the passage — only what is stated or directly implied counts.
- Confusing "True" with "Can be inferred" — strict reading.
- Spending too long on one passage.

---

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### True/False/Can't Tell

True: the statement is supported by the passage (directly stated or unambiguously implied).
False: the statement is contradicted by the passage, or the passage contains information that makes it false.
Can't Tell: the passage does not contain enough information to decide.

#### Common traps

- Statements that look true but include a word that the passage doesn't say (e.g. "always" vs "usually").
- Statements that are partly true but partly false.
- Statements that contradict a single phrase in the passage.

#### Strategy

- Read the question stem first. Note the key terms.
- Scan the passage for those terms. Read the relevant sentence(s) carefully.
- For each statement, decide whether the passage supports it, contradicts it, or doesn't address it.

---

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Why Verbal Reasoning is heavily weighted

- Verbal Reasoning is one of four subtests; medical schools weight VR heavily for English-medium courses.
- Time pressure is the main challenge.

#### Practice pattern

- One Verbal Reasoning drill per day in the 6-8 weeks before the test.
- Use the official UCAT practice tests on ucat.edu.au.
- Time every drill strictly.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "True means plausible." No — True means supported by the passage.
- "Can't Tell is the safe choice." No — only when the passage really doesn't address it.
- "VR is just reading comprehension." No — VR requires critical evaluation, not just understanding.

#### Specification reference
