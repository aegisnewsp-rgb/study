# Theory of Production

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your WASSCE Economics paper.

**Theory of production** analyses how a Ghanaian firm combines **land, labour, capital and entrepreneurship** to produce goods, and how output responds when inputs change. It splits into a **short run** (at least one factor fixed, usually capital) and a **long run** (all factors variable).

> Quick mnemonic — *TP → AP → MP*: Total Product is output; Average Product = TP ÷ L; Marginal Product = ΔTP ÷ ΔL.

Three product concepts drive the topic: **Total Product (TP)**, **Average Product (AP)** and **Marginal Product (MP)**. In the short run, MP eventually falls — this is the **Law of Diminishing Marginal Returns**. Costs mirror production: **TC = TFC + TVC**, **AC = TC ÷ Q**, and **MC = ΔTC ÷ ΔQ**.

- Profit-maximising output occurs where **MC = MR** and MC is rising.
- **Returns to scale** (long run) measures what happens when *all* inputs rise together.
- Always state units: output in units, cost in GH₵, cost-per-unit in GH₵/unit.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard coverage for students with days to months before the exam.

#### Product concepts and the short run

In the short run, capital (K) is fixed and labour (L) is variable. The firm adds workers one at a time and observes how output changes. **TP** is total units produced; **AP = TP/L** measures output per worker; **MP = ΔTP/ΔL** is the extra output from one more worker.

> WASSCE trap: AP is at its **maximum** exactly where **MP = AP** — not where MP peaks.

#### Three stages of production

| Stage | MP behaviour | AP behaviour | TP behaviour | Firm's decision |
|-------|-------------|--------------|--------------|------------------|
| I | Rising | Rising | Rising at increasing rate | Irrational to stop here |
| II | Positive but falling | Falling (after max) | Rising at decreasing rate | **Rational zone — firm operates here** |
| III | Negative | Falling | Falling | Irrational — produce less |

#### Cost curves derived from production

Because TP eventually rises more slowly, MC eventually rises, producing the familiar **U-shaped MC and AC curves**. The relationships to memorise:

- **AC = AFC + AVC**; as Q rises, AFC falls continuously while AVC first falls then rises.
- **MC intersects AC at AC's minimum point.**
- **MC intersects AVC at AVC's minimum point**, and AVC's minimum lies *below* AC's minimum.

> Common error: students write that AVC is lowest at the same point as AC. AC's minimum is *higher and to the right* of AVC's minimum.

#### Producer's equilibrium

In perfect competition, a Ghanaian firm maximises profit where **MC = MR**, with the second-order condition that **MC must be rising** (cutting MR from below). In long-run perfect competition, **P = MC = AC** at the minimum of AC, so economic profit is zero.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive treatment for distinction-level mastery.

#### Short run vs. long run — the distinction examiners love

The **Law of Diminishing Marginal Returns** applies only in the **short run** because only one factor is variable. **Returns to scale** is a **long-run** concept: *all* factors rise in the same proportion. Three cases exist:

| Case | Output response | Typical source |
|------|-----------------|----------------|
| Increasing returns to scale | Output rises more than proportionally | Specialisation, indivisibilities |
| Constant returns to scale | Output rises proportionally | Mature technology |
| Decreasing returns to scale | Output rises less than proportionally | Coordination problems |

> WASSCE essay cue: a 20-mark question may ask "Distinguish between the law of diminishing returns and returns to scale." State the time horizon, what is fixed, and what is being changed.

#### Economies and diseconomies of scale

Internal economies include **technical** (specialisation, indivisibilities), **managerial**, **marketing**, **financial** (cheaper credit) and **risk-bearing** economies. Internal diseconomies arise from coordination breakdown, principal–agent problems and communication delays. External economies come from industry-wide factors such as skilled labour pools near Accra or Tema.

#### Worked micro-example

A cassava-processing firm in Ghana employs labour (L) with capital fixed at 1 machine:

| L | TP (bags) | AP | MP |
|---|-----------|-----|-----|
| 1 | 10 | 10.0 | 10 |
| 2 | 24 | 12.0 | 14 |
| 3 | 36 | 12.0 | 12 |
| 4 | 44 | 11.0 | 8 |
| 5 | 48 | 9.6 | 4 |

MP peaks at L = 2, then falls (diminishing returns from L = 3). **AP is maximised at L = 3 (AP = 12)**, exactly where MP = AP. The rational zone lies between L = 3 and L = 5, where MP > 0 but declining.

#### Common mistakes to avoid

- Confusing **diminishing returns** with **negative returns**; diminishing means MP falls, not that TP falls.
- Reporting **economic profit** (TR − explicit − implicit costs) as **accounting profit** (TR − explicit costs only).
- Drawing AC as tangent to MC instead of **intersecting** it at the minimum.
- Treating Stage I as efficient — the firm never voluntarily stops before Stage II.

#### Practice prompts

1. With capital fixed, a firm's MP schedule is 20, 18, 14, 8, 0, −6. Identify the three stages and the rational operating range.
2. Explain why the MC curve must cut the AC curve from below at AC's minimum, using the relationship MC < AC (AC falling) and MC > AC (AC rising).
