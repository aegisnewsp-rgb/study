# Active and Passive Voice

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Active voice** places the doer (subject) before the action (verb) and the receiver (object) after it. **Passive voice** flips this: the receiver becomes the subject, and the verb becomes a form of **be** (am/is/are/was/were/been) plus the **past participle (V3)** of the main verb.

The conversion formula: **Active — S + V + O → Passive — O + be + V3 + (by + Agent)**. Only **transitive verbs** (those that take a direct object — *write, build, teach, break*) can be passivised; **intransitive verbs** (*happen, sleep, arrive*) cannot. The **tense of "be"** must match the original tense: *writes → is written, wrote → was written, will write → will be written, has written → has been written*.

**High-yield HAT-UG pointers:** (1) Match the auxiliary to the tense, not just the verb form. (2) Never use V2 where V3 is required ("was wrote" is always wrong). (3) Drop the "by-agent" when the doer is unknown, obvious, or irrelevant.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Mechanism
Voice is a grammatical choice describing the relationship between the **subject** and the **verb**. In active voice the subject is the **agent** (doer) — *The chef baked the cake.* In passive voice the subject is the **patient** (receiver) — *The cake was baked by the chef.* The transformation is grammatical, not semantic: meaning is preserved, but the information focus shifts.

#### The Tense-to-Auxiliary Map
The auxiliary **be** carries the tense; the main verb always appears as V3.

| Active Tense | Active Form | Passive Form |
|---|---|---|
| Simple Present | writes | is/am/are written |
| Simple Past | wrote | was/were written |
| Present Continuous | is writing | is being written |
| Past Continuous | was writing | was being written |
| Present Perfect | has written | has/have been written |
| Past Perfect | had written | had been written |
| Future (will) | will write | will be written |
| Modals | can write | can be written |

#### Transitivity Test
Before converting, ask: does the verb accept a direct object? *He **arrived** late* — no object, no passive. *He **wrote** a letter* — object present, "A letter was written (by him)" works. Verbs like *happen, occur, sleep, die, lie, exist* are intransitive and resist passivisation.

#### Question Patterns in Passive
- **Yes/No:** *Was the letter written by John?*
- **Wh-:** *Who was the letter written by?* (preposition stays at the end)
- **Subject question:** *Who wrote the letter?* — when the question word itself is the new subject, the answer can remain active.

#### Typical HAT-UG Item Types
1. **Sentence transformation** — rewrite the active sentence in passive form within a word limit.
2. **Fill-in-the-blank** — choose the correct passive auxiliary + V3.
3. **Error spotting** — identify wrongly formed passives (wrong tense, V2 for V3, intransitive misuse).
4. **Voice-shift MCQ** — pick the sentence that correctly converts active → passive.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Restrictions
- **Stative passives** (adjectival): *He is interested in music* is passive in form but the "-ed" word has become an adjective; it does not derive from an active "*He interests in music*". Do not "convert" these — they are not passivisable actionals.
- **Causative *get*:** Informal passive uses *get* + V3 (*He got promoted*). HAT-UG prefers *be* + V3; treat *get*-passives as advanced-only.
- **Prepositional verbs:** Some active sentences carry meaning in the preposition — *The nurse looked after the patient* → *The patient was looked after by the nurse* (preposition retained, not split).
- **Two-object verbs:** With ditransitive verbs (*give, send, tell*), the indirect object often becomes the passive subject: *He gave me a book* → *I was given a book (by him)* / *A book was given to me (by him)*.
- **Impersonal agent:** When the doer is people in general, use a generic *by*-phrase or omit it: *The law was passed (by Parliament)*; avoid invented or anonymous agents in formal writing.

#### Common Traps
- **V2/V3 confusion:** Irregular verbs (*write/wrote/written, break/broke/broken, speak/spoke/spoken*) — memorise the V3 column, not the V2.
- **Tense drift:** Converting *He is writing a letter* to "*A letter is written by him*" loses the continuous aspect. Correct: *A letter is being written by him.*
- **Modals:** *should, must, may* do not change form in passive: *should write → should be written* (not "should written").
- **Intransitive misuse:** *The accident was happened* is ungrammatical; rewrite as *The accident happened* or recast the meaning.
