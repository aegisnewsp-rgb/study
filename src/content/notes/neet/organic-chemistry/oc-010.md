# Chemistry in Everyday

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your NEET UG paper.

Chemistry in Everyday Life applies organic functional-group chemistry to drugs, food additives, cleansing agents, polymers, and dyes. NEET UG draws 1–2 questions (≈ 4 marks) from this unit, almost always as direct MCQs on drug classification, soap vs detergent behaviour, or preservative chemistry.

- **Drug classes by function:** **analgesic** (pain relief — paracetamol non-narcotic, morphine narcotic), **antacid** (neutralises gastric HCl — Mg(OH)₂, NaHCO₃), **antimicrobial** (kills/inhibits microbes — penicillin, sulphonamides), **antihistamine** (blocks H₁ receptors — cetirizine).
- **Soap vs detergent:** Soap = sodium salt of long-chain fatty acid (RCOO⁻Na⁺, R = C₁₇H₃₅ stearate); detergent = sodium alkylbenzene sulphonate (R–C₆H₄–SO₃⁻Na⁺). Detergents work in hard water because Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺ sulphonates stay soluble.
- **Food additives:** Sodium benzoate (C₆H₅COONa) is a **preservative**, active mainly below pH 4.5; saccharin and aspartame are **artificial sweeteners** (aspartame ≈ 200× sucrose).
- **Cleansing mechanism:** Micelle — hydrophobic tails inward (trap grease), hydrophilic –COO⁻Na⁺ or –SO₃⁻Na⁺ heads outward (water soluble).

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"Sweet ≠ Safe ≠ Preserve"** — saccharin/aspartame *sweeten* taste buds, sodium benzoate *preserves* by killing microbes, BHA/BHT *antioxidise* by trapping radicals. Never swap these roles in an MCQ.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Structure–Activity Relationship (SAR)

Drug action depends on which functional group binds to which receptor. Paracetamol works because its phenolic –OH and amide –NHCOCH₃ fit the cyclooxygenase active site; morphine's tertiary amine and two –OH groups mimic endogenous endorphins at opioid receptors. NEET tests this link between functional group and physiological effect rather than rote names.

#### Drug Classification Matrix

| Drug class | Example | Functional group that matters | Distinction NEET tests |
|---|---|---|---|
| Non-narcotic analgesic | Paracetamol | –OH, –NHCOCH₃ | Non-opioid, no sedation |
| Narcotic analgesic | Morphine | Tertiary –N, two –OH | Opioid receptor, addictive |
| Antacid | Mg(OH)₂, NaHCO₃ | –OH, HCO₃⁻ | Neutralises HCl, no H₂ blocker mechanism |
| Antimicrobial | Penicillin G | β-lactam ring | Bactericidal; sulpha drugs have –SO₂NH– |
| Antihistamine | Cetirizine | Tertiary –N, –OCH₂COOH | H₁ receptor antagonist |
| Antifertility | Norethindrone | Steroid C=O, 17α-ethynyl | Synthetic progesterone mimic |

#### Soap, Detergent and Hard Water

Saponification: (RCOOCH₂)₃C₃H₅ + 3 NaOH → 3 RCOONa + C₃H₅(OH)₃, where R = C₁₇H₃₅ (stearate) or C₁₅H₃₁ (palmitate). Soaps fail in hard water because Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺ ions replace Na⁺ and form insoluble (RCOO)₂Ca scum. Synthetic detergents such as sodium dodecylbenzene sulphonate (SDBS, C₁₂H₂₅–C₆H₄–SO₃⁻Na⁺, M ≈ 348.48 g mol⁻¹) keep their Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺ salts soluble because the sulphonate group has a delocalised charge that resists precipitation.

#### Cleansing Action — Micelle Mechanism

1. Hydrocarbon tail (R = C₁₂H₂₅ to C₁₇H₃₅) dissolves in grease droplet.
2. Polar head (–COO⁻Na⁺ or –SO₃⁻Na⁺) orients toward water.
3. Aggregates form spherical **micelles** above the critical micelle concentration (CMC of SDS ≈ 8.2 × 10⁻³ mol L⁻¹ at 25 °C, unit = mol L⁻¹, dimension [N L⁻¹]).
4. Grease-laden micelles disperse in water and rinse away.

#### Food Additives and Preservatives

Sodium benzoate (C₆H₅COONa) ionises to benzoate (C₆H₅COO⁻) above pH 4.5, but the **undissociated benzoic acid** is the antimicrobial species — it diffuses through the microbial membrane and acidifies the cytoplasm. Hence benzoate is added to acidic foods (juice, pickles) where pH stays below ~4.5. Aspartame (methyl ester of a dipeptide) loses sweetness on cooking because its peptide bond hydrolyses.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** A detergent sample contains sodium dodecylbenzene sulphonate (SDBS, M = 348.48 g mol⁻¹). 25.0 mL of hard water required 18.0 mL of 0.010 mol L⁻¹ EDTA for titration against Ca²⁺. Calculate (a) moles of Ca²⁺ in the sample and (b) hardness as CaCO₃ in mg L⁻¹ (M of CaCO₃ = 100.09 g mol⁻¹).

#### Solution:
(a) Moles of Ca²⁺ = moles of EDTA (1 : 1 complex) = V × M = 0.0180 L × 0.010 mol L⁻¹ = 1.80 × 10⁻⁴ mol.
Concentration of Ca²⁺ = 1.80 × 10⁻⁴ mol / 0.0250 L = 7.20 × 10⁻³ mol L⁻¹.

(b) Hardness as CaCO₃ = (V_EDTA × M_EDTA × M_CaCO₃ × 1000) / V_sample
= (18.0 × 0.010 × 100.09 × 1000) / 25.0
= 18018 / 25 = 720.7 mg L⁻¹ of CaCO₃.

Since hardness > 300 mg L⁻¹, the water is **very hard**.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students multiply by 1000 thinking it changes the unit, but the constant already converts mL × (mol L⁻¹) × (g mol⁻¹) → g; the 1000 converts g L⁻¹ to mg L⁻¹. Skip the 1000 and your answer is off by a factor of 1000.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Exceptions

| Case | What students assume | Correct NEET-grade fact |
|---|---|---|
| Aspirin hydrolysis | Stable in stomach | First-order hydrolysis k ≈ 5 × 10⁻⁷ s⁻¹ at pH 2; t₁/₂ ≈ 1.39 × 10⁶ s (16 days) — stored tablets lose potency slowly |
| BHA vs BHT | Both are preservatives | Both are **antioxidants** (radical scavengers); preservatives inhibit microbes |
| Polyethylene vs PVC | Both addition polymers | PVC comes from CH₂=CHCl; PE from CH₂=CH₂ — chlorine changes Tg and density |
| Phenolphthalein endpoint | pH 7 | Transition range pH 8.2–10.0 (colourless → pink); acid–base endpoint for weak acid + strong base titrations |
| Branched ABS detergent | Biodegradable | Linear alkylbenzene sulphonate (LAS) is biodegradable; branched-chain ABS (historic) is not — this is the classic NEET "biodegradability" MCQ |
| Aspartame sweetness | Same as sucrose | ≈ 200× sweeter than sucrose; contraindicated in phenylketonuria patients (releases phenylalanine) |

#### Mathematical and Conceptual Boundaries

- **Micelle formation** is a cooperative process: below CMC the surfactant exists as monomers; above CMC, free monomer concentration stays ≈ CMC while micelles grow. NEET rarely asks for numerical CMC but expects the qualitative *threshold* idea.
- **Hardness thresholds (NEET factual recall):** 0–60 mg L⁻¹ soft, 61–120 moderate, 121–180 hard, > 180 very hard (as CaCO₃).
- **Tautomerism** appears in drug chemistry: barbiturates exist as keto–enol tautomers; only the triketo form is pharmacologically active.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. **Numerical:** A 250 mL sample needs 12.5 mL of 0.0050 mol L⁻¹ EDTA. Classify the water (soft/moderate/hard/very hard) and predict whether sodium stearate soap will scum in it. Show work using the hardness formula above.
2. **Conceptual:** Sodium benzoate is ineffective in a neutral-pH soup but works in tomato juice (pH 4.2). Explain using the equilibrium C₆H₅COOH ⇌ C₆H₅COO⁻ + H⁺ and microbial membrane transport theory. Identify which species diffuses into the microbial cell.

#### Exam Strategy

This unit is low-weight but high-scoring because the questions are recall-based. Spend 30 minutes memorising the drug-classification matrix, the soap-vs-detergent distinction, and the preservative mechanism. Skip polymer derivations unless you are aiming for 720+ — NEET rarely tests polymerisation conditions in this unit.
