# Growth and Development

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Growth** is the permanent, irreversible increase in size, dry mass or volume of an organism, driven by **mitosis** (cell division), **cell enlargement**, and **cell differentiation**. **Development** is the complete sequence of orderly changes — from zygote formation through growth, differentiation and maturation — that converts a fertilised egg into a fully formed adult.

In plants, growth is **localised** at **meristems** (apical, lateral, intercalary) and is **indeterminate**, meaning it can continue throughout life. In animals, growth is **diffuse** across body tissues and is **determinate**, ceasing once maturity is reached.

A typical population or organism shows a **sigmoid (S-shaped) growth curve** with four phases: **lag** (slow start), **log/exponential** (rapid multiplication), **stationary** (growth decelerates as limits appear), and **senescence/death** (decline).

Plant **hormones** to memorise: **auxins** (cell elongation, apical dominance), **gibberellins** (stem elongation, seed germination), **cytokinins** (cell division), **abscisic acid** (dormancy, abscission), and **ethylene** (fruit ripening).

For NECO: expect 2–3 MCQs testing hormone function, germination types (**epigeal** in beans, **hypogeal** in maize), and metamorphosis stages.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Growth vs Development
**Growth** is quantitative — measurable increase in length, mass, cell number or volume. It results from an increase in protoplasm and is irreversible under normal conditions. **Development** is qualitative — the progressive series of changes in form, structure and function that transform a zygote into an adult. Development includes growth but also **differentiation** (cells becoming specialised), **morphogenesis** (formation of shape and organs), and **maturation**.

#### The Sigmoid Growth Curve
When the size of an organism (or population) is plotted against time, four phases appear:
- **Lag phase:** cells adjust to environment; little visible growth.
- **Log (exponential) phase:** rapid cell division under ideal conditions; growth rate is highest.
- **Stationary phase:** growth slows as nutrients deplete or space limits expansion; cell division balances cell death.
- **Senescence/death phase:** growth becomes negative; mass and function decline.

Absolute growth = size at time *t*. **Relative growth rate** = (change in size ÷ initial size) × 100 ÷ time interval.

#### Plant Growth Is Localised and Indefinite
Plant growth occurs only at **meristematic tissues** containing actively dividing cells.
- **Apical meristem** — tips of shoots and roots; produces **primary growth** (length).
- **Lateral (cambium) meristem** — vascular and cork cambium; produces **secondary growth** (girth, found in dicots and gymnosperms).
- **Intercalary meristem** — base of internodes and leaves (e.g. grasses); allows regrowth after grazing or cutting.

Because meristems persist, most plants exhibit **indeterminate** (continuous) growth, unlike animals, which show **determinate** growth that stops at maturity.

#### Phytohormones and Their Roles
| Hormone | Main Effect |
|---|---|
| Auxins (IAA) | Cell elongation, apical dominance, root initiation |
| Gibberellins | Stem elongation, break seed dormancy, mobilise endosperm |
| Cytokinins | Promote cell division, delay leaf senescence |
| Abscisic acid (ABA) | Induces dormancy, closes stomata, promotes abscission |
| Ethylene | Fruit ripening, leaf fall, ageing |

#### Germination and Dormancy
**Germination** is the resumption of growth of the embryo inside a seed. Three external conditions are essential: **water** (activates enzymes), **oxygen** (for aerobic respiration) and a **suitable temperature**. Light is required by small seeds like *lettuce* but not by maize or beans.

Two germination patterns:
- **Epigeal** — cotyledons are pushed above soil (e.g. *Phaseolus*, bean).
- **Hypogeal** — cotyledons remain below soil (e.g. maize, *Zea mays*).

**Seed dormancy** is a resting state caused by abscisic acid, an impermeable seed coat, or immature embryos. It prevents germination until conditions become favourable.

#### Metamorphosis in Animals
**Metamorphosis** is the developmental transformation from larva to adult.
- **Complete (holometabolous):** egg → larva → pupa → adult (butterfly, mosquito, housefly).
- **Incomplete (hemimetabolous):** egg → nymph → adult (cockroach, grasshopper, dragonfly).

#### Human Adolescence
During **puberty**, the hypothalamus releases gonadotrophins that stimulate the gonads to secrete **testosterone** (males) and **oestrogen** (females). These hormones produce **secondary sexual characteristics** — beard growth, deepening voice, breast development, widened hips. Bone lengthening ceases when the **epiphyseal plates** of long bones fuse (ossify).

Questions are usually 2–3 MCQs and a structured/theory item. Common traps: confusing auxin (elongation) with cytokinin (division); calling maize germination epigeal instead of hypogeal; confusing incomplete metamorphosis (no pupa) with complete.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Measuring Growth Accurately
For plants, growth is recorded as **increase in height, leaf area, fresh mass, dry mass, or cell number**. Dry mass is preferred because fresh mass fluctuates with water content. A **growth curve** plots any of these against time, yielding the characteristic sigmoid pattern only when resources (light, nutrients, water) become limiting at high density. Under unlimited resources, growth follows a **J-curve** instead — seen in bacteria and some algal blooms, where doubling time remains constant.

#### Molecular Basis of Plant Hormones
**Auxins** (chiefly indole-3-acetic acid, IAA) acidify cell walls by activating H⁺-ATPase pumps, loosening cellulose microfibrils and allowing turgor-driven expansion. They also inhibit lateral bud growth — the **apical dominance** effect demonstrated when decapitating a bean plant releases dormant buds.

**Gibberellins** stimulate the synthesis of hydrolytic enzymes (α-amylase) in barley aleurone layers, mobilising stored starch in the endosperm — this is the basis of the **malting** process in brewing.

**Cytokinins** are produced in roots and travel upward; their ratio to auxins determines whether a tissue mass forms roots (high auxin) or shoots (high cytokinin) — the principle exploited in plant tissue culture.

**Abscisic acid** accumulates under water stress, triggering **stomatal closure** and **leaf abscission** by inducing enzyme activity in the abscission zone at the petiole base.

**Ethylene** is a gaseous hormone; one ripe apple can ripen a whole bunch through airborne diffusion — applied commercially to **bananas, tomatoes and citrus**.

#### Animal Growth and Its Limits
Animal growth is **diffuse** because most tissues retain some mitotic activity, but it is **determinate** because hormonal feedback (e.g. closure of growth plates by oestrogen in both sexes) enforces a fixed adult size. Body proportions change during development because different parts grow at different rates — **allometric growth** — which is why a human baby's head is one-quarter of body length, but an adult's is one-eighth.

#### Common Student Errors to Avoid
1. Confusing **primary growth** (length, apical meristem) with **secondary growth** (girth, lateral meristem) — grasses and most monocots have only primary growth.
2. Saying light is *always* needed for germination — many seeds (maize, peas) germinate in darkness; light is needed by positively photoblastic seeds (lettuce).
3. Misidentifying **hypogeal** germination in maize or broad bean — only cotyledons remaining *below* the soil qualify.
4. Treating **metamorphosis** as growth — it is development, not increase in size.
5. Citing growth as the *cause* of differentiation — differentiation is genetically programmed and depends on selective gene expression, not size.

#### Worked Example
A maize seedling is 2 cm tall at day 7 and 8 cm at day 21. Calculate the **absolute growth rate**.
Absolute growth rate = (8 − 2) cm ÷ (21 − 7) days = 6 ÷ 14 ≈ **0.43 cm/day**.
Relative growth rate = [(8 − 2) ÷ 2] × 100 ÷ 14 = **21.4 % per day**.
