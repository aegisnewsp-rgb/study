# Common Points Scale

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your CAO application.

**Common Points Scale** — Quick Facts for Irish Leaving Certificate students

**What is the Common Points Scale?**
The Common Points Scale (CPS) is the single conversion table that turns your six best Leaving Certificate subject grades into a points aggregate for undergraduate admissions through the Central Applications Office (CAO). It has applied to every CAO applicant since 2017, replacing the older pre-2017 grid. Higher Level (HL) grades run from H1 = 100 points down to H8 = 0; Ordinary Level (OL) grades run from O1 = 56 down to O8 = 0.

**At a glance:**
- HL band: H1=100, H2=88, H3=77, H4=66, H5=56, H6=46, H7=37, H8=0
- OL band: O1=56, O2=46, O3=37, O4=28, O5=20, O6=12, O7=0, O8=0
- 25-point Higher Level Maths bonus is added separately (see topic cao-002)
- Six best subjects are counted (see topic cao-003); Foundation Level Maths contributes 0
- Maximum aggregate is 625 points (six H1s + 25 bonus)

**Why it matters for offers:**
Your CPS aggregate is compared directly with the published course cut-off (see topic cao-007). Higher cut-offs mean more competitive courses — Veterinary Medicine in UCD, Dentistry in Trinity, and the top Engineering programmes typically run in the 600+ band. Mid-range Level 8 programmes are 350–500 points; Level 7/6 programmes accept lower aggregates.

⚡ **Quick rule:** Aim for the highest HL grades you can realistically achieve in your strongest six subjects. The difference between H4 (66) and H2 (88) is 22 points — more than a full grade band.

**Common Points Scale** — CAO Points Study Guide

**The full Higher Level band explained:**
Leaving Certificate HL is graded on an eight-point scale from H1 (the highest) to H8 (the lowest passing grade; anything below H7/H8 is still a pass but scores 0 points). Each HL grade maps to a single CPS value:

| HL grade | CPS points | What it means |
|----------|-----------:|---------------|
| H1 | 100 | Highest — strong university-course foundation |
| H2 | 88 | High second — competitive for Level 8 programmes |
| H3 | 77 | Solid upper-third — usually meets most Level 8 cut-offs |
| H4 | 66 | Upper-mid — many Level 7/8 programmes accept |
| H5 | 56 | Mid — meets minimum Level 7 in many courses |
| H6 | 46 | Lower-mid — bonus-point threshold for HL Maths |
| H7 | 37 | Lowest pass at HL — generally does not earn points competitively |
| H8 | 0 | Below H7 — still a pass but no points awarded |

**The full Ordinary Level band explained:**
OL grades run from O1 (highest) to O8 (lowest). OL grades earn fewer CPS points than the same tier at HL, because OL is a less demanding course of study:

| OL grade | CPS points | What it means |
|----------|-----------:|---------------|
| O1 | 56 | Equivalent of a high H5 |
| O2 | 46 | Equivalent of a high H6 |
| O3 | 37 | Equivalent of a high H7 |
| O4 | 28 | Mid-OL — moderate contribution |
| O5 | 20 | Below mid — modest contribution |
| O6 | 12 | Common matriculation-floor grade (see cao-005) |
| O7 | 0 | Lowest passing grade that does not earn points |
| O8 | 0 | Below passing — does not earn points |

**Foundation Level Maths contributes 0 points to your CPS aggregate** (see topic cao-004). A pass in Foundation Maths is recorded on your Leaving Certificate but does not change your six-subject total.

**How the CPS changed in 2017:**
The Common Points Scale replaced the older pre-2017 grid that awarded points in different increments and treated some grades differently. The 2017 reform equalised the points awarded for matched percentage bands across HL and OL (H1 aligned to ~90-100%, H2 to ~80-90%, and so on). Since 2017, the same CPS values have applied every year — but the **bonus-points threshold** for HL Maths sits at H6 or above (25 points), and the **maximum aggregate** sits at 625 (see topic cao-002).

**Course-allocation workflow:**
1. You sit your Leaving Certificate subjects at HL or OL (some subjects can be taken at either level).
2. CAO converts your grades into CPS values via the published grid.
3. CAO applies the 25-point HL Maths bonus once (if your HL Maths is H6 or above).
4. CAO picks your **six best subjects** after the bonus and caps the total at 625.
5. CAO compares your aggregate with the round-by-round course cut-offs (see cao-007).
6. If your aggregate clears a cut-off and you listed that course, CAO offers it to you at the highest preference you are entitled to (see cao-008).

**Reading the official grid:**
CAO publishes the grid as a static page (cao.ie/index.php?page=scoring&s=lcepointsgrid). The same page lists the rules for the bonus points, the six-best-subjects cap, the QQI FET/FETAC and GCE A-Level/GCSC scoring rules, and the round-by-round points history. Always read the grid directly before planning — the CAO Handbook (cao.ie/handbook/) is republished each year and is the source of record.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

**Common Points Scale** — Comprehensive CAO Notes

**How the scale maps to subject difficulty:**
The Common Points Scale is **not** a simple percentage-to-points table. CAO does not publish your raw Leaving Certificate percentage; they publish a letter grade. The grade is set by the State Examinations Commission (SEC) at a common percentage band each year — H1 is roughly the top ~90-100% band, H2 is ~80-90%, and so on. The exact band can move slightly year to year, which means the CPS values themselves are stable but the *meaning* of each grade shifts a little with the difficulty of the exam paper.

**Reading between the grades:**
The biggest practical gap is between H4 and H3 (66 vs 77 — an 11-point gap) and between H3 and H2 (77 vs 88 — an 11-point gap). These are the bands where a Level 8 course cut-off usually sits. A 22-point swing on a single subject can move you from below a course cut-off to comfortably above it.

**The Ordinary Level ceiling:**
OL caps at O1 = 56 — which is the same points value as HL H5. If you take a subject at OL you cannot earn more than 56 points in that subject under the CPS, even if you score the maximum mark. Subjects that are commonly taken at HL (English, Maths, Irish, a language) are the highest-leverage HL choices; a strong H1 in your strongest subject is worth nearly twice as many points as an O1 in the same subject (100 vs 56).

**Why some grades score 0:**
H8, O7, and O8 all score 0 points. This is a deliberate design choice: the CPS counts the grades that meet a meaningful achievement bar. A H7 (the lowest passing HL grade) still scores 37 points — it is a low-contribution grade but not zero. A H8 (the second-lowest) scores 0. The same logic applies to O7 and O8 at OL.

**Foundation Level policy:**
Foundation Level Maths (F1, F2, F3) does not appear on the CPS grid. It is recorded on your Leaving Certificate but contributes 0 to your aggregate. Some Level 8 courses (engineering, science, computing) require either a pass at OL Maths (O6 or above) or a minimum grade at Foundation Maths as a matriculation requirement, but Foundation does not contribute to points — see topic cao-004.

**HEAR and DARE:**
The Higher Education Access Route (HEAR) and the Disability Access Route (DARE) are supplementary application schemes managed alongside the CPS. HEAR applicants may receive reduced-points offers (typically a 5-10% reduction in the cut-off for participating courses) if they meet the published socio-economic indicators. DARE applicants compete on the standard CPS aggregate but receive additional supports. Both schemes require separate applications through CAO; neither changes the CPS grid itself.

**QQI FET/FETAC applicants:**
Applicants via the QQI FET / FETAC pathway is scored on a published FETAC scoring grid, NOT on the CPS, but the same CAO application process applies. The FETAC grid maps QQI awards to points values and a separate points cap. Always check the current FETAC grid on cao.ie before planning a FETAC entry route.

**GCE A-Level/GCSE applicants:**
Applicants presenting GCE A-Levels or GCSEs sit outside the CPS entirely — CAO publishes a parallel scoring grid for these applicants. The structure is similar (A* = high points, grade bands descending) but the values and conversion rules are different from the CPS. The grid is published on cao.ie.

**Mature applicants:**
Mature applicants (23 or older on 1 January of the application year) apply through a separate route and do not compete on the CPS aggregate. Their applications are assessed on a combination of Leaving Certificate results (if any), life experience, and other criteria set by each participating HEI. The CPS does apply to Leaving Certificate results they present, but their overall offer is not CPS-only.

**Where to verify before submission: the Live CAO Grid**
The grid is at cao.ie/index.php?page=scoring&s=lcepointsgrid (Common Points Scale Grid + bonus points + six-subject cap) and the Common Points Scale Calculator at cao.ie/index.php?bb=cps&page=newcps (interactive calculator that applies the bonus and six-best-subjects rule). CAO republishes both every year — confirm the grid before locking in a revision plan.
