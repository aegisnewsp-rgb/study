# Indian Parliament: Structure, Bicameral Dynamics & Legislative Procedures

The Parliament of India is the supreme legislative institution of the Republic. Under Part V, Chapter II of the Indian Constitution (Articles 79 to 122), Parliament embodies the representative voice of the people, exercises democratic oversight over the Union Executive, and controls the finances of the nation. In the UPPSC PCS examination (General Studies Prelims Paper I and Mains GS Paper II), parliamentary composition, legislative passage of bills, budgetary mechanisms, parliamentary privileges, and committee scrutiny constitute one of the highest-weight subject clusters.

## Complete 3-Tier Study Guide

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Revision (1h–1d sprint)

For last-minute revision before Prelims, focus on these constitutional anchors, numeric limits, and fundamental differences:

- **Constitutional Basis (Article 79)**:
  - Parliament comprises three distinct components: the **President of India**, the **Council of States (Rajya Sabha)**, and the **House of the People (Lok Sabha)**.
  - The President is an integral component of Parliament even though they do not sit or participate in parliamentary debates, because no bill passed by both Houses can become law without Presidential Assent (Article 111).
- **Composition of the Two Houses**:
  - **Rajya Sabha (Article 80)**: Maximum constitutional strength is **250** (up to 238 representatives of States and Union Territories + 12 members nominated by the President for specialized practical experience in literature, science, art, and social service). Current strength is 245.
  - **Lok Sabha (Article 81)**: Maximum constitutional strength is now **550** (up to 530 from States + up to 20 from Union Territories). Following the **104th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2019**, the nomination of 2 Anglo-Indian members was discontinued. Current strength is 543 elected members.
- **Tenure & Continuity**:
  - **Rajya Sabha**: A permanent chamber not subject to dissolution (Article 83(1)). One-third of its members retire every second year. Individual member tenure is **6 years** (prescribed by Section 154 of the Representation of the People Act, 1951, not directly in the constitutional text).
  - **Lok Sabha**: Normal tenure is **5 years** from the date appointed for its first meeting (Article 83(2)), subject to premature dissolution by the President on the advice of the Prime Minister. During a proclamation of National Emergency (Article 352), Parliament may extend the life of Lok Sabha by law for a period not exceeding **1 year at a time**, and not extending beyond 6 months after the emergency ceases to operate.
- **Presiding Officers**:
  - **Rajya Sabha**: The Vice-President of India is the *ex-officio* Chairman (Article 89(1)). The Deputy Chairman is elected from amongst Rajya Sabha members. Note that the Chairman is not a member of the House.
  - **Lok Sabha**: The Speaker and Deputy Speaker are elected by Lok Sabha members from amongst themselves (Article 93). The Speaker exercises a **casting vote** in case of equality of votes (Article 100(1)).
- **Core Legislative Boundaries**:
  - **Money Bills (Article 110)**: Can originate **only in the Lok Sabha** and only on the recommendation of the President. The Rajya Sabha has only 14 days to suggest recommendations; it cannot reject or amend a Money Bill. The Speaker's decision certifying a bill as a Money Bill is final (Article 110(3)).
  - **Joint Sitting (Article 108)**: Summoned by the President to resolve deadlocks on Ordinary and Financial Bills; presided over by the **Speaker of Lok Sabha**. Joint sittings are **never permitted** for Money Bills (Article 109) or Constitutional Amendment Bills (Article 368).

### 🟡 Standard — Core Exam Concepts (2d–2mo preparation)

In this standard preparation tier, we examine legislative procedure, budgetary stages, parliamentary privileges, and motions of executive accountability.

#### 1. Legislative Procedure: Classification of Bills

Understanding the precise differences among the four categories of bills is essential for UPPSC PCS answer writing:

| Feature | Ordinary Bill (Art 107) | Money Bill (Art 110) | Financial Bill (I) (Art 117(1)) | Financial Bill (II) (Art 117(3)) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Introduction House** | Either Lok Sabha or Rajya Sabha | **Lok Sabha only** | **Lok Sabha only** | Either Lok Sabha or Rajya Sabha |
| **Prior Recommendation of President** | Not required | **Mandatory** | **Mandatory** | Not required for introduction; required for consideration |
| **Rajya Sabha Powers** | Equal: can amend, pass, or delay up to 6 months | Limited: 14 days only; cannot reject or amend | Equal: can amend or reject like Ordinary Bill | Equal: can amend or reject like Ordinary Bill |
| **Speaker's Certificate** | Not required | **Mandatory certification** | Not required | Not required |
| **Joint Sitting (Art 108)** | Applicable in case of deadlock | **Not applicable** | Applicable in case of deadlock | Applicable in case of deadlock |

#### 2. Stages of the Annual Financial Statement (Budget)

The budget process under Article 112 encompasses six distinct constitutional and procedural stages:
1. **Presentation of Budget**: Introduced by the Union Finance Minister with the Budget Speech; no discussion takes place on the day of presentation.
2. **General Discussion**: Takes place in both Houses over several days; covers broad fiscal principles, but no cut motions can be moved.
3. **Scrutiny by Departmentally Related Standing Committees (DRSCs)**: Both Houses adjourn for 3–4 weeks. The 24 DRSCs examine demands for grants in detail and submit comprehensive reports without altering fiscal allocations.
4. **Voting on Demands for Grants**: Carried out **exclusively in Lok Sabha** (Rajya Sabha has no power to vote on demands). Members move cut motions:
   - *Disapproval of Policy Cut*: Demands amount reduced to Re 1 (expresses fundamental rejection of underlying policy).
   - *Economy Cut*: Demands amount reduced by a specific lump sum.
   - *Token Cut*: Demands amount reduced by Rs 100 (ventilates a specific grievance).
   - *Guillotine*: On the final day allotted for voting on demands, all outstanding demands that have not been discussed are put to vote simultaneously without further debate.
5. **Passing of Appropriation Bill (Article 114)**: No money can be withdrawn from the Consolidated Fund of India without the enactment of an Appropriation Act.
6. **Passing of Finance Bill (Article 117)**: Enacts taxation and revenue proposals; must be passed within 75 days of presentation under the *Provisional Collection of Taxes Act, 1931*.

#### 3. Parliamentary Devices for Executive Accountability

Members of Parliament utilize specialized procedures to enforce executive accountability (Article 75(3)):

- **Question Hour**: The first hour of every parliamentary sitting.
  - *Starred Questions*: Require an oral answer on the floor; supplementary questions can follow.
  - *Unstarred Questions*: Require a written answer laid on the table; no supplementary questions permitted.
  - *Short Notice Questions*: Relate to urgent public importance; asked with less than 10 clear days' notice.
- **Zero Hour**: An informal Indian parliamentary innovation originating in 1962 (not mentioned in the Rules of Procedure). Starts immediately after Question Hour around 12:00 noon. Members raise matters of urgent public importance without prior notice.
- **Adjournment Motion**: An extraordinary device to draw the attention of the House to a definite matter of urgent public importance having serious consequences. Requires the support of at least **50 members** to be admitted. Involves an element of censure against the government; available **only in Lok Sabha**.
- **No-Confidence Motion**: Grounded in Article 75(3), stating that the Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha. Needs support of **50 members** for leave to be granted. Does not need to specify specific grounds. If passed, the Council of Ministers must immediately resign.
- **Calling Attention Motion**: Introduced by a member to call the attention of a Minister to a matter of urgent public importance and request an authoritative statement. Unlike the adjournment motion, it involves no element of censure and is available in both Houses.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Analytical Study (3mo+ mastery)

In this advanced tier, we analyze parliamentary sovereignty vs. constitutional supremacy, anti-defection mechanics, the decline of parliamentary sittings, and the architecture of legislative committees.

#### 1. Indian Parliament: Sovereign or Constitutionally Bound?

A classic UPPSC Mains GS Paper II question examines whether the Indian Parliament is a sovereign legislature modeled after the British Westminster system:

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|               PARLIAMENTARY SOVEREIGNTY (UK) vs CONSTITUTIONAL SUPREMACY (INDIA)   |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| BRITISH DOCTRINE (Dicey):       | INDIAN CONSTITUTIONAL MODEL:                    |
| • Parliament can make or unmake | • Written Constitution with entrenched limits.  |
|   any law whatsoever.           | • Judicial Review (Articles 13, 32, 226).       |
| • No court can declare an Act   | • Federal Distribution of Powers (7th Schedule) |
|   of Parliament ultra vires.    | • Basic Structure Doctrine (Kesavananda, 1973). |
| • Executive and judiciary are   | • Fundamental Rights as justiciable boundaries. |
|   completely subordinate.       |                                                 |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

In India, Parliament is **not a sovereign body**. Its authority is limited by:
1. **The Written Constitution**: Sets clear boundaries on legislative competence through the Union, State, and Concurrent Lists.
2. **Fundamental Rights**: Article 13(2) prohibits any law taking away Part III freedoms.
3. **Federal Division of Powers**: Any encroachment into the exclusive State domain under List II (Article 246) is struck down by constitutional courts under the doctrine of *ultra vires*.
4. **The Basic Structure Doctrine**: The Supreme Court in *Kesavananda Bharati (1973)* established that Parliament's constituent amending power under Article 368 is limited and cannot abrogate the essential framework of the Constitution (secularism, federalism, judicial review, free and fair elections).

#### 2. Anti-Defection Law (Tenth Schedule) & Reforms

Introduced by the **52nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1985**, the Tenth Schedule disqualifies legislators for defection:
- **Grounds for Disqualification**:
  1. Voluntarily giving up membership of the political party on whose ticket the member was elected.
  2. Voting or abstaining from voting in the House contrary to any direction (whip) issued by the political party without prior permission or condonation within 15 days.
  3. An independently elected member joining any political party after election.
  4. A nominated member joining any political party after the expiry of 6 months from taking their seat.
- **Exceptions**:
  - The **91st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003** deleted the provision protecting "one-third split" from disqualification. Currently, protection applies only to a **merger** involving not less than **two-thirds of the members** of the legislative party agreeing to merge with another party.
- **Presiding Officer's Role & Judicial Review**:
  - Disqualification questions are decided by the Speaker or Chairman of the House.
  - In *Kihoto Hollohan v. Zachillhu (1992)*, the Supreme Court held that the Speaker acts as a tribunal when deciding Tenth Schedule disputes; therefore, their final decision is subject to **judicial review** on grounds of mala fides, perversity, or violation of natural justice (though courts cannot intervene at an interlocutory stage).

#### 3. The Parliamentary Committee System

Because Parliament cannot deliberate every bill and budgetary estimate in full assembly due to time constraints, the committee system serves as the operational engine of legislative oversight:

- **Financial Committees**:
  - **Public Accounts Committee (PAC)**: Established in 1921 under the Government of India Act 1919. Comprises 22 members (15 Lok Sabha, 7 Rajya Sabha) elected annually via proportional representation by single transferable vote. Since 1967, the Chairman is appointed by the Speaker from the **opposition party**. Examines the appropriation accounts, financial audits, and reports of the Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) of India. It acts as the "watchdog of the public purse".
  - **Estimates Committee**: Established in 1950 on the recommendation of Finance Minister John Mathai. Consists of **30 members, all from Lok Sabha** (Rajya Sabha has zero representation). Examines whether economies, organizational improvements, or administrative reforms consistent with policy can be achieved. Known as the "Continuous Economy Committee".
  - **Committee on Public Undertakings (COPU)**: Created in 1964 on the recommendation of the Krishna Menon Committee. Comprises 22 members (15 Lok Sabha, 7 Rajya Sabha). Examines reports and accounts of public sector enterprises and CAG commercial audits.
- **Departmentally Related Standing Committees (DRSCs)**:
  - First introduced in 1993; expanded in 2004 to **24 committees** (16 working under Lok Sabha, 8 under Rajya Sabha).
  - Each committee consists of **31 members** (21 from Lok Sabha, 10 from Rajya Sabha), nominated by the Speaker and Chairman.
  - Key functions: Scrutinize Demands for Grants of related ministries, examine bills referred by the Presiding Officers, consider Annual Reports, and examine national policy documents. Their recommendations are advisory in nature.

## Worked UPPSC Exam Questions

### Question 1 (UPPSC Prelims GS Paper I Pattern)

**Question:** With reference to the Parliament of India, consider the following statements:
1. A resolution approving a Proclamation of Financial Emergency under Article 360 must be passed by both Houses of Parliament by a special majority.
2. The Rajya Sabha has equal powers with the Lok Sabha in amending the Constitution under Article 368.
3. The Estimates Committee is the largest standing committee of Parliament and includes members from both Houses.
4. Under Article 249, Rajya Sabha can declare by resolution that it is necessary in the national interest that Parliament should make laws with respect to any matter enumerated in the State List.

Which of the statements given above are correct?
- (A) 1 and 3 only
- (B) 2 and 4 only
- (C) 2, 3 and 4 only
- (D) 1, 2 and 4 only

**Detailed Solution:**
- **Statement 1 is incorrect**: A proclamation of Financial Emergency under Article 360 must be approved by both Houses of Parliament by a **simple majority** (majority of members present and voting), not a special majority. Only a National Emergency (Article 352) requires approval by a special majority under Article 352(6).
- **Statement 2 is correct**: In amending the Constitution under Article 368, both Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha possess **completely equal constituent powers**. A Constitutional Amendment Bill must be passed by each House separately by a special majority. There is no provision for a joint sitting to resolve a disagreement between the two Houses on a constitutional amendment.
- **Statement 3 is incorrect**: While the Estimates Committee is indeed the largest committee (30 members), it consists **entirely of members from Lok Sabha**. The Rajya Sabha has **no representation** on the Estimates Committee.
- **Statement 4 is correct**: Article 249 specifically empowers the Rajya Sabha, as the federal council representing the States, to pass a resolution by a two-thirds majority of members present and voting authorizing Parliament to legislate on a State List subject in the national interest.
- *Correct Answer:* **(B) 2 and 4 only**.

### Question 2 (UPPSC Mains GS Paper II Model Analytical Answer)

**Question:** "The Departmentally Related Standing Committees have revitalized legislative scrutiny in India, but their effectiveness is increasingly constrained." Discuss the role, contributions, and challenges faced by the committee system. (15 Marks, 250 Words)

**Model Answer Framework:**

1. **Introduction**:
   - Establish context: The 24 Departmentally Related Standing Committees (DRSCs) introduced in 1993 act as "mini-parliaments", providing year-round institutional oversight over government departments away from the glare of party-line floor debates.
2. **Vital Contributions to Legislative Scrutiny**:
   - *Non-Partisan Deliberation*: Held *in camera* (behind closed doors), allowing members across party affiliations to discuss legislative provisions and budgetary demands constructively without political posturing.
   - *Detailed Financial Vetting*: Scrutinize Demands for Grants of all 50+ ministries during the parliamentary recess, ensuring public expenditures are examined before Guillotine is applied in Lok Sabha.
   - *Stakeholder Consultation*: Empowered to invite civil society experts, academic specialists, and affected citizens to provide empirical feedback on complex technical bills.
3. **Emerging Constraints & Structural Challenges**:
   - *Circumvention of Committees*: Significant decline in the proportion of bills referred to committees (dropped from 71% in the 15th Lok Sabha to under 25% in the 17th Lok Sabha), with key legislations passed via voice votes as ordinary bills.
   - *Short Tenures & Absenteeism*: Committee members serve a brief 1-year tenure, disrupting continuity and specialization. Meeting attendances often struggle to meet quorum.
   - *Advisory Character*: Recommendations are non-binding on executive ministries; Action Taken Reports (ATRs) are frequently tabled without substantive executive compliance.
4. **Conclusion & Forward Reforms**:
   - As recommended by the National Commission to Review the Working of the Constitution (NCRWC), mandatory reference of all substantive bills to DRSCs, multi-year tenures for members, and public hearings would fortify parliamentary oversight and restore legislative equilibrium.

## Common Preparation Traps for UPPSC PCS

- **Trap 1: Maximum Strength of Lok Sabha**: Beware of old textbooks stating 552. The 104th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2019 ceased the reservation and nomination of 2 Anglo-Indian seats under Article 331. The maximum constitutional strength is now **550** (530 states + 20 UTs).
- **Trap 2: Prorogation vs. Adjournment**: Adjournment terminates a **sitting** of Parliament and is exercised by the Presiding Officer (Speaker/Chairman). Prorogation terminates a **session** of Parliament and is exercised by the **President of India** under Article 85(2)(a).
- **Trap 3: Lapsing of Bills on Dissolution**: Under Article 107, a bill pending in Rajya Sabha which has **not** been passed by Lok Sabha does **not lapse** on the dissolution of Lok Sabha. However, any bill pending in Lok Sabha or passed by Lok Sabha and pending in Rajya Sabha lapses unless a joint sitting was already notified prior to dissolution.
- **Trap 4: Money Bill Deadlock**: Remember that there is **never** a joint sitting for a Money Bill. If Rajya Sabha does not return the Money Bill within 14 days, it is deemed to have been passed by both Houses in the form passed by Lok Sabha.
