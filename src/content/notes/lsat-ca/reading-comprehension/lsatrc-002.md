# Main Idea and Primary Purpose — LSAT Reading Comprehension

Main idea and primary purpose items ask the test-taker to identify the central claim, theme, or purpose of the passage. Often the first and highest-weighted question type on each LSAT Reading Comprehension passage.

> Verify the live section count, item count, and any in-year changes on https://www.lsac.org/ before planning around the figures below.

---

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### What main idea items test

- The central claim the passage is making.
- The theme or topic that unifies the passage.
- The primary purpose of the author.

#### One-pass drill

1. Read the passage. Identify the central claim in the first and last paragraphs.
2. Summarise the passage in one sentence.
3. Match the summary to the answer choices.

#### Examiner traps

- Picking a detail as the main idea. The main idea is the central claim, not a specific fact.
- Confusing the topic with the main idea. The topic is what the passage is about; the main idea is what the author says about the topic.
- Picking a choice that is too broad or too narrow.

---

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Strategy for main idea items

1. **Read the first paragraph.** The main idea is often stated or implied here.
2. **Read the last paragraph.** The main idea is often restated or reinforced here.
3. **Identify the central claim** that the rest of the passage supports, illustrates, or argues for.
4. **Summarise the passage in one sentence.**
5. **Match to the answer choices.** Pick the choice that captures the central claim.

#### Common main idea patterns

- **Argument:** the author argues a position on a topic.
- **Explanation:** the author explains a phenomenon, process, or concept.
- **Critique:** the author critiques a position, work, or idea.
- **Reflection:** the author reflects on a personal experience or thought.
- **Comparison:** the author compares two or more positions or phenomena.

#### Primary purpose vs main idea

**Primary purpose:** why the author wrote the passage (to argue, explain, critique, etc.).
**Main idea:** what the passage says.

The two are related but not identical. A passage can have the same main idea but different primary purposes (e.g., to argue for vs to explain a position).

#### Practice routine

- Take 10 RC passages. For each, identify the main idea in one sentence before reading the items.
- Time yourself: under 30 seconds for the main-idea pass.

---

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How main idea connects to the other RC skills

- **Inference** depends on the main idea; the inference should be consistent with the main idea.
- **Author's tone** is consistent with the main idea.
- **Strengthen/weaken** items target the main idea or the argument that supports it.

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"The main idea is stated in the title."** Sometimes, but not always. Read the passage to confirm.
- **"The main idea is the topic."** The main idea is what the author says about the topic.
- **"Primary purpose and main idea are the same."** Primary purpose is why the author writes; main idea is what the author says.

#### Exam technique

- For main idea items, read the first and last paragraphs first.
- Summarise the passage in one sentence.
- Pick the choice that captures the central claim.
- Use the platform's flag-for-review function for items you cannot answer.
