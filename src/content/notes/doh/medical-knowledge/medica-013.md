# Health Promotion and Patient Education

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Health promotion enables individuals and communities to gain control over the determinants of health, while patient education is the one-to-one teaching clinicians deliver during consultations. Both fall under DOH (UAE) clinical-governance standards and must be documented in the medical record. For the DOH exam, the three highest-yield ideas are the **Ottawa Charter's five action areas**, the **levels of prevention** (primary, secondary, tertiary), and the **five-step patient-education process** ending with teach-back verification.

- **Primary prevention** = stop disease before it starts (vaccination, smoking cessation).
- **Secondary prevention** = early detection (mammography, HbA1c screening).
- **Tertiary prevention** = limit complications (insulin foot-care in diabetic neuropathy).
- **Teach-back** is a mandatory comprehension check, not optional courtesy.
- Match your **learning objective** to Bloom's domain: cognitive (knowledge), affective (attitude), psychomotor (skill).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Ottawa Charter Framework (1986)

The WHO Ottawa Charter defines five action areas that examiners frequently test in scenario-based MCQs: build healthy public policy, create supportive environments, strengthen community action, develop personal skills, and reorient health services toward prevention. UAE initiatives such as **Weqaya** (cardiovascular screening) and **FitforLife** map directly onto "develop personal skills" and "reorient health services." A student who cannot recall all five areas typically loses marks on "which action is missing" stems.

#### Levels of Prevention Applied to Clinical Scenarios

The levels-of-prevention model is the single most tested framework in DOH Medical-Knowledge items on this topic. Examiners present a clinical action and ask you to classify it.

| Level | Goal | DOH/UAE clinical example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary | Prevent disease onset | HPV vaccine, anti-smoking campaign, salt-reduction policy |
| Secondary | Detect disease early | HbA1c screening, mammography, blood-pressure check |
| Tertiary | Reduce complications, rehab | Cardiac rehab post-MI, diabetic foot-care, stroke physiotherapy |

A common DOH MCQ lists "annual retinal screening in a known diabetic" and asks the prevention level; the answer is **secondary** (early detection of retinopathy), not tertiary. Labelling all diabetic care as tertiary is a frequent trap.

#### Patient-Education Process and Andragogy

The structured patient-education workflow is: assess learning needs, set SMART objectives, plan content and method, deliver using adult-learning principles (Knowles' andragogy — relevance, autonomy, problem-centred), then verify with **teach-back**. Domain mapping (Bloom's) is essential because insulin-injection training is **psychomotor**, while adherence counselling targets the **affective** domain. Plain-language delivery and culturally adapted materials address health literacy, which is critical across the multilingual UAE patient population (Arabic, English, Hindi, Urdu, Tagalog, Malayalam).

#### Behaviour-Change Counselling

For lifestyle diseases, motivational interviewing using OARS (Open questions, Affirmations, Reflections, Summaries) is the DOH-recognised technique. Pair it with the transtheoretical (stages of change) model — precontemplation, contemplation, preparation, action, maintenance — so your counselling tone matches the patient's readiness.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Exam Traps

DOH MCQs exploit subtle distinctions. **Health education** is information transfer; **health promotion** is the broader socio-environmental strategy — do not equate them. A leaflet is education; a smoke-free workplace law is promotion. **Therapeutic patient education (TPE)** is a structured, evaluated programme for chronic disease (WHO definition), distinct from ad-hoc advice.

Documentation is a DOH licensing requirement, not a courtesy. Every teach-back episode, every counselling contact, and every refusal must be recorded, because the **DOH Patient Education Standard** is auditable. A candidate who treats education as "informal" loses marks on governance items.

#### Worked Micro-Example

A 54-year-old UAE national with newly diagnosed Type 2 diabetes (HbA1c 8.4%), BMI 31, smoker, on metformin 1 g BD.

1. **Primary prevention** addressed: smoking cessation referral to a DOH cessation clinic; family-level lifestyle counselling because the patient's spouse and children share dietary patterns.
2. **Secondary prevention** addressed: annual retinal screening, foot examination, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, lipid panel — these detect early complications.
3. **Tertiary prevention** addressed: foot-care education to prevent ulceration; structured diabetes self-management education (DSME).
4. **Patient-education process**: assess literacy (Arabic preferred), set objective ("demonstrate correct insulin technique"), deliver via demonstration, verify with **teach-back** ("show me how you will inject tomorrow morning"), document in the chart.
5. **Counselling**: motivational interviewing (OARS) matched to the patient's stage of change; he is in **contemplation** about smoking, so use reflections rather than prescriptions.

#### Common Mistakes to Avoid

| Mistake | Correction |
| --- | --- |
| Calling insulin-foot care "primary prevention" | It is **tertiary** (limiting complications) |
| Omitting the teach-back step | Teach-back is mandatory under DOH standards |
| Ignoring health literacy | Use plain language and the patient's preferred language |
| Skipping documentation | Education must be charted for DOH audit compliance |
| Targeting only the cognitive domain | Insulin technique is psychomotor; adherence is affective |

#### Practice Prompts

1. A DOH MCQ asks which Ottawa Charter action area a school-based nutrition programme fits. Answer: **develop personal skills** (also acceptable: create supportive environments).
2. An OSCE station asks you to counsel a precontemplative smoker. Use OARS, avoid confrontation, and document the encounter.
