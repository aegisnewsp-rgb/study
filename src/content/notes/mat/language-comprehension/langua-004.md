# Vocabulary in Context

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your MAT slot.

Vocabulary in Context (ViC) is the skill of decoding an unfamiliar word's meaning from the linguistic environment around it, instead of recalling a memorised dictionary entry. MAT tests this skill inside Reading Comprehension passages and as stand-alone "choose the nearest meaning" items, carrying roughly 3% weight in Language Comprehension.

- **Five clue types to scan for:** definition/restatement, synonym, antonym/contrast, example/illustration, cause-effect inference.
- **Three decoding levels:** denotation (literal), connotation (associative), pragmatic (speaker intent/tone).
- **High-yield rule:** pick the meaning supported by at least two independent cues; reject any option that fits only one signal.

| Clue marker word | What it signals |
| --- | --- |
| "that is", "in other words" | Definition or restatement |
| "unlike", "whereas" | Antonym or contrast |
| "for example", "such as" | Illustration |
| "because", "therefore" | Cause-effect |
| "similarly", "likewise" | Synonym reinforcement |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months before MAT.

#### How the skill works in MAT passages
MAT examiners deliberately insert rare, polysemous, or low-frequency words (e.g. *erstwhile*, *salutary*, *equitable*) into RC passages and pair them with rich surrounding cues. The candidate must triangulate the in-text meaning from context clues, affix/root analysis, collocation, and the passage's overall register.

#### Decoding levels you must distinguish
Every target word in MAT carries three overlapping meanings. The trick is reading which level the passage is operating on.

| Level | What it captures | Example cue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Denotation | Dictionary-literal sense | "a *salubrious* climate" = health-giving |
| Connotation | Emotional/evaluative shade | "salubrious" additionally implies pleasant, restorative |
| Pragmatic | Speaker intent, irony, tone | "salubrious" used sarcastically to mock a polluted city |

#### The five context-clue families
Memorise these patterns because MAT items re-use them with cosmetic changes:

- **Definition/Restatement:** punctuation cues such as commas, dashes, parentheses, or "or", "i.e.", "that is" introduce a paraphrase.
- **Synonym clue:** "similarly", "also", "in the same way" point to a near-equivalent earlier in the sentence.
- **Antonym/Contrast clue:** "unlike", "in contrast", "however", "on the contrary" mark an opposing meaning nearby.
- **Example/Illustration clue:** "for example", "such as", "including" supply a concrete instance that narrows the field.
- **Cause-Effect/Inference clue:** "because", "since", "as a result" force a meaning consistent with the stated consequence.

#### Affix and collocation shortcuts
When the surrounding text is thin, fall back on morphemic decomposition. Roots like *bene-* (good), *mal-* (bad), *-phobia* (fear), *-cide* (kill), and *ante-/post-* (before/after) eliminate roughly half the options instantly. Collocation matters equally: a candidate meaning that breaks the natural adjective-noun or verb-preposition pairing (*commit a crime*, not *perform a crime*) is almost always wrong, even if it sounds plausible in isolation.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge cases that trap high scorers
The standard five-clue framework covers roughly 80% of MAT items. The remaining 20% hinge on subtler signals:

- **Register shift:** a single polysemous word (e.g. *charge*) changes meaning when the surrounding register jumps from legal ("bring charges") to financial ("no charge") to military ("charge the fort"). Detect the register before choosing a meaning.
- **Discourse coherence:** anaphora (a word referring back, e.g. *this*, *such*) and cataphora (referring forward, e.g. *the following*) tie the target word to a previously stated concept; ignore the reference and you misread the clue direction.
- **Semantic field clustering:** MAT sometimes places several near-synonyms in one paragraph. The correct option must respect the field (e.g. *melancholy, lugubrious, dolorous* all share a "sorrow" field); a meaning from an unrelated field is the distractor.

#### Common mistakes examiners exploit

| Mistake | Why it loses marks |
| --- | --- |
| Picking the most frequent dictionary sense | The passage uses a rarer, context-fit meaning |
| Misreading "unlike" as "like" | Swaps synonym for antonym, flipping the answer |
| Trusting only the affix | Root fits grammar but contradicts tone or register |
| Choosing a meaning that breaks collocation | e.g. "deep debt" instead of "heavily indebted" |
| Ignoring passage theme | A correct denotation may oppose the author's argument |

#### Two practice prompts
1. *"The minister's *equitable* redistribution of funds drew bipartisan praise, though critics argued the policy was merely a veneer of fairness."* — Identify the connotation of *equitable* and explain how "merely a veneer" reframes it.
2. *"Unlike his *gregarious* predecessor, the new CEO was taciturn, preferring memos to meetings."* — State whether *gregarious* is decoded by synonym or antonym clue, and give the opposite trait implied.

#### Exam strategy
Allocate roughly 45 seconds per ViC item. Read two sentences before and one sentence after the target word, circle any discourse marker, then test each option against collocation and register before locking the answer.
