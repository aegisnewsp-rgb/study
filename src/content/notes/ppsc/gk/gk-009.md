# Major Cities and Landmarks

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Pakistan's urban geography is anchored by **four provincial capitals** (Lahore-Punjab, Karachi-Sindh, Peshawar-KPK, Quetta-Balochistan), two regional capitals (Muzaffarabad-AJK, Gilgit-GB), and the **federal capital Islamabad**. PPSC questions cluster around three recall points: which city belongs to which province, which landmark sits in which city, and the official epithet attached to each major city.

- **Karachi** = largest city, Sindh capital, on the Arabian Sea — NOT Pakistan's capital.
- **Islamabad** = planned capital designed by Constantinos Doxiadis; **Faisal Mosque** is its signature landmark.
- **K2** (8,611 m) lies in Gilgit-Baltistan's Karakoram range — second-highest peak on Earth.

| City | Province/Region | Nickname |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lahore | Punjab | Heart of Pakistan |
| Multan | Punjab | City of Saints |
| Quetta | Balochistan | Fruit Garden of Pakistan |
| Karachi | Sindh | City of Lights |

Six **UNESCO World Heritage Sites** sit inside Pakistan: Moenjodaro, Taxila, Takht-i-Bahi, Lahore Fort & Shalimar Gardens, Makli, and Rohtas.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Provincial and Regional Capitals

Each administrative unit has exactly one capital — confusing them is the single most common PPSC trap. Faisalabad (formerly **Lyallpur**) is Punjab's industrial hub but not its capital; Sahiwal was formerly called Montgomery, not Lyallpur. Hyderabad, Sukkur, and Larkana are major Sindhi cities but only Karachi holds capital status for Sindh.

| City | Status | Defining River | Key Landmark |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Islamabad | Federal capital | Soan | Faisal Mosque |
| Lahore | Punjab capital | Ravi | Badshahi Mosque, Minar-e-Pakistan |
| Karachi | Sindh capital | Arabian Sea coast | Mazar-e-Quaid, Mohatta Palace |
| Peshawar | KPK capital | Kabul River | Bala Hissar, Mahabat Khan Mosque |
| Quetta | Balochistan capital | — | Hanna Lake, Quetta Fort |

#### Landmark-to-City Mapping

PPSC MCQs often pair a landmark with an incorrect city. **Minar-e-Pakistan** commemorates the 1940 Lahore Resolution and stands in Iqbal Park, Lahore — not Islamabad. **Pakistan Monument** in Islamabad symbolizes the four provinces and three territories. **Mazar-e-Quaid** in Karachi is Muhammad Ali Jinnah's mausoleum. The **Tombs of the Talpur Mirs** sit in Hyderabad, Sindh, along the Indus.

- Multan's **Shrine of Bahauddin Zakariya** and **Shams Tabriz** earned it the title *Madinah-tul-Auliya*.
- **Lansdowne Bridge** (1889) at Sukkur was once the longest cantilever bridge in British India.
- **Faisal Mosque** (1986) was funded by King Faisal of Saudi Arabia and designed by Turkish architect Vedat Dalokay.

#### Natural Landmarks in the North

The Karakoram, Hindu Kush, and western Himalaya converge in northern Pakistan. **K2 (Chhogori / Godwin-Austen)** at 8,611 m ranks second globally; **Nanga Parbat** (8,126 m) is called the *Killer Mountain*. **Khunjerab Pass** at 4,693 m is the highest paved border crossing on Earth, linking Pakistan to China via the **Karakoram Highway**. **Deosai Plains** average 4,100 m, making them the second-highest plateau on Earth.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### UNESCO Sites and Their Eras

Pakistan's six World Heritage Sites span four civilisations. **Moenjodaro** (Sindh, ~2500 BCE) belongs to the Indus Valley Civilisation alongside **Harappa** in Punjab. **Taxila** (KPK-Punjab border) layers Gandharan, Maurya, Kushan, and Mughal occupations across three cities. **Takht-i-Bahi** is a 1st-century Buddhist monastery complex. **Makli Hill** near Thatta holds over 500,000 tombs of Sindhi rulers from the 14th–18th centuries. **Rohtas Fort** (Sheikhupura, Punjab) was built by Sher Shah Suri in 1541 to block Mughal advances.

#### River–City Associations

PPSC sometimes frames questions as "which river passes through city X?" The **Indus** flows through Attock, D.I. Khan, Sukkur, and Hyderabad. The **Ravi** runs only through Lahore. The **Chenab** passes near Sialkot and Multan. The **Jhelum** gives its name to the city of Jhelum and continues to Muzaffarabad. The **Kabul River** enters Pakistan at Warsak and flows past Peshawar before joining the Indus at Attock.

#### Common Examiner Traps

1. Citing Faisal Mosque as the **largest mosque in the world** — Masjid al-Haram in Mecca holds that distinction; Faisal Mosque is among the largest.
2. Placing Mohenjo-daro in Punjab — the site sits in **Larkana District, Sindh**.
3. Treating Gilgit-Baltistan as a full province — it is a **self-governing region**, not a province.
4. Confusing K2's location — K2 belongs to Pakistan's **Karakoram** range, not the Himalayas.

| Trap | Correct Fact |
| --- | --- |
| Islamabad = largest city | Karachi is largest |
| Lyallpur = Sahiwal's old name | Lyallpur = Faisalabad |
| K2 in Himalaya | K2 is in the Karakoram |
| Mohenjo-daro in Punjab | Mohenjo-daro is in Sindh |

#### Practice Prompts

1. Match each provincial capital to its river or coastline and name one UNESCO-listed site within that province.
2. Identify which of the following epithets is wrongly paired: Multan–City of Saints; Lahore–Heart of Pakistan; Quetta–Fruit Garden; Karachi–City of Gardens.
