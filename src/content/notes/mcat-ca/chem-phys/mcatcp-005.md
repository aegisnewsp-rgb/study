# Phases, Gases and Solutions — MCAT Chem/Phys

Phases, gases, and solutions form a core part of AAMC Foundational Concept 5 of the Chem/Phys section. The MCAT tests phase changes, phase diagrams, the ideal gas law, partial pressures, solubility, and colligative properties in the context of biological fluids (blood, alveolar gas, intracellular solution). Items pair a passage describing a physical setup with a multiple-choice item that requires applying the relevant formula.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### Core formulas

- **Ideal gas law:** PV = nRT. P in atm; V in L; n in mol; R = 0.0821 L·atm/(mol·K); T in K.
- **Combined gas law:** P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂.
- **Dalton's law:** P_total = ΣP_partial = (n_total × RT) / V.
- **Henry's law:** C = k × P_gas. Solubility is proportional to partial pressure.
- **Molarity:** M = mol solute / L solution.
- **Osmolarity:** osmoles per litre. Osmoles = mol particles (count ions for ionic solutes).
- **Freezing-point depression and boiling-point elevation:** ΔT = K × m × i. m = molality; i = van't Hoff factor.

#### One-pass drill

1. Identify the topic: phase, gas, solution, or colligative.
2. List the known quantities and convert to SI units (atm, L, mol, K).
3. Pick the formula; solve algebraically.
4. Check the answer's units.

#### Examiner traps

- Forgetting to convert temperature to Kelvin. T(K) = T(°C) + 273.15.
- Treating molarity and molality as the same. Molarity = mol/L of solution; molality = mol/kg of solvent.
- Forgetting the van't Hoff factor for ionic solutes. NaCl dissociates into 2 particles; CaCl₂ into 3.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Phases and phase diagrams

A phase diagram plots pressure against temperature. The triple point is where solid, liquid, and gas coexist; the critical point is above which liquid and gas are indistinguishable. The MCAT tests:

- Reading the phase at a given (P, T).
- Predicting which way a phase boundary moves with changes in pressure or temperature.
- Identifying the phase transition (melting, boiling, sublimation, deposition) at each boundary.

Phase changes involve heat transfer. The MCAT tests latent heats (heat of fusion, heat of vaporisation) and specific heats.

#### Ideal and real gases

The ideal gas law (PV = nRT) is exact for an ideal gas and a good approximation for real gases at low pressure and high temperature. The MCAT uses the ideal gas law in most contexts; deviations from ideality are tested only in advanced passages.

**Dalton's law:** partial pressure of a gas in a mixture = mole fraction × total pressure. The MCAT tests this for alveolar gas exchange and for blood-gas measurements.

**Henry's law:** the concentration of a dissolved gas is proportional to its partial pressure above the liquid. The MCAT tests this for oxygen and carbon dioxide in blood.

#### Solutions and colligative properties

**Molarity** is mol solute per litre of solution. **Molality** is mol solute per kilogram of solvent.

**Osmolarity** is the total concentration of dissolved particles. The MCAT uses osmolarity in the context of intravenous fluids, kidney function, and tonicity (isotonic, hypotonic, hypertonic).

**Colligative properties** depend on the number of dissolved particles, not on their identity. Freezing-point depression, boiling-point elevation, and osmotic pressure are all colligative. The van't Hoff factor (i) accounts for dissociation of ionic solutes.

#### Practice routine

- Take 5 phase-diagram items. Identify the phase at a given (P, T); predict phase transitions.
- Take 10 gas-law items. Mix ideal gas law, Dalton's law, and Henry's law.
- Take 5 solution items. Mix molarity, osmolarity, and colligative properties.
- Pair each set with an AAMC-style passage.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How this topic connects to the other Chem/Phys topics

- **Acid-base chemistry** depends on solution chemistry — pH, buffers, and titration curves.
- **Kinetics and thermodynamics** depend on gas laws (reaction rates in the gas phase) and on solution chemistry (activation energy in solution).
- **Spectrophotometry** uses solution concentrations in the Beer-Lambert law.

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"Pressure and volume are inversely proportional."** Only at constant temperature (Boyle's law). At constant pressure, V and T are directly proportional.
- **"Henry's law applies to all solutes."** Henry's law applies to gases dissolving in liquids. Solids dissolving in liquids follow solubility rules, not Henry's law.
- **"Osmolarity and molarity are the same for ionic solutes."** No — for NaCl, osmolarity = 2 × molarity. For CaCl₂, osmolarity = 3 × molarity.

#### Exam technique

- Convert temperature to Kelvin before plugging into any gas-law formula.
- Use R = 0.0821 L·atm/(mol·K) for atm-L-mol-K units; R = 8.314 J/(mol·K) for SI energy units.
- For colligative properties, multiply by the van't Hoff factor.
- Read the phase diagram carefully — locate the (P, T) point first, then identify the phase.
- Use the platform's calculator.
