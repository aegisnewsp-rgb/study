# Ghana History and Independence Movement

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The Gold Coast colony became the Republic of Ghana on **6 March 1957**, the first Sub-Saharan African state to break free from European colonial rule. Prime Minister **Kwame Nkrumah** led the campaign through constitutional agitation and the **Positive Action** campaign of 1950, after the **Convention People's Party (CPP)** split from the **United Gold Coast Convention (UGCC)** in 1949.

- **Colonial anchor:** British Gold Coast Colony formally declared in **1874**, after the **Bond of 1844** signed with coastal Fante chiefs.
- **First mass party:** UGCC founded **4 August 1947** by J.B. Danquah, Nkrumah, and others; the **1948 ex-servicemen's riots** led to the Watson Commission.
- **Militant breakaway:** CPP founded **12 June 1949** by Nkrumah; launched Positive Action in **1950**.
- **Crowning date:** Independence declared **6 March 1957**; Republic declared **1 July 1960** with Nkrumah as President.
- **Symbol:** Ghana's flag — red, gold, green with the **Black Star** — was adopted by Nkrumah and inspired later African liberation flags.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"CUB-PP-NI"** — **C**asely Hayford (NCBWA, 1920) → **U**GCC (1947) → **B**ig split → **P**ositive Action (1950) → **P**rime Minister (1957) → **I**ndependence Star. Think **"CUB"** for the nationalist chain, ending with the **Black Star** on the flag.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Key Phases of the Independence Movement

The Gold Coast independence struggle ran on **three overlapping tracks**: pan-West African intellectual agitation (1920s–1940s), constitutional politics (1947–1951), and mass civil disobedience (1950–1957).

1. **Pan-West African nationalism:** Joseph Casely Hayford founded the **National Congress of British West Africa (NCBWA)** in **1920** in Accra, demanding representation and self-governance.
2. **Moderate constitutional phase:** The **UGCC (1947)** used petitions and Watson Commission testimony to seek reform.
3. **Militant mass phase:** The **CPP (1949)** used strikes, boycotts, and **Positive Action (1950)** to force the colonial government to hand power.

> 📌 **Formula Check:** Independence year minus founding of NCBWA = 1957 − 1920 = **37 years** of organised agitation. Memorise this interval to anchor any timeline question.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix

| Feature | UGCC (1947) | CPP (1949) |
|---|---|---|
| Founding leader | J.B. Danquah (president), with Nkrumah as secretary | Kwame Nkrumah |
| Ideology | Moderate constitutional nationalism | Militant, mass-based "self-government now" |
| Key tactic | Petitions, Watson Commission | Positive Action, strikes, civil disobedience |
| Outcome of 1951 election | Won few seats | Won **34 of 38** elected seats outright |
| Post-independence role | Opposed via NLM | Ruling party until 1966 coup |

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** Which of the following correctly sequences the major organisations of the Gold Coast independence movement?

A. NCBWA → CPP → UGCC
B. UGCC → NCBWA → CPP
C. NCBWA → UGCC → CPP
D. CPP → UGCC → NCBWA
E. NCBWA → UGCC → CPP → NLM

#### Solution:

- **Step 1 — Earliest body:** The **NCBWA was founded in 1920** by Casely Hayford, making it the oldest of the three.
- **Step 2 — Moderate party:** The **UGCC was formed in 1947** by Danquah and colleagues to channel post-war grievances.
- **Step 3 — Militant breakaway:** The **CPP was formed in 1949** when Nkrumah split from the UGCC and adopted Positive Action.
- **Step 4 — Verify dates:** 1920 (NCBWA) → 1947 (UGCC) → 1949 (CPP). This matches option **C** and the extended form **E**.

**Answer: C** (or **E** if the National Liberation Movement post-1954 is included).

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students often write **UGCC (1947) before CPP (1949)** correctly but place the **NCBWA (1920) second**, forgetting that Casely Hayford's congress predates both by nearly three decades. Another trap: confusing the **Watson Commission (1948)** with the **Coussey Committee (1949)** — Watson investigated the riots; Coussey drafted the new constitution.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Constitutional Milestones and Their Triggers

| Year | Event | Trigger / Significance |
|---|---|---|
| 1844 | Bond of 1844 signed | Fante chiefs accept British jurisdiction |
| 1874 | Gold Coast Colony declared | Formal British colonial administration begins |
| 1920 | NCBWA founded | First pan-West African political body |
| 1948 | Ex-servicemen's riots | Unemployed WWII veterans march in Accra; Watson Commission set up |
| 1949 | Coussey Committee report | Recommends elected African majority in legislature |
| 1950 | Positive Action launched | Nkrumah imprisoned; CPP wins by-elections |
| 1951 | First CPP electoral victory | Nkrumah released, becomes **Leader of Government Business** |
| 1954 | Constitutional Review | Gold Coast achieves **internal self-government** |
| 1956 | Plebiscite | Ashanti, Northern Territories, and Togoland vote to join independent Gold Coast |
| 1957 | **6 March — Independence** | Nkrumah becomes PM; Queen remains Head of State |
| 1960 | **1 July — Republic** | Nkrumah becomes President after referendum |
| 1965 | Aburi Pact | Nkrumah–military agreement on regional command (often tested as a distractor) |

#### Common Traps and Edge Cases

- **"First African colony" trap:** Ghana was the **first Sub-Saharan** independent state, not the first on the continent. **Egypt (1922), Libya (1951), Sudan (1956)** all gained independence earlier.
- **"Armed struggle" trap:** Independence was achieved through **constitutional politics and Positive Action (non-violent civil disobedience)**, not armed revolt.
- **Aburi Pact confusion:** The **Aburi Pact (1965)** is a *post*-independence event between Nkrumah and the military police commanders, not an independence milestone.
- **Nkrumah's titles:** Nkrumah was styled **Osagyefo** ("Redeemer") after the 1960 republic, but became **Prime Minister in 1957** — do not swap the dates.
- **Flag symbolism:** The **Black Star** symbol came from the **Black Star Line** of Marcus Garvey; the flag was designed by **Theodosia Okoh**.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. **Cause-and-effect essay prompt:** "Explain why the 1948 ex-servicemen's riots accelerated the Gold Coast independence movement rather than the UGCC's earlier constitutional petitions." Frame the answer around the Watson Commission → Coussey Committee → 1951 election chain.
2. **Comparative analysis prompt:** "Compare the UGCC's moderate strategy (1947–1950) with the CPP's Positive Action (1950–1957), and assess which was more effective in securing independence by 1957." Expected structure: ideology, leadership, methods, results, post-1957 legacy.
