# Indian Geography & Environment

> AILET asks 1–3 factual MCQs from this GK unit. Memorising the physical map, monsoon calendar, and a few protected-area numbers is enough to bank these marks.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Quick India map: north mountains, north rivers, peninsular block, west desert, two coastlines with islands. Anything that survives one reading belongs here.

India's physiography compresses into five units running almost north to south. The **Himalayan arc** borders the **Indo-Gangetic Plain**, which gives way to the **Peninsular Plateau**, flanked by **Coastal Plains** with the **Indian Desert** tucked into the west. Climate is **tropical monsoon**, meaning one wet quarter (June–September, SW monsoon) and a long dry tail.

- **SW monsoon** drives ~75% of India's annual rainfall. Onset over Kerala: **1 June** (with a ±4-day window). Withdrawal from northwest begins early September.
- **Himalayan rivers** (Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra) are perennial. **Peninsular rivers** (Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri) are rain-fed.
- **India has 4 biodiversity hotspots**: Himalaya, Western Ghats, Indo-Burma, Sundaland (Andaman segment).
- **Forests** cover ~21–22% of geographic area; **National Forest Policy 1988** targets 33%.
- **Indian Standard Time** = 82°30'E longitude; offset **UTC +5:30**.
- **K2 lies in PoK** — India's highest peak inside its administered boundary is Kangchenjunga (8,586 m). Highest in Indian-administered Ladakh is disputed on maps; most exam-safe answer is Kangchenjunga.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook (Memory Hook):** "**HIGCC**" — **H**imalaya, **I**ndo-Gangetic plain, **G**hats/Peninsular plateau, **C**oastal plains, **C**oastal islands. Walk north-to-south in this order when a question asks "physiographic divisions from north to south".

| Division | Highest / Key Landmark | River pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Northern Mountains | Kangchenjunga 8,586 m | Antecedent + consequent |
| Northern Plains | Flat alluvial belt | Dendritic, perennial |
| Peninsular Plateau | Anamudi 2,695 m | Radial (Godavari, Kaveri) |
| Indian Desert | Thar (extends into Rajasthan) | Inland drainage |
| Coastals + Islands | Andaman (volcanic) / Lakshadweep (coral) | Short west-flowing rivers |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard depth. Drop the All-Karnataka, all-river-name-level granularity; this tier cares about how the monsoon engine works and which statutory body administers which category of protected land.

#### Monsoon Mechanism and Rainfall Distribution

The SW monsoon is not one wind but a layered system. Heating of the Tibetan plateau in late May pulls the **Inter-Tropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ)** north of the equator; simultaneously, a low forms over the Thar and a high over the southern Indian Ocean. Pressure gradient, the **Somali jet** cross-equatorial flow, and the **Coriolis deflection** push moist air against the Western Ghats, Meghalaya hills (Cherrapunji/Mawsynram), and the Himalayan foothills. The **Mascarene High** supplies moisture; its weakening in El-Niño years often suppresses monsoon rainfall.

The **NE monsoon (Oct–Dec)** is technically the *retreating* SW monsoon carrying residual moisture to Tamil Nadu's coast — different from the **NE trade winds** that replace it later. **Western Disturbances** are mid-latitude cyclones travelling east from the Mediterranean; they hit Punjab, Haryana, and UP in winter and cause the fog/cold-wave events that disrupt north Indian rail traffic.

#### Rivers: Himalayan vs Peninsular

| Feature | Himalayan rivers | Peninsular rivers |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Glaciers, young fold mountains | Old plateau, rainfall-fed |
| Flow regime | Perennial | Seasonal |
| Pattern | Dendritic, antecedent | Radial, trellis, rectangular |
| Stage of valley | Deep gorges, youthful | Mature, broad |
| Delta / estuary | Mostly deltas | Deltas (Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri); **Narmada & Tapi form estuaries** (rift-valley rivers) |
| Example | Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra | Godavari 1,465 km (longest Peninsular), Krishna, Kaveri, Narmada, Tapi |

#### Protected-Area Categories

| Category | Statutory basis | Governing body |
|---|---|---|
| National Park | Wildlife Protection Act 1972 (Sch. II) | State govt with National Board for Wildlife clearance |
| Wildlife Sanctuary | WPA 1972 (Sch. III–V) | State govt |
| Biosphere Reserve | UNESCO MAB programme (India has 18) | MoEFCC with state coordination |
| Tiger Reserve | Project Tiger / Wildlife Protection Act (Sch. I core) | National Tiger Conservation Authority |
| Ramsar Site | Ramsar Convention 1971 (India signatory 1981) | MoEFCC; 80+ sites designated |
| Conservation Reserve / Community Reserve | WPA 1972 amendment 2003 | Community / state |

#### Environmental Statutes — At a Glance

- **Wildlife Protection Act 1972** — first umbrella wildlife law; six schedules grade protection (Schedule I = absolute, e.g., lion-tailed macaque, Great Indian Bustard).
- **Forest Conservation Act 1980** — restricts diversion of forest land for non-forest use; needs central approval.
- **Environment Protection Act 1986** — enacted after Bhopal; gives Centre power to set standards, conduct EIA, and prosecute polluters. Umbrella statute.
- **National Green Tribunal Act 2010** — adjudicates environmental disputes; fast-track.

#### Worked Conceptual MCQ

Consider the question: *"Which of the following statements about Indian rivers is INCORRECT?"*
(1) Narmada and Tapi flow westward and form estuaries.
(2) Godavari is the longest Peninsular river.
(3) Kaveri forms a delta on the east coast.
(4) Indus originates near Lake Mansarovar and enters India through Ladakh.

All four read true at first glance. The trap is the framing — the question type usually asks for the odd one out, but here every statement checks out as fact. The intended incorrect option in the paper set is often **(4)** because the precise origin phrase "near Lake Mansarovar" is contested, and many examiners instead credit **Chemayungdung glacier (Tibet)**. AILET has previously tested the difference between Mansarovar (a sacred lake) and the actual glacier source. Another frequent design: option stating "Kaveri forms an estuary" — false, Kaveri delta is one of the oldest deltas in the world.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** "Western Ghats are discontinuous" is a trap line in itself — the *Western* Ghats are the continuous chain (UNESCO World Heritage, 2,695 m Anamudi); it is the **Eastern** Ghats that are broken by the Godavari, Mahanadi, and Kaveri.

> Extended depth. The trap here is volume — don't try to memorise every national park. Instead, learn the rule that creates the question.

#### Edge Cases and Boundary Conditions

The Tropic of Cancer (23°5'N) bisects India almost through the middle, dividing the country into a tropical south and a sub-tropical north. Eight states fall on or near this line, but only **Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, and West Bengal** record the line passing meaningfully through them. Mizoram, Tripura, and the southern districts of Manipur fall south of it.

The **Coriolis effect** determines whether an Indian river system erodes its right bank more. The Ganga at Rajmahal and the Brahmaputra's northward bend are textbook outcomes. Peninsular rivers, originating close to the Ghats, flow east or west depending on slope, but their courses are short of enough length for significant Coriolis drift; the **Krishna's** lean west of its expected channel is more often attributed to local tectonic tilt.

The **Soils–Climate–Crop triad** generates repeated statement-based questions:

| Soil | Parent / climate | Crop association | Misconception tested |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alluvial | Indo-Gangetic, river deposits | Rice, wheat, sugarcane | "Found in Peninsular India" — false; Peninsular is red/black |
| Black / Regur | Deccan basalt, low rainfall | Cotton (self-ploughing) | "Black soil needs irrigation" — false, it retains moisture |
| Red | Crystalline / metamorphic rock, low rainfall | Groundnut, millet | "Red is volcanic" — false |
| Laterite | Heavy rainfall, leaching | Tea, coffee, cashew | "Laterite is fertile" — false; needs manuring |
| Desert / Arid | Western Rajasthan | Bajra, drought-tolerant | "Desert soil is rich in humus" — false |
| Mountain / Forest | Himalayan / Western Ghats slopes | Tea, spices, temperate fruits | Confused with laterite |

#### Current-Affairs Linkages for AILET

The AILET GK section has shifted weight toward statutory-news linkage over the past three years. Concrete patterns to expect:

1. **New Ramsar designation** → ask the wetland's state and the count of Indian sites.
2. **Tiger Reserve addition** → ask which core-buffer model applies and which state houses it.
3. **Cyclone naming** → the Indian Ocean basin follows a 13-country rotation; name contributions come from member states.
4. **COP / climate negotiations** → ask India's NDC targets (500 GW non-fossil capacity, net-zero by 2070) — but the precise target year and percentage are set by the most recent official notification, which students should verify against the Ministry of External Affairs' COP dossier each year before the exam.
5. **Disaster events** → ask the disaster-management institutional hierarchy: NDMA (policy), SDMA (state), DDMA (district), NDRF battalions (operational).

#### Common Traps the Examiner Exploits

1. Confusing the **Western Ghats** (continuous, higher, biodiversity hotspot) with the **Eastern Ghats** (discontinuous, lower, broken by river valleys).
2. Calling **Narmada/Tapi** delta rivers when they are rift-valley rivers forming estuaries.
3. Treating **IST** as a Kolkata (88°30'E) or Mumbai (72°50'E) reference instead of 82°30'E.
4. Marking **K2** as India's peak — it is in PoK/Gilgit-Baltistan; the peak claimed by India is Kangchenjunga.
5. Writing **NE monsoon = NE trade winds**; the **retreating monsoon** of October–December is a different mechanism from the dry continental NE trades.
6. Assuming **Western Disturbances** affect only Punjab — they reach Delhi, NCR, and sometimes trigger hailstorms in MP and Chhattisgarh.
7. Saying **Cherrapunji is the wettest place on Earth today** — the title is increasingly contested by Mawsynram and the distinction depends on the reference period used.
8. Citing **Project Tiger 1973** as the origin of *all* wildlife protection — wildlife sanctuary status predates it; Project Tiger addresses a single flagship programme.
9. Assuming **mangroves grow only in Sundarbans** — significant mangroves exist in Bhitarkanika (Odisha), Gulf of Kutch, Andaman & Nicobar, and Godavari delta.
10. Treating the **Forest (Conservation) Act 1980** as the statute that *created* national parks — the WPA 1972 does; FCA only restricts diversion.
