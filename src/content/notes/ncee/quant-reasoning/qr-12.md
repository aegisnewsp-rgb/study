# Distance, Speed and Time Problems

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The three quantities **distance**, **speed**, and **time** are linked by one master equation that you rearrange depending on what the question asks you to find. NCEE Quantitative Reasoning treats this trio as a single sub-topic worth roughly 4% of the paper, and it almost always appears as a one- or two-step word problem with whole-number answers.

- **Speed = Distance ÷ Time**, **Distance = Speed × Time**, **Time = Distance ÷ Speed**
- **1 km/h = 5/18 m/s** and **1 m/s = 18/5 km/h** — convert before substituting.
- Average speed = **Total distance ÷ Total time** (not the mean of two speeds).
- Relative speed = **sum** when moving towards each other, **difference** when moving in the same direction.
- Boat/streams: downstream adds the current, upstream subtracts it.

| Quantity | Unit (metric) | Unit (other) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Distance | metres (m) or kilometres (km) | — |
| Speed | m/s or km/h | — |
| Time | seconds (s) or hours (h) | minutes (min) |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core formulas and the triangle method

The kinematic relationship can be memorised as a triangle with **Speed** at the apex and **Distance** and **Time** at the base. Cover the quantity you want and read the rest. The defining equation is **S = D / T**, which rearranges into D = S × T and T = D / S. Always write the unit beside every number so an accidental mismatch (km with minutes, for example) shows up immediately.

#### Unit conversion before substitution

NCEE word problems often mix units within one sentence: "A car covers 150 km in 2 hours 30 minutes." Convert 2 hours 30 minutes to 2.5 h **before** dividing, giving 60 km/h. The conversion factors **5/18** (km/h → m/s) and **18/5** (m/s → km/h) appear whenever speeds are given in one system and distances in another. Multiplying by 60/60 or 1000/1000 silently inside the formula is the most common source of wrong answers.

#### Average speed and the harmonic trap

When a vehicle travels from A to B at one speed and returns at a different speed, average speed is **not** (v₁ + v₂) / 2 unless the two **times** are equal. The correct rule is **total distance ÷ total time**. If the one-way distance is d, then average speed = 2d / (d/v₁ + d/v₂), which is the harmonic mean of the two speeds.

#### Relative motion and boat-and-stream

Two trains approaching each other close the gap at the **sum** of their speeds; two trains moving in the same direction separate at the **difference**. A boat in still water at speed **b** with a stream of speed **c** travels downstream at **b + c** and upstream at **b − c**, which is why return journeys against the current take longer.

| Concept | Formula to apply | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Average speed | Total distance ÷ Total time | Not (v₁+v₂)/2 unless times equal |
| Relative speed (opposite) | v₁ + v₂ | Meeting-time questions |
| Relative speed (same) | v₁ − v₂ | Catching-up questions |
| Downstream / Upstream | b + c / b − c | Stream speed must be subtracted upstream |

- Convert all units **first**; do not mix km with seconds or minutes with m/s.
- Draw the triangle for S, D, T to pick the right rearrangement quickly.
- Round-trip average speed uses the **harmonic** form, not the arithmetic mean.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge cases that appear as distractors

Examiners for the NCEE often set traps using partial journeys. If a cyclist rides at 12 km/h for the first half of the time and 8 km/h for the second half, average speed is the **arithmetic mean** (10 km/h) because equal times are involved. If instead the cyclist covers half the **distance** at each speed, the harmonic form applies and the answer becomes 9.6 km/h. Recognising whether "half" refers to time or to distance is what separates full marks from a wrong choice.

Another classic trap: a train 200 m long passes a platform 100 m long in 15 seconds. The distance covered is **200 + 100 = 300 m**, not 200 m, because the engine must travel its own length plus the platform length before the tail clears.

#### Worked micro-example

A boat goes 24 km downstream in 2 hours and the same 24 km upstream in 4 hours. Let still-water speed be **b** and stream speed be **c**.
Downstream: b + c = 24 / 2 = 12 km/h.
Upstream: b − c = 24 / 4 = 6 km/h.
Adding: 2b = 18, so **b = 9 km/h**. Subtracting: 2c = 6, so **c = 3 km/h**. Speed of the boat in still water is 9 km/h and the stream flows at 3 km/h.

#### Common mistakes and exam strategy

- Treating the return trip as taking the same time as the outward trip when speeds differ.
- Dividing by (v₁ + v₂)/2 for average speed instead of total distance over total time.
- Forgetting the platform/train-length addition in "passes a pole" versus "passes a platform" problems.
- Misreading "in how many seconds will A meet B" — use **closing** speed (sum), not difference.

| Mistake | Why it fails | Correct move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Average = (v₁+v₂)/2 | Ignores time asymmetry | Use 2d / (d/v₁ + d/v₂) |
| Mixing km/h with seconds | Units inconsistent | Convert km/h to m/s with ×5/18 |
| Sum of speeds in same direction | Wrong relative motion | Use \|v₁ − v₂\| for chasing |
| Ignoring train length | Underestimates distance | Add train + platform lengths |

1. A car travels 90 km at 60 km/h and returns the same 90 km at 40 km/h. Find the average speed for the whole journey.
2. Two runners start from points 600 m apart and run towards each other at 4 m/s and 6 m/s. After how many seconds do they meet?
