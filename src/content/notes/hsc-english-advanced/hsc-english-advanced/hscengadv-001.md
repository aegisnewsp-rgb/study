# Reading to Write — HSC English Advanced (NESA 2024) Notes

Reading to Write: Transition to English Advanced is the Year 11 common module shared with English Standard and English Studies. It is the foundation module for senior English study and consolidates the skills students need for analysis, composition and evidence-based response. The module is school-assessed in Year 11 and not directly examined in the HSC; however, the skills developed here carry forward into every Year 12 module and into the HSC examination itself.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Module structure.** One common module shared by English Standard, English Advanced and English Studies in Year 11. Focuses on the relationship between reading and writing; students read widely and write in multiple forms.
- **Outcomes.** Students demonstrate understanding of how composers use language forms and features to achieve particular purposes; compose texts using a range of language forms and features for specific audiences and purposes.
- **Texts.** Schools select a range of texts (print, non-print, multimedia) that span a variety of contexts. There are no prescribed texts for Year 11.
- **Assessment.** School-based assessment tasks. Each task is associated with one or more outcomes and must include a written component.

#### Examiner traps

- Treating Reading to Write as a generic reading module — the module is specifically about the relationship between reading and writing, not about reading comprehension in isolation.
- Forgetting to teach the language features vocabulary (tone, register, modality, syntax, diction, narrative voice) that underpins the analytical work in Year 12.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Module purpose

Reading to Write: Transition to English Advanced bridges the gap between Stage 5 English and the HSC. Students who have not consolidated Stage 5 outcomes find this module challenging, but it is the explicit NESA strategy for Year 11 to bring students to a common floor.

#### Reading skills

Students analyse how texts construct meaning through language forms and features. They identify textual integrity, the ways a composer shapes meaning through deliberate choices about form, structure and language. They respond to texts in a range of modes — analytic, personal, imaginative, discursive — and across a range of purposes — to entertain, to argue, to inform, to reflect.

#### Writing skills

Students compose their own texts in a range of forms (essay, speech, short story, narrative, reflection, feature article, blog post). They learn to control their language choices for specific audiences and purposes, and to revise their work in response to feedback.

#### Key terminology

Tone = the writer's attitude toward the subject or audience. Register = the level of formality (formal, semi-formal, informal, colloquial). Modality = the degree of certainty expressed (must, may, might, could). Diction = word choice. Syntax = sentence structure and arrangement. Narrative voice = the perspective from which the story is told (first person, third person limited, third person omniscient). Medium = the platform through which the text is delivered (print, screen, audio, video).

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Why Reading to Write matters for Year 12 and beyond

- **The HSC Common Module (Texts and Human Experiences)** assumes students can write analytical responses supported by textual evidence. Reading to Write is the Year 11 module that builds this skill.
- **The Craft of Writing (Year 12 Module C)** assumes students can write in multiple forms. Reading to Write is where students first encounter forms beyond the essay.
- **Textual Conversations (Year 12 Module A)** assumes students can compare texts. Reading to Write introduces the foundational skill of comparing two texts on the same theme or idea.

#### Year 11 planning pattern

- **Term 1.** Module introduction; teach language features vocabulary; set regular analytical response tasks.
- **Term 2.** Composition tasks in multiple forms (essay, short story, speech, reflection); peer and self-assessment cycles.
- **Term 3.** Comparative response tasks (pair texts on the same theme).
- **Term 4.** Revision and assessment tasks; introduce the Year 12 prescribed text list and the HSC examination structure.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Reading to Write is just reading comprehension." No — the module is about the relationship between what you read and what you write; it is composition-heavy.
- "School assessment only means the HSC won't test these skills." The HSC does not examine this module directly, but every HSC examination question rewards the skills built here.

#### Specification reference

This module is school-assessed in Year 11 and is the foundation for all Year 12 modules and the HSC examination. The skills, not the texts, carry forward. Confirm the live module descriptor and any NESA updates on curriculum.nsw.edu.au for the current specification before final revision.
