# Pakistan Studies

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Pakistan Studies** is the multidisciplinary school subject covering the land, people, history, constitution, economy, and foreign policy of Pakistan. For HAT-UG Subject Knowledge (≈3% weightage), expect short MCQs on dates, personalities, and constitutional provisions. The single most-tested idea is the **Two-Nation Theory** — the claim that Hindus and Muslims were distinct nations, articulated by **Allama Iqbal's Allahabad Address (1930)** and operationalised by **Muhammad Ali Jinnah's leadership** through the **Lahore Resolution (23 March 1940)**. Other must-know anchors: **Partition (14 August 1947)**, the **1973 Constitution**, the **Indus Waters Treaty (1960)**, and the four **provinces** plus **AJK, Gilgit-Baltistan**, and the merged tribal districts. Revision rule: memorise the chronology 1857 → 1940 → 1947 → 1956 → 1962 → 1973 and the names attached to each milestone.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Foundations of the Pakistan Movement
The separatist case rested on a separate Muslim socio-political identity. **Sir Syed Ahmad Khan** founded the **Aligarh Movement (1875)** and the **Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental College** to modernise Muslim education through English-medium learning. The **Hindi-Urdu controversy (1867)** showed the religious dimension of language politics. The **All-India Muslim League (AIML)** was founded in **Dhaka, 30 December 1906**. Early failures — the **Nehru Report (1928)** ignoring separate electorates, the Allahabad Address proposing a Muslim homeland within a federated India — pushed Jinnah from constitutionalism to demand. The **Lahore Resolution (1940)** formalised the demand for independent states.

#### Constitutional Evolution
Pakistan's constitutional history runs through three full constitutions: **1956** (parliamentary, Islamic republic declared), **1962** (presidential, imposed by Ayub Khan), and the current **1973 Constitution** (parliamentary, federal; restored after the 1971 war). Key amending acts include the **8th Amendment (1985)** enlarging presidential powers and the **18th Amendment (2010)** devolving them back. The **Objectives Resolution (1949)** was later made a substantive part of the Constitution via Article 2A; Articles 227–231 enforce Islamic provisions.

#### Geography and Economy
Physiographic divisions are the **Northern and Western Mountains**, the **Indus Plain**, the **Balochistan Plateau**, and the **Thar Desert**. The **Indus River system** (Indus, Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Sutlej) irrigates the country under the **Indus Waters Treaty** with India. Major **Kharif** crops are **cotton, rice, sugarcane**; **Rabi** crops are **wheat, gram, barley**. Pakistan's cotton and rice exports, plus overseas **remittances**, anchor the foreign-exchange earnings. **CPEC** (China-Pakistan Economic Corridor) added a long-term infrastructure and energy axis from **Gwadar** to **Kashgar**.

#### Foreign Policy and Society
Quaid-i-Azam's principles of **peace, non-alignment, and self-determination** (Aug 11, 1947 Constituent Assembly speech) remain the doctrinal baseline. The **Kashmir dispute** persists since 1947; the **Simla Agreement (1972)** converted the ceasefire into the **Line of Control**. Pakistan's nuclear capability, established in May 1998, is a stated deterrence against existential threats. Socially, the country has five major ethnic groups — **Punjabi, Sindhi, Pashtun, Baloch, Muhajir** — plus religious minorities protected under law.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Common Exam Traps
- **Allama Iqbal's address** was delivered in **December 1930 at Allahabad**, not 1929; confusion with the Round Table Conferences is common.
- The **Cabinet Mission (1946)** proposed a weak-federation model that was rejected by AIML on 29 July 1946; the **3rd June Plan (Mountbatten Plan, 1947)** is distinct and followed.
- The **One-Unit scheme (1955–1970)** merged all West Pakistan provinces into one unit to counter East Pakistan's majority; students often misdate its dissolution to 1969 instead of 1970.
- The **Indus Waters Treaty** allocated the three **western rivers (Indus, Jhelum, Chenab)** to Pakistan and the three **eastern rivers (Ravi, Beas, Sutlej)** to India — not the other way around.
- The **Federation of Pakistan** has **four provinces, plus AJK and Gilgit-Baltistan**, and formerly FATA, which was merged with Khyber Pakhtunkhwa via the **25th Amendment (2018)**.

#### Worked Example — NFC Award Logic
The **National Finance Commission Award** distributes the federal divisible pool between federation and provinces. The successive awards (1st in 1951, 7th in 2009, 8th in 2015, 9th in 2018, 10th in 2025) revised the provincial share using population, poverty, revenue collection, and inverse-population-density weights. Tracking which award increased Balochistan's share above 9% and which retained Punjab's weight near 51% is a frequent MCQ trap.

#### Practice Prompts
1. Trace how the failure of the **Khilafat Movement (1919–1924)** and the **Nehru Report (1928)** together radicalised the Muslim League's constitutional position between 1928 and 1940.
2. Compare the **1962 Constitution's** Basic Democracies structure with the **1973 Constitution's** parliamentary system; identify two institutional changes that explain why the 1973 document restored federalism.
