# Line Graphs & Trend Analysis

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your MAT Data-Analysis slot.

A **line graph** plots ordered data points on a 2D Cartesian plane and joins them with straight segments; **trend analysis** reads the resulting line's slope, direction, and shape to describe how a variable moves with respect to another (in most keys time on the x-axis).

- The slope between two points is $m = \dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$, with units of y per x (e.g. ₹ crore per quarter).
- Percent change uses $\% \Delta = \dfrac{\text{New} - \text{Old}}{\text{Old}} \times 100$, the figure MAT questions ask for most in many papers.
- Always read the **axis label and grid interval** before judging direction; a 0–100 scale behaves nothing like a 0–10 scale.

| Movement on graph | Interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| Rising line | Positive slope — variable grew over the period |
| Falling line | Negative slope — variable shrank |
| Flat segment | Zero slope — period of stagnation |
| Peak then trough | Reversal — identify the inflection point |

MAT tests this topic under Data Analysis & Sufficiency (≈3% weight), in most keys with 3–5 multi-line graphs per paper asking for percent change or slope comparison.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Anatomy of a MAT line graph

The horizontal axis carries the **independent variable** (almost always a time period — year, quarter, month), while the vertical axis carries the **dependent variable** (revenue, units sold, cost, profit ratio). Each plotted point is an observation, and the line segments joining them are visual aids, not data themselves. MAT examiners routinely disguise this by omitting gridlines, using irregular intervals, or plotting two series on the same chart with very different magnitudes.

#### The four questions the paper keeps asking

1. Compute the **slope** between two labelled years — straight subtraction after aligning the axes.
2. Compute **percent change** between two points — formula above, unitless.
3. Identify the **peak**, **trough**, or **crossover** point between two series.
4. Compare two lines using **ratios or indices**, not raw numbers, when scales differ.

#### Slope, secant, and average rate of change

A single segment's slope is an **instantaneous visual rate**. The slope between two non-adjacent points (a secant) is the **average rate of change** over that span. Students who treat the chart as one straight line lose marks whenever the line bends.

| Concept | Formula | When MAT uses it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Slope of a segment | $m = (y_2 - y_1)/(x_2 - x_1)$ | Compare steepness of two periods |
| Percent change | $[(N - O)/O] \times 100$ | Growth/decline questions |
| Average rate over n intervals | $\Sigma \Delta y / n$ | "Steady" or "uniform" trend wording |
| CAGR | $[(V_f/V_i)^{1/n} - 1] \times 100$ | Multi-year compounding tables |

#### Standard MAT traps

- Lines with different y-axis ranges plotted on the same chart — the visual gap is meaningless.
- A line that dips once before rising is **not** a declining trend; isolate the segment slopes.
- Crossover point ≠ peak; mark the exact (x, y) where the two series meet.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Reading mechanism: from raw pixels to a numerical claim

A defensible MAT answer follows three steps. First, anchor the axes: write down the unit (₹ lakh, million units, % share) and the gridline interval for both x and y. Second, read off the two coordinates asked about — never estimate by eye when the question supplies a table. Third, apply the correct operator (slope, percent change, ratio, CAGR) to those coordinates.

#### Worked micro-example

Suppose a chart shows Company A's revenue: 2019 = ₹200 cr, 2021 = ₹320 cr, 2023 = ₹440 cr (points every 2 years). Slope 2019→2021 = (320 − 200)/2 = **₹60 cr/year**. Percent change 2019→2023 = (440 − 200)/200 × 100 = **120%**. CAGR over 4 years = [(440/200)^(1/4) − 1] × 100 = **21.9% per year**. Three numbers from the same line, three different questions MAT can ask.

#### Edge cases that surface in higher-difficulty sets

- **Non-uniform x-axis spacing**: when x-values are 2017, 2018, 2020, 2023, divide by the actual $\Delta x$, not by the count of segments.
- **Different units on twin axes**: a left axis in crores and a right axis in millions makes a 1:1 visual crossover mathematically a 10:1 ratio mismatch — read the legend.
- **Compound vs simple growth**: a question that says "average annual growth" without specifying compound expects the simple mean of yearly percent changes, not CAGR.
- **Trend reversal detection**: locate the **inflection point** where slope changes sign, then compute the magnitude of the swing.

#### Exam strategy for MAT Data Analysis (3%)

Three to five line-graph questions appear in the Data Analysis & Sufficiency section, in most keys paired with a 3–4 line chart of revenue or cost across 5–7 years. Allocate ~90 seconds per question; spend the first 15 seconds annotating axes and unit before touching the calculator. If two answers look close, recompute percent change rather than slope — examiners weight the percentage question more in many papers.

#### Practice prompts

1. Given sales of 120, 150, 135, 180, 210 over five years, compute the slope 2019→2022 and percent change year 1 to year 5.
2. Two product lines cross in 2020; one ends at 400 units, the other at 360, after starting at 100 and 300 respectively. Which line had the higher CAGR, and by how many percentage points?
