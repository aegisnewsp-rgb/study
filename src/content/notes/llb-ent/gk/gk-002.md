# South Africa's Legal System and Court Structure

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The Constitution of 1996 is the supreme law (Section 2). Every court below it must follow it. Roman-Dutch law is the inherited backbone, English common law rides alongside it, and customary law is recognised under Section 211 of the Constitution.

Court ladder from bottom to top:

| Level | Court |
|-------|-------|
| 1 | Magistrates' Court (District) |
| 2 | Magistrates' Court (Regional) |
| 3 | High Court |
| 4 | Supreme Court of Appeal (SCA) |
| 5 | Constitutional Court |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** "**D**istrict **R**egional **H**igh **S**CA **C**onCourt" — read it as **D-R-H-S-C** and you have the full five-rung ladder in the order cases climb on appeal.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Working understanding for serious preparation over a few days to two months.

#### Sources of South African Law

Four streams feed South African law today. **Roman-Dutch law** arrived with Jan van Riebeeck's settlement at the Cape in 1652 and remained after the Union of 1910; it still governs property, succession, and delicts. **English common law** came with British rule after 1806 and brought contract doctrine, the law of evidence, and equity. **Customary law** governs marriage, inheritance, and land in many indigenous communities and is constitutionally recognised. The **Constitution of 1996** overrides every other source where conflict arises.

#### Hierarchy and Appeal Pathway

The court pyramid is set by Chapter 8 of the Constitution and the Superior Courts Act 10 of 2013. Cases usually start in a Magistrates' Court (District for minor matters; Regional for more serious criminal matters and civil claims up to R400 000 in most provinces). Serious criminal trials (such as murder) and major civil disputes go to a **High Court**. The High Court is split into divisions — for example, Gauteng, Western Cape, KwaZulu-Natal — and there are specialised High Court seats such as the Free State Division's equality court.

Appeals move from High Court to the **Supreme Court of Appeal (SCA)** in Bloemfontein, which is the highest court of appeal on every matter that is not constitutional. The **Constitutional Court** in Johannesburg sits at the apex with exclusive jurisdiction over constitutional matters under Section 167, and its decisions bind every other forum.

#### Comparison Matrix: Constitutional Court vs Supreme Court of Appeal

| Feature | Constitutional Court | Supreme Court of Appeal (SCA) |
|---------|----------------------|-------------------------------|
| Apex status | Highest court in all matters | Highest court of appeal, except constitutional matters |
| Jurisdiction under the Constitution | Section 167 — exclusive on constitutional issues | Section 168 — non-constitutional appeals |
| Location | Johannesburg | Bloemfontein |
| Binding effect | Binding on all other courts and organs of state | Binding on High Courts and Magistrates' Courts on non-constitutional issues |
| Typical case | Bill of Rights disputes, executive overreach | Contract, delict, criminal appeals |

#### Specialist Courts and Tribunals

Outside the general pyramid sit subject-matter forums. The **Labour Court** and **Labour Appeal Court** handle employment disputes under the Labour Relations Act. The **Land Claims Court** deals with restitution under the Restitution of Land Rights Act. The **Electoral Court** resolves disputes about elections. The **Competition Tribunal** hears matters under the Competition Act 89 of 1998. The **Tax Court** adjudicates tax disputes.

#### Step-by-Step: How a Case Climbs the Ladder

1. Charge is filed or summons issued in a District or Regional Magistrate's Court.
2. On conviction or judgment, the losing party notes an appeal to the relevant High Court division.
3. The High Court hears the appeal or, for serious matters, sits as a court of first instance.
4. A further appeal lies to the Supreme Court of Appeal (leave required).
5. Constitutional issues bypass the SCA and go directly to the Constitutional Court, which may also hear matters on appeal directly from the High Court.

> 📌 **Formula Check:** The rule of precedent flows downward — Constitutional Court decisions bind the SCA, High Courts and Magistrates' Courts; SCA decisions bind High Courts; High Court decisions bind Magistrates' Courts within their division.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** A person is convicted of murder in a Regional Magistrate's Court. The accused wishes to appeal. Identify (a) the correct first appeal court, and (b) the highest court that could finally hear the matter if a constitutional issue is raised at any stage.

#### Solution:
- (a) Murder is a **Schedule 6** offence under the Criminal Law Act 51 of 1977, so the Regional Magistrate's Court does not have jurisdiction to try it. The matter would in fact be moved to the **High Court** for trial. The first appeal from a High Court conviction lies to the **Supreme Court of Appeal** in Bloemfontein.
- (b) If the accused raises a constitutional matter (for example, an alleged violation of the right to a fair trial under Section 35 of the Constitution), the **Constitutional Court** in Johannesburg becomes the final arbiter with binding authority.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Many candidates write "appeal to the Constitutional Court" as the first step. The Constitutional Court is *not* a general appeal court; it only steps in when a constitutional question is properly raised on the record.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Edge cases, the mechanics behind the rule, and what examiners can still ask after the obvious questions are exhausted.

#### Where the Doctrine Breaks Down

The neat pyramid dissolves in two places. First, the **Constitutional Court** can hear a matter directly from the High Court under Section 167(6) when it is in the interests of justice to do so, bypassing the SCA entirely. Second, the doctrine of precedent is not absolute: a High Court may depart from a previous decision of another High Court if convinced it is wrong, although it cannot overrule the SCA or the Constitutional Court.

#### Adjacent Topics Worth Linking

- **Judicial appointments** — the Judicial Service Commission (JSC) interviews candidates and recommends them; the President appoints judges on this advice.
- **Customary law conflicts** — Section 211 binds customary law to the Bill of Rights; courts have tested this in cases involving lobola, polygyny, and customary inheritance.
- **Prosecutorial independence** — the National Prosecuting Authority (NPA) Act 32 of 1998 created the NPA as a single national body, distinct from the courts but central to criminal proceedings.
- **Alternative dispute resolution** — the Magistrates' Courts Act 32 of 1944 allows small claims courts (recovery up to R20 000 in most provinces) which are not part of the main hierarchy.

#### Advanced Traps and Exceptions

1. The SCA is not the highest court — a common trap even among law students.
2. Magistrates' Courts are not one tier but two (District and Regional) with different jurisdictional thresholds.
3. Specialist tribunals such as the Competition Tribunal are courts despite the word "tribunal" appearing in their names.
4. Section 2 supremacy means an Act of Parliament can be struck down — Parliament does not override the Constitution.
5. The Electoral Court is a specialised court under the Electoral Act 73 of 1998, not part of the general civil/criminal ladder.
6. The Land Claims Court is a superior court under the Superior Courts Act 10 of 2013 despite its narrow subject matter.
7. The Labour Court and Labour Appeal Court sit under the Labour Relations Act 66 of 1995 and are independent of the Department of Justice.
8. Recognition of customary law under Section 211 is constitutional, not parliamentary — a trap for those who look for a statute.

#### Two Advanced Practice Prompts

1. **Sequencing question.** Place these five courts in the correct appeal order and explain what happens if the High Court disagrees with an earlier SCA decision on a non-constitutional point: Constitutional Court, SCA, Regional Magistrate's Court, High Court, District Magistrate's Court.
2. **Source-of-law question.** A customary court in Limpopo applies lobola rules that contradict the Constitution's equality clause. Which source prevails, and which constitutional section governs the resolution? Cite the section number.

> 📌 **Exam Strategy:** Three-percent weight means roughly one to two questions. The examiner usually tests (i) ordering of the courts, (ii) which court hears which matter, and (iii) the supremacy of the Constitution. Memorise the ladder and the Section 2 / Section 167 / Section 168 trio — they answer most prompts.
