# Ecosystem

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

An **ecosystem** is a functional unit of biotic components (producers, consumers, decomposers) interacting with abiotic factors (light, temperature, water, soil, gases) through unidirectional energy flow and cyclic nutrient movement. For MDCAT Botany, two quantitative ideas are tested repeatedly: the **Lindeman 10% Law** and the **GPP → NPP** relationship.

- **Energy flow rule:** Only 10% of energy transfers to the next trophic level; 90% is lost as heat via respiration. Eₙ = Eₙ₋₁ × 0.10 (kcal m⁻² yr⁻¹).
- **Productivity rule:** NPP = GPP − R, where R = respiratory loss of producers (g C m⁻² yr⁻¹).
- **Pioneer organisms in primary succession:** Lichens (on bare rock) and mosses follow them.
- **Nitrogen cycle bacteria:** *Nitrosomonas* (NH₃ → NO₂⁻), *Nitrobacter* (NO₂⁻ → NO₃⁻), *Rhizobium* in legume root nodules.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** "**L**ichens **L**ead **P**rimary succession" (LLP) — and for nitrification, remember "**N**itrosomonas **N**itrifies ammonia first, **N**itrobacter **N**itrifies nitrite Next" (NN-NN).

#### Key Terms at a Glance

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Biome | Large regional community (forest, desert, tundra) shaped by climate |
| Ecotone | Transition zone between two adjacent ecosystems |
| Niche | Functional role of a species in its ecosystem |
| Standing crop | Biomass present at a given trophic level at one time |
| Climax community | Stable, mature end-stage of ecological succession |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Structure and Function

The ecosystem has two structural layers. The **abiotic** layer supplies light, temperature, water, soil minerals, and gases. The **biotic** layer is divided into **producers** (photoautotrophs), **consumers** (herbivores, carnivores, omnivores — heterotrophs), and **decomposers** (saprotrophs like fungi and bacteria).

The functional side of an ecosystem runs on two parallel processes. **Energy flow** is unidirectional — it enters as sunlight, is fixed by producers, and exits as heat at every transfer. **Nutrient cycling** (carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus, water) is cyclic — elements move through biotic and abiotic pools repeatedly. This contrast between linear energy and cyclic matter is the single most-tested conceptual axis in MDCAT MCQs.

#### Lindeman's 10% Law and Productivity

Lindeman quantified energy transfer: only about 10% of energy at trophic level *n* − 1 reaches trophic level *n*. The remainder is lost as metabolic heat, undigested matter, and respiratory CO₂.

- Formula: **Eₙ = Eₙ₋₁ × 0.10**, units = kcal m⁻² yr⁻¹, dimensions [M L⁰ T⁻²].
- Standing crop biomass: **B = N × W**, where N = individuals per m², W = mean individual mass in kg; dimensions [M L⁻²].
- **GPP** = total rate of photosynthesis by producers; **R** = producer respiration; **NPP = GPP − R**, units g C m⁻² yr⁻¹, dimensions [M L⁻² T⁻¹].

#### Ecological Pyramids — Comparison Matrix

| Feature | Pyramid of Numbers | Pyramid of Biomass | Pyramid of Energy |
|---|---|---|---|
| What is plotted | Count of individuals | Dry weight (kg m⁻²) | Energy per area per time (kcal m⁻² yr⁻¹) |
| Shape in terrestrial forest | Inverted (few large trees) | Upright | Always upright |
| Shape in aquatic/pond ecosystem | Upright | **Inverted** (phytoplankton biomass < zooplankton at one instant) | Always upright |
| Includes decomposers? | No | No | No |
| Why MDCAT tests it | Confuses students on shape | Aquatic inversion is a classic trap | Reminds the 10% rule visually |

#### Ecological Succession

**Primary succession** begins on sterile substrate (bare rock, lava, newly exposed glacial moraine) where no soil exists. **Lichens** are pioneer species — they secrete acids to weather rock and contribute to initial humus. **Mosses** follow, then herbs, shrubs, and finally a **climax community** (typically forest).

**Secondary succession** starts on a previously vegetated site where soil and seed bank survive disturbance (fire, flood, abandoned farmland). It is faster because soil is already present.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** In a grassland ecosystem, the gross primary productivity (GPP) is 20,000 kcal m⁻² yr⁻¹. Producers respire 5,000 kcal m⁻² yr⁻¹. Assuming Lindeman's 10% law applies strictly, how much energy is available at the secondary consumer (tertiary trophic) level?

#### Solution:

1. Net primary productivity: NPP = GPP − R = 20,000 − 5,000 = **15,000 kcal m⁻² yr⁻¹** (this is energy at producer / first trophic level).
2. Primary consumer (2nd trophic): E₂ = E₁ × 0.10 = 15,000 × 0.10 = 1,500 kcal m⁻² yr⁻¹.
3. Secondary consumer (3rd trophic): E₃ = E₂ × 0.10 = 1,500 × 0.10 = **150 kcal m⁻² yr⁻¹**.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students often multiply 20,000 × (0.10)³ directly, forgetting that GPP includes the 5,000 kcal lost to producer respiration. The energy entering the food chain is **NPP**, not GPP. Always subtract R first.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Boundaries

The **10% law is an empirical average**, not a constant. Real transfer efficiency ranges from 2% (deep ocean) to 24% (some planktonic systems). MDCAT sometimes offers "exactly 10%" as a distractor when the ecosystem in the stem is unusual.

**Inverted biomass pyramid in ponds** is a standing-crop phenomenon, not an energy-flow one. Phytoplankton reproduce and are grazed so rapidly that at any single moment their biomass is lower than that of zooplankton feeding on them. The energy pyramid for the same pond remains upright because energy is measured as flow per unit time, not snapshot biomass.

**Decomposers** act on dead organic matter (detritus) from all trophic levels. They are usually omitted from ecological pyramids because including them collapses the diagram's clarity — but they are essential for nutrient return. Without saprotrophs, biogeochemical cycling halts.

#### Nitrogen Cycle — Bacterial Roles

| Step | Process | Organism | Reaction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fixation | Atmospheric N₂ → NH₃ | *Rhizobium* (legume nodules), *Azotobacter* (free-living soil) | N₂ + 8H⁺ + 8e⁻ + 16 ATP → 2NH₃ + H₂ + 16 ADP + 16 Pi |
| Nitrification I | NH₃ → NO₂⁻ | *Nitrosomonas* | 2NH₃ + 3O₂ → 2NO₂⁻ + 2H⁺ + 2H₂O |
| Nitrification II | NO₂⁻ → NO₃⁻ | *Nitrobacter* | 2NO₂⁻ + O₂ → 2NO₃⁻ |
| Denitrification | NO₃⁻ → N₂ | *Pseudomonas*, *Thiobacillus* | 2NO₃⁻ → 3N₂ |

#### Advanced Traps and Exceptions

1. **Habitat ≠ Niche.** Habitat is the *address* (location); niche is the *profession* (role + resources + interactions). Two species can share habitat but not niche (competitive exclusion).
2. **Rhizobium is legume-specific.** Non-legumes lack nodules; they rely on free-living fixers like *Azotobacter* or *Cyanobacteria* (e.g., *Anabaena* in *Azolla*).
3. **Pyramid of numbers inversion** in a forest occurs because one tree (T₁) supports thousands of insects (T₂), which support fewer birds (T₃) — the shape is upright only at higher levels, inverted at the base.
4. **GPP is always > NPP** for any respiring autotroph. A student who picks NPP > GPP has confused the sign of R.

#### Practice Prompts

1. A pond shows an inverted pyramid of biomass. Predict the shape of its energy pyramid and explain in two sentences why the two pyramids can have opposite shapes.
2. A forest fire burns an established woodland but leaves soil intact. Identify the succession type, list the first three coloniser groups in order, and state the climax community expected under stable rainfall.

> 📌 **Formula Check:** NPP = GPP − R uses [M L⁻² T⁻¹] (mass of carbon per area per time). Lindeman transfer uses [M L⁰ T⁻²] (energy per area per time). Do not mix the two when comparing across trophic levels.
