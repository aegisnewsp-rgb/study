# Mathematics: Coordinate Geometry

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Coordinate Geometry links algebra and geometry by placing points as ordered pairs (x, y) on the **Cartesian plane**, where x is the **abscissa** and y the **ordinate**. The four core computations are: **distance** between two points, **midpoint** of a segment, **slope** of a line, and the **equation of a line** in slope-intercept form.

- **Distance:** d = √[(x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²]
- **Midpoint:** M = ((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2)
- **Slope:** m = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁)
- **Line (slope-intercept):** y = mx + c
- **Circle (standard form):** (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r²

| Pair condition | Slope relationship |
| --- | --- |
| Parallel lines | m₁ = m₂ |
| Perpendicular lines | m₁ × m₂ = −1 |

For HAT-UG, expect short MCQs asking you to find a missing coordinate, slope, or radius from given data.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Lines and Their Equations

A straight line can be written in three equivalent forms: **slope-intercept** y = mx + c, **point-slope** y − y₁ = m(x − x₁), and **standard** ax + by + c = 0. To find **intercepts**, set the other variable to zero: the x-intercept is the x-value when y = 0, and the y-intercept is c in y = mx + c.

#### Circle Equations

The circle with centre (h, k) and radius r has **standard form** (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r². Expanding gives the **general form** x² + y² + Dx + Ey + F = 0, where the centre is (−D/2, −E/2) and the radius is √[(D/2)² + (E/2)² − F]. To convert, complete the square on x and y separately.

#### Section Formula

A point dividing P₁(x₁, y₁) and P₂(x₂, y₂) internally in ratio m : n is P = ((mx₂ + nx₁)/(m + n), (my₂ + ny₁)/(m + n)). Use this when one endpoint is "weighted" m parts and the other n parts.

| Concept | Quick rule |
| --- | --- |
| x-intercept of ax + by + c = 0 | (−c/a, 0) |
| y-intercept of ax + by + c = 0 | (0, −c/b) |
| Tangency (line to circle) | Distance from centre to line = radius |
| Centre from general circle | (−D/2, −E/2) |

#### High-Yield Pointers

- Perpendicular slope condition: m₁ × m₂ = −1, not just reciprocal.
- HAT-UG questions usually present two points and ask for slope, midpoint, or distance — practice without a calculator-friendly setup.
- Converting general form to standard form requires completing the square on x **and** y separately; missing one gives the wrong centre.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Adjacent Connections

The slope formula is undefined when x₂ = x₁ (vertical line, infinite slope). The distance formula reduces to |y₂ − y₁| or |x₂ − x₁| on horizontal and vertical lines respectively. The section formula fails when m + n = 0, meaning the division is external. Coordinate Geometry also feeds directly into **Vectors**, **3-D Geometry**, and **Conic Sections** (parabola, ellipse, hyperbola), which extend the same (x, y) idea.

#### Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Writing the perpendicular condition as m₁ = 1/m₂ (missing the negative sign).
- Misreading centre from x² + y² − 4x + 6y + 9 = 0: here D = −4, E = 6, so centre = (2, −3), not (−2, 3).
- Using the distance formula when the question asks for the midpoint (or vice versa).
- Plugging x = 0 to find the x-intercept (correct) but treating the result as the y-intercept.

#### Worked Example

Find the equation of the circle passing through (1, 2), (3, 4), and (5, 0). Solving the simultaneous equations from x² + y² + Dx + Ey + F = 0 gives D = −6, E = −2, F = 5. Centre = (3, 1), radius = √[(3)² + (1)² − 5] = √5. Equation: (x − 3)² + (y − 1)² = 5.

#### Practice Prompts

1. Points A(−2, 5) and B(4, −1): find the midpoint, slope of AB, and the slope of a line perpendicular to AB.
2. Convert x² + y² − 8x + 10y − 12 = 0 to standard form and state its centre and radius.

| Slope pairs | Correct check |
| --- | --- |
| 2 and −1/2 | Product = −1 ✓ perpendicular |
| 3 and 3 | Equal slopes ✓ parallel |
| 0 and undefined | Horizontal vs vertical ✓ perpendicular |
