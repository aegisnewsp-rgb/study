# Constitution of Pakistan

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your PPSC exam.

The Constitution of Pakistan 1973 is the supreme written law, enforced on **14 August 1973**, passed by the National Assembly on 10 April 1973 and authenticated on 12 April 1973. Pakistan is declared a **federal parliamentary republic** with **Islam as the state religion** (Articles 1 and 2).

- **Three organs:** Legislature (Majlis-e-Shoora), Executive (PM + Cabinet), Judiciary (Supreme Court at apex).
- **National Assembly total membership = 342** seats; Senate = 96; amendment needs **two-thirds of total membership** in each House (Article 239(3)).
- **Fundamental Rights** in Part II (Articles 8–28) are enforceable; any inconsistent law is void.
- **Islamic provisions:** Articles 227–231 forbid laws repugnant to the Quran and Sunnah; the Federal Shariat Court (Article 203C) checks compliance.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook (Memory Hook):** "**10-12-14**" — the three magic dates: Passed **10** April, Authenticated **12** April, Enforced **14** August 1973.

| House | Total seats | 2/3 needed for amendment |
|---|---|---|
| National Assembly | 342 | ≥ 228 |
| Senate | 96 | ≥ 64 |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Working explanation of structure, amendment procedure, and landmark shifts.

#### Sources of the Constitution and Ideology

The 1973 Constitution is the third constitutional document after the 1956 and 1962 versions. Article 1 names the territory "the Islamic Republic of Pakistan". Article 2 declares Islam the state religion. The Preamble states that sovereignty belongs to Allah and is exercised by elected representatives within the limits prescribed by Islam. Article 4 guarantees the inalienable right of every citizen to be treated in accordance with law.

#### Structure: Parts, Schedules and Three Lists

The text is divided into **Articles** (currently over 280), grouped into **Parts**, supported by **Schedules** (seven in number, the Fourth Schedule lists legislative powers).

| Schedule | What it contains (current status) |
|---|---|
| First | Oaths of office for President, PM, Speaker, Judges |
| Second | Election of President, Speaker, PM — rules |
| Third | Oaths and election of Governor, CM, Provincial Ministers |
| Fourth | Federal Legislative List (single list after 18th Amendment) |
| Fifth / Sixth | Joint sitting, Rules of Procedure |
| Seventh | Lists of laws excluded from Federal Shariat Court jurisdiction |

Until the **18th Amendment (2010)**, the Fourth Schedule carried three lists: Federal, Concurrent and Provincial. The 18th Amendment **abolished the Concurrent List** and transferred its subjects to the provinces, leaving residual powers with the federation under Article 142(c).

#### Comparison Matrix — Pakistan's Three Constitutions

| Feature | 1956 | 1962 | 1973 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Republic | Islamic Republic | Republic (Islamic dropped) | Islamic Republic |
| System | Parliamentary | Presidential | Parliamentary |
| Head of State | President (ceremonial) | President (executive) | President (ceremonial after 18th Amend.) |
| Head of Govt | PM | None (President directly) | PM with Cabinet |
| Repealed/Restored | Abrogated 1958 | Replaced 1969 | Restored 1973, ongoing |

#### Worked Resolution — Amendment Math

The question that recurs: *“How many votes are needed in the National Assembly to amend the Constitution?”*

Under **Article 239(3)**, a constitutional amendment bill must be passed by **two-thirds of the *total membership*** of each House separately, then ratified by not less than half the total membership of the provincial Assemblies. Total NA membership = **342**. Two-thirds of 342 = 342 × 2 ÷ 3 = 228.

**Result:** **At least 228 NA members** must vote in favour, plus **at least 64 Senators**, plus ratification by **at least half** of all provincial legislators combined.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students write "two-thirds of members *present and voting*". That phrase applies to ordinary bills under Article 70 — for **constitutional amendments**, Article 239(3) uses **total membership**, not those present.

#### Fundamental Rights and Enforcement

Part II (Articles 8–28) covers equality, freedom of movement, speech, assembly, association, trade, religion; safeguards against double punishment, retrospective crime, and forced labour. **Article 8(1)** makes any law, custom or usage inconsistent with Fundamental Rights **void**. The remedy lies in the High Courts (Article 199) and the Supreme Court (Article 185). Directive Principles (Articles 29–40) are **not enforceable** by courts but guide state policy.

#### Islamic Provisions and Federal Shariat Court

Articles 227–231 require that all existing and future laws conform to the Injunctions of Islam as laid down in the Quran and Sunnah. The Federal Shariat Court (Article 203C) examines whether a law is repugnant to these Injunctions. **Article 203D** bars the Court from questioning the validity of any law relating to the Constitution itself.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Edge cases, post-2010 federalism, and the traps advanced PPSC papers set.

#### Post-18th Amendment Federalism

Before April 2010, the Concurrent List allowed overlapping federal-provincial legislation. Its abolition fundamentally rebalanced Pakistan as a federation: provinces gained legislative space on education, health, police, local government, population planning, criminal procedure and prisons. Yet **Article 142(c)** still vests residual powers in the federation, and the Council of Common Interests (Article 153) was strengthened by the 18th Amendment to adjudicate disputes.

- **18th Amendment (2010):** abolished Concurrent List; curbed Article 58(2)(b) dissolution; renamed NWFP to Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.
- **19th Amendment (2010):** re-inserted a judicially-managed check on Article 58(2)(b) dissolution via an impartial parliamentary committee.
- **25th Amendment (2018):** merged FATA into Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.
- **26th Amendment (2024):** recent constitutional change affecting judicial appointments.

#### Money Bills and Senate Limits

Article 73 defines a Money Bill — it must originate in the National Assembly, and the Senate has only **fourteen days** to recommend amendments, which the NA may accept or reject. The Senate cannot block, withhold or amend a Money Bill substantively. This is a high-yield distinction for PPSC MCQs.

#### Critical Traps in Advanced MCQs

1. **Authentication vs Enforcement:** Passing date (10 Apr) ≠ authentication date (12 Apr) ≠ enforcement date (14 Aug 1973). Confusing these is the most common error.
2. **Federal Shariat Court reach:** Cannot strike down the Constitution, the Muslim Family Laws Ordinance 1961, or laws relating to fiscal/tribal matters (Fifth Schedule exclusions).
3. **Concurrent List:** Many textbooks still print it; state the truth — **abolished in 2010**.
4. **National Assembly seats:** 266 general + 60 women + 10 non-Muslim = 342, not 272.
5. **Article 58(2)(b):** Once allowed the President to dissolve the NA at discretion; effectively neutralised by the 18th and 19th Amendments.
6. **Basic Structure Doctrine:** Adopted by the Supreme Court; an amendment violating the federal or Islamic character may be reviewed, though no amendment has been struck down on this ground.
