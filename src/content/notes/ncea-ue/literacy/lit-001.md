# Demonstrate understanding of ideas and information in written texts (US32403) — NCEA UE Literacy Notes

Unit Standard 32403 (US32403) is the dedicated co-requisite unit standard for the University Entrance literacy reading requirement. It is internally assessed by the school (or by an approved provider) and carries 5 credits at Level 1 or above. Students who achieve US32403 have demonstrated the reading comprehension and analysis skills needed for university-level study, and the 5 credits count toward both the NCEA Level 1 certificate (where applicable) and the UE literacy requirement.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **5 credits**, internally assessed, NZQA moderated.
- **Level 1 or above**, can be used for the NCEA Level 1 certificate AND for UE literacy.
- **Reading focus:** read and understand ideas and information in a range of written texts; analyse how language features and structure convey meaning.
- The "Y" (Yes) column in the UE literacy standards spreadsheet confirms this standard counts toward UE literacy.
- The standard is the dedicated co-requisite reading standard, so it can be relied upon to provide the reading half of the 10-credit UE literacy requirement.

#### Examiner traps

- Treating it as a "free" standard — the marking schedule is rigorous; the standard requires analysis, not just identification.
- Skipping the language features analysis — NZQA markers look for engagement with how the writer achieves their effects.
- Confusing US32403 (reading) with US32405 (writing) — they are separate standards.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### What the standard tests

US32403 tests the ability to:

1. **Read** a range of written texts (the texts are provided as part of the assessment).
2. **Understand** the ideas and information in those texts.
3. **Analyse** how language features and structure convey meaning.

The texts are typically a mix of prose types (e.g., articles, essays, reports, short stories, poems). The student reads them and answers questions that require both literal comprehension (what does the text say?) and inferential comprehension (what does the text imply?).

#### The marking schedule

US32403 uses a four-grade scale (Not Achieved, Achieved, Merit, Excellence). The grade descriptors typically assess:

| Grade | What it requires |
|---|---|
| Achieved | Demonstrate understanding of ideas and information in written texts. |
| Merit | Demonstrate clear understanding of ideas and information; analyse how language features are used to convey meaning. |
| Excellence | Demonstrate perceptive understanding of ideas and information; analyse how language features and structure are used to convey meaning, with developed personal response. |

#### How it fits into UE

UE literacy requires 10 credits at Level 2 or above, with at least 5 reading and 5 writing credits. The dedicated co-requisite unit standards are US32403 (reading, 5 credits) and US32405 (writing, 5 credits) — together they satisfy the full 10-credit UE literacy requirement.

This is the fastest path to UE literacy for students who have not earned enough reading and writing credits through other Level 2 or Level 3 achievement standards.

#### Internal assessment logistics

- The school or training provider delivers the assessment using approved materials.
- Results are submitted to NZQA by the school.
- External moderation by NZQA samples student work to check marking consistency.
- Resubmission is at the provider's discretion, usually once per standard.

#### Preparation strategy

For students preparing for US32403:

1. **Practice reading diverse texts** — articles, essays, reports, stories, poems. The standard tests comprehension across text types.
2. **Practice identifying language features** — metaphor, simile, alliteration, personification, repetition, contrast, juxtaposition, tone, mood.
3. **Practice analysing effects** — not just "the author uses metaphor" but "the metaphor of X evokes Y, suggesting the author wants the reader to feel Z".
4. **Practice extended answers** — Merit and Excellence require paragraphs of developed analysis, not single sentences.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### The Excellence move

The single biggest gap between Merit and Excellence on US32403 is the depth of analysis. Merit identifies and explains language features; Excellence connects them to a developed personal response.

For example, on the question "Analyse how the writer conveys their attitude toward technology":

- Merit: "The writer uses sarcasm ('our wonderful new gadget') to mock the technology."
- Excellence: "The writer's use of sarcasm in 'our wonderful new gadget' creates distance between the writer and the reader's likely enthusiasm, suggesting a critique of unthinking tech adoption. This connects to the writer's broader argument about cultural complacency, which the reader is invited to share by the end of the essay."

The Excellence version shows the same identification but adds a connection to meaning, purpose, and reader response.

#### Connecting to UE readiness

US32403 is one of the dedicated co-requisite standards. The other dedicated co-requisite is US32405 (writing). Together they are the simplest path to UE literacy. For students who prefer a subject-based path, achievement standards in English, History, Geography, Art History, Media Studies, Classical Studies, and Religious Studies can also contribute to UE literacy.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "US32403 is just reading comprehension." No — it is reading comprehension plus analysis of language features.
- "Achieved grade is easy." The Achieved grade requires demonstration of understanding, which is more than just identifying literal content.
- "The texts are random." The texts are selected to span a range of styles, purposes, and audiences; preparation across text types is essential.
- "Internal assessment means easy marking." Internal marking is moderated by NZQA; standards are applied consistently.
