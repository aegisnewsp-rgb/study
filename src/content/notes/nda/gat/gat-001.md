# English Grammar

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

English Grammar in NDA GAT covers the candidate's ability to recognise and apply standard English usage at the Class XII level set by UPSC. The section contributes to the 300-mark Paper B through 50 objective MCQs, and the grammar items are folded into the broader English portion that includes vocabulary and comprehension.

The highest-yield items are Spot-the-Error, Sentence Improvement, and Ordering of Words/Sentences. Mastering subject–verb agreement, tense consistency, articles, prepositions, and active–passive transformation secures most marks.

- **Parts of Speech** — eight classes; each word belongs to exactly one at a time.
- **Subject–Verb Agreement** — singular subject, singular verb; collective and "each/every" stay singular.
- **Tense Backshift** — Direct to Indirect Speech shifts present → past, will → would.
- **Articles** — "a" before consonant sound, "an" before vowel sound, "the" for definite reference.

| Grammar Item | Tested Format |
| --- | --- |
| Spotting Errors | One underlined segment contains the mistake |
| Sentence Improvement | Replace underlined phrase with the most idiomatic option |
| Ordering of Words | Rearrange jumbled words into a meaningful sentence |
| Ordering of Sentences | Rearrange jumbled sentences into a logical paragraph |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Areas Tested by UPSC

NDA Paper B places English Grammar alongside vocabulary and comprehension. The grammar MCQs are written at CBSE Class XII standard, and UPSC sets them so that a candidate who knows the rule and reads carefully can spot the correct option without ambiguity.

#### Parts of Speech and Tenses

Every English word in a sentence functions as one of the eight parts of speech: noun, pronoun, verb, adjective, adverb, preposition, conjunction, interjection. A word shifts class when its role shifts (e.g., "work" as noun vs. verb). Tense consistency matters — if the principal clause is past tense, the subordinate clause also stays in past unless the subordinate clause expresses a universal truth.

#### Subject–Verb Agreement

Singular subjects take singular verbs; plural subjects take plural verbs. Collective nouns (committee, team) take singular verbs when treated as a unit. The pronouns "each", "every", "either", "neither", and "anyone" take singular verbs even when followed by a plural phrase ("Each of the boys is present").

| Rule | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Each/Every + singular verb | Each of the players has arrived |
| Collective noun = singular | The committee has decided |
| Relative pronoun = antecedent | He is one of the boys who are absent |
| Subject with "as well as" = first subject | The captain, as well as the team, is ready |

#### Articles, Prepositions, and Modals

Articles depend on sound, not spelling: "an hour", "a university", "an MBA". Prepositions are governed by fixed collocations: "interested in", "good at", "superior to", "oblivious of" (incorrect; correct is "oblivious to"). Modals ("can", "can", "can", "can", "should", "would", "must", "ought to") carry fixed meanings that examiners exploit in sentence improvement items.

- Use "since" for a point of time (since 2020); use "for" for a period (for five years).
- Use "between" with two items, "among" with more than two.
- Use "made of" when material is visible; "made from" when material has changed form.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Active–Passive and Direct–Indirect Transformations

Active voice: "The captain led the troops." Passive voice: "The troops were led by the captain." The transformation preserves tense, shifts the object to the subject position, and adds a suitable form of "be" plus the past participle. Intransitive verbs (arrive, happen, sleep) have no passive form — a classic trap.

Direct–Indirect conversion requires backshift of tense (present → past, will → would, can → can), change of reporting verb ("say" → "said", "tell" → "told"), and conversion of pronouns ("I" → "he/she", "my" → "his/her", "today" → "that day", "tomorrow" → "the next day", "here" → "there"). Universal truths are exempt from backshift: "The teacher said that the earth revolves around the sun."

#### Edge Cases and Examiner Traps

Spot-the-error questions in many papers hide the mistake inside an idiomatic phrase rather than in the highlighted word. Candidates who skim the underlined segment miss the wrong preposition in "oblivious of", the wrong article in "a historical event", or the wrong verb form in "he lay on the bed" (past of "lie", not "lay" as in "to lay something down").

| Trap | Correct Form |
| --- | --- |
| "An university" | "A university" (consonant sound) |
| "Since five years" | "For five years" |
| "Each of the boys are" | "Each of the boys is" |
| "Discuss about the matter" | "Discuss the matter" |
| "He is more smarter" | "He is smarter" |

#### Practice Prompts

1. Identify the error: "Neither of the candidates (a) / have submitted (b) / their application (c) / before the deadline. (d) / No error (e)."
2. Improve the sentence: "He is one of those persons who never believes in superstitions."

#### Exam Strategy

GAT carries 300 marks and English forms a sizeable chunk alongside General Knowledge and Current Affairs. Allocate roughly 25–30 minutes to the English section in the 2½-hour window. Attempt vocabulary and grammar items first, leave comprehension for the second pass. Reading one editorial daily from *The Hindu* or *Indian Express* builds the instinctive grammar sense NDA questions reward.
