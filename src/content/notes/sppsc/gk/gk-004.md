# Sindh Province - Geography, Culture, Economy

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your SPSC exam.

Sindh is Pakistan's southeastern province, capital **Karachi**, covering about **140,914 km²** with a 350 km Arabian Sea coastline. The **Indus River** dominates the landscape, feeding three major barrages — **Sukkur, Guddu, and Kotri** — that irrigate the plain. The **Kirthar Mountains** bound it west, the **Thar Desert** lies in the east, and the **Indus Delta** hosts Pakistan's largest mangrove forest.

| Quick Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Capital / Largest city | Karachi |
| Area | ~140,914 km² |
| Key river | Indus (only major one) |
| GDP share | ~30–35% of Pakistan's GDP |
| UNESCO site | Mohenjo-daro (Larkana) |

**High-yield exam pointers:** identify the three barrages by district, remember **Mohenjo-daro** is in Sindh (not Harappa), and note **Shah Abdul Latif Bhittai** as Sindh's iconic Sufi poet. The province joined Pakistan in **1947**.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content covering the core geography, history, and economy tested by SPSC.

#### Geographic Boundaries and Climate
Sindh sits in southeastern Pakistan, framed by **Punjab** and **Balochistan** to the north and west, the Indian states of **Rajasthan and Gujarat** to the east, the **Arabian Sea** to the south, and the **Rann of Kutch** to the southeast. The terrain divides into three zones: the western **Kirthar range**, the central **Indus alluvial plain**, and the eastern **Thar/Cholistan Desert**. Summers reach **45–50°C**, winters are mild (10–20°C), and annual rainfall averages a low **100–200 mm**, mostly during the July–September monsoon that mainly affects Tharparkar and Umerkot districts.

#### Irrigation and Water System
The **Indus River** is the only major river flowing through Sindh. Three barrages regulate its water:

| Barrage | District / Location | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Sukkur Barrage | Sukkur | Largest, oldest (1932); irrigates upper Sindh |
| Guddu Barrage | Ghotki | Feeds rice-growing areas |
| Kotri Barrage | Jamshoro | Supplies water to southern districts and Karachi |

#### Historical Timeline
Sindh cradles **Mohenjo-daro** (c. 2500 BCE), a UNESCO-listed Indus Valley Civilization site near Larkana. Arab conquest came under **Muhammad bin Qasim in 711 CE**, followed by local dynasties (Soomra, Samma), the **Kalhora** and then **Talpur Mirs** who ruled until British annexation in 1843. The province joined Pakistan in 1947.

#### Administrative Setup
Sindh has **six divisions** — Karachi, Hyderabad, Sukkur, Larkana, Mirpurkhas, and Shaheed Benazirabad — subdivided into 30+ districts governed by the **Provincial Assembly of Sindh** with a Chief Minister and Governor.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for thorough preparation, edge cases, and practice prompts.

#### Cultural Identity
Sindhi identity hinges on **Sufi traditions** rooted in the poetry of **Shah Abdul Latif Bhittai** (Risalo), **Sachal Sarmast**, and **Shah Noorani**. Annual **urs** festivals at their shrines draw pilgrims from across Pakistan. The visual culture is unmistakable: the geometric block-printed **Ajrak** shawl and the embroidered **Sindhi topi** are worn at cultural and political gatherings. Music forms include the devotional **Kafi** and the ecstatic **Dhamal**.

#### Economy and Industry
Karachi alone hosts the **Pakistan Stock Exchange**, the **State Bank** head office, both major seaports (**Karachi Port and Port Qasim**), and industrial estates such as **SITE** and **Korangi**. Under CPEC, **Special Economic Zones** at Dhabeji and Keti Bandar expand the maritime economy.

| Sector | Sindh's Contribution |
|---|---|
| Agriculture | Top rice and Sindhri-mango producer |
| Energy | Thar Coalfield (~175 bn tonnes lignite); Jhimpir wind corridor |
| Trade | ~60% of Pakistan's port throughput via Karachi |
| Industry | Oil refining, textiles, cement, automotive assembly |

> **SPSC Trap:** Do not confuse the **Thar** (Sindh) with the Indian Thar or with **Cholistan** (Punjab). Tharparkar is also Pakistan's only district bordering India on three sides.

#### Common Mistakes to Avoid
- Naming **Lahore or Islamabad** as Sindh's capital — the answer is **Karachi**.
- Stating that the **Chenab, Ravi or Jhelum** flow through Sindh — only the **Indus** does.
- Confusing **Mohenjo-daro (Sindh)** with **Harappa (Punjab)** — both belong to the same civilization but are distinct sites.

#### Practice Prompts
1. List the three barrages on the Indus in Sindh with their districts and the year the Sukkur Barrage became operational.
2. Identify the largest coalfield in Sindh, the wind-power corridor district, and the UNESCO heritage site near Thatta besides Mohenjo-daro.
