# IV. Health and Diseases — HKDSE Biology Notes

This topic covers Topic IV of the Compulsory Part of HKDSE Biology. It addresses personal health (lifestyle choices, immunity, nutrition); diseases (pathogens, communicable and non-communicable diseases, prevention and treatment); and body defence mechanisms (non-specific and specific immunity, antibodies, vaccination, immunological memory).

> Verify the live HKDSE Biology Assessment Framework on https://www.hkeaa.edu.hk/ before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Personal health**: lifestyle choices (diet, exercise, sleep, smoking, alcohol, stress); nutrition (macronutrients: carbohydrates, proteins, lipids; micronutrients: vitamins, minerals); immunity (non-specific and specific).
- **Diseases**: pathogens (bacteria, viruses, fungi, protozoa, helminths); communicable diseases (transmitted person-to-person, e.g. influenza, COVID-19, tuberculosis); non-communicable diseases (not transmitted, e.g. cardiovascular disease, cancer, diabetes).
- **Body defence mechanisms**: non-specific (physical barriers — skin, mucous membranes; chemical — lysozyme in tears, stomach acid; cellular — phagocytes); specific (lymphocytes — B cells produce antibodies, T cells kill infected cells); antibodies are proteins produced by plasma cells that bind to specific antigens; vaccination induces active immunity through exposure to a harmless form of the pathogen.

#### Examiner traps

- Treating antibiotics as effective against viruses — antibiotics work against bacteria, not viruses.
- Confusing active and passive immunity — active immunity is produced by the body's own response; passive immunity is conferred by transferring antibodies.
- Forgetting that antibodies are specific to one antigen.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Personal health

**Lifestyle choices**:

- **Diet**: balanced diet with appropriate macronutrient and micronutrient intake; reduced saturated fat, salt and added sugar; increased dietary fibre, fruit and vegetables.
- **Exercise**: regular moderate-intensity aerobic exercise; at least 150 minutes per week for adults (WHO recommendation).
- **Sleep**: 7-9 hours for adults; less than 6 hours is associated with increased risk of cardiometabolic disease.
- **Smoking**: tobacco smoking is a leading cause of preventable disease — cardiovascular disease, lung cancer, COPD.
- **Alcohol**: excessive consumption damages the liver, brain and cardiovascular system.
- **Stress**: chronic stress contributes to anxiety, depression, cardiovascular disease.

**Nutrition**:

- **Macronutrients**: carbohydrates (main energy source, 4 kcal/g); proteins (building blocks, 4 kcal/g, essential amino acids); lipids (energy-dense, 9 kcal/g, essential fatty acids).
- **Micronutrients**: vitamins (e.g. vitamin C — collagen synthesis, vitamin D — calcium absorption); minerals (e.g. iron — haemoglobin, calcium — bone health).
- **Water**: the most important nutrient; 2-3 litres per day for adults.

#### Diseases

**Pathogens**:

- **Bacteria**: single-celled prokaryotes; cause tuberculosis, cholera, bacterial pneumonia, syphilis.
- **Viruses**: non-cellular particles requiring a host cell; cause influenza, COVID-19, HIV/AIDS, measles.
- **Fungi**: cause athlete's foot, candidiasis, histoplasmosis.
- **Protozoa**: single-celled eukaryotes; cause malaria, amoebic dysentery.
- **Helminths**: parasitic worms; cause schistosomiasis, hookworm disease.

**Communicable diseases**: transmitted person-to-person (direct contact, airborne, waterborne, vector-borne, bloodborne). Prevention: vaccination, hygiene, sanitation, vector control, treatment of infected individuals.

**Non-communicable diseases**:

- **Cardiovascular disease**: coronary artery disease, stroke. Risk factors: hypertension, hyperlipidaemia, smoking, diabetes, obesity, family history.
- **Cancer**: uncontrolled cell division. Risk factors: genetic predisposition, smoking, alcohol, UV radiation, certain viral infections (HPV, hepatitis B).
- **Diabetes**: type 1 (autoimmune destruction of β-cells), type 2 (insulin resistance). Risk factors for type 2: obesity, sedentary lifestyle, family history.

#### Body defence mechanisms

**Non-specific (innate) immunity**:

- **Physical barriers**: skin, mucous membranes, cilia in respiratory tract.
- **Chemical barriers**: lysozyme in tears, stomach acid, antimicrobial peptides.
- **Cellular barriers**: phagocytes (neutrophils, macrophages) engulf and destroy pathogens.
- **Inflammation**: redness, swelling, heat, pain — increased blood flow and immune cell recruitment.
- **Fever**: raised body temperature inhibits pathogen growth and enhances immune function.

**Specific (adaptive) immunity**:

- **B lymphocytes**: mature in the bone marrow; produce antibodies that bind to specific antigens.
- **T lymphocytes**: mature in the thymus; helper T cells coordinate the immune response; cytotoxic T cells kill infected cells.
- **Antibodies**: immunoglobulin proteins (IgG, IgM, IgA, IgD, IgE); bind to antigens and neutralise pathogens, opsonise them for phagocytosis, or activate the complement system.
- **Memory cells**: long-lived B and T cells that respond rapidly upon re-exposure to the same antigen.

**Active immunity**: produced by the body's own response to an antigen (infection or vaccination). Long-lasting.

**Passive immunity**: conferred by transferring pre-formed antibodies (e.g. maternal antibodies across the placenta, antivenom, antibody therapy). Short-lived.

**Vaccination**: introduction of a harmless form of a pathogen (live attenuated, killed, subunit, mRNA, viral vector) to induce active immunity without causing disease. Herd immunity occurs when a sufficient proportion of the population is immune, reducing transmission.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-topic links

- **I. Cells and Molecules of Life**: cellular immunity depends on cell-cell recognition (MHC).
- **II. Genetics and Evolution**: pathogen evolution (e.g. influenza antigenic drift and shift) drives vaccine development.
- **III. Organisms and Environment**: epidemiology is applied population biology.

#### Exam technique

- **For disease questions**: identify the pathogen type, the mode of transmission, the prevention strategies, and the treatment options.
- **For immunity questions**: distinguish non-specific from specific immunity; name the cell types and their functions.
- **For vaccination questions**: explain how vaccination induces active immunity and memory.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Antibiotics work against viruses." No — antibiotics work against bacteria. Antivirals are needed for viral infections.
- "The common cold is caused by cold weather." The common cold is caused by rhinoviruses and other respiratory viruses; cold weather may increase transmission by driving people indoors.
- "Once vaccinated, you can never get the disease." Vaccines reduce the risk of severe disease but do not always prevent infection entirely (e.g. breakthrough COVID-19 infections in vaccinated individuals).
