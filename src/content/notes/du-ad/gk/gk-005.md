# Important Days and International Observances (Bangladesh & World)

## Syllabus Breakdown for Dhaka University Admission

Dhaka University general knowledge questions on national and international days appear every single year. In the Kha Unit (now Arts, Law and Social Sciences) and Ga Unit (Business Studies), candidates face two to four direct multiple-choice questions on this single topic. With a 0.25 penalty for every wrong answer, memorizing the exact date, the sponsoring agency, and the historical event is one of the quickest ways to secure marks.

Over the last ten admission cycles, the questions follow four predictable patterns:
1. Exact calendar days paired with their Bengali calendar dates (such as Pahela Boishakh on 14 April or Pahela Falgun on 14 February).
2. The specific events of 1971 that gave rise to our national days, especially Operation Searchlight on 25 March versus the Declaration of Independence on 26 March.
3. United Nations days, including the exact agency responsible for the declaration (UNESCO, WHO, ILO, or UNEP).
4. The timeline of International Mother Language Day. Examiners love testing whether you know the difference between 1952 (the language movement), 1999 (UNESCO declaration in Paris), and 2000 (first worldwide celebration).

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before exam morning.

If your exam is tomorrow, prioritize these eight Bangladesh national days and six high-frequency UN observances. In our mock tests, students often lose marks by confusing consecutive days.

#### Essential Bangladesh National Days

- **21 February**: Shaheed Dibas and International Mother Language Day. Salam, Barkat, Rafiq, Jabbar, and Shafiur died when police fired on language protesters near Dhaka Medical College in 1952. UNESCO declared the global day on 17 November 1999.
- **25 March**: National Genocide Day (Jatiya Gonohattya Dibas). The Pakistan army launched Operation Searchlight on the night of 25 March 1971. Parliament formally recognized this day on 11 March 2017.
- **26 March**: Independence Day (Swadhinata Dibas). Bangabandhu declared independence early on 26 March 1971 right before his arrest at Dhanmondi 32.
- **17 April**: Mujibnagar Day. The provisional government of Bangladesh took its official oath at Baidyanathtala (Mujibnagar) in Meherpur in 1971.
- **15 August**: National Mourning Day (Jatiya Shok Dibas). Bangabandhu Sheikh Mujibur Rahman was assassinated with his family members in 1975.
- **14 December**: Martyred Intellectuals Day (Shaheed Buddhijibi Dibas). Pakistani forces and collaborators killed top professors, physicians, and journalists right before the war ended.
- **16 December**: Victory Day (Bijoy Dibas). General Niazi surrendered 93,000 Pakistani soldiers to General Aurora at the Ramna Race Course in Dhaka in 1971.

## Bangladesh National Days and Historical Timeline

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Detailed review for regular study sessions across two weeks to two months.

When preparing for Dhaka University, you cannot treat national days as isolated calendar dates. The admission committee expects you to connect each day to the constitutional history and political struggle of Bangladesh.

#### The Three Milestones of 21 February

Do not confuse the three historical years associated with 21 February:
- **21 February 1952**: Students broke Section 144 around Dhaka University campus and Dhaka Medical College. The police firing martyred Abul Barkat, Abdul Jabbar, Rafiquddin Ahmed, Abdus Salam, and schoolboy Shafiur Rahman.
- **17 November 1999**: At the 30th General Conference of UNESCO in Paris, member states unanimously approved Bangladesh's proposal. Expatriates Rafiqul Islam and Abdus Salam in Canada first drafted the idea, which the Bangladesh government formally submitted.
- **21 February 2000**: UNESCO celebrated International Mother Language Day across all member nations for the first time.

#### Cultural Observances on the Bengali Calendar

- **Pahela Boishakh (14 April)**: The first day of the Bengali new year. Emperor Akbar introduced the Bengali solar calendar in the sixteenth century for tax collection. The Mangal Shobhajatra procession, started by the Faculty of Fine Arts at Dhaka University, was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2016.
- **Pahela Falgun (14 February)**: The first day of spring (Boshonto). Under the revised calendar from the Bangla Academy, Pahela Falgun falls on 14 February.
- **Rokeya Day (9 December)**: Marks the birth (1880) and death (1932) of Begum Rokeya Sakhawat Hossain. She founded the Sakhawat Memorial Girls' School and wrote *Sultana's Dream*. The government awards the Begum Rokeya Padak on this day.
- **National Constitution Day (4 November)**: The Constituent Assembly of Bangladesh passed the constitution on 4 November 1972. It took effect on 16 December 1972.

## International UN Observances and Global Themes

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive reference for candidates competing for top ranks in the admission merit list.

Dhaka University questions often ask which specific agency administers an international day. Below is a breakdown of the specialized agencies and the days they direct:

- **United Nations General Assembly (UNGA)**: Declares worldwide days such as Human Rights Day (10 December), Earth Day (22 April), and International Peace Day (21 September).
- **UNESCO**: Handles education, science, culture, and free expression. Directs World Press Freedom Day (3 May), International Literacy Day (8 September), and World Teachers' Day (5 October).
- **World Health Organization (WHO)**: Coordinates public health days like World Health Day (7 April), World No Tobacco Day (31 May), and World AIDS Day (1 December).
- **International Labour Organization (ILO)**: Protects worker rights on International Workers' Day (1 May).
- **UNEP**: Directs World Environment Day (5 June) and International Ozone Day (16 September).

#### Common Traps in Admission Questions

Examiners design multiple-choice questions to exploit easy memory lapses. Keep these distinctions sharp:

1. **25 March versus 26 March**: 25 March is Genocide Day commemorating Operation Searchlight. 26 March is Independence Day celebrating the proclamation of independence.
2. **10 April versus 17 April**: On 10 April 1971, the Proclamation of Independence was formally adopted. On 17 April 1971, the cabinet members took their oath of office at Baidyanathtala.
3. **4 November versus 16 December**: 4 November 1972 is Constitution Day when the Constituent Assembly passed the document. 16 December 1972 is the date the constitution took practical effect.
4. **21 February 1952 versus 17 November 1999**: The student martyrs died on 21 February 1952 in Dhaka. UNESCO adopted the day on 17 November 1999 in Paris.

## Dhaka University Past Question Practice

Test your memory with these four questions from past admission exams:

#### Practice Question 1
**Question:** In which year did UNESCO proclaim 21 February as International Mother Language Day, and when was it first observed globally?
- (A) 1952 and 1953
- (B) 1971 and 1972
- (C) 1999 and 2000
- (D) 2000 and 2001
*Answer:* **(C)**. The 30th General Conference of UNESCO in Paris declared it on 17 November 1999. The world first observed it on 21 February 2000.

#### Practice Question 2
**Question:** On which date did the Bangladesh Parliament formally pass the resolution declaring 25 March as National Genocide Day?
- (A) 16 December 1971
- (B) 26 March 1972
- (C) 11 March 2017
- (D) 15 August 1975
*Answer:* **(C)**. Parliament unanimously passed the resolution on 11 March 2017 to mark Operation Searchlight as National Genocide Day.

#### Practice Question 3
**Question:** Which United Nations agency leads the observance of World Press Freedom Day on 3 May?
- (A) UNDP
- (B) UNESCO
- (C) UNICEF
- (D) UNHCR
*Answer:* **(B)**. UNESCO coordinates World Press Freedom Day and awards the Guillermo Cano Prize every year on 3 May.

#### Practice Question 4
**Question:** In which year was the Mangal Shobhajatra of Dhaka University added to UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage list?
- (A) 1999
- (B) 2008
- (C) 2016
- (D) 2020
*Answer:* **(C)**. UNESCO inscribed the Mangal Shobhajatra on 30 November 2016 during its eleventh session in Addis Ababa.
