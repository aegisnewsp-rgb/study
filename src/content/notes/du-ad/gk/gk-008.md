# Famous Personalities of Bangladesh and South Asia

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

> Scan the 60-second map of the personalities the DU Admission GK paper pulls from most often.

The single most-tested fact: **Bangabandhu Sheikh Mujibur Rahman** is the Father of the Nation of Bangladesh, while his daughter **Sheikh Hasina** is the longest-serving Prime Minister — examiners routinely swap the two in options.

| Personality | Field | Defining Tag (Memory Hook) |
|---|---|---|
| Sheikh Mujibur Rahman | Politics (Bangladesh) | Father of the Nation, read the 7 March 1971 speech |
| Kazi Nazrul Islam | Literature (Bangladesh) | National Poet, rebel poet, "Bidrohi Kabi" |
| Rabindranath Tagore | Literature (India) | Author of both "Amar Shonar Bangla" and "Jana Gana Mana" |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook — "MINT-P"**: **M**ujib (Father), **I**qbal (Pakistan idea), **N**azrul (B'desh poet), **T**agore (anthem author) — the four names that show up in nearly every MCQ set.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

> The working layer: match each name to field, nation, and signature award before sitting the paper.

#### Bangladesh: The Core Five

The DU paper treats five Bangladeshi figures as compulsory recall.

- **Sheikh Mujibur Rahman** — leader of the Awami League, architect of the Six Point Movement, proclaimed independence on 26 March 1971, assassinated 15 August 1975. Title: *Jatir Pita* (Father of the Nation).
- **Sheikh Hasina** — daughter of Mujib, four-time PM, Awami League president. Do not call her the founder.
- **Kazi Nazrul Islam** — declared Bangladesh's National Poet in 1972. Wrote "Bidrohi" (The Rebel), "Agni-Veena". Died 1976.
- **Ziaur Rahman** — sector commander in 1971, later President (1977–1981), founded the Bangladesh Nationalist Party (BNP).
- **Muhammad Yunus** — awarded the 2006 Nobel Peace Prize jointly with Grameen Bank; microcredit pioneer.

#### Subcontinental Figures That Cross Over

These names appear even though the candidate sits the Dhaka University test.

- **Rabindranath Tagore** (1861–1941) — first non-European Nobel Literature laureate (1913), wrote both national anthems.
- **Allama Iqbal** (1877–1938) — philosopher-poet, *Sare Jahan Se Achha*, conceptualised the idea of a separate Muslim state in the 1930 Allahabad Address.
- **Mahatma Gandhi** — Non-Cooperation, Salt March (1930), Quit India (1942); assassinated 1948.
- **C. V. Raman** — 1930 Nobel in Physics for the Raman Effect.
- **A. P. J. Abdul Kalam** — India's 11th President (2002–2007), DRDO/ISRO scientist, "Missile Man of India".
- **Mother Teresa** — born in Skopje, worked in Kolkata; 1979 Nobel Peace Prize.
- **Amartya Sen** — 1998 Sveriges Riksbank Prize in Economic Sciences; born in Santiniketan, West Bengal (Indian citizen).
- **Mahatma Gandhi, Jawaharlal Nehru, Liaquat Ali Khan** — first PM of India and first PM of Pakistan respectively; the latter is often mis-tagged as a Bengali leader.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question.** The author of "Jana Gana Mana" and the National Poet of Bangladesh are respectively:

(A) Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay and Rabindranath Tagore
(B) Rabindranath Tagore and Kazi Nazrul Islam
(C) Rabindranath Tagore and Rabindranath Tagore
(D) Kazi Nazrul Islam and Rabindranath Tagore

**Resolution.** "Jana Gana Mana" was composed by Rabindranath Tagore in 1911 and adopted as India's anthem in 1950. The title of National Poet of Bangladesh was conferred on Kazi Nazrul Islam in 1972, in part to honour him with a national designation paralleling Tagore's status in India. Therefore the correct answer is **(B)**.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Option (C) tempts students who know Tagore's anthem connection and assume he also holds the Bangladeshi poet title. Nazrul is specifically chosen as Bangladesh's *national poet* to balance Tagore, whose cultural footprint already dominates West Bengal and India.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

> The mastery layer: chronological placement, regional extensions, and the questions examiners write when they want to separate top scorers.

#### Chronological Anchoring

Tie each personality to the era the paper uses as a clue.

1. **Pre-1857 (Colonial Bengal)**: Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay, Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar.
2. **1857–1905**: Sri Ramakrishna, Swami Vivekananda, Rabindranath Tagore (b. 1861), Allama Iqbal (b. 1877).
3. **1906–1946 (partition build-up)**: Mahatma Gandhi, Jawaharlal Nehru, Muhammad Ali Jinnah, Liaquat Ali Khan, Subhas Chandra Bose.
4. **1947 (Partition)**: Jinnah becomes Governor-General of Pakistan, Nehru becomes PM of India.
5. **1947–1970 (Pakistan phase)**: Sheikh Mujibur Rahman rises through Awami League, Allama Iqbal's 1930 address retroactively cited.
6. **1971 (Liberation War)**: Mujib declares independence, Ziaur Rahman announces it on behalf of Mujib from Kalurghat radio.
7. **1972–present (Bangladesh)**: Sheikh Hasina, Muhammad Yunus (2006), Zia (assassinated 1981), Khaleda Zia.
8. **Modern India/Sri Lanka/Nepal**: Abdul Kalam, Amartya Sen, Mother Teresa, Aung San Suu Kyi (Myanmar — included in wider South Asia lists), Mahathir Mohamad (Malaysia, sometimes grouped).

#### Edge Cases the Paper Targets

- **Authors of national anthems** is a recurring question type. "Amar Shonar Bangla" (Tagore) became Bangladesh's anthem; "Jana Gana Mana" (Tagore) is India's. Sri Lanka's anthem is in Sinhala, Nepal's was "Sayaun Thunga Phulka", Pakistan's uses lyrics by Hafeez Jalandhari set to Rabindra Sangeet melody.
- **Nobel discipline split**: Peace (Yunus 2006, Mother Teresa 1979, Gandhi 0 — nominated but never awarded), Literature (Tagore 1913, V. S. Naipaul 2001, Han Kang 2024 — note Naipaul is Trinidad-born of Indian heritage), Physics (C. V. Raman 1930), Economics (Amartya Sen 1998).
- **SAARC membership vs personality list**: Afghanistan, Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Maldives, Nepal, Pakistan, Sri Lanka — the paper may test whether a candidate is aware that a particular figure belongs to one of the smaller member states (e.g., a Maldivian or Bhutanese name).
- **Field misclassification**: Iqbal is classified as philosopher-poet first, politician second; Kalam is classified as aerospace engineer + head of state, not "pure scientist"; Amartya Sen is a welfare economist, not a physicist.

#### Common Mistakes Inventory

1. Calling Allama Iqbal the "founder" of Pakistan — he died in 1938, nine years before the state was created.
2. Attributing the Bangladesh National Anthem to Nazrul — it is Tagore's "Amar Shonar Bangla", kept in 1972.
3. Placing Amartya Sen as Bangladeshi — he holds Indian citizenship and was born in Santiniketan.
4. Mixing Ziaur Rahman (BNP founder, President) with Khaleda Zia (BNP leader, his widow, two-time PM).
5. Saying Sheikh Hasina "won independence" — that role belongs to Mujib and the 1971 fighters.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. "He was assassinated on 15 August 1975, was a Bengali lawyer, and is the Father of the Nation of Bangladesh." Identify and state one movement he led. *(Answer: Sheikh Mujibur Rahman; the Six Point Movement of 1966.)*
2. "Born in Skopje, she worked among the poor of Kolkata, and received the 1979 Nobel Peace Prize." Name her and the religious order she founded in 1950. *(Answer: Mother Teresa; the Missionaries of Charity.)*
