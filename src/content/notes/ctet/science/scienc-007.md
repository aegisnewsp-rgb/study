# Light, Sound, and Heat

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your CTET Science paper.

The CTET Paper-II Science unit on **Light, Sound, and Heat** bundles three physical phenomena that Class VI–VIII students must reason about: optics (mirrors, lenses, the human eye), acoustics (production, propagation, echo, noise), and thermometry/heat transfer. Light and sound are both wave phenomena, but **light is transverse and travels through vacuum**, while **sound is longitudinal and needs a medium**. Heat is energy in transit (joules), distinct from **temperature** (kelvin), which measures hotness.

| Concept | Key formula / fact | SI unit |
|---|---|---|
| Speed of a wave | v = f × λ | m s⁻¹ |
| Time period | T = 1 / f | s |
| Echo minimum distance | d = v × t / 2 ≈ 17.2 m at 20 °C | m |
| Linear expansion | ΔL = α L₀ ΔT | m |
| Heat (sensible) | Q = m c ΔT | joule (J) |
| Heat (phase change) | Q = m L | joule (J) |
| °C → K | T(K) = T(°C) + 273.15 | kelvin |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"CHIEF"** for heat transfer modes — **C**onduction (solids, contact), **C**onvection (fluids, bulk motion), **R**adiation (vacuum too, EM waves). And for eye defects: **"Myopia = Minus lens"** (concave/diverging corrects short-sight) — the image falls **in front** of the retina, so push it back using a diverging lens.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months before the exam.

#### Core Mechanisms: Light, Sound, Heat

**Light** travels in straight lines at 3 × 10⁸ m/s in vacuum and obeys two key laws at an interface. Reflection: angle of incidence equals angle of reflection, image distance = object distance in a plane mirror, with **lateral inversion** (left–right swap). Refraction: light bends **towards the normal** entering a denser medium and obeys Snell's law (n₁ sin i = n₂ sin r). Mirrors and lenses converge or diverge rays depending on shape.

**Sound** is a longitudinal mechanical wave: particles oscillate parallel to propagation, producing alternating **compressions** and **rarefactions**. It cannot cross vacuum because it needs a material medium. Three auditory attributes map directly to wave parameters: **pitch ↔ frequency**, **loudness ↔ amplitude**, **timbre/quality ↔ waveform shape**.

**Heat** flows from higher to lower temperature. **Temperature** measures degree of hotness. SI unit of temperature is the **kelvin (K)**, with absolute zero at 0 K = −273.15 °C. Heat is energy, measured in joules.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix

| Easily confused pair | Distinction | Memory cue |
|---|---|---|
| Myopia vs Hypermetropia | Myopia = image forms **before** retina (eyeball too long); corrected by **concave** lens. Hypermetropia = image forms **behind** retina (eyeball too short); corrected by **convex** lens. | "My = Minus" |
| Convex mirror vs Concave mirror | Convex always forms **virtual, erect, diminished** image; wider field of view (rear-view mirrors). Concave forms real inverted image for objects beyond F, virtual erect magnified image for objects between P and F. | Convex = car mirror = small image |
| Conduction vs Convection vs Radiation | Conduction = molecule-to-molecule vibration, no bulk motion (solids). Convection = bulk fluid motion carrying heat (liquids/gases only). Radiation = EM waves, needs no medium. | "C-Fluid, R-Empty" |
| Heat vs Temperature | Heat = energy transferred (J). Temperature = degree of hotness (K). Two objects can share temperature but receive different amounts of heat because of mass and specific heat capacity. | "Heat is joules; temp is kelvin" |
| Echo vs Reverberation | Echo = distinct repeat, requires ≥ 0.1 s gap (≥ 17.2 m path difference at 20 °C). Reverberation = prolonged sound from many overlapping reflections in enclosed spaces. | Echo = one clear repeat |
| Clinical vs Laboratory thermometer | Clinical: 35–42 °C range, has **constriction** to retain reading. Laboratory: −10 °C to 110 °C, no constriction. | Clinical has a kink |
| Latent heat (fusion) vs (vaporisation) | Fusion: solid → liquid, L_f of ice = 3.34 × 10⁵ J/kg. Vaporisation: liquid → gas, L_v of water = 2.26 × 10⁶ J/kg. Both occur at constant temperature. | "Fusion melts; Vaporisation boils" |

#### Key Principles for CTET MCQs

1. **Lateral inversion** in a plane mirror flips left–right but not top–bottom — text "AMBULANCE" is printed reversed so it reads correctly in a driver's mirror.
2. **Echo distance** uses v × t / 2 because sound covers the distance twice (there and back).
3. **Dark, dull surfaces** absorb and emit heat radiation best; **shiny, polished** surfaces reflect radiation and are poor emitters — hence shiny cooking pots and light-coloured summer clothing.
4. **Anomalous expansion of water**: density is maximum at 4 °C; water expands on cooling from 4 °C to 0 °C, so ice floats and aquatic life survives under frozen surfaces.
5. **Noise above ~80 dB** is harmful; prolonged exposure damages hair cells of the cochlea.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** A student stands 18 m away from a wall and claps. The speed of sound at room temperature is 344 m/s. Will the student hear a distinct echo? (Take the minimum time gap for distinct echo perception as 0.1 s.)

#### Solution:
- Total path of sound = 2 × 18 m = 36 m.
- Time taken: t = total distance / speed = 36 / 344 ≈ 0.1047 s.
- Compare with minimum perceptible gap = 0.1 s.
- Since 0.1047 s > 0.1 s, the reflected sound arrives as a **distinct echo**.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students often compute t as 18 / 344 = 0.0523 s, forgetting the **factor of 2** for the return trip. Use distance = v × t / 2 → t = 2d / v, never t = d / v, when checking echo conditions.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Boundary Conditions

Some "rules of thumb" in this unit break under specific physical limits. CTET rewards teachers who can name those exceptions precisely.

| Edge case | What happens | Why it matters for CTET |
|---|---|---|
| Water between 0 °C and 4 °C | Water **expands on cooling** instead of contracting. Density peaks at 4 °C. | Aquatic life survives under ice; pipes burst in cold climates. |
| Sound in solids vs air | Sound travels **fastest in solids** (~5000 m/s in steel) and **slowest in gases** (~344 m/s in air at 20 °C). | Distractor option: "Sound travels fastest in gases" — false. |
| Convex lens with object between F and optical centre | Image is **virtual, erect, magnified** (used as a magnifying glass). | Students wrongly claim all convex-lens images are real. |
| Angle of incidence = 0° (normal incidence) | Refracted ray is **undeviated**; Snell's law still holds (sin 0 = 0). | Tests whether students grasp that "no bending" is a special case of refraction, not its absence. |
| Bimetallic strip heating | Two metals with different α (linear expansion coefficient) bend towards the metal with **lower α**. | Foundation of thermostats; tests α ≠ α' understanding. |
| Reading a clinical thermometer | Must be read **without removing** from the mouth/shown steady; the constriction prevents mercury return. | Misuse damages the constriction and ruins the instrument. |

#### Common Examiner Traps to Avoid

1. Confusing **incident ray** (incoming) with **reflected ray** (bouncing off); in refraction diagrams the ray entering the second medium is the **refracted ray**, not the reflected one.
2. Stating that **heat rises** — heat does not rise; the **hot fluid** rises because of decreased density (convection currents).
3. Believing **white clothes absorb heat** — white surfaces reflect most visible and infrared radiation; they keep us cooler in summer.
4. Treating **temperature and heat as synonyms** in numerical questions — different units (K vs J), different physical meaning.
5. Mixing up **pitch and loudness** — a baby's cry is high-pitched *and* loud; pitch is not loudness.
6. Writing that **sound needs a medium to reflect** — echo requires a reflecting *surface*, but sound itself only needs a medium to **propagate**, not specifically to reflect.
7. Assuming **all metals expand equally** — α for brass ≈ 19 × 10⁻⁶ /°C, for invar ≈ 0.9 × 10⁻⁶ /°C, a 20× difference used in precision instruments.
8. Forgetting the **273** in °C ↔ K conversion — adding is correct, subtracting is the trap.

#### Two Advanced Practice Prompts

1. **Numeric drill:** A 2 kg iron block (specific heat capacity 450 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹) cools from 80 °C to 30 °C. How much heat is released? (Answer: Q = 2 × 450 × 50 = 45 000 J = 45 kJ.) Then check the dimensional formula: [M][L²T⁻²][θ⁻¹] × K → [M L² T⁻²] ✓.
2. **Conceptual reasoning:** Why does a wooden handle on a frying spoon stay cooler than the metal blade when the blade is on a hot stove? (Answer: Wood is a poor conductor; heat energy travels slowly through it by conduction, while metal conducts heat rapidly. This also explains why metals feel colder than wood at room temperature — they conduct heat away from your hand faster, not because they are at a lower temperature.)

#### Exam-Specific Strategy for CTET

CTET Paper-II (Classes VI–VIII) Science tests this integrated unit through **1–3 MCQs** per paper, usually conceptual rather than computational. Typical question stems ask: "Which lens corrects which defect?", "Why is the clinical thermometer not used for boiling water?", "A man hears an echo after 0.2 s — what is the distance to the wall?", "Why are cooking vessels copper-bottomed but blackened outside?" Practise the formula sheet above cold, since calculators are not allowed and quick recall of v = f λ, ΔL = α L₀ ΔT, and Q = m c ΔT saves precious seconds. Expect at least one **assertion–reason** or **case-based** question linking two of the three sub-topics — for instance, connecting dark-coloured winter clothing (radiation absorption) with anomalous expansion of water (density at 4 °C).
