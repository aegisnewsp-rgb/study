# Environmental Pathology

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

> One-line anchor for the day before the exam: every environmental disease is an **exposure → agent → organ lesion → cancer (sometimes)** triad, and INI CET rewards you for nailing the middle two.

**Core definition.** Environmental Pathology studies structural and functional injury caused by **physical, chemical, and biological agents** encountered in ambient, domestic, and occupational settings — air pollutants, heavy metals, pesticides, tobacco, alcohol, ionizing radiation, and climatic extremes.

#### Must-know classification of agents.

| Class | Examples | Principal lesion |
|---|---|---|
| **Physical** | Ionizing radiation, heat, cold, noise, mechanical trauma | DNA DSBs, burns, HAPE, decompression sickness |
| **Chemical — metals** | Pb, Hg, Cd, As | Encephalopathy (Pb, children), Minamata (Hg), Itai-itai (Cd), arsenicosis |
| **Chemical — air pollutants** | PM2.5, PM10, SO2, NO2, O3, CO | COPD, lung cancer, bronchoconstriction, functional hypoxia |
| **Chemical — organics** | Tobacco smoke, alcohol, pesticides (OPs) | Carcinogenesis; fatty liver → cirrhosis; AChE inhibition |
| **Biological** | Moulds, pollens, microbial toxins | Hypersensitivity, infection-associated malignancy |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook — "PM-SON-CAR"**: **P**M2.5/10, **S**O2, **O**3, **N**O2, **C**O, **A**sbestos/silica, **R**adiation — the eight agents that carry ~80% of the marks in this sub-topic. Pair each with its signature lesion (PM → COPD, CO → left-shift ODC via HbCO, asbestos → mesothelioma) and recall becomes mechanical.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

> The triad to remember: agent enters → **mechanism of cellular injury** → organ-specific lesion, with cancer as a stochastic endpoint.

#### Mechanisms of toxicity (apply to almost every MCQ)

Receptor-mediated injury, free-radical/oxidative stress, DNA adduct formation, enzyme inhibition, membrane injury, and epigenetic modification are the six recurring mechanisms. Ionizing radiation damages DNA by **direct double-strand breaks** and indirectly via **hydroxyl radical (•OH)** generated from water radiolysis — the basis of both deterministic tissue injury and stochastic carcinogenesis.

#### Heavy metals — the comparison matrix examiners love

| Metal | Source/exposure | Signature lesion | Cancer link |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lead (Pb)** | Paint, battery, water pipes | Microcytic hypochromic anaemia with **basophilic stippling**, abdominal colic, cognitive deficit (children), nephropathy | Renal (in high exposure) |
| **Mercury (Hg)** | Fish (methyl-Hg), industrial | **Minamata disease** — cerebellar, cortical, visual deficits; renal tubular injury | None established |
| **Cadmium (Cd)** | Mining, Ni-Cd batteries, rice | **Itai-itai disease** — renal tubular injury + osteomalacia; emphysema | Lung (occupational) |
| **Arsenic (As)** | Contaminated groundwater, pesticides | Hyperpigmentation, hyperkeratosis, peripheral neuropathy | Skin (SCC), lung, bladder |

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Don't write "arsenic causes Minamata disease" or "mercury causes Itai-itai." Each metal has a named disease, and seat-of-the-pants answers are the easiest two negative marks in the paper.

#### Pneumoconioses — distribution and complication matter

| Disease | Exposure | Histology | Distribution | Key complication |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Coal workers' pneumoconiosis** | Coal dust | Anthracotic macules, PMF | **Upper zone** | Progressive massive fibrosis |
| **Silicosis** | Silica (mining, stone cutting) | **Silicotic nodules** with birefringent crystals | Upper zone | **TB susceptibility**, lung Ca |
| **Asbestosis** | Asbestos (shipyard, insulation) | **Ferruginous bodies** | **Lower zone** | **Mesothelioma** + bronchogenic Ca |

Smoking multiplies asbestos-related bronchogenic carcinoma risk **synergistically**, not additively — a high-yield AIIMS-style MCQ stem.

#### Carcinogenesis: initiation → promotion → progression

Direct-acting carcinogens (e.g., alkylating agents) hit DNA directly. **Procarcinogens** (PAHs, nitrosamines, aflatoxin B1) require **CYP450** bioactivation. Phorbol esters are classic *promoters* — they drive clonal expansion of an already-initiated cell without themselves mutating DNA.

#### CO poisoning — a mechanism trap

Carbon monoxide binds Hb with ~240× the affinity of O2, forming **carboxyhaemoglobin (HbCO)** that shifts the **oxygen–haemoglobin dissociation curve to the LEFT**. The lesion is therefore **functional tissue hypoxia** with normal PaO2, not anaemia.

#### Worked exam-style resolution

**Stem.** A 45-year-old battery-recycling worker presents with wrist drop, abdominal colic, and a peripheral smear showing microcytic hypochromic RBCs with coarse basophilic stippling. Serum ferritin is normal. Which metal is responsible, and what is the antidote principle?

#### Working.
1. **Exposure → lead**: battery recycling + basophilic stippling + normal ferritin (rules out iron deficiency) + abdominal colic = classic lead toxicity.
2. **Mechanism**: Pb inhibits **δ-ALA dehydratase** and **ferrochelatase**, blocking haem synthesis and producing a microcytic hypochromic picture that mimics iron deficiency without the ferritin drop.
3. **Wrist drop** = motor neuropathy from segmental demyelination in adults (children get encephalopathy instead — a frequent trap).
4. **Antidote logic**: CaNa2-EDTA (chelation) for symptomatic disease; dimercaprol (BAL) when encephalopathy or high blood lead is present; succimer (DMSA) for oral outpatient chelation.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Options usually include "iron supplementation" because the smear looks iron-deficient. Always check ferritin and the exposure history — the answer is chelation, not iron.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

> Two themes earn the extra marks here: (1) the deterministic-vs-stochastic split in radiation injury, and (2) the organophosphate pharmacology that INI CET frames as a clinical-pathology integration stem.

#### Radiation injury — deterministic vs stochastic, and the phase order

| Type | Dose relation | Example | Latency |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Deterministic** | Threshold dose, severity rises with dose | Skin burns, marrow aplasia, cataract, teratogenesis | Weeks–months |
| **Stochastic** | No threshold; probability rises with dose | Carcinogenesis, germline mutation | Years–decades |

**Acute radiation syndrome** progresses through three dose-banded phases that examiners list in reverse to test you:

1. **Haemopoietic** — >1–2 Gy, lymphopenia, pancytopenia, infection.
2. **Gastrointestinal** — >6 Gy, denuded crypts, bloody diarrhoea, sepsis.
3. **Cerebrovascular / CNS** — >10 Gy, cerebral oedema, ataxia, death within hours–days.

#### Organophosphate poisoning — the SLUDGE / DUMBELS pair

OP compounds (and carbamates) inhibit **acetylcholinesterase**, producing muscarinic and nicotinic overactivity. Recognising the syndrome is half the INI CET pharmacology-pathology cross-question; the other half is the antidote pair — **atropine** (muscarinic) and **pralidoxime / 2-PAM** (regenerates the enzyme before "aging").

#### Endocrine disruptors and the DES lesson

Diethylstilbestrol in pregnancy caused **clear-cell vaginal adenocarcinoma in daughters** exposed in utero — a textbook example of transplacental carcinogenesis. Bisphenol A and DDT remain active research targets for reproductive and developmental effects.

#### Altitude, pressure, and thermal extremes

- **High-altitude pulmonary oedema (HAPE)** — hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction with patchy oedema.
- **Decompression sickness ("caisson disease")** — nitrogen gas emboli in joints, spinal cord, brain; treated by hyperbaric recompression.
- **Heat stroke > hypothermia** — both cause direct cellular and vascular injury; rhabdomyolysis and DIC are common sequelae of heat stroke.

#### Dose–response vocabulary INI CET expects you to recognise

- **LD50** — lethal dose in 50% of test population.
- **Threshold dose** — below which no measurable adverse effect occurs.
- **NOAEL** — No-Observed-Adverse-Effect Level, the basis of regulatory safety standards.

#### Advanced practice prompts

1. A silica-exposed stonemason with weight loss and apical cavitation — name the histology of his lung lesion, the organism most likely responsible for the cavity, and the way silica drives macrophage-mediated injury.
2. A chronic smoker with painless haematuria — list three distinct organ systems where tobacco causes malignancy and one non-neoplastic lesion per system, then explain why asbestos exposure multiplies (rather than adds to) his bronchogenic carcinoma risk.
