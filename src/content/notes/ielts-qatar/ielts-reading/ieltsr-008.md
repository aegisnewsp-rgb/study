# Short-Answer Questions — IELTS Reading (Qatar)

The short-answer question type is the simplest variant in the IELTS Academic
Reading test. Each question asks you to write **a short answer — usually no
more than three words and/or a number** — drawn from the passage itself. Unlike
multiple choice or matching, there are no options; you produce the answer from
your own knowledge of the passage.

Short-answer questions are commonly placed on **Passage 1** (the easiest
passage) or scattered through **Passages 2 and 3** as a contrast to the more
cognitively demanding question types. They are graded on a 1:1 basis — one
correct answer = one mark — and they usually appear in **groups of 4–6**
questions, all anchored to a single passage.

In the Qatari cohort, short-answer questions frequently anchor on dates, names,
places, or specific details from texts about Qatari / GCC history, environmental
science, or industrial processes. Because short-answer questions rely on the
**specific wording of the passage**, this is also where the test distinguishes
candidates who read superficially from candidates who read carefully.

This note shows the LCA (Long-Read Cohort Appetite) version: a **Lite**
(5-minute) read, a **Standard** (15-minute) read, and the **Exam Essentials**
table a Qatari candidate can use to revise in the last hour before the test.

## Lite (4-Step Method)

1. **Read the questions first, not the passage.** Each short-answer question
   contains the *keyword* that locates the answer in the passage. Note the
   keyword — usually a name, date, place, or technical term — and the **word
   limit** (e.g. *NO MORE THAN THREE WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER*).
2. **Scan the passage for the keyword.** When you see the keyword in the
   passage, read the **whole sentence** that contains it. The answer is usually
   in the same sentence or the sentence immediately before/after.
3. **Pick the EXACT wording from the passage.** Short-answer questions are
   graded strictly: you must copy the wording **verbatim** from the passage,
   including plural forms (*years* vs *year*) and spelling. Re-wording in your
   own words is a wrong answer.
4. **Check the word limit.** If the limit is *THREE WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER*,
   you cannot write four words, and you cannot write a long phrase. If you are
   over the limit, edit to the **most essential** words.

**Insight: short-answer questions are a passage-comprehension test, not a
language-production test.** You are not being asked to *write*; you are being
asked to *locate and copy*. Every word you write should appear in the passage.

### 6 Examiner Traps (short-answer questions)

- **The wrong keyword** — you scan the passage for the right keyword but find
  a similar one (e.g. *1950* vs *1951*). Always re-check the **exact** keyword
  in the question.
- **The word limit** — you write a four-word answer to a *THREE WORDS* limit.
  Count your words carefully before moving on.
- **The singular/plural** — the passage says *years*, you write *year* (or
  vice versa). The answer is wrong even if the meaning is correct.
- **The paraphrase** — you re-word the answer in your own words instead of
  copying verbatim. The answer is wrong even if the meaning is correct.
- **The off-topic** — you write about a different detail in the passage. The
  answer is wrong because it does not answer the question asked.
- **The number / format** — you write *12* instead of *twelve*, or *1907*
  instead of *1907 AD*. The answer is wrong if the format does not match the
  expected answer.

## Standard (7-Step Method with Long-Form Guidance)

### Step 1 — Pre-read the questions

Before you touch the passage, read **every short-answer question** for the
passage. Note the **keyword** in each question and the **word limit**. The
word limit is non-negotiable — exceeding it is an automatic wrong answer.

### Step 2 — Skim the passage for the keywords

You are not reading the passage for meaning yet. You are scanning for the
**keywords** in each question. Use a pencil (or the on-screen highlight tool)
to mark each keyword when you see it.

### Step 3 — Read around each keyword

Once you have marked all the keywords you can find, go back to each keyword
and read the **full sentence or paragraph** that contains it. The answer is
usually in the same sentence or in the sentence immediately before/after.

### Step 4 — Pick the exact wording

Short-answer questions are graded strictly. You must copy the wording **verbatim**
from the passage, including plural forms and spelling. Re-wording in your own
words is a wrong answer even if the meaning is correct.

### Step 5 — Check the word limit

If the limit is *THREE WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER*, count your words. You cannot
write four words. You cannot write a long phrase. If you are over the limit,
edit to the **most essential** words.

### Step 6 — Check the format

If the answer is a number, check the format expected (e.g. *12* vs *twelve*,
*1907* vs *1907 AD*). If the answer is a name, check the spelling. If the
answer is a place, check the spelling.

### Step 7 — Verify your answers

If you have time, re-read each question and your answer to confirm the answer
is correct. Do not change an answer unless you have a clear reason to.

## High-Yield Passages (7 high-yield passage contexts)

These are the kinds of passage contexts the IELTS Academic Reading test uses
for short-answer questions. Knowing the context can save you 30 seconds per
question.

1. **History of a place / culture** — dates, names, places. (Common.)
2. **Industrial history** — dates, names, places, machinery.
3. **Environmental science** — dates, species names, places.
4. **Medical / health research** — dates, researcher names, study places.
5. **Qatari / GCC context** — dates, names, places of Qatar's development.
6. **Educational history** — dates, founder names, institution places.
7. **Sports / cultural history** — dates, player names, event places.

## Previous Year Patterns (IELTS 2018–2025)

IELTS has used short-answer questions in **Passage 1 on most Academic Reading
tests since 2018**, and scattered through Passages 2 and 3. Over 2018–2025:

- 2018–2020: short-answer questions commonly anchored in Passage 1 with
  4–6 questions per passage.
- 2021–2023: short-answer questions increased in Passage 3, paired with
  matching or multiple choice.
- 2024–2025: short-answer questions remain a Passage 1 staple, with
  increasing frequency of number / date questions on texts about technology
  and the environment.

The pattern: short-answer questions are the **simplest** question type in the
IELTS Academic Reading test, but they are graded **strictly** on exact wording
and word limit. Candidates who read carefully score high; candidates who
paraphrase or exceed the word limit score zero.

## Pro Tips (10 enumeration)

1. **Read the questions first.** It tells you what keywords to scan for.
2. **Note the word limit.** It is non-negotiable.
3. **Scan for keywords.** Use a pencil to mark each keyword.
4. **Read around each keyword.** The answer is usually in the same sentence
   or the sentence immediately before/after.
5. **Copy verbatim.** Do not paraphrase. Do not re-word.
6. **Check singular/plural.** Match the passage form exactly.
7. **Check spelling.** Match the passage spelling exactly.
8. **Check the format.** If the answer is a number, write it in the format
   expected.
9. **Time budget: 1 minute per question.** Do not dwell on any one question.
10. **Re-verify before submitting.** A 30-second re-check can save a
    1-point penalty.
