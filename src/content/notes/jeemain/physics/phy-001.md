# Units and Measurement

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

A **physical quantity** is expressed as *Q = n · u*, where *n* is the numerical magnitude and *u* is the unit. The **SI system** fixes seven **base units**: metre (m), kilogram (kg), second (s), ampere (A), kelvin (K), mole (mol), and candela (cd). Every other unit — newton, joule, pascal — is a **derived unit** built algebraically from these seven.

- **Force:** F = ma → SI unit kg·m·s⁻² (newton, N); dimensions [M L T⁻²]
- **Energy:** W = Fd → SI unit kg·m²·s⁻² (joule, J); dimensions [M L² T⁻²]
- **Power:** P = W/t → SI unit kg·m²·s⁻³ (watt, W); dimensions [M L² T⁻³]
- **Pressure:** P = F/A → SI unit kg·m⁻¹·s⁻² (pascal, Pa); dimensions [M L⁻¹ T⁻²]

| Quantity | SI Unit | Symbol | Dimensions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Length | metre | m | [L] |
| Mass | kilogram | kg | [M] |
| Time | second | s | [T] |
| Force | newton | N | [M L T⁻²] |
| Energy | joule | J | [M L² T⁻²] |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"Seven Kings March Past A King Mole's Candle"** — Metre, Kilogram, Second, Ampere, Kelvin, Mole, Candela (in SI base-unit order). For dimension of force, remember **"M L T-square"** — students most in many papers drop the square on T and write [M L T⁻¹], losing a mark.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Dimensions and Dimensional Analysis

The **dimensional formula** of a quantity expresses its dependence on the seven base quantities using exponents. Force = [M L T⁻²], work = [M L² T⁻²], and Planck's constant = [M L² T⁻¹]. A quantity whose every exponent is zero is **dimensionless** — examples include angle (radian), strain (ΔL/L), refractive index, relative density, and Mach number.

The **principle of homogeneity** states that an equation is dimensionally valid only if every additive term carries identical dimensions. You cannot add force to energy, even though both are "mechanical". Dimensional analysis can **verify** a formula but **cannot fetch dimensionless constants** such as 2π in T = 2π√(L/g) — the method is blind to pure numbers.

#### Systems of Units and Conversion

Four historical systems precede the SI: **CGS** (centimetre–gram–second), **FPS** (foot–pound–second), **MKS** (metre–kilogram–second), and **SI**. The **dimensional conversion formula** is:

> n₂ = n₁ · (M₁/M₂)^a · (L₁/L₂)^b · (T₁/T₂)^c

where *a, b, c* are dimensional exponents of the base quantities M, L, T.

#### Errors, Accuracy, and Precision

Errors split into **systematic** (instrumental zero error, personal bias, faulty calibration — reproducible and correctable) and **random** (fluctuations beyond control — reduced by repeated averaging). For a compound quantity Z = A·B/C, the **maximum relative error** is the sum of individual relative errors:

> ΔZ/Z = ΔA/A + ΔB/B + ΔC/C

**Accuracy** measures closeness to the true value; **precision** measures reproducibility across repeated readings. A set can be precise yet inaccurate if every reading carries the same systematic offset.

#### Measuring Instruments: Least Count

- **Vernier calliper:** LC = 1 MSD − 1 VSD (in standard papers 0.01 cm = 0.1 mm)
- **Screw gauge:** LC = pitch ÷ number of head-scale divisions (in standard papers 0.01 mm)
- **Least count** sets the resolution — no reading can be trusted beyond one-tenth of the LC.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix

| Feature | Dimensionless Quantity | Dimensional Quantity |
|---|---|---|
| All exponents a,b,c,… = 0 | Yes | No |
| Independent of unit choice | Always | Numerical value changes with unit |
| Examples | Angle, strain, refractive index, relative density, Mach number | Force, energy, pressure, viscosity |
| Can be added to another quantity? | Only if both are dimensionless | Only if both share the same dimensions |

|---|---|---|

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** The dimensional formula of the universal gravitational constant G in F = Gm₁m₂/r² is:
(a) [M⁻¹ L³ T⁻²] (b) [M L² T⁻²] (c) [M L³ T⁻²] (d) [M L⁻³ T⁻²]

#### Solution:

1. Rearrange the equation: G = F · r² / (m₁ · m₂)
2. Substitute dimensions: [F] = [M L T⁻²], [r²] = [L²], [m] = [M]
3. Compute: [G] = [M L T⁻²] · [L²] / ([M] · [M]) = [M L³ T⁻²] / [M²] = **[M⁻¹ L³ T⁻²]**
4. Match with option (a).

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students in many papers forget to square the distance in the denominator's numerator and write [M⁻¹ L³ T⁻²] without the inverse on mass — the most common wrong answer is (c) [M L³ T⁻²]. Track every factor through the algebra before cancelling.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Significant Figures and the Zeros Problem

**Significant figures** communicate measurement precision. Rules:

- **Non-zero digits** are always significant (435 g → 3 s.f.).
- **Leading zeros** are never significant (0.0042 → 2 s.f.).
- **Embedded zeros** are always significant (4025 → 4 s.f.).
- **Trailing zeros** are significant **only if a decimal point is present**: 1500 m is ambiguous (can be 2, 3, or 4 s.f.), while 1500. m = 4 s.f. and 1500 = 2 s.f.

#### Dimensional Conversion: Stellar Units

Three specialised length units recur in astrophysics problems linked to the parallax method:

| Unit | Symbol | Definition | Value in metres |
|---|---|---|---|
| Astronomical Unit | AU | Mean Earth–Sun distance | 1.496 × 10¹¹ m |
| Light-year | ly | Distance light travels in 1 Julian year | 9.46 × 10¹⁵ m |
| Parsec | pc | Distance at which 1 AU subtends 1 arc-second | 3.08 × 10¹⁶ m |

Conversion anchor: **1 pc = 3.26 ly**, derived from the small-angle identity 1 pc = 1 AU / tan(1″) ≈ 1 AU / (1/206265) rad.

#### Advanced Traps and Edge Cases

1. **Dimensional analysis cannot derive formulas containing trigonometric arguments, exponentials, or logarithms** — those functions demand dimensionless inputs. Verifying T = 2π√(L/g) gives the right dimensions for T but says nothing about 2π.
2. **Angle is dimensionless but radian is not the only unit** — degrees, minutes, seconds of arc are angular units; the *physical quantity* "angle" carries no dimensions regardless of unit.
3. **Viscosity (η)** has dimensions [M L⁻¹ T⁻¹] — the surface-area factor in Stokes' law (F = 6πηrv) is the easiest place to misplace an exponent.
4. **Surface tension (γ)** has dimensions [M T⁻²] — same as force per unit length — students occasionally write [M L T⁻²] by forgetting the length denominator.
5. **Planck's constant (h)** has dimensions [M L² T⁻¹] — the action quantum — frequently confused with angular momentum [M L² T⁻¹], which is *identical in dimensions* but distinct in physical meaning.

#### Common Errors That Cost Marks

| Mistake | Correction |
|---|---|
| Writing dimension of force as [M L T⁻¹] | Correct form is [M L T⁻²] |
| Treating angle as dimensional | Angle is dimensionless; so are strain, refractive index |
| Using dimensional analysis to find 2π or e | Dimensional methods cannot recover pure numbers |
| Counting 1500 m as 4 s.f. without decimal | Ambiguous; 1500. m = 4 s.f., 1500 = 2 s.f. |
| Converting pc to ly using 1:1 ratio | 1 pc = 3.26 ly; factor of 3.26 required |

#### Practice Prompts for Mastery

1. **Verify dimensionally** the equation for the period of a simple pendulum T = 2π√(l/g) and identify what the method *cannot* tell you about the constant 2π.
2. **A screw gauge** has a pitch of 0.5 mm and 50 head-scale divisions. State the least count in mm and convert a measured reading of 3.42 mm into metres with correct significant figures.
