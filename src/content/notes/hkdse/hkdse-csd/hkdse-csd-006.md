# Theme 3 — Sustainable Development and Public Health — HKDSE CSD Notes

This topic covers Topics 3 and 4 of Theme 3 of the HKDSE Citizenship and Social Development curriculum. Topic 3 addresses sustainable development — the rationale and the practical experiences of environmental conservation of the country, Hong Kong and other regions, and the roles and responsibilities of different stakeholders. Topic 4 addresses public health and human health — the functions and roles of the World Health Organization, the contributions of the country and Hong Kong to global public health, and individuals' responsibilities in promoting public health.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Topic 3 — sustainable development**: the rationale (meeting present needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet theirs); the practical experiences of environmental conservation of the country, Hong Kong and other regions (the dual carbon goals, the HKSAR Waste Blueprint, country parks, marine parks); the roles and responsibilities of different stakeholders (government, business, civil society, individuals).
- **Topic 4 — public health and human health**: the functions and roles of the World Health Organization (WHO); the contributions of the country and Hong Kong to global public health (the COVID-19 response, the Health Silk Road, the Hong Kong Jockey Club Global Health Institute); individuals' responsibilities in promoting public health (vaccination, hygiene, compliance with health guidance).
- **Assessment**: data-response and short-essay items on the 2-hour CSD paper.

#### Examiner traps

- Treating sustainability as purely about the environment — sustainable development covers environmental, social and economic dimensions.
- Reducing public health to clinical medicine — public health is the science and practice of protecting and improving the health of populations through prevention, promotion and policy.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Sustainable development — rationale and practice

The Brundtland Report (1987) defined sustainable development as development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs. The three pillars of sustainable development are environmental, social and economic sustainability.

**Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)**: the UN General Assembly adopted the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development in 2015, with 17 SDGs covering poverty, hunger, health, education, gender, water, energy, work, infrastructure, inequality, cities, consumption, climate, oceans, land, peace and partnerships.

**Country-level practice**:

- **Dual carbon goals**: announced by President Xi Jinping in 2020 — peak emissions by 2030, carbon neutrality by 2060.
- **Energy transition**: rapid expansion of solar and wind capacity; the world's largest installed renewable energy capacity by 2023.
- **Forest and biodiversity conservation**: the Natural Forest Protection Program, the Sloping Land Conversion Program (returning farmland to forest), the Belt and Road South-South Cooperation Programme on Biodiversity.
- **Air pollution control**: the Air Pollution Prevention and Control Action Plan (2013, 2018), measurable improvements in PM2.5 concentrations in major cities.

**Hong Kong practice**:

- **Waste management**: the Waste Blueprint for Hong Kong 2035 (published 2021) — targets for waste reduction, recycling, and landfill diversion.
- **Country parks and marine parks**: over 40% of Hong Kong's land area is country park; marine parks protect ecologically important areas.
- **Air quality**: roadside and roadside air quality monitoring; progressively tighter motor-vehicle emission standards.
- **Climate Action Plan 2050**: published 2021 — carbon neutrality target by 2050.

**Stakeholders' roles**:

- **Government**: legislation, regulation, public investment, international cooperation.
- **Business**: sustainable procurement, environmental management systems, disclosure of ESG metrics, transition to cleaner technologies.
- **Civil society**: NGOs, community organisations, advocacy.
- **Individuals**: lifestyle choices, citizen science, voting, advocacy.

#### Public health and human health

**The World Health Organization (WHO)**: a specialised agency of the United Nations, established in 1948. Functions:

- Setting norms and standards (the International Classification of Diseases, the WHO Model Lists of Essential Medicines).
- Providing technical assistance to member states.
- Monitoring and surveillance (e.g. the Global Influenza Surveillance and Response System).
- Coordinating international action on health emergencies (e.g. the COVID-19 pandemic, the mpox public health emergency).
- Promoting health research.

**Country-level contributions**:

- **The COVID-19 response**: China's response included the Wuhan lockdown, mass testing, contact tracing, the construction of field hospitals, the development and deployment of vaccines (Sinovac, Sinopharm), and vaccine diplomacy (vaccines provided to over 100 countries).
- **Health Silk Road**: part of the Belt and Road Initiative, focused on health-system cooperation.
- **Traditional Chinese Medicine**: integrated into the national health system; exported through the Belt and Road Initiative.

**Hong Kong contributions**:

- **Centre for Health Protection**: established 2004 in response to SARS; coordinates Hong Kong's response to communicable diseases.
- **Hospital Authority**: operates public hospitals and clinics.
- **Research and innovation**: Hong Kong's medical schools and the Hong Kong Jockey Club Global Health Institute.

**Individuals' responsibilities**:

- Vaccination compliance with the Hong Kong Government's vaccination programme.
- Personal hygiene (hand washing, mask wearing during outbreaks).
- Compliance with public-health guidance during outbreaks (testing, isolation, quarantine).
- Lifestyle choices (diet, exercise, smoking cessation) that affect non-communicable disease risk.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-topic links

- **Theme 2 (international affairs)**: the dual carbon goals connect to international climate negotiations; the Health Silk Road connects to the Belt and Road Initiative.
- **Theme 3 Topics 1 and 2 (economic globalisation and technology)**: technological innovation is part of the solution to sustainability challenges; public health is increasingly digital (telemedicine, health data analytics).
- **National Security Education**: biosafety and ecological security are part of the holistic approach to national security.

#### Exam technique

- **For short-essay items on sustainability**: use the three-pillar framework (environmental, social, economic) and give specific examples for each pillar.
- **For short-essay items on public health**: distinguish between communicable and non-communicable diseases; use the COVID-19 pandemic as a worked example.
- **For data-response items**: identify the issue, the stakeholders, the policy framework, and the implications. Use specific examples (e.g. the HKSAR Waste Blueprint, the dual carbon goals).

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Sustainable development is only about the environment." It covers environmental, social and economic sustainability in an integrated way.
- "The WHO can enforce its recommendations." The WHO's authority is normative — it sets standards and provides guidance, but enforcement depends on member states.
- "Carbon neutrality is the same as zero emissions." Carbon neutrality balances residual emissions with carbon removals (e.g. afforestation, carbon capture); zero emissions is a stricter target.
