# Cell Division

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> One-screen summary for the night before the exam.

Mitosis doubles cells faithfully; meiosis halves the chromosome count to make gametes. Both start after **interphase**, the G1 → S → G2 sequence where DNA goes from 2C → 4C during the S-phase.

| Phase | Key event | DNA content |
|---|---|---|
| G1 | Cell growth, organelle duplication | 2C |
| S | Replication (each chromosome → 2 sister chromatids) | 2C → 4C |
| G2 | Spindle prep, growth | 4C |
| Mitosis | Karyokinesis + cytokinesis | 4C → 2C (per daughter) |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook — "PMAT × 2 for meiosis":** **P**rophase, **M**etaphase, **A**naphase, **T**elophase run once in mitosis, twice in meiosis (Meiosis I is reductional, Meiosis II is equational). The extra round is where the n count halves.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Working understanding of stages, ploidy logic, and a worked problem.

#### Interphase: the quiet before the split

Interphase is not rest. G1 builds proteins and organelles; **S-phase replicates every chromosome** so each becomes a pair of **sister chromatids joined at the centromere**; G2 stockpils tubulin and checks for replication errors. Chromosomes remain diffuse as chromatin and are not visible under a light microscope during interphase.

#### Mitosis — one nuclear division, two identical cells

- **Prophase** — chromatin condenses, nuclear envelope breaks down, spindle microtubules emerge from opposite poles. *Longest phase of mitosis.*
- **Metaphase** — chromosomes line up on the **equatorial plate** (metaphase plate). Best stage for **karyotyping** because chromosomes are most condensed and individually identifiable.
- **Anaphase** — *Shortest phase.* Centromeres split; **sister chromatids** are pulled to opposite poles.
- **Telophase** — nuclear envelope re-forms around each pole, chromosomes decondense.
- **Cytokinesis** — In **plant cells** a **cell plate** grows outward from the centre through the **phragmoplast** (microtubule-rich region) and becomes the middle lamella, then new cell wall. In animal cells, a cleavage furrow pinches the cell in two.

#### Meiosis — two divisions, four haploid cells

#### Prophase I sub-stages (high-yield, frequently tested)

| Sub-stage | What happens |
|---|---|
| Leptotene | Chromosomes begin to condense |
| Zygotene | **Synapsis** — homologous chromosomes pair into **bivalents / tetrads** |
| Pachytene | **Crossing over** occurs at **chiasmata** (recombination visible) |
| Diplotene | Homologs start separating, chiasmata visible |
| Diakinesis | Terminal condensation, nucleolus disappears |

**Anaphase I** separates **homologous chromosomes**, not sister chromatids — sister chromatids stay glued at the centromere. **Anaphase II** then splits sister chromatids, mimicking mitotic anaphase.

#### DNA content and ploidy across the cycle

| Stage | Ploidy | DNA content |
|---|---|---|
| G1 | 2n | 2C |
| After S | 2n | 4C |
| After Meiosis I | n (each chromosome still 2 chromatids) | 2C → halved to 2C per cell |
| After Meiosis II | n | C |
| Mitotic daughter | 2n | 2C |

Quantitatively, for any organism: **2n = diploid chromosome count**, **n = haploid count**, and **C = haploid DNA mass (in picograms)**. So if 2n = 46 in humans, n = 23, and a gamete carries exactly half the DNA of a somatic G1 cell.

#### Worked problem and trap

A cell of an organism with 2n = 20 chromosomes completes S-phase but then enters meiosis. At the end of Meiosis I, how many chromosomes and how much DNA (in C units) does each daughter cell hold?

Step 1 — Start state: 2n = 20, DNA = 2C in G1.
Step 2 — After S-phase: chromosomes still 20 (each as two chromatids), DNA = 4C.
Step 3 — Meiosis I is reductional: homologs separate, so chromosome count halves → 10 chromosomes per cell. Each chromosome still has two chromatids, so DNA = 2C per cell.
Step 4 — Meiosis II splits sister chromatids → 10 chromosomes, 1C DNA per gamete. (Note that each "chromosome" post-II is now a single chromatid — easy to miscount.)

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Many candidates answer "10 chromosomes, 1C DNA" right after Meiosis I. The correct figure at the *end of Meiosis I* is **10 chromosomes, 2C DNA per cell** — the second division is what trims DNA to 1C per gamete.

> 💡 **Concept Comparison Matrix**

| Feature | Mitosis | Meiosis I | Meiosis II |
|---|---|---|---|
| Type | Equational | Reductional | Equational |
| Ploidy change | 2n → 2n | 2n → n | n → n |
| Synapsis / crossing over | Absent | Present (Prophase I) | Absent |
| What separates in anaphase | Sister chromatids | Homologous chromosomes | Sister chromatids |
| Outcome | 2 cells | 2 cells | 4 cells (total) |

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Edge cases, mathematical limits, and where this topic bleeds into the rest of the syllabus.

#### When 2n ≠ the usual: polyploidy and aneuploidy

The general relation **total chromosomes = n × x** (where *x* is the **basic chromosome number**, *n* the ploidy multiplier) covers a lot of plant diversity. **Autopolyploidy** (genome multiplied within one species, e.g. *Triticum aestivum* lineages through hexaploidy 6x = 42) and **allopolyploidy** (genomes combined from two species, the wheat series is again the canonical example) are routine in plants but rare in animals because of dosage-sensitive sex determination. Aneuploidy — gain or loss of one or a few chromosomes — arises from **non-disjunction**, the failure of homologs (Meiosis I) or sister chromatids (Meiosis II, mitosis) to separate. In humans the only viable autosomal aneuploidies are trisomies 13, 18, 21; sex-chromosome aneuploidies (Turner XO, Klinefelter XXY) survive because of X-inactivation.

#### Mitotic index as a quantitative tool

**Mitotic index (%) = (Number of dividing cells / Total cells observed) × 100.** Tissues with high mitotic indices — root apical meristem, shoot apical meristem, vascular cambium — are the ones NEET may quiz on because they are where mitosis is most observable in a longitudinal section. The **shoot apex** versus **root apex** distinction matters: only the root apex has a well-defined quiescent centre just behind the actual meristematic region.

#### Edge cases the examiner likes

1. **Anaphase I in plants vs animals:** spindle fibres attach to centromeres through kinetochores; plants lack centrioles but still form spindles via **microtubule-organising centres (MTOCs)** — an MCQ favourite.
2. **Crossing over ≠ gene conversion.** Crossing over is reciprocal exchange between non-sister chromatids of homologous chromosomes; gene conversion is non-reciprocal information transfer at the same locus. A 2019 NEET assertion-reason item conflated the two.
3. **Cell plate is not the cell wall.** Middle lamella forms first (Ca²⁺-crosslinked pectins), then primary cell wall is laid down on either side.
4. **Microsporogenesis** in flowering plants is meiosis + maturation. After Meiosis II the four microspores develop into pollen; the **tapetum** feeds them. This connects Cell Division directly to the **Sexual Reproduction in Flowering Plants** chapter — both contribute to NEET's high-scoring areas.

#### Advanced traps and exceptions

- **Meiosis needs DNA replication exactly once.** A second S-phase between Meiosis I and Meiosis II would double the chromatid count again — it does not happen under normal control. MPF (M-phase promoting factor) keeps the cell out of S.
- **Spindle poisoning agents** (colchicine, vinblastine) arrest cells in metaphase. Colchicine applied to dividing cells also doubles the chromosome number — the *exploited trick behind autopolyploid induction*.
- **Interphase chromosomes are single but visible as 46 units after condensation** because each condensed "chromosome" already contains two chromatids post-S — answer keys repeatedly confuse chromosome number with chromatid number across the cell cycle.
- **Bivalent formation does not occur in mitosis**, even when homologs are physically near. No synapsis → no crossing over → no genetic variation from mitosis. State the underlying cause, not the symptom, when an assertion-reason asks why mitosis is genetically conservative.

#### Two advanced practice prompts

1. A cell shows 24 chromosomes with each chromosome composed of two sister chromatids at the start of anaphase I. After telophase I, how many chromosomes and chromatids are present in each daughter cell before cytokinesis finishes? Justify using the rule "homologs separate, chromatids stay paired."
2. The mitotic index of an onion root tip section is 8.4%. If 1,200 cells were scored, how many cells were actually in mitosis? Convert your result into an estimate of how long a typical cell spends in mitosis if the full cell cycle is known to be roughly 18 hours.
