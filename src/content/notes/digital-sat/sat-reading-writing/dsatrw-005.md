# Information and Ideas: Command of Evidence — Digital SAT Reading and Writing Notes

Command of Evidence questions require selecting textual statements or analyzing quantitative graphs and tables that directly support or weaken a researcher's stated hypothesis. In the official Digital SAT examination, this topic carries a weight rating of 5 out of 5 and appears consistently across testing administrations. Candidates preparing for high percentiles must master both theoretical fundamentals and rapid practical problem-solving strategies.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Weight & Frequency**: Rated 5/5 in priority; this topic appears reliably in every sitting.
- **Timing Goal**: Aim for disciplined pacing per item to leave sufficient buffer for complex multi-step problems at the end of the section.
- **Core Principle**: Match the proposed hypothesis with the specific experimental outcome or data trend that proves or disproves it.
- **Common pitfall**: Check both axes and unit labels on graphs carefully. Ensure the data choice supports the exact mechanism hypothesized, not just a general positive correlation.
- **Systematic Strategy**: Always eliminate obviously incorrect distractors before attempting detailed calculations or granular textual analysis.
- **Answer Verification**: Cross-check final values or textual line references against the explicit wording of the prompt to avoid answering the wrong question.

#### Examiner traps

- Misreading key constraint words in the prompt (such as *except*, *not*, *least*, *integer*, *constant*).
- Falling for attractive partial answers that represent an intermediate calculation step rather than the final required quantity.
- Basing textual answers on outside background knowledge rather than direct evidence supplied within the passage.
- Spending excessive time struggling on a single difficult problem instead of flagging it, making an educated guess, and maintaining forward momentum.

### 🔴 Deep Dive — Mastery & Edge Cases (3mo–2yr)

#### Advanced variations and high-difficulty edge cases

Each proposed hypothesis has a signature in the data, and matching them is mechanical:

- **Evidence Match**: For each answer, name the specific detail that supports it and explain the exact step linking that detail to the claim.
- **Citation Precision**: Distinguish direct wording from paraphrase, avoid quoting fragments that lack meaning, and attach each citation to the idea it actually proves.
- **Elimination Audit**: Remove choices that cite real details but connect them to the wrong claim, tone, speaker, or cause rather than the requested point.

#### Systematic drill schedule

Command-of-evidence items are search tasks, so drill the search:

- **Signature prediction**: For each proposed hypothesis, name the trend that would support it and the trend that would break it before reading options.
- **Exact quotation**: Quote the exact line carrying the evidence. Paraphrase at this stage is where support is quietly invented.
- **Wrong-quantity filter**: Discard data that is real but measures a different quantity. A true reading from the wrong axis or unit is a distractor.
