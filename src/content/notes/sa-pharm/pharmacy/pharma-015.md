# Toxicology and Emergency Pharmacy

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Toxicology and Emergency Pharmacy** covers the recognition, triage, and pharmaceutical care of poisoning and overdose within the framework of the South African Pharmacy Council (SAPC). The discipline blends toxicokinetics, antidote pharmacology, decontamination, and the legal duties of the pharmacist under the Medicines and Related Substances Act 101 of 1965.

- **LD50** = dose (mg/kg) killing 50% of the test population; **TD50** = median toxic dose; **ED50** = median effective dose.
- **Therapeutic Index (TI) = TD50 ÷ ED50** — higher TI = wider safety margin (e.g., diazepam ≈ 100; digoxin ≈ 2–3).
- **First responder sequence:** ABCDE → resuscitation → supportive care → decontamination (activated charcoal within 1–2 h) → enhanced elimination → specific antidote.
- **High-yield SA poisonings:** organophosphates, paraquat, paracetamol, benzodiazepines, opioids, snakebite.
- **Key antidotes:** N-acetylcysteine (paracetamol), naloxone (opioids), flumazenil (benzodiazepines), atropine + pralidoxime (organophosphates).

#### Toxicokinetics and Dose Metrics

Toxicology applies the same ADME framework as pharmacokinetics, but xenobiotics frequently follow **zero-order kinetics** at high doses (e.g., ethanol, paracetamol), where a constant amount — not a constant fraction — is eliminated per unit time.

| Parameter | Formula | Clinical meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LD50 | mg/kg causing 50% mortality | Lethal threshold (animal data) |
| TD50 | mg/kg producing toxicity in 50% | Toxic threshold |
| TI | TD50 ÷ ED50 | Safety margin |
| Vd | Dose (mg) ÷ Plasma C (mg/L) | Tissue sequestration |
| CrCl | [(140 − age) × wt] ÷ [72 × SCr] × 0.85 (♀) | Adjust elimination strategies |

#### ABCDE Triage and Decontamination

The poisoned patient is approached exactly like any emergency: **Airway with C-spine protection, Breathing, Circulation, Disability, Exposure.** Only after stabilisation does decontamination begin. **Activated charcoal** (1 g/kg, preferably within 60 minutes) binds unabsorbed drug in the gut but is contraindicated in caustic ingestion, hydrocarbons with high aspiration risk, and unprotected airways. **Gastric lavage** is rarely used beyond 1 hour post-ingestion. **Whole bowel irrigation** suits sustained-release preparations, lithium, and iron.

#### Common South African Poisonings and Antidotes

| Toxin | First-line antidote | Dose / route |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paracetamol | N-acetylcysteine | 150 mg/kg IV over 15 min, then 50 mg/kg over 4 h, then 100 mg/kg over 16 h |
| Opioids | Naloxone | 0.04–0.4 mg IV, titrated to respiration |
| Benzodiazepines | Flumazenil | 0.2 mg IV over 15 s, repeat up to 1 mg |
| Organophosphates | Atropine + Pralidoxime | 2–4 mg atropine IV q5 min + 30 mg/kg pralidoxime IV |
| Iron | Desferrioxamine | 15 mg/kg/h IV (max 80 mg/kg/24 h) |
| Methanol / ethylene glycol | Ethanol or fomepizole | Fomepizole 15 mg/kg IV load |
| Digoxin | Digoxin-specific Fab | Based on serum level or ingested dose |

#### Pharmacist's Legal and Ethical Role

- Schedule 1–6 control under the **Medicines and Related Substances Act 101 of 1965**.
- Emergency supply permitted per **Pharmacy Act 53 of 1974** scope-of-practice rules.
- **Hazardous Substances Act 15 of 1973** governs pesticides and toxic chemicals.
- Mandatory reporting to a **Poison Information Centre** (e.g., Tygerberg, Johannesburg) for notifiable exposures.
- Pharmacovigilance adverse-event reporting via the **South African Health Products Regulatory Authority (SAHPRA)**.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Context-Specific Toxicology

Paraquat remains a leading intentional poisoning agent in rural South Africa. **No effective antidote exists**; management is supportive plus **haemoperfusion within 2 hours** and avoidance of supplemental oxygen (oxygen potentiates paraquat lung fibrosis). **Aluminium phosphide** (rodenticide) releases phosphine on contact with gastric acid — treat with early gastric lavage using potassium permanganate, IV sodium bicarbonate, and supportive care. **Traditional medicine** toxicity (e.g., *Callilepis laureola* — "impila") frequently presents with fulminant hepatic failure; pharmacists must counsel against unsupervised herbal use.

#### Envenomation Protocols

Snakebite management follows **South African Snakebite Committee** guidelines: pressure immobilisation for neurotoxic elapids (e.g., black mamba, Cape cobra) and **avoidance** for cytotoxic viper bites (e.g., puff adder). Antivenom dosing is species-specific and titrated to clinical response rather than fixed milligram amounts.

#### Common Mistakes in Exam Answers

1. Giving flumazenil to a benzodiazepine-dependent patient — risk of seizures.
2. Omitting the **Rumack–Matthew nomogram** before starting N-acetylcysteine.
3. Treating organophosphate poisoning with atropine only — missing the **oxime window** (pralidoxime must run before acetylcholinesterase "ages").
4. Applying LD50 to humans — LD50 values come from animal studies and translate poorly to clinical risk.
5. Forgetting that activated charcoal does **not** bind lithium, iron, or alcohols.

#### Practice Prompts

1. A 28-year-old ingests 15 g paracetamol at 09:00. At 14:00 her paracetamol level plots above the treatment line on the Rumack–Matthew nomogram. Outline the N-acetylcysteine regimen, duration, and discharge criteria.
2. A farmworker collapses after spraying pesticides; cholinergic signs include salivation, bradycardia, and fasciculations. State the antidote sequence, why pralidoxime cannot be delayed, and the SAPC scheduling classification of atropine.
