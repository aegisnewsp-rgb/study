# Active Passive

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Active voice places the doer (subject) before the action (verb), while passive voice places the receiver of the action before the verb, using a form of **"be"** + **past participle (V3)**. The active object becomes the passive subject, and the active subject shifts into a **"by"** phrase.

- **Active:** *Ramesh writes a letter.* (Subject = Ramesh, Verb = writes, Object = letter)
- **Passive:** *A letter is written by Ramesh.* (Subject = A letter, Verb = is written, Agent = by Ramesh)
- Only **transitive verbs** (verbs taking a direct object) can be made passive. *He runs fast* has no passive form because "runs" has no object.
- The auxiliary "**be**" carries the tense — it never stays as the bare infinitive. *He wrote a letter* → *A letter **was** written by him.*

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"Object ↑ → Subject, Subject ↓ → by-phrase, be + V3 is the core"** — read the mnemonic as *Object goes up to subject position, Subject goes down to "by" position*. Three steps every time: (1) find the object, (2) promote it, (3) write *be + V3*.

| Tense (Active) | Active Form | Passive Form |
|---|---|---|
| Simple Present | writes / write | is / am / are + written |
| Simple Past | wrote | was / were + written |
| Simple Future | will write | will be + written |
| Present Perfect | has / have + written | has / have + been + written |
| Modal | can / should / must + write | can / should / must + be + written |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Transformation Rule
Every passive sentence is built from three slots: **new subject (old object) + appropriate form of "be" + V3 + (by + agent)**. The verb "be" flexes to match tense, number, and person. *Rohit is teaching the students* → *The students **are being taught** by Rohit* (Present Continuous, plural subject → "are").

#### Concept Comparison Matrix
Candidates confuse several related constructions. The table below isolates the patterns that SSC CGL examiners exploit.

| Pattern | Active Example | Correct Passive | Wrong Version (Examiner Trap) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simple Past tense | He wrote a letter. | A letter was written by him. | A letter was wrote by him. (V2 instead of V3) |
| make / let / have + obj + bare infinitive | I made him work. | He was made to work. | He was made work. (missing "to") |
| need / want + V-ing | The car needs washing. | The car needs to be washed. | The car needs washed. |
| Double object | He gave me a book. | I was given a book by him. / A book was given to me by him. | A book was given me by him. (preposition dropped) |
| Interrogative | Did he write the letter? | Was the letter written by him? | Did the letter be written by him? |

#### Standard Conversion Procedure
1. Locate the **object** of the active verb (the noun/pronoun receiving the action).
2. Shift the object to the **subject** position at the start of the passive sentence.
3. Insert the correct **form of "be"** matching the original tense and the new subject's number.
4. Replace the active verb with its **V3 form**.
5. Add **"by + original subject"** if the agent is relevant.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem
**Question:** Choose the correct passive form of: *"The gardener waters the plants every morning."*
(a) The plants are watered by the gardener every morning.
(b) The plants were watered by the gardener every morning.
(c) The plants is watered by the gardener every morning.
(d) The plants have been watered by the gardener every morning.

#### Solution:
- Step 1: Object = *the plants*; Subject = *the gardener*; Verb = *waters*.
- Step 2: Promote *the plants* to subject position.
- Step 3: Original tense = Simple Present (habitual "every morning"). The new subject *plants* is plural, so the auxiliary is **are**, not "is".
- Step 4: V3 of *water* = **watered**.
- Step 5: Append *by the gardener*.

Final answer: **(a) The plants are watered by the gardener every morning.**

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Option (c) tempts students who ignore subject-verb agreement — *plants* is plural, so "is" is wrong. Option (b) "were watered" shifts the tense to Simple Past; the marker "every morning" confirms habitual Present. Read the time marker before choosing the auxiliary.

#### Impersonal Passive
General statements without a specific agent often use *It is said / believed / reported / thought + that-clause*.
*People say that he is honest.* → *It is said that he is honest.*
*Everyone knows that the earth is round.* → *It is known that the earth is round.*

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Exception Patterns

| Construction | Active | Passive | Rule Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Present Continuous | She is writing a poem. | A poem is being written by her. | Continuous needs "being" + V3 |
| Past Perfect | He had finished the work. | The work had been finished by him. | "been" + V3 |
| Future Perfect | They will have built the house. | The house will have been built by them. | "been" sits between "have" and V3 |
| Modal Perfect | She should have sent the mail. | The mail should have been sent by her. | "have been" splits after modal |
| Reporting verbs | We believe him to be honest. | He is believed to be honest. | Infinitive → "to be + V3" with raising |

#### Advanced Traps Worth Memorising
1. **Stative verbs resist passivisation.** Verbs of possession (*own, possess*), sensation (*taste, smell*), and emotion (*love, prefer*) generally do not take a passive object in SSC-level items. *He owns a car* has no clean passive.
2. **Preposition retention matters.** *The children laughed at the clown* → *The clown was laughed at by the children*. The preposition "at" must travel with the verb.
3. **Sentences with reflexive or reciprocal objects fail.** *She hurt herself* cannot be converted because the object refers back to the subject.
4. **"Get" passive.** Informal English uses *got/was getting + V3* (e.g., *He got arrested*). SSC CGL keys stick to "be" + V3.
5. **Negation and question tags.** *They do not play cricket* → *Cricket is not played by them*. Place "not" immediately after the auxiliary "be".

#### Connections to Adjacent Topics
Active–Passive feeds directly into **Sentence Improvement**, **Spotting Errors**, and **Cloze Test** items. The same "be + V3" diagnostic catches errors in participle usage elsewhere — for instance, spotting "The work is **do**" instead of "is **done**". Mastering the auxiliary-by-tense map therefore speeds up three different question types in the English paper.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts
1. Convert: *"The jury has been considering the evidence since morning."* Decide whether "is being considered" or "has been being considered" is grammatically defensible, and explain why SSC CGL avoids this construction.
2. Convert: *"Someone must have stolen my wallet."* Identify the impersonal passive equivalent and the agent phrase — note why "by someone" is normally dropped in the converted form.

> 💡 **High-Yield Revision Hook:** When the active verb is a *continuous* tense, the passive carries **"being" + V3**; when it is *perfect*, the passive carries **"been" + V3**; when it is *perfect continuous*, SSC items usually convert only to simple perfect passive to avoid the clunky "been being + V3" form.
