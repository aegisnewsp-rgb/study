# Inference and Must Be True — LSAT Logical Reasoning

Inference items ask which conclusion follows necessarily from the passage. The LSAT tests whether the test-taker can distinguish conclusions that must be true from those that could be true, probably true, or false. Inference items are similar to sufficient-assumption items but require only that the conclusion follow from the given information.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### What inference items test

- **Must be true:** the choice that follows necessarily from the passage. The strongest inference.
- **Most likely true:** the choice that is most supported by the passage.
- **Could be true:** the choice that is consistent with the passage but not required.
- **Cannot be true:** the choice that contradicts the passage.

#### One-pass drill

1. Read the passage carefully. Note the explicit facts and the reasonable implications.
2. For each choice, ask: "does this follow from the passage?"
3. Eliminate choices that contradict the passage, that add unsupported information, or that are too strong.

#### Examiner traps

- Picking a choice that is true in real life but not supported by the passage.
- Picking a choice that is too strong ("must" or "always" when the passage supports "could" or "often").
- Picking a choice that requires new information.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Strategy for "must be true" items

1. **Identify the explicit facts** in the passage.
2. **Identify the reasonable implications** (what must follow from those facts).
3. **Evaluate each choice:** does it follow? Is it too strong? Too weak?
4. **Pick the choice that is most strongly supported** by the passage.

#### Strategy for "most strongly supported" items

1. **Identify the explicit facts** and the reasonable implications.
2. **Evaluate each choice:** how strongly is it supported?
3. **Pick the choice that is most directly supported** by the passage.

#### Strategy for "could be true" items

1. **Identify what the passage rules out.**
2. **Find the choice that is consistent with the passage** but not necessarily required.
3. **Eliminate choices that contradict** any part of the passage.

#### Common patterns

- **Numerical inference:** "the passage says X. Y is greater than X. Therefore Y is greater than X." Direct calculation.
- **Causal inference:** "the passage says A causes B. Therefore B is evidence of A." Careful with directionality.
- **Categorical inference:** "the passage says all X are Y. Z is X. Therefore Z is Y." Categorical reasoning.

#### Practice routine

- Take 10 inference items. For each, identify the type (must, most, could, cannot).
- Time yourself: under 1.5 minutes per item.
- Re-take the missed items. Note the pattern of error.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How inference items connect to the other LR skills

- **Assumption items** ask for the missing link; inference items ask what must follow.
- **Strengthen items** add evidence; inference items use only the given evidence.
- **Method items** describe how the author argues; inference items describe what the author concludes.

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"Must be true means proved."** Must be true means it follows from the passage; it does not need to be proved in the real world.
- **"Could be true means probably true."** Could be true means it is consistent with the passage; it does not need to be the most likely.
- **"Inference items are about reading between the lines."** Inference items are about following the passage carefully; the conclusion is in the passage, not in the reader's view.

#### Exam technique

- For "must be true" items, eliminate choices that add unsupported information.
- For "most strongly supported" items, pick the choice that is most directly supported.
- For "could be true" items, eliminate choices that contradict the passage.
- Use the platform's flag-for-review function for items you cannot answer.
