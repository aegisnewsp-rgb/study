# Cost Theory

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Cost Theory maps how a firm's spending rises, falls, and reaccelerates as output expands. RBI Grade B Phase-II Economics tests this 3% weightage area through cost-curve MCQs, shut-down numericals, and a banking-cost application angle.

- **Total Cost (TC)** = Total Fixed Cost (TFC) + Total Variable Cost (TVC), measured in ₹.
- **Average Cost (AC)** = TC / Q, in ₹ per unit; **AC = AFC + AVC**.
- **Marginal Cost (MC)** = ΔTC / ΔQ, the cost of producing one more unit in ₹.
- **Long Run:** all inputs variable, LRAC is the envelope of SRAC curves.
- **Shut-down rule:** firm operates if P ≥ min AVC; exits when P < min AVC.
- **Break-even:** P = AC at AC's minimum.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"MC cuts at the Bottom, AVC lies Below AC"** — MC always intersects AVC and AC at their *minimum* points, and AC sits above AVC because the gap equals AFC/Q.

| Cost term | Formula | Shape in SR | SI / monetary unit |
|---|---|---|---|
| TFC | — (given) | Horizontal line | ₹ (lakh/crore in macro Qs) |
| TVC | rises with Q | Slope steepens then flattens | ₹ |
| AFC | TFC / Q | Strictly falling hyperbola | ₹ per unit |
| AVC | TVC / Q | U-shaped | ₹ per unit |
| AC | TC / Q (or AFC + AVC) | U-shaped, flatter than AVC at min | ₹ per unit |
| MC | ΔTC / ΔQ | U-shaped, cuts AVC & AC at minima | ₹ per additional unit |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Cost Identities

The seven accounting identities behind every cost-curve question in the RBI paper are: TC = TFC + TVC; AC = TC / Q; AFC = TFC / Q; AVC = TVC / Q; AC = AFC + AVC; MC = ΔTC / ΔQ; and the geometric rule that **AC = AVC + AFC/Q**. Because AFC/Q → 0 as Q grows, AC and AVC converge asymptotically but never touch — AC always lies above AVC by exactly the falling AFC gap.

#### Why Short-Run Curves Are U-Shaped

Short-run curves arise from one fixed factor (typically capital) and one variable factor (labour). AFC declines continuously because a constant ₹ is spread over more units. AVC, and therefore MC, are U-shaped for two reasons: initially **division of labour and task specialisation** raise the marginal product of labour, then the **law of diminishing marginal returns** sets in as more workers crowd a fixed plant. The crossover — MC = AVC at minimum AVC — is the central graph tested.

#### Long-Run Average Cost and Returns to Scale

In the long run, every input is variable, so firms pick the plant size that minimises AC at any Q. The **LRAC is the envelope** of all possible SRACs, touching each SRAC at its minimum but never rising above the lowest-cost SRAC for that output. Its shape encodes **economies of scale** (LRAC falls — labour specialisation, bulk purchasing, technical indivisibilities, marketing economies, financial economies), **constant returns** (LRAC flat), and **diseconomies of scale** (LRAC rises — managerial hierarchies, communication bottlenecks, principal–agent problems).

#### Concept Comparison Matrix

| Concept | Short Run | Long Run |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed inputs | At least one (plant, capital) | None — all inputs variable |
| Exit decision | Operate if P ≥ min AVC | Exit if P < AC (no fixed cost to cover) |
| Supply curve of firm | Portion of MC above min AVC | Portion of LRMC above min LRAC |
| Cost curve relevant | SRAC, SRMC | LRAC, LRMC |
| Shape driver | Law of diminishing returns to variable factor | Returns to scale |

#### Shut-Down vs Break-Even vs Exit

- **Break-even point:** TR = TC, i.e. P = AC at AC's minimum. Economic profit is zero.
- **Shut-down point:** P = min AVC. Below this, the firm cannot cover variable costs.
- **Exit (long run):** P < min LRAC. With no fixed costs in the long run, a persistent price below AC forces exit.

> 📌 **Formula Check:** All monetary values must be in the same currency unit (₹, lakh, or crore) before you compute MC = ΔTC / ΔQ; mixing scales is the most common arithmetic error.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** A firm has TFC = ₹2,000 and TVC = 200Q + 2Q² (TC in ₹, Q in units). The market price is ₹260 per unit. Calculate (i) AC at Q = 20, (ii) MC at Q = 20, and (iii) state whether the firm should continue operating in the short run.

#### Solution:
- TC = 2,000 + 200Q + 2Q² → at Q = 20, TC = 2,000 + 4,000 + 800 = ₹6,800.
- AC = TC / Q = 6,800 / 20 = ₹340 per unit.
- MC = dTC / dQ = 200 + 4Q → at Q = 20, MC = 200 + 80 = ₹280 per unit.
- Profit per unit = P − AC = 260 − 340 = −₹80 (loss). Loss = ₹80 × 20 = ₹1,600.
- Minimum AVC occurs where MC = AVC. AVC = (200Q + 2Q²)/Q = 200 + 2Q. Setting MC = AVC: 200 + 4Q = 200 + 2Q → Q = 0 (theoretical only). Recheck: MC = AVC gives 200 + 4Q = 200 + 2Q ⇒ 2Q = 0 ⇒ Q = 0, so the algebraic minimum lies at the boundary. Comparing P = ₹260 to AVC at small Q: AVC = 200 + 2Q is below 260 for all Q < 30. Hence **P > AVC throughout the relevant range**, so the firm **continues to operate in the short run**, absorbing part of its fixed cost.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students equate "loss" with "shut-down". A loss-making firm should still operate when P ≥ min AVC, because shutting down would lose the entire TFC = ₹2,000. The shut-down rule uses AVC, not AC.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Accounting vs Economic Cost

**Accounting profit** uses only **explicit costs** — wages, rent, raw materials, interest paid. **Economic profit** deducts **implicit costs** too: the opportunity cost of owner's time, owner-supplied capital (foregone interest), and unpaid family labour. A farm family earning a normal accounting profit may register **zero economic profit**, signalling that resources are only just sufficient to stay in their current use. RBI macro-finance questions on bank profitability (Net Interest Margin) often require economic-cost reasoning because equity capital has an implicit cost.

#### Internal vs External Economies of Scale

| Type | Source | Affects | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Internal economies | Inside the plant/firm | LRAC of the expanding firm only | Bulk discount on inputs, in-house R&D |
| External economies | Industry-wide spillovers | LRAC of all firms in the industry | Skilled-labour pool, ancillary suppliers, shared infrastructure |
| Internal diseconomies | Managerial/coordination limits | LRAC of the expanding firm only | Layered hierarchy, communication lag |
| External diseconomies | Industry congestion | LRAC of all firms in the industry | Input price inflation, traffic congestion |

#### Marginal Cost of Funds — Banking Application

RBI policy papers and Phase-II applied questions use the **Marginal Cost of Funds (MCoF)** = weighted marginal cost of deposits, borrowings, and equity (post-tax). Unlike a firm's textbook MC, MCoF is a **weighted average**, includes a **minimum investment-return spread** under the liquidity adjustment facility framework, and feeds into base-rate calculation. Treat this as a specialised cost-of-capital measure, not the production-MC from micro theory.

#### Advanced Traps and Exceptions

1. **Envelope confusion:** LRAC is *not* an average of SRACs; it is the **lower envelope**. Drawing LRAC above any SRAC at non-tangency points is wrong.
2. **AFC never reaches zero mathematically**, only asymptotically; AC therefore never equals AVC at finite Q.
3. **Sunk costs are not fixed costs** for forward-looking decisions; only *avoidable* fixed costs matter for exit.
4. **MC = AC at AC's minimum**, not at the profit-maximising output (which is MC = MR).
5. **Public finance trap:** "Marginal Cost of Public Funds" (MCPF) in cost–benefit analysis is the welfare cost of raising ₹1 of tax revenue, not a firm's MC; MCPF > 1 implies deadweight loss.
6. **Learning-curve shift:** Repeated production lowers the entire LRAC over time — the envelope itself drifts downward, distinct from scale economies.

#### Exam Strategy for RBI Grade B

Cost Theory contributes about 3% of the Economics paper. Expect 1–2 direct conceptual MCQs (cost-curve shapes, MC–AVC–AC relationships) and 2–3 applied MCQs (banking cost of funds, break-even vs shut-down). In a 25-question Economics section, allocate roughly 1.5–2 minutes per cost-theory question and draw the curves on the rough sheet before committing — visual anchoring prevents the classic MC–AC crossover mistake.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. A firm has TFC = ₹5,000 and TVC = 50Q + Q². Price falls from ₹150 to ₹90. Determine break-even output, shut-down output, and whether the firm operates in the short run at each price. Show calculations in ₹.
2. Two banks report identical accounting returns on equity. Bank A holds 12% core capital, Bank B holds 8%. Using economic-cost reasoning, explain why Bank A may be economically more profitable. Relate to **Marginal Cost of Funds** and RBI's capital-adequacy framework.
