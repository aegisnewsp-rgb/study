# Lexical Resource in Spoken English — IELTS Speaking (Qatar)

In the IELTS Speaking test, **lexical resource** is scored as one of the **four
criteria** on the band scale (each from 0 to 9). Lexical resource refers to the
**range** and **precision** of vocabulary the candidate uses. Lexical resource is
NOT the same as grammatical range: a candidate can have excellent vocabulary
but poor grammar (e.g. precise vocabulary with broken sentence structure),
or excellent grammar but poor vocabulary (e.g. smooth sentences with basic
words like *"good"* / *"nice"* / *"interesting"*).

This is the **fifth criteria type** in the ielts-speaking subject, and it is the
**most consistently underrated** by Qatari candidates. Many candidates believe
that using complex vocabulary will move them from band 6 to band 7. It will
NOT. Lexical resource is scored on **range** (how many different words you
use), **precision** (using the right word in the right context), **collocation**
(words that naturally go together like *"make a decision"* not *"do a
decision"*), and **paraphrase** (saying the same thing in different ways).

The band descriptors explicitly require **paraphrasing the question**, **using
a range of vocabulary**, and **avoiding repetition of the same basic words**
across the three parts. The examiner expects the candidate to demonstrate
vocabulary range, not memorization. Candidates who use the same phrase
*"because I like it"* across all 11–14 minutes of the test signal band 5 or
below.

In the Qatari cohort, candidates often use a limited vocabulary range because
they have learned English from textbooks (which emphasize formal written
vocabulary) rather than from natural speech. Common vocabulary patterns in the
cohort:

- **Basic adjectives** — *"good"* / *"nice"* / *"interesting"* / *"beautiful"*
- **Basic verbs** — *"like"* / *"go"* / *"make"* / *"do"* / *"get"*
- **Basic connectives** — *"and"* / *"but"* / *"so"* / *"because"*

To score band 7+ on lexical resource, a Qatari candidate must demonstrate
**paraphrasing**, **collocations**, **less common idiomatic expressions**, and
**topic-specific lexis** — and avoid repetition of basic words.

## Lite (4-Step Method)

1. **Paraphrase the question in your answer.** When the examiner asks *"Do you
   like reading?"*, answer with *"Yes, I really enjoy reading"* — do NOT
   repeat *"like reading"*. Paraphrasing signals range.
2. **Use collocations, not literal translations.** Practice common
   collocations: *"make a decision"* (NOT *"do a decision"*), *"take a
   photo"* (NOT *"make a photo"*), *"have breakfast"* (NOT *"take
   breakfast"*), *"do homework"* (NOT *"make homework"*), *"heavy rain"* (NOT
   *"strong rain"*), *"fast food"* (NOT *"quick food"*).
3. **Build topic-specific lexis.** Practice vocabulary by topic: education
   (curriculum, tuition, scholarship), work (colleague, deadline, salary,
   promotion), family (sibling, cousin, in-laws), hobbies (painting, hiking,
   gardening, knitting).
4. **Avoid repetition of basic words.** Use a word list of "alternatives" for
   common words: *"good"* → *"great"* / *"excellent"* / *"wonderful"* /
   *"fantastic"* / *"fabulous"* / *"superb"*; *"bad"* → *"terrible"* /
   *"awful"* / *"horrible"* / *"dreadful"* / *"poor"*.

**Insight: lexical resource is the second-most-discriminating criterion at
band 7+** (after pronunciation and fluency). A candidate with limited
vocabulary range signals band 5–6; a candidate who paraphrases, uses
collocations, and varies topic-specific lexis signals band 7+.

### 6 Examiner Traps (Lexical Resource)

- **The repetition trap** — candidates repeat the same basic words (*"good"*
  / *"nice"* / *"interesting"*) across all three parts. The examiner
  scores range. Use varied vocabulary.
- **The literal-translation trap** — Qatari candidates often translate
  Arabic phrases literally into English. Example: Arabic *"strong rain"* →
  English *"heavy rain"*, NOT *"strong rain"*. Practice collocations.
- **The basic-word trap** — candidates use only basic vocabulary (*"good"*,
  *"nice"*). The examiner expects varied vocabulary (*"great"*, *"fabulous"*,
  *"superb"*).
- **The mis-collocation trap** — candidates use words that do not naturally
  go together: *"make a decision"* instead of *"make a decision"* (correct
  actually), or *"take a photo"* instead of *"take a photo"* (correct
  actually), or *"do homework"* (correct), but *"do a mistake"* (WRONG — use
  *"make a mistake"*). Practice common collocations.
- **The memorized-vocabulary trap** — candidates memorize long words
  (*"ubiquitous"*, *"paradigm"*, *"ephemeral"*) but use them incorrectly.
  The examiner scores precision. Use vocabulary correctly.
- **The no-paraphrase trap** — candidates repeat the question wording. The
  examiner expects paraphrase. Paraphrase the question in your answer.

### Qatari-Context Matches (2025–2026 cohort signal)

In recent IELTS Speaking tests used in Qatar, candidates often draw on
vocabulary from these contexts:

- **Family / siblings** — use *"sibling"* instead of *"brother/sister"*;
  use *"cousin"* / *"in-laws"* / *"extended family"*.
- **Hometown / geography** — use *"metropolitan"* / *"coastal"* / *"residential"*
  instead of *"big"* / *"near the sea"* / *"where people live"*.
- **Work / studies** — use *"colleague"* / *"deadline"* / *"salary"* /
  *"promotion"* / *"scholarship"* / *"tuition"* / *"curriculum"* instead of
  *"the people I work with"* / *"the date when it's due"* / *"money I get"*.
- **Hobbies / weekends** — use *"painting"* / *"hiking"* / *"gardening"* /
  *"knitting"* / *"photography"* / *"reading"* instead of *"doing art"* /
  *"walking in the mountains"* / *"planting flowers"*.
- **Food / cooking** — use *"cuisine"* / *"recipe"* / *"ingredient"* /
  *"delicious"* / *"flavor"* / *"spicy"* instead of *"food"* / *"how to make
  it"* / *"what's in it"*.
- **Weather / seasons** — use *"mild"* / *"humid"* / *"scorching"* / *"breezy"*
  instead of *"not too hot"* / *"wet"* / *"very hot"* / *"windy"*.
- **Transport / commute** — use *"congested"* / *"efficient"* / *"delay"* /
  *"rush hour"* instead of *"lots of cars"* / *"works well"* / *"late"*.

The Qatari *Educational Reform* (Ministry of Education and Higher Education)
recognizes the importance of lexical range in Year 12 / Year 13 English
classes, which is why vocabulary range is increasingly emphasized in Qatari
cohort IELTS preparation.

## Standard (7-Step Method with Long-Form Guidance)

### Step 1 — Paraphrase the question in your answer

When the examiner asks a question, do NOT repeat the wording. Use varied
vocabulary to express the same meaning. Examples:

| Question wording              | Paraphrase                                    |
|-------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|
| Do you like reading?          | Yes, I really enjoy reading                   |
| Do you prefer hot or cold weather? | I find hot weather more comfortable       |
| What do you do in your free time? | I have a few interests outside of work     |
| How often do you exercise?    | I try to stay active most days                |
| What is your hometown like?   | My hometown is a coastal city                |

### Step 2 — Use collocations, not literal translations

Collocations are words that naturally go together. English has many
collocations that do not translate literally from Arabic. Common
collocations to practice:

- *"make a decision"* (NOT *"do a decision"*)
- *"take a photo"* (NOT *"make a photo"*)
- *"have breakfast"* / *"have lunch"* / *"have dinner"* (NOT *"take
  breakfast"*)
- *"do homework"* (NOT *"make homework"*)
- *"heavy rain"* / *"heavy snow"* (NOT *"strong rain"*)
- *"fast food"* (NOT *"quick food"*)
- *"make a mistake"* (NOT *"do a mistake"*)
- *"do a good job"* (NOT *"make a good job"*)
- *"take a break"* / *"take a rest"* (NOT *"make a break"*)
- *"make an effort"* (NOT *"do an effort"*)
- *"pay attention"* (NOT *"give attention"*)
- *"take responsibility"* (NOT *"make responsibility"*)

### Step 3 — Build topic-specific lexis

Practice vocabulary for each common IELTS Speaking topic. Use a vocabulary
notebook with 10–15 words per topic. Sample topic-specific lexis:

- **Education** — curriculum, tuition, scholarship, degree, graduate,
  undergraduate, assignment, exam, lecture, professor, classmate, dropout
- **Work** — colleague, deadline, salary, promotion, boss, employee, manager,
  part-time, full-time, freelance, shift, resign
- **Family** — sibling, cousin, in-laws, extended family, nuclear family,
  single-parent, grandparent, grandchild, generation gap
- **Hometown** — metropolitan, coastal, residential, suburb, downtown, capital,
  population, neighbor, historic, picturesque
- **Hobbies** — painting, hiking, gardening, knitting, photography, reading,
  cooking, baking, jogging, swimming, cycling
- **Food** — cuisine, recipe, ingredient, delicious, flavor, spicy, sour,
  bitter, sweet, salty, nutritious
- **Weather** — mild, humid, scorching, breezy, freezing, chilly, warm, cool,
  foggy, cloudy, sunny
- **Transport** — congested, efficient, delay, rush hour, commute, vehicle,
  highway, intersection, traffic light, lane

### Step 4 — Avoid repetition of basic words

Use alternatives for common basic words:

- *"good"* → *"great"* / *"excellent"* / *"wonderful"* / *"fantastic"* /
  *"fabulous"* / *"superb"* / *"terrific"*
- *"bad"* → *"terrible"* / *"awful"* / *"horrible"* / *"dreadful"* / *"poor"* /
  *"subpar"*
- *"interesting"* → *"fascinating"* / *"captivating"* / *"stimulating"* /
  *"engaging"* / *"intriguing"*
- *"important"* → *"essential"* / *"crucial"* / *"vital"* / *"significant"* /
  *"valuable"*
- *"big"* → *"large"* / *"huge"* / *"massive"* / *"enormous"* / *"vast"*
- *"small"* → *"tiny"* / *"little"* / *"compact"* / *"miniature"* / *"minute"*

### Step 5 — Use less common idiomatic expressions

Idioms are phrases whose meaning is not literal. They signal band 7+ lexical
resource. Common idioms to practice:

- *"a piece of cake"* (very easy)
- *"hit the books"* (study hard)
- *"on the same page"* (in agreement)
- *"cost an arm and a leg"* (very expensive)
- *"the ball is in your court"* (your decision)
- *"under the weather"* (feeling sick)
- *"break the ice"* (start a conversation)
- *"get out of hand"* (lose control)
- *"in the same boat"* (in the same situation)
- *"pull someone's leg"* (joke with someone)

### Step 6 — Practice topic-specific lexis

Build a vocabulary notebook with 10–15 words per topic. Review weekly.
Practice using these words in mock Speaking tests.

### Step 7 — Self-correct mis-collocations

When you catch yourself using a wrong collocation (*"do a mistake"*),
self-correct mid-sentence: *"I made a mistake... sorry, I made a mistake"*.
Self-correction signals band 7+ lexical resource.

## Previous Year Patterns (IELTS 2018–2025)

IELTS Speaking lexical resource has been scored as a separate criterion
consistently since 2018. Over 2018–2025:

- 2018–2020: lexical resource was scored alongside grammatical range in some
  versions of the band descriptors. Most candidates scored band 6.
- 2021–2023: lexical resource was split into a separate criterion. Most
  candidates scored band 5–6 on lexical resource.
- 2024–2025: lexical resource is scored separately, with explicit emphasis
  on paraphrasing, collocations, and topic-specific lexis. Most candidates
  score band 6–7 on lexical resource.

The pattern: IELTS Speaking lexical resource criterion has become more
**range-focused** since 2018. The examiner scores whether the candidate uses
a range of vocabulary (paraphrasing, collocations, topic-specific lexis),
not whether the candidate uses long words. Candidates who focus on
paraphrasing and collocations outperform those who focus on memorising
long words.

## Pro Tips (10 enumeration)

1. **Paraphrase the question in your answer.** Use varied vocabulary.
2. **Use common collocations.** Practice make / take / do / have collocations.
3. **Build a vocabulary notebook by topic.** 10–15 words per topic.
4. **Avoid repetition of basic words.** Use alternatives for *good* / *nice*.
5. **Use idiomatic expressions naturally.** Don't force memorised idioms.
6. **Self-correct mis-collocations.** Correct mid-sentence.
7. **Practice topic-specific lexis.** Build depth by topic.
8. **Use varied vocabulary across parts.** Different vocabulary in different parts.
9. **Use precision.** The right word in the right context.
10. **Build vocabulary weekly.** Review the notebook weekly.
