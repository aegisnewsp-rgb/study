# Oxygen Therapy and Respiratory Care

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Single fact the DOH examiner will test first: which device, which flow, which target.

Start every oxygen decision with the **ABG or SpO₂ target**, not with the device.

- **Most patients**: aim SpO₂ **94–98 %** (PaO₂ ≥ 75 mmHg).
- **COPD / risk of CO₂ retention**: aim SpO₂ **88–92 %**; start low, use a Venturi mask.
- Hypoxaemia cut-off = **SpO₂ < 94 %**, or **PaO₂ < 60 mmHg (8 kPa)**.

| Device | Flow (L/min) | Approximate FiO₂ |
|---|---|---|
| Nasal cannula | 1–6 | 0.24–0.44 |
| Simple face mask | 5–10 | 0.35–0.60 |
| Venturi mask | 2–15 | 0.24–0.60 (precise) |
| Non-rebreather | 10–15 | 0.60–0.85 |

**FiO₂ rule for nasal cannula**: FiO₂ ≈ 0.21 + (0.04 × L/min). At 4 L/min ≈ 0.37.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"Low for COPD, High for Heart."** Use Venturi 24–28 % in chronic CO₂ retainers; reserve 100 % for arrest, anaphylaxis, and major haemorrhage.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Mechanism the marker rewards: matching device physiology to the patient's gas-exchange problem.

#### Targets and the reason behind them

Healthy lungs keep PaO₂ around 80–100 mmHg on room air (FiO₂ 0.21). When PaO₂ drops below 60 mmHg, tissue oxygen delivery falls steeply because haemoglobin saturation is on the steep part of the oxyhaemoglobin dissociation curve. The first task is to decide **what SpO₂ you want**, then pick the device that gets there without overshooting.

Most acutely unwell adults tolerate SpO₂ 94–98 %. COPD patients rely partly on hypoxic drive; pushing FiO₂ too high washes out CO₂ and precipitates type 2 respiratory failure. The 88–92 % target leaves a safety margin above the desaturation cliff (≈ 88 %) while protecting the ventilatory drive.

#### Choosing a delivery device

| Device | Flow range | FiO₂ delivered | Best indication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nasal cannula | 1–6 L/min | 0.24–0.44 | Mild hypoxia, eating, talking |
| Simple mask | 5–10 L/min | 0.35–0.60 | Moderate hypoxia, mouth-breathers |
| Venturi mask | 2–15 L/min | 0.24–0.60 (fixed) | COPD — predictable FiO₂ |
| Non-rebreather (NRB) | 10–15 L/min | 0.60–0.85 | Severe hypoxia pre-intubation |
| HFNC | up to 60 L/min | 0.21–1.00 | Acute hypoxaemic respiratory failure |
| CPAP / BiPAP | Device-set | Variable | Pulmonary oedema, COPD, OSA |

Flows above 4 L/min via nasal cannula, or any flow through a tracheostomy, **need humidification**; dried mucosa thickens secretions and blocks airways.

#### Comparing things students confuse

| Confusion | Correction |
|---|---|
| "NRB delivers 100 % O₂" | True FiO₂ ≈ 0.60–0.85; depends on mask seal and reservoir refill |
| "SpO₂ reflects CO₂" | SpO₂ measures oxygenation only — confirm PaCO₂ on ABG when concerned |
| "CPAP = BiPAP" | CPAP = one continuous pressure; BiPAP = IPAP (higher) + EPAP (lower) |
| "Type 1 vs Type 2 failure" | Type 1: PaO₂ < 60 mmHg, PaCO₂ normal/low. Type 2: PaCO₂ > 50 mmHg with acidosis |

#### Cylinder duration arithmetic

E-cylinder in the US system:

#### Hours remaining = (Gauge pressure [psi] × 1.56) / Flow [L/min]

UK CD cylinder:

#### Hours = ((Pressure [bar] − 0.5) × 1.7) / Flow [L/min]

#### Worked problem

A 70 kg man with a COPD exacerbation has SpO₂ 84 % on room air, RR 28, pH 7.31, PaCO₂ 58 mmHg, PaO₂ 52 mmHg. Choose the device, flow, and target.

Step 1 — Classify failure. PaO₂ < 60 mmHg **and** PaCO₂ > 50 mmHg with acidotic pH → **type 2 respiratory failure**.

Step 2 — Set target. COPD at risk of CO₂ retention → **SpO₂ 88–92 %**, not the general 94–98 %.

Step 3 — Pick device. A Venturi mask gives a **fixed** FiO₂ and prevents accidental overdose. Start at **24 % (FiO₂ 0.24) at 2 L/min**; titrate upward by 2 % increments if SpO₂ remains < 88 %, checking ABG at 30–60 min.

Step 4 — Reassess. Repeat ABG: target PaO₂ ≥ 60 mmHg **and** PaCO₂ trending toward 45 mmHg without pH falling below 7.25.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** The marker is watching for two specific errors: (a) starting at 15 L/min via NRB because the SpO₂ "looks bad" — this abolishes hypoxic drive; (b) titrating to SpO₂ 98 % "to be safe" — the safe range here is 88–92 %.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Edge cases the marker sets when the obvious answer is wrong.

#### The P/F ratio and ARDS stratification

PaO₂/FiO₂ ratio stratifies ARDS severity on the Berlin definition:

| Category | P/F ratio |
|---|---|
| Mild ARDS | 200–300 mmHg |
| Moderate | 100–200 mmHg |
| Severe | < 100 mmHg |

P/F is dimensionless because both numerator and denominator are pressures in the same units. The ratio is only valid when measured on a ventilator with PEEP ≥ 5 cm H₂O; spontaneous-breathing values are softer indicators.

#### Alveolar gas equation in practice

PAO₂ = FiO₂ × (Patm − PH₂O) − PaCO₂ / RQ. With Patm = 760 mmHg, PH₂O = 47 mmHg, RQ = 0.8:

PAO₂ = FiO₂ × 713 − (1.25 × PaCO₂). The A–a gradient (PAO₂ − PaO₂) should be < 15 mmHg in a healthy young adult; rising A–a gradient on a fixed FiO₂ signals worsening V/Q mismatch even when SpO₂ looks acceptable.

#### Oxygen toxicity — two distinct syndromes

- **Paul Bert effect (CNS)**: seizures and CNS toxicity at high partial pressures, classically seen with hyperbaric oxygen or brief 100 % exposures.
- **Lorrain Smith effect (pulmonary)**: prolonged FiO₂ > 0.60 damages alveolar epithelium, causes absorptive atelectasis (nitrogen washout collapses alveoli), and produces diffuse alveolar damage after ~24–48 h.

Keep FiO₂ ≤ 0.60 whenever the SpO₂ target allows; titrate PEEP first, not FiO₂.

#### Advanced traps

1. **Tracheostomy patient on nasal cannula**: nasal route is anatomically bypassed; FiO₂ at the trachea is lower than predicted. Switch to a tracheostomy mask or collar.
2. **Carbon monoxide poisoning**: pulse oximetry reads falsely normal because carboxyhaemoglobin absorbs light similarly to oxyhaemoglobin. Use co-oximetry; deliver 100 % O₂ to shorten CO half-life from ~5 h to ~90 min.
3. **Cluster headache**: high-flow 100 % O₂ via NRB at 12 L/min for 15 min aborts attacks; this is a rare therapeutic indication for near-maximal FiO₂ in a non-hypoxic patient.
4. **Palliative ceiling of care**: drop the target to SpO₂ ≥ 90 % or use symptom-driven oxygen only; do not escalate to NIV if the ceiling is ward-based care.
5. **Cylinder transport off-ICU**: miscalculating E-cylinder duration — at 15 L/min via NRB, a full E-cylinder (≈ 2000 psi) lasts ≈ 2000 × 1.56 / 15 ≈ 208 min ≈ 3.5 h. Always round down.
6. **HFNC vs NIV in COVID-type hypoxaemia**: HFNC at 30–60 L/min with FiO₂ 0.60–0.80 often delays or avoids intubation in acute hypoxaemic respiratory failure without hypercapnia.

> 🔁 **Boundary reminder:** a "normal" SpO₂ of 97 % does not exclude type 2 failure — oxygenation and ventilation are separate axes. Always correlate SpO₂ with the clinical picture, ETCO₂, or ABG before declaring a patient stable.
