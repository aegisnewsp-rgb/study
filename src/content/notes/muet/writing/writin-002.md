# Task 1 — Writing an Information Transfer Letter

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review

Task 1 requires you to interpret a graph, table, chart or diagram and transfer the information into a **150–200-word formal letter**. It accounts for approximately **30% of the Writing paper**. Your job is to describe, select and organise visual information accurately. You should not give opinions, guess reasons for the pattern or argue for a position unless the prompt explicitly asks for that.

The visual can show:
- **Line graphs:** trends over time
- **Bar charts:** comparisons between categories
- **Pie charts:** proportions of a whole
- **Tables:** structured rows and columns
- **Diagrams:** processes or structures

Identify the main trend, key figures, noticeable features and useful comparisons.

The prompt can require a complaint letter, application letter, request for information or letter to the editor. A basic layout contains your address, date, recipient's details, salutation, opening purpose, organised body, closing request and formal sign-off. Use **“Yours faithfully”** with “Dear Sir or Madam” and **“Yours sincerely”** with a named recipient.

Use accurate data language:
- Increase: *rose, climbed, grew*
- Sharp increase: *surged, jumped dramatically*
- Decrease: *fell, declined, dropped*
- Sharp decrease: *plummeted, nosedived*
- Little change: *remained stable, stayed constant*
- Extremes: *peaked at, reached a trough*

Paraphrase the task rather than copying long stretches of the question. Do not report every figure; select significant points. Keep the register formal, avoid contractions and stay within 150–200 words. During practice, complete graph responses within 25 minutes, count the words and build a phrase bank for trends, comparisons and generalisations.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study


Task 1 of the MUET Writing paper tests your ability to interpret visual data and present it in formal letter format. You are given a graph, table, chart, or diagram, and you must write a **150–200 word** formal letter that accurately conveys the information shown. This task accounts for approximately 30% of your Writing paper marks, and because it is more structured than Task 2, it is frequently the easier task to score well in with the right preparation.

#### Understanding the Task

The key word in "information transfer" is *transfer* — you are moving information from a visual format into written text. You are **not** expected to:

- Give opinions about the data
- Explain why the data looks the way it does (unless explicitly asked)
- Argue for or against any position

Your job is purely to **describe, select, and organise** the information from the visual stimulus in a clear, accurate, and logically structured letter.

#### Types of Visual Stimuli

You can encounter any of the following:

- **Line graphs** — show trends over time (e.g., temperature changes, sales figures)
- **Bar charts** — compare quantities across categories (e.g., preferred modes of transport)
- **Pie charts** — show proportions or percentages of a whole (e.g., household budget allocation)
- **Tables** — present structured data in rows and columns (e.g., exam results by subject)
- **Diagrams** — illustrate a process or structure (e.g., the water cycle, a factory process)

When reading the visual, ask yourself: What is the main trend? What are the key figures? What stands out? What comparisons can I make?

#### Letter Formats and Types

The purpose of the letter varies. Common letter types include:

#### Complaint Letter
> "The graph shows noise levels recorded near your factory over six months. Write a letter to the factory manager complaining about the noise and requesting action."

#### Application Letter
> "Based on the table showing scholarship eligibility criteria, write a letter to the Education Ministry applying for a scholarship."

#### Request for Information
> "The chart below shows Malaysia's tourist arrivals from 2018 to 2022. Write a letter to the Tourism Board requesting further information about promotion activities."

#### Letter to the Editor
> "The diagram shows the increase in solid waste in Malaysian cities. Write a letter to a newspaper editor raising awareness about waste management."

#### General Structure of the Letter

```
[Your Address]
[Date]

[Recipient's Name/Title]
[Organisation]
[Address]

Dear [Recipient's Name / Sir or Madam],

[Opening — State the purpose briefly]

[Body — Describe key information from the visual, organised logically]

[Closing — Restate purpose or request action]

Yours faithfully / Yours sincerely,
[Your Name]
```

> **Note:** MUET Task 1 letters typically end with **"Yours faithfully,"** (when you use "Dear Sir or Madam") or **"Yours sincerely,"** (when you use a named recipient).

#### Describing Data Accurately

One of the biggest challenges in Task 1 is describing data without copying the exact words from the question. Here are essential data description phrases:

#### For Trends (increasing/decreasing/stable)

| Trend | Example Phrases |
|---|---|
| **Increase** | rose steadily, showed a significant increase, climbed, grew by |
| **Sharp increase** | skyrocketed, surged, jumped dramatically |
| **Decrease** | fell, declined, dropped, decreased gradually |
| **Sharp decrease** | plummeted, nosedived, fell sharply |
| **No change** | remained stable, stayed constant, showed little change |
| **Peak** | reached a peak of, peaked at, hit the highest point |
| **Lowest point** | fell to its lowest, reached a trough, bottomed out |

#### For Comparisons

- "The figure for X was considerably higher than Y's..."
- "X accounted for the largest share at 45%, whereas Y accounted for only 12%."
- "In contrast to X, Y showed a declining trend."
- "X and Y were similar in that both..."

#### For Generalisations

- "Overall, the data indicates that..."
- "In general, there was a clear trend of..."
- "It is notable that X was significantly higher than all other categories."
- "As shown in the graph, X experienced the most dramatic change."

#### Model Paragraph

> The line graph illustrates the number of university students enrolled in online courses in Malaysia from 2018 to 2023. Overall, there was a steady increase in online enrolment over the period shown. In 2018, approximately 20,000 students were enrolled, and this figure rose gradually to about 35,000 in 2020. A sharper increase was observed between 2020 and 2022, when enrolment jumped to 80,000 students, before levelling off at around 85,000 in 2023. Notably, the most significant growth occurred during the pandemic years of 2020 to 2022. In contrast, enrolment in traditional face-to-face courses declined slightly during the same period, falling from 150,000 in 2018 to 120,000 in 2023.

#### Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1. **Copying the question wording** — Use your own words to describe the data. Avoid repeating exact phrases from the prompt.
2. **Reporting every single number** — Select the most significant data points. You do not need to mention every figure.
3. **Guessing the reasons** — Unless the question asks you to explain causes, stick to describing what the data shows.
4. **Using informal language** — This is a formal letter. Avoid contractions (don't, can't), slang, or casual phrases.
5. **Going over 200 words** — Discipline yourself to stay within the 150–200 word range. Excessively long answers suggest poor time management.

#### Practice Tips

- Every time you do a graph question, write your answer within 25 minutes and count your words.
- Build a personal phrase bank of data description vocabulary.
- Practise reading bar charts, line graphs, and pie charts quickly — speed and accuracy in interpretation matter.
- Have a teacher or peer mark your practice scripts against the official MUET criteria.

### 🔴 Extended — Mastery

#### Decode the stimulus before drafting

Spend a few seconds identifying:

- **Visual type:** Is it showing change over time, categories, proportions, structured rows or a process?
- **Measure and unit:** Are the figures percentages, thousands, millions, households or another unit?
- **Time span or categories:** Which dates, places or groups are actually compared?
- **Overall pattern:** Is the dominant pattern growth, decline, stability or variation?
- **Salient evidence:** Which starting point, ending point, peak, trough or contrast deserves space?
- **Required action:** What must the letter request, complain about, apply for or raise?

A diagram showing a process can require you to describe stages and relationships, not invent numerical trends. A graph showing similar annual changes can support *rose steadily* but not *surged*.

#### Worked mini-example: a practice letter

**Practice stimulus:** The line graph shows daily bicycle journeys at three stations.

| Station | 2019 | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| River Park | 120 | 180 | 240 | 310 | 420 |
| Central Market | 250 | 300 | 350 | 400 | 450 |
| Stadium | 200 | 240 | 280 | 320 | 350 |

**Prompt:** Write to the city council requesting action to support rising bicycle use.

Dear Sir or Madam,

I am writing to request further action to support rising bicycle use in the city. The line graph compares daily bicycle journeys at River Park, Central Market and Stadium from 2019 to 2023.

Overall, use increased at all three sites, although Central Market recorded the highest figure in 2023. At River Park, daily journeys rose from 120 in 2019 to 240 in 2021, before climbing more sharply to 420 in 2023. Stadium increased more steadily, from 200 to 350, while Central Market rose from 250 to 450.

By 2023, Central Market exceeded River Park by 30 journeys and Stadium by 100. River Park experienced the largest absolute increase, adding 300 daily journeys over the period, compared with 200 at Central Market and 150 at Stadium.

Although the graph does not explain the reasons for this change, it clearly shows growing demand for cycling support. I therefore request that the council consider expanding cycle facilities, particularly at River Park where use grew fastest.

Yours faithfully,  
[Your Name]

The response identifies the overall pattern, selects meaningful points, compares the stations and connects the request to the visual evidence. It does not invent a cause such as a new cycling lane. The word *absolute* also prevents confusion between the largest increase and the highest final total.

#### Data-language traps

- **“Skyrocketed” is not a general word for increase.** Reserve it for a very sharp change supported by the scale of the visual.
- **“Remained stable” requires little or no change.** If a figure rises gradually, use *increased steadily* instead.
- **“Increased rapidly” and “rose slowly” are contradictory labels.** Check the intervals before choosing the adverb.
- **“The highest” and “the largest increase” are different claims.** A line can finish highest while another line grows more.
- **“Percentage points” and “per cent” are not interchangeable.** A move from 20% to 30% is a ten-percentage-point rise.

#### Common wrong reasoning

- **“Listing every number makes the description complete.”** Selection demonstrates judgement; a long number dump can obscure the pattern.
- **“Copying titles proves that the information has been transferred.”** Titles identify categories, but your sentences must still explain relationships and changes.
- **“A request for action allows personal opinions.”** The request can be direct, but the body should still remain evidence-led.
- **“A complaint needs accusations.”** Unsupported claims about an organisation's motives weaken the factual basis of the letter.
- **“Every increase can be called a surge.”** Accuracy takes priority over a large vocabulary display.

#### Safe response versus strong response

A safe response can repeat every data label and reproduce all fifteen figures in separate sentences. It remains within the word range but gives the reader little sense of development. A stronger response selects the opening, ending, main trend, largest change and most relevant comparison. It then reserves space for the letter's required purpose.

Before submitting, check the sign-off pairing: use *Dear Sir or Madam* with *Yours faithfully*, and a named recipient with *Yours sincerely*. Also check the measure, dates, spelling of the recipient's title and whether your closing request repeats the actual purpose of the prompt.
