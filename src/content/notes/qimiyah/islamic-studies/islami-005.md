# Topic 5

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Topic 5 in the **Qimiyah General Studies** section tests **quantitative and verbal-analytical reasoning** at a 3% syllabus weight. The five high-yield formulas you must internalise:

- **Percentage change**: Δ% = ((New − Old) / Old) × 100
- **Simple interest**: SI = (P × R × T) / 100
- **Average**: A = Σxᵢ / n
- **Ratio proportion**: a / b = c / d
- **Work-rate**: 1/T = 1/T₁ + 1/T₂

Most items are **multiple-choice** with a single correct option and three distractors built from common calculation slips. Solve the **series rule** before plugging numbers, and always check whether the question asks for *percentage of a number* or *percentage change between two numbers* — they require different setups.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core concept families

**Arithmetic reasoning** is built on five recurring operations: percentage, ratio, average, interest, and work-rate. Each follows a fixed algebraic pattern that converts a word statement into a single equation.

**Number series** items present a finite sequence (numeric or letter-based) and ask for the next term. Three pattern families dominate the Qimiyah pool: constant arithmetic difference, geometric ratio, and alternating two-subsequence rules.

**Logical deduction** items test syllogistic validity — given two premises, the examinee judges which conclusion *must* follow, *may* follow, or *cannot* follow. Venn-diagram sketching resolves these in under a minute.

#### Worked relationships

Take a word problem such as: *"A trader bought goods for 800 SAR and sold them for 920 SAR."* The percentage change is ((920 − 800) / 800) × 100 = **15%**. A frequent trap asks for "the selling price as a percentage of cost" — that answer is 115%, not 15%.

For simultaneous equations, isolate one variable in the first equation and **substitute** into the second; many candidates solve only one constraint and stop.

#### Typical question patterns

- **MCQ**: "Find the next number in 3, 6, 12, 24, ..."
- **MCQ**: "If a : b = 2 : 3 and b : c = 4 : 5, find a : c."
- **MCQ**: "Which conclusion follows from premises P and Q?"

> Tip: under timed conditions, eliminate two clearly wrong options first — the Qimiyah distractors are often numerical near-misses.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge cases and adjacent links

The percentage-change formula breaks when the *Old* value is zero or negative; the item bank avoids this, but recognising it prevents wasted effort on trick questions. Work-rate problems assume the two workers operate **independently** and additively — a 6-hour worker and a 12-hour worker complete the job in 1/T = 1/6 + 1/12 = 1/4, so T = 4 hours, not 18.

Topic 5 bridges to **higher-weighted verbal reasoning** (Topic 3) and **spatial reasoning** (Topic 6): a series-completion question that uses letter coding is a hybrid of pattern recognition and alphabet-shift logic, and a ratio question framed inside a geometry diagram tests proportion skills under visual load.

#### Common mistakes catalogue

- **Unit drift**: substituting 18 months as "18 years" into the simple-interest formula.
- **Sum-of-parts violation**: writing a : b : c = 2 : 3 : 5 and then assuming a + c = b.
- **Series misread**: detecting an arithmetic step of +3 in 2, 5, 9, 14 and missing the secondary pattern of squares.
- **Logical inversion**: concluding "all A are C" from "some A are B" and "all B are C."

#### Practice prompts

1. A principal of 5,000 SAR earns simple interest at 4% annually for 3 years. Compute the total amount receivable.
2. In a class of 42 students the ratio of boys to girls is 4 : 3. How many girls must transfer out so the new ratio becomes 3 : 2?
