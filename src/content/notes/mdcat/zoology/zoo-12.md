# Reproductive System

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The **reproductive system** comprises organs that produce **gametes** (sperm and ova), enable **fertilization**, and sustain embryonic development under hormonal control. In males, **spermatogenesis** occurs in the **seminiferous tubules** of the testes, supported by **Sertoli cells** (nurturing) and **Leydig cells** (testosterone secretion). In females, **oogenesis** in ovarian **follicles** releases a **secondary oocyte** at ovulation, after which the **corpus luteum** secretes progesterone to maintain the **endometrium**. The **hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal axis** governs both: **GnRH** → **FSH** (gamete/follicle development) and **LH** (ovulation and Leydig stimulation), with negative feedback by estrogen, progesterone, and testosterone. For MDCAT: remember that **spermatogenesis yields 4 functional sperm per primary spermatocyte**, while oogenesis yields **1 ovum + 3 polar bodies**, and that **fertilization normally occurs in the ampulla of the fallopian tube**.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Male Reproductive System
Sperm form via **spermatogenesis** inside the **seminiferous tubules**, beginning with **spermatogonia** (2n) that undergo mitosis to form **primary spermatocytes** (2n), which complete **meiosis I** to yield two **secondary spermatocytes** (n), then **meiosis II** to produce four **spermatids** (n) — these differentiate into spermatozoa through **spermiogenesis**. **Sertoli cells** form the blood–testis barrier and nourish developing germ cells; **Leydig cells** in interstitial tissue secrete **testosterone**. Sperm mature and gain motility in the **epididymis**, travel through the **vas deferens**, and mix with secretions from the **seminal vesicles** (fructose-rich fluid, ~60% of semen volume), **prostate gland** (alkaline milky fluid activating sperm), and **bulbourethral glands** (pre-ejaculate lubricant).

#### Female Reproductive System
Each **ovarian follicle** contains a **primary oocyte** arrested in **prophase I** until puberty. With each cycle, FSH drives follicular maturation; the dominant follicle releases its **secondary oocyte** at **ovulation** into the **fallopian tube**, where fertilization usually occurs in the **ampulla**. The ruptured follicle becomes the **corpus luteum**, secreting progesterone and estrogen to maintain the **endometrium** for implantation.

#### Hormonal Regulation
**GnRH** from the hypothalamus pulses to the anterior pituitary, releasing **FSH** (gametogenesis, follicle growth, Sertoli activity) and **LH** (ovulation, Leydig cell testosterone release). Sex steroids exert **negative feedback** on the hypothalamus and pituitary, except the mid-cycle estrogen surge which triggers a positive LH surge causing ovulation.

#### Menstrual Cycle Phases
| Phase | Days (approx.) | Key Event |
|---|---|---|
| Menstrual | 1–5 | Endometrium sheds |
| Follicular | 6–13 | Rising estrogen, follicle growth |
| Ovulation | 14 | LH surge releases oocyte |
| Luteal | 15–28 | Progesterone from corpus luteum |

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Fertilization, Implantation, and Early Development
A capacitated sperm penetrates the **corona radiata** via hyaluronidase, then binds **ZP3** receptors on the **zona pellucida**, releasing **acrosin** to bore through. Membrane fusion of sperm and oocyte triggers the **cortical reaction**, blocking polyspermy. The **male and female pronuclei** fuse to form a **zygote** (diploid restoration). **Cleavage** divisions yield a **morula** (~16 cells, days 3–4), then a **blastocyst** with **inner cell mass** (embryo) and **trophoblast** (placenta). **Implantation** into the thickened endometrium occurs around **day 6–7 post-fertilization**, after which the trophoblast secretes **hCG**, sustaining the corpus luteum until the **placenta** takes over hormone production by ~week 10.

#### Gametogenesis Comparison
Spermatogenesis is **continuous** post-puberty, producing ~200–300 million sperm daily, all four meiotic products functional. Oogenesis is **discontinuous and cyclical**: meiosis I completes only at ovulation, meiosis II only upon fertilization, yielding **one ovum and three polar bodies** — an asymmetric cytokinesis that conserves cytoplasm in the gamete. Primary oocytes remain arrested in **prophase I** from fetal life until ovulation, sometimes for decades — increasing **nondisjunction risk** with maternal age (relevant for Down syndrome).

#### Common MDCAT Traps and Connections
- Confusing **Sertoli vs Leydig cells**: Sertoli = support/nurse + inhibin + anti-Müllerian hormone; Leydig = interstitial + testosterone.
- Misplacing fertilization: it occurs in the **fallopian tube**, not the uterus or vagina.
- Mixing **hCG with progesterone**: hCG maintains the corpus luteum; progesterone maintains the endometrium.
- Linking this topic to **endocrine** (feedback loops), **embryology** (cleavage, germ layers), and **genetics** (meiosis, nondisjunction).
