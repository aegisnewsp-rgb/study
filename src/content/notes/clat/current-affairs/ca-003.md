# Legal News

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Legal News in CLAT's Current Affairs & GK section tests your awareness of Indian judicial pronouncements, parliamentary legislation, and constitutional developments reported in the **12 months preceding the exam**. Questions typically arrive as passage-based comprehension, factual MCQs, or principle-application sets anchored in a recent judgment or statute.

The highest-yield area for 2025–26 is the **criminal law recodification**: the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), and Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA) replaced the IPC, CrPC and Indian Evidence Act from **1 July 2024**. Section 302 IPC is now **BNS Section 302**; Section 376 IPC maps to **BNS Section 63**; cheating under 420 IPC is now **BNS Section 318**.

- **Read daily**: *The Hindu* or *Indian Express* for Supreme Court/High Court verdicts, plus *LiveLaw*, *Bar & Bench*, or *Supreme Court Observer* for case analysis.
- **Track**: ordinances under **Article 123**, Article 32/226 PILs, and **Article 142** complete justice orders.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### What CLAT Tests Under "Legal News"

CLAT's Current Affairs section contributes roughly **25% of the total marks**, with Legal News estimated at about **5%** of the paper. Items are drawn from the 12-month window before the test date set by the Consortium of NLUs. You will be asked to identify the correct court, the statutory provision interpreted, the relief granted, and occasionally the judge who authored the ruling.

#### Core Reading Sources and Tracking Method

Build a weekly log using one national daily and one law-specific outlet. Note the case name, citation, bench strength, and the exact legal issue — not the headline.

| Source | Use it for |
| --- | --- |
| The Hindu / Indian Express | Supreme Court and High Court verdicts, ordinances, bills |
| LiveLaw / Bar & Bench | Detailed case analysis and PIL coverage |
| Supreme Court Observer | Constitutional bench rulings and academic commentary |
| Ministry of Law & Justice notifications | Statutory amendments, rules under BNS/BNSS/BSA |
| NALSA releases | Legal aid, ADR, Lok Adalat drives |

#### Recodified Criminal Law — Section Mapping

The IPC–CrPC–Evidence Act triad ceased operation on 1 July 2024. Several old section numbers were renumbered under BNS, so quoting an IPC section on a post-1 July 2024 fact pattern is a common error.

| Old (IPC/CrPC/IEA) | New code | New section |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IPC Section 302 (murder) | BNS | 302 |
| IPC Section 376 (rape) | BNS | 63 |
| IPC Section 420 (cheating) | BNS | 318 |
| CrPC Section 154 (FIR) | BNSS | 154 |
| Indian Evidence Act Section 65B | BSA | 63 |

#### Distinguishing Binding Law from Commentary

- **Ratio decidendi**: the binding legal principle on which a judgment rests.
- **Obiter dicta**: observations by the judge that are persuasive, not binding.
- **Per incuriam**: a decision that ignored binding precedent — cite with caution.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### How to Decode a Judgment-Based Question

Passage items usually present three or four paragraphs summarising a recent ruling, then ask what principle was affirmed, which Article was invoked, or what remedy was granted. Read for the **issue**, the **statutory provision interpreted**, and the **relief** — not the political framing. A typical trap is asking you to treat obiter dicta as the ratio; another is asking which court finally decided the matter when the question is about a High Court division bench that has been appealed.

#### Article-by-Article High-Yield Triggers

1. **Article 32** — writ jurisdiction of the Supreme Court; fundamental rights enforcement.
2. **Article 226** — wider writ jurisdiction of High Courts; alternative remedy doctrine.
3. **Article 142** — "complete justice" power; used sparingly in landmark rulings.
4. **Article 123** — promulgation of ordinances when Parliament is not in session.
5. **Article 370 abrogation** — continues to surface in constitutional bench references.

#### Worked Comprehension Pattern

A passage describes a 2025 ruling holding that live-in relationships carry a presumption of legitimacy under Section 50 of the BSA. A question asks: "The ruling binds:" — the answer is only the parties and those in like circumstances under Article 141, not all citizens. The principle stated is binding as **ratio**, the judge's observations on social policy are **obiter**.

#### Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Quoting IPC sections after 1 July 2024 instead of the BNS equivalent.
- Confusing the Law Commission's recommendations with enacted law.
- Citing a High Court ruling as a Supreme Court judgment.
- Writing only the political slug instead of the legal issue and relief.

#### Two Practice Prompts

1. The CrPC was replaced by the BNSS on 1 July 2024. State the BNSS section corresponding to CrPC Section 154 and the constitutional provision enabling such recodification.
2. A 2024 bench of two Supreme Court judges observed that mandatory death penalty for a certain offence was unconstitutional. Is this statement binding precedent? Justify using *ratio decidendi* and *obiter dicta*.
