# Organic Chemistry: Carboxylic Acids and Esters

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

- **Carboxylic acids** carry the **carboxyl group –COOH**: a carbonyl (C=O) bonded directly to a hydroxyl (–OH) on the same carbon. General formula **R–COOH**.
- **Esters** carry the **–COO– linkage** with two different carbon chains: **R–COO–R′**, where R comes from the acid and R′ from the alcohol.
- **Fischer esterification** is reversible and acid-catalysed: **R–COOH + R′–OH ⇌ R–COO–R′ + H₂O** (concentrated H₂SO₄, reflux).
- **Boiling points** rise with chain length but stay unusually high for acids because two molecules dimerise through **two hydrogen bonds** between –COOH groups.
- NECO hot points: write balanced equations for ester formation, name esters as **alkyl alkanoate**, and explain why acids boil higher than alcohols of similar molar mass.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Nomenclature and Structure

The IUPAC suffix for a carboxylic acid is **-anoic acid** (methanoic, ethanoic, propanoic, butanoic). For esters, name the alkyl group from the alcohol first, then the acid stem with the suffix **-oate**: ethanol + ethanoic acid → **ethyl ethanoate** (CH₃COOC₂H₅).

#### Physical Properties

Carboxylic acids are polar and form **dimers** in the liquid state, held by two O–H···O=C hydrogen bonds. This doubles their effective molecular mass and pushes boiling points above those of isomeric alcohols. Solubility in water decreases as the alkyl chain lengthens past C₃.

| Acid | Formula | Boiling point (°C) |
|---|---|---|
| Methanoic acid | HCOOH | 100.8 |
| Ethanoic acid | CH₃COOH | 118.1 |
| Propanoic acid | C₂H₅COOH | 141.2 |

#### Acidity

Carboxylic acids are **weak acids** (Kₐ ≈ 10⁻⁵). The released proton leaves a **carboxylate anion R–COO⁻** whose negative charge is delocalised equally over both oxygen atoms by resonance, stabilising the ion and driving ionisation forward.

#### Key Reactions

| Reaction | Equation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Esterification | R–COOH + R′–OH ⇌ R–COO–R′ + H₂O | conc. H₂SO₄, reflux, reversible |
| Acid hydrolysis | R–COO–R′ + H₂O ⇌ R–COOH + R′–OH | H⁺ catalyst |
| Saponification | R–COO–R′ + NaOH → R–COONa + R′–OH | produces soap + glycerol from fats |
| Ammonolysis | R–COO–R′ + NH₃ → R–CONH₂ + R′–OH | forms an amide |

#### Common Exam Question Patterns

NECO SSCE Paper II usually asks for **balanced equations**, **IUPAC names of esters** from given alcohols and acids, and **explanations** of boiling-point anomalies. Paper I tests recognition of the –COOH and –COO– groups and identification of fruity-smelling esters.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Mechanism and Equilibrium Detail

Fischer esterification proceeds via **nucleophilic acyl substitution**. The carbonyl oxygen is protonated by the acid catalyst, making the acyl carbon electrophilic; the alcohol oxygen attacks, and after proton transfers water is expelled. Because the reverse reaction is the same mechanism with water as the nucleophile, the system is a **classic equilibrium**. Removing water (using a Dean–Stark trap or anhydrous conditions) drives ester yield up; adding excess water pushes the reaction back toward the acid.

#### Distinguishing Acids from Esters in the Lab

- **Na₂CO₃ test**: carboxylic acids effervesce and release CO₂; esters do not react.
- **Smell**: simple aliphatic esters smell fruity (pear, banana, pineapple); carboxylic acids smell sharp or vinegary.
- **Solubility**: short-chain acids and esters dissolve in water; long-chain variants do not.

#### Connection to Fats and Oils

Natural fats and oils are **triglycerides**: glycerol triesters of long-chain fatty acids (e.g., glyceryl tristearate). Boiling with NaOH hydrolyses them into sodium salts of the fatty acids (soap) and glycerol — this is **saponification**, the industrial route to soap.

#### Common Mistakes in NECO Scripts

- Writing the ester formula as R–COOR instead of **R–COO–R′**, blurring the two carbons.
- Naming esters backwards: "ethanoic acid ethyl" instead of **ethyl ethanoate**.
- Treating saponification as a reversible equilibrium; it is **irreversible** because the carboxylate formed is resonance-stabilised and unreactive toward the alcohol.
- Assuming esters are acidic. Esters are **neutral** and do not liberate CO₂ from carbonates.
- Drawing the C=O on the alcohol's carbon. The carbonyl always belongs to the **acyl carbon** of the original acid.

#### Worked Example

Name the ester formed when propan-1-ol reacts with butanoic acid under concentrated H₂SO₄ and write its hydrolysis equation with NaOH.

- Propyl group from propan-1-ol + butanoate from butanoic acid → **propyl butanoate**, CH₃CH₂CH₂COOCH₂CH₂CH₃.
- Alkaline hydrolysis: CH₃CH₂CH₂COOCH₂CH₂CH₃ + NaOH → CH₃CH₂CH₂COONa + CH₃CH₂CH₂OH (sodium butanoate + propan-1-ol).

#### Practice Prompts

1. Write the equation for the formation of methyl benzoate from methanol and benzoic acid, and state the catalyst used.
2. Explain, with diagrams, why ethanoic acid (M = 60 g mol⁻¹) boils at 118 °C while propan-1-ol (M = 60 g mol⁻¹) boils at 97 °C.
