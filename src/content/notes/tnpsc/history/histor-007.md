# Post-Mauryan Period and Regional Kingdoms

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The Post-Mauryan Period (c. 200 BCE–300 CE) began when Pushyamitra Shunga overthrew the last Mauryan ruler Brihadratha in 185 BCE, ending the first pan-Indian empire. **Shungas (185–73 BCE)**, **Kanvas (73–28 BCE)**, **Satavahanas**, **Indo-Greeks**, **Shakas-Pahlavas**, and **Kushanas** filled the political vacuum, each issuing distinctive coins and patronising distinct art styles. The **Saka era (78 CE)** inaugurated by Kanishka remains India's official calendar for the national almanac.

- **Kanishka** convened the **Fourth Buddhist Council at Kundalvanah (Kashmir)** and patronised the **Gandhara School** of art.
- **Gautamiputra Shakaari** of the **Satavahanas** defeated Shaka ruler **Nahapana**; their capital was **Pratishthana (Paithan)**.
- **Rudradaman I** (Shaka) is documented in the **Junagadh rock inscription**; he held the title **Mahakshatrapa**.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Major Dynasties and Founders

After Brihadratha's assassination, the Brahmin general **Pushyamitra Shunga** established the Shunga line, famous for reviving Vedic Brahmanism and performing the **Ashvamedha sacrifice**. His son **Agnimitra** features in Kalidasa's drama *Malavikagnimitram*. The brief **Kanva dynasty** (Vasudeva Kanva, 73–28 BCE) was ended by the **Simuka**, founder of the **Satavahana** line in the Deccan. Satavahana rulers used Prakrit inscriptions and issued **lead and potin coins**; **Gautamiputra Shakaari** (c. 78–106 CE) is celebrated for halting Shaka expansion.

#### Indo-Greeks, Shakas, and Kushanas

**Demetrius** (c. 200 BCE) initiated Indo-Greek rule from **Bactria** into the Gangetic plains. **Menander** (Milindapanho) converted to Buddhism under the sage Nagasena. The **Shakas** (Central Asian Scythians) and **Pahlavas** (Parthians) superseded the Greeks. **Rudradaman I** (c. 130–150 CE) governed from Ujjain and repaired the **Sudarshana lake** at Girnar. **Kadphises I** founded the Kushana state; **Kanishka I** (c. 78–144 CE) stretched it from Central Asia to Varanasi, patronising **Ashvaghosha** and convening the **Fourth Buddhist Council**.

#### Economy and Trade

Indo-Roman trade prospered through western ports like **Barygaza (Bharuch)**, **Muziris (Kerala)**, and **Tamralipti (Bengal)**. Roman **aurei** flowed into India in exchange for spices, textiles, and gems, creating a bullion drain that Pliny the Elder lamented in his *Natural History*.

- **Charana system** (Bhatta, Suta, Magadha) preserved royal genealogies.
- Distinguish **Saka era (78 CE, Kanishka)** from **Vikrama era (58 BCE, Azes)** — a common TNPSC trap.
- **Sangam** texts name Chera, Chola, and Pandya chieftains with titles like Vel, Sengani, Thenmavan.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Examiner's Traps and Mechanisms

TNPSC frequently pairs dynasties with the wrong founder or capital — for example, attributing **Pataliputra** to the Satavahanas instead of Pratishthana. The **Mahakshatrapa** title belongs exclusively to Shaka rulers, distinct from the Kushana **Devaputra** (Son of God) used by Kanishka. Coins serve as the safest evidence: Kanishka's gold **dinars** depict Shiva (**Oesho**), Buddha, and Zoroastrian **Oado**, reflecting religious syncretism.

#### Worked Identification Drill

**Prompt:** Match the ruler to dynasty, era, and a single monument or inscription.

1. **Rudradaman I** → Shaka → 130–150 CE → Junagadh rock inscription.
2. **Gautamiputra Shakaari** → Satavahana → c. 106 CE → Nashik cave inscriptions.
3. **Menander** → Indo-Greek → c. 150 BCE → Milindapanho dialogue.

#### Integration with Adjacent Topics

This block dovetails with **Sangam Age polity** (Cheras, Cholas, Pandyas), **Mauryan decline** (Brahminical resurgence under Shungas), and **early historic art** (transition from Ashokan pillars to Gandhara Buddha figures). Watch for **assertion-reason** items that claim the Saka era was instituted by Vikramaditya — it is Kanishka's.

1. Explain why Kanishka's South-Indian-ceding policy accelerated the **Saka-Pahlava** presence in the Deccan.
