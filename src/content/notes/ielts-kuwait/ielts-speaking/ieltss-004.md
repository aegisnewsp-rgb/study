# Pronunciation and Fluency Markers — IELTS Speaking Notes

Pronunciation is one of the four official IELTS Speaking band descriptors (alongside Fluency and Coherence, Lexical Resource, and Grammatical Range and Accuracy). It is scored independently and contributes to the Speaking band. Pronunciation rewards clarity, not accent.

> Verify the live Speaking band descriptors on https://ielts.org/ before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Word stress:** emphasise the correct syllable in each word. Example: im-POR-tant (not IM-portant).
- **Sentence stress:** emphasise the content words (nouns, verbs, adjectives) and de-emphasise function words (articles, prepositions).
- **Intonation:** rise for yes/no questions; fall for statements and wh-questions; rise-fall for lists.
- **Chunking:** group words into meaningful phrases with brief pauses between them.
- **Discourse markers:** well, actually, I mean, you know, to be honest, in my opinion.
- **Hesitation patterns:** natural pauses are fine; excessive hesitation is penalised.

#### Examiner traps

- Speaking too fast to be understood — clarity beats speed.
- Over-emphasising every word — natural speech has rhythm.
- Using one intonation pattern throughout — speech should rise and fall.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Word stress

English is a stress-timed language, not syllable-timed. Some languages (e.g. French) give each syllable equal time; English gives stressed syllables more time and unstressed syllables less.

Examples:

- PHOtograph (noun) — stress on first syllable.
- phoTOGraphic (adjective) — stress on third syllable.
- photographED (past tense verb) — stress shifts to the final syllable.

The position of the stress changes the meaning of the word.

#### Sentence stress

In a sentence, content words (nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs) are stressed. Function words (articles, prepositions, auxiliary verbs) are unstressed.

Example: "I BOUGHT a BOOK at the STORE yesterday." (stressed words in CAPS)

#### Intonation

Intonation patterns:

- **Statement:** "I'm a student." (fall)
- **Yes/no question:** "Are you a student?" (rise)
- **Wh-question:** "What do you study?" (fall)
- **List:** "I study English, maths, and physics." (rise-rise-fall)
- **Tag question:** "You're a student, aren't you?" (fall-rise)

#### Chunking

Group words into meaningful phrases:

- "I'm a student" / "studying at the university" / "in Kuwait City." (three chunks)

Pause briefly between chunks to give the listener time to process.

#### Discourse markers

Use discourse markers to connect ideas:

- **Sequencing:** first, then, next, after that, finally.
- **Adding:** also, in addition, furthermore, moreover, besides.
- **Contradicting:** however, on the other hand, although, even though.
- **Concluding:** in conclusion, to sum up, overall, all in all.

#### Self-correction

Self-correction is acceptable and shows language awareness. Example: "I went to... no, I should say I am going to the store tomorrow."

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where pronunciation appears across IELTS Speaking

Pronunciation is one of the four band descriptors and contributes to the Speaking band. Strong pronunciation helps fluency and intelligibility.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "I need a British accent." No — IELTS examiners accept any intelligible accent.
- "Speaking fast scores higher." No — clarity beats speed.
- "Hesitation is always bad." Natural hesitation is fine; excessive hesitation is penalised.

#### Exam technique

- Practice with shadowing — listen to a recording and repeat immediately.
- Record yourself and listen back to identify pronunciation issues.
- Focus on word stress and intonation patterns.
- Use discourse markers to connect ideas.
