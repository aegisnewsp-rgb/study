# Variety of Life

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Biodiversity** = the range of variation among living organisms, studied at three levels: **genetic**, **species**, and **ecosystem** diversity. NEET Botany frames this chapter around **taxonomy**, **nomenclature**, **type specimens**, **biodiversity indices**, **latitudinal gradients**, and **conservation strategies**.

#### Must-know formulas and facts

| Item | Expression / Detail |
|---|---|
| **Binomial nomenclature** (Linnaeus, 1753) | *Genus* (capitalised) + *species epithet* (lowercase); both italicised, e.g., *Mangifera indica* |
| Shannon-Wiener index | H = − Σ (Ni/N) ln(Ni/N) |
| Simpson's diversity (D) | D = 1 − Σ [Ni(Ni−1) / N(N−1)] |
| Species-area relationship | log S = log C + Z log A; Z = 0.1–0.3 mainland, 0.6–1.2 islands |
| Latitudinal gradient | Species richness rises from poles toward equator |

#### High-yield pointers

- **Division** (not Phylum) is used in plant taxonomy per ICN; **Phylum** is for animals (ICZN).
- India has **4 biodiversity hotspots**: Himalaya, Indo-Burma, Western Ghats–Sri Lanka, Sundaland.
- **Evil Quartet** = habitat loss, over-exploitation, alien species invasion, **co-extinction**.
- **In-situ** = National Parks, Wildlife Sanctuaries, Biosphere Reserves, sacred groves; **ex-situ** = seed banks, zoos, cryopreservation, botanical gardens.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Levels of Biodiversity

Biodiversity is examined at three nested levels. **Genetic diversity** refers to variation within a species (e.g., the dozens of mango varieties, or different cultivars of *Brassica*). **Species diversity** counts the number and variety of species in a defined region, combining **richness** (how many species) with **evenness** (how equally individuals are distributed among species). **Ecosystem diversity** captures variation across habitats, biomes, and ecological processes such as nutrient cycling and pollination.

#### Taxonomic Hierarchy and Nomenclature

Taxonomy rests on four pillars: **identification, classification, nomenclature**, and **systematic ordering** into taxa. The plant hierarchy is **Kingdom → Division → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species**, with the species treated as the fundamental natural unit — a population of interbreeding individuals reproductively isolated from other such populations. Binomial nomenclature (Linnaeus, 1753) assigns each species a two-part Latin name; both parts are italicised, the genus capitalised, the epithet lowercase, and the author's name appears in Roman (e.g., *Mangifera indica* Linn.).

Two parallel codes govern naming: the **ICN** (algae, fungi, plants) and the **ICZN** (animals). Knowing which code applies is a frequent NEET differentiator.

#### The Type Concept

| Specimen | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Holotype** | Single specimen designated by the original author at publication |
| **Isotype** | Duplicate of the holotype collected simultaneously, kept in another herbarium |
| **Syntype** | Any specimen cited when no holotype was designated |
| **Lectotype** | Specimen selected later from syntypes to serve as the nomenclatural type |
| **Neotype** | Replacement specimen chosen when the original type is lost or destroyed |

#### Taxonomic Aids

A **herbarium** stores pressed, dried, mounted plant specimens with field labels; acronyms identify collections (e.g., CAL for Central National Herbarium, Kolkata). **Botanical gardens** maintain living collections — the Indian Botanical Garden at Howrah holds over 12,000 species. Other aids include **floras, monographs, manuals, catalogues**, and **keys** (dichotomous or polyclave) that step through paired character choices to identify an unknown specimen.

#### Diversity Patterns

Species richness follows a **latitudinal gradient**: tropical regions such as Colombia host ~1,400 bird species, while temperate New York holds only ~56. The **species–area relationship** (Arrhenius) shows log S = log C + Z log A, where **Z** ranges from 0.1–0.3 for mainland samples but climbs to 0.6–1.2 for islands or very large areas. Whittaker partitioned biodiversity into **alpha** (within a community), **beta** (turnover between communities), and **gamma** (landscape-scale total).

#### Conservation

India has **4 biodiversity hotspots** and runs **in-situ** protection through National Parks, Wildlife Sanctuaries, Biosphere Reserves (Nilgiri, Sundarbans, Gulf of Mannar) and sacred groves. **Ex-situ** methods include seed banks, germplasm banks, cryopreservation, and zoos. The **Botanical Survey of India (BSI, 1890)** and **Zoological Survey of India (ZSI, 1916)** document national flora and fauna.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Worked Example: Calculating Species Diversity

A researcher samples a forest plot and counts individuals across four species: A = 30, B = 20, C = 40, D = 10 (N = 100).

#### Shannon-Wiener index
H = −[(30/100) ln(30/100) + (20/100) ln(20/100) + (40/100) ln(40/100) + (10/100) ln(10/100)]
H = −[0.3(−1.204) + 0.2(−1.609) + 0.4(−0.916) + 0.1(−2.303)]
H ≈ −[−0.361 − 0.322 − 0.366 − 0.230] = **1.279**

**Simpson's diversity**: D = 1 − Σ [Ni(Ni−1)/N(N−1)]
D = 1 − [30·29 + 20·19 + 40·39 + 10·9] / [100·99]
D = 1 − [870 + 380 + 1560 + 90] / 9900 = 1 − 2900/9900 = 1 − 0.293 ≈ **0.707**

#### Common Mistakes and Traps

> **Trap:** Writing *Mangifera Indica* (capitalised epithet) — the species epithet is always lowercase, even when derived from a proper noun.

> **Trap:** Treating Z = 0.26 as universal — Z is **area-dependent**. NEET may present an island scenario and expect the 0.6–1.2 range.

> **Trap:** Swapping beta and gamma diversity. Beta = turnover *between* communities; gamma = total *across* the landscape.

> **Trap:** Listing only 3 Indian hotspots. India has **4** — Sundaland is often forgotten.

> **Trap:** Categorising seed banks or zoos as in-situ. In-situ protects organisms *in their natural habitat*; everything removed from the wild is ex-situ.
