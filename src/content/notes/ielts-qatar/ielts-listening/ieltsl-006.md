# Question Types — Multiple Choice, Matching, Labelling, Short Answer

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **What it covers:** the four choice-family question types used across the four parts of IELTS Listening — multiple choice, matching, plan/map/diagram labelling, and short-answer questions.
- **Distribution:** roughly 50% of all Listening questions are choice-family items (the other 50% come from the completion family covered in ieltsl-05).
- **Core rule:** every choice-family answer is selected from the options provided in the question paper. The candidate does NOT write words from the recording; they choose from A/B/C or A-E options or write the literal word from the recording (short-answer only).
- **Cognitive-load marker:** choice-family items are the highest-cognitive-load items in IELTS Listening because the candidate must read options while listening AND track the recording AND decide which option is correct.
- **Marking:** +1 correct, 0 incorrect (no negative marking). Multiple choice and matching accept the EXACT letter (A/B/C/D/E); short-answer accepts the literal word within the word limit.

#### The four families at a glance

| Family | Most common part | Answer source |
|---|---|---|
| Multiple choice | Parts 1, 2, 3 | letter A/B/C/D/E from options |
| Matching | Parts 2, 3, 4 | letter from box, matched to list |
| Plan / map / diagram labelling | Parts 2, 4 | letter from box, written on map |
| Short-answer | Parts 1, 4 | literal word from recording (within word limit) |

---

#### Examiner traps

- Reading the options TOO LATE. The binding constraint in choice-family items is reading speed, not listening speed. Use the gap between questions to read options.
- Falling for the matching DISTRACTOR. The recording mentions an item the candidate does NOT need; the question asks for the correct one.
- Confusing NSEW directions on a map labelling. Maps are drawn with a compass rose; the candidate must use the same orientation.
- Writing MORE than the word limit on short-answer. "NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS" is strict. "the library" is 2 words; "the public library" is 3 — wrong.
- Spelling the short-answer vocabulary correctly. Training with a daily 5-minute dictation drill is essential.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Multiple choice (Parts 1, 2, 3)

The candidate chooses one of three (A/B/C) or four (A/B/C/D) options. In some cases, the candidate chooses TWO correct answers from a list of 5-7 options (the more complex choice).

**Strategy:**
1. **Predict the answer type from the stem.** "What is the speaker's main point?" expects a conclusion; "What does the speaker mean by X?" expects a paraphrase; "Which of the following is TRUE?" expects a fact.
2. **Read ALL options before the recording starts.** Note which options are similar and which are unique. The recording often mentions several options; the correct one is the most precise.
3. **Listen for the speaker's PARAPHRASE.** The options paraphrase the recording. The correct option is the one that matches the speaker's settled position.
4. **Watch for option D and E.** In 4-option multiple choice, the correct answer is sometimes hidden in the longer option. Read all four options carefully.

**Qatar-relevant contexts:** course content (QU, Education City); admission options (QU, Education City); choice of accommodation (Hamad airport hotel, Marriott, Hilton, Radisson); travel options (Qatar Airways routes, Doha Metro lines, taxi services).

#### Matching (Parts 2, 3, 4)

The candidate matches a list of items (e.g. opinions, advantages, disadvantages, features) to options in a box. Common patterns: speakers to opinions (Part 3); features to categories (Part 4); events to dates (Part 2).

**Strategy:**
1. **Identify the matching key.** The question paper lists the items on one side and the options on the other. The candidate must identify which option matches which item.
2. **Listen for paraphrase markers.** The recording uses different words than the question paper. The match requires paraphrase recognition.
3. **Eliminate options as they are used.** Each option can be used only ONCE in most matching items; some items may use an option MORE than once. Read the question stem carefully.
4. **Watch for distractors.** The recording often mentions an item the candidate does NOT need; the question asks for the correct one.

**Qatar-relevant contexts:** speakers to opinions (academic discussion Part 3); features to categories (research methods, language families); events to dates (QU events, Education City events).

#### Plan / map / diagram labelling (Parts 2, 4)

The candidate labels a plan, map, or diagram with letters from a box. Common patterns: building plan labelling (Part 2); campus map labelling (Part 4); process diagram labelling (Part 4); diagram of a region or system labelling (Part 4).

**Strategy:**
1. **Identify the orientation.** Maps and plans are drawn with a compass rose or NSEW markers. The candidate must use the same orientation.
2. **Identify the labelling key.** The question paper lists the items on one side and the options on the other. The candidate must identify which option matches which item.
3. **Listen for relative-position cues.** "to the left of", "next to", "behind", "in front of", "across from" are direct cues for map labelling.
4. **Watch for distractors.** The recording often mentions an item the candidate does NOT need; the question asks for the correct one.

**Qatar-relevant contexts:** Souq Waqif map (Part 2); Museum of Islamic Art plan (Part 2); Education City campus map (Part 4); QU campus map (Part 4); Hamad International Airport terminal map (Part 2).

#### Short-answer (Parts 1, 4)

The candidate writes a brief open-ended answer (1-3 words or 1-3 words and/or a number). Less common than completion-family items but appears in roughly 15-20% of Listening sets.

**Strategy:**
1. **Read the stem carefully.** Know what TYPE of answer you are listening for (name, place, number, date, noun phrase).
2. **Listen for the literal answer.** Short-answer uses the same source as completion-family items — the literal word from the recording.
3. **Manage the word limit.** Strict word-limit enforcement. "Tuesday" is 1 word; "the day after tomorrow" is 4 — wrong.
4. **Spell the word correctly.** Spelling errors are usually forgiven if the meaning is unchanged; a missing letter is not.

**Qatar-relevant contexts:** registration forms (Part 1); library records (Part 1); research notes (Part 4); academic definitions (Part 4).

### 🔴 Deep — Mastery (2mo+)

#### The cognitive architecture of choice-family questions

Choice-family questions test the candidate's ability to read options while listening, track the recording, and decide which option is correct. They appear in roughly 50% of all Listening questions across the four parts.

The differentiation between choice-family questions and completion-family questions is structural: choice questions require the candidate to choose from options (letter A-E or letter from box), while completion questions require the candidate to write literal words from the recording. Choice-family questions have a higher interpretation cost (must read options AND listen AND decide) but a lower writing cost (no need for verbatim word recall). The choice family has the highest cognitive load of any Listening family.

A candidate scoring below 6 on the choice family typically has an option-reading, paraphrase-decoding, or decision-making issue that no targeted choice practice will resolve. The deeper intervention is to build reading endurance (e.g. extended listening with simultaneous option-reading) and option-decision speed (e.g. daily option-elimination drills).

#### Practice strategy for the choice family

1. **Use the British Council and IDP official IELTS practice tests.** The question styles and difficulty are calibrated against the live test. Third-party materials may diverge.
2. **Practise with Qatari-context recording scenarios.** Doha-based hotel booking, tour booking, course enrolment, library registration, university lecture, research seminar build the local vocabulary you will hear on test day.
3. **Practise option-reading with a stopwatch.** Most multiple-choice options can be read in 5-8 seconds each; without practice, candidates spend 10-15 seconds per option and run out of time for the section.
5. **Matching drill:** take 20 matching items and identify the distractors. Build from 1-distractor to 5-distractor drills.
6. **Map-labelling drill:** take 5 maps and label them with NSEW orientation. Build from 3-directional to 6-directional drills.
7. **Short-answer drill:** take 30 short-answer items and count the words in each candidate answer. Build from 1-word limits to 4-word limits.

#### When choice marks reflect a deeper issue

- **Persistent option-decision errors:** the issue is paraphrase-decoding, not listening. Train with academic-podcast listening and option-elimination drills.
- **Persistent matching-errors:** the indicator is distractor tracking. Build a 50-row table of common distractors (item mentioned but not asked; paraphrase that sounds right; opposite paraphrase).
- **Persistent map-labelling errors:** the indicator is orientation tracking. Train with a daily 5-minute NSEW orientation drill.
- **Persistent short-answer failures:** the issue is word-counting, not listening. Train with a daily 10-minute "count and check" drill where you count the words in each candidate answer.
- **Persistent reading-too-late errors:** the issue is reading speed, not listening. Train with a "read first, listen second" drill where you read ALL options BEFORE the recording starts.

#### Edge cases and known traps

1. **Matching options may be used MORE than once.** Some matching items allow an option to be used twice or three times. Read the question stem carefully.
2. **Map labels include NSEW markers.** Some maps use a compass rose; others use a directional arrow. The candidate must use the same orientation as the recording.
3. **Multiple choice may have TWO correct answers.** In some sets, the candidate chooses two options from a list of 5-7. Read the stem carefully.
4. **Short-answer word limit is strict.** "NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS" is strict. "the library" is 2 words; "the public library" is 3 — wrong.
5. **Plural vs singular.** The answer must match the EXACT form from the recording. If the speaker says "sodium chloride" and you write "sodium chlorides", it is wrong. Singular and plural are not interchangeable.
6. **Capitalisation.** IELTS accepts lower-case names unless the recording explicitly capitalises (proper nouns, country names). Do not waste time capitalising "london" — both are accepted.
7. **American vs British spelling.** IELTS accepts either American or British spelling as long as it is consistent within a single answer. "color" and "colour" are both accepted for the same item; do not switch mid-answer.

### High-Yield — IELTS Listening Choice Family

The seven most-testable contexts and the calibrated response framework:

1. **Hotel booking multiple choice (Part 1).** A caller discusses hotel options. Multiple choice captures the room type and amenities. Qatar context: Hamad International Airport hotel, Marriott Doha, Hilton Doha, Radisson Blu Doha.
2. **Tour booking matching (Part 1 or Part 2).** A caller or guide discusses tour options. Matching captures the tours and dates. Qatar context: Souq Waqif tour, Museum of Islamic Art tour, Katara Cultural Village tour.
3. **Course enrolment multiple choice (Part 1).** A caller discusses course options. Multiple choice captures the course content and schedule. Qatar context: QU Continuing Education, British Council Qatar.
4. **Souq Waqif map labelling (Part 2).** A guide describes a tour of Souq Waqif. Map labelling captures the locations of vendors and restaurants. Qatar context: Souq Waqif.
5. **Education City campus map labelling (Part 4).** A lecturer or guide describes Education City. Map labelling captures the locations of branch campuses (CMU-Q, TAMU-Q, Northwestern Qatar, Georgetown Qatar, Weill Cornell Medicine-Q). Qatar context: Education City.
6. **Research methodology matching (Part 4).** A researcher describes a study. Matching captures the methods and findings. Qatar context: QU research seminar, Education City research summary.
7. **Academic discussion matching (Part 3).** Two speakers discuss an academic topic. Matching captures the speakers and opinions. Qatar context: methodology debate, study-habit comparison.

### Previous Year Patterns — IELTS Listening Choice Family

The four choice families have been structurally stable across every IELTS cycle since the Academic / General Training split in 1989. The format (multiple choice, matching, plan/map/diagram labelling, short-answer) is published verbatim on ielts.org and is not expected to change.

Common year-on-year patterns:
- The choice family accounts for roughly 50% of all Listening questions per cycle — stable across cycles.
- Multiple choice is concentrated in Parts 1, 2, and 3; matching is concentrated in Parts 2, 3, and 4; map labelling is concentrated in Parts 2 and 4; short-answer is concentrated in Parts 1 and 4.
- The cognitive-load marker has been stable: choice-family items are the highest-cognitive-load items in IELTS Listening.
- Matching options may be used MORE than once — published on ielts.org.
- A previous-year choice set is NOT reused verbatim in the same year; recycled recordings appear with at most small contextual changes.

A previous-year record is not a deterministic indicator of a future test item. The British Council and IDP refresh the question bank annually, calibrated against the same difficulty curve.

### Pro Tips — IELTS Listening Choice Family

1. **Read ALL options before the recording starts.** The binding constraint in choice questions is reading speed, not listening speed. Use the gap between questions to read options.
2. **Predict the answer type from the stem.** "What is the speaker's main point?" expects a conclusion; "What does the speaker mean by X?" expects a paraphrase; "Which of the following is TRUE?" expects a fact.
3. **Eliminate options as they are used.** Each option can be used only ONCE in most matching items; some items may use an option MORE than once. Read the question stem carefully.
4. **Use NSEW markers.** Maps are drawn with a compass rose or directional arrow. The candidate must use the same orientation as the recording.
5. **Listen for relative-position cues.** "to the left of", "next to", "behind", "in front of", "across from" are direct cues for map labelling.
6. **Manage short-answer word limits.** "NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS" is strict. Count words, including numbers ("30%" = 1 word; "30 percent" = 2 words).
7. **Watch for matching distractors.** The recording often mentions an item the candidate does NOT need; the question asks for the correct one.
8. **Practise with Qatari-context recording scenarios.** Doha-based hotel booking, tour booking, course enrolment, library registration, university lecture, research seminar cut cognitive load on test day.
9. **Don't panic on a missed item.** Move on. The next item is independent. Two or three lost choice items does not change your overall band.
10. **Spelling is forgiving, but missing letters are not.** Train with a daily 5-minute spelling drill on the 570-word Academic Word List.
