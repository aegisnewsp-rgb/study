# Biomolecules and Enzymes

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Biomolecules are organic compounds — **carbohydrates**, **lipids**, **proteins**, and **nucleic acids** — built from specific monomers that living cells assemble and break apart continuously. **Enzymes** are globular protein biocatalysts classified by the IUBMB into six EC classes, accelerating reactions by lowering activation energy without altering ΔG. The Michaelis-Menten equation v = (Vmax[S]) / (Km + [S]) governs almost every FMGE numerical on this topic, and its Lineweaver-Burk reciprocal form is the trick to reading inhibition graphs.

- **Km** = substrate concentration at half-Vmax; lower Km = higher enzyme affinity.
- **Diagnostic enzymes** to memorise: ALT/SGPT (liver), CK-MB (heart), amylase/lipase (pancreas), ALP (bone/liver), troponin I/T (gold-standard cardiac marker).
- **Coenzymes** are vitamin-derived: NAD+ (niacin, B3), FAD (riboflavin, B2), CoA (pantothenate, B5), TPP (thiamine, B1), PLP (pyridoxine, B6), THF (folate, B9).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Monomers and macromolecules
Each biomolecule class has characteristic monomers: monosaccharides (glucose, fructose) form disaccharides and polysaccharides; fatty acids + glycerol form triglycerides; amino acids (20 standard, all α-L except glycine) link via peptide bonds; nucleotides (base + ribose/deoxyribose + phosphate) polymerise into DNA and RNA. DNA is double-stranded with A=T and G≡C Watson-Crick pairing, while RNA is single-stranded and replaces thymine with uracil.

#### Protein structural hierarchy
Primary structure = the linear amino-acid sequence. Secondary structure gives the **α-helix** and **β-pleated sheet**, both stabilised by backbone hydrogen bonds. Tertiary structure folds the polypeptide into a 3-D shape stabilised by disulphide bridges, hydrophobic interactions and salt links. Quaternary structure assembles multiple subunits, exemplified by haemoglobin (2α + 2β).

#### Enzyme kinetics essentials
The Michaelis-Menten model assumes a rapid equilibrium for ES complex formation. Its linearised form, 1/v = (Km/Vmax)(1/[S]) + 1/Vmax, gives slope = Km/Vmax, y-intercept = 1/Vmax, x-intercept = −1/Km. Catalytic efficiency is expressed as kcat/Km (M⁻¹ s⁻¹).

#### Inhibition patterns on Lineweaver-Burk plot

| Inhibitor type | Effect on Km (app) | Effect on Vmax | Plot signature |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Competitive | Increases | Unchanged | Lines intersect y-axis |
| Non-competitive | Unchanged | Decreases | Lines intersect x-axis |
| Uncompetitive | Decreases | Decreases | Parallel lines |
| Mixed | Variable | Decreases | Intersect left of y-axis |

#### High-yield pointers
- **Apoenzyme** (protein) + **cofactor** (metal) ± **coenzyme** (vitamin-derived) = **holoenzyme**.
- Prosthetic groups (e.g. FAD in succinate dehydrogenase) stay tightly bound; coenzymes (e.g. NAD+) dissociate.
- **pI** for neutral amino acids = (pKa1 + pKa2) / 2; for acidic or basic amino acids, average all three pKa values.
- **Isozymes** (LDH 1–5) catalyse the same reaction but differ in tissue distribution — LDH-1 (H4) is cardiac, LDH-5 (M4) is hepatic.
- **Zymogens** (trypsinogen, chymotrypsinogen, pepsinogen) are inactive precursors activated by proteolytic cleavage.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### IUBMB enzyme classification (EC system)
The Enzyme Commission assigns a four-digit EC code: EC 1 **oxidoreductases** (transfer H/e⁻, e.g. lactate dehydrogenase), EC 2 **transferases** (move groups, e.g. ALT), EC 3 **hydrolases** (cleave with water, e.g. amylase), EC 4 **lyases** (add across double bonds, e.g. aldolase), EC 5 **isomerases** (rearrange geometry, e.g. phosphoglucomutase), EC 6 **ligases** (ATP-dependent joining, e.g. DNA ligase).

#### Worked micro-example (Lineweaver-Burk)
Without inhibitor: Vmax = 100 µmol·min⁻¹, Km = 1 mM. With a competitive inhibitor at 2 mM, the apparent Km doubles to 2 mM but Vmax stays at 100 µmol·min⁻¹. On the reciprocal plot the y-intercept (1/Vmax = 0.01) is unchanged, the slope doubles from 0.01 to 0.02 (min·mM·µmol⁻¹), and the x-intercept moves from −1.0 to −0.5 mM⁻¹. The student must read this intersection pattern, not memorise it.

#### Common traps examiners exploit
- **ΔG confusion**: enzymes alter kinetics, never thermodynamics.
- **Units of Vmax**: must be expressed as a rate (mol L⁻¹ s⁻¹), not concentration.
- **Cardiac markers**: troponin I/T has replaced CK-MB as the preferred marker; CK-MB peaks earlier than AST.
- **Allosteric enzymes** (PFK-1, aspartate transcarbamoylase) show sigmoidal v vs [S] curves and are insensitive to Michaelis-Menten analysis; Hill coefficient h > 1 quantifies cooperativity.

#### Practice prompts
1. Calculate Km and Vmax from Lineweaver-Burk intercepts −1.0 mM⁻¹ and 0.01 min·µmol⁻¹, and classify an inhibitor that doubles slope without shifting the y-intercept.
2. A patient with elevated LDH-1, CK-MB and troponin I most likely has myocardial infarction — justify which marker is most specific and why AST elevation lags behind.
