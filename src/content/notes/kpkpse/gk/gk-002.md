# Pakistan's Constitution and Governance Structure

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Pakistan operates under the **1973 Constitution** (promulgated 14 August 1973), the supreme law of the Islamic Republic of Pakistan. It establishes a **federal parliamentary democratic republic** with Islam as the state religion and organises state power into three organs: **Executive, Legislature, and Judiciary**. The **bicameral Majlis-e-Shoora** consists of the **National Assembly (336 seats, including reserved seats)** and the **Senate (96 general seats + 4 minority seats = 100 total)**. Legislative authority is split between the **Federal List, Provincial List, and Concurrent List** under the **Fourth Schedule**.

- **State religion:** Islam (Article 2).
- **State territory:** Article 1 — provinces, FATA (merged into KP, 25th Amendment, May 2018), AJK, GB, ICT.
- **Executive head (real):** Prime Minister; ceremonial head: President.
- **Highest court:** Supreme Court of Pakistan (Islamabad).
- **Amendment threshold:** two-thirds majority of **each House** of Parliament (Article 239).

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"FLPC–SJI"** = **F**ederal List, **L**egislature (Majlis-e-Shoora), **P**rime Minister (executive), **C**oncurrent List — **S**upreme Court, **J**udicial review, **I**slamic provisions (Article 2/31). Recite this acronym on the answer sheet to anchor the four pillars before you list articles.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Salient Features of the 1973 Constitution
The document was drafted by an elected parliament under Zulfikar Ali Bhutto and consolidated three earlier constitutions (1956, 1962, plus the 1972 Interim Constitutional Order). It has 280 Articles grouped into Parts (I–XII) plus 7 Schedules. Federalism, parliamentary democracy, independence of the judiciary, Islamic provisions, and fundamental rights are its defining pillars.

#### Federation and Legislative Lists
The **Fourth Schedule** divides powers:
- **Federal List:** defence, foreign affairs, currency, railways, posts, telegraph.
- **Provincial List:** local government, police (provincial), health, education (provincial subjects).
- **Concurrent List:** criminal law, civil procedure, population planning, environmental protection — both federal and provincial assemblies can legislate; federal law prevails in conflict.

> 📌 **Formula Check:** **Amendment formula** = majority of total membership **of each House** (not joint session) + ratification by **not less than one-half** of the provincial assemblies (Article 239). Misreading "each House" as "joint sitting" is the most common paper-spoiler.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem
**Question:** Which Article of the 1973 Constitution declares Islam as the state religion of Pakistan, and what is the minimum parliamentary majority required to amend the Constitution under Article 239?
(A) Article 2; two-thirds majority of members present and voting
(B) Article 2; two-thirds majority of total membership of each House
(C) Article 31; simple majority in joint sitting
(D) Article 227; two-thirds majority of National Assembly alone

#### Solution:
1. Identify the article: **Article 2** declares "Islam shall be the State religion of Pakistan." Article 31 obliges Muslims to arrange their lives per Quran and Sunnah but is not the state-religion declaration.
2. Identify the amendment threshold: Article 239 requires a **two-thirds majority of the total membership of each House separately** — i.e., National Assembly and Senate vote independently, not in joint sitting.
3. Apply both to options: Option B matches both facts. Option A fails on the threshold wording ("present and voting" is the ordinary statute formula). Option C reverses Article 31's purpose. Option D misnames the article.
4. **Answer: (B) Article 2; two-thirds majority of total membership of each House.**

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students routinely select option A because every other amendment in Pakistan uses "present and voting." Article 239 is the exception — it demands a fraction of **total membership**, not of those who bothered to vote. Memorise this distinction.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Modern Constitutional Shifts
The **18th Amendment (2010)** devolved several concurrent subjects to provinces and renamed NWFP as **Khyber Pakhtunkhwa** — directly relevant to KPK PMS aspirants. The **25th Amendment (May 2018)** merged **FATA into KP**, removing tribal areas from the federal list and adding 25 new general seats + 4 women + 1 minority seat to the KP Provincial Assembly. The **26th Amendment (2024)** restructured the Judicial Commission for superior-court appointments, replacing the previous Article 175-A setup — any answer relying on pre-2024 commission composition is now obsolete.

#### Provincial vs Federal Court Hierarchy
The **Federal Shariat Court** (Article 203-D to 203-J) examines whether laws conform to Islamic injunctions; its appellate jurisdiction covers Hudood offences. The **Supreme Court** (Article 176) sits in Islamabad with appellate and original jurisdiction. **High Courts** exist in each province plus the Islamabad High Court (2010, 18th Amendment). The **Chief Justice of Pakistan** heads the superior judiciary.

#### Edge-Case Traps Table

| Trap | Wrong belief | Correct position |
|---|---|---|
| Senate size | Senate has 100 elected seats | Senate has 96 general + 4 minority = **100 total**; minority seats nominated by parties |
| FATA status | FATA still exists | Merged into KP via 25th Amendment, May 2018 |
| AJK status | AJK is the 5th province | AJK is a self-governing territory; Pakistan's federal units are 4 provinces + ICT + GB; AJK is administered under its own Interim Constitution Act, 1974 |
| President's role | President runs the country | President is ceremonial; PM and Cabinet exercise executive authority under parliamentary form |
| Amendment count | Pakistan has 26 amendments only | 26th Amendment (2024) is latest confirmed; always verify the current count in the official Gazette |
| Article 239 wording | Provincial ratification is one-third | It is **one-half** of the provincial assemblies |
