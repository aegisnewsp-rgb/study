# Atoms

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Atomic physics in JEE Main rests on **Rutherford's nuclear model** (from α-scattering) and **Bohr's quantised orbits** for hydrogen and hydrogen-like ions. The Bohr model gives discrete energy levels from quantisation of angular momentum, and transitions between these levels produce the spectral series (Lyman, Balmer, Paschen, Brackett, Pfund).

- **Impact parameter:** $b = \dfrac{Z e^{2}}{4\pi\varepsilon_{0}\, KE}$ (m), where $Z$ is atomic number, $e = 1.6\times10^{-19}$ C, $\varepsilon_{0}=8.85\times10^{-12}$ C²/(N·m²), $KE$ in joules.
- **Distance of closest approach:** $r_{0}=\dfrac{Z e^{2}}{4\pi\varepsilon_{0}\,KE}$ — gives the upper limit of nuclear size, ~10⁻¹⁵ m for heavy nuclei.
- **Bohr quantisation:** $mvr = \dfrac{nh}{2\pi}=n\hbar$, $n=1,2,3\ldots$
- **Radius of nth orbit:** $r_{n}=\dfrac{n^{2} a_{0}}{Z}$, where $a_{0}=0.529$ Å (Bohr radius).
- **Energy of nth orbit:** $E_{n}=-\dfrac{13.6\,Z^{2}}{n^{2}}$ eV; hydrogen ionisation energy = 13.6 eV.
- **Rydberg formula (wavenumber):** $\bar{\nu}=\dfrac{1}{\lambda}=R Z^{2}\!\left(\dfrac{1}{n_{1}^{2}}-\dfrac{1}{n_{2}^{2}}\right)$, $R=1.097\times10^{7}$ m⁻¹, $n_{1}<n_{2}$.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"Lyman Balmer Paschen Brackett Pfund"** maps to $n_{1}=1,2,3,4,5$ and to the spectrum regions **"UltraViolet, Visible, InfraRed, InfraRed, InfraRed"** — sing the initials: **LUVPIR²**.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Rutherford's Nuclear Model

The Geiger–Marsden α-scattering experiment (1909–1911) showed that a tiny fraction of α-particles rebounded at large angles. Rutherford interpreted this as a hard, positively charged nucleus concentrated within ~10⁻¹⁵ m, surrounded by electrons whose total mass is negligible. The key derived quantities are the **impact parameter** $b$ (perpendicular distance of the α-particle's trajectory from the nucleus if undeflected) and the **distance of closest approach** $r_{0}$ (the turning point for a head-on collision).

- $b=\dfrac{Z e^{2}}{4\pi\varepsilon_{0} KE}$ — determines the scattering angle $\theta$ via $\tan(\theta/2)=\dfrac{b}{r_{0}}$.
- $r_{0}=\dfrac{Z e^{2}}{4\pi\varepsilon_{0} KE}$ — directly measures nuclear size; for gold ($Z=79$) with 5 MeV α-particles, $r_{0}\approx 4.5\times10^{-14}$ m.

#### Bohr's Postulates and Hydrogen-like Ions

Bohr applied three postulates to hydrogen and hydrogen-like ions (He⁺, Li²⁺, Be³⁺ …): (1) electrons orbit without radiating in **stationary orbits**, (2) angular momentum is quantised as $mvr=n\hbar$, and (3) photon energy matches the orbit jump, $h\nu=E_{i}-E_{f}$. Combining electrostatic centripetal force $mv^{2}/r=kZe^{2}/r^{2}$ with $mvr=n\hbar$ yields $r_{n}=n^{2}a_{0}/Z$ and $E_{n}=-13.6\,Z^{2}/n^{2}$ eV.

| **Lyman** | 1 | 2,3,4… | Ultraviolet | 121.6 nm |
| **Balmer** | 2 | 3,4,5… | Visible | 656.3 nm (Hα, red) |
| **Paschen** | 3 | 4,5,6… | Infrared | 1875 nm |
| **Brackett** | 4 | 5,6,7… | Infrared | 4051 nm |
| **Pfund** | 5 | 6,7,8… | Infrared | 7458 nm |

| Confusion | Correct fact |

| First excitation energy | $E_{2}-E_{1}=10.2$ eV (NOT 13.6 eV) |
| Ionisation energy of H | $E_{\infty}-E_{1}=13.6$ eV |
| Angular momentum quantisation | $mvr=nh/(2\pi)=n\hbar$ (NOT $mvr=nh$) |
| Radius of hydrogen-like ion | $r_{n}=n^{2}a_{0}/Z$ (Z in denominator) |

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** An electron in a hydrogen atom drops from the $n=4$ level to $n=2$. Find (a) the wavelength emitted, and (b) the de Broglie wavelength of the electron in the $n=4$ orbit. Given: $R=1.097\times10^{7}$ m⁻¹, $a_{0}=0.529$ Å, $h=6.626\times10^{-34}$ J·s.

#### Solution:

(a) Use Rydberg formula with $Z=1$, $n_{1}=2$, $n_{2}=4$:
$$\bar{\nu}=\dfrac{1}{\lambda}=R\!\left(\dfrac{1}{2^{2}}-\dfrac{1}{4^{2}}\right)=R\!\left(\dfrac{1}{4}-\dfrac{1}{16}\right)=R\cdot\dfrac{3}{16}$$
$$\lambda=\dfrac{16}{3R}=\dfrac{16}{3\times1.097\times10^{7}}=4.86\times10^{-7}\text{ m}=486\text{ nm}$$
This is the Hβ line of the **Balmer series** (visible, blue-green).

(b) de Broglie wavelength in nth orbit: $\lambda_{n}=\dfrac{2\pi r_{n}}{n}$.
$$r_{4}=4^{2}\cdot a_{0}=16\times0.529\times10^{-10}=8.46\times10^{-10}\text{ m}$$
$$\lambda_{4}=\dfrac{2\pi\times 8.46\times10^{-10}}{4}=1.33\times10^{-9}\text{ m}=1.33\text{ nm}$$

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students often write $mvr=nh$ (dropping $2\pi$), giving an orbit radius 4× too large and energy 4× wrong in magnitude. Always confirm the angular momentum relation is $mvr=n\hbar$ before plugging in.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Quantum Limits

Bohr's model is a hybrid of classical and quantum ideas. It works exactly only for **one-electron systems** (H, He⁺, Li²⁺). For multi-electron atoms, electron–electron repulsion invalidates the central-force assumption, and the model fails. It also cannot explain spectral line intensities, the Zeeman effect, or fine structure. The de Broglie standing-wave interpretation rescues part of Bohr's picture: the circumference $2\pi r_{n}$ must equal an integer number of wavelengths, i.e. $\lambda=2\pi r_{n}/n$, which is mathematically equivalent to $mvr=n\hbar$.

For a hydrogen-like ion, every quantity inherits a $Z$ correction. The **ionisation energy scales as $Z^{2}$**: ionising He⁺ requires 54.4 eV, Li²⁺ requires 122.4 eV. The **Lyman-α transition** of He⁺ (3→1) sits at 30.4 nm, deep in the ultraviolet.

#### Common Advanced Traps

1. **Mixing KE units.** The impact parameter uses $KE$ in joules. If given in eV, convert: $KE_{J}=KE_{eV}\times1.6\times10^{-19}$. Plugging eV into the formula gives a $r_{0}$ value wrong by ~19 orders of magnitude.
2. **Scattering formula misuse.** Rutherford's $\cot(\theta/2)=\dfrac{2b\,KE}{Z e^{2}/(4\pi\varepsilon_{0})}$ must use $KE$ in joules. Students forget the factor of 2 and lose half the marks.
3. **Energy sign confusion.** Total energy $E_{n}$ is negative (bound state); kinetic energy $KE_{n}=-E_{n}=+13.6\,Z^{2}/n^{2}$ eV is positive; potential energy $U_{n}=2E_{n}=-27.2\,Z^{2}/n^{2}$ eV is twice as negative as total. This $E=KE+U$ relation, with $U=-2\,KE$, holds for any inverse-square orbit.
4. **Series wavelength ordering: as $n_{1}$ increases, wavelengths grow longer** and shift redward — a quick check: Lyman ~120 nm, Balmer ~650 nm, Paschen ~1875 nm.

#### Connection to Adjacent Topics

- **Dual Nature of Matter and Radiation:** the de Broglie wavelength in Bohr orbits ($\lambda=h/(mv)$) links to the standing-wave condition.
- **Nuclei:** Rutherford's nuclear size estimate feeds into nuclear density ($\sim10^{17}$ kg/m³) covered in the Nuclei chapter.
- **Communication Systems (semiconductors/lasers):** the principle of stimulated emission between Bohr levels underpins laser action.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. Compute the speed of the electron in the ground state of He⁺. (Use $v_{n}=Ze^{2}/(2\varepsilon_{0}nh)$ or $v_{n}=Z\alpha c/n$, with $\alpha\approx1/137$.) Compare to $c$.
2. Show that the ratio of magnetic moment to angular momentum for a Bohr orbit equals $e/(2m)$, independent of $n$ and $Z$ — this is the classical **gyromagnetic ratio** later refined in quantum mechanics.
