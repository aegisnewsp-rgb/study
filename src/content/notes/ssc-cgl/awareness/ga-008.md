# Books and Authors

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

The Books and Authors segment under SSC CGL General Awareness evaluates factual recall across Indian classical texts, Indian freedom struggle literature, contemporary award-winning novels, and prominent global classics.

SSC CGL examination questions typically follow three direct formats: identifying the author of a specified book, identifying the literary work associated with an author, or identifying the book that earned a major literary accolade such as the Jnanpith Award, Sahitya Akademi Award, Booker Prize, or Nobel Prize in Literature.

High-yield historical pairings include:
- *Gitanjali* by Rabindranath Tagore (Nobel Prize in Literature, 1913; first Asian recipient).
- *Anandmath* by Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay (origin of national song *Vande Mataram*).
- *Discovery of India* and *Glimpses of World History* by Jawaharlal Nehru.
- *Unhappy India* by Lala Lajpat Rai; *India Wins Freedom* by Maulana Abul Kalam Azad.
- *The White Tiger* by Aravind Adiga (Man Booker Prize 2008).
- *Midnight's Children* by Salman Rushdie (Booker Prize 1981, Booker of Bookers).

⚡ **SSC CGL Exam Strategy:** When reviewing modern Indian fiction, associate each author with their primary recurring themes. Mulk Raj Anand focused on social realism and caste barriers in *Untouchable*, whereas R.K. Narayan captured fictional small-town South Indian daily life in *Malgudi Days* and *The Guide*.

#### 3. Standard Exam Pitfalls and Distinctions
- **Confusing Similar Titles:** *Young India* as a journal was edited by Mahatma Gandhi, but the book *Young India* was written by Lala Lajpat Rai in the United States.
- **Anandmath vs. Gitanjali:** Anandmath was written in Bengali by Bankim Chandra in 1882 during the Sanyasi rebellion; Gitanjali was published by Tagore in 1910 and translated into English in 1912.
- **Autobiographies of Prime Ministers:** *The Discovery of India* is by Jawaharlal Nehru; *Matters of Discretion* is by I.K. Gujral; *My Country My Life* is by L.K. Advani.

#### 2. Prominent Global Classics and Nobel Laureates

SSC CGL regularly includes world literature questions, particularly works by influential sociopolitical authors:
- **George Orwell:** *Animal Farm* (political allegory on totalitarianism) and *1984* (concept of Big Brother and dystopia).
- **Charles Dickens:** *A Tale of Two Cities* (French Revolution setting) and *David Copperfield*.
- **Leo Tolstoy:** *War and Peace* (Napoleonic invasion of Russia) and *Anna Karenina*.
- **Victor Hugo:** *Les Misérables* and *The Hunchback of Notre-Dame*.
- **Ernest Hemingway:** *The Old Man and the Sea* (Pulitzer 1953, Nobel Prize 1954).
- **Gabriel García Márquez:** *One Hundred Years of Solitude* (magic realism, Nobel Prize 1982).
