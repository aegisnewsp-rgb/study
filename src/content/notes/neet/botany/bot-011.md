# Transport

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Plant transport splits into two highways: **xylem** (water + minerals, root → leaf, one-way) and **phloem** (sugars + hormones, source → sink, two-way). The single formula that NEET repeats every year is **Ψw = Ψs + Ψp** — water potential drives osmosis.

Three facts cover 70% of the questions:

- **Water moves from high Ψw to low Ψw** (never the other way around).
- **Xylem ascent** is pulled, not pushed — transpiration pull ≈ −2 MPa at the leaf, sustained by cohesion of water columns.
- **Phloem transport** follows Münch's mass-flow model: sugar loaded at source → osmotic water entry → turgor pressure pushes sap to sink.

| Term | One-line meaning | NEET favourite? |
|---|---|---|
| Diffusion | Passive movement down a concentration gradient | Yes |
| Osmosis | Water movement across a semipermeable membrane down Ψw gradient | Very |
| Plasmolysis | Protoplast shrinks away from wall in hypertonic solution | Very |
| Imbibition | Water adsorption by dry seeds/spongy structures | Moderate |
| Active transport | Movement against gradient using ATP (e.g., K⁺ into guard cells) | Often paired with stomata |

> 💡 **Memory Hook — "SAPS" for the four transport modes: Simple diffusion, Active transport, Plasmolysis trigger, Symplast/Apoplast.** For xylem vs phloem direction, remember **"X = eXit only (one-way up), P = Push both ways"**.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Water potential is the master variable — every other transport process is a special case of it.

#### Water Potential and Its Components

Water potential (Ψw) is the chemical potential of water relative to pure water at standard conditions, expressed in **Pa (or MPa)**. The dimension is M L⁻¹ T⁻².

- **Ψw = Ψs + Ψp**, where Ψs = solute (osmotic) potential, Ψp = pressure potential.
- **Ψs = −i C R T**, with i = van't Hoff factor (dimensionless), C = molar concentration (mol L⁻¹), R = 8.314 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹, T = temperature (K).
- **Osmotic pressure π = i C R T** — numerically equal to |Ψs| but always positive; Ψs is always negative (or zero) for a solution.
- **Pressure potential** is zero in a flaccid/open cell, positive in a turgid cell, negative in xylem under tension (Ψp can reach −2 MPa in transpiring leaves).

> 📌 **Formula Check:** Pure water has Ψw = 0. Adding solute lowers Ψw (more negative Ψs). For a fully turgid plant cell, Ψw = 0 because the positive Ψp cancels the negative Ψs — this is the equilibrium used in plasmolysis diagrams.

#### The Two Long-Distance Pathways

| Feature | Xylem | Phloem |
|---|---|---|
| Direction | Unidirectional (root → leaf) | Bidirectional (source ↔ sink) |
| Content | Water + mineral ions | Sucrose + amino acids + hormones |
| Driving force | Transpiration pull (cohesion–tension) | Pressure-flow (Münch hypothesis) |
| Living elements required | Dead tracheids + vessels only | Sieve tubes (alive, enucleate) + companion cells |
| Typical pressure inside | Negative (tension) | Positive (up to ~30 atm experimentally) |

The **cohesion–tension theory** (Dixon–Joly, 1894) holds because: (1) water molecules hydrogen-bond to each other, (2) water adheres to xylem walls, and (3) evaporation from mesophyll creates the pulling gradient. Root pressure (positive Ψp from ion accumulation in roots) and capillarity are supplementary — they matter most at night when transpiration is near zero.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix — Easily Confused Pairs

| Confused terms | Correct distinction |
|---|---|
| Osmotic pressure (π) vs Solute potential (Ψs) | Both = iCRT in magnitude; π is a positive pressure value, Ψs is a negative component of Ψw |
| Symplast vs Apoplast | Symplast = cytoplasm + plasmodesmata (crosses membranes? no — passes cell-to-cell through plasmodesmata); Apoplast = cell wall + intercellular space (no membrane crossed) |
| Source vs Sink | Source = net exporter of sugar (mature leaf, storage organ during mobilisation); Sink = net importer (root, fruit, growing tip) — roles can reverse seasonally |
| Transpiration vs Guttation | Transpiration = vapour loss through stomata; guttation = liquid water exuded through hydathodes at night when root pressure is high |
| Plasmolysis vs Deplasmolysis | Plasmolysis = hypertonic, water leaves; deplasmolysis = hypotonic, water re-enters, protoplast returns to wall |
| Mass flow (phloem) vs Diffusion | Mass flow needs pressure difference and a bulk solvent; diffusion needs only a concentration gradient |

#### Worked Problem — Water Potential Calculation

A plant cell has a solute potential Ψs = −0.8 MPa and is placed in pure water. After equilibrium it becomes fully turgid with Ψw = 0 MPa. Find the pressure potential Ψp at equilibrium.

1. At equilibrium the cell's Ψw equals the external Ψw = 0 MPa (pure water).
2. Apply Ψw = Ψs + Ψp → 0 = −0.8 + Ψp.
3. Ψp = +0.8 MPa.

The pressure potential equals +0.8 MPa, representing the turgor pushing the protoplast against the cell wall. The cell is fully turgid because Ψs and Ψp exactly balance. Numerically, this also equals the osmotic pressure π inside the cell — an important identity NEET tests by asking which of the two equals 0.8 MPa here.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students write π = Ψs = −0.8 MPa, which mixes sign convention. Osmotic pressure is always positive (0.8 MPa here); solute potential is always negative (−0.8 MPa). Confusing them loses the mark even when the magnitude is right.

#### Stomatal Mechanics (K⁺-Flux Model)

Stomata open in light because blue light activates H⁺-ATPases in guard-cell membranes, pumping H⁺ out. The resulting electrochemical gradient drives K⁺ uptake through voltage-gated channels; Cl⁻ and malate²⁻ follow as counter-ions. Water enters osmotically, Ψp rises, guard cells become turgid, and the asymmetric wall thickenings (thicker on the inner side) force them to bow apart. ABA reverses the process during drought, closing stomata and conserving water.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Beyond the textbook picture lie quantitative limits, exceptions and questions NEET designers love to ambush with.

#### Edge Cases and Boundary Conditions

- **Negative pressure in xylem is metastable.** A water column at −2 MPa remains continuous only if no air seeding occurs. Cavitation breaks the column, and the plant refills embolised vessels by root pressure at night — this is why root pressure matters even though it cannot raise water to a 100 m tree.
- **Pressure-flow hypothesis requires sieve plates** to offer enough resistance for a pressure differential to build. If plates were absent, sap would equalise and stop moving.
- **Imbibition precedes true osmosis** during seed germination — water is adsorbed by hydrophilic colloids (proteins, starch, cellulose) and is not freely available as a solvent until the cell membrane reorganises.
- **Active transport is essential at two points:** loading sucrose into phloem at the source (via sucrose–H⁺ symporters) and pumping K⁺ into guard cells for stomatal opening. Passive bulk flow handles everything in between.
- **Casparian strip in the endodermis** blocks apoplastic flow, forcing water and ions to cross a membrane before entering the xylem — this gives the plant selective control over what reaches the transpiration stream.

#### Advanced Traps and Numerical Limits

1. Treating transpiration pull (~−2 MPa) as equivalent to atmospheric pressure (~0.1 MPa) — they differ by 20×, which is exactly why tall trees can pull water 100+ m upward.
2. Writing Ψs = +iCRT — solute potential is always zero (pure water) or negative.
3. Believing that phloem sap moves only downwards — radioactive-tracer studies (Münch, 1930) showed simultaneous upward and downward flow in the same plant.
4. Forgetting that diffusion rate scales with 1/√M (Graham's law analogue) — heavier ions like SO₄²⁻ move slower than Cl⁻ even at the same concentration.
5. Assuming stomatal closure prevents all water loss — cuticular transpiration (5–10% of total) continues regardless.
6. Confusing guttation fluid with dew; guttation appears at leaf tips through hydathodes in high-humidity nights.
7. Using "ascent of sap" and "transpiration pull" interchangeably — ascent of sap is the phenomenon; transpiration pull, root pressure and capillarity are its three proposed forces.

#### Adjacent Topic Connections

- **Mineral nutrition** depends on the same active transporters in root hair cells that set up root pressure.
- **Photosynthesis** rate is indirectly regulated by stomatal aperture — linking Transport with Photosynthesis is a frequent assertion–reason pair.
- **Seed germination** physiology uses imbibition before vascular transport matures.
- **Plant growth regulators** (auxin, cytokinin, ABA) move partly through phloem, partly through specialised polar transport.
