# Reading — Comprehension, Vocabulary and Strategies — Qatar Secondary Certificate English Notes

The MoEHE English Curriculum Standards at Grade 12 publish five Reading sub-strands (R1-R5): responding to main ideas, responding to details, working with sounds-spelling relationships at advanced word level, developing appropriate reading strategies, and showing interest in reading. The Grade 12 CEFR exit level is B2 Low-Mid, which requires reading authentic texts on abstract and concrete topics, recognising writers' attitudes and viewpoints, and selecting information from long passages.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Skimming:** reading quickly for gist (main idea of each section).
- **Scanning:** reading quickly for specific information (a name, date, figure).
- **Inferring:** reading between the lines — what the writer implies but does not state.
- **B2 vocabulary:** academic word list (AWL) high-utility items; collocations; phrasal verbs.
- **Text types:** narrative, expository, argumentative, descriptive, persuasive.
- **Reading strategies:** preview headings, predict content, annotate, summarise paragraphs.

#### Examiner traps

- Reading too slowly — B2 requires speed as well as accuracy.
- Treating "Not Given" as "False" — they are different in IELTS-style reading but the same reading comprehension skill applies: state what the passage says.
- Over-relying on prior knowledge — the question asks what THIS passage says.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### R1-R2: Responding to main ideas and details

Main idea: identify the topic sentence (often the first or last sentence of a paragraph) and the supporting details. For a 5-paragraph text, the main idea is often stated in the introduction and re-stated in the conclusion.

Details: locate specific information using scanning. Practice with WH-questions (who, what, when, where, why, how) — the answer is usually in a single sentence, often within three lines of the question's reference point.

#### R3: Sounds and spelling at advanced word level

B2 readers recognise spelling patterns for prefixes and suffixes (un-, in-, dis-, -tion, -ment, -able, -ible), and use them to infer the meaning of unfamiliar words. Etymology helps: words from Latin and Greek roots cluster into meaning families (graph = write, photo = light, bio = life).

#### R4: Reading strategies

Preview: read the first sentence of each paragraph before reading the full text. Predict: what do you expect the text to argue or describe? Annotate: mark topic sentences, key terms, transitions. Summarise: at the end of each section, write a 1-sentence summary. Review: check predictions against the actual content.

Active reading: ask questions before each section (what do I expect?), read, then check. Compare: what does the section say vs what you expected?

#### R5: Showing interest in reading

B2 readers engage with texts beyond the minimum: they identify the writer's purpose, recognise tone and bias, and relate the content to broader issues. Practice by reading editorial pages, opinion columns, and academic articles outside class.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### Where reading appears at the terminal

Reading comprehension is tested across the Grade 12 English terminal paper. Texts are drawn from authentic sources — articles, essays, reports, literary extracts — and include both literary and informational texts (MoEHE R5).

#### Exam technique

- Read the questions first, then read the passage — you know what to look for.
- For "Not Given" questions, check that the passage does not mention the topic at all. If it mentions but contradicts, the answer is "False".
- For inference questions, the answer is supported by the passage but not stated directly — combine two or three pieces of evidence.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "The main idea is always in the first sentence." Often, but not always. Check the whole paragraph.
- "Vocabulary in context = look up the word." No — the question tests the meaning AS USED in THIS passage. Use context clues, not dictionary definitions.
- "Skimming = skipping details." Skimming is for main idea; scanning is for specific information. Use the right one for the question.
