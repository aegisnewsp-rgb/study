# Income Tax — Company Taxation

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

A **domestic company** is taxed on its worldwide income at a flat rate — **25%** if total turnover ≤ ₹400 Cr in the previous year, else **30%**. Three alternate regimes sit on top: the **normal regime with MAT**, the **115BAA regime (22% + 10% surcharge + 4% cess = 25.168% effective)**, and the **115BAB regime (15%, new manufacturing companies only = 17.16% effective)**.

**MAT under section 115JB** = **15% of Book Profit**, payable when it exceeds normal tax. Book Profit starts from Net Profit as per the Statement of Profit and Loss and is adjusted by additions/deductions listed in section 115JB(2).

| Item | Rate | Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Normal tax | 25% / 30% | Slab based on turnover |
| 115BAA | 22% + SC + Cess | Irrevocable option, no MAT |
| 115BAB | 15% + SC + Cess | New mfg co. incorporated on/after 01-10-2019 |
| MAT u/s 115JB | 15% of Book Profit | Book Profit > normal tax |

High-yield pointers: **Health & Education Cess = 4%** on (tax + surcharge); **Surcharge = 7%** (income > ₹1 Cr) or **12%** (> ₹10 Cr). MAT credit is carry-forwardable for **15 assessment years**.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Scope and Rate Structure
Every company registered under the Companies Act, 2013 (or earlier Acts) is a "company" for the Income-tax Act, 1961. A **domestic company** — one whose entire control and management is situated in India — pays tax on **worldwide income** at flat corporate rates. There is **no slab benefit**: 25% applies when total turnover ≤ ₹400 Cr, else 30%.

The effective tax under the normal regime is therefore:
**Tax + Surcharge + Health & Education Cess @ 4%**,
where surcharge is 7% when total income exceeds ₹1 Cr but does not exceed ₹10 Cr, and 12% above ₹10 Cr.

#### Sections 115BAA and 115BAB
Section **115BAA** (introduced by the Taxation Laws (Amendment) Ordinance, 2019) offers **22% + 10% surcharge + 4% cess** — a blanket 25.168% effective rate. The option is **irrevocable**, removes MAT liability, blocks set-off of earlier MAT credits, denies deduction under sections 10AA, 80IA to 80RRB (other than R&D), and disallows brought-forward business loss/depreciation.

Section **115BAB** grants a sharper **15% rate** but only to a **new domestic manufacturing company** incorporated on or after 1 October 2019, which commenced manufacturing by 31 March 2024, does not use second-hand plant and machinery (except used imported plant), and is not formed by splitting or reconstructing an existing business.

#### Minimum Alternate Tax — Section 115JB
MAT ensures companies with low taxable income still pay a floor tax based on **book profit**:
**Tax payable = max(Normal tax on Total Income, 15% of Book Profit) + SC + Cess**.
Book Profit = Net Profit shown in the P&L **plus** amounts inadmissible under section 115JB(2) (e.g. provisions, losses of associate, dividend from Indian subsidiary) **less** amounts creditable under section 115JB(2). Foreign companies are **outside** MAT.

#### Dividend Taxation Post-FY 2020-21
DDT under old section 115-O was abolished. Dividends are now taxed in the hands of shareholders at slab rates; the company pays **TDS u/s 194** at 10% (resident) or 20% (NRI). The dividend is paid out of post-tax profits and is **not deductible** in company hands.

#### Set-off and Audit Quick Hooks
- **Set-off of losses** of a closely-held company against another person's income is restricted under section 79 unless **≥26% voting power** is held unchanged throughout.
- **Tax Audit u/s 44AB** is compulsory when turnover exceeds **₹1 Cr** (₹10 Cr if ≥75% of transactions are digital and cash transactions ≤ 5%).

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Worked Mini-Example — Normal Regime vs 115BAA
X Pvt. Ltd., a domestic company with turnover ₹500 Cr, has Total Income ₹100 Cr under the normal regime.

- **Normal regime:** Tax = 30% × 100 Cr = 30 Cr; Surcharge @ 12% = 3.6 Cr; Cess @ 4% on (30 + 3.6) = 1.344 Cr → **Total = 34.944 Cr**.
- **115BAA regime:** Tax = 22% × 100 Cr = 22 Cr; Surcharge @ 10% = 2.2 Cr; Cess @ 4% on (22 + 2.2) = 0.968 Cr → **Total = 25.168 Cr**.

> **Trap:** The 12% surcharge in the normal regime costs ~9.8 Cr extra. But option 115BAA forfeits brought-forward MAT credit — run the numbers **over 15 years**, not one year.

#### Edge Cases and Adjacent Connections
**Deemed dividend u/s 2(22)(e)** lifts the corporate veil when a shareholder (≥10% voting power / beneficial owner / director) receives a loan or advance from the company without declaration as dividend — taxed in the shareholder's hands. Buy-back is now taxed as **capital gain for the shareholder u/s 45(2A)** with the company paying buy-back tax **u/s 115QA at 20%**.

Section **115JC (AMT)** applies to **non-corporates** (LLPs, AOPs, partnership firms) paying concessional rates under Chapter XII-BA — at **18.5% of Adjusted Total Income**. Do not confuse AMT with MAT: MAT is corporate-specific.

Under section **115BAA, the option must be filed before the due date u/s 139(1)** via Form 10-IC. Once filed for an AY, the option cannot be withdrawn and applies to **all subsequent AYs**.

#### Common Mistakes Students Lose Marks On
- Using outdated surcharge slabs (5% / 10%) instead of **7% / 12%**.
- Quoting 15% as the rate for *all* domestic companies and missing the 115BAB conditions.
- Treating DDT as still deductible in FY 2020-21 onwards — it is not.
- Treating MAT credit as lapsing after 10 years — it is now **15 AYs**.
- Believing a company with Nil income need not file a return — **ITR-6 is compulsory** for every company.

#### Practice Prompts
1. M/s Beta Ltd. (Indian, turnover ₹350 Cr) reports Total Income ₹20 Cr and Book Profit ₹80 Cr under MAT adjustments. Compute tax under the **normal regime with MAT** and under **section 115BAA**, recommending the cheaper option given ₹5 Cr MAT credit carry-forward.
2. R (holding 30% voting power in PQR Ltd.) takes ₹40 lakh from the company as an advance on 1 March 2026. The accumulated profit of PQR Ltd. exceeds ₹1 Cr. State the implication under section **2(22)(e)** and explain whether PQR Ltd. can route this through 115BAA to escape the consequence.
