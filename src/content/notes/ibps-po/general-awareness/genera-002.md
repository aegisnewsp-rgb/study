# RBI and Monetary Policy

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

- **RBI's Monetary Policy** is the toolkit the central bank uses to control money supply, credit cost, and credit availability in pursuit of price stability with growth support.
- The **Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)** is a 6-member body (3 RBI + 3 external experts) that sets the **Repo Rate**; the Governor casts the tie-breaking vote.
- The formal inflation target is **4% CPI** with a **±2% tolerance band (2%–6%)**; deviation for 3 consecutive quarters forces RBI to report to the Central Government.

| Rate / Ratio | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Repo Rate** | RBI lends short-term to banks against G-secs (de facto policy rate) |
| **SDF (Standing Deposit Facility)** | Banks park excess funds with RBI at repo − 25 bps (new floor since April 2022) |
| **MSF Rate** | Overnight penal borrowing at repo + 25 bps by dipping into SLR |
| **Bank Rate** | Aligned with MSF since April 2022 |
| **CRR / SLR** | Cash with RBI / liquid assets held, both as % of NDTL |

> **Exam tip:** A "rate hike" raises Repo, MSF, Bank Rate and the SDF floor together — memorize them as a ladder spaced 25 bps apart.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Institutional Architecture
The **Monetary Policy Committee**, created by the **Finance Act 2016** amending the RBI Act, meets at least **four times a year**. It has **6 members**: the RBI Governor (Chair), the Deputy Governor in charge of monetary policy, one RBI Board nominee — and **3 external experts** appointed by the Central Government. Decisions are by majority; the Governor holds the casting vote. The published resolution declares the policy **stance**: *accommodative* (eases), *neutral*, or *withdrawal of accommodation / hawkish*.

#### The Policy Rate Corridor
The Repo Rate anchors a corridor of standing facilities:

| Facility | Rate | Liquidity Direction |
|---|---|---|
| MSF (ceiling) | Repo + 25 bps | RBI lends overnight to banks |
| Repo (mid-point) | Repo | RBI lends against G-secs |
| SDF (floor) | Repo − 25 bps | Banks park funds with RBI |

The corridor replaced the old Reverse Repo Rate as the floor in **April 2022**.

#### Reserve Requirements
- **CRR** under Section 42(1), RBI Act: % of **Net Demand and Time Liabilities (NDTL)** kept as cash with RBI; **no interest** is paid — effectively a zero-cost funding source for RBI.
- **SLR** under Section 24, Banking Regulation Act, 1949: % of NDTL held as liquid assets (government securities, gold, or cash).
- **Money Multiplier** = 1 / CRR — a 1 percentage point cut in CRR mechanically expands the deposit base.

#### Inflation Targeting
Since 2014, RBI targets **CPI (Combined) inflation at 4% with a band of ±2%**. If inflation breaches **6%** or falls below **2%** for **three consecutive quarters**, the RBI must send a statutory report to the Central Government detailing causes and corrective steps.

> **Trap:** Many aspirants quote the upper band (6%) as the target. The **target is 4%**; the band is the tolerance window.

#### Exam Pattern in IBPS PO
Expect 1–3 questions — direct numerical (current Repo Rate, CRR/SLR, inflation target) or conceptual (MPC size, SDF vs Reverse Repo). Shift-1 of Mains 2023 had a question on SDF, reflecting the post-2022 emphasis.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Monetary Transmission Mechanism
Transmission is the lag between a repo-rate decision and its effect on bank lending, deposit, and bond yields. The RBI has publicly pushed for a **sharper, faster** transmission since 2019. Empirical studies (RBI's *Monetary Policy Report*, April 2024) show lending rates re-price within **3–6 months**, deposit rates faster (≈2 months), and G-sec yields essentially instantly. Frictions arise from **indebted sectors (MUDRA, MSME), bulk deposits, and floating-rate loan resets**.

#### Measuring Money: M0 → M3

| Aggregate | Components | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **M0 (Reserve Money)** | Currency in circulation + Bankers' deposits with RBI + Other deposits with RBI | RBI's operating target |
| **M1 (Narrow Money)** | Currency with public + Demand deposits + Other deposits with RBI | Transaction money |
| **M3 (Broad Money)** | M1 + Time deposits with banks | Most-watched for policy impact |

> **Common mistake:** Equating "money supply" with M1 alone. IBPS answers typically use **M3**.

#### Open Market Operations and LAF
The **Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF)** is the daily auction window through which RBI uses repo/reverse repo (now SDF) to absorb or inject liquidity. **OMO** — outright purchase or sale of G-secs — handles durable liquidity. When durable liquidity turns persistently negative (deposits < currency + demand), RBI conducts **Variable Rate Reverse Repo (VRRR)** auctions; when positive, it conducts **fine-tuning Variable Rate Repo (VRR)** operations.

#### Recent Operational Changes
- **April 2022**: SDF introduced as the new floor; Reverse Repo retained only as a policy reference, no longer the operative floor.
- **Bank Rate** aligned with MSF, removing the 25 bps gap that previously existed.
- **MPC meeting schedule**: bi-monthly since 2016, with the off-cycle meeting (May 2020) being the only historical exception.

#### Edge Cases and Traps
- A repo **cut** of 25 bps automatically pulls MSF down by 25 bps, Bank Rate by 25 bps, and SDF up by 25 bps — the **corridor moves as one**.
- **CRR vs SLR confusion**: CRR is *only cash with RBI*; SLR includes G-secs, gold, and cash. Both are % of NDTL, not of deposits alone.
- **Inflation targeting trigger** needs *three consecutive quarters* of breach — a single quarter does not invoke the statutory report.

#### Practice Prompts
1. *Numerical:* If CRR falls from 4.5% to 4.0% on an NDTL base of ₹200 lakh crore, by how much does required cash with RBI fall, and what is the theoretical deposit-multiplier expansion?
2. *Conceptual:* Explain why RBI does not pay interest on CRR balances and how this affects the cost of monetary tightening relative to an OMO sale of equivalent liquidity.
