# Passage Types and Disciplines — LSAT Reading Comprehension

The LSAT Reading Comprehension section includes four passages, drawn from four discipline areas: humanities, social sciences, natural sciences, and law-related topics. One of the four passages is a comparative reading pair (two passages on related topics). Each discipline demands a slightly different reading approach: law passages reward tracking the argument, humanities passages reward tracking the author's voice, social sciences passages reward tracking claims and evidence, and natural sciences passages reward tracking the explanation of a phenomenon.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### What passage type items test

- Recognising the discipline of the passage (humanities, social sciences, natural sciences, law).
- Adjusting reading strategy to the passage type.
- Identifying the central claim, theme, or purpose appropriate to the discipline.

#### One-pass drill

1. Skim the passage. Identify the discipline.
2. Read carefully with the discipline-appropriate strategy.
3. Answer items with the discipline in mind.

#### Examiner traps

- Reading a law passage as if it were a science passage. Law passages reward argument tracking; science passages reward explanation tracking.
- Treating all RC passages as the same. Each discipline has its own patterns.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Humanities passages

- Excerpts from literature, philosophy, art history, music, religion.
- Often feature personal voice, narrative, or reflective argument.
- Reading strategy: track the author's central theme or claim; pay attention to tone and word choice.

#### Social sciences passages

- Excerpts from sociology, political science, economics, education, anthropology, psychology.
- Often feature empirical claims, theoretical frameworks, or policy debates.
- Reading strategy: track the central claim and the evidence; identify the methodology if the passage describes a study.

#### Natural sciences passages

- Excerpts from biology, chemistry, physics, earth science, medicine.
- Often feature explanation of a phenomenon, a process, or a finding.
- Reading strategy: track the explanation; identify the phenomenon, the cause, and the implications.

#### Law-related passages

- Excerpts from legal scholarship, judicial opinions, law reviews.
- Often feature argument about a legal principle, a case, or a doctrine.
- Reading strategy: track the argument structure (claim, evidence, counter-argument, response); identify the legal principle.

#### Comparative reading passages

- Two passages on related topics, with items asking how they agree, disagree, or address the same issue.
- Reading strategy: read both passages, identify the central claim of each, then map the relationships.

#### Practice routine

- Take one passage from each discipline. Read each in 5 minutes.
- Identify the discipline, the central claim, and the structure.
- Answer the items.
- Time yourself: under 8 minutes per passage.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How passage type connects to the other RC skills

- **Main idea** depends on identifying the discipline; different disciplines reward different central claims.
- **Inference** depends on the discipline; law inferences are about the argument, science inferences are about the explanation.
- **Author's tone** depends on the discipline; humanities passages have distinctive voice; science passages are usually neutral.

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"All RC passages are the same."** Different disciplines reward different reading strategies.
- **"Comparative reading is harder."** Comparative reading is a separate skill; practice the comparative items separately.
- **"Law passages require legal knowledge."** Law passages are written for a general audience; the items test reasoning, not legal expertise.

#### Exam technique

- Identify the discipline at the start of each passage.
- Adjust reading strategy to the discipline.
- Answer items with the discipline in mind.
- Use the platform's flag-for-review function for items you cannot answer.
