# Critical Reasoning Based on Passages

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Critical Reasoning passages on HAT-UG present a 200–400 word argument and ask you to **infer**, **assume**, **evaluate**, or **conclude** from it. The section carries about **4%** of the overall test weight in English. You will face single best-answer MCQs — no true/false, no multi-select — anchored to a specific stimulus passage.

- **Inference** = must be *strongly supported*, not merely possible.
- **Assumption** = an unstated belief the author needs to be true.
- **Tone** = the author's *attitude* (sarcastic, laudatory, concerned).
- **Purpose** = the author's *goal* (to criticise, inform, persuade).

| Sub-skill | What the stem asks |
| --- | --- |
| Inference | What is most likely true based on the passage? |
| Assumption | What must the author believe for the argument to hold? |
| Weaken | Which option makes the argument less convincing? |
| Tone / Purpose | Single adjective capturing attitude or intent |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### How HAT-UG Tests This Skill

HEC frames each item around a short argumentative or expository passage. Expect four recurring stem types: "It can be inferred that…", "The author assumes…", "Which would weaken the argument?", and "The author's primary purpose is to…" Length is fixed; difficulty comes from how the **premises** connect to the **conclusion**.

#### The Four Argument Parts

Every critical-reasoning question revolves around the relationship between a **claim**, its **support**, the **assumption** linking them, and the **conclusion** drawn. Spotting the gap between claim and support is the single highest-yield skill.

#### Tone vs Purpose

- **Tone** = emotion-laden label (cynical, optimistic, neutral).
- **Purpose** = action-oriented label (to inform, to argue, to criticise).

Conflating the two is the most common HAT-UG error in this sub-area.

#### Worked Pattern

Passage claim: "Universities should expand online programmes because digital delivery cuts costs."
- **Premise:** digital delivery cuts costs.
- **Conclusion:** universities *should* expand online programmes.
- **Assumption:** cost savings benefit students or academic quality.
- **Weakening choice:** evidence that cost cuts reduce teaching quality.

#### High-Yield Pointers

- Eliminate answers that introduce new facts not in the passage.
- Prefer the *most strongly supported* inference, not the most interesting one.
- A real-world truth that contradicts the passage is still wrong if the passage supports the opposite.

| Step | Action |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Read the stem first, then the passage. |
| 2 | Underline the claim and the evidence. |
| 3 | Paraphrase the author's argument in one sentence. |
| 4 | Test each option against the passage, not against outside knowledge. |

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Logical Fallacies

HAT-UG occasionally embeds a known fallacy inside the passage and asks you to identify how the argument fails. Recognising the fallacy speeds up the answer.

| Fallacy | Pattern to recognise | Why it weakens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hasty generalisation | One case → sweeping rule | Sample size is too small. |
| False cause | A before B → A caused B | Correlation mistaken for causation. |
| Straw man | Distorts opponent's view | Refutes a position no one holds. |
| Ad hominem | Attacks the speaker, not the claim | Irrelevant to argument merit. |

#### Weaken vs Disprove

A common trap: "weaken" does **not** mean "disprove." An answer that makes the conclusion *unlikely* suffices; you do not need to overturn it entirely. Conversely, a real-world fact that contradicts the passage is irrelevant if the passage never relied on it.

#### Practice Prompts

1. *Passage:* "Since reading scores rose in schools that added morning library periods, library periods cause higher literacy." — Which assumption underlies the claim?
2. *Passage:* "The new policy is fair because it treats everyone identically." — Which option most weakens the argument?

#### Exam Strategy for HAT-UG

- Allocate roughly **60 seconds per question**; the English section is time-pressed.
- Skip and return: lengthy philosophy or science passages reward a second pass.
- Read every option; HAT-UG distractors are tight paraphrases, not obvious decoys.
- Anchor each chosen answer to a specific sentence in the passage and write a one-line justification — this habit filters out plausible-but-unsupported choices.
