# Creating Texts — VCE English (VCAA 2023) Notes

Creating Texts is the Unit 3 Area of Study 2 of VCE English. Students create a sustained piece of writing (imaginative, persuasive or discursive) that demonstrates craft in language, form and structure. The Area of Study is assessed as part of the Unit 3 School-Assessed Coursework.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Area of Study.** Composition in a chosen form (imaginative, persuasive, discursive).
- **Outcomes.** Students compose texts that demonstrate craft in language, form and structure for a stated purpose and audience.
- **Assessment.** Unit 3 School-Assessed Coursework (SAC).
- **No prescribed text.** The student's own composition is the artefact.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing Creating Texts (composition) with Reading and Responding (analysis).
- Writing without a clear sense of form or audience.

---

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Composition forms

Imaginative: short story, scene, narrative, descriptive passage. Persuasive: opinion piece, speech, letter, open letter. Discursive: essay, reflection, balanced argument.

Each form has conventions. Short stories use scene-setting, dialogue, pacing and a turning point. Persuasive pieces use direct address, rhetorical questions, repetition and an emphatic close.

#### Craft techniques

Strong verbs, precise nouns, deliberate sentence rhythm (varying short and long), figurative language (metaphor, simile, personification), sound devices (alliteration, assonance), structural choices (openings, closings, paragraphing).

#### The composition cycle

Plan: identify the form, audience and purpose. Outline the structure. Draft the composition in full. Edit for craft. Edit for conventions.

---

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Why Creating Texts is its own Area of Study

- **Composition is a skill distinct from analysis.** Students who write well analytically are not automatically strong creative writers.
- **The 2023 Study Design's portfolio approach** rewards a sustained writing habit across Year 12.

#### Year 12 planning pattern

- One composition per fortnight in different forms across Year 12.
- Peer review and self-assessment cycles.
- Build a portfolio across forms.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Composition is just putting words on paper." No — composition is the deliberate shaping of language for effect.
- "More adjectives = better writing." No — strong verbs and precise nouns do more work.

#### Specification reference

This Area of Study is assessed in the Unit 3 School-Assessed Coursework. Confirm the live study design and any Notice to Schools on vcaa.vic.edu.au before each cycle.

#### VCAA performance descriptors for top-band (Score 9–10) compositions

The VCAA English assessment criteria for Unit 3 Area of Study 2 establish clear thresholds for high-scoring student writing:

1. **Deliberate Control of Form**: The piece adopts a clear, sustained form (imaginative narrative, persuasive opinion piece, or discursive exploration) without slipping into confused hybrid styles or unexamined conventions.
2. **Precision and Economy of Language**: Word choice is purposeful and economical. Rather than stacking descriptive adjectives, the writer relies on active verbs, concrete sensory imagery, and controlled syntactic variation (alternating short emphatic declarations with rhythmic compound-complex sentences).
3. **Cohesive Voice and Tone**: The perspective remains stable throughout. Persuasive writing maintains authority without resorting to melodramatic rhetoric; imaginative writing develops narrative voice without exposition dumping; discursive writing balances nuanced viewpoints without becoming indecisive.

#### 20-minute pre-SAC revision checklist

- [ ] Does your composition clearly declare its primary form, target audience, and rhetorical intent?
- [ ] Have you trimmed decorative adjectives and replaced weak passive constructions with active transitive verbs?
- [ ] Is every paragraph transition motivated by a shift in argument, chronology, or emotional tone?
- [ ] Have you read the text aloud to test auditory cadence and eliminate awkward syntactic rhythms?
