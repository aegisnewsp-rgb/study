# Mineral Metabolism

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your INI CET.

Mineral metabolism covers absorption, transport, storage, and excretion of inorganic elements divided into **principal (macro) minerals** — Na, K, Ca, Mg, Cl, P, S — and **trace (micro) elements** — Fe, Zn, Cu, I, Se, Mn, Cr, Mo, Co, F.

- Adult total body Fe ≈ **3.5–4.5 g**; ~70% in haemoglobin, ~25% in ferritin/haemosiderin, ~5% in myoglobin and enzymes.
- Daily Fe loss ≈ **1 mg/day**; matched by 1–2 mg absorbed from a 10–15 mg dietary intake (only 5–15% absorbed).
- **Corrected Ca (mg/dL) = measured total Ca + 0.8 × (4.0 − serum albumin [g/dL])** — always correct before interpreting hypocalcaemia.
- **Anion gap (mmol/L) = Na⁺ + K⁺ − (Cl⁻ + HCO₃⁻)** — used to classify mineral/acid–base disturbances.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Working understanding for the INI CET Biochemistry paper (~3% weight).

#### Iron: the model trace element
Iron exists as haem (15–35% absorbed) and non-haem (1–10% absorbed) forms. **DMT1** on enterocyte apical membranes transports Fe²⁺; **ferroportin** exports Fe²⁺ basolaterally, where hephaestin oxidises it to Fe³⁺ for binding to **transferrin**. **Hepcidin**, a 25-amino-acid hepatic peptide, binds ferroportin → internalisation → degradation, blocking iron release. Hepcidin rises with iron loading and inflammation (IL-6), and falls with iron deficiency, erythropoiesis, and hypoxia.

#### Calcium–phosphate axis
**Calcitriol** (1,25-(OH)₂-D₃) is the active vitamin D metabolite, made by 25-hydroxylation in liver and 1α-hydroxylation in kidney (upregulated by **PTH**, hypophosphataemia). It raises intestinal Ca²⁺ absorption via **TRPV6** and phosphate via **NaPi-IIb**.

- **PTH** ↑serum Ca²⁺ (bone resorption, renal Ca²⁺ reabsorption, 1α-hydroxylase activation); ↓serum PO₄³⁻ (renal phosphaturia).
- **Calcitonin** acutely lowers serum Ca²⁺ (osteoclast inhibition).
- **FGF-23** (osteocytes) causes phosphaturia and suppresses 1α-hydroxylase.

| Hormone | Serum Ca²⁺ | Serum PO₄³⁻ | Renal Ca²⁺ reabsorption |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PTH | ↑ | ↓ | ↑ |
| Calcitriol | ↑ | ↑ | ↑ (indirect) |
| Calcitonin | ↓ (acute) | ↓ | ↓ |
| FGF-23 | — | ↓ | — |

#### Other trace elements — clinical hooks
- **Iodine**: trapped by **NIS** (Na⁺/I⁻ symporter); organified by thyroid peroxidase into T4/T3. Deficiency → goitre, cretinism.
- **Copper**: absorbed via **Ctr1**, transported loosely on albumin, oxidised by **ceruloplasmin** (Fe²⁺→Fe³⁺), excreted via biliary **ATP7B**. Wilson disease = ATP7B mutation, low ceruloplasmin, Kayser–Fleischer rings. Menkes disease = ATP7A mutation, kinky hair, copper deficiency.
- **Selenium**: incorporated as **selenocysteine** via UGA recoding into glutathione peroxidase; deficiency → Keshan cardiomyopathy, Kashin-Beck osteochondral dystrophy.
- **Zinc**: ZIP transporters, stored with metallothionein; cofactor for >300 enzymes; required for insulin storage in β-cells.
- **Fluoride**: forms **fluorapatite [Ca₁₀(PO₄)₆F₂]**, reducing enamel acid solubility; excess → fluorosis.

#### Common traps
> Always correct total calcium for albumin before treating hypocalcaemia. A patient with serum albumin 2.0 g/dL and total Ca 7.6 mg/dL actually has normal ionised Ca.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Mastery-level coverage with worked examples and edge cases.

#### Worked calculation — fractional excretion of sodium (FENa)
FeNa (%) = (U_Na × P_Cr) / (P_Na × U_Cr) × 100. In prerenal AKI, FeNa is typically <1%; in intrinsic renal failure, FeNa is >2%. The same formula applied to calcium (FECa) or magnesium (FEMg) helps localise tubular handling defects.

#### Worked calculation — corrected calcium
Patient: total Ca = 8.0 mg/dL, albumin = 2.5 g/dL. Corrected Ca = 8.0 + 0.8 × (4.0 − 2.5) = 8.0 + 1.2 = **9.2 mg/dL** (normal). Uncorrected value would falsely suggest hypocalcaemia.

#### Magnesium–calcium interdependence
Hypomagnesaemia impairs **PTH secretion** and end-organ response; hypocalcaemia will not correct until magnesium is replete. This is a classic INI CET vignette: a postsurgical patient with refractory hypocalcaemia responding only to IV Mg²⁺.

#### Iron absorption enhancers and inhibitors
- **Enhanced**: ascorbic acid (reduces Fe³⁺ → Fe²⁺), gastric acid, meat/factor (MFP factor).
- **Inhibited**: phytates (cereals), oxalates (spinach), tannins (tea), calcium, antacids.
