# Gravitation

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

- **Newton's law**: F = G m₁m₂/r², with G = 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²·kg⁻², r being centre-to-centre distance.
- **Field and potential** of a point mass: E_g = GM/r² (radial, inward); V = −GM/r (negative, zero at infinity).
- **Circular orbit**: v_o = √(GM/r); period T = 2π√(r³/GM); total energy E = −GMm/(2r); binding energy = +GMm/(2r).
- **Escape speed**: v_e = √(2GM/R) = √2 · v_o, independent of the escaping body's mass.
- **Variation of g**: drops with altitude, drops linearly with depth, and is smaller at the equator than at the poles because Earth rotates.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Newton's Law and the Superposition Trick

Gravitation is a conservative **inverse-square central force** acting between every pair of masses. For two uniform spheres, treat each as a point mass at its centre when the field point lies outside either sphere. For points **inside** a uniform sphere, only the enclosed mass contributes (M_enclosed ∝ r³), so the field rises linearly from zero at the centre. This shell theorem lets you handle extended bodies without integration.

#### Field, Potential, and Energy

| Quantity | Formula (point mass M at distance r) | Sign / Nature |
|---|---|---|
| Field E_g | GM/r² | Vector, radial inward |
| Potential V | −GM/r | Scalar, **negative** everywhere |
| P.E. of pair | −GMm/r | Zero at infinity |
| Total orbit energy | −GMm/(2r) | **Negative** ⇒ bound |

> **Trap**: total orbital energy is half the potential energy (virial theorem result), not equal to KE. Binding energy = −E_total.

#### Kepler's Laws in Exam Form

Kepler's three laws follow from (a) inverse-square force, (b) conservation of angular momentum L = mvr sinθ, and (c) conservation of energy. The law that gets numerical questions is **T² = (4π²/GM) a³**, where *a* is the semi-major axis — valid for **elliptical** orbits, not just circles.

#### Escape Velocity

v_e = √(2GM/R) requires only the field strength at the launch surface. Because v_e² = 2v_o², doubling the orbital radius does **not** double the escape speed at the new orbit — recompute against the local g. Escape speed is independent of direction and of the projectile's mass, so a heavy rocket and a light molecule launched from the same surface need the same minimum speed.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Variation of g: Altitude, Depth, Latitude

#### Altitude
Exact: g(h) = GM_E/(R_E + h)². For h ≪ R_E, expand to g_h ≈ g(1 − 2h/R_E). Always use the exact form once h exceeds a few percent of R_E (≈ 6.4 × 10⁶ m).

#### Depth
Assuming uniform density, g(d) = g(1 − d/R_E), dropping linearly to **zero at Earth's centre** (not to −g). Real Earth is denser at the core, so observed g(d) decreases slightly faster than linear.

#### Latitude
Earth's rotation makes the effective g: g(φ) = g₀ − ω²R_E cos²φ. Poles (φ = 90°) keep the full g₀ ≈ 9.83 m/s²; equator (φ = 0°) loses the ω²R term, giving ≈ 9.78 m/s². The contribution is **ω²R cosφ** as the perpendicular distance from the rotation axis, not ω²r.

#### Two-Body Reduction

Two attracting masses orbit their common centre of mass. Replace m by the **reduced mass** μ = m₁m₂/(m₁+m₂) when applying Kepler's third law to a binary: T² = 4π²a³/[G(m₁+m₂)], where *a* is the separation.

#### Common Mistakes

- Using the body's radius instead of centre-to-centre distance in F = GMm/r².
- Forgetting the minus sign on V or U — exam answers must state "negative".
- Equating binding energy with kinetic energy; KE = +GMm/(2r), binding energy = GMm/(2r), but E_total = −GMm/(2r).
- Applying g(d) = g(1 − d/R_E) when density is non-uniform.

#### Practice Prompts

1. A satellite is moved from circular orbit r to 3r. Find the ratio of new to old (a) orbital speed, (b) period, (c) total energy.
2. A projectile is fired vertically from Earth's surface at v = √(gR_E). Using energy conservation, show whether it escapes or returns, and find its maximum height.
