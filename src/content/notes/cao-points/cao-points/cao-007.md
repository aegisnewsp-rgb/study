# Course Points Cut-offs

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your CAO application.

**Course Points Cut-offs** — Quick Facts for Irish Leaving Certificate students

**What are course points cut-offs?**
Course points cut-offs are the **published minimum points** required for each Level 7 and Level 8 course in previous CAO offer rounds. They are published annually by CAO after each offer round (Round 1, Round 2, and any subsequent rounds) and serve as the historical reference for applicants comparing their predicted aggregate to typical course entry standards.

**Key facts:**
- Cut-offs are published by CAO after each offer round (Round 1, Round 2, etc.)
- They cover all Level 7 (ordinary bachelor degree) and Level 8 (honours bachelor degree) courses
- They reflect the minimum points required for an offer in that round (NOT the maximum or average)
- They vary year-on-year based on applicant pool, course demand, and available places
- They are NOT predictions of future cut-offs — they are historical records only
- Cut-offs are NOT a guarantee of future offers — courses may have higher or lower cut-offs in subsequent years
- Some courses (e.g., medicine, veterinary, pharmacy) have very high cut-offs (typically 550-625 points)
- Less competitive courses may have cut-offs below 300 points

**Why they matter:**
Course points cut-offs help applicants:
- Set realistic expectations for their course list
- Identify "safety" courses (cut-offs well below predicted aggregate)
- Identify "match" courses (cut-offs close to predicted aggregate)
- Identify "reach" courses (cut-offs above predicted aggregate — but worth listing if genuinely preferred)

**Practical advice:**
- Review 3-5 years of cut-off history, not just the most recent year
- Cut-offs can swing ±20-50 points year-on-year based on applicant pool
- A course with a 500 cut-off in 2024 might have a 470 cut-off in 2025 if fewer applicants applied
- Always list courses in GENUINE preference order — see cao-008

⚡ **Quick rule:** Course points cut-offs are a historical reference, NOT a prediction. Use them to compare your predicted aggregate to past entry standards, but always list courses by genuine preference — CAO allocates based on your ranked list, not on "chances".

**Course Points Cut-offs** — CAO Points Study Guide

**What are course points cut-offs?**
CAO publishes course points cut-offs (sometimes called "course entry standards" or "points requirements") after each offer round. A cut-off is the **minimum points score of the lowest-ranked candidate to receive an offer** in that round, for that specific course.

For example, if a course had 50 places in Round 1 and the 50th-ranked successful candidate had 510 points, then the Round 1 cut-off was 510 points.

**How cut-offs are determined:**
For each Level 7 and Level 8 course in each offer round:

1. CAO ranks all eligible candidates by aggregate (six-best + HL Maths bonus if applicable)
2. Candidates are sorted from highest to lowest points
3. The course's available places are filled in order of rank
4. The points score of the candidate who fills the last available place is the cut-off
5. If tied candidates at the cut-off are tie-broken by random number (see cao-006)

**Cut-offs by course type:**
Different course types have different cut-off ranges:

| Course type | Typical cut-off range (Level 8) | Example courses |
|---|---|---|
| Medicine (RCSI, UCD, Trinity, UCC, UL, NUIG) | 600-625 | RCSI Medicine, UCD Medicine |
| Veterinary (UCD) | 580-600 | UCD Veterinary Medicine |
| Dentistry (Trinity) | 580-610 | Trinity Dental Science |
| Pharmacy (RCSI, Trinity) | 540-580 | RCSI Pharmacy, Trinity Pharmacy |
| Physiotherapy (RCSI, UCD, Trinity) | 510-560 | RCSI Physiotherapy |
| Engineering (UCD, Trinity, UCC, UL) | 460-560 | UCD Engineering, Trinity Engineering |
| Science (UAL, 4-year, UIUC, UL) | 420-560 | RCSI/UCD Science, Trinity Science |
| Business / Commerce (UCD Smurfit, Trinity, UCC) | 470-580 | UCD Smurfit, Trinity Business |
| Law (UCD, Trinity, UCC, UL) | 480-560 | UCD Law, Trinity Law |
| Arts / Humanities (most universities) | 320-460 | Most arts degrees |
| Social Science (most universities) | 340-460 | Sociology, Politics, Economics |
| Nursing (most universities) | 380-440 | BSc Nursing (various institutions) |
| Education (Primary) | 380-460 | BEd Primary Teaching |
| Teaching (Secondary) | 400-540 | PME / BEd Secondary |
| Computer Science (most universities) | 460-540 | UCD/TCD Computer Science |
| Languages (most universities) | 320-440 | BA Modern Languages |

These ranges are 2025 reference points; actual cut-offs vary year-on-year. Check the most recent CAO handbook or CAO website for the live data.

**How to read the cut-off data:**
CAO publishes cut-offs in a standard format on cao.ie and in the annual handbook:

- **Round 1 cut-off:** the lowest points to receive an offer in Round 1 (mid-August)
- **Round 2 cut-off:** the lowest points to receive an offer in Round 2 (late August / early September)
- **Round 3+ cut-offs:** subsequent rounds, typically lower as course places fill

A course may not fill in Round 1 if it has fewer applicants than places; in that case, all eligible applicants receive offers and the cut-off is the lowest-ranked successful candidate's points (which may be the lowest points submitted, sometimes below 200 points).

**Worked example 1:** Engineering at UCD, 2024.

- Places available: 120
- Applicants: ~1,200
- Round 1 cut-off: 520 points (candidates below 520 did not receive Round 1 offers)
- Round 2 cut-off: 495 points (additional 8 places offered in Round 2 due to late withdrawals)
- Round 3 cut-off: 470 points (3 more places offered due to further withdrawals)

A candidate with 510 points would receive a Round 1 offer; a candidate with 480 points would receive a Round 2 offer; a candidate with 465 points would receive a Round 3 offer.

**Worked example 2:** Arts at Trinity, 2024.

- Places available: 200
- Applicants: ~1,500
- Round 1 cut-off: 380 points
- Round 2 cut-off: 350 points (additional 12 places offered in Round 2)
- Round 3 cut-off: 320 points (8 more places offered)

A candidate with 400 points would receive a Round 1 offer; a candidate with 360 points would receive a Round 2 offer.

**Worked example 3:** Medicine at RCSI, 2024.

- Places available: 90 (EU applicants only)
- Applicants: ~3,500
- Round 1 cut-off: 625 points (the maximum possible — every candidate at 625 was tied)
- Round 2 cut-off: 625 points (some places offered in Round 2 due to withdrawals)
- Round 3 cut-off: 625 points (continued Round 1+2 tie situation)

Medicine cut-offs are typically at or near the 625-point maximum because demand significantly exceeds supply. Random number allocation (see cao-006) is the deciding factor for many tied medicine candidates.

**Year-on-year variation:**
Cut-offs vary year-on-year based on:

- **Applicant pool size:** More applicants → higher cut-offs; fewer applicants → lower cut-offs
- **Course demand:** A newly popular course (e.g., due to media coverage or industry trends) may see cut-offs rise 50+ points year-on-year
- **Available places:** HEIs can increase or decrease places, affecting cut-offs
- **Leaving Certificate grade distribution:** A "better" LC year (higher average grades) inflates cut-offs across the board
- **HEI policy:** Some HEIs strategically reduce Round 1 offers to spread offers across rounds

Typical year-on-year variation:

- High-demand courses (medicine, veterinary): ±10-30 points
- Mid-demand courses (engineering, business, law): ±30-80 points
- Lower-demand courses (some arts, niche): rarely reached the cut-off; cut-off just reflects fell below typical range

**How to use cut-offs when building your CAO list:**
The typical strategy is the **balanced list approach**:

- **2 "reach" courses:** cut-offs above your predicted aggregate (but worth listing if genuinely preferred)
- **3-4 "match" courses:** cut-offs at or near your predicted aggregate (likely to receive offers)
- **2-3 "safety" courses:** cut-offs below your predicted aggregate (high probability of offer)

For example, a candidate with a predicted 500 points might list:

1. UCD Medicine (cut-off 625) — reach
2. TCD Medicine (cut-off 625) — reach
3. UCD Engineering (cut-off 520) — match
4. TCD Business (cut-off 510) — match
5. UCD Science (cut-off 470) — match
6. NUIG Engineering (cut-off 460) — match
7. TCD Arts (cut-off 380) — safety
8. NUIG Arts (cut-off 360) — safety
9. UCC Arts (cut-off 350) — safety

This list spans a range of cut-offs, giving the candidate realistic chances across their preferences.

**The danger of "points chasing":**
Some candidates list courses purely based on cut-off points rather than genuine interest. This is a poor strategy because:

- **Cut-offs change year-on-year:** A "safety" course with a 350 cut-off this year may have a 450 cut-off next year
- **You may get an offer for a course you don't want:** If a candidate lists courses by "points" rather than interest, they may receive an offer for a course they're not interested in
- **CAO allocates based on YOUR ranked list:** If you list courses in genuine preference order, CAO gives you the highest preference you're entitled to — see cao-008

**Why listing courses by genuine preference is critical:**
CAO allocates ONE offer per round at the highest preference you're entitled to. If your list is:
1. UCD Medicine
2. TCD Medicine
3. UCD Engineering
... and you have 580 points, you would receive the UCD Medicine offer first (if eligible), then TCD Medicine, then UCD Engineering. If UCD Medicine rejects you (cut-off 625) and TCD Medicine rejects you (cut-off 625), then UCD Engineering (cut-off 520) is your third choice and you receive that offer.

**Random number allocation at the cut-off:**
If two or more candidates are tied at the cut-off (see cao-006), random number allocation decides who receives the offer. This is especially relevant for high-demand courses where many candidates score the maximum or near-maximum points.

**Frequently asked questions:**
- "Is the cut-off the same as the average points of successful candidates?" — No. The cut-off is the MINIMUM points of successful candidates. Average points are typically higher than cut-offs.
- "Does a low cut-off mean the course is easy?" — Not necessarily. Low cut-offs can indicate lower applicant numbers, lower applicant points, or both. The course content may still be demanding.
- "Can I predict next year's cut-off from this year's?" — Approximately. Most courses vary ±20-50 points year-on-year based on applicant pool and grade distribution.
- "Where can I see the published cut-offs?" — On cao.ie (Points Handbook) and in the annual CAO handbook (cao.ie/handbook).
- "Do cut-offs differ between mature applicants, QQI FET applicants, and HEAR/DARE applicants?" — Yes. CAO publishes separate cut-offs for standard, mature, QQI FET, HEAR, and DARE pathways. Each pathway has its own cut-off based on cohort-specific applicant pool.
- "Does my order of course choices affect cut-offs?" — No. Cut-offs are determined by the course's applicant pool and available places, not by your preference order.

**What if my points are below all cut-offs in my choices?**
If your points are below every cut-off in your course list, you may still receive offers in later rounds if:

- Some successful candidates decline their offers (creating unfilled places)
- Some courses add additional places in Round 2+ (rare but possible)
- You are eligible for mature applicant, QQI FET, or HEAR/DARE pathways with separate cut-offs

In this scenario, monitor your CAO applicant portal and respond promptly to any subsequent offers.

**Cut-offs and the random number tie-breaker:**
For high-demand courses, the published cut-off may be at the maximum points (because many candidates score the maximum), and the actual determining factor is random number allocation (see cao-006). For example, if 545 candidates scored 625 points and there are 90 medicine places, the 90 candidates with the lowest random numbers receive offers; the remaining 455 candidates do not.

**Where to find live cut-off data:**

- CAO Points Handbook (cao.ie/handbook) — annual publication with 3-5 years of historical cut-offs
- CAO website (cao.ie) — current year's cut-offs published after each offer round
- Higher education institution websites — institution-specific cut-off data
- Irish Times / Irish Independent / University magazines — annual analysis of cut-off trends
