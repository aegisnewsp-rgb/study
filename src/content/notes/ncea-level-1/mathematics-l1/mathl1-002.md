# Use mathematical methods to explore problems that relate to life in Aotearoa New Zealand or the Pacific (91945) — NCEA Level 1 Mathematics and Statistics Notes

Achievement Standard 91945 is the second of four NCEA Level 1 Mathematics and Statistics standards. It is internally assessed, carries 5 credits, and asks students to apply number, measurement, algebra, geometry, statistics, or probability methods to a problem with a clear Aotearoa New Zealand or Pacific context. Most schools schedule 91945 across Terms 2 and 3, which gives students time to research the chosen context and apply at least two different mathematical methods.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **5 credits**, internally assessed, NZQA moderated.
- The problem must have an **Aotearoa or Pacific context** — not generic, not imported.
- Students must use **mathematical methods** (number, measurement, algebra, geometry, statistics, probability or a combination) to explore the problem.
- **Merit** requires relational thinking — choosing methods that fit the context, comparing results.
- **Excellence** requires extended abstract thinking — justifying method selection, generalising the conclusion, and reflecting on the reasonableness of the answer.
- The context is usually chosen by the student in consultation with the teacher.

#### Examiner traps

- Picking a context that has no data — "explore the culture of Samoa" is not a 91945 problem; "compare the population growth of Samoa and New Zealand from 2000 to 2023 using percentage change" is.
- Using only one mathematical method — the standard explicitly expects multiple methods applied to the problem.
- Ignoring the context in the final write-up — NZQA markers look for sentences that tie each mathematical conclusion back to the Aotearoa/Pacific setting.
- Choosing a context where the data is locked behind paywalls or impossible to find within the term.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### What counts as an Aotearoa or Pacific context

NZQA's clarifications document lists the kinds of contexts that qualify. Common examples in school reports include:

- Population or demographic data about New Zealand regions or iwi.
- Environmental data about New Zealand rivers, beaches, native bush, or marine reserves.
- Economic data about New Zealand industries (tourism, dairy, wine, kiwifruit).
- Pacific migration patterns, remittance flows, or population projections.
- Sports data involving New Zealand teams or athletes.
- Cultural or language data with a numerical anchor (numbers of speakers, place names, waka counts).

A context qualifies if the data and the conclusion sit in the Aotearoa or Pacific setting. A generic context ("the population of a small town") does not qualify; the same data presented as "the population of Patea" does.

#### Mathematical methods at Level 1

Level 1 students should be able to choose from:

- **Number** — four operations with integers, fractions, decimals, percentages, ratio, rates, standard form, and indices.
- **Measurement** — length, area, volume, time, mass, money; conversions between units; perimeters, areas and volumes of common shapes.
- **Algebra** — substituting into formulae, simplifying expressions, solving linear equations, plotting linear relationships.
- **Geometry** — angle properties, similar shapes, scale factor, Pythagoras' theorem.
- **Statistics** — summary statistics, displays, comparing two groups, time series.
- **Probability** — experimental and theoretical probability, sample spaces, two-way tables.

A Merit-grade report applies at least two methods and shows how each gives a different angle on the problem. An Excellence-grade report selects methods deliberately and explains why each method is the right choice for this particular problem.

#### A worked structure

1. **Pose the problem.** State the Aotearoa/Pacific context and the question to be explored. Make it specific.
2. **Plan.** Identify the data needed, the sources (Statistics New Zealand, NZ government open data, school-collected data), and the mathematical methods that will be used.
3. **Manage data.** Clean, organise and present data in tables ready for analysis.
4. **Apply methods.** Carry out the calculations, displays, and analyses. Show all working.
5. **Conclude.** Tie each mathematical result back to the original context. Discuss reasonableness (does the answer make sense in the world?) and limitations (sample size, data source, method choice).
6. **Reflect.** For Excellence: reflect on whether a different method would have given a different conclusion, and why.

#### Internal assessment logistics

- 91945 is usually the second internal assessment on the school's Mathematics and Statistics programme.
- The school can use a range of contexts across the cohort; students do not all need to study the same problem.
- Resubmission for a higher grade is at the teacher's discretion and at most once per standard.
- NZQA external moderation checks a sample of student work each year.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Excellence-level reasoning

Merit work applies methods. Excellence work reflects on method. The two phrases that move a report from Merit to Excellence:

- "I chose the median rather than the mean because the data was skewed by the 2020 cyclone season which produced several outlier values." (Method justification.)
- "If I had used linear extrapolation instead of percentage change, the projected 2030 population would have been 8% higher; the percentage method is the more conservative estimate for a small regional population." (Method comparison.)
- "The result depends on the assumption that the migration data was collected consistently across the decade; the 2018 census methodology changed the way 'Pacific' was recorded, which may affect the comparison." (Limitation analysis.)

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "I can pick any Pacific country and call it context." Yes — but the data and the conclusion must be grounded in that country, not imported from a generic problem.
- "One method is enough." No — NZQA markers expect multiple methods applied to the problem; a single-method report caps at low Merit.
- "I can copy the data from Wikipedia." Wikipedia is fine for orientation; the data source should be named (Statistics New Zealand, NZ government open data portal, etc.).
- "Excellence means longer." No — Excellence means more thoughtful. A two-page report that justifies method selection is stronger than a five-page report that lists every possible method.
