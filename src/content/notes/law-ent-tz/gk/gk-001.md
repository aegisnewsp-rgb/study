# Tanzania General Knowledge I

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your Law School of Tanzania Qualifying Examination.

Tanzania General Knowledge I is the civic-literacy backbone of the LST pre-entry syllabus, testing the political history, constitutional architecture, and core institutions of the United Republic of Tanzania. The three dates examiners ask most often are **9 December 1961** (Tanganyika's independence from Britain), **10 December 1963** (Zanzibar's independence), and **26 April 1964** (the Union of Tanganyika and Zanzibar). The supreme law is the **1977 Constitution of the United Republic** (as amended), not the 1965 Interim Constitution.

The state runs on a **two-tier government**: Union matters handled by the Government of the United Republic, and non-Union matters by the Revolutionary Government of Zanzibar. Union matters are exhaustively listed in the First Schedule and include foreign affairs, defence, police, citizenship, immigration, and the High Court/Court of Appeal of Tanzania.

- **Head of State & Government of the URT**: the President (currently Samia Suluhu Hassan since 19 March 2021).
- **Official capital**: Dodoma (since 1996); Dar es Salaam is the commercial hub.
- **Languages**: Kiswahili (national) and English (official).
- **Ruling party**: Chama Cha Mapinduzi (CCM), in power continuously since independence.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months before the LST entrance test.

#### Constitutional architecture

The 1977 Constitution establishes Tanzania as a **unitary state with a two-tier government structure**. This is the most tested structural point on the paper. The First Schedule carves out **Union matters** for the Government of the United Republic, while every residual matter falls to the **Revolutionary Government of Zanzibar** as a **non-Union matter**. Article 65 of the Constitution is the operative provision.

#### Union versus non-Union matters

| Category | Union matters (URT) | Non-Union matters (Zanzibar) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Examples | Foreign affairs, defence, police, citizenship, immigration, High Court, Court of Appeal, NEC, CAG | Local taxation, land (within Zanzibar), Islamic courts, Zanzibar House of Representatives, ZEC |
| Government | Government of the United Republic | Revolutionary Government of Zanzibar |
| Head | President of the URT | President of Zanzibar |
| Legislature | Bunge (Parliament) | House of Representatives |

#### Independence timeline and party history

- **1954**: Tanganyika African National Union (TANU) formed under Julius Nyerere.
- **9 December 1961**: Tanganyika attains independence from Britain.
- **12 January 1964**: Zanzibar Revolution overthrows the Sultan.
- **26 April 1964**: Tanganyika and Zanzibar unite to form the United Republic.
- **5 February 1967**: Arusha Declaration — *Ujamaa na Kujitegemea* (African Socialism).
- **1977**: TANU merges with the Afro-Shirazi Party (ASP) to form **Chama Cha Mapinduzi (CCM)**.

#### The court system

The judiciary is a **hybrid** blending received English common law, statutory law, customary law, and Islamic law (the latter principally in Zanzibar). The hierarchy for Union matters runs: Primary Courts → District Courts → Resident Magistrate Courts → **High Court of Tanzania** → **Court of Appeal of Tanzania**. The High Court also has constitutional jurisdiction under Part III of the 1977 Constitution.

#### Common traps examiners exploit

- Citing Dar es Salaam as the official capital.
- Citing the 1965 Interim Constitution instead of the 1977 Constitution.
- Treating the President of the URT and the President of Zanzibar as one office rather than two co-existing offices that a single person may hold simultaneously.
- Forgetting that Zanzibar keeps its own Public Service Commission, police force, and Electoral Commission (ZEC).

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for candidates preparing months in advance, including edge cases and exam strategy.

#### Bill of Rights and constitutional interpretation

Chapter I, **Part III** of the 1977 Constitution contains the **Bill of Rights**, enforceable against the state and, in certain contexts, private parties. The **Basic Rights and Duties Enforcement Act** is the procedural vehicle for constitutional petitions. The High Court of Tanzania is the court of first instance for constitutional matters; appeals lie to the Court of Appeal. This matters for LST candidates because constitutional litigation is a recurring practice area.

#### Edge cases and frequent misclassifications

| Office / Body | Correct description | Frequent error |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Attorney General | Principal legal adviser to the Government; also a Member of Parliament | Confused with the Director of Public Prosecutions (DPP) |
| CAG (Controller and Auditor General) | Independent officer auditing public funds; reports to Parliament | Confused with the Treasury Registrar |
| NEC (National Electoral Commission) | Conducts Union-level elections; ZEC handles Zanzibar elections | Treated as a single body covering both |
| Bunge | Parliament of the URT, situated in Dodoma | Confused with the Zanzibar House of Representatives |

#### International and regional membership

Tanzania is a founding member of the **East African Community (EAC)**, the **Southern African Development Community (SADC)**, the **African Union (AU)**, the **Commonwealth of Nations**, and the **United Nations**. The EAC is the most-tested bloc, currently comprising Tanzania, Kenya, Uganda, Rwanda, Burundi, South Sudan, the Democratic Republic of Congo, and Somalia.

#### Worked micro-example

> **Prompt:** *Identify the constitutional provision that authorises the Government of the United Republic to conduct foreign affairs, and state the date the Union was formally constituted.*
> **Answer:** Foreign affairs are a Union matter under **Article 65 read with the First Schedule** of the 1977 Constitution of the United Republic. The Union of Tanganyika and Zanzibar was formally constituted on **26 April 1964**, pursuant to the Articles of Union later consolidated in the 1977 Constitution.

#### Exam strategy (3% weight paper)

1. Memorise three dates as a single block: 9 Dec 1961, 10 Dec 1963, 26 April 1964.
2. Memorise the **First Schedule** list of Union matters verbatim — at least five items.
3. Learn the **presidential succession order** (Vice-President, then Speaker, then Chief Justice) under the Constitution.
4. Pair revision of this note with the current text of the 1977 Constitution (latest revised edition) and the LST Bar Examination Regulations.

#### Practice prompts

1. Distinguish, with constitutional authority, between Union matters and non-Union matters, giving three examples of each.
2. Trace the evolution of Tanzania's ruling party from TANU to CCM, identifying the founding date of each and the 1977 merger partners.
