# Pakistan's Culture, Heritage and Society

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Pakistan is a multi-ethnic state where **Urdu** is the national language and English serves as an official language alongside it. The country observes **23 March (Pakistan Day, commemorating the 1940 Lahore Resolution)** and **14 August (Independence Day, 1947)**, while **9 November (Iqbal Day)** and **25 December (Quaid-e-Azam Day)** honour Allama Iqbal and Muhammad Ali Jinnah.

| Identity Marker | Detail |
|---|---|
| National language | Urdu |
| Official languages | Urdu + English |
| National dress | Shalwar kameez |
| National symbols | Flag (green + white crescent/star); Qaumī Tarāna anthem |
| UNESCO World Heritage Sites | Taxila, Mohenjo-daro, Makli, Lahore Fort, Rohtas Fort |

> **Exam trap:** 23 March = Pakistan Day; 14 August = Independence Day. The 1940 Lahore Resolution was moved by **A.K. Fazlul Huq**, not Jinnah.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Demographic and Ethnic Composition
Pakistan's population of roughly **241 million** is distributed across four provinces plus AJK, GB, FATA-successor areas, and Islamabad. Each province carries a major ethnic identity: **Punjabi** (Punjab), **Sindhi** (Sindh), **Pashtun** (KP), and **Baloch** (Balochistan), with **Muhajir**, **Saraiki**, **Hazara**, **Kalash**, and **Brahui** minorities. The Constitution names Islam as the state religion while constitutionally protecting Hindus, Christians, Sikhs, Parsis, Ahmadis, and Kalash communities.

#### National Observances and Heroes
- **23 March 1940** — Minar-e-Pakistan (Iqbal Park, Lahore) marks the Lahore Resolution site.
- **14 August 1947** — Independence from British India under Jinnah's leadership.
- **Allama Iqbal's 1930 Allahabad address** articulated the Two-Nation Theory that underpins Pakistan Movement ideology.

#### Heritage Landmarks
Five UNESCO-inscribed sites anchor Pakistan's tangible heritage. **Taxila** (Punjab, c. 5th century BCE) was the Gandhara Buddhist university. **Mohenjo-daro** (Sindh, c. 2500 BCE) belongs to the Indus Valley Civilization, alongside Harappa in Punjab. **Makli Hill** (Thatta) holds the world's largest medieval funerary complex. **Lahore Fort and Shalimar Gardens** showcase Mughal architecture, and **Rohtas Fort** (Jhelum) reflects 16th-century Afghan military engineering.

| Site | Province | Era |
|---|---|---|
| Mohenjo-daro | Sindh | c. 2500 BCE |
| Taxila | Punjab | Gandhara period |
| Makli | Sindh | 14th–18th c. |
| Lahore Fort | Punjab | Mughal |
| Rohtas Fort | Punjab | Sher Shah Suri, 1541 |

> **Trap:** Rohtas Fort is near Jhelum — students confuse it with Ranigat (KP), an entirely separate Buddhist site.

#### Festivals, Music, and Material Culture
Religious festivals (**Eid ul-Fitr, Eid ul-Adha**) coexist with seasonal ones such as **Basant** (kite-flying) and **Shandur Polo Festival** (Chitral). Sufi **Urs** observances at Data Darbar (Lahore) and shrines of Shah Abdul Latif Bhittai and Sachal Sarmast sustain folk devotional traditions. **Ajrak** block-print and **Sindhi topi** are signature textiles; **qawwali** and **ghazal** dominate classical music.

NLT questions on this tier typically arrive as single-fact MCQs: matching a heritage site to its province, naming the author of the Lahore Resolution, or selecting the correct national day date.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Constitutional and Religious Frame
Article 1 declares Pakistan a federation, Article 2 names Islam the state religion, and Article 260 clarifies the **"Muslim"** definition that legally excludes Ahmadis for electoral rolls — a point examiners exploit. Articles 14 (dignity), 20 (religious freedom), and 25 (equality) collectively shape cultural rights.

#### Archaeological Nuance
Distinguish the **Indus Valley Civilization pair**: Mohenjo-daro sits on the Indus floodplain in Sindh, while Harappa lies in Punjab's Sahiwal district — both c. 2500 BCE, both UNESCO tentative-list candidates yet only Mohenjo-daro is inscribed. **Taxila** is not purely Buddhist; layers span the Achaemenid, Maurya, Indo-Greek, Kushan, and Hindu Shahi occupations, peaking as a **Gandhara Buddhist** learning centre where the Mahabodhi-influenced stupa art flourished.

#### Cultural Institutions and Soft Power
The federal cultural apparatus includes **Lok Virsa** (Islamabad Heritage Centre) for folk heritage, the **Pakistan National Council of the Arts (PNCA)** for performing and visual arts, and provincial bodies like the **Sindh Adabi Board**. Cricket and field hockey both claim "national sport" status — the **Pakistan Hockey Federation** formally designates hockey, while cricket dominates popular culture.

> **Exam trap:** When asked "national sport," look at phrasing. If Hockey Federation is cited in options, hockey wins; otherwise cricket is the standard schoolbook answer.

#### Cuisine, Dress, and Performing Arts
Cuisine is regionally codified: Sindhi **biryani**, Punjabi **nihari**/**haleem**, Peshawari **chapli kebab**, Balochi **sajji**, and Karachi-style **daal chawal**. The **shalwar kameez** plus waistcoat is the default national dress; the **Ajrak** anchors Sindhi identity. Dance forms — **Bhangra**, **Khattak**, **Luddi** — accompany weddings and political rallies.

#### Practice Prompts
1. **Match-the-pairs:** (i) Makli, (ii) Taxila, (iii) Rohtas Fort — match each to its dynasty/era and province. Justify why Taxila is Gandhara-period and not Hindu-classical.
2. **Day-and-hero identification:** State the date, the exact individual honoured, and the historical event associated with 23 March, 9 November, and 25 December. Explain why 14 August is constitutionally different from 23 March.

> **Time-per-question budget:** Aim for ≤45 seconds per MCQ in this section; rote memorisation of dates and UNESCO pairings yields the bulk of marks.
