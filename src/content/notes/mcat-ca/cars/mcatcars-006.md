# Natural Sciences-Related Passages for Non-Scientists — MCAT CARS

Natural Sciences passages for non-scientists are one of the three CARS passage categories. They touch on scientific topics but are written for a general audience, not for specialists. The MCAT tests the ability to interpret scientific reasoning without needing specialist knowledge. About one-fifth to one-quarter of CARS passages are natural-sciences passages for non-scientists.

> Verify the live CARS section length and passage distribution on https://students-residents.aamc.org/ before planning around the figures below.

---

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### What natural-sciences passages for non-scientists look like

- A scientific topic (biology, chemistry, physics, earth science, astronomy) explained for a general reader.
- Often from a magazine or a popular-science publication.
- May include anecdotes, historical context, or policy implications.
- Does not assume specialist knowledge: terms are explained in context.

#### What these passages test

- The ability to interpret a scientific argument for a non-specialist.
- The ability to evaluate evidence and reasoning without content knowledge.
- The same four CARS skills: Foundations of Comprehension, Reasoning Within the Text, Reasoning Beyond the Text, Quantitative Reasoning.
- The ability to read tables and graphs presented in the passage.

#### One-pass drill

1. Read the passage in 4 minutes. Identify the scientific topic and the central claim.
2. Identify the evidence: what observation or study does the author cite?
3. Answer the items. The right answer can be reached from the passage alone.

#### Examiner traps

- Assuming you need content knowledge. CARS passages for non-scientists are written so the items can be answered without specialist knowledge.
- Skipping the passage because the topic seems familiar. The argument is what matters, not your background.
- Misreading the methodology. The passage may describe a study, but the items test the author's interpretation.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Strategy for natural-sciences passages

- Identify the scientific topic: biology, chemistry, physics, earth science, or astronomy.
- Identify the central claim: what is the author arguing or explaining?
- Identify the evidence: what observation, study, or example does the author cite?
- For items, focus on the author's argument, not on whether the science is correct.

The MCAT tests natural-sciences CARS passages for argument evaluation, claim-evidence reasoning, and application of the reasoning to new situations.

#### Strategy for science popularisations

- These passages often have a narrative structure: anecdote, claim, evidence, implication.
- The author may use analogies or metaphors to explain the science.
- For items, focus on the structure of the argument and the central claim.

#### Strategy for science policy passages

- These passages discuss the policy implications of a scientific finding.
- The author may take a position for or against the policy.
- For items, focus on the author's position and the evidence offered for it.

#### Practice routine

- Take one natural-sciences CARS passage. Read it in 4 minutes.
- Identify the scientific topic and the central claim.
- Answer the items. Time yourself: under 8 minutes per passage.
- Take 5 passages in a row. Repeat the routine.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How natural-sciences passages connect to the other CARS passage categories

- **Humanities passages** share the argument-evaluation skill with policy passages.
- **Social Sciences passages** share the methodological language with science passages that describe studies.
- **All four CARS skills** apply, with emphasis on Reasoning Within the Text and Reasoning Beyond the Text.

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"I need to know the science to answer the items."** No — the passage provides enough context to answer without specialist knowledge. The test is reasoning, not content.
- **"The author must be correct."** The items test the author's argument, not whether the science is right.
- **"I should skip the science terms."** Read the passage carefully; the author defines the terms in context.

#### Exam technique

- Read the passage in 4 minutes. Identify the scientific topic and the central claim.
- For argument items, focus on the structure (claim, evidence, assumption).
- For application items, extend the author's reasoning to a new situation.
- For methodology items, identify the type of study or observation before picking the answer.
- Use the platform's flag-for-review function for items you cannot answer.
