# Logical Sequences

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

A **logical sequence** is an ordered list of numbers, letters, symbols, or words governed by a single hidden rule, and the test-taker must either continue the series or spot the term that breaks it. The first move is **classification**: arithmetic progression (AP), geometric progression (GP), alternating series, Fibonacci-type, square/cube series, or a digit-operation series. For AP the rule is **aₙ = a + (n−1)d** where *a* is the first term and *d* is the common difference; for GP the rule is **aₙ = a·rⁿ⁻¹** where *r* is the common ratio. For CLAT, 1–2 questions appear in the Logical Reasoning section with 4–5 term series and a single missing/odd entry. **Always verify the rule on three consecutive terms** before extrapolating, and for letter-coding remember **A = 1 … Z = 26** with opposites summing to 27 (A↔Z, B↔Y).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Pattern Types You Must Recognise

**Arithmetic Progression (AP):** constant difference *d* between consecutive terms. Test by subtracting term 2 from term 1, term 3 from term 2. If both differences match, the series is AP. Example: 7, 12, 17, 22, 27 → *d* = 5, next term = 32.

**Geometric Progression (GP):** constant ratio *r*. Test by dividing term 2 by term 1, term 3 by term 2. Example: 3, 6, 12, 24 → *r* = 2, next term = 48.

**Alternating Series:** two independent sub-series live in odd and even positions. Example: 2, 10, 4, 20, 6, 30 — odd positions (2, 4, 6) increase by 2; even positions (10, 20, 30) increase by 10. The next term belongs to the odd-position sub-series → 8.

**Difference Series:** compute first differences; if they themselves form an AP, the original is quadratic. Second differences constant ⇒ pattern is **aₙ = n²** type (1, 4, 9, 16, 25). Third differences constant ⇒ pattern is **aₙ = n³** type (1, 8, 27, 64).

**Fibonacci-type:** each term equals the sum of the previous two — 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, 34.

**Digit-operation Series:** operate on individual digits — sum of digits (e.g., 27 → 2+7 = 9), digit reversal (e.g., 18 → 81), or squaring digits.

#### Letter-Series Coding

Map letters to positions: **A=1, B=2, … Z=26**. Opposite letters sum to 27, so the opposite of letter with position *p* is **28 − p** (opposite of D, position 4, is 28−4 = 24 = X). Reverse coding assigns Z=1, Y=2, … A=26. Number-series rules may be applied to these positions.

#### Odd-Man-Out Strategy

Find a property shared by four of the five given options; the fifth is the odd one. Test categories in order: divisibility, digit sum, vowel/consonant structure, square/cube nature, alphabetical position.

#### CLAT Question Formats

You will see a series with a blank (find the missing term), a full series with one wrong term (find and replace it), or four options where only one continues the pattern. Options are designed to be one-step-off distractors.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Combined Operations

The hardest CLAT items layer two operations: e.g., *add 3, then multiply by 2, alternating*. Series: 2, 5, 10, 13, 26, 29 — odd positions ×2, even positions +3. Some series combine digit-sum with arithmetic (e.g., 16, 17, 19, 20, 22, 23 where the digit sum stays at 7). When first and second differences are both non-constant, suspect an **alternating** pattern before assuming a cubic.

#### Worked Example

Series: 3, 6, 11, 18, 27, ?

- Differences: 3, 5, 7, 9, ? → odd numbers increasing by 2 → next difference = 11.
- Next term = 27 + 11 = **38**.
- Verification: positions 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 yield 3, 6, 11, 18, 27 — confirms n²+2 pattern (aₙ = n² + 2), giving term 6 = 36 + 2 = 38. ✓

#### Common Mistakes

- Jumping to a cubic or exponential rule when a simple alternating AP fits three consecutive pairs.
- Forgetting that in odd-man-out, the rejected term may share *some* property — the property must be **uniquely violated**.
- Misreading reverse-alphabet coding: Z = 1, not 26.
- Skipping verification on a third term, then extrapolating from a coincidence.

#### Practice Prompts

1. Find the next term: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, **?**
2. Odd one out: 121, 144, 169, 200, 196.
