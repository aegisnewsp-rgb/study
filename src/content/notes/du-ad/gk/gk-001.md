# Geography of Bangladesh

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Fast facts for the General Knowledge MCQ in DU Admission — Bangladesh sits on the world's largest river delta, and the examiner leans hard on that.

Bangladesh lies between **20°34′N–26°38′N** latitude and **88°01′E–92°41′E** longitude, framed by India on three sides, Myanmar to the south-east, and the **Bay of Bengal** to the south. Total area: **1,47,570 sq km**, almost entirely the Ganges–Brahmaputra–Meghna (GBM) alluvial delta. The Tropic of Cancer does **not** cross Bangladesh — it runs through India.

- **Largest division by area:** Chattogram. **Smallest:** Dhaka division.
- **Longest river (within Bangladesh):** Surma–Meghna, ~1,049 km.
- **Highest peak:** Keokradong, Chattogram Hills, ~1,230 m (per BBS).
- **Largest mangrove:** Sundarbans, ~6,017 sq km — UNESCO World Heritage.
- **Mean rainfall:** ~2,200 mm/year; Sylhet exceeds 4,000 mm.

| Physiographic unit | Where | Quick recall |
|---|---|---|
| Madhupur Tract | Dhaka–Mymensingh | Pleistocene terrace, red soil |
| Barind Tract | Rajshahi–Rangpur | Old alluvium, drought-prone |
| Haor basin | Sylhet | Bowl-shaped, seasonal flooding |
| Sundarbans | Khulna Bagerhat | Tidal mangrove delta |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook — "J-B-S-P-M = Jamuna, Brahmaputra, Surma, Padma, Meghna"** → read in flow order: Surma + Kushiyara form the **Meghna**, Meghna + Padma (Ganges + Jamuna) meet near **Chandpur**. Mnemonic trigger: **"S-M meets P-J at Chandpur"**.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Location, boundaries and the misconception that traps most students

Bangladesh is bounded by West Bengal, Meghalaya, Assam and Tripura on the west, north and east; by Mizoram and Myanmar's Chin and Rakhine states to the south-east; and the Bay of Bengal to the south. The northernmost point lies on the **Kurigram–West Bengal border**, the southernmost tip on the **Bay of Bengal near the Sundarbans**. The Tropic of Cancer (23°30′N) passes well to the north, through Madhya Pradesh and Tripura in India — never through Bangladesh, despite what many textbooks print.

The country sits on the **Bengal Basin**, a tectonic sag filled by Holocene alluvium carried by the GBM system. That single fact explains almost every other geographic feature downstream: floodplain width, soil fertility, cyclone exposure and population density (~1,100/km² per SVRS-BBS).

#### Physiographic divisions (BBS National Atlas classification)

The surface divides into four broad units, and the examiner tests them by asking which division matches which unit.

| Division | Dominant physiography | Signature landform |
|---|---|---|
| Chattogram | Tertiary hills + coastal plain | CHT ranges, Cox's Bazar beach |
| Sylhet | Haor basin + Pleistocene terrace | Bowl-shaped wetlands, tea gardens |
| Rajshahi / Rangpur | Barind tract | Drought-prone old alluvium |
| Khulna / Barishal | Tidal and deltaic plain | Sundarbans, polder coast |
| Dhaka / Mymensingh | Madhupur tract + floodplain | Dissected terrace, red soil |

#### Rivers and confluences — the most-tested mechanism

Three river systems dominate: the **Padma** (Ganges enters from India as the Padma), the **Jamuna** (Brahmaputra's main Bangladesh channel), and the **Meghna** (formed by the Surma–Kushiyara confluence near Ajmiriganj). Padma and Jamuna meet at **Goalundo**. The combined Padma then joins the Meghna at **Chandpur**, and the river flows into the Bay of Bengal through an enormous estuary. The Teesta, Feni, Karnaphuli and Sangu are important tributaries — the Karnaphuli drains Chattogram and hosts the Kaptai hydropower dam.

#### Climate, seasons and rainfall pattern

The climate is **tropical monsoon**, governed by south-west summer monsoon (May–September) and north-east winter monsoon (November–February). Four conventional seasons: **Grishmo (summer), Barsha (monsoon), Sharat (autumn), Hemanto (winter), with a short transitional Basanto (spring)** — five if the spring window is counted. Mean annual rainfall is ~2,220 mm; **Sylhet division is the wettest**, often above 4,000 mm, while the Barind tract receives under 1,400 mm.

#### Worked problem reflecting what the DU MCQ actually asks

**Item.** The Padma–Meghna confluence at Chandpur forms the start of which major geographic feature?

A. The Brahmaputra's main Bangladesh channel
B. The Meghna estuary draining into the Bay of Bengal
C. The Surma–Kushiyara basin
D. The Old Brahmaputra floodplain

**Resolution.** The Padma carries the combined flow of the Ganges (as Padma) and the Jamuna (Brahmaputra's Bangladesh channel) — that confluence sits at Goalundo, not Chandpur. At Chandpur, the Padma meets the Meghna, and the merged river opens into a wide tidal estuary that empties into the Bay of Bengal. The Surma–Kushiyara system (option C) feeds the Meghna upstream near Ajmiriganj, well north of Chandpur. The Old Brahmaputra floodplain (option D) is a relic channel east of the Jamuna, unrelated to this junction. Hence **B**.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students confuse the Goalundo confluence (Ganges + Jamuna = Padma) with the Chandpur confluence (Padma + Meghna → Bay of Bengal). The two junctions are ~150 km apart and produce different rivers.

#### Concept comparison matrix — features students swap in the exam hall

| Feature A | Feature B | How they differ |
|---|---|---|
| Jamuna | Brahmaputra | Same river; Jamuna = Bangladesh channel name |
| Padma | Ganges | Padma = Ganges after it enters Bangladesh |
| Meghna | Surma | Meghna = Surma + Kushiyara; Surma is upper reach |
| Madhupur Tract | Barind Tract | Madhupur = Dhaka side, more dissected; Barind = NW, drier |
| Haor | Beel | Haor = large seasonal lake basin; Beel = smaller dead-river lake |
| Sundarbans | Coastal plain | Sundarbans = mangrove tidal forest; coastal plain = broader geomorphic unit |

> 📌 **Formula Check (numeric recall):** Area = **1,47,570 sq km**; Sundarbans in Bangladesh ≈ **6,017 sq km**; Keokradong ≈ **1,230 m**; Surma–Meghna length ≈ **1,049 km**. Treat these as fixed MCQ anchors.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

#### Edge cases and boundary conditions that separate top rankers

The **Chittagong Hill Tracts (CHT)** — covering Rangamati, Bandarban and Khumail districts — are Bangladesh's only extensive hill region, geologically distinct from the rest of the country's alluvial surface. The peak question is a soft trap: Keokradong (~1,230 m) is the figure BBS uses, but Mowdok Mual (also in the CHT) is reported higher in some surveys; the DU MCQ keys off the BBS figure, so quote Keokradong.

The **haor basin** of Sylhet is functionally a wetland trap during monsoon: depressed floodplain areas between the Old Brahmaputra and the Meghna hold water for months. The dominant soil there is **non-calcareous grey floodplain**, whereas the **coastal saline soils** of Khulna and Patuakhali constrain Rabi cropping and force salt-tolerant varieties. Around **55% of Bangladesh's soils are alluvial**, the rest split among terrace, hill, coastal saline and peaty/peat soils.

Population geography has crossed a critical line: Bangladesh now exceeds **1,100 people/km²**, placing it among the densest non-city-state countries. Combined with **climate exposure indices**, this makes the country structurally vulnerable to two hazard systems — **riverine flood** in the monsoon and **cyclone + storm surge** in the pre-monsoon (April–May) and post-monsoon (October–November) windows. The Meghna estuary and the Sundarbans act as the country's natural cyclone buffer, which is why Sundarbans conservation intersects directly with human-geography questions.

#### Connections to adjacent topics

Geography of Bangladesh interlocks with **Bangladesh History** (the delta's role in the 1947 partition, the 1971 war's riverine geography), with **Economics** (Garments, jute, tea — tea concentrated in Moulvibazar, jute in the floodplain, rice everywhere) and with **Environment & Disaster** (cyclone shelters, embankments, char lands). A student revising General Knowledge should treat this topic as the **spine** that other Bangladesh-specific GK items hang on.

#### Two advanced practice prompts

1. Map-based: a question lists **five divisions** and asks which one pairs with **haor, tea, CHT, Sundarbans and Barind tract**. The correct match is Sylhet, Chattogram (or Moulvibazar inside Sylhet for tea specifically), Chattogram, Khulna, Rajshahi. The trap: tea is often mis-tagged to Chattogram because the syllabus sometimes lists "tea" near CHT hills; it is in **Moulvibazar, Sylhet**.
2. Numerical: a question asks for the area of Bangladesh in sq km. Three options cluster near 1,47,570; one offers 1,47,570 sq km (correct), one offers 1,47,570 sq km expressed as ~56,000 sq mi (a distractor unit trap), and one offers 1,47,570 sq km phrased as "1.47 lakh sq km". The examiner tests whether you recognise the standard BBS figure regardless of unit.

#### Common advanced traps

| Trap | Why students fall | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| "Brahmaputra enters Bangladesh as Padma" | Mixing river names | Padma = Ganges, not Brahmaputra |
| "Sundarbans lies in India only" | Confusing Indo-Bangladesh share | ~60% lies inside Bangladesh |
| "Keokradong > 2,000 m" | Inflating peak height | BBS figure is ~1,230 m |
| "Cox's Bazar = longest sea beach worldwide" | Plausible but needs caveat | True; ~120 km continuous — quote only if confirmed |
| "Haor = beel = char" | All are wetlands | They are different in scale and origin |
