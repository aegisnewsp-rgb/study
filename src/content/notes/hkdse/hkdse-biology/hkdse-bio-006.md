# VI. Applied Ecology (Elective) — HKDSE Biology Notes

This topic covers Elective Topic VI of the HKDSE Biology Elective Part. Candidates sit any two of the four electives on Paper 2. The topic addresses human impact on the environment (pollution, habitat destruction, climate change); pollution control (monitoring, mitigation, legislation); conservation (in-situ and ex-situ, biodiversity hotspots, protected areas); and global issues (acid rain, ozone depletion, eutrophication, sustainable development).

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Human impact**: pollution (air, water, soil); habitat destruction (deforestation, urbanisation, land conversion); climate change (greenhouse gas emissions, rising global temperatures, ocean acidification); overexploitation (overfishing, hunting); invasive species.
- **Pollution control**: monitoring (air quality indices, water quality parameters, biodiversity indicators); mitigation (cleaner technologies, waste treatment, recycling); legislation (Clean Air Ordinance, Waste Disposal Ordinance, Environmental Impact Assessment Ordinance in Hong Kong).
- **Conservation**: in-situ conservation (national parks, country parks, marine parks, biodiversity hotspots); ex-situ conservation (zoos, botanical gardens, seed banks, captive breeding); international agreements (Convention on Biological Diversity, CITES, Ramsar Convention).
- **Global issues**: acid rain (SO₂ and NOₓ emissions, lowered pH of precipitation); ozone depletion (CFCs, destruction of stratospheric ozone); eutrophication (excess nutrients in water, algal blooms); sustainable development (meeting present needs without compromising future generations).

#### Examiner traps

- Treating pollution as one-dimensional — pollution has chemical, biological and physical components.
- Confusing in-situ and ex-situ conservation — in-situ is conservation in the natural habitat; ex-situ is conservation outside the natural habitat.
- Forgetting that biodiversity has three components — genetic, species and ecosystem diversity.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Human impact on the environment

**Air pollution**: emissions of SO₂, NOₓ, particulate matter, ozone, VOCs. Sources: vehicle exhaust, power generation, industrial processes. Effects: respiratory disease, acid rain, smog.

**Water pollution**: discharge of sewage, industrial effluent, agricultural runoff (fertilisers, pesticides). Effects: eutrophication, hypoxia, loss of aquatic biodiversity, contamination of drinking water.

**Soil pollution**: deposition of heavy metals, pesticides, industrial waste. Effects: loss of soil fertility, contamination of crops.

**Habitat destruction**: deforestation (tropical forests, mangroves), urbanisation, land conversion for agriculture. Effects: loss of habitat, fragmentation, reduced biodiversity.

**Climate change**: greenhouse gas emissions (CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, halocarbons) trap heat in the atmosphere. Effects: rising global temperatures, sea-level rise, ocean acidification, more frequent extreme weather events.

**Overexploitation**: overfishing (collapse of fish stocks), hunting (endangered species), illegal wildlife trade.

**Invasive species**: species introduced outside their native range that outcompete native species (e.g. water hyacinth, cane toad).

#### Pollution control

**Monitoring**:

- **Air quality**: Air Quality Health Index (AQHI) in Hong Kong; PM2.5, PM10, NO₂, SO₂, O₃ measurements.
- **Water quality**: dissolved oxygen (DO), biochemical oxygen demand (BOD), pH, nutrient concentrations, bacterial counts.
- **Biodiversity**: species richness, abundance, indicator species.

**Mitigation**:

- **Cleaner technologies**: catalytic converters, flue gas desulfurisation, low-NOx burners.
- **Waste treatment**: sewage treatment (primary, secondary, tertiary), solid waste management (reduce, reuse, recycle), hazardous waste treatment.
- **Carbon reduction**: renewable energy, energy efficiency, carbon capture and storage.

**Legislation in Hong Kong**:

- **Air Pollution Control Ordinance**: controls emissions from vehicles, power stations and industrial processes.
- **Water Pollution Control Ordinance**: regulates discharges into Hong Kong waters.
- **Waste Disposal Ordinance**: regulates waste collection, treatment and disposal.
- **Environmental Impact Assessment Ordinance**: requires EIA for designated projects.
- **Country Parks Ordinance**: protects country parks.
- **Marine Parks Ordinance**: protects marine parks.

#### Conservation

**In-situ conservation**: protection of species in their natural habitats. Examples: country parks and marine parks in Hong Kong; national parks worldwide; biodiversity hotspots identified by Conservation International.

**Ex-situ conservation**: protection of species outside their natural habitats. Examples: zoos (e.g. Ocean Park, Hong Kong; Smithsonian's National Zoo), botanical gardens (e.g. Hong Kong Zoological and Botanical Gardens), seed banks (e.g. the Svalbard Global Seed Vault), captive breeding programmes.

**International agreements**:

- **Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD)**: 1992; conservation of biodiversity, sustainable use of biological resources, fair and equitable sharing of benefits.
- **CITES** (Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species): regulates international trade in endangered species.
- **Ramsar Convention**: protection of wetlands of international importance.

#### Global issues

**Acid rain**: SO₂ and NOₓ from fossil fuel combustion and vehicle exhaust react with water in the atmosphere to form sulfuric and nitric acids, lowering the pH of precipitation. Effects: damage to forests, lakes, buildings; mobilisation of toxic metals.

**Ozone depletion**: chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) release chlorine atoms in the stratosphere, which catalytically destroy ozone. Effects: increased UV-B reaching the Earth's surface, increased skin cancer and cataract risk. The Montreal Protocol (1987) phased out CFCs; the ozone layer is recovering.

**Eutrophication**: excess nutrients (nitrogen, phosphorus) in water bodies cause algal blooms, which block light and deplete dissolved oxygen when they decompose. Effects: fish kills, loss of biodiversity.

**Sustainable development**: the Brundtland definition (1987) — development that meets present needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs. Three pillars: environmental, social, economic. The UN 2030 Agenda (2015) sets 17 Sustainable Development Goals.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-topic links

- **III. Organisms and Environment**: population dynamics and biogeochemical cycles are the basis of applied ecology.
- **IV. Health and Diseases**: pollution effects on human health.
- **VIII. Biotechnology (Elective)**: bioremediation uses biological organisms to clean up pollution.

#### Exam technique

- **For pollution questions**: identify the pollutant, the source, the effect, and the control measure. Use specific examples.
- **For conservation questions**: distinguish in-situ from ex-situ conservation; cite specific protected areas and international agreements.
- **For sustainable development questions**: use the three-pillar framework and the SDG framework.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Climate change is the same as the greenhouse effect." The greenhouse effect is a natural phenomenon that keeps the Earth warm; climate change refers to the human-driven changes in the global climate since the Industrial Revolution.
- "Biodiversity is only about species." It has three components: genetic, species, and ecosystem diversity.
- "Recycling is always good." Recycling has environmental benefits but is not free of impact — the optimal hierarchy is reduce, reuse, recycle.
