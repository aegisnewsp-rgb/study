# Topic 9

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your UNDANA admission test.

**Topic 9** tests language competency in **Bahasa Indonesia** and **English** at SMA/sederajat level. It contributes roughly 3% of the total admission score, so even a few correct answers can shift ranking near the cut-off.

#### Must-Know Areas

- **Sinonim, antonim, dan makna kata** (synonyms, antonyms, contextual meaning) in Indonesian and English.
- **Kalimat efektif** (effective sentences): parallelism, concision, logical cohesion, and avoidance of redundancy.
- **Ejaan yang disempurnakan / PUEBI**: capitalisation of proper nouns, correct comma use in compound sentences, and standard affixation.
- **English basics**: tenses, subject-verb agreement, articles (a/an/the), prepositions, and singular/plural third-person forms.
- **Reading comprehension** of short factual and narrative passages, including cloze-type fill-in-the-blank vocabulary.

#### High-Yield Pointers

- Always re-read the surrounding sentence before choosing a synonym — near-synonyms like *pengaruh* vs *dampak* are context-dependent.
- For cloze items, test collocational fit, not answer length; the shortest option is often correct.
- In English error-spotting, the verb almost always carries the error when the subject is third-person singular.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months of preparation.

#### Bahasa Indonesia: Tata Bahasa dan Kosakata

UNDANA's Indonesian items frequently probe **persamaan kata, lawan kata**, and **makna kata sesuai konteks**. A solid approach is to learn roots and standard affixes (*me-, pe-, -an, -kan, -i*) so that new words can be decoded on the spot.

The second frequent format is **kalimat efektif**: candidates must pick the option that is parallel, concise, and free of redundancy. A sentence with double subjects, mixed tenses, or a dangling participle is automatically non-effective.

#### Ejaan yang Disempurnakan (PUEBI)

PUEBI rules tested include capitalisation of proper nouns and the first word of a sentence, correct use of commas before conjunctions in compound sentences, and the spelling of borrowed words such as *kualitas, aktual, kategori*. Memorise the small set of words that are still written with *e/*u/* foreign spelling.

#### English: Grammar and Usage

English items centre on **tenses, subject-verb agreement, articles, and prepositions**. A typical stem reads, "The committee ____ its decision yesterday," testing the simple past against a tempting present perfect. Articles (*a/an/the*) and prepositions (*in, on, at, to, for*) are tested through short cloze sentences rather than abstract rules.

#### Reading Comprehension

Passages are short (100–180 words), factual or narrative. Expect one or two **inference** questions alongside literal recall. Underline the pronoun's referent and the contrast marker (*however, although, whereas*) before answering.

#### Common Question Patterns

| Format | What is tested | Strategy |
|---|---|---|
| Sinonim/antonim | Near-synonym disambiguation | Use context, not dictionary memory alone |
| Kalimat efektif | Parallelism, concision | Reject redundancy, double subjects, mixed tenses |
| PUEBI | Spelling, capitalisation, comma rules | Memorise PUEBI exception list |
| English cloze | Collocation, grammar | Test grammar first, then meaning |
| Reading comp | Literal and inferential | Locate pronoun and contrast markers |

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases in Near-Synonym Distinctions

The hardest sinonim items pair words with overlapping but non-identical meanings. *Pengaruh* refers to a causal force that shapes opinion or behaviour, while *dampak* emphasises a tangible consequence, often negative. *Efek* leans toward scientific or technical outcomes. When the stem mentions *kebijakan pemerintah* or *peraturan baru*, *dampak* is usually the safer pick; for *pendapat masyarakat*, *pengaruh* fits better.

#### Effective-Sentence Traps

Examiners test four recurring defects: (1) **subjek ganda** (double subject, e.g., *Para siswa mereka pergi*), (2) **predikat yang tidak sejalan** (mismatched predicate form), (3) **klausa berlebihan** (redundant clauses), and (4) **kohesi yang lemah** (weak cohesion, e.g., *karena itu, sehingga*). The fix is to keep one subject, one parallel predicate, and one logical connector.

#### English Error-Spotting Mechanism

In "find the incorrect word" items, scan in this order: **verb (tense/agreement) → article → preposition → pronoun → word form**. Roughly 60% of UNDANA-level English errors hide in the verb slot, especially the third-person singular *-s* in present simple (*He go* → *He goes*) and the past form of irregular verbs.

#### Connections to Adjacent Topics

Topic 9 reinforces reading stamina needed for **sains** and **soshum** passages elsewhere in the UNDANA paper. Vocabulary from this topic overlaps with **penalaran umum** items, where synonym substitution is a standard distractor.

#### Practice Prompts

1. Choose the most effective rewrite: *"Para guru-guru sedang membahas mengenai tentang kurikulum baru di sekolah."*
2. Identify the error: *"Each of the students have submitted their assignment before the deadline."*

#### Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Choosing the longest answer option on cloze items instead of testing collocational fit.
- Translating English idioms literally into Indonesian when the stem asks for a contextual equivalent.
- Applying *me-* and *di-* affixes by ear rather than by rule (*menulis* but *ditulis*, not *di-tulis*).
