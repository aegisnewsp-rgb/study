# Health Promotion and Patient Education

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Health promotion enables individuals and communities to raise control over health determinants, while patient education is the one-to-one clinical teaching that turns evidence into behaviour change. Within DOH (UAE) practice, both must follow the Patient Education standards and be documented in the medical record.

#### Core framework — Levels of prevention:
- **Primary:** stop disease before it starts — vaccination, smoking cessation, healthy diet.
- **Secondary:** detect early — HbA1c screening, mammography, blood-pressure checks.
- **Tertiary:** limit complications — diabetic foot care, post-MI cardiac rehab.

#### High-yield exam pointers:
- Apply Bloom's domains (cognitive / affective / psychomotor) when writing learning objectives.
- Use **teach-back** to verify comprehension; a leaflet alone does not satisfy DOH governance.
- Match counselling to the patient's **stage of change** (precontemplation → maintenance).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Defining the Two Pillars

**Health promotion** is the broader, socio-ecological strategy set out in the **Ottawa Charter (1986)**: build healthy public policy, create supportive environments, strengthen community action, develop personal skills, and reorient health services. **Patient education** is the clinical, bedside application of the same goals — assessing needs, setting objectives, delivering content, and evaluating understanding during a consultation.

#### The Patient-Education Process

A DOH-compliant encounter follows a five-step loop: assess learning needs → set SMART objectives → plan method (verbal, written, demonstration, digital) → deliver → evaluate using **teach-back**. The teach-back step is non-negotiable under DOH Patient Education standards because it documents comprehension, not just information transfer.

#### Behaviour-Change Counselling

Motivational interviewing (OARS — Open questions, Affirmations, Reflections, Summaries) and the **transtheoretical model** move patients from precontemplation through contemplation, preparation, action, and maintenance. Match the intervention to the stage; advice-giving alone fails in precontemplation.

| Concept | Key point |
| --- | --- |
| Primary prevention | Vaccination, smoking cessation, salt/sugar reduction |
| Secondary prevention | Screening: HbA1c, mammography, BP measurement |
| Tertiary prevention | Cardiac rehab, diabetic foot care, asthma action plans |
| Teach-back | Patient re-states instructions in own words — DOH requirement |
| Health literacy | Plain language + culturally adapted materials in Arabic, English, Hindi, Urdu |

#### UAE-Specific Priorities

DOH campaigns such as **Weqaya** (cardiovascular screening) and **FitforLife** (physical activity) shape local practice. Diabetes, obesity, tobacco, mental health, and vaccine uptake dominate counselling scenarios.

- Every patient-education session must be **recorded** with content, method, and comprehension check.
- Use adult-learning principles (Knowles): relevance, autonomy, prior experience.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Common Traps

Examiners frequently test the boundary between **health education** (information transfer) and **health promotion** (policy + environment + community). A leaflet is education; a workplace smoking ban is promotion. Confusing the levels of prevention is the single highest-yield mistake: foot care in established diabetic neuropathy is **tertiary**, not primary, even though it prevents amputation.

#### Domains of Learning

Bloom's taxonomy forces you to choose the right objective. Teaching a patient to **draw up insulin correctly** is psychomotor; explaining why glucose monitoring matters is cognitive; addressing fear of injections is affective. A complete plan addresses all three.

#### Worked Scenario

> A 52-year-old male, HbA1c 8.9 %, BMI 31, smoker. Apply the **Ottawa Charter**: develop personal skills (diet, exercise), create supportive environment (refer to Weqaya programme), reorient services (smoking-cessation clinic). Stage of change for smoking is assessed; if precontemplation, motivational interviewing precedes action planning. Teach-back is documented.

#### Practice Prompts

1. A DOH MCQ asks which level of prevention a breast-screening campaign represents — answer **secondary**, with rationale on early detection.
2. An OSCE station requires you to counsel an asthmatic patient — set one cognitive, one affective, and one psychomotor objective, then demonstrate teach-back.

1. Ottawa Charter, WHO 1986 — the global framework DOH aligns with.
2. DOH Patient Education standards, 2023 — documentation mandate.
3. CDC Health Literacy Universal Precautions Toolkit — teach-back guidance.
