# Trial Balance

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

A **trial balance (TB)** is a working paper, not a financial statement, that lists every ledger account balance at a given date to confirm that total debits equal total credits under the double-entry system. ICAN tests this topic in Financial Accounting & Reporting (Foundation/ATS) for roughly 3% weight, usually as a 4–8 mark MCQ or short computation.

- **Purpose:** tests the *arithmetical accuracy* of the double-entry records only.
- **Core rule:** Σ Debit balances (₦) = Σ Credit balances (₦); a difference is parked in the **Suspense Account**.
- **Two preparation methods:** *totals method* (sum each ledger account's two sides and write the larger side as the balance) and *balances method* (extract only the net debit or credit balance of each account).
- **Adjusted TB:** Unadjusted TB ± Adjusting journal entries (₦) for accruals, prepayments, depreciation, bad debts, and closing stock.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"BALANCE ≠ BOOK IS RIGHT"** — a Balanced TB only proves the *math*, never the *truth*. Errors of omission, principle, commission, original entry, compensation, and complete reversal still hide behind an agreed trial balance. Also remember: **TB = Test Bookkeeping, not Truth**.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Suspense Account | Temporary plug used when TB totals disagree (₦) |
| Unadjusted TB | Balances extracted before year-end adjustments |
| Adjusted TB | Balances after passing adjusting entries (₦) |
| Post-closing TB | Contains only real accounts: assets, liabilities, capital |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Purpose and Limitations

The trial balance is a checkpoint between the ledger and the final accounts. It confirms that every debit posting has an equal and opposite credit posting, but it cannot detect errors that do not disturb this equality. ICAN examiners exploit this misconception repeatedly: a question will state that a TB "agrees" and then ask which error is still possible.

#### Methods of Preparation

1. **Totals (Balance) Method:** Add the debit and credit sides of each ledger account separately and insert the *higher total* as the balance on the corresponding side of the TB.
2. **Balances Method (more common in exams):** Subtract the smaller side from the larger side and post only the *net balance* to the TB on its correct side.

The order of listing typically mirrors the ledger sequence: non-current assets → current assets → non-current liabilities → current liabilities → capital → income/revenue → expenses.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

#### Question:
Obi's ledger extracted the following balances at 31 March 2025: Land ₦1,200,000 (Dr); Motor Vehicle ₦950,000 (Dr); Stock 1 April 2024 ₦300,000 (Dr); Debtors ₦480,000 (Dr); Cash ₦120,000 (Dr); Provision for Doubtful Debts ₦40,000 (Cr); Creditors ₦360,000 (Cr); Capital ₦1,800,000 (Cr); Sales ₦2,100,000 (Cr); Purchases ₦1,250,000 (Dr); Wages ₦180,000 (Dr); Rent ₦120,000 (Cr — error in posting). Prepare the Trial Balance and state the Suspense Account figure.

#### Solution:
1. Sum debit column: 1,200,000 + 950,000 + 300,000 + 480,000 + 120,000 + 1,250,000 + 180,000 = **₦4,480,000**.
2. Sum credit column (treating Rent as credit per the data): 40,000 + 360,000 + 1,800,000 + 2,100,000 + 120,000 = **₦4,420,000**.
3. Difference = 4,480,000 − 4,420,000 = **₦60,000** shortfall on the credit side.
4. Plug into Suspense Account on the **credit side** for ₦60,000 so that the TB agrees.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students often place the Suspense Account on the *debit* side. Rule of thumb: the Suspense figure sits on the **shorter** column to balance the TB. If debits exceed credits, Suspense goes to credit, and vice versa.

| Error Type | TB Agrees? | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Error of original entry | Yes | Purchases of ₦5,000 posted as ₦500 |
| Error of omission (complete) | Yes | Sale to B. Musa omitted entirely |
| Error of commission | Yes | B. Musa posted as B. Mosa |
| Error of principle | Yes | Motor van debited to Repairs |
| Compensating errors | Yes | Overcast Sales by ₦10k, undercast Purchases by ₦10k |
| Complete reversal of entries | Yes | Correct accounts, wrong sides |
| Single-sided posting | **No** | Only debit side of a transaction recorded |
| Incorrect totals carried to TB | **No** | Arithmetic mistake in TB itself |

#### Adjusted and Post-Closing Trial Balance

The **adjusted TB** incorporates year-end adjustments so that the income statement and statement of financial position can be prepared directly from it. Common adjustments: closing stock, depreciation, accruals, prepayments, bad debts, and provision for doubtful debts. The **post-closing TB** is prepared after closing entries and lists only permanent/real accounts, proving that temporary accounts (income, expenses, drawings) have been transferred to the income summary and reset to zero.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Suspense Account Mechanics

The Suspense Account is a *temporary* clearing account opened solely to force the TB to balance. Its lifecycle: (1) credited or debited with the TB difference; (2) debited/credited as each discovered error is rectified through a journal entry; (3) closed to nil once all errors are located. Any residual balance indicates an unlocated error or a rectification error.

#### Edge Cases and Boundary Conditions

1. **Contra entries that fully cancel** (e.g. cash sales banked: Dr Bank, Cr Cash) produce no TB balance and are invisible in the TB.
2. **One-sided contra** (e.g. only Dr Bank recorded) imbalances the TB and forces a Suspense entry.
3. **Bank reconciliation** must be performed *before* finalising the TB because unpresented cheques and uncredited lodgements directly alter the cash/bank balance.
4. **Opening stock vs closing stock:** opening stock appears in the unadjusted TB as a debit; closing stock is an adjustment and does *not* enter the TB until the adjusted TB (unless a stock account is maintained continuously).
5. **Drawings** are always debit balances; reducing them requires a credit to Drawings and a debit to the relevant source (Bank, Cash, or Purchases).
6. **Provision for doubtful debts** is a credit balance reducing debtors; it is *not* netted against debtors in the TB.

#### Connection to Final Accounts

The adjusted TB is the direct feeder for the **Trading Account**, **Profit or Loss Account**, and **Statement of Financial Position**. Items on its debit side flow into the debit column of the trading/P&L (expenses and assets), while credit balances feed the income/revenue lines and liabilities/equity.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. **Prompt A:** Given a TB that disagrees by ₦1,800 on the debit side, and two discovered errors — (i) sales daybook overcast by ₦900, (ii) purchases returns credit side undercast by ₦900 — show the journal entries to clear Suspense and prove the residual balance is nil.
2. **Prompt B:** A TB agrees at ₦5,420,000 each side. Identify which of these errors *can* still exist: (a) ₦25,000 cash sale not posted at all; (b) wages paid to the owner's daughter wrongly debited to Wages; (c) purchases invoice from K. Ade posted to K. Adé; (d) the purchases journal was overcast by ₦10,000.

> 📌 **Formula Check:** TB Difference = Σ Debit balances − Σ Credit balances (₦); if non-zero, the absolute value is the Suspense Account figure placed on the shorter column. Adjusted TB Total = Unadjusted TB Total ± Σ Adjusting entries (₦).

| Mistake | Consequence in TB | Remedy |
|---|---|---|
| Listing all balances as debits | TB fails to balance | Re-classify each balance individually |
| Ignoring closing stock | Trading account understated/overstated | Pass closing stock adjustment |
| Placing Suspense on wrong side | Wrong suspense figure, errors not cleared | Side = shorter column |
| Skipping bank reconciliation | Cash balance wrong | Reconcile before finalising TB |
