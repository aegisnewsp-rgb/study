# Radioactivity and Nuclear Physics

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your NABTEB exam.

Radioactivity is the **spontaneous disintegration** of unstable nuclei (radionuclides) with the emission of α particles, β particles, or γ rays. The nucleus contains **Z protons** and **(A − Z) neutrons**, written as ᴬ_Z X. The decay law N = N₀ e^(−λt) governs how many unstable nuclei remain after time t, and half-life T½ = 0.693/λ is the time for half the sample to decay regardless of starting amount. In NABTEB, expect balanced nuclear equations, half-life numerics, and short notes distinguishing α, β, γ.

- **α (⁴₂He):** charge +2e, mass 4 u, highly ionising, stopped by paper.
- **β (⁰₋₁e):** charge −1e, mass ~1/1836 u, stopped by 3 mm aluminium.
- **γ:** no charge, no rest mass, needs several cm of lead to stop.

| Property | α | β | γ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Charge | +2e | −1e | 0 |
| Ionising power | Highest | Medium | Lowest |
| Penetrating power | Lowest | Medium | Highest |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months to revise.

#### Detection and Balancing Equations
A **Geiger-Müller (GM) tube** detects ionising radiation by counting pulses produced when radiation ionises argon gas inside the tube; a **cloud chamber** reveals α and β tracks as visible condensation trails. Before any count-rate calculation, **background radiation** (typically 0.1–0.3 µSv h⁻¹ in Nigeria) must be subtracted from the GM reading to obtain the true activity from the source. When writing a nuclear equation, conserve both the **nucleon number A** and the **proton number Z** on each side. For example, the α decay of uranium-238 is written ²³⁸₉₂U → ²³⁴₉₀Th + ⁴₂He; here A balances (238 = 234 + 4) and Z balances (92 = 90 + 2).

#### The Decay Law and Half-Life
The number of undecayed nuclei obeys N = N₀ e^(−λt), and the activity A = λN decays as A = A₀ (1/2)^(t/T½). A common NABTEB style question gives a count of 1200 counts per minute at noon and 150 counts per minute 24 hours later, then asks for T½. The factor is 1200/150 = 8 = 2³, so three half-lives have passed, giving T½ = 24/3 = **8 hours**.

- Convert all times to the **same unit** before using (1/2)^(t/T½).
- Half-life is independent of physical conditions (temperature, pressure, concentration).
- Activity A is measured in **becquerels** (Bq), where 1 Bq = 1 disintegration per second.

#### Energy from the Nucleus
The **mass defect Δm** is the difference between the summed masses of separated nucleons and the mass of the assembled nucleus. It is converted into **binding energy** by E = Δm c², with c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹. A typical NABTEB question provides masses of ²H and asks for the energy released when it fuses to form ³He.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Fission, Fusion and the Curve of Binding Energy
**Nuclear fission** is the splitting of a heavy nucleus (e.g. U-235) into two roughly equal fragments after capturing a slow neutron, releasing ~200 MeV per event and additional neutrons that can sustain a chain reaction in a **critical mass**. **Nuclear fusion** combines light nuclei (e.g. ²H + ³H → ⁴He + n + 17.6 MeV) and powers the Sun at ~15 million kelvin. The **curve of binding energy per nucleon** peaks near iron-56 (~8.8 MeV per nucleon), explaining why both fission of heavy elements and fusion of light elements release energy, while splitting iron absorbs it.

#### Edge Cases and Examiner Traps
Several distinctions are routinely tested. **Activity (Bq)** is disintegrations per second; **absorbed dose (Gy, J kg⁻¹)** is energy deposited per kilogram of tissue; **equivalent dose (Sv)** multiplies absorbed dose by a quality factor (1 for γ, 20 for α). Confusing these three quantities is a frequent mark-losing error. Background radiation, dominated by radon-222, cosmic rays and terrestrial potassium-40, must always be subtracted before quoting net count rates. Carbon-14 dating assumes a constant atmospheric ¹⁴C/¹²C ratio and a half-life of 5730 years; the method fails on samples older than ~50 000 years because too few nuclei remain.

#### Worked Numeric and Practice Prompts
A 2.0 µg sample of Po-210 (T½ = 138 days, M = 210 g mol⁻¹) contains N = (2.0 × 10⁻⁹ × 6.02 × 10²³)/210 ≈ 5.73 × 10¹² nuclei. Using λ = 0.693/138 = 5.02 × 10⁻³ day⁻¹ = 5.81 × 10⁻⁸ s⁻¹, the initial activity A₀ = λN ≈ 3.33 × 10⁵ Bq.

1. A bone fragment has a ¹⁴C activity 25% of a living sample. Find its age in years. *(Answer: 2 half-lives = 11 460 years.)*
2. Balance ²³⁹₉₄Pu → α + ? and identify the daughter nuclide.
3. Explain why α sources are safe in paper-wrapped containers but β sources require aluminium shielding.

> **Exam tip:** NABTEB rewards clear identification of α as ⁴₂He (not an electron) and explicit mention of conserved A and Z. Always subtract background count before quoting net activity.
