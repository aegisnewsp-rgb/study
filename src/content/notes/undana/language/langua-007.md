# Vocabulary and Word Meaning

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your UNDANA entrance exam.

The Indonesian subtest of the UNDANA entrance exam (Ujian Masuk UNDANA) tests two intertwined competencies under **Vocabulary and Word Meaning**: the size and accuracy of your **leksikon** (the stock of words you command) and your grasp of **makna kata** (the relationship between a word form and the concept it points to). For UNDANA specifically, about **3% of the language items** target these skills, usually as 4–6 multiple-choice questions asking you to pick a synonym, an antonym, the contextual meaning of an underlined word, or the correct hypernym/hyponym pair.

| Term | Definition (per KBBI) |
|---|---|
| Sinonim | Kata yang memiliki kesamaan makna, e.g. *indah* ≈ *cantik*. |
| Antonim | Lawan makna biner timbal-balik, e.g. *panas*–*dingin*. |
| Hiponim | Kata spesifik di bawah kategori lebih luas (*anjing* adalah hiponim dari *hewan*). |
| Hipernim | Kata kategori lebih luas yang menaungi kata spesifik (*hewan* hipernim dari *anjing*). |
| Homonim | Bentuk sama, makna berbeda, asal kata berbeda (*bank* lembaga vs *bank* tepi sungai). |
| Polisemi | Satu kata, beberapa makna saling terkait (*kepala* keluarga, sekolah, surat). |
| Denotasi | Makna harfiah sesuai kamus. |
| Konotasi | Makna tambahan emosional atau nilai. |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"S-A-H-H-D-K-P"** — Sinonim, Antonim, Hiponim/Hipernim, Homonim, Denotasi, Konotasi, Polisemi. Run this checklist on every underlined word in the UNDANA stem: match the question keyword (e.g. *lawan kata*) to the correct letter, then eliminate options that belong to a different relation.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Build working mastery of word-relation types, ambiguity, and context-driven meaning for the language subtest.

#### Core Word Relations

UNDANA examiners most often test whether you can classify a pair into the **right semantic relation**. Treat each pair as a relation, not just two words. A *sinonim* pair shows meaning overlap but is rarely 100% interchangeable: *takut* triggers an immediate present threat, while *khawatir* signals anticipatory anxiety, so swapping them in a sentence changes tone. An *antonim* must be binary and reciprocal; *besar* and *buruk* are merely co-occurring opposites, not antonyms. The hyp**er**nym–hyp**o**nym ladder requires you to ask "which word is the *kategori umum*?" — *binatang* includes *kucing, gajah, kupu-kupu*, so *binatang* is the hypernym.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix

| Relation | Same form? | Same meaning? | Same origin? | UNDANA trigger word |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sinonim | No | Yes (with nuansa) | Irrelevant | *persamaan kata*, *padanan* |
| Antonim | No | Opposite, binary | Irrelevant | *lawan kata* |
| Hiponim–Hipernim | No | One is subset of the other | Irrelevant | *bagian dari*, *jenis dari* |
| Homonim | **Yes** | No | **No** | *bentuk sama, makna beda* |
| Homofon | Different spelling, same sound | Usually different | Irrelevant | *bunyi sama, ejaan beda* |
| Homograf | Same spelling, different sound | Different | Irrelevant | *ejaan sama, lafal beda* |
| Polisemi | Same | Several related senses | **Yes** | *bermakna ganda*, *bermakna luas* |

#### Ambiguity and Context

- **Homonim** = same form, different etymology (e.g. *bank* from English *bank* 'financial institution' vs Dutch *bank* 'shore'). Polisemi is **not** homonim because the senses share one root.
- **Homofon** in Indonesian: *sang* (honorific) vs *sangkar* (first syllable only), or *sri* (title) vs *sari* (essence). Confusions surface in listening or dictation items.
- **Homograf**: Indonesian *apel* is written the same way but pronounced [apél] 'fruit' and [apəl] 'roll call' — examiners use this to trap students who assume one meaning per spelling.

#### Borrowing and PUEBI Spelling

Loanwords already entered KBBI follow **PUEBI** rules. Memorise these high-frequency traps:

- *aktifitas* → **aktivitas**; *metoda* → **metode**; *frekwensi* → **frekuensi**; *resiko* → **risiko**; *analisa* → **analisis** (noun, not verb).
- The suffix *-si* or *-ti* replaces foreign *-tion/-sion*; *-f* replaces *-ph*; *-ny-* replaces the Dutch *-gn-*.

#### Morphology That Changes Meaning

Indonesian **afiksasi** can shift class and meaning predictably:

- *bekerja* (V) → *pekerja* (N, agent); *pekerjaan* (N, nominalised); *mengerjakan* (V, transitive causative).
- *tulis* → *penulis* (agent), *tulisan* (result), *menulis* (active), *ditulis* (passive).
- A wrong affix breaks the sentence grammar and gives away the answer in completion-type items.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** Perhatikan kalimat berikut.
*"Siauw Giie, seorang bankir senior, duduk termenung di bank sungai sambil menatap air yang tenang."*
Kata *bank* yang terdapat pada kalimat tersebut termasuk hubungan makna….

A. Homonim karena bentuk dan makna sama, asal berbeda  
B. Homonim karena bentuk sama, makna berbeda, asal berbeda  
C. Polisemi karena satu kata memiliki makna yang berkaitan  
D. Sinonim karena keduanya merujuk pada lembaga keuangan  
E. Homograf karena ejaan sama, lafal berbeda  

#### Solution:

1. Identify the two senses: (i) *bank* = financial institution (from English *bank*), (ii) *bank* = river edge (from Dutch *bank*).
2. Check the criteria for the listed relations:
   - Sinonim (D) requires similar meaning — false, the two senses are unrelated.
   - Polisemi (C) requires one etymology with multiple related senses — false, the two *bank* words have **different origins** (English vs Dutch).
   - Homograf (E) requires identical spelling but different pronunciation — both senses here are pronounced [bangk], so this is also false.
3. **Homonim** (definition: same form, different meaning, different etymology) is the only relation that fits all three criteria — option **B**.
4. Cross-check against UNDANA's favourite phrasing: examiners almost always write *homonim* as "bentuk sama, makna berbeda, asal berbeda." If the stem omits the "asal berbeda" clue, you still need to recognise that two unrelated senses cannot be polisemi.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students pick **C (polisemi)** because the sentence reads naturally. Polisemi demands one shared etymology with senses that branch off a core meaning (like *kepala* of a person, of a letter, of a school — all rooted in "top/leader"). Two *bank* senses come from two languages, so it is homonim, not polisemi. Always ask: "Do these senses share one origin?" If no, it is homonim.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Mastery material: edge cases, PUEBI traps, and advanced practice prompts for sustained UNDANA preparation.

#### Edge Cases and Advanced Traps

1. **Gradasi antonim.** Some antonym pairs in UNDANA stem from items with intermediate degrees (*panas–hangat–dingin*). A correct option must sit at the **extreme** of the scale; a middle term like *hangat* is wrong even though it is "less hot" than *panas*.
2. **Hipernim palsu.** *Mawar, melati, anggrek* are not hypernyms of each other; their true hypernym is *bunga*. Examiners plant one flower name as a distractor to test whether you can climb one level up, not sideways.
3. **Sinonim ber nuansa.** *Meninggal, wafat, mangkat, tutup usia, tewas* share a core meaning "to die," but only *tewas* implies an accidental or violent end. In context-aware synonym items, only one fits the register.
4. **Konotasi vs denotasi in argumentative passages.** UNDANA sometimes asks for the "intended meaning" of an underlined word in an opinion column. The literal KBBI sense (*konotasi negatif* of *bengkok* = "tidak lurus") differs from the **figurative** sense ("tidak jujur/korup"). Pick the contextual reading, not the dictionary's first gloss.
5. **PUEBI high-frequency misspellings to drill:** *praktik* (not *praktek* as noun), *sistem* (not *sistim*), *izin* (not *iijin*), *masjid* (not *mesjid*). Each appears in cloze-type items asking for the correct Indonesian form of a foreign term.

#### Advanced Comparison: Polisemi vs Homonim vs Homofon vs Homograf

| Criterion | Polisemi | Homonim | Homofon | Homograf |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Same spelling | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ | ✓ |
| Same pronunciation | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ |
| Same etymology | ✓ | ✗ | Irrelevant | Irrelevant |
| Related senses | ✓ | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ |
| Example | *kepala surat, kepala sekolah* | *bank* (finance vs shore) | *sang* vs *sangkar* (partial) | *apel* fruit vs roll call |
| UNDANA keyword | *makna ganda saling terkait* | *bentuk sama, asal beda* | *bunyi sama, ejaan beda* | *ejaan sama, lafal beda* |

#### Practice Prompts

1. **Prompt A (contextual meaning).** In the sentence *"Kebijakan moneter bank sentral menahan laju inflasi,"* the underlined *moneter* most nearly means… (A) terkait uang atau keuangan, (B) terkait militer, (C) terkait moneter dalam arti sempit, (D) bersifat sementara, (E) bersifat darurat. Justify using the root Latin *moneta* (coin).
2. **Prompt B (PUEBI correction).** Rewrite the following paragraph using PUEBI-compliant spelling: *"Aktifitas mahasiswa di laboratorium frekwensi tinggi memerlukan analisa yang sistematis dan metoda yang teruji."* Identify each correction and cite the PUEBI rule.
