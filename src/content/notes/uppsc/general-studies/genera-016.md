# Topic 16

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Indian Polity and Governance** is the backbone of UPPSC RO/ARO General Studies, with **3% weightage** typically split between the Constitution, Centre-State relations, Panchayati Raj and District Administration. The Indian Constitution's **salient features** borrow heavily from the **Government of India Act 1935** (federal scheme, emergency provisions, public service commissions), the **British Constitution** (parliamentary system, rule of law, single citizenship), the **US Constitution** (fundamental rights, judicial review, federal structure), the **Irish Constitution** (Directive Principles) and the **Canadian Constitution** (residual powers with the Centre). For UPPSC, the **73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendments (1992)** are the most tested sub-topics because they directly govern UP's rural and urban local bodies. Memorise the **three lists** of the Seventh Schedule and Article 51A's **Fundamental Duties** verbatim. In Prelims, 2–3 MCQs come from Panchayati Raj and the District Magistrate's role; in Mains, expect a 150-word note on Centre-State relations.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Constitutional Framework
The Indian Constitution opens with the **Preamble**, which declares India a **Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic** with **justice, liberty, equality and fraternity**. Part III enshrines six **Fundamental Rights** — Right to Equality (Art. 14–18), Right to Freedom (Art. 19–22), Right against Exploitation (Art. 23–24), Right to Freedom of Religion (Art. 25–28), Cultural and Educational Rights (Art. 29–30) and Right to Constitutional Remedies (Art. 32). Part IV contains **Directive Principles of State Policy**, classified as **Socialist** (e.g. Art. 39 equitable distribution), **Gandhian** (e.g. Art. 40 village panchayats, Art. 48 cow slaughter prohibition) and **Liberal** (e.g. Art. 44 uniform civil code). **Fundamental Duties** under **Article 51A** were inserted by the **42nd Amendment, 1976** on the Swaran Singh Committee's recommendation, originally ten duties, the eleventh added in 2002.

#### Federalism and Centre-State Relations
India follows a **quasi-federal** structure with a unitary bias. **Article 245** defines the territorial jurisdiction of Parliament and State Legislatures. The **Seventh Schedule** distributes legislative powers through **List I (Union, 97 subjects)**, **List II (State, 66 subjects)** and **List III (Concurrent, 47 subjects)**; residuary powers rest with Parliament under **Article 248**. Emergency provisions include **Art. 352** (National Emergency on war/external aggression/armed rebellion — the word "internal disturbance" was replaced by the 44th Amendment, 1978), **Art. 356** (President's Rule in a state) and **Art. 360** (Financial Emergency). The **Rajya Sabha** safeguards state interests under **Art. 249 and Art. 312**.

#### Local Self-Government
The **73rd Amendment, 1992** constitutionalised the **three-tier Panchayati Raj** — **Gram Panchayat (village)**, **Kshetra Panchayat / Panchayat Samiti (intermediate/block)** and **Zila Parishad (district)** — through Part IX and the **Eleventh Schedule** (29 subjects). The **74th Amendment, 1992** created Part IX-A for municipalities: **Nagar Panchayat** (transition area), **Municipal Council** (smaller urban area) and **Municipal Corporation** (larger urban area), with the **Twelfth Schedule** listing 18 functions. **Articles 243D and 243T** mandate **reservation of seats for SCs/STs in proportion to population** and **not less than one-third reservation for women** (including the OBC-Backward Classes rotation per the UP provision).

#### UPPSC and District Administration
The **Uttar Pradesh Public Service Commission (UPPSC)** is constituted under **Article 315**, with the Chairman and members appointed by the Governor. The **Review Officer (RO)** and **Assistant Review Officer (ARO)** posts are recruited through a **Prelims–Mains–Typing** pattern; the **District Magistrate (DM)** functions as the **chief revenue officer, magistrate, developmental coordinator and link between state and district**. Under the **UP Land Revenue Act, 1901** and **CrPC 1973**, the DM supervises **tehsildars, naib tehsildars and police**.

#### Key Facts
- Constitution length: **448 articles in 25 parts, 12 schedules** (original); **395 articles in 22 parts, 8 schedules** at adoption.
- Emergency declared **three times** in India: 1962 (Sino-Indian War), 1971 (Bangladesh), 1975–77 (Internal disturbance, revoked 1977).
- RTI Act **2005** applies to the whole of India including J&K, replacing the older Freedom of Information Act 2002.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Borrowed Features — Exact Mapping
- **British**: parliamentary system, single citizenship, writs, legislative procedure, bicameralism.
- **American**: fundamental rights, judicial review, independence of judiciary, impeachment of President.
- **Irish**: Directive Principles, nomination of Rajya Sabha members by the President, freedom of trade.
- **Canadian**: federation with strong centre, residuary powers, advisory jurisdiction of Supreme Court.
- **Australian**: concurrent list, freedom of trade, joint sitting of Parliament under **Art. 108**.
- **Government of India Act 1935**: federal scheme, emergency, public service commissions, administrative details, judiciary.

#### Emergency and Federal Distortion
A **National Emergency (Art. 352)** converts the federal structure into a unitary one: Parliament can legislate on **State List subjects**, **fundamental rights except Art. 20 and 21** can be suspended, and the **tenure of Lok Sabha and State Legislatures** can be extended by one year at a time. **Article 356 (President's Rule)** has been imposed **over 100 times** on states, often used to topple hostile governments — a misuse repeatedly flagged by the **Sarkaria Commission (1987)** and **Punchhi Commission (2007)**.

#### Panchayati Raj — Operational Detail
The **73rd Amendment** mandates a **five-year term** for Panchayats with elections conducted by the **State Election Commission (Art. 243K)**. The **District Planning Committee (Art. 243ZD)** consolidates plans from rural and urban bodies. In UP, the **UP Kshetra Panchayat and Zila Parishad Adhiniyam, 1961** predates, but is now governed by, the constitutional framework. Gram Sabha under **Art. 243A** is the **assembly of all registered voters** of a village.

#### Worked Solution — Typical UPPSC MCQ
**Q.** "The 11th Fundamental Duty was added to Article 51A by which amendment?"
**Approach:** Recall that **51A originally listed 10 duties**; the 11th — "to strive for excellence in all spheres of individual and collective activity" — was added by the **86th Amendment, 2002**, alongside the Right to Education under Art. 21A. The answer: **86th Constitutional Amendment, 2002**.

#### Common Mistakes
- Confusing **5th and 6th Schedules** (tribal administration) with **11th and 12th** (Panchayat and Municipality subjects).
- Saying "Preamble is a part of the Constitution" — SC in **Berubari Union (1960)** said it is not, though in **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)** it was held the basic structure cannot be amended.
- Confusing **Art. 32** (Supreme Court writs) with **Art. 226** (High Court writs); both are constitutional remedies.

#### Practice Prompts
1. Compare the **73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendments**: subjects of Schedules, reservation provisions, and election authority.
2. Critically analyse the impact of **President's Rule (Art. 356)** on Centre-State relations, citing at least two commission recommendations.
