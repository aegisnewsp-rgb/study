# Oxidation & Reduction Reactions

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your Makerere Chemistry paper.

A redox reaction is a coupled process: one species loses electrons (oxidation) while another gains them (reduction). The two halves cannot happen alone in a net reaction.

- **Oxidation** = loss of electrons, oxidation number **increases**.
- **Reduction** = gain of electrons, oxidation number **decreases**.
- **Oxidising agent** = species that **gains** electrons (it itself gets reduced).
- **Reducing agent** = species that **loses** electrons (it itself gets oxidised).
- Faraday's law of electrolysis: **m = (M · I · t) / (n · F)**, where *m* in kg, *M* in kg/mol, *I* in A, *t* in s, *n* = electrons per ion, *F* = 96485 C/mol.

| Half-reaction type | General form | Electrons appear as |
|---|---|---|
| Oxidation | M → M^(n+) + n e⁻ | product (right) |
| Reduction | X^(m+) + m e⁻ → X | reactant (left) |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook (OIL RIG):** **O**xidation **I**s **L**oss, **R**eduction **I**s **G**ain — of electrons. Pair it with "**O**xidising agent **a**ccepts, **R**educing agent **d**onates" to avoid the most common reversal in Makerere scripts.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

> The working understanding: how oxidation numbers are assigned, how half-reactions are written, and how electrolytic calculations are set up.

#### Oxidation number rules you must apply without hesitation

The rules below are applied in order; the first rule that fits decides the number.

| Priority | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Free (uncombined) elements = 0 | O₂, S₈, Fe(s) all 0 |
| 2 | Group I metals in compounds = +1 | Na in NaCl = +1 |
| 3 | Group II metals in compounds = +2 | Mg in MgO = +2 |
| 4 | Fluorine in compounds = −1 | F in HF = −1 |
| 5 | Hydrogen = +1 generally, but **−1 in metal hydrides** | H in NaH = −1 |
| 6 | Oxygen = −2 generally, but **−1 in peroxides**, −½ in superoxides | O in H₂O₂ = −1 |
| 7 | Sum = 0 for a neutral compound, = charge for a polyatomic ion | SO₄²⁻: S + 4(−2) = −2 → S = +6 |

#### The ion-electron (half-reaction) method in acidic medium

A reliable sequence for any acidic-medium balance:

1. Split into oxidation and reduction half-reactions.
2. Balance all atoms **except O and H** first.
3. Balance O by adding **H₂O**, then balance H by adding **H⁺**.
4. Balance charge by adding **electrons** to the more positive side.
5. Multiply the half-reactions so the electrons cancel, then add.
6. Cancel anything that appears on both sides (H₂O, H⁺).

In basic medium, after step 5 add the same number of OH⁻ to both sides as there are H⁺ on one side; the H⁺ + OH⁻ pairs convert to H₂O, which is then cancelled.

#### Concept comparison matrix

| Feature | Oxidising agent | Reducing agent |
|---|---|---|
| Electron action | Accepts / takes | Donates / gives |
| What happens to it | Gets reduced | Gets oxidised |
| Change in oxidation number | Decreases | Increases |
| Typical examples | KMnO₄, K₂Cr₂O₇, Cl₂, O₂ | KI, H₂, C, CO, Fe²⁺ |
| Half-reaction role | Reactant side with e⁻ | Product side releases e⁻ |

| Reaction context | KMnO₄ product | Colour change |
|---|---|---|
| Acidic (e.g. H₂SO₄) | Mn²⁺ (colourless) | Purple → colourless |
| Neutral / weakly basic | MnO₂ (brown ppt) | Purple → brown |
| Strongly basic | MnO₄²⁻ (green) | Purple → green |

#### Electrolytic mass deposited

The single equation used in 3–5 mark calculations:

> 📌 **Formula Check:** m = (M · I · t) / (n · F). m = kg liberated, M = molar mass in kg/mol, I = amperes, t = seconds, n = electrons per ion, F = 96485 C mol⁻¹.

Q = I·t (coulombs), moles of electrons = Q/F, moles of substance = (Q/F)/n, mass = moles × M.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

> Edge cases, traps the Makerere Chemistry paper sets, and how this topic links to electrochemistry and thermodynamics.

#### Why the medium changes the permanganate product

KMnO₄ is Mn(VII). The product depends entirely on pH because the available protons determine how many oxygen atoms can leave as water:

- Acidic: Mn⁷⁺ + 5 e⁻ → Mn²⁺ (gain of 5 electrons per Mn)
- Neutral: Mn⁷⁺ + 3 e⁻ → Mn⁴⁺ as MnO₂
- Strongly basic: Mn⁷⁺ + 1 e⁻ → Mn⁶⁺ as MnO₄²⁻

Students who memorise only "Mn goes to +2" lose marks whenever the question specifies neutral or basic conditions. The number of electrons transferred per mole of KMnO₄ is **not constant** — it is **5 in acid, 3 in neutral, 1 in strong base**. This single fact decides the equivalent weight and therefore the titration result.

#### Disproportionation: when one species plays both roles

In disproportionation the same element is simultaneously oxidised and reduced. Three to memorise for Makerere-level papers:

1. **Hydrogen peroxide:** 2 H₂O₂ → 2 H₂O + O₂; oxygen moves from −1 to −2 (reduction) and −1 to 0 (oxidation).
2. **Chlorine in hot NaOH:** 3 Cl₂ + 6 OH⁻ → 5 Cl⁻ + ClO₃⁻ + 3 H₂O; chlorine 0 → −1 and 0 → +5.
3. **Copper(I) in solution:** 2 Cu⁺ → Cu²⁺ + Cu(s); copper +1 → +2 and +1 → 0.

#### Boundaries and links to adjacent topics

- **Electrochemistry connection:** A galvanic cell separates the two half-reactions physically; the anode hosts oxidation, the cathode hosts reduction. E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode (V), and ΔG° = −nFE°cell (J mol⁻¹) — the same *n* that appears in Faraday's law appears in the Gibbs energy expression.
- **Faraday's first vs second law:** the first law gives the mass–charge proportionality above; the second law states that equal charges liberate masses in the ratio of equivalent weights (M/n).
- **Limiting case of electrolysis:** if t → 0, m → 0; if current efficiency < 100%, the actual mass is the theoretical mass multiplied by the efficiency fraction.

#### Advanced practice prompts

1. Balance the reaction of K₂Cr₂O₇ with FeSO₄ in dilute H₂SO₄ and identify which atoms change oxidation number. Predict the colour change from orange to green.
2. A current of 2.5 A passes through a CuSO₄ solution for 40 minutes. Calculate the mass of copper deposited (Cu = 63.5 g mol⁻¹, n = 2, F = 96485 C mol⁻¹). State one assumption you must make about current efficiency.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Mixing up moles of electrons with moles of substance. The chain is Q = I·t, then n(e⁻) = Q/F, then n(substance) = n(e⁻)/z, where z is the electron change per ion. Skipping the final division gives an answer that is too large by exactly the factor z.
