# Magnetism

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Scan this tier the night before CUET UG Physics — six bullets, one formula card, one recall trick.

A moving charge makes a magnetic field. That single sentence carries half the CUET Magnetism syllabus, because every coil, solenoid, and galvanometer question starts from it.

- **Biot–Savart law (in vacuum):** dB = (μ₀/4π) · (I dℓ sinθ / r²) — B in tesla, I in ampere, r in metre, θ between current element and position vector, μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ T·m/A (exact, defined).
- **Straight long wire:** B = μ₀ I / (2π r). Forget the 2π and the answer is wrong by a factor of π — examiners love this.
- **Centre of circular coil (N turns):** B = μ₀ N I / (2R).
- **Force on charge:** F = q v B sinθ, direction from Fleming's left-hand rule.
- **Inside a long solenoid:** B = μ₀ n I, where n = N/ℓ (turns per metre).
- **Earth's field components:** declination (angle between geographic and magnetic meridian), inclination or dip (angle the field makes with horizontal), and horizontal component Bₕ = B cos I.

| Symbol | Meaning | SI unit | Quick check |
|---|---|---|---|
| B | magnetic flux density | T (kg·s⁻²·A⁻¹) | vector field |
| Φ | magnetic flux = BA cosθ | Wb | scalar |
| μ₀ | permeability of free space | T·m/A | 4π × 10⁻⁷ exactly since 2019 |
| m | magnetic dipole moment | A·m² | m = NIA for a coil |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"Motor = Left, Generator = Right."** Fleming's *left*-hand rule gives the direction of *force* on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field. Fleming's *right*-hand rule gives the direction of *induced* current when a conductor moves in a field. Swap them and the answer's sign flips — instant negative marking bait.

#### Oersted to Ampere — the logical chain

Oersted's 1820 experiment showed a compass needle deflecting near a current-carrying wire. That observation forces three questions: how strong is the field at a point, what direction does it point, and what does it do to another current? The Biot–Savart law answers the first quantitatively, the right-hand thumb rule answers the second, and the Lorentz force answers the third. Ampere's circuital law — ∮ B·dℓ = μ₀ I_enc — is the same physics expressed in integral form, exactly analogous to Gauss's law for E.

#### Concept comparison matrix

| Feature | Diamagnetic | Paramagnetic | Ferromagnetic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Susceptibility χ | χ < 0 (small, ≈ −10⁻⁵) | χ > 0 (small, ≈ +10⁻³) | χ ≫ 1 (up to 10⁵) |

#### Worked exam-style problem

A circular coil of 50 turns, radius 8 cm, carries 2 A and lies in a uniform magnetic field of 0.4 T. The plane of the coil makes 30° with the field. Find the torque, and state which way the coil rotates to reach equilibrium.

*Step 1 — area of one turn:* A = πR² = π × (0.08)² ≈ 2.011 × 10⁻² m².

*Step 2 — magnetic dipole moment:* m = N I A = 50 × 2 × 2.011 × 10⁻² ≈ 2.011 A·m².

*Step 3 — angle between m (normal to coil) and B:* if the plane is at 30° to B, the normal is at 90° − 30° = 60° to B. So θ = 60°, sinθ = √3/2 ≈ 0.866.

*Step 4 — torque magnitude:* τ = m B sinθ = 2.011 × 0.4 × 0.866 ≈ 0.697 N·m.

*Step 5 — direction:* the dipole rotates so that m aligns with B, i.e. the coil's plane becomes perpendicular to B (plane normal parallel to B).

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students read "the plane makes 30° with the field" and feed sin30° straight into τ = NIAB sinθ. The angle θ in that formula is between the **area vector (normal)** and **B**, not between the plane and B. Use sin(90° − 30°) = cos30°. Many solvers lose one mark here without realising.

#### Galvanometer conversion — the rule of thumb

- To ammeter (measure larger current I): connect a small **shunt** S = (I_g · G) / (I − I_g) **in parallel** with the galvanometer of resistance G and full-scale current I_g.
- To voltmeter (measure larger voltage V): connect a large **series** resistance R = (V / I_g) − G in series.

Mnemonic: **"Ammeter = shunt (parallel, small). Voltmeter = multiplier (series, large)."** The reason: an ammeter must draw a fraction of the line current, so it sits across a low-resistance bypass; a voltmeter must drop most of the potential difference, so it carries the same tiny current across a large resistor.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Read this only after the Standard tier feels easy. Targets multi-step CUET numericals and assertion–reason items.

#### Where NCERT diagrams hide trick MCQs

CUET UG draws roughly 4–6 Magnetism items every year, and three of them are usually centred on the cyclotron, the tangent galvanometer, and the hysteresis loop. In the cyclotron diagram, the dees are the two semicircular electrodes, the frequency of the applied AC must equal the cyclotron frequency qB/(2πm) — if a question changes B without changing f, the resonance condition fails and particles spiral inward instead of gaining energy. In the tangent galvanometer, the working equation Bₕ = (μ₀ n I) / (2 Rₕ · tanθ) gives Earth's horizontal field from the deflection θ; examiners often swap tan and sin to test whether the candidate actually memorised the derivation rather than the formula.

#### Hysteresis — the loop, and what each corner means

A hysteresis loop traces B inside a ferromagnetic sample against H (the applied external field intensity). The residual B when H returns to zero is **remanence**; the reverse H needed to bring B to zero is **coercivity**. Soft iron has a narrow loop (low coercivity, low hysteresis loss) — used in transformer cores. Steel has a wide loop (high remanence) — used in permanent magnets. Above the Curie temperature the domains randomise and the material stops being ferromagnetic, becoming merely paramagnetic — the loop collapses to a straight line through the origin.

#### Edge cases and limits

- **μᵣ → 1** for vacuum, just above 1 for air; almost all textbook problems take μᵣ = 1 unless the medium is named.
- **μᵣ → ∞** idealises a perfect magnetic core — leads to the assumption that all flux stays inside the core in transformer analysis.
- **Cyclotron limit:** the classical radius r = mv/(qB) breaks down when v approaches c; CUET stays non-relativistic, but the formula assumes uniform B and non-relativistic speeds.
- **Direction of B around a wire:** if current flows into the page, field lines curl clockwise; out of the page, counter-clockwise. Cross-product form: dB ∝ I dℓ × r̂.
- **Two parallel wires carrying equal currents in opposite directions:** the net field far away falls faster than 1/r because the dipole terms cancel only at infinity.

#### Common traps list (advanced)

1. Treating Φ and B as interchangeable — Φ = ∫B·dA (Weber), B = dΦ/dA (Tesla).
2. Writing τ = NIAB and forgetting sinθ when the coil is not broadside-on.
3. Assuming Earth's magnetic pole and geographic pole coincide — they are roughly 11° apart (declination).
4. Equating magnetic field lines with electric field lines — magnetic lines always close on themselves; no magnetic monopole exists.
5. Using right-hand rule for a solenoid but curling the fingers in the direction of current flow; thumb gives N pole.
6. Confusing magnetic susceptibility χ with relative permeability μᵣ — relation is μᵣ = 1 + χ.
7. In torque problems, treating angle θ as between B and the coil's plane, not the coil's normal.

#### Advanced practice prompts

1. **Cyclotron resonance:** A proton enters a cyclotron with B = 1.2 T. The oscillator supplies 12 MHz. Show whether resonance holds, then find the dee radius needed for a 5 MeV exit kinetic energy. (Answer keys: qB/(2πm) ≈ 18.3 MHz — not resonant at 12 MHz; increase B or lower f.)
2. **Galvanometer conversion design:** A galvanometer has G = 100 Ω, I_g = 5 mA. Convert it to read (a) 0–1 A ammeter, (b) 0–10 V voltmeter. Compute S and R exactly and check the resulting instrument resistance is much smaller (ammeter) or much larger (voltmeter) than the circuit it measures.

> 📌 **Exam Strategy for CUET UG Magnetism (4% weightage):** expect 1–2 direct numericals (Biot–Savart, solenoid, Lorentz force), 1 galvanometer conversion, 1 conceptual MCQ on Earth's magnetism or hysteresis, and possibly 1 assertion–reason item on Fleming's rules. Spend ≤90 seconds per numerical — the formulas are short and the numbers usually clean (μ₀ carries the 4π, so π cancels neatly). Skip the long-hysteresis derivation unless you have ≥2 minutes spare.
