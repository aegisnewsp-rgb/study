# Ratio and Proportion

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

A **ratio** compares two like quantities by division: `a:b = a/b` (dimensionless). A **proportion** equates two ratios: `a:b = c:d` holds when `ad = bc`. The two terms of a ratio are the **antecedent** (a) and the **consequent** (b). When a whole Q is split in ratio m:n, the parts are `Q·m/(m+n)` and `Q·n/(m+n)`. Direct variation is `y = kx` (k constant), inverse variation is `y = k/x`.

- **Simplify** a ratio by dividing both terms by their HCF (e.g., 24:36 → 2:3).
- **Divide** ₹500 in 3:2 → ₹300 and ₹200.
- **Direct vs inverse**: more hours → fewer workers means *inverse* proportion.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Definitions

A ratio `a:b` is dimensionless — cancel units before forming it (e.g., 150 g : 1 kg = 150 : 1000 = 3 : 20). Two ratios are **equivalent** when one is a constant multiple of the other. The **mean proportional** between a and c is `b` where `a:b = b:c`, so `b = √(ac)`. A **continued proportion** chains three or more ratios so each adjacent pair keeps the same common multiplier.

#### Cross-Multiplication Test

`a:b = c:d` is true if and only if `ad = bc`. This test converts ratio equality into a single multiplication check and is the fastest way to solve "find the fourth proportional" questions (e.g., 3, 5, 12 → find x: `3·x = 5·12` → x = 20).

#### Dividing a Quantity in a Ratio

To divide Q in ratio m:n, compute parts `Q·m/(m+n)` and `Q·n/(m+n)`. For three-part ratios a:b:c, weights are `a/(a+b+c)`, `b/(a+b+c)`, `c/(a+b+c)`.

| Operation | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Simplify ratio | Divide each term by HCF |
| Divide Q in m:n | Part₁ = Q·m/(m+n), Part₂ = Q·n/(m+n) |
| Compound ratio of a:b, c:d | ac : bd |
| Fourth proportional | a:b = c:x → x = bc/a |

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Direct vs Inverse Variation

Direct proportion: `y = kx`, so `x₁/x₂ = y₁/y₂`. If quantity doubles, the other doubles. Inverse proportion: `y = k/x`, so `x₁/x₂ = y₂/y₁`. Time-and-work problems are inverse — 6 workers finish in 10 days, so 12 workers finish in `6·10/12 = 5` days.

#### Partnership and Mixture Applications

Partners share profit in ratio of (capital × time). If a partner withdraws mid-year, recompute their `capital × time` against the others before applying the ratio. In mixtures, the **mean price** of two ingredients at prices p₁, p₂ mixed in ratio m:n is `(m·p₁ + n·p₂)/(m+n)` — a weighted average, not a simple average.

#### Common Mistakes

- Adding ratio terms directly (3:4 ≠ 7 parts of the whole — convert to fractions 3/7 and 4/7 first).
- Treating "more of x, more of y" as inverse proportion by reflex — always check whether y *grows* or *shrinks* with x.
- Forgetting to update partnership ratios after a capital change.

| Trap | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Confusing `m/(m+n)` with `m:n` | Convert to weights before splitting |
| Inverse vs direct in word problems | Track whether y rises or falls with x |
| Cross-multiply sign error | Verify `ad = bc` with units cancelled |

#### Practice Prompts

1. If 8 pumps empty a tank in 6 hours, how long do 12 pumps take? (Answer: 4 hours — inverse.)
2. Divide Rs. 980 among A, B, C in ratio 2:3:5. (Answer: 196, 294, 490.)
