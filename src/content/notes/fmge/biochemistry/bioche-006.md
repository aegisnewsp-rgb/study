# Glycolysis and Gluconeogenesis

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Glycolysis** is the ten-step cytosolic pathway that cleaves one glucose into two pyruvates, producing a **net 2 ATP and 2 NADH**. It has two stages: a preparatory (investment) phase that consumes 2 ATP, and a payoff phase that returns 4 ATP per glucose. **Phosphofructokinase-1 (PFK-1)** catalyses the committed, rate-limiting step (fructose-6-phosphate → fructose-1,6-bisphosphate).

**Gluconeogenesis** is the largely hepatic pathway that synthesises glucose from lactate, glycerol, and glucogenic amino acids (e.g., alanine). It is essentially glycolysis in reverse but bypasses three irreversible steps using **pyruvate carboxylase, PEPCK, fructose-1,6-bisphosphatase, and glucose-6-phosphatase**, costing **6 high-energy phosphate equivalents (4 ATP + 2 GTP)** per glucose made.

High-yield pointers: (1) **Fructose-2,6-bisphosphate** activates PFK-1 and simultaneously inhibits fructose-1,6-bisphosphatase, the molecular switch between the two pathways; (2) **Glucokinase** (liver/β-cells, Km ≈ 10 mM) handles postprandial glucose, while **hexokinase** (peripheral tissues, Km ≈ 0.1 mM) handles basal uptake and is feedback-inhibited by glucose-6-phosphate; (3) the **Cori cycle** recycles anaerobic muscle lactate back to hepatic glucose.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Pathway overview
Glycolysis (Embden–Meyerhof–Parnas) occurs entirely in the cytosol and does not require oxygen. Its ten enzymes convert glucose to two pyruvates through phosphorylated intermediates. Steps 1–5 (hexokinase, phosphoglucose isomerase, PFK-1, aldolase, triose phosphate isomerase) constitute the **preparatory phase**; steps 6–10 (G3P dehydrogenase, phosphoglycerate kinase, phosphoglycerate mutase, enolase, pyruvate kinase) form the **payoff phase**.

#### Key enzymes and regulation
| Enzyme | Step | Regulation |
|---|---|---|
| Hexokinase (Km ~0.1 mM) | Glucose → G6P | Inhibited by G6P; ubiquitous |
| Glucokinase (Km ~10 mM) | Glucose → G6P (liver/β-cell) | Induced by insulin; not product-inhibited |
| PFK-1 | F6P → F1,6BP | Activated by AMP, F2,6BP; inhibited by ATP, citrate |
| Pyruvate kinase | PEP → pyruvate | Activated by F1,6BP (feed-forward); inhibited by ATP, alanine |

Gluconeogenesis uses **four bypass enzymes** for the three glycolytic steps that are thermodynamically irreversible: pyruvate carboxylase (mitochondrial, requires biotin, allosterically activated by acetyl-CoA), PEPCK (cytosolic and mitochondrial isoforms), fructose-1,6-bisphosphatase, and glucose-6-phosphatase (ER lumen, present in liver and kidney but absent from muscle — hence muscle cannot release free glucose).

#### Energy and reducing equivalents
**Net reaction:** Glucose + 2 NAD⁺ + 2 ADP + 2 Pᵢ → 2 Pyruvate + 2 NADH + 2 H⁺ + 2 ATP + 2 H₂O. The NADH must be reoxidised — aerobically via the malate–aspartate or glycerol-3-phosphate shuttle, or anaerobically by lactate dehydrogenase. Gluconeogenesis from 2 pyruvates consumes **4 ATP + 2 GTP = 6 ATP equivalents**, with an additional 2 NADH needed at the glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase step.

#### Reciprocal hormonal control
Insulin favours glycolysis (induces glucokinase, PFK-1, pyruvate kinase; represses PEPCK). Glucagon and cortisol favour gluconeogenesis (induce PEPCK, fructose-1,6-bisphosphatase; inactivate pyruvate kinase via cAMP/PKA phosphorylation).

#### Exam pointers for FMGE
Expect direct MCQs on PFK-1 regulation, the Cori cycle, hexokinase vs glucokinase Km values, and the four gluconeogenic bypass enzymes. A common MCQ asks which enzyme is **absent in skeletal muscle** — answer: glucose-6-phosphatase. Another frequent stem names **fructose-2,6-bisphosphate** as the most potent allosteric activator of PFK-1.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Fructose-2,6-bisphosphate as a metabolic toggle
PFK-2/fructose-2,6-bisphosphatase is a bifunctional enzyme whose activity is dictated by a serine phosphorylation driven by the glucagon–PKA axis. In the fed state, dephosphorylated PFK-2 kinase activity dominates, raising F2,6BP and stimulating glycolysis. During fasting, phosphorylation flips the enzyme towards its phosphatase mode, dropping F2,6BP, relieving PFK-1 inhibition and lifting F1,6BPase inhibition — the molecular pivot that simultaneously accelerates gluconeogenesis.

#### Tissue compartmentalisation and shuttle logic
Pyruvate carboxylase is mitochondrial; PEPCK exists as a cytosolic isoform (induced by glucagon/cortisol) and a mitochondrial isoform (induced by thyroid hormone). PEPCK transcription is a classical target of FOXO1, glucocorticoid receptor, and cAMP-response element binding protein, and is also induced by **AMPK** during energy-stress states — a subtlety tested in advanced FMGE stems. The malate–aspartate and glycerol-3-phosphate shuttles determine whether cytosolic NADH yields 3 ATP (heart/liver) or 2 ATP (skeletal muscle) per NADH when reoxidised in the mitochondrion.

#### Clinical integration
- **Cori cycle:** exercise-muscle lactate → hepatic gluconeogenesis → blood glucose → muscle. Energy cost ≈ 4 ATP per glucose cycled (6 made − 2 net from glycolysis).
- **Warburg effect:** proliferating tumour cells favour aerobic glycolysis with lactate export; the FDG-PET scan exploits upregulated **glucokinase-like hexokinase (HK-II)** binding on mitochondria.
- **Fructose intolerance (Aldolase B deficiency):** F1,6BP cannot be cleaved; trapped F1P depletes Pᵢ and inhibits glycogenolysis/gluconeogenesis, causing hypoglycaemia after fructose ingestion.
- **Type 2 diabetes:** loss of the glucokinase–PFK-2/F2,6BP axis contributes to inappropriate hepatic glucose output; metformin activates **AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK)**, suppressing gluconeogenic gene expression.

#### Common pitfalls to avoid
- Confusing **PEP carboxykinase (PEPCK, GTP-dependent, gluconeogenesis)** with **PEP carboxylase** (plants) or **acetyl-CoA carboxylase** (fatty-acid synthesis).
- Forgetting that **pyruvate kinase deficiency** causes haemolytic anaemia (ATP depletion, 2,3-BPG accumulation) — an FMGE favourite.
- Mis-stating net ATP: glycolysis yields a **net 2 ATP**, not 4; gluconeogenesis consumes **6 ATP-equivalents**, not 4.

#### Practice prompts
1. Predict the effect of a high-protein, carbohydrate-free meal on hepatic PEPCK, F2,6BP, and blood lactate.
2. A patient with a homozygous *PFK-M* (muscle isoform) deficiency presents with haemolytic anaemia and myopathy after exercise — explain why glycogen accumulates and why muscle cannot release glucose into the blood.
