# Aldehydes Ketones

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Aldehydes (**R–CHO**) and ketones (**R–CO–R′**) both carry the carbonyl group **C=O**, but only aldehydes have a hydrogen directly attached to the carbonyl carbon. The C=O bond length is ~1.23 Å with a dipole moment near 2.3 D, making the carbon strongly electrophilic.

- **General formulas:** Aldehyde R–CHO; Ketone R–CO–R′; saturated acyclic isomers share **C_nH_(2n)O**.
- **IUPAC suffixes:** aldehyde → **–al** (ethanal, benzaldehyde); ketone → **–one** (propanone, butan-2-one).
- **α-Hydrogen** pKa ≈ 17–20; enables aldol condensation, halogenation, and the iodoform reaction.
- **Key distinctions:** Tollen's / Fehling's → aldehydes only; 2,4-DNP → both; Iodoform → CH₃CHO and CH₃COR only.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** "**A**ldehydes **A**dd **Ag**mirror — **K**etones **K**eep **K**opper away." Use **T**ollen's (**T**) for aldehydes, **F**ehling's (**F**) for aliphatic aldehydes, and **I**odoform (**I**) for the methyl-ketone trio: CH₃CHO, CH₃COCH₃, CH₃CH₂OH.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Structure and Polarity of C=O
The carbonyl carbon is **sp² hybridised** with bond angles of ~120°, producing a planar, trigonal geometry. Oxygen is more electronegative than carbon, so the bond is polarised **Cδ⁺=Oδ⁻**. This polarity is the engine for every reaction in this chapter — nucleophiles attack the carbon, electrophiles (H⁺) attack the oxygen.

#### Nucleophilic Addition Mechanism
A nucleophile (Nu⁻) attacks the electrophilic carbon; a proton then bonds to oxygen:

#### R₂C=O + H–Nu → R₂C(OH)(Nu)

Reagents that follow this route include **HCN** (cyanohydrin), **NaHSO₃** (bisulfite adduct), **RMgX** (Grignard → alcohol), and **NH₂OH** (oxime). Steric + electron-donating alkyl groups make **ketones less reactive than aldehydes**.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix

| Feature | Aldehyde (R–CHO) | Ketone (R–CO–R′) |
|---|---|---|
| C=O position | Terminal (–CHO end) | Internal (between two carbons) |
| Tollen's test | Positive (silver mirror) | Negative |
| Fehling's test | Positive (aliphatic only); benzaldehyde **negative** | Negative |
| 2,4-DNP test | Positive (orange ppt) | Positive (orange ppt) |
| Iodoform (CHI₃) | Positive for CH₃CHO only | Positive for CH₃–CO–R only |
| Relative reactivity | Higher (less steric, no +I alkyl) | Lower |
| α-H pKa | ~17 | ~20 |
| Oxidation product | Carboxylic acid (R–COOH) | Requires strong oxidant; cleavage |

#### α-Hydrogen and Aldol Condensation
α-Hydrogens are acidic because removal gives a **resonance-stabilised enolate** (negative charge delocalised onto oxygen). With dilute OH⁻, two aldehyde/ketone molecules condense:

#### 2 R–CH₂–CHO →(dilute OH⁻)→ R–CH₂–CH(OH)–CH(R)–CHO →(Δ)→ R–CH₂–CH=C(R)–CHO + H₂O

#### Distinguishing Tests at a Glance
- **Tollen's reagent [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺:** R–CHO → R–COO⁻ + **Ag↓** (silver mirror).
- **Fehling's solution (Cu²⁺ tartrate):** R–CHO → R–COO⁻ + **Cu₂O↓** (brick-red ppt).
- **2,4-DNP (Brady's reagent):** both aldehydes and ketones → orange/yellow hydrazone.
- **Schiff's reagent:** aldehydes restore pink colour; ketones do not.
- **Iodoform:** CH₃CHO, CH₃COR, CH₃CH₂OH → yellow **CHI₃** ppt.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** An organic compound (X) with molecular formula **C₃H₆O** gives a **silver mirror with Tollen's reagent**, a **brick-red precipitate with Fehling's solution**, and a **yellow precipitate on warming with I₂/NaOH**. Identify (X) and write the iodoform reaction.

#### Solution:
Step 1 — C₃H₆O is the functional-isomer formula for both propanal (CH₃CH₂CHO) and propanone (CH₃COCH₃).

Step 2 — Tollen's positive + Fehling's positive rules out the ketone (and also rules out aromatic aldehydes such as benzaldehyde, which are C₇H₆O).

Step 3 — Iodoform-positive narrows the choice to compounds carrying a **CH₃–C(=O)–** or **CH₃–CH(OH)–** unit. Propanal (CH₃CH₂CHO) contains the CH₃–CH₂–C(=O)H unit but **not** a CH₃ directly attached to C=O; however, it **does** give iodoform because the CH₃CHO fragment forms after enolisation under alkaline I₂.

Therefore **(X) = Propanal, CH₃CH₂CHO**, and the iodoform reaction is:

#### CH₃CH₂CHO + 3 I₂ + 4 NaOH → CHI₃↓ (yellow) + HCOONa + 3 NaI + 3 H₂O

(Here CHI₃ = iodoform, m = 393.7 g mol⁻¹; mass of I₂ used per mole of aldehyde = 3 × 254 = 762 g.)

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students pick **propanone** because "C₃H₆O + iodoform = methyl ketone" — but propanone is **Tollen's-negative**, so the silver-mirror clue eliminates it. Always apply **two tests together** to lock the answer.

#### Reduction Tools
- **NaBH₄ / LiAlH₄:** C=O → 1° (from aldehyde) or 2° (from ketone) alcohol.
- **Clemmensen (Zn–Hg / conc. HCl):** C=O → CH₂ (acid medium).
- **Wolff–Kishner (NH₂NH₂ / KOH, Δ):** C=O → CH₂ (basic medium).
- Use Wolff–Kishner when the substrate is acid-sensitive; Clemmensen when base-sensitive.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Selection Rules

1. **Benzaldehyde:** Gives positive Tollen's but **negative Fehling's** because the aryl ring withdraws electron density via resonance, disfavouring Cu²⁺ reduction under the alkaline tartrate medium.
2. **Formaldehyde (HCHO):** Has no α-H, so it **cannot** undergo aldol condensation; instead it undergoes **Cannizzaro disproportionation** — 2 HCHO + conc. OH⁻ → HCOO⁻ + CH₃OH.
3. **Crossed (Claisen–Schmidt) aldol:** Aromatic aldehyde (no α-H) + aliphatic aldehyde/ketone (with α-H) gives a single cross-aldol product — a favourite NEET assertion–reason item.
4. **NaBH₄ vs LiAlH₄:** NaBH₄ is milder, works in methanol/water; LiAlH₄ must be anhydrous (reacts violently with H₂O) and reduces esters/acids too.
5. **Perkin reaction:** Aromatic aldehyde + acid anhydride + sodium salt of the acid → α,β-unsaturated acid (e.g., benzaldehyde + (CH₃CO)₂O + CH₃COONa → cinnamic acid).
6. **Stephen reaction:** R–C≡N + SnCl₂/HCl → R–CH=NH → R–CHO (reduction of nitrile to aldehyde).
7. **Rosenmund reduction:** Acid chloride + H₂/Pd-BaSO₄ → aldehyde (ketone suppressed by poisoning the catalyst).

#### Acidity Order of α-H and Reference Acids

| Species | Approximate pKa |
|---|---|
| HCN | 9.2 |
| α-H of aldehyde | ~17 |
| α-H of ketone | ~20 |
| Ethyne (HC≡CH) | 25 |
| Ethanol | 16 (O–H, not α-H) |
| Water | 15.7 |

The enolate conjugate base is stabilised by resonance, which is why α-H is acidic enough to be removed by OH⁻.

#### Acetal vs Hemiacetal
- **Hemiacetal:** C bears **one –OR and one –OH** (e.g., glucose cyclisation).
- **Acetal:** C bears **two –OR** groups, formed when a hemiacetal reacts with a second alcohol molecule (acid-catalysed, with loss of H₂O). Acetals are stable in base but hydrolyse in acid.

#### Cannizzaro Reaction Mechanism
For aldehydes **without α-H** (HCHO, benzaldehyde, trimethylacetaldehyde):

#### 2 R–CHO + conc. OH⁻ → R–COO⁻ (oxidised) + R–CH₂OH (reduced)

It is a **disproportionation** — one molecule is oxidised while the other is reduced; hydride transfer goes through a six-membered transition state.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. **Conversion chain:** Propan-1-ol → Propanal → Propan-1-ol (via aldol, oxidation, and reduction). State each reagent with conditions and justify why NaBH₄ is preferred over LiAlH₄ for the final step.
2. **Assertion–Reason:** "Aldehydes are more reactive than ketones in nucleophilic addition" **(A)** because "alkyl groups are electron-withdrawing by induction" **(R)**. Decide whether A and R are true and whether R is the correct explanation. *(Trap: alkyl groups are electron-**donating** by induction, so R is false — the real reason is steric + electronic donation combined.)*

- Memorise the colour of precipitates: **silver mirror (Ag)**, **brick red (Cu₂O)**, **yellow (CHI₃)**, **orange (2,4-DNP)** — they appear verbatim in options.
