# Paper 3 — Listening and Integrated Skills — HKDSE English Language Notes

Paper 3 of the HKDSE English Language examination tests Listening and Integrated Skills. It is about 2 hours long and carries 30% of the subject mark (15% for Part A and 15% for Part B). The paper uses the same graded approach as Paper 1: Part A is compulsory for all candidates, and Part B requires a choice between Part B1 (easier, Level 4 max) and Part B2 (full range). Parts B1 and B2 are integrated listening, reading and writing tasks of different difficulty based on the same theme. Candidates process information by selecting and combining data from spoken and written sources to complete listening and writing tasks in a practical work or study situation. At least one writing task requires an extended piece of writing (100-200 words).

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Paper 3 weight**: 30% of the subject mark (Part A 15% + Part B 15%).
- **Duration**: about 2 hours.
- **Structure**: Part A compulsory; Part B choice between B1 (Level 4 max) and B2 (full range).
- **Task types**: listening tasks (comprehension, note completion, multiple-choice, matching); integrated listening, reading and writing tasks based on a single theme.
- **Writing tasks**: at least one extended piece of writing (100-200 words).
- **Tested abilities**: understand and interpret purpose and meaning of spoken texts; identify key details; interpret speakers' feelings, views, attitudes, intentions; understand speakers with a range of accents at moderate pace.

#### Examiner traps

- Choosing Part B1 thinking it gives a better grade — the cap at Level 4 still applies.
- Skimming the listening material before it plays — read the questions first.
- Failing to take structured notes during the integrated tasks — partial information makes the writing task hard.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Part A — Listening tasks

Part A consists of a variety of listening tasks:

- **Listening to monologues and dialogues**: comprehension questions, note completion, summary completion.
- **Identifying details**: names, dates, places, numbers.
- **Identifying views and attitudes**: interpreting the speaker's feelings, opinions and intentions.
- **Recognising language features**: identifying the function of phrases (e.g. "would you mind...?" as a polite request).

Approach:

1. Read all the questions for a recording before the recording plays.
2. Underline the key word in each question (the information you need to identify).
3. Listen actively — focus on the key word, not every word.
4. Answer immediately as you hear the answer. Don't wait — you'll miss the next one.
5. During the second listening (if available), confirm or correct your answers.

#### Part B — Integrated listening, reading and writing

Part B is the integrated section. The theme is shared across listening, reading and writing tasks. Candidates process information by selecting and combining data from spoken and written sources to complete practical work-or-study tasks.

Example tasks:

- A tourism scenario: listen to a tour guide's introduction, read a brochure, write a short itinerary for a client.
- An academic scenario: listen to a study group discussion, read a course handout, write a summary.
- A workplace scenario: listen to a meeting, read a project brief, write a short report.

The integrated tasks test the ability to:

- Identify relevant information from spoken and written sources.
- Combine information from both sources.
- Produce a short written text (100-200 words) in an appropriate genre.

#### Writing tasks in Part B

At least one task requires an extended piece of writing (100-200 words). The genre may be an email, a short report, a proposal, a notice, or a summary.

Approach:

1. Read the writing task and the audience/purpose.
2. Skim the listening and reading materials for relevant points.
3. Select the 3-5 most relevant points; do not try to include everything.
4. Draft the text: opening sentence addressing the audience, body paragraphs with the selected points, a closing sentence.
5. Revise for clarity, register, and word count.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-paper links

- **Reading (Paper 1)**: the Part B reading tasks in Paper 3 mirror the integrated tasks in Paper 1. The same level-cap logic applies.
- **Writing (Paper 2)**: the Part B writing tasks in Paper 3 use the same genre conventions as Paper 2 Part B.
- **Speaking (Paper 4)**: the listening skills in Paper 3 Part A build the language awareness needed for Paper 4.

#### Exam technique

- **Allocate time**: Part A ~30 minutes, Part B ~80 minutes (listening, reading and writing), review ~10 minutes.
- **Read questions before listening**: this is the single most effective Paper 3 technique. Read all questions for a recording before it plays.
- **Take structured notes during integrated tasks**: use a table or list format with the task headings as labels. Don't write full sentences — that wastes time.
- **Cross-reference spoken and written sources**: in integrated tasks, both sources often contain overlapping information; cross-reference to fill gaps.
- **Keep the writing task brief**: 100-200 words. Use the same PEEL pattern as Paper 2 Part A.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Part B1 is easier so I get more marks." No — Part B1 caps the level at 4. Choosing B2 gives access to the full Level 1-5** range.
- "I should write everything I hear in the integrated tasks." No — selective, structured notes are more useful than verbatim transcription.
- "The writing task is the most important part of Paper 3." It carries marks, but the listening and reading sections also contribute. Balance your effort.
