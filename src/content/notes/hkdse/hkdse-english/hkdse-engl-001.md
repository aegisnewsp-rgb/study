# Paper 1 — Reading — HKDSE English Language Notes

Paper 1 of the HKDSE English Language examination tests Reading. It is 1.5 hours long and carries 20% of the subject mark (10% for Part A and 10% for Part B). The paper uses a graded approach: Part A is compulsory for all candidates, and Part B requires a choice between Part B1 (easier, with Level 4 as the highest attainable level) and Part B2 (more difficult, with the full range of possible levels attainable). Candidates respond to a variety of written texts of different lengths and difficulty. Question types include multiple-choice items, short responses, and more extended, open-ended responses.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Paper 1 weight**: 20% of the English Language subject mark (10% Part A + 10% Part B).
- **Duration**: 1.5 hours.
- **Structure**: Part A compulsory; Part B choice between B1 (Level 4 max) and B2 (full range).
- **Question types**: multiple-choice items, short responses, open-ended extended responses.
- **Text types**: a variety of written texts of different lengths and difficulty drawn from the curriculum themes.
- **Assessment objectives tested**: understand and interpret purpose and meaning; identify main theme and key details; identify contextual meaning of words and phrases; interpret tone and mood; distinguish and evaluate views, attitudes and arguments; understand the use of language features.

#### Examiner traps

- Choosing Part B1 thinking it is the "easier route to a better grade" — Part B1 caps the attainable level at 4, while Part B2 is the only route to Level 5.
- Skimming Part A before attempting — Part A contains easy marks; complete it first.
- Running out of time on Part B by writing too much on Part A — allocate ~45 minutes to Part A and ~45 minutes to Part B.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Reading skills tested

The HKDSE Reading paper tests these skills across both Part A and Part B:

1. **Locating information** — scanning the text for a specific detail (e.g. a date, a name, a number).
2. **Understanding main ideas** — identifying the central theme or argument of a paragraph, a section, or the whole text.
3. **Inferring meaning** — drawing a conclusion that is not stated explicitly but is supported by the text.
4. **Interpreting tone and mood** — recognising the writer's attitude or the emotional atmosphere of the text.
5. **Evaluating arguments** — distinguishing strong from weak evidence, recognising bias or persuasion.
6. **Understanding vocabulary in context** — using surrounding text to determine the meaning of unfamiliar words.

#### Question types and how to approach them

**Multiple-choice items**: read the question stem first, then scan the text for the relevant section. Eliminate distractors that are mentioned in the text but do not match the question.

**Short responses**: answer in the same word as the text (no paraphrasing) and within the word limit specified. Common limits: "ONE word", "NO MORE THAN TWO words", "NO MORE THAN THREE words".

**Open-ended extended responses**: structure the answer using the PEEL pattern — Point, Evidence, Explanation, Link. Quote the text, explain the quotation, and link back to the question. Typically 80-150 words.

#### The graded approach (Part A / B1 / B2)

The graded approach is unique to HKDSE English Language and matters for university admission.

- **Part A**: compulsory for all candidates. Tests straightforward comprehension at Levels 1-4. Easier texts, multiple-choice and short-response items.
- **Part B1**: the easier section. Tests comprehension at Levels 1-4. Choosing Part B1 caps the attainable level at 4, even if a candidate performs well on Part A.
- **Part B2**: the more difficult section. Tests comprehension at Levels 4-5**. Choosing Part B2 is the only route to Level 5 and above.

**Strategy**: choose Part B1 if your reading level is solidly Level 4 (you risk losing marks on B2 that would drag the overall level down). Choose Part B2 if your reading level is Level 5 or above (B2 gives access to the full range). Do not choose B1 simply because it feels easier — the cap is the trap.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-paper links

- **Listening (Paper 3)**: the Part A/B1/B2 graded approach is mirrored on Paper 3. The same level-cap logic applies.
- **SBA**: the SBA reading/viewing programme is the venue to build the reading stamina needed for the exam; read widely and reflect in writing.
- **Writing (Paper 2)**: the texts in Paper 1 are the model for Paper 2's expected register and vocabulary range.

#### Exam technique

- **Allocate time carefully**: Part A ~45 minutes, Part B ~45 minutes. Do not spend more than 50 minutes on Part A even if it is comfortable.
- **Read the question before the text**: in Part B, the question stem often tells you what to look for in the text. Read the question first, then locate the relevant paragraph.
- **Underline key words in the text**: a quick physical mark helps you find the answer quickly and reduces re-reading time.
- **For open-ended responses, plan the structure**: write a one-sentence topic, two supporting points with evidence, and a closing sentence. This is the PEEL pattern.
- **Check word count for short responses**: an answer with one word too many is wrong. Re-read the instruction ("NO MORE THAN TWO words") before submitting.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Part B1 is easier so it gives a higher grade." No — Part B1 caps the level at 4. Choosing B2 gives access to the full Level 1-5** range.
- "Skimming is enough for Paper 1." Skimming is for gist; close reading is for specific detail questions. Use both.
- "Memorising vocabulary lists helps with reading." It helps with the contextual meaning questions but not with inference or evaluation. Read widely and reflect in writing — this builds inference skills.
