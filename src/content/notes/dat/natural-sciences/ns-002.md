# Organic Chemistry Structure and Reactivity

The Survey of the Natural Sciences is the largest section of the DAT at 100 items, and the ADA's Candidate Guide divides it into Biology (40 items), General Chemistry (30 items) and Organic Chemistry (30 items). It runs for 90 minutes inside a total administration time the ADA states as five hours and 15 minutes.

Before you revise anything: the ADA announced updated Organic Chemistry Test Specifications for 2026, and its official DAT page states that candidates planning to test from May 2026 onward should review the new specifications. The 2026 Candidate Guide contains both the pre-update and post-update specifications and directs candidates to use the version in effect on their administration date. Revising from the wrong version wastes a third of your largest section.

## The four questions organic chemistry asks

An organic chemistry item is almost always one of four types, and the type determines the method.

What is it? Identify the structure or functional group. Reactions are decided by the group, so misreading the structure loses the whole item.

What will react with this? Match reagent to group. The reagents below are the ones that appear repeatedly, and knowing them cold is worth more than knowing more reactions.

What happens? Predict the product and the mechanism type.

Which is faster, or more stable? Compare.

## Functional groups and their reactions

Know a closed set. An alcohol oxidises to a carbonyl with a mild oxidant and to a carboxylic acid with a strong one; it converts to an alkene with concentrated sulfuric acid; it reacts with sodium to release hydrogen. An aldehyde oxidises to a carboxylic acid and a ketone does not — that is the classic identification test. A carboxylic acid reacts with an alcohol under acid catalysis to give an ester and water. A primary or secondary halide undergoes substitution with hydroxide; a tertiary halide cannot, and goes for elimination instead.

That last point is the mechanism decision. Substitution needs a better leaving group or a better nucleophile; elimination needs heat and a weak base. For a tertiary halide with hydroxide in the conditions given, elimination is the product.

## Stereochemistry

Stereoisomers have the same connectivity and a different spatial arrangement. Enantiomers are non-superimposable mirror images and have identical physical properties except for how they rotate plane-polarised light. Diastereomers are stereoisomers that are not mirror images and do have different physical properties. Conformers interconvert by bond rotation and are the same molecule.

Assigning R and S requires the same three steps every time, in order: find the highest-priority group, orient the lowest priority away, then read clockwise as R and anticlockwise as S. Candidates lose these by reordering the steps, not by getting the chemistry wrong.

The carbon with four different groups is the chiral centre. A molecule with an internal plane of symmetry is achiral even if it looks asymmetric in a flat drawing, which is the meso compound case and a recurring trap.

## Spectroscopy and the rest

Infrared identifies functional groups by their characteristic absorptions — broad O–H stretch, carbonyl stretch, N–H — and it tells you which group is present, not how the molecule is arranged. Proton NMR tells you about the number and environment of hydrogen environments: chemical shift, integration, splitting. Together they will let you identify a structure from a partial formula, which is a common DAT item type.

## Common mistakes

Revising from a superseded specification. ADA updated the Organic Chemistry specifications for 2026 and directs candidates to the version in effect on their administration date.

Confusing enantiomers with diastereomers, and then reasoning about their properties with the wrong one. Diastereomers have different physical properties; enantiomers have identical ones apart from optical rotation.

Assuming a ketone and an aldehyde behave identically under oxidation. They do not, and the test is standard.

Forgetting to check the degree of substitution before choosing substitution over elimination.

Losing stereochemistry on a reaction arrow. If the question shows a stereocentre, a plain product line is usually wrong or incomplete.
