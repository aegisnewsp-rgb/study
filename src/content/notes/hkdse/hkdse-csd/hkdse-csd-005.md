# Theme 3 — Economic Globalisation and Technology — HKDSE CSD Notes

This topic covers Topics 1 and 2 of Theme 3 of the HKDSE Citizenship and Social Development curriculum. Topic 1 addresses economic globalisation — the interdependence of economies, the cooperation of international economic organisations, the development of multinational corporations, global labour and financial market integration, the development of new economies, and the impact on individuals and on Hong Kong and the country. Topic 2 addresses technological development and information literacy — artificial intelligence, big data, cloud storage of data, the development features of information technology, the relationship between information literacy and positive values, identifying false information, morals and conduct in using technologies, and observing the relevant laws.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Economic globalisation**: the increasing interdependence of national economies through trade, investment, finance, technology and labour flows; the cooperation of international economic organisations (WTO, IMF, World Bank); multinational corporations; global labour and financial markets; new economies (digital, sharing, green); the impact on individuals (consumption and employment) and on Hong Kong and the country.
- **Technological development**: artificial intelligence, big data, cloud storage of data; the development of the Internet, social networking sites, instant messaging software; information literacy and positive values; identifying false information (fake news, deepfakes, scams); morals and conduct in using technologies (privacy, intellectual property, cyberbullying); observing the relevant laws.
- **Assessment**: data-response and short-essay items on the 2-hour CSD paper.

#### Examiner traps

- Treating globalisation as inevitable — the topic requires critical analysis of both benefits and costs.
- Confusing "information literacy" with "computer literacy" — information literacy is the ability to find, evaluate and use information critically.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Economic globalisation

**Interdependence**: the cross-border flows of goods, services, capital, technology and labour have intensified since the 1990s. Trade as a share of global GDP has grown significantly; foreign direct investment (FDI) flows have grown; financial flows have grown even faster.

**International economic organisations**:

- **World Trade Organization (WTO)**: trade liberalisation, dispute settlement; the successor to GATT (1947).
- **International Monetary Fund (IMF)**: surveillance of the global economy, financial assistance to countries with balance-of-payments problems.
- **World Bank**: development finance, poverty reduction.
- **Regional bodies**: the European Union, ASEAN, RCEP (Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership), CPTPP (Comprehensive and Progressive Agreement for Trans-Pacific Partnership).

**Multinational corporations**: large firms operating across multiple countries. Examples: Apple, Toyota, Huawei, Alibaba. Their global supply chains, foreign direct investment and brand power are central to the modern global economy.

**Global labour and financial market integration**: migration of skilled labour; remittance flows (over USD 800 billion in 2023); the integration of capital markets (the 24-hour trading day, the cross-listing of companies).

**New economies**:

- **Digital economy**: e-commerce, digital services, fintech, cryptocurrency (volatile in HKDSE 2026 — covered lightly).
- **Sharing economy**: ride-sharing (Uber, Didi), accommodation (Airbnb, Tujia).
- **Green economy**: renewable energy, carbon trading, circular-economy businesses.

**Impact on individuals, Hong Kong and the country**: increased consumer choice and competition; wage pressure on low-skill workers; opportunities for high-skill workers; rising income inequality; pressure on social safety nets; environmental pressure. For Hong Kong, globalisation has reinforced its role as an international financial centre; for the country, it has supported rapid industrialisation and export-led growth.

#### Technological development and information literacy

**Artificial intelligence**: machine learning, deep learning, large language models, computer vision, robotics. AI is being deployed in finance, healthcare, transportation, manufacturing, education and government.

**Big data**: the collection, storage and analysis of large datasets to identify patterns. Applications: targeted advertising, fraud detection, epidemiology, urban planning.

**Cloud storage of data**: the remote storage of data on servers accessible via the Internet; providers include Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud, Alibaba Cloud, Tencent Cloud.

**Information technology features**: the Internet, social networking sites (Facebook, Instagram, X, WeChat, Weibo), instant messaging software (WhatsApp, Telegram, Signal), live-streaming platforms, short-video platforms (TikTok, Douyin, Kuaishou).

**Information literacy and positive values**: the ability to find, evaluate and use information critically; recognising bias, distinguishing fact from opinion, identifying false information; ethical conduct in online environments.

**Identifying false information**: recognising fake news (sensational headlines, anonymous sources, no corroboration), deepfakes (synthetic media), scams (phishing, advance-fee fraud), misinformation cascades.

**Morals and conduct in using technologies**: privacy (data protection, consent), intellectual property (copyright, plagiarism), cyberbullying, online harassment, the ethical use of AI.

**Observing the relevant laws**: data protection laws (the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance in Hong Kong, the Personal Information Protection Law in the Mainland), cybercrime laws, intellectual property laws, content regulations.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-topic links

- **Theme 2 (international affairs)**: economic globalisation is the backdrop for the country's international participation.
- **Theme 3 Topic 3 (sustainable development)**: economic globalisation and technology have environmental impacts.
- **National Security Education**: information literacy and cyber security are part of the holistic approach to national security.

#### Exam technique

- **For short-essay items on globalisation**: structure with a clear thesis (e.g. "Globalisation brings both benefits and costs"), then develop the argument with examples for both sides, then conclude with a balanced view.
- **For data-response items on technology**: identify the technology, the issue, the stakeholders, the regulatory framework, and the implications.
- **For "evaluate" items**: use specific examples (e.g. the impact of AI on a particular industry, the impact of e-commerce on small businesses).

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Globalisation means the end of national sovereignty." No — governments retain policy levers (trade policy, immigration policy, regulation). Multinational corporations operate within national jurisdictions.
- "AI is neutral." AI systems can reflect the biases of their training data. Information literacy includes recognising the limitations of AI-generated content.
- "Free trade is always good." Free trade has distributional effects — some sectors gain, others lose. The political backlash in many countries reflects these distributional effects.
