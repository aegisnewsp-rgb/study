# Data Interpretation

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your MDCAT.

Data Interpretation (DI) is the skill of reading values from **bar graphs, pie charts, line graphs, tables, and caselets**, then performing arithmetic to answer what-if questions. MDCAT places 1–2 DI items in the Logical Reasoning block, worth roughly **4%** of the paper. Master four operations: **percentage change, ratio, average, and pie-angle conversion**.

> **Mnemonic:** "Read the legend, then read the axis, then do the math."

| Operation | Formula (with units) |
|---|---|
| Percentage change | ((New − Old) / Old) × 100, both in same units |
| Pie value | (Angle / 360°) × Total |
| Average | Sum of observations ÷ Count (count dimensionless) |
| Ratio | A : B, both in same units |

Speed comes from spotting whether the question wants a **comparison, a change, or a share** before touching numbers.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### How MDCAT tests DI

MDCAT frames DI as short numerical MCQs. The PMC syllabus lists it under logical reasoning at **4% weightage**, usually appearing as a single set with 1–2 linked questions on a shared chart. The visual is supplied inside the test booklet; arithmetic must be done without a calculator, so rounding and estimation are scored skills.

#### Reading the stimulus correctly

Before computing anything, identify the **chart type, axis labels, legend, and unit (thousands, millions, lakhs, %).** Bar and line graphs encode values via bar height or plotted point; pie charts encode share via central angle. A 90° slice equals exactly one-quarter of the total, a 72° slice equals one-fifth, and so on — memorising these five anchor angles removes the need for division.

#### The four core calculations

1. **Percentage change** between two periods uses ((New − Old) / Old) × 100. A rise from 80 to 100 is +25%, not +20 percentage points.
2. **Ratios** must be reduced to a common base before comparison. A 3:5 ratio of boys to girls (40% boys) differs from a 3:5 ratio of girls to boys.
3. **Averages** from grouped tables require summing the column and dividing by the number of rows, never by the number of categories.
4. **Caselet arithmetic** chains multiple steps — record every intermediate result to the same decimal place to avoid cascading error.

#### Common traps

- Confusing **percentage points with percent change** (the PMC item bank repeats this distractor).
- Misreading **non-zero baselines** on bar charts.
- Adding values expressed in **different units** (₨ in thousands vs. millions).

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge cases that separate top scorers

When a chart mixes **stacked and grouped bars**, isolate the relevant segment before computing percentage share. **Compound annual growth rate (CAGR)** appears in occasional DI sets: ((End / Start)^(1/n) − 1) × 100, where *n* is the number of compounding periods (years here), dimensionless. **Weighted averages** — where categories have different frequencies — demand a sum-of-products divided by sum-of-weights, not a simple mean of the percentages.

#### Adjacent-topic links

DI overlaps with **percentage, ratio–proportion, and profit–loss** chapters of MDCAT mathematics. A caselet about revenue and cost effectively becomes a profit-percentage problem once the table is decoded. Linking these topics reduces the formulas you must memorise separately.

#### Worked micro-example

A pie chart shows company spending: Salaries 144°, Rent 72°, Supplies 108°, Misc 36° (total 360°). Total spend = ₨ 5,00,000.
Supplies value = (108/360) × 5,00,000 = **₨ 1,50,000**.
Percentage spent on Rent = (72/360) × 100 = **20%**.
If rent rises 25% next year, new rent = 1.25 × (72/360 × 5,00,000) = **₨ 1,25,000**.

#### Practice prompts

1. A table lists yearly admissions: 2021 → 400, 2022 → 460, 2023 → 506. Find the CAGR and the percentage change from 2021 to 2023.
2. A bar graph shows boys 180, girls 270 in three colleges. Compute the boy-to-girl ratio and the percentage of total students who are girls.

#### Mistake log to keep

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Treating 144° as 14.4% | Convert angle ÷ 360, not ÷ 1000 |
| Averaging percentages | Re-weight by category size |
| Skipping units in tables | Write unit next to every intermediate total |
