# Multiple Choice (Reading) — IELTS Reading Notes

Multiple choice is one of the official IELTS Academic Reading question types. Each question has a stem (a partial sentence or question) and four options (A-D) or five options (A-E) for sentence-completion multiple choice. The question tests understanding of main idea, detail, inference, and opinion.

> Verify the live Reading format on https://ielts.org/ before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Format:** choose one of four options (A-D) for a question, or one of five options (A-E) for sentence-completion.
- **Skills tested:** main idea, detail, inference, opinion.
- **Stem types:** partial sentences ("The author mentions X because..."), questions ("What is the main idea of paragraph 3?"), or sentence-completion ("The author believes that...").
- **Distractors:** the passage often mentions the wrong options explicitly; the question asks for the correct one.
- **Order of answers:** questions follow the passage in order.

#### Examiner traps

- Choosing the option that appears in the passage rather than the one that fully answers the question.
- Confusing "the author says" (factual) with "the author implies" (inference).
- Choosing the first plausible option instead of reading all options carefully.

---

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Strategy

1. Read the question stem carefully — identify the keyword.
2. Scan the passage for the keyword or related terms.
3. Read the surrounding sentence or paragraph carefully.
4. Match the relevant passage text against each option.
5. Eliminate options that are mentioned in the passage but do not answer the question.
6. Choose the option that best fits.

#### Main idea questions

The main idea is usually stated in the first sentence of the relevant paragraph or the passage's introduction. Look for a topic sentence that introduces the paragraph's theme.

#### Detail questions

Detail questions ask for a specific fact, name, date, or example. Scan the passage for the keyword and read the surrounding sentence.

#### Inference questions

Inference questions ask what the author implies rather than what they state directly. The correct option is the one that is consistent with the passage but goes beyond the explicit text.

#### Opinion questions

Opinion questions ask for the author's view or attitude. Look for evaluative language ("unfortunately," "remarkably," "I believe") and the author's tone.

---

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where multiple choice appears across IELTS Reading

Multiple choice appears in all three passages, usually 3-5 questions per passage. It is a high-frequency question type and a strong band discriminator.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "All options are in the passage." Usually all four are mentioned; the question asks for the correct one.
- "The longest option is correct." No — the correct option is the most accurate, not the longest.
- "Paraphrasing is required." No — the option may paraphrase the passage, but the meaning is the same.

#### Exam technique

- For main-idea questions, look at the first sentence of each paragraph; the topic sentence is usually the main idea.
- For detail questions, scan for the keyword in the question stem.
- For inference questions, eliminate options that are too literal or that contradict the passage.
- For opinion questions, look for evaluative language and the author's tone.
