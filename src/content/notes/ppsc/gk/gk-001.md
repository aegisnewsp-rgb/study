# Punjab Province and Its Administration

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> One fact every PPSC candidate must internalise before walking into the hall: Pakistan's largest province by population runs on a five-tier local hierarchy, and the Governor is NOT the real executive.

- **Population rank:** Punjab is Pakistan's most populous province, headquartered in **Lahore**.
- **Real executive head:** **Chief Minister of Punjab**, appointed under **Article 130** of the Constitution, commands a cabinet collectively responsible to the **Provincial Assembly of the Punjab**.
- **Ceremonial head:** **Governor of Punjab**, appointed by the President under **Article 101**, is the nominal head who assents to bills.
- **Top bureaucrat:** **Chief Secretary** heads the **Punjab Civil Secretariat** and is the principal accounting officer of the province.
- **Administrative pyramid (top-down):** **Province → Division → District → Tehsil → Union Council**.
- **Recruitment body:** **Punjab Public Service Commission (PPSC)** conducts competitive exams for provincial civil service posts under **Article 192**.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook — "GCD-DT-U":** Governor (rubber stamp) → **C**M (real boss) → **D**ivisional Commissioner → **D**eputy Commissioner → **T**ehsildar → **U**nion Council. Walk it top-down to remember who supervises whom.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

> The 18th Amendment changed everything for the provinces — many subjects once federally controlled now sit with the Punjab Government through its own Cabinet and Secretariat.

#### Constitutional Framework

The province operates under **Articles 1–288** of the Constitution of Pakistan, with three articles doing most of the exam work:

- **Article 101** — appointment of the Governor by the President on the advice of the Chief Minister of the province.
- **Article 106 / 106A** — composition of the Provincial Assembly: general seats (delimitation-based) plus reserved seats for **women** and **non-Muslim minorities**.
- **Article 130** — executive authority of the Province vests in the **Chief Minister**, who is the head of the **Provincial Cabinet**. Cabinet sits on the principle of **collective responsibility** to the Assembly (Article 130(5)).
- **Article 139** — Provincial Public Service Commission, operationalised in Punjab as **PPSC**.
- **Article 192** — qualifications, appointment, and terms of PPSC members.

#### The Two Apex Functionaries

| Functionary | Who appoints | Legal basis | Real power |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Governor** | President on CM's advice | Article 101 | Ceremonial — assent, summoning, proroguing Assembly |
| **Chief Minister** | Majority party in Assembly | Article 130 | Real executive — runs cabinet, controls policy, budget |
| **Chief Secretary** | PM on CM's recommendation via Establishment Division | Rules of Business 1974 | Top civil servant, principal accounting officer of the province |

#### Field Administration: Divisions → Districts → Tehsils

A **Divisional Commissioner** supervises the districts of a Division. Each district is headed by a **Deputy Commissioner (DC)** who combines three roles: **revenue collector** (Land Revenue Act 1967), **district magistrate** (CrPC), and **chief coordinator** of all federal and provincial departments within the district. The **Assistant Commissioner (AC)** assists the DC at sub-divisional level. A **Tehsildar** heads a Tehsil for revenue and land records, while the **Sub-Registrar** handles property registration under the Registration Act 1908.

#### Local Government Act 2013

Punjab's current local government system, established by the **Local Government Act 2013**, is a three-tier structure: **District Council** (district level), **Tehsil Council / Tehsil Municipal Administration (TMA)** (tehsil level), and **Union Council** (village/neighbourhood level). Mayors and chairpersons are elected directly. The DC remains the chief coordinator even under this devolved setup.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix

| Often confused | Correct distinction |
|---|---|
| **Governor vs CM** | Governor = nominal/constitutional head; CM = real executive with cabinet support |
| **Division vs District** | Division = administrative convenience grouping districts; District = formal statutory unit under provincial law |
| **DC vs District Magistrate** | DC = administrative + revenue + magisterial; District Magistrate = judicial-only role under CrPC, separated in the 2008 magistracy reforms |
| **Provincial Cabinet vs Cabinet Committee** | Cabinet = all ministers, bound by collective responsibility; Cabinet Committee = smaller group for a specific function |
| **PPSC vs FPSC** | PPSC = provincial civil service posts in Punjab; FPSC = federal civil service posts |
| **Federal Secretariat vs Punjab Civil Secretariat** | Federal sits in Islamabad; Punjab Secretariat is in Lahore |

#### Worked Problem: Identifying the Constitutional Head

A PPSC-style MCQ reads: *"Under the Constitution of Pakistan, who is the head of the Provincial Government of Punjab?"* Options: (A) Governor (B) Chief Minister (C) Chief Secretary (D) Speaker of the Assembly.

**Step 1.** Read Article 130 carefully — *"The executive authority of the Province shall vest in the Chief Minister."*
**Step 2.** The **Governor** is appointed under Article 101 as the constitutional head of province in the same way the President is for Pakistan — but actual executive power lies with the CM.
**Step 3.** The **Chief Secretary** is a civil servant, never the executive head.
**Step 4.** The **Speaker** presides over the Assembly, not the Government.

#### Correct answer: (B) Chief Minister.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students who pick "Governor" forget that after the 18th Amendment, real executive power in every province sits with the CM, not the ceremonial Governor. This is the single most-tested misconception in PPSC General Knowledge.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

> The hard part of this topic is not memorising titles — it is keeping straight which authority sits where, and remembering that Punjab's number of districts and divisions is a moving target that the home department notifies from time to time.

#### Boundary Cases and Advanced Traps

1. **Collective responsibility of the Cabinet.** A vote of no-confidence against the CM in the Assembly brings down the entire cabinet, not just the CM. Individual ministers can be dropped or reshuffled, but the cabinet as a body stands or falls together (Article 130(5)).
2. **Reserved seats arithmetic.** Punjab's Assembly combines general seats (population/delimitations-based) with reserved seats for women and minorities under Articles 106–106A. Reserved seats are allocated on the basis of general seats won by each party — a candidate who can read party-list arithmetic scores a guaranteed mark.
3. **Divisional Commissioner's ambiguous status.** The Commissioner is **not** a constitutional post. The position exists because of executive directions and the **Punjab Government Rules of Business 1974**, and can be reorganised by the provincial government without a constitutional amendment.
4. **Chief Secretary's appointment loop.** CS is **not** appointed directly by the CM. The recommendation travels: CM → Establishment Division (federal) → PM → notification. This is the loop students most often get wrong in MCQs.
5. **DC's continuing coordination role.** Even after the Local Government Act 2013 created elected District Councils, the DC retained **coordination** responsibilities across federal, provincial, and local tiers. Confusing "coordination" with "control" is a frequent answer-killer.
6. **Article 220 and local government.** Article 220 allows the province to constitute local bodies — but the structure itself (three tiers, election cycle, mayoral system) comes from the **provincial** statute (LGA 2013), not the Constitution. Federal changes do not automatically alter it.

#### Adjacent Topics Worth Linking

- **18th Amendment (2010)** — what moved from the Concurrent List to the provinces, and why Punjab gained legislative muscle in education, health, and policing.
- **Land Revenue Act 1967** — explains why the DC still collects land revenue and supervises the **patwari** and **kanungo** chain.
- **PPSC's own jurisdiction** — article **192** recruitment vs the FPSC's federal counterpart; same constitutional logic, separate operational bodies.
- **Provincial Finance Department** — prepares the Annual Budget Statement; CM presents it; Assembly passes the **Appropriation Act**.

#### Two Advanced Practice Prompts

1. **Tracing authority down the hierarchy.** A flood relief consignment arrives at a district. List, in order, every official who can lawfully divert or release it: CM → Chief Secretary → Divisional Commissioner → DC → AC → Tehsildar → Union Council Nazim. Now identify at which link the link becomes *elected* (not bureaucratic).
2. **Constitutional amendment impact.** Read the text of the **18th Amendment** (summary acceptable) and explain how Articles 97, 130, and 142 collectively shifted fiscal and legislative power toward the Punjab Government. Note which subjects moved and which stayed on the Federal List.

#### Common Mistakes in the Hall

- Writing "President governs the province" — the President appoints the Governor; he does not govern the province directly.
- Calling the Provincial Assembly the "Parliament of Punjab" — Parliament is the federal legislature; the correct name is **Provincial Assembly of the Punjab**.
- Treating the **Provincial Cabinet** and the **Provincial Cabinet Committee** as interchangeable bodies.
- Listing the district count from memory without checking the latest Punjab home department notification — district numbers have shifted over the years, and an outdated figure costs the mark.
