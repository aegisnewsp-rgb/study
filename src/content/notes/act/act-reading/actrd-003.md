# Craft and Structure: Point of View and Rhetorical Purpose — ACT Reading Notes

Point of view questions assess the author's attitude, tone, narrative voice, and rhetorical motivation. In the official ACT examination, this topic carries a weight rating of 4 out of 5 and appears consistently across testing administrations. Candidates preparing for high percentiles must master both theoretical fundamentals and rapid practical problem-solving strategies.

#### Conceptual framework and solving methodology

To secure consistent accuracy on Craft and Structure: Point of View and Rhetorical Purpose, candidates should follow a structured three-step execution framework:

1. **Identify What the Item Actually Asks**:
   Begin by identifying the exact question asked. For point of view, mark separate who is speaking (narrator, a named character, or the author) from what that speaker wants the reader to accept.

2. **Execute Systematic Solution Protocol**:
   Apply official methodology rather than intuition. Examine adjectives and tone-bearing verbs to determine whether the author is admiring, skeptical, neutral, or critical. Mark your evidence by tagging pronoun shifts, signal verbs of saying, and stated reasons the voice gives.

### 🔴 Deep Dive — Mastery & Edge Cases (3mo–2yr)

#### Advanced variations and high-difficulty edge cases

When targeting top-decile scores, students must prepare for non-standard variations:

- **Perspective Signals**: Track first-person pronouns, chosen details, and attitude to determine whose interests frame the passage and what that framing emphasizes or suppresses in the presentation.
- **Purpose Tagging**: Summarize the opening, development, and ending, then tag the passage as explanation, persuasion, narration, reflection, or mixed purpose in one precise phrase.
- **Viewpoint Contrast**: Compare how two speakers describe the same event, highlighting distinctive wording that reveals viewpoint rather than repeating shared facts for each speaker.
