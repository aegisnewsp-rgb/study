# Pronouns and Antecedent Agreement

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

A **pronoun** is a word that replaces a noun; the **antecedent** is the noun the pronoun refers back to. The agreement rule states that a pronoun must match its antecedent in three areas: **number** (singular/plural), **person** (first/second/third), and **gender** (masculine/feminine/neuter). Indefinite pronouns such as *each, everyone, somebody, neither, anyone* are grammatically singular and take singular pronouns. Collective nouns (*committee, team, family*) take a singular pronoun when the group acts as a unit and a plural pronoun when members act individually. Relative pronouns are restricted: use **who/whom** for people, **which** for things, and **that** for either. Keep the antecedent clear and close to its pronoun to avoid ambiguous reference.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Number Agreement
The most frequently tested dimension in NABTEB is number. A singular antecedent requires a singular pronoun, and a plural antecedent requires a plural pronoun. Singular antecedents include count nouns modified by *a, an, the, each, every, either, neither*, and indefinite pronouns such as *anybody, somebody, nobody, everyone, someone, anything, everything, nothing, one*. Compare:

- *Each student submitted **his or her** script.* ✅
- *The boys raised **their** hands.* ✅

#### Person Agreement
A pronoun must remain in the same person as its antecedent. Shifting from third person to second person is a common error:

- *A driver must renew **your** licence.* ❌ (antecedent = *a driver*, third person)
- *A driver must renew **his or her** licence.* ✅

#### Gender Agreement
Pronouns agree with the biological or grammatical gender of the antecedent: *he/him/his* (masculine), *she/her/hers* (feminine), *it/its* (neuter). When gender is unknown, traditional formal English uses *his or her*; modern usage often permits the singular *they*.

#### Collective Nouns
Decide by function: a unit acting together → singular; members acting separately → plural.

| Collective as Unit | Collective as Individuals |
|---|---|
| The jury **has** reached **its** verdict. | The jury **have** cast **their** votes. |
| The team **is** confident in **its** coach. | The team **are** returning to **their** homes. |

#### Relative Pronouns
- **Who / whom** — persons (*The girl who won*).
- **Which** — things and animals (*The car which he bought*).
- **That** — restrictive clauses referring to either persons or things (*The man that came, the book that I read*).

NABTEB tests this topic through: (a) error-identification items where one underlined word or phrase is wrong, (b) sentence-re-write tasks using a given opening, and (c) multiple-choice options where candidates choose the pronoun that correctly fills a gap. Mark allocations are usually 1 mark per item.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Ambiguous and Remote Antecedents
A pronoun must point unmistakably to one noun. Two classic traps:

1. *When Chidi met Emeka, **he** was late.* — Who is late? Chidi or Emeka?
2. *The car hit the bus, and **it** was badly damaged.* — Which vehicle?

Rephrase to remove the ambiguity: *The car hit the bus, and the bus was badly damaged.* In longer essays, keep the pronoun within a few words of its antecedent; if distance is unavoidable, repeat the noun.

#### Case Errors That Mimic Agreement Errors
NABTEB sometimes disguises a case problem inside an agreement item.

- *Between you and **I**, the answer is wrong.* ❌ (objective case needed)
- *Between you and **me**, the answer is wrong.* ✅

Always identify the grammatical role (subject, object, possessive) before choosing the form.

#### Worked Micro-Example
**Item:** *Everyone must bring _________ own pen.*
Options: A) their B) his or her C) our D) their own
**Solution:** *Everyone* is a singular indefinite pronoun. The strictly correct answer is **B) his or her**, although A) is widely accepted in contemporary informal writing. In NABTEB, option B is the expected formal answer.

#### Common NABTEB Traps
- Treating *each, either, neither* as plural (they are singular).
- Using *which* for people or *who* for things.
- Using plural pronouns with *mathematics, physics, news* (these take singular verbs and singular pronouns).
- Forgetting that *each other* refers to two parties while *one another* refers to more than two.

#### Two Practice Prompts
1. Rewrite to correct the error: *Neither of the candidates submitted their form on time.*
2. Choose the correct option: *The committee has released ______ report.* A) its B) their C) it's

This topic carries roughly 3% of NABTEB English marks, translating to one or two direct questions per paper. Master the seven singular indefinite pronouns (*each, every, either, neither, anyone, everyone, somebody*), the collective-noun rule, and the who/which/that distinction — these three sub-rules account for nearly all items that appear.
