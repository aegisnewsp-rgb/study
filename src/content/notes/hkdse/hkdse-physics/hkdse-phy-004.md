# IV. Electricity and Magnetism — HKDSE Physics Notes

This topic covers Topic IV of the Compulsory Part of HKDSE Physics. It addresses electrostatics (electric charge, Coulomb's law, electric field, electric potential, capacitors); circuits and domestic electricity (Ohm's law, series and parallel circuits, electrical energy and power, AC circuits, the domestic ring main, fuses and circuit breakers); and electromagnetism (magnetic fields, the magnetic effect of a current, the force on a current-carrying conductor, electromagnetic induction, transformers, motors and generators).

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Electric charge**: measured in coulombs (C). Charge is quantised (elementary charge e ≈ 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C). Like charges repel; unlike charges attract. Charge is conserved.
- **Coulomb's law**: F = kq₁q₂/r² (electrostatic force between two point charges is proportional to the product of the charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance). k = 1/(4πε₀) ≈ 9 × 10⁹ N·m²/C².
- **Electric field**: the force per unit positive charge at a point. E = F/q. Units: N/C or V/m.
- **Electric potential**: the work done per unit positive charge in bringing a small positive test charge from infinity to the point. V = W/q. Units: V (volts).
- **Capacitor**: stores electric charge and energy. Capacitance C = Q/V. Units: F (farads). Energy stored = ½CV².
- **Ohm's law**: V = IR for ohmic conductors. Resistance R depends on the conductor.
- **Series and parallel circuits**:
  - Series: same current, voltages add, R_total = R₁ + R₂ + R₃.
  - Parallel: same voltage, currents add, 1/R_total = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃.
- **Electrical energy and power**: P = IV = I²R = V²/R. Energy: E = Pt.
- **AC vs. DC**: AC alternates direction; DC flows in one direction. Domestic mains is AC.
- **Domestic ring main**: a ring circuit with parallel branches, fused plugs, circuit breaker at the consumer unit, and the live/earth/neutral wires.
- **Magnetic field**: produced by magnets and by electric currents. Lines of force go from N to S outside the magnet; S to N inside.
- **Magnetic force on a current**: F = BIL (force on a straight conductor of length L in a magnetic field B, perpendicular to field). Direction by Fleming's left-hand rule.
- **Electromagnetic induction**: a changing magnetic flux through a coil induces an EMF (Faraday's law). The induced EMF opposes the change (Lenz's law).
- **Transformer**: two coils on an iron core. AC in the primary induces AC in the secondary. V₁/V₂ = N₁/N₂ (ideal transformer).

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing series and parallel formulas — series resistances add; parallel conductances add.
- Forgetting to use peak vs. RMS values in AC calculations.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Electrostatics

**Charging**: by friction (triboelectric), by conduction (contact with a charged object), by induction (bringing a charged object near without contact).

**Coulomb's law**: F = kq₁q₂/r² (in vacuum or air; modified in materials). Vector force: repulsive for like charges, attractive for unlike.

**Electric field**: a region where a charged particle experiences a force. Field lines go from positive to negative charges. The field of a point charge q at distance r: E = kq/r².

**Electric potential**: the potential energy per unit charge. ΔV = -∫E·dl (work done per unit charge against the field). For a point charge q at distance r: V = kq/r.

**Capacitors**: a capacitor stores electric charge on two conductors separated by an insulator (dielectric). Capacitance C = Q/V.

- For a parallel-plate capacitor: C = ε₀ε_r·A/d (ε_r is the relative permittivity of the dielectric; A is the plate area; d is the plate separation).
- Energy stored: U = ½CV² = ½QV = Q²/(2C).
- Capacitors in parallel: C_total = C₁ + C₂ + ...
- Capacitors in series: 1/C_total = 1/C₁ + 1/C₂ + ...

#### DC circuits

**Ohm's law**: V = IR. R is constant for ohmic materials at constant temperature.

**Series and parallel**:

- Series: same current; voltages add; R_total = R₁ + R₂ + ...; for capacitors, charges are equal; 1/C_total = 1/C₁ + 1/C₂ + ...
- Parallel: same voltage; currents add; 1/R_total = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + ...; for capacitors, voltages are equal; C_total = C₁ + C₂ + ...

**Kirchhoff's laws**:

- Current law (KCL): sum of currents into a node = sum of currents out.
- Voltage law (KVL): sum of EMFs around a closed loop = sum of IR drops.

**Electrical energy and power**: P = IV = I²R = V²/R. Energy = ∫P dt.

#### AC circuits

**RMS (root-mean-square) values**: for an AC voltage varying sinusoidally, the RMS value gives the equivalent DC voltage for the same power dissipation. V_rms = V_peak/√2.

**Domestic ring main**: live, neutral and earth wires; parallel branches; fuse and circuit breaker for protection.

#### Electromagnetism

**Magnetic field of a current**: a straight wire produces concentric circles around it; a solenoid produces a uniform field inside.

**Magnetic force**: F = BIL sin θ (force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field). For a moving charge: F = qvB sin θ.

**Electromagnetic induction** (Faraday's law): EMF = -dΦ/dt, where Φ = BA cos θ is the magnetic flux. The negative sign indicates the direction of the induced EMF opposes the change in flux (Lenz's law).

**Transformers**: step-up (V₂ > V₁), step-down (V₂ < V₁). For an ideal transformer: V₁/V₂ = N₁/N₂ = I₂/I₁. Real transformers have losses: copper losses (resistance of windings), iron losses (hysteresis, eddy currents), and stray losses.

**Motors**: convert electrical energy to mechanical energy. DC motors use commutators; brushless motors use electronic commutation.

**Generators**: convert mechanical energy to electrical energy. AC generators (alternators) produce sinusoidal EMF; DC generators use commutators.

#### Exam technique

- **For electrostatics**: state Coulomb's law explicitly; identify charges and signs.
- **For DC circuits**: apply Kirchhoff's laws to find currents and voltages.
- **For AC circuits**: convert between peak and RMS values; distinguish the two.
- **For electromagnetic induction**: state Faraday's and Lenz's laws; identify what changes.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Current flows from one terminal to another in a battery." In a battery, conventional current flows from + to - externally and from - to + internally.
- "AC is safer than DC." High-voltage AC and DC are both dangerous; AC at 50-60 Hz is particularly dangerous because it can cause sustained muscle contraction.
- "The Earth wire carries current under normal conditions." No — the Earth wire is a safety wire; it carries current only in a fault condition.
