# The Companies Act, 2013

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The **Companies Act, 2013** (Act No. 18 of 2013) is the principal Indian statute governing incorporation, regulation, and winding up of companies. It replaced the Companies Act, 1956 and applies to the whole of India. Under **Section 2(20)**, a company means a company incorporated under this Act or under any earlier company law.

#### Must-Know Statutory Thresholds

| Concept | Section | Threshold / Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Company definition | 2(20) | Incorporated under the Act or prior law |
| Private company minimum members | 2(68) | 2 (maximum 200) |
| Public company minimum members | 2(71) | 7 |
| One Person Company (OPC) | 2(62) | Single member, private |
| Small company | 2(85) | Paid-up capital ≤ ₹5 crore **AND** turnover ≤ ₹50 crore |
| Board meetings (public co.) | 173 | Minimum 4 per year, max 120-day gap |
| AGM | 96 | Within 6 months from close of financial year |

- **Separate Legal Entity**, **Limited Liability**, **Perpetual Succession**, **Common Seal**, **Transferability of Shares** are the six cardinal features of a company.
- **Memorandum of Association (MOA)** contains six clauses; **Articles of Association (AOA)** governs internal management.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** "**PSLPTC**" for company features — **P**erpetual succession, **S**eparate legal entity, **L**imited liability, **P**roperty separate, **T**ransferable shares, **C**ommon seal. For MOA clauses, recall "**N-R-O-L-C-A**" — Name, Registered office, Object, Liability, Capital, Association (subscription).

#### Types of Companies at a Glance

| Type | Section | Member Range | Distinctive Feature |
|---|---|---|---|
| Private | 2(68) | Min 2, max 200 | Restricts share transfer; no public invitation |
| Public | 2(71) | Min 7, no max | Can invite public to subscribe shares |
| One Person Company | 2(62) | Exactly 1 | Private company with single member |
| Small | 2(85) | Same as Pvt/Public | Paid-up cap ₹5 cr AND turnover cap ₹50 cr |
| Section 8 | 8 | Min 2 (Pvt) / 7 (Public) | Not-for-profit, promotes charity/sports/science |
| Nidhi | 406 + Nidhi Rules | Min 200 | Mutual benefit, lending to members only |
| Producer | Producer Co. Rules | Min 10 / 2 institutions | Agriculture producer body |
| Dormant | 455 | As per type | Inactive company, "DSCV" suffix |
| Foreign | 2(42) | — | Incorporated outside India, place of business in India |

#### Memorandum of Association — Six Clauses

The **MOA** is the company's charter defining its constitution. Its six clauses under Table A are:

1. **Name Clause** — with "Limited" or "Private Limited" suffix per type.
2. **Registered Office Clause** — state in which the office is situated.
3. **Object Clause** — main objects, ancillary objects, and other objects (sub-divided since 2014).
4. **Liability Clause** — limited by shares or by guarantee; unlimited also permitted.
5. **Capital Clause** — amount of share capital with division into shares of fixed value.
6. **Association/Subscription Clause** — subscribers' agreement to form the company.

A company acts through agents; acts beyond the MOA are **ultra vires** and void even against third parties.

#### Doctrines Linked to MOA and AOA

- **Doctrine of Ultra Vires** — acts beyond objects clause are void; cannot be ratified even by unanimous resolution.
- **Doctrine of Constructive Notice** — MOA and AOA are public documents; everyone is deemed to know them.
- **Indoor Management (Turquand's Rule)** — outsiders may assume internal procedures were followed, except in cases of **forgery** and **knowledge irregularities**.
- **Lifting the Corporate Veil** — though a company is a separate legal entity, courts may look behind it in cases of fraud, tax evasion, group enterprise oppression, Benami holdings, or national interest (ss. 2(87)–2(89)).

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** A private limited company has 180 members and a paid-up share capital of ₹4 crore with a turnover of ₹45 crore in the previous financial year. Is the company eligible to be classified as a "Small Company" under the Companies Act, 2013?

(A) Yes, because it has fewer than 200 members.
(B) Yes, because both thresholds are within the statutory limits.
(C) No, because a private company with 180 members cannot be a small company.
(D) No, because a private company by definition is not a small company.

#### Solution:

- **Step 1 — Apply Section 2(85).** A small company means a company (other than a public company, Section 8 company, holding/subsidiary of foreign company, or Section 25 company of 1956 Act) whose paid-up capital does not exceed **₹5 crore** and whose turnover does not exceed **₹50 crore**.
- **Step 2 — Test capital threshold.** Paid-up capital = ₹4 crore ≤ ₹5 crore ✅
- **Step 3 — Test turnover threshold.** Turnover = ₹45 crore ≤ ₹50 crore ✅
- **Step 4 — Verify type eligibility.** Private companies qualify; public, Section 8, and certain holding/subsidiary companies do not ✅
- **Step 5 — Conclusion.** All conditions are satisfied → the company **is a small company**.

#### Correct Answer: (B)

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Option (A) is the typical wrong answer. Membership count (200 limit) is irrelevant to small-company classification; the test is purely on **paid-up capital AND turnover thresholds**, both of which must be satisfied **simultaneously** — satisfying one alone is insufficient.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Corporate Veil vs. Other Doctrines

| Doctrine | Trigger | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Lifting Corporate Veil | Fraud, evasion, group oppression | Liability extended to members/directors |
| Piercing Corporate Veil | Same as above (US term) | Often used interchangeably in India |
| Ultra Vires | Act beyond MOA objects | Absolute nullity, no ratification |
| Indoor Management | Defect in internal procedure | Outward bound acts protected |
| Alteration of MOA | Special resolution + Central Govt approval in some cases | Modifies charter within limits |

#### Membership, Directors and Meetings — Critical Thresholds

- **Members and Directors:** Private co. — min 2 directors (Sec 149). Public co. — min 3 directors, of which at least one **Woman Director** and one **Resident Director**. Every director must obtain a **Director Identification Number (DIN)** under Section 153.
- **First AGM** must be held within **9 months** from the close of the first financial year. Subsequent AGMs must be held within **6 months** from the close of the relevant financial year, with a maximum gap of **15 months** between two AGMs.
- **Board Meetings** for public companies — minimum **4 per year**, with no gap exceeding **120 days** between two meetings. OPC, Section 8, and small companies have relaxed norms.
- **Auditor Appointment:** First auditor by Board within 30 days; reappointment via ratification by members; CAG auditor for government companies.

#### Winding Up and NCLT

Winding up under the 2013 Act is overseen by the **National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT)** under Sections 271–274. Modes include:

1. **Petition by contributors, company, registrar, or Central/State Government** on grounds like inability to pay debts, oppression, or just and equitable winding up.
2. **Voluntary winding up** under Sections 304–323 — by special resolution or Board resolution if the company has no debts or has fully paid debts.
3. **Fast-track exit mode** for defunct companies under Section 248 (Strike Off).

The **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC)** interlinks with the Companies Act, 2013 for corporate insolvency resolution — the NCLT admits petitions under IBC Section 7, 9, or 10, and the CIRP is supervised by Interim Resolution Professionals.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. **Distinguish** between lifting the corporate veil under Sections 2(87)–2(89) versus the doctrine of constructive notice. When may a court ignore the separate legal entity of a Section 8 company engaged in fraudulent activity?
2. **Analyze** whether an OPC, after a member's death, can continue under a nominee structure for **beyond 6 months** under Rule 6 of the Companies (Incorporation) Rules, 2014. What is the consequence if no successor is registered within that window?
