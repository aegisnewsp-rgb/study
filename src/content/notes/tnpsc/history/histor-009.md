# Medieval India and South Indian Polities

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

> Core chronologies, administrative terminology, and South Indian medieval dynasties for rapid TNPSC Group 1 revision.

The medieval period in Indian history spans from the rise of regional polities in the 8th century CE to the decline of the Mughal Empire in the mid-18th century. For TNPSC aspirants, South Indian developments (Cholas, Pandyas, Vijayanagara, Nayaks, and Thanjavur Marathas) carry equal weight with northern imperial centers.

**High-Yield Chronological Anchors:**
1. **Delhi Sultanate Dynasties (1206–1526 CE):**
   - Mamluk / Slave Dynasty (1206–1290 CE): Qutb-ud-din Aibak, Iltutmish (Turkan-i-Chahalgani, Iqta system, silver Tanka and copper Jital).
   - Khalji Dynasty (1290–1320 CE): Alauddin Khalji (Dagh and Chehra branding, market control under Shahna-i-Mandi, southern raids by Malik Kafur reaching Madurai).
   - Tughlaq Dynasty (1320–1414 CE): Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq, Muhammad bin Tughlaq (capital transfer to Daulatabad, token copper currency, Diwan-i-Kohi agricultural department), Firuz Shah Tughlaq (Diwan-i-Khairat, canals, Jizya on Brahmins).
   - Sayyid Dynasty (1414–1451 CE): Founded by Khizr Khan following Timur's invasion (1398 CE).
   - Lodi Dynasty (1451–1526 CE): Bahlul Lodi, Sikandar Lodi (founded Agra, Gaz-i-Sikandari), Ibrahim Lodi (defeated by Babur at First Battle of Panipat, 1526 CE).

2. **Imperial Cholas of Thanjavur (850–1279 CE):**
   - Vijayalaya Chola (founder, captured Thanjavur from Muttaraiyars).
   - Parantaka I (issued historic **Uttaramerur Inscriptions** detailing autonomous village administration and Kudavolai system).
   - Rajaraja I (985–1014 CE): Built **Brihadisvara Temple** at Thanjavur (1010 CE), maritime expedition to northern Sri Lanka (Anuradhapura), comprehensive land revenue survey (*Kani-kadan*).
   - Rajendra I (1014–1044 CE): Assumed title *Gangaikonda Chola*, built Gangaikonda Cholapuram, naval expedition to Srivijaya (Maritime Southeast Asia).

3. **Vijayanagara Empire (1336–1646 CE):**
   - Founded by Harihara I and Bukka Raya I under guidance of sage Vidyaranya.
   - Four Dynasties: Sangama $\rightarrow$ Saluva $\rightarrow$ Tuluva $\rightarrow$ Aravidu.
   - Krishnadevaraya (1509–1529 CE, Tuluva dynasty): Golden age of art and letters; authored *Amuktamalyada* in Telugu and *Jambavati Kalyanam* in Sanskrit; patronized Ashtadiggajas including Allasani Peddana and Tenali Ramakrishna.
   - Battle of Talikota / Rakshasi-Tangadi (1565 CE): Decisive defeat by alliance of Deccan Sultanates (Bijapur, Golconda, Ahmednagar, Bidar).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

> Administrative structures, local self-government, revenue systems, and religious transformations.

#### Chola Village Administration: The Uttaramerur Epigraphs
The two stone inscriptions of Parantaka I at Vaikunda Perumal temple in Uttaramerur (Kanchipuram district, dated 919 and 921 CE) preserve the most sophisticated local self-government framework in pre-modern Asia.
- **Village Assemblies:**
  - *Ur:* General assembly of landholders in non-Brahmadeya villages.
  - *Sabha* or *Mahasabha:* Autonomous assembly of Brahmins in *Brahmadeya* (tax-free gift) villages.
  - *Nagaram:* Assembly of merchants and traders in commercial hubs.
- **The Kudavolai (Pot-Ticket) Electoral Method:**
  - The village was partitioned into 30 wards (*Kudumbus*).
  - Eligible candidates had to meet strict qualifications: age between 35 and 70, ownership of at least a quarter *Veli* of taxable land, residence on own property, and proficiency in Vedic texts and mantras.
  - Disqualifications: Failure to submit audited accounts while serving on previous committees, moral turpitude, or relationship to corrupt former officials.
  - Palm-leaf ballots (*Olais*) bearing candidates' names were placed in an earthenware pot (*Kudam*). In the presence of the full assembly and temple priests, a young child drew out the slips to select members for executive committees (*Variyams*).
- **Major Variyams (Executive Working Committees):**
  - *Samvatsara Variyam:* Annual executive steering committee.
  - *Totta Variyam:* Garden and orchard administration.
  - *Eri Variyam:* Irrigation and water-tank maintenance committee (vital for monsoon resilience).
  - *Pon Variyam:* Gold bullion and currency standards committee.
  - *Pancha Variyam:* Standing committee for emergency relief.

#### The Nayak System of Tamil Nadu
Following the disruption of the Vijayanagara central authority at Talikota (1565 CE), the provincial military governors (*Nayakas*) established independent sovereign kingdoms in Tamil Nadu:
1. **Nayaks of Madurai (1529–1736 CE):**
   - Founded by Viswanatha Nayak and his able minister Ariyanatha Mudaliar.
   - Introduced the **Palayakkarar (Poligar) System**: The kingdom was divided into 72 *Palayams* (military fiefs), each assigned to a feudal chieftain responsible for revenue collection, law enforcement (*Padikaval*), and maintaining military levies.
   - **Thirumalai Nayak (1623–1659 CE):** Consolidated Madurai, built the majestic **Thirumalai Nayak Mahal** blending Dravidian and Islamic architectural styles, and renovated the Meenakshi Amman Temple complex (Pudumandapam).
   - **Rani Mangammal (1689–1704 CE):** Celebrated woman administrator; expanded road networks, planted avenue trees, dug irrigation canals, and established public choultries (*Chattrams*).
2. **Nayaks of Thanjavur (1532–1673 CE):**
   - Founded by Sevappa Nayak; patronized classical Carnatic music and Telugu literature; built the Sivaganga Fort and expanded Brihadisvara Temple enclosures.
3. **Nayaks of Gingee (Senji) (1509–1649 CE):**
   - Established by Tubaki Krishnappa Nayak; transformed Gingee Fort into an impregnable citadel with three fortified hillocks (Rajagiri, Krishnagiri, and Chandrayandurg), later praised by Chhatrapati Shivaji as the most impregnable fortress in India.

#### Land Revenue Mechanics: Zabt, Dahsala, and Todar Mal's Bandobast
In 1580 CE, Akbar and his finance minister Raja Todar Mal introduced the **Ain-i-Dahsala** system to eliminate annual price fluctuations and revenue corruption:
- Average production of each crop and average market prices over the preceding 10 years (1570–1580 CE, regnal years 15 to 24) were calculated.
- The state demand was fixed at **one-third** ($33.3\%$) of average produce, payable in cash or kind.
- Land was systematically measured using the **Ilahi Gaz** (41 digits) and iron-ringed bamboo rods (**Tanab**), replacing hemp ropes which stretched in humid weather.

#### The Bhakti and Sufi Movements: Social and Linguistic Transformation
Medieval India witnessed a profound democratization of religious life led by mystic saints singing in vernacular idioms:
- **South Indian Roots:**
  - **Alvars (12 Vaishnavite saints):** Composed the *Nalayira Divya Prabandham* (4,000 hymns), compiled by Nathamuni. Celebrated saints include Nammalvar, Periyalvar, and **Andal** (the only female Alvar, who composed *Thiruppavai* and *Nachiyar Thirumozhi*).
  - **Nayanars (63 Shaivite saints):** Composed devotional hymns compiled as the *Thirumurai* by Nambiyandar Nambi. Chief among them were the *Muvars*—Appar, Sambandar, and Sundarar (authors of the *Thevaram*)—alongside Manikkavasagar (*Thiruvasagam*).
- **Philosophical Triad:**
  - **Adi Shankara (8th c. CE):** Propounded *Advaita* (Absolute Monism; Brahman is sole reality; world is *Maya*).
  - **Ramanuja (1017–1137 CE):** Propounded *Vishishtadvaita* (Qualified Non-Dualism; soul attains liberation through devotional surrender, *Prapatti*).
  - **Madhvacharya (1238–1317 CE):** Propounded *Dvaita* (Strict Dualism between God, individual souls, and matter).

## Worked TNPSC Group 1 Exam Questions

### Question 1 (Paper-I Modern/Medieval South Indian Administration)

**Question:** With reference to the local village self-government under the Imperial Cholas, consider the following statements:
1. The election to the executive committees (*Variyams*) was conducted through the *Kudavolai* system as recorded in the Uttaramerur inscriptions of Parantaka I.
2. An individual who had served on any *Variyam* in the preceding three years and failed to submit audited financial accounts was eligible to re-contest provided he owned two *Velis* of land.
3. The *Eri Variyam* was responsible for the desiltation, embankment repair, and maintenance of village irrigation tanks.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
- (A) 1 only
- (B) 1 and 3 only
- (C) 2 and 3 only
- (D) 1, 2, and 3

**Detailed Solution:**
1. **Analysis of Statement 1:** Correct. The two epigraphs at Uttaramerur (919 and 921 CE) under Chola king Parantaka I explicitly detail the *Kudavolai* (pot-ticket) balloting system for committee members.
2. **Analysis of Statement 2:** Incorrect. A strict disqualification clause specified that anyone who had been a committee member for the past three years, or who had failed to submit audited accounts of his committee, was completely **barred** from standing for election, regardless of his landholdings. This served as an institutional anti-corruption safeguard.
3. **Analysis of Statement 3:** Correct. The *Eri Variyam* (Tank Committee) was one of the most critical committees in Chola villages, managing irrigation water sluices, bunds, and desiltation funded through dedicated tank endowments (*Eri-patti*).
- *Correct Answer:* **(B) 1 and 3 only**.

- (C) a-2, b-4, c-1, d-3
- (D) a-3, b-1, c-4, d-2

**Detailed Solution:**
- **a. Gangaikonda Cholapuram Temple:** Built by Rajendra I (1014–1044 CE) to commemorate his triumphant military march to the banks of the Ganges $\rightarrow$ **(2)**.
- **b. Pudumandapam at Madurai:** Constructed by Thirumalai Nayak (1623–1659 CE) directly opposite the Meenakshi temple tower as a summer retreat and festival pavilion $\rightarrow$ **(1)**.

## Critical TNPSC Preparation Traps & Heuristic Checkpoints

- **Trap 1: Brihadisvara Temple Attribution**: Never confuse Rajaraja I with Rajendra I. The Brihadisvara temple at **Thanjavur** was built by **Rajaraja I** (completed 1010 CE). The Brihadisvara temple at **Gangaikonda Cholapuram** was built by his son **Rajendra I** (completed ~1035 CE).
- **Trap 2: Mansab is Rank, Not Post**: A Mansab in the Mughal system was an administrative and military rank, not a specific office or land grant. The *Zat* indicated personal salary and administrative precedence; the *Sawar* determined the number of cavalrymen the official had to maintain. Mansabs were strictly **non-hereditary** and ceased upon death.
- **Trap 3: Poligar System Origin**: The Poligar (*Palayakkarar*) system did not originate under British colonial rule. It was established in 1529 CE by Viswanatha Nayak and Ariyanatha Mudaliar in Madurai based on Vijayanagara Kakatiya models (*Nayankara* system).
- **Trap 4: Uttaramerur Inscription King**: Do not attribute the Uttaramerur inscription to Rajaraja I. It belongs to **Parantaka I** (early 10th century CE), almost a century before Rajaraja I ascended the Chola throne.
