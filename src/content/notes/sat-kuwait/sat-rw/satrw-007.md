# Standard English Conventions — Sentence Boundaries and Form — SAT R&W Notes

Sentence boundaries and form is part of the SAT R&W Standard English Conventions content domain. It tests the candidate's ability to recognise and correct run-on sentences, comma splices and sentence fragments, and to choose the most concise and effective sentence form.

> Verify the live SAT specifications on https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/ before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Run-on sentence:** two independent clauses joined without punctuation or conjunction.
- **Comma splice:** two independent clauses joined only by a comma.
- **Sentence fragment:** an incomplete sentence (missing subject, verb, or independent clause).
- **Concise writing:** short sentences are clearer than long ones.
- **Effective form:** choose the form that communicates the idea most clearly.

#### Examiner traps

- Treating a comma splice as correct.
- Adding words that don't change the meaning.

---

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Run-on sentences

To fix a run-on sentence:

1. Add a period: "I went to the store. I bought milk."
2. Add a comma and conjunction: "I went to the store, and I bought milk."
3. Add a semicolon: "I went to the store; I bought milk."
4. Restructure: "I went to the store to buy milk."

#### Comma splices

A comma splice uses only a comma to join two independent clauses. To fix it, apply the same solutions as for run-on sentences.

Example of comma splice: "I went to the store, I bought milk." (wrong)

#### Sentence fragments

A sentence fragment is missing a subject, verb, or independent clause. To fix it, complete the sentence.

Example of fragment: "Running through the park." (no main verb or independent clause)

#### Concise writing

Concise writing uses the fewest words to communicate the idea. Remove redundant words, simplify phrases, and combine sentences.

Example: "In spite of the fact that it was raining" → "Although it was raining" (conciseness)

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where Sentence Boundaries and Form appears across the SAT R&W section

Sentence Boundaries and Form items appear across the SAT Reading and Writing section. They test editing and revising at a level higher than recall.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Run-on sentences are acceptable." No — they obscure the meaning.
- "Long sentences are more sophisticated." No — concise sentences are clearer.

#### Exam technique

- For run-on questions, identify the two independent clauses.
- For comma-splice questions, recognise the missing conjunction or punctuation.
- For fragment questions, complete the sentence.
- For conciseness questions, choose the option with the fewest words that still communicates the meaning.
