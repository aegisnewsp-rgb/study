# Complex Numbers — VCE Specialist Mathematics (VCAA 2023) Notes

Complex Numbers is a Year 12 topic in VCE Specialist Mathematics Units 3 and 4. The 2023 Study Design covers the imaginary unit, complex arithmetic, Argand diagrams, modulus and argument, De Moivre's theorem, roots of complex numbers and applications to polynomial equations. The topic is assessed in Examination 1.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Imaginary unit.** i² = −1, i³ = −i, i⁴ = 1.
- **Complex arithmetic.** z = a + bi. Addition: (a + bi) + (c + di) = (a + c) + (b + d)i. Multiplication: (a + bi)(c + di) = (ac − bd) + (ad + bc)i.
- **Modulus.** |z| = √(a² + b²).
- **Argument.** arg(z) = arctan(b/a) (with quadrant adjustment).
- **Conjugate.** z̄ = a − bi. Note z × z̄ = |z|².
- **Polar form.** z = r (cos θ + i sin θ) where r = |z|, θ = arg(z).
- **Exponential form.** z = r e^(iθ).
- **De Moivre's theorem.** (cos θ + i sin θ)^n = cos nθ + i sin nθ.
- **Roots.** The n-th roots of z = r e^(iθ) are r^(1/n) e^(i(θ + 2πk)/n) for k = 0, 1, ..., n − 1.

#### Examiner traps

- Forgetting that z̄ = a − bi, not −z.
- Mixing up argument in degrees and radians — VCE uses radians.
- Confusing multiplication and division in polar form — division: z₁/z₂ = (r₁/r₂) e^(i(θ₁−θ₂)).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Complex arithmetic

For z = a + bi, w = c + di:
- z + w = (a + c) + (b + d)i.
- z − w = (a − c) + (b − d)i.
- zw = (ac − bd) + (ad + bc)i.
- z/w = (zw̄)/(ww̄) = (zw̄)/|w|².

For polynomial division by a complex factor, use the same long division as for real polynomials.

#### Modulus and argument

|z| = √(a² + b²) is the distance from z to the origin in the Argand diagram. arg(z) = arctan(b/a) is the angle from the positive real axis.

For z = a + bi in the first quadrant: arg(z) = arctan(b/a). For other quadrants, add π or subtract π as appropriate to get the angle in (−π, π].

#### Polar and exponential form

Polar: z = r (cos θ + i sin θ). Exponential: z = r e^(iθ). r = |z| ≥ 0, θ = arg(z).

Conversion: from rectangular to polar: r = √(a² + b²), θ = arctan(b/a). From polar to rectangular: a = r cos θ, b = r sin θ.

#### De Moivre's theorem

(cos θ + i sin θ)^n = cos nθ + i sin nθ. Extends to negative and rational n for any nonzero r.

Application: cos 3θ + i sin 3θ = (cos θ + i sin θ)³. Expanding gives cos 3θ = 4 cos³ θ − 3 cos θ and sin 3θ = 3 sin θ − 4 sin³ θ.

#### Roots of complex numbers

The n-th roots of z = r e^(iθ) are r^(1/n) e^(i(θ + 2πk)/n) for k = 0, 1, ..., n − 1. The roots lie on a circle of radius r^(1/n) centred at the origin, equally spaced by 2π/n radians.

For real z < 0: the square roots are ±i√|z|. For z = 1: the n-th roots are e^(2πik/n) — these are the n-th roots of unity.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Why Complex Numbers appears in Examination 1

- **Complex numbers reward fluency with algebraic manipulation in rectangular and polar forms.**
- **De Moivre's theorem and root finding are common Higher-tariff questions.**
- **Applications to polynomial equations** (factor theorem, root-product relations) link to Algebra and Number.

#### Exam technique

- For arithmetic, choose the form (rectangular or polar) that makes the operation easiest.
- For equation-solving, isolate the complex number and apply De Moivre's theorem.
- For roots, give all n roots in the form r^(1/n) e^(i(θ + 2πk)/n).

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "i is imaginary." i is a useful mathematical abstraction that extends the real numbers to the complex numbers.
- "i has no real-world applications." Complex numbers are used in electrical engineering (AC circuits), quantum mechanics, and signal processing.
- "arg(z) is unique." arg(z) is determined modulo 2π.
