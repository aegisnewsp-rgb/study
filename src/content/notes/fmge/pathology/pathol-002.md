# Inflammation and Repair

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Inflammation** is the protective vascular and cellular reaction of living tissues to injury, recognised by the cardinal signs **rubor, tumor, calor, dolor**, and the later-added **functio laesa** (Virchow). **Repair** follows once the stimulus is cleared, via parenchymal **regeneration** or **fibrosis** (scarring).

| Event | Key fact |
|---|---|
| Vascular changes | Transient vasoconstriction → vasodilation → increased permeability → exudate |
| Cellular chronology | Neutrophils (24–48 h) → Macrophages (48–72 h) → Lymphocytes/plasma cells (chronic) |
| Granuloma | Epithelioid macrophages + Langhans/foreign-body giant cells + lymphocyte rim |
| Healing phases | Haemostasis → Inflammation (0–3 d) → Proliferation (3 d–2 wk) → Remodelling (up to 1 yr) |
| Tensile strength | Recovers to ~70–80% of normal by ~3 months — never 100% |

> **Exam pointer:** *Functio laesa* is **not** a Celsus sign — FMGE repeatedly tests this.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Vascular and Cellular Sequence
Injury triggers brief vasoconstriction (seconds), then **vasodilation** mediated by **histamine** and **NO**, producing redness and warmth. Endothelial contraction widens intercellular junctions, leaking plasma proteins into the interstitium to form **exudate** (oedema). **Stasis** allows leukocytes to **marginate, roll, adhere** (selectins → ICAM-1/VCAM-1), **transmigrate**, and **chemotax** toward microbial products (C5a, LTB4, IL-8).

#### Chemical Mediators

| Mediator | Source | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Histamine | Mast cells, basophils | Vasodilation, ↑ permeability |
| Prostaglandins (PGI2, PGE2) | COX pathway | Vasodilation, **pain**, fever |
| Leukotrienes (LTC4, D4, E4) | LOX pathway | Bronchoconstriction, ↑ permeability |
| TNF-α, IL-1, IL-6 | Macrophages | Fever, adhesion, acute-phase response |
| C3a, C5a | Complement | Anaphylatoxins, chemotaxis (C5a) |
| Bradykinin | Kinin system | Pain, vasodilation, permeability |

#### Acute vs Chronic Inflammation
**Acute** is short-lived, exudative, neutrophil-rich. **Chronic** persists weeks to years, with mononuclear infiltrate (macrophages, lymphocytes, plasma cells), ongoing **destruction**, and simultaneous attempted **repair** — eventually leading to **fibrosis**. Granulomatous inflammation is a special chronic pattern with epithelioid cells and giant cells, classically seen in **TB, leprosy, sarcoidosis**, fungal infections, foreign bodies, and **Crohn disease**.

> **Trap:** Don't label neutrophils as the chronic-inflammation cell — **macrophages, lymphocytes, plasma cells** are correct.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Healing in Depth
Haemostasis seals the wound with a fibrin-platelet clot within minutes. Inflammation (0–3 d) clears debris and microbes. The **proliferative phase** (3 d–2 wk) builds **granulation tissue**: **angiogenesis** (VEGF-driven), **fibroblast** proliferation, and deposition of **type III collagen**. **Remodelling** converts type III → **type I collagen**, while **myofibroblasts** contract the wound. Tensile strength climbs to ~10% at 1 week, ~30% at 3 weeks, and plateaus at **~70–80% of normal skin** by ~3 months — a classic FMGE assertion-reason stem.

> **High-yield fact:** Maximum tensile strength is **not** 100%; the scar remains biomechanically inferior to native dermis.

#### Primary vs Secondary Intention
**First intention** (clean, sutured edges) heals with minimal granulation tissue and a fine scar. **Second intention** (open, infected, or wide wound) requires more granulation tissue, more contraction, and yields a larger scar.

#### Factors That Impair Healing
Infection, ischaemia, **diabetes**, malnutrition — especially **vitamin C deficiency** (blocks proline/lysine hydroxylation, causing scurvy), **corticosteroids**, foreign bodies, and mechanical tension. **Mineralocorticoids** in adrenal insufficiency do not impair healing; this is a deliberate distractor.

#### Adjacent Topics and Practice Prompts

- **Microbiology link:** *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* → **caseating** granuloma; leprosy → TT (well-formed) vs LL (foamy macrophage) granulomas.
- **Pharmacology link:** NSAIDs block **COX** → ↓ prostaglandins → reduced pain and fever, but **leukotrienes persist**, worsening bronchospasm in aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease.

**Prompt 1 — One-liner:** "A 45-year-old diabetic presents with a non-healing foot ulcer. List the local and systemic factors you would address." (Expected: glycaemic control, debridement, offloading, infection control, nutrition.)

**Prompt 2 — Vignette:** "A 60-year-old on long-term steroids has delayed wound healing. Mechanism?" (Expected: corticosteroids inhibit phospholipase A2 → ↓ prostaglandins/leukotrienes, and suppress macrophage and fibroblast function.)

> **Exam tip:** FMGE loves integrating **vitamin C → collagen hydroxylation → scurvy** and **Langhans vs foreign-body giant cells** into Microbiology or Surgery stems — keep both in one mental block.
