# Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Province

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your PPSC exam.

Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KP), formerly known as the North-West Frontier Province (NWFP), occupies Pakistan's northwestern frontier, bordered by Afghanistan to the west and north (along the 2,640 km Durand Line), Gilgit-Baltistan to the northeast, Azad Jammu & Kashmir to the east, Punjab to the southeast, and Balochistan to the south. Master these core historical, geographic, and constitutional facts for the Punjab Public Service Commission (PPSC) General Knowledge paper:

- **Renaming**: Renamed from NWFP to Khyber Pakhtunkhwa under the **18th Constitutional Amendment** in April 2010 (Article 1 of the Constitution of Pakistan).
- **FATA Merger**: The Federally Administered Tribal Areas (FATA) and Frontier Regions (FRs) were formally merged into KP via the **25th Constitutional Amendment** (passed by the National Assembly on 24 May 2018 and signed into law on 31 May 2018).
- **Provincial Capital**: Peshawar, one of the oldest living cities in South Asia, situated near the eastern terminus of the historic Khyber Pass.
- **Highest Peak**: **Tirich Mir** (7,708 m / 25,289 ft) in the Chitral District, which is also the highest mountain in the Hindu Kush range worldwide. *(Examiner trap: Nanga Parbat is 8,126 m, located in Gilgit-Baltistan, not KP).*
- **Archaeological Distinction**: **Takht-i-Bahi** (Buddhist monastic complex founded in the 1st century CE, UNESCO World Heritage site since 1980) is situated in Mardan District, KP. *(Examiner trap: Taxila is located in Rawalpindi District, Punjab, not KP).*
- **Strategic Passes**: Khyber Pass (connects Peshawar with Jalalabad, Afghanistan via Landi Kotal), Malakand Pass (gateway to Swat and Dir), Lowari Pass / Lowari Tunnel (connects Dir with Chitral through the Hindu Raj range), and Babusar Pass (connects Kaghan Valley with Chilas, Gilgit-Baltistan).
- **Administrative Scale**: 7 divisions, 36 districts (including the 7 Newly Merged Districts / NMDs: Bajaur, Mohmand, Khyber, Orakzai, Kurram, North Waziristan, South Waziristan Upper/Lower), and 145 provincial assembly seats.

#### 1. Constitutional & Administrative Evolution

Khyber Pakhtunkhwa has experienced three profound administrative transformations since the colonial era:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│      1901: Lord Curzon carves NWFP out of Punjab           │
└─────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────┘
                              │
                              ▼
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ 2010 (18th Amendment): Renamed NWFP to Khyber Pakhtunkhwa │
└─────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────┘
                              │
                              ▼
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│   2018 (25th Amendment): FATA merged into KP (NMDs)        │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

1. **Colonial Formation (1901)**: Viceroy Lord Curzon separated the frontier districts of Punjab to form the North-West Frontier Province (NWFP) in 1901, primarily to enforce direct central control over the strategic Afghan borderlands under the "Forward Policy".
2. **Princely State Accessions (1969)**: The frontier princely states of **Swat, Dir, and Chitral** formally merged into West Pakistan in 1969 under the West Pakistan (Dissolution) Order, later becoming regular administrative districts of NWFP when the One Unit scheme was dissolved in 1970. Amb state merged in 1972.
3. **The 18th Amendment (2010)**: Fulfilled a century-long political demand by renaming the province to reflect its Pashtun demographic majority while recognizing non-Pashtun linguistic regions (notably Hindko-speaking Hazara and Saraiki-speaking Dera Ismail Khan).
4. **The 25th Amendment (2018)**: Dissolved the colonial **Frontier Crimes Regulation (FCR 1901)**, abolished the office of the Political Agent, extended the jurisdiction of the Supreme Court of Pakistan and Peshawar High Court to tribal territories, and integrated the seven tribal agencies into KP as administrative districts.

#### 3. Physical Geography, River Basins & Mountain Passes

The geography of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa transitions from perpetual snowfields and glaciated alpine horns in the north to fertile alluvial floodplains in the center and dissected sandstone plateaus in the south.

##### Mountain Ranges
- **Hindu Kush**: Dominates northern KP (Chitral). Its apex, **Tirich Mir** (7,708 m), towers over Chitral town. Other major peaks include Noshaq (7,492 m, on the Pak-Afghan border) and Istor-o-Nal (7,403 m).
- **Hindu Raj**: Lies between the Hindu Kush and the Karakoram, acting as the watershed between the Gilgit and Kunar/Chitral river systems. Highest peak is **Buni Zom** (6,551 m).
- **Himalayan Western Flank**: Traverses the Hazara division east of the Indus River. Encloses the Kaghan Valley, culminating in peaks like Malika Parbat (5,290 m).
- **Safed Koh (Spin Ghar)**: Extends westward from Kohat and Peshawar toward Afghanistan. Its highest summit is **Mount Sikaram** (4,755 m), which overlooks the Khyber Pass.
- **Sulaiman Range**: Forms the boundary between southern KP (D.I. Khan), southwestern Punjab, and eastern Balochistan.

##### River Hydrography
1. **Indus River**: Enters KP from Gilgit-Baltistan near Besham/Kohistan, flows past Tarbela Dam (world's largest earth-and-rock-filled dam, capacity 4,888 MW), separates Hazara from Swabi and Nowshera, and exits into Punjab near Attock.
2. **Kabul River**: Originates in the Sanglakh Range in Afghanistan, enters KP north of the Khyber Pass, feeds the **Warsak Dam** (commissioned 1960 under the Colombo Plan), receives the Swat River at Nisatta near Charsadda, and joins the Indus near Attock Khurd.
3. **Swat River**: Formed by the confluence of the Gabral and Ushu rivers at Kalam, traverses the Swat Valley, passes through Amandara headworks, and provides irrigation to the fertile Peshawar-Mardan valley via the Upper and Lower Swat Canals.
4. **Kurram River**: Rises in Paktia province (Afghanistan), flows through Kurram and North Waziristan districts, enters the Bannu basin, and joins the Indus near Isa Khel.
5. **Gomal River**: Drains eastern Afghanistan and South Waziristan, harnessed by the multi-purpose **Gomal Zam Dam** (17.4 MW, 133 m high roller-compacted concrete gravity dam in South Waziristan).

#### 1. Gandhara Civilization & UNESCO Heritage Architecture

The Gandhara cultural horizon flourished in the Peshawar Valley, Taxila, and Swat from the 6th century BCE to the 6th century CE under Achaemenian, Mauryan, Indo-Greek, Kushan, and White Hun (Hephthalite) rule.

##### Takht-i-Bahi (The Throne of Springs)
- **Status**: UNESCO World Heritage Site inscribed in **1980** alongside the neighboring ruins of **Sahr-i-Bahlol**.
- **Location**: Crest of a 152-meter hill, approximately 15 km northwest of Mardan city.
- **Architectural Layout**: Composed of four principal contiguous sectors:
  1. *Stupa Court*: A cluster of votive stupas adorned with carved schist reliefs depicting the life and *Jataka* tales of Siddhartha Gautama.
  2. *Monastic Quadrangle*: Individual residential cells surrounding a central open-air assembly courtyard.
  3. *Temple Complex*: Featuring vaulted chapels and colossal standing Buddha niches.
  4. *Tantric Monastic Catacombs*: Subterranean meditation chambers designed for low-light ascetic contemplation.
- **Key Distinctions**: Escaped destruction by the White Huns due to its secluded hilltop elevation, making it the most complete Buddhist monastic establishment surviving in South Asia.

##### Jamal Garhi & Butkara Stupa
- **Jamal Garhi**: Located 13 km north of Mardan; famous for its distinctive circular stupa courtyard surrounded by poly-lobed chapels.
- **Butkara I (Mingora, Swat)**: Established by the Mauryan Emperor Ashoka in the 3rd century BCE; excavated by the Italian Archaeological Mission (*IsMEO*) under Domenico Faccenna, proving sequential enlargement over five distinct structural phases.

#### 2. Pashtunwali: Anthropological & Legal Framework

Pashtunwali represents the unwritten customary legal and moral code governing traditional Pashtun society. PPSC examiners test candidates on precise terminology and functional definitions:

| Principle | Pashto Term | Functional Meaning & Social Obligation |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Hospitality** | *Melmastiya* | Unconditional obligation to host, feed, and protect any guest, traveler, or visitor without expectation of material remuneration, regardless of religion, ethnicity, or past enmity. |
| **Sanctuary / Asylum** | *Nanawatai* | The formal plea for protection, asylum, or reconciliation. When a supplicant enters an adversary's house carrying a humble offering (or sheep/goat), the host is honor-bound to grant refuge and suspend hostilities. |
| **Retribution / Justice** | *Badal* | The obligation to exact proportionate retribution for murder, physical dishonor, or insult. Unlike indiscriminate blood feuds, *badal* is strictly targeted at the perpetrator and has no statute of limitations. |
| **Honor / Integrity** | *Nang* / *Namus* | The defense of personal integrity, family reputation, collective land (*watan*), and particularly female dignity (*tor* / *namus*). |
| **Consultative Council** | *Jirga* | An assembly of elders (*masharan*) convened to arbitrate disputes, determine restorative penalties, or negotiate community treaties. Distinct from the governmental *Loya Jirga* (Grand Assembly). |
| **Collective Assistance** | *Ashar* | Reciprocal communal labor mobilization for agricultural harvesting, house construction, or canal maintenance without cash wages. |

#### 3. Political Economy, Energy & Mineral Wealth

Although historically constrained by rugged topography and cross-border security challenges, KP possesses high-density comparative advantages in clean hydroelectric energy, mineral extraction, and specialized cash crops.

##### Hydroelectric Dominance
KP generates the overwhelming majority of Pakistan's low-cost hydroelectric power:
- **Tarbela Dam** (Haripur/Swabi on Indus): 4,888 MW installed capacity (expanding toward 6,418 MW with Tarbela 5th Extension).
- **Warsak Dam** (Peshawar on Kabul River): 243 MW.
- **Duber Khwar** (Kohistan): 130 MW run-of-the-river project.
- **Allai Khwar** (Battagram): 121 MW.
- **Golen Gol** (Chitral): 108 MW on the Golen Gol tributary of the Mastuj/Chitral River.
- **Dasu Hydropower Project** (Kohistan Upper): Under construction on the Indus River; Stage-I capacity of 2,160 MW (ultimate 4,320 MW).

##### Hydrocarbon & Mineral Deposits
- **Oil and Gas**: The southern districts of **Karak, Kohat, and Hangu** (Kohat Basin) produce substantial shares of domestic crude oil, natural gas, and liquefied petroleum gas (LPG). Key operational fields include Nashpa, Makori Deep, Chanda, and Maramzai.
- **Tobacco**: The alluvial plains of **Swabi, Mardan, Charsadda, and Mansehra** produce over 75% of Pakistan's Virginia flue-cured (VFC) tobacco, providing significant provincial excise and federal tax revenues.
- **Marble and Gemstones**: Buner and Mohmand produce high-grade white marble. Swat holds premier **emerald** mines (notably Mingora and Gujjar Killi); Katlang (Mardan) is globally renowned for rare **pink topaz**; and Kohistan yields peridot.

### 📝 PPSC High-Yield Exam Traps & Multiple-Choice Drills

#### Frequent Pitfalls to Avoid in PPSC General Knowledge
1. **Taxila vs Takht-i-Bahi**: Taxila is in Punjab (Rawalpindi district); Takht-i-Bahi is in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (Mardan district). Both are UNESCO World Heritage sites inscribed in 1980, which leads candidates to conflate them.
2. **Highest Peak Misconception**: The highest peak within Khyber Pakhtunkhwa is **Tirich Mir** (7,708 m, Chitral). Do not select K2 (8,611 m) or Nanga Parbat (8,126 m); both are located in Gilgit-Baltistan.
3. **Renaming Amendment vs Merger Amendment**:
   - Renaming NWFP → Khyber Pakhtunkhwa: **18th Amendment (2010)**.
   - Merging FATA → Khyber Pakhtunkhwa: **25th Amendment (2018)**.
4. **Lowari Tunnel Length**: 10.4 km dual-tunnel system beneath the Lowari Pass, opening all-weather vehicular transit between Dir and Chitral.
5. **Indus Highway Designation**: Motorway **M-14** (Hakla–D.I. Khan Motorway, part of CPEC Western Alignment) terminates at Dera Ismail Khan; Hazara Motorway is **M-15** (Burhan to Thakot).

#### Practice Questions & Explanations

##### Question 1
Under which constitutional amendment act was the Federally Administered Tribal Areas (FATA) officially merged with the province of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa?
- (A) 18th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2010
- (B) 21st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2015
- (C) 25th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2018
- (D) 26th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2019

**Answer: (C) 25th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2018.**  
*Explanation*: Passed by parliament in May 2018, the 25th Amendment amended Articles 1, 51, 59, 106, and 247 of the Constitution of Pakistan, bringing the 7 tribal agencies and 6 frontier regions into KP's provincial administration and abolishing the Frontier Crimes Regulation.

##### Question 2
The ancient Buddhist monastery complex of Takht-i-Bahi is situated in which district of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa?
- (A) Peshawar
- (B) Swat
- (C) Mardan
- (D) Charsadda

**Answer: (C) Mardan.**  
*Explanation*: Takht-i-Bahi is located on a ridge 15 km northwest of Mardan city. It was established around the 1st century CE and listed as a UNESCO World Heritage site in 1980.

##### Question 3
Which mountain pass connects the district of Dir with Chitral and features an all-weather 10.4 km road tunnel opened in 2017?
- (A) Babusar Pass
- (B) Lowari Pass
- (C) Malakand Pass
- (D) Shandur Pass

**Answer: (B) Lowari Pass.**  
*Explanation*: Lowari Pass (3,118 m) was historically closed by heavy snow for several months each winter until the completion and operational opening of the Lowari Tunnel.

##### Question 4
What is the highest mountain peak located within the provincial boundary of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa?
- (A) Nanga Parbat
- (B) Rakaposhi
- (C) Tirich Mir
- (D) Malika Parbat

**Answer: (C) Tirich Mir.**  
*Explanation*: Standing at 7,708 meters in the Chitral District, Tirich Mir is the crowning summit of the Hindu Kush mountain system and the highest peak in KP. Nanga Parbat (8,126 m) and Rakaposhi (7,788 m) reside in Gilgit-Baltistan.
