# Measurement and Units

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Measurement compares an unknown quantity with a standard **unit**, expressing the result as a number × unit. The seven **SI base quantities** are length (metre, m), mass (kilogram, kg), time (second, s), electric current (ampere, A), thermodynamic temperature (kelvin, K), amount of substance (mole, mol), and luminous intensity (candela, cd). Every other unit is **derived**, e.g. **newton** N = kg·m·s⁻², **joule** J = kg·m²·s⁻², **watt** W = kg·m²·s⁻³. The **dimensional formula** of a quantity is written as [MᵃLᵇTᶜ]; for force it is [M L T⁻²]. **Accuracy** = closeness to the true value; **precision** = closeness of repeated readings to each other. ECAT expects you to (i) name the SI unit of a derived quantity, (ii) write its dimensional formula, and (iii) convert using prefixes (1 nm = 10⁻⁹ m, 1 µm = 10⁻⁶ m, 1 km = 10³ m).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Base, Derived, and Supplementary Units
The **SI system** (Système International d'Unités) is the modern form of the metric system. Seven quantities are independent — length, mass, time, current, temperature, amount, luminous intensity — and define the seven base units. Anything else (velocity, force, energy, pressure, charge, resistance, capacitance, magnetic flux, etc.) is a **derived unit** built from these seven without introducing new dimensions. Example reductions:

| Quantity | SI unit | In base units |
|---|---|---|
| Velocity | m s⁻¹ | m·s⁻¹ |
| Acceleration | m s⁻² | m·s⁻² |
| Force (N) | kg·m·s⁻² | kg·m·s⁻² |
| Work/Energy (J) | kg·m²·s⁻² | kg·m²·s⁻² |
| Power (W) | kg·m²·s⁻³ | kg·m²·s⁻³ |
| Pressure (Pa) | kg·m⁻¹·s⁻² | kg·m⁻¹·s⁻² |

#### Dimensional Analysis
Every physical quantity is written as **[Mᵃ Lᵇ Tᶜ]** (extended with [A] for current, [K] for temperature, [mol], [cd] when needed). The **principle of dimensional homogeneity** requires that every term added in an equation has the same dimensions, and that the LHS and RHS match. If x = k·aᵖ·bᶜ, find p, c by matching [M], [L], [T] on both sides. Pure numbers, π, e, and trigonometric arguments are **dimensionless** ([M⁰ L⁰ T⁰]) — that is why dimensional analysis cannot fix the constant ½ in s = ut + ½at².

#### Significant Figures and Error
**Significant figures** count all certain digits plus the first doubtful one. For addition/subtraction, the result is limited by the term with the largest absolute uncertainty (fewest decimal places). For multiplication/division, the result has the same number of significant figures as the input with the fewest. The **percentage error** in a quantity A is %error = (ΔA / A_avg) × 100; for products and quotients, percentage errors add.

#### Accuracy vs Precision
**Accuracy** depends on systematic error (instrument calibration), while **precision** depends on random error (reading scatter). A metre stick with its zero worn away can produce readings that are tightly clustered (high precision) yet wrong (low accuracy); zero-error correction is therefore a standard ECAT sub-question.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Worked Example — Dimensional Check and Conversion
Test the homogeneity of v² = u² + 2as (equations of motion). LHS: [L²T⁻²]. RHS first term: [L²T⁻²]. Second term: a has [LT⁻²], s has [L], so 2as has [L²T⁻²]. All three terms match → the equation is dimensionally consistent. Now convert 72 km·h⁻¹ to m·s⁻¹: 72 × (1000 m / 1 km) × (1 h / 3600 s) = 20 m·s⁻¹. Note how each conversion factor is exactly 1, so multiplying does not change the physical value, only its numerical representation.

#### Edge Cases and Adjacent Links
- **Planck's constant h** has dimensions of action [M L² T⁻¹] = J·s — recognise it from atomic/quantum chapters.
- **Dimensional analysis cannot yield dimensionless constants** (the 2 in F = (1/4πε₀)·q₁q₂/r² hides geometry, not physics).
- **Trigonometric arguments** (θ in sin θ) must be dimensionless, so an angle is a pure number, and "30°" is dimensionless in equations.
- SI is **coherent**: derived units are products of base units without numerical factors (contrast horse-power = 746 W).
- Watch capitalisation: **s** = second, **S** = siemens (conductance); **m** = metre, **m** = milli-prefix only when written as a prefix attached to a unit (mm, mg). Lowercase **k** is the kilo-prefix, while **K** is kelvin.

#### Practice Prompts
1. The period of a simple pendulum is assumed to depend on length L, mass m, and g. Using dimensional analysis, find the exponents in T = k·Lᵖ·mᵍ·gʳ and predict whether m appears.
2. A resistance is measured as 4.7 Ω, 4.9 Ω, 4.8 Ω across three trials. Compute mean, mean absolute deviation, and percentage error; state whether the measurement is accurate, precise, or both.
