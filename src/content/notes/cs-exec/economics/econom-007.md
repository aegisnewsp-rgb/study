# Supply and Market Equilibrium

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your CS Executive paper.

Market equilibrium is the single price (Pe) at which quantity demanded equals quantity supplied. With linear curves, **Qd = a − bP** and **Qs = c + dP**, equilibrium solves to **Pe = (a − c)/(b + d)** and **Qe = a − bPe**. Above Pe, a surplus (Qs − Qd > 0) forces price down; below Pe, a shortage pushes price up — the Walrasian adjustment process.

- **Equilibrium price** = intersection of downward-sloping demand and upward-sloping supply.
- **Surplus** = Qs − Qd (positive above Pe); **Shortage** = Qd − Qs (positive below Pe).
- **Elasticity of supply** Es = (%ΔQs)/(%ΔP), always positive for a normal good.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Linear demand–supply solution
Take a market with Qd = 100 − 4P and Qs = 20 + 2P (P in rupees, Q in units). Equating gives 100 − 4P = 20 + 2P, so **80 = 6P → Pe = ₹13.33** and **Qe = 100 − 4(13.33) = 46.67 units**. Always verify Pe lies within the economically relevant range of both curves.

#### Movement vs shift of supply
A *change in quantity supplied* is movement along the curve caused by the **own price** of the good. A *change in supply* is a parallel shift of the entire curve caused by **non-price determinants**: input costs, technology, number of sellers, taxes/subsidies, and prices of jointly or competitively supplied goods.

#### Equilibrium vs disequilibrium adjustment
At Pe both sides of the market clear simultaneously. If a price floor is imposed above Pe, Qs exceeds Qd and a surplus emerges, often absorbed by government procurement or wasted. A price ceiling below Pe creates a shortage, rationing, and a parallel black market.

| Concept | Key point |
| --- | --- |
| Equilibrium price (Pe) | Price that clears the market: Qd = Qs |
| Surplus | Qs − Qd > 0, occurs when imposed price > Pe |
| Shortage | Qd − Qs > 0, occurs when imposed price < Pe |
| Movement along SC | Caused only by change in own price |
| Shift of SC | Caused by input prices, technology, taxes, expectations |

- A **price ceiling** below Pe benefits some buyers but shrinks Qe and creates deadweight loss.
- A **price floor** above Pe (e.g. MSP for wheat) raises producer surplus but generates buffer stocks.
- **Tax incidence**: an excise of t shifts Qs upward by t; buyers absorb b/(b+d) of t, sellers absorb d/(b+d).

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Time horizon and elasticity of supply
Supply elasticity rises with time. Perishable agricultural supply is near-perfectly inelastic in a single day, fairly elastic across a season, and highly elastic in the long run as farmers reallocate land and inputs. Any welfare or incidence calculation that omits the time frame is incomplete.

#### Worked numerical — excise tax incidence
Demand: Qd = 200 − 5P; Supply before tax: Qs = 40 + 5P. Pre-tax equilibrium: 200 − 5P = 40 + 5P → **Pe = ₹16, Qe = 120**. Impose ₹4 specific excise, shifting supply to Qs = 40 + 5(P − 4) = 20 + 5P. New equilibrium: 200 − 5P = 20 + 5P → **Pe_new = ₹18** paid by buyers, **Ps = ₹14** received by sellers. Buyers bear ₹2 of the ₹4 tax, sellers bear ₹2 because elasticities are symmetric.

#### Cobweb model and stability
With lagged agricultural supply Qs,t = a + b·P(t−1) and demand Qd,t = c − d·Pt, the price dynamics converge when b/d < 1 and diverge otherwise. This explains observed potato and onion price cycles tested in ICSI illustrative problems.

#### Common examiner traps
- Confusing *change in quantity supplied* (movement) with *change in supply* (shift).
- Reporting Pe without checking the sign or magnitude against the economically relevant range.
- Computing tax incidence using absolute slopes only — the correct formula uses relative elasticities.

1. Practice: solve for Pe and Qe when Qd = 80 − 2P, Qs = −10 + 3P, then a ₹3 tax.
2. Practice: compute new equilibrium surplus/deficit after a price ceiling set 20% below Pe.
