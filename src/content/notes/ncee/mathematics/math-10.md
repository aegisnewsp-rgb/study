# Volume and Surface Area

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Volume** measures the 3-D space a solid occupies (cubic units: cm³, m³). **Surface area** measures the outer boundary (square units: cm², m²). NCEE almost always tests the five standard solids: **cube, cuboid, cylinder, cone, sphere**. Memorise the core formulas:

- Cube (side *l*): V = *l*³; TSA = 6*l*²
- Cuboid (*l, b, h*): V = *l* × *b* × *h*; TSA = 2(*lb* + *bh* + *lh*)
- Cylinder (*r, h*): V = π*r*²*h*; CSA = 2π*rh*; TSA = 2π*r*(*r* + *h*)
- Cone (*r, h, l*): V = (1/3)π*r*²*h*; CSA = π*rl*; TSA = π*r*(*r* + *l*)
- Sphere (*r*): V = (4/3)π*r*³; SA = 4π*r*²

Use **π ≈ 22/7**. Cones need the 1/3 factor; spheres have no flat base. When a solid is melted and recast, **volume is conserved** — surface area changes. Convert all lengths to the **same unit** first; 1 litre = 1000 cm³.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Definitions
**Volume** is the cubic space a solid encloses. **Surface area** is the sum of the areas of all its faces. For a closed solid there are two surface measures: **curved (lateral) surface area (CSA)**, which counts only the curved or side faces, and **total surface area (TSA)**, which adds the base(s).

#### Standard formulas at a glance
| Solid | Volume | TSA |
|---|---|---|
| Cube (side *l*) | *l*³ | 6*l*² |
| Cuboid (*l, b, h*) | *l b h* | 2(*lb + bh + lh*) |
| Cylinder (*r, h*) | π*r*²*h* | 2π*r*(*r + h*) |
| Cone (*r, h, l*) | (1/3)π*r*²*h* | π*r*(*r + l*) |
| Sphere (*r*) | (4/3)π*r*³ | 4π*r*² |

A cone's **slant height** *l* is found from the Pythagorean rule: *l* = √(*r*² + *h*²). A hemisphere's CSA = 2π*r*² and TSA = 3π*r*² (the closed flat disc adds π*r*²).

#### Why a cone is one-third of its enclosing cylinder
Geometrically, three cones of equal base and height exactly fill a cylinder of the same dimensions — Archimedes' proof. The same principle gives the sphere's volume as (2/3) of the cylinder that just contains it (radius *r*, height 2*r*).

#### Conservation of volume on recasting
When a solid is melted and poured into a different mould, only the **shape** changes. Equate the two volume expressions to find a missing dimension:
> π*r*₁² *h*₁ = (1/3)π*r*₂² *h*₂

#### Unit handling
1 m = 100 cm ⇒ 1 m² = 10 000 cm² and 1 m³ = 1 000 000 cm³. Also: 1 litre = 1000 cm³, so 1 m³ = 1000 litres. **Always convert before substituting.**

#### Typical NCEE question patterns
- Direct substitution (find volume/TSA given *r* and *h*).
- Ratio or comparison (e.g., volumes of two spheres given radii ratio 2 : 3 ⇒ volume ratio 8 : 27).
- Recasting: cylinder melted into *n* cones of equal size.
- Capacity worded problems (cm³ ↔ litres).
- Husk/cloth/gold-leaf problems where CSA or TSA is wrapped.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Common mistakes that cost marks
1. **Slant height vs. vertical height.** A cone's CSA uses *l* (slant), not *h*. NCEE word problems sometimes give *h*; compute *l* = √(*r*² + *h*²) first.
2. **Diameter confusion.** Formulas use **radius** *r*. If 2*r* is given, halve it before substituting.
3. **Missing base.** TSA of a closed cylinder = 2π*r*² (both caps) + 2π*rh*. Dropping one cap gives the "open cylinder" case.
4. **Unit mismatch.** Plugging metres into a formula and writing cm³ is a silent error — keep units consistent or convert at the start.
5. **Forgetting 1/3 for cones.** Volume of a cone is one-third of its enclosing cylinder; many wrong answers come from omitting this factor.
6. **Cube-conversion errors.** 1 m = 100 cm, so 1 m³ = 100³ = 1 000 000 cm³, **not** 100 000. Cross-check with the linear factor.

#### Worked micro-example
A cylinder of radius 7 cm and height 10 cm is melted and recast into 10 identical cones of radius 3.5 cm. Find the height of each cone.
- Cylinder volume = π × 7² × 10 = 490π cm³.
- One cone = 490π / 10 = 49π cm³.
- 49π = (1/3) × π × 3.5² × *h* ⇒ *h* = (49 × 3) / 12.25 = **12 cm**.

#### Connections to other NCEE topics
- **Mensuration of 2-D shapes:** circles (π*r*²) and rectangles supply the base areas used here.
- **Algebraic manipulation:** recasting problems reduce to linear/quadratic equations in one unknown.
- **Ratio and proportion:** volume scales as the cube of the linear scale factor (k³); area scales as k².
- **Unit conversion:** reinforces the metric system used elsewhere in the paper.

#### Exam strategy
Volume & Surface Area contributes roughly **4 %** of NCEE Mathematics (about 3–4 questions). Allocate ~2 minutes per question. Prefer π = 22/7 when *r* is a multiple of 7; otherwise keep π symbolic. In MCQs, eliminate options by sanity-checking the magnitude (e.g., doubling *r* multiplies sphere volume by 8, not 4).

#### Practice prompts
1. A hemisphere of radius 3 cm sits on a cylinder of the same radius and height 6 cm. Compute the total surface area of the combined solid.
2. A sphere of radius 6 cm is recast into a right circular cone of height 8 cm. Find the radius of the cone's base.
