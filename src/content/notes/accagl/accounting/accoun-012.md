# Standard Costing

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Standard costing records predetermined costs for materials, labour, and overhead, then compares them with actuals to surface **variances**. Management uses these variances to investigate causes and apply corrective action under absorption costing.

- **Material Price Variance** = (Standard Price − Actual Price) × Actual Quantity
- **Material Usage Variance** = (Standard Quantity − Actual Quantity) × Standard Price
- **Labour Rate Variance** = (Standard Rate − Actual Rate) × Actual Hours
- **Labour Efficiency Variance** = (Standard Hours − Actual Hours) × Standard Rate
- **Fixed Overhead Expenditure Variance** = Budgeted Fixed OH − Actual Fixed OH
- **Fixed Overhead Volume Variance** = (Std Hours for Actual Output − Budgeted Hours) × FOAR

Exam pointers: flex the budget to actual output before computing variances, label every variance F (favourable) or A (adverse), and reconcile standard cost of production with cost of sales using opening/closing inventory valuations.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Setting the standards
Standards are set per unit or per standard hour using historical data, engineering studies, or market benchmarks. Two common benchmarks appear in exam scenarios: **ideal standards** (best-ever conditions, no idle time, no waste) and **attainable standards** (efficient but achievable with normal spoilage and breaks). Attainable standards usually motivate staff better and produce variances that reflect genuine operational drift.

#### Material and labour variances
Materials split into price (purchasing responsibility) and usage (production responsibility). Labour splits into rate (personnel/hiring) and efficiency (floor supervision). The formulas above are applied directly; always state the variance in rupees and tag it F or A after subtraction.

#### Overhead variances under absorption costing
Variable overhead has an expenditure and an efficiency variance. Fixed overhead has an expenditure variance and a volume variance, because absorbed fixed cost depends on output volume. The volume variance captures idle capacity and efficiency of resource use.

#### Costing P&L reconciliation
A reconciliation statement bridges budgeted profit to actual profit by adjusting for sales volume variance, sales price variance, then each cost variance in turn.

| Variance type | Responsibility centre | Typical cause |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Material Price | Purchasing manager | Market price movement, supplier change |
| Material Usage | Production manager | Waste, poor quality, wrong mix |
| Labour Rate | HR / Hiring | Grade mix, overtime premiums |
| Labour Efficiency | Production supervisor | Skill level, motivation, batch size |
| Fixed OH Volume | Production / Capacity planning | Idle time, low output |

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge cases and flexed-budget logic
A common trap is comparing actual overhead to the original (un-flexed) budget. Always flex the budget to actual output first; otherwise the expenditure variance hides a volume effect. Under marginal costing, fixed overhead is a period cost, so only a single expenditure variance exists and the volume variance disappears.

#### Inter-relationship of variances
Total material variance equals price + usage. Total labour variance equals rate + efficiency. For fixed overhead, total = expenditure + volume. Reconciling these with the costing P&L requires the **sales volume variance × standard contribution per unit** plus a fixed overhead absorption adjustment when absorption costing is used.

#### Worked micro-example
Standard for Product X: 4 kg at Rs 50/kg material; 3 hours at Rs 200/hr labour; FOAR Rs 60/hr. Actual: 4,200 kg purchased at Rs 52 for 1,000 units output; 3,100 hours at Rs 210. Actual output = 1,000 units, so standard material allowed = 4,000 kg and standard hours allowed = 3,000.

| Variance | Calculation | Result (Rs.) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Material Price | (50 − 52) × 4,200 | 8,400 A |
| Material Usage | (4,000 − 4,200) × 50 | 10,000 A |
| Labour Rate | (200 − 210) × 3,100 | 31,000 A |
| Labour Efficiency | (3,000 − 3,100) × 200 | 20,000 A |

#### Practice prompts
1. Re-compute the worked example using attainable standards of 4.1 kg and 3.05 hours, and comment on how the usage/efficiency variances shift.
2. Prepare a reconciliation statement from budgeted profit to actual profit when given a flexed budget, actual results, and inventory movements valued at standard cost.
