# Narratives that Shape our World — HSC English Advanced (NESA 2024) Notes

Narratives that Shape our World is a Year 11 module in the HSC English Advanced syllabus. Students explore how narratives — literary, non-fiction, visual and multimodal — shape attitudes, values and understandings of self and others. The module is school-assessed in Year 11 and bridges into the Year 12 Common Module (Texts and Human Experiences).

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Module focus.** The ways narratives — across literary, non-fiction, visual and multimodal forms — shape the attitudes, values and understandings of individuals and societies.
- **Texts.** Schools select a range of narratives. There are no prescribed texts for Year 11.
- **Outcomes.** Students demonstrate understanding of the relationships between language, textual features and meaning; analyse how narratives position responders; compose texts that position an audience.
- **Assessment.** School-based tasks. Typically a textual analysis task and a composition task.

#### Examiner traps

- Treating narratives as a synonym for "stories" — narratives include documentary, essay, podcast, blog post and other non-fiction forms.
- Forgetting the responder in narrative analysis — the question is not just "what does the narrative say" but "what does the narrative make the reader feel, think and do."

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Narrative concepts

A narrative is a structured telling of events, real or imagined. Narrative can be literary (short story, novel, drama, poetry) or non-fiction (essay, memoir, biography, documentary, podcast). Multimodal narratives use a combination of text, image, sound and movement.

Narratives shape the world by:
- Constructing identities (national, cultural, gendered, classed).
- Reinforcing or challenging social norms.
- Carrying values across generations.
- Influencing policy and public debate.

#### Texts and contexts

Every text is produced in a context (time, place, culture, history). Context shapes both the form (a YouTube video is shaped differently from a Victorian novel) and the meaning (a war memoir means something different post-2022 than pre-2001).

Students should be able to identify how a text reflects, reinforces or challenges the values of its context, and how the text's meaning shifts when read in a different context.

#### Analytical framework

When analysing a narrative, consider:
- **Composer.** Who created the narrative? What is their context and purpose?
- **Form.** What medium and genre? What conventions of the form are used and subverted?
- **Content.** What is the narrative about — events, characters, themes?
- **Language features.** What are the dominant diction, imagery, syntax, sound patterns?
- **Values.** What values does the narrative reinforce or challenge?
- **Responder.** How does the text position its audience — what does it want the reader to feel, think or do?

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Why Narratives that Shape our World matters

- **It teaches the analysis framework.** The five-step framework (composer, form, content, language, responder) is the same framework used in every Year 12 module and in the HSC examination.
- **It bridges to Texts and Human Experiences.** The Year 12 Common Module asks students to consider how texts give insight into human experiences; Narratives that Shape our World gives students the foundation for this.
- **It develops composition skills.** The composition tasks in this module are similar in structure to the Year 12 Craft of Writing composition tasks.

#### Year 11 planning pattern

- **Term 1.** Introduce the analytical framework; analyse one literary and one non-fiction text.
- **Term 2.** Analyse a multimodal narrative (film, podcast, graphic novel). Practice composition in imaginative, persuasive and discursive forms.
- **Term 3.** Comparative analysis (two narratives on the same theme).
- **Term 4.** Revision and assessment.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Narratives that shape our world are always literary fiction." No — narratives include essay, memoir, documentary, podcast and other non-fiction.
- "The composer is always the author." For multimodal narratives the composer may be a team (writer + director + cinematographer).
- "Values are what the narrative is about." Values are what the narrative endorses or challenges. A narrative about war may or may not endorse war.
