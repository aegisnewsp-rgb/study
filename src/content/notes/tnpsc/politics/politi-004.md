# Traditional vs Modern Definition

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Politics** is the study of how groups make collective decisions through **power**, **authority** and **conflict resolution** within a **state**. The four dominant scholarly approaches are:

- **Traditional** (Aristotle): politics = the master science of the state.
- **Modern / behavioural** (David Easton): the authoritative allocation of values for a society.
- **Power view** (Robert Dahl): A has power over B when A can get B to do something B would not otherwise do.
- **Liberal** vs **Marxist**: deliberation and consent among interests, versus class struggle over the means of production.

> Exam tip: A common 5-mark question is "Define politics and distinguish between the traditional and modern views." Memorise Aristotle, Easton and Dahl verbatim.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Traditional vs Modern Definition

Aristotle called politics the "master science" because the state encompassed every other form of association. The traditional view therefore centres on the **state**, **sovereignty** and **government**. David Easton redefined it as the **authoritative allocation of values** for a society — shifting attention from institutions to the *process* of binding decisions.

#### The Power View

Robert Dahl's classic formulation: "A has power over B to the extent that A can get B to do something B would not otherwise do." This treats politics as observable behaviour rather than moral philosophy, and is the foundation of the behavioural approach.

#### Liberal and Marxist Views

The **liberal** view sees politics as reasoned discussion, compromise and the working of a "market of ideas" within a pluralist framework. The **Marxist** view treats politics as **superstructure** reflecting the **economic base**; the state is an instrument of the dominant class, and politics is the surface expression of **class conflict**.

#### Authority and Legitimacy

Max Weber distinguished **traditional**, **charismatic** and **rational-legal** authority. Power is the *capacity* to compel; authority is the *recognised right* to command; legitimacy is the *belief* in that rightfulness. Conflating these three is a frequent examiner trap.

| Concept | Core idea | Key thinker |
|---|---|---|
| Traditional politics | Master science of the state | Aristotle |
| Authoritative allocation of values | Binding collective decisions | David Easton |
| Power view | A compels B against B's preference | Robert Dahl |
| Marxist view | Class struggle over means of production | Marx & Engels |
| Types of authority | Traditional, charismatic, rational-legal | Max Weber |

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### State, Government and Regime

**State** refers to the permanent political association holding sovereignty over a territory. **Government** is the current set of persons exercising power. **Regime** denotes the rules, norms and institutions that structure political life. Conflating "change of government" with "change of state" or "change of regime" is a classic descriptive error in essays.

#### Pluralism and Political Culture

The liberal view rests on **pluralism** — multiple competing groups influencing policy through bargaining. **Political culture** (Almond and Verba) describes the orientations of citizens toward politics: parochial, subject or participant. These concepts connect Topic 4 to later units on Indian democracy and federalism.

#### Common Mistakes

- Writing that Marxists discuss *only* class; they also address **ideology**, **hegemony** (Gramsci) and the **state's relative autonomy**.
- Treating "politics" and "political science" as synonyms — the former is the activity, the latter its systematic study.
- Using "authority" where "power" is meant, and vice versa.
- Citing Dahl without naming the compulsory/inductive distinction between power, influence and authority.

#### Worked Essay Frame (10 marks)

> "Politics is the authoritative allocation of values for a society." (Easton) Compare this with the traditional and Marxist views. (200 words)

Body 1: state-centric Aristotelian view. Body 2: Easton's process-based shift, why values matter. Body 3: Marxist critique that values are shaped by class. Conclude: the three are complementary lenses, not rivals.

#### Practice Prompts

1. Distinguish **power**, **authority** and **legitimacy** using Weber's typology. (8 marks)
2. Critically examine the Marxist view that politics is the expression of class conflict. (15 marks)
