# Metallurgy

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Metallurgy is the science of extracting a metal from its ore and shaping it for use. The four stages are **concentration of ore**, **conversion to oxide/free metal**, **reduction**, and **refining**. Choice of reductant is read off the **Ellingham diagram** (ΔG° vs T plot for oxide formation); a lower line means a more stable oxide and a stronger reductant above its crossover.

For JEE Advanced numericals on refining, Faraday's law applies: m = (I·t·M) / (n·F), with F = 96485 C·mol⁻¹.

- **Calcination** → carbonate/hydrate ores, no SO₂ released.
- **Roasting** → sulphide ores, SO₂ released.
- **Hall-Héroult** electrolyses Al₂O₃ dissolved in molten **cryolite** (Na₃AlF₆) ≈ 950 °C.
- **Mond process** purifies Ni via volatile Ni(CO)₄.
- **Van Arkel** purifies Ti/Zr via volatile TiI₄.
- **Zone refining** purifies Si and Ge (semiconductors).
- **Cyanide process** dissolves Au using NaCN + O₂.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### The Four Stages

Every metallurgical flow has four blocks. First, **concentration**: hydraulic washing for heavy oxide ores, magnetic separation for magnetite (Fe₃O₄), froth flotation for sulphides, and leaching for bauxite (NaOH) or gold/silver ore (NaCN + O₂). Gangue is the worthless rocky matrix; **flux** is added later to convert gangue into **slag** (e.g., CaO + SiO₂ → CaSiO₃).

Second, **conversion to oxide**: carbonates/hydrates decompose on heating (**calcination**, e.g. CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂); sulphides oxidise to oxides + SO₂ (**roasting**, e.g. 2ZnS + 3O₂ → 2ZnO + 2SO₂). Calcination never releases SO₂ — that one fact kills an entire MCQ family.

#### Reduction and the Ellingham Diagram

Third, **reduction**: the reductant must form an oxide whose ΔG° lies *below* the metal oxide's line on the Ellingham diagram at the operating temperature (ΔG° overall < 0). Carbon is special because the C→CO line slopes **upward** (ΔS > 0 as gas moles go 1 → 2), so it eventually crosses most metal-oxide lines and becomes the dominant reductant at high T — exactly why coke reduces Fe₂O₃, ZnO, and SnO₂.

#### Refining Routes

Fourth, **refining** picks the method by purity target and metal chemistry.

| Method | Best for | Principle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Electrolytic | Cu, Zn | Anode = impure metal, cathode = pure strip, electrolyte = metal salt solution |
| Distillation | Zn, Hg, Ni(CO)₄ route | Boiling-point gap |
| Liquation | Sn, Pb | Metal melts and drains away from solid impurities |
| Zone refining | Si, Ge, Ga, In | Impurity partitioning between molten zone and solid |
| Van Arkel–de Boer | Ti, Zr, Hf | Metal + I₂ → volatile MI₄ → decomposes on hot W filament |
| Mond | Ni | Ni + 4CO → Ni(CO)₄ at 330 K, decomposes at 450 K |

Standard mistakes here: students swap anode/cathode in Cu refining, or assume cryolite is the "ore" in Hall-Héroult.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Reading Ellingham Diagrams with Precision

Each line on the diagram is the plot of ΔG° for one oxide formation reaction against T. Its **slope is −ΔS°**: more gas moles on the product side ⇒ larger positive ΔS ⇒ steeper negative slope (for most 2M + O₂ → 2MO, ΔS < 0, slope is positive). Carbon's two lines behave oppositely — C → CO (1 → 2 gas moles, slope **positive**) and C → CO₂ (1 → 1 gas moles overall in 2C + O₂ → 2CO₂, slope **negative**). Above the crossover with Al or Mg, carbon wins; below it, Al/Mg are thermodynamically superior, which is why **Aluminothermy** (Cr₂O₃ + 2Al → Al₂O₃ + 2Cr) works.

#### Faraday's Law in Refining Calculations

For Hall-Héroult, mass of Al deposited in time t at current I is m = (I·t·27) / (3·96485), since n = 3 for Al³⁺. For Cu refining, n = 2; for Ag, n = 1. Common trap: writing the formula with the atomic mass but using the valency of a different metal.

#### Specific Extraction Chains

- **Iron**: Fe₂O₃ → Fe₃O₄ → FeO → Fe in the blast furnace by CO; CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂; CaO + SiO₂ → CaSiO₃ (slag) floats off.
- **Copper**: CuFeS₂ roasted, then self-reduced by FeS; blister Cu cast as anode, refined electrolytically.
- **Aluminium**: Bayer process gives pure Al₂O₃ from bauxite; Hall-Héroult dissolves it in cryolite ≈ 950 °C; graphite anode is consumed (C + O₂ → CO/CO₂).

#### Worked Micro-Example

A Cu refining cell runs at I = 200 A for t = 9650 s. Mass deposited (n = 2, M = 63.5): m = (200 × 9650 × 63.5) / (2 × 96485) ≈ 635 g of pure Cu.

2. In Hall-Héroult, write the net cell reaction and explain the role and fate of each species (cryolite, graphite anode, Al₂O₃).
