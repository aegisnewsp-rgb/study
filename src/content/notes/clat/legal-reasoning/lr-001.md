# Legal Maxims

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Legal Maxims** are short Latin or English phrases that pack an entire legal rule into a few words, and the CLAT Legal Reasoning section uses them to test whether you can identify the right maxim for a fact pattern, not just translate it. Most maxim questions are scenario MCQs where a paragraph describes a dispute and you must pick the doctrine that resolves it.

- **Mens Rea** = guilty mind; **Actus Reus** = guilty act. Both are needed for most crimes under Section 6 IPC.
- **Audi Alteram Partem** = hear the other side; the procedural backbone of natural justice.
- **Nemo Judex In Causa Sua** = no one should be a judge in their own cause; the bias rule.
- **Volenti Non Fit Injuria** = consent to risk is a defence in tort, not in negligence or trespass.
- **Caveat Emptor** = buyer beware; displaced in modern consumer sales by the Consumer Protection Act, 2019.
- **Ignorantia Juris Non Excusat** = ignorance of law is no excuse, codified in Section 76 IPC.

| Maxim | English meaning | One-line use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Res Ipsa Loquitur | The thing speaks for itself | Negligence inferred from the very nature of the injury |
| Stare Decisis | To stand by decided cases | Binding-precedent rule followed by Indian courts |
| Bona Fide | In good faith | Shields honest acts from liability in many statutes |
| Pacta Sunt Servanda | Agreements must be kept | Foundation of contract enforcement |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Three families of maxims

Legal Maxims in CLAT fall into three functional groups. Recognising the family tells you which maxim the examiner is hinting at before you read the choices.

| Family | What it governs | Maxims to learn first |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Substantive criminal law | Guilt, liability, defences | Mens Rea, Actus Reus, Ignorantia Juris Non Excusat, Volenti Non Fit Injuria |
| Procedure and evidence | Fair trial, proof, judicial conduct | Audi Alteram Partem, Nemo Judex In Causa Sua, Res Ipsa Loquitur, Habeas Corpus |
| Contracts and property | Interpretation, transfer, risk | Pacta Sunt Servanda, Caveat Emptor, Noscitur a Sociis, Ejusdem Generis, Bona Fide |

#### Mapping maxims to Indian statutes

CLAT expects you to anchor each maxim to the statute that codifies it. A bare translation without a statutory hook is a half-answer and loses marks on assertion-reason items.

- **Mens Rea** → Section 6, Indian Penal Code 1860 (definition of "wrongful gain/loss"); general exceptions in Chapter IV.
- **Ignorantia Juris Non Excusat** → Section 76 IPC mistake-of-fact clause, read with the general rule in Tolentino's case.
- **Free consent** (linked to bona fide contract formation) → Section 10 and Sections 13–18, Indian Contract Act 1872.
- **Caveat Emptor** → Section 14, Sale of Goods Act 1930 implied conditions; substantially modified by the Consumer Protection Act 2019.
- **Audi Alteram Partem** → Article 14 read with the *Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India* (1978) procedural-fairness doctrine.

#### Reading a maxim-based CLAT passage

- Identify the legal **issue** in the first sentence (contract, crime, tort, evidence, administrative action).
- Note the **party relationship**: state vs accused, buyer vs seller, employer vs employee, judge vs litigant.
- Match the issue + relationship to one of the three families, then scan the choices for that family.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge cases and easy-to-confuse pairs

The CLAT setter earns marks by pairing two maxims that sound similar. Train yourself to separate them on first read.

1. **Audi Alteram Partem vs Nemo Judex In Causa Sua** — first is the *hearing* rule (you must listen), second is the *bias* rule (you must be disinterested). A fact pattern mentioning a one-sided hearing tests the first; a fact pattern mentioning a judge who owns shares in a litigant company tests the second.
2. **Mens Rea vs Actus Reus** — a sleeping pill administered by a doctor who forgot the patient was allergic satisfies Actus Reus but not Mens Rea unless negligence is added; CLAT scenarios often isolate only one element.
3. **Volenti Non Fit Injuria vs Contributory Negligence** — Volenti requires *free and knowing* consent to risk, not mere carelessness; contributory negligence is partial, Volenti is a complete defence.
4. **Caveat Emptor vs Consumer Protection Act, 2019** — Caveat still binds private sales of old goods, but any sale to a "consumer" as defined in Section 2(7) CPA attracts strict liability and a reverse burden.
5. **Pacta Sunt Servanda vs Rebus Sic Stantibus** — the first assumes stability of obligation; the second excuses performance when fundamental circumstances change. CLAT 2024–25 papers tested both in the same question.

#### Worked micro-example

A factory chimney falls on a passerby during a calm day. The plaintiff cannot show how the chimney fell. Which maxim applies?

- Apply the Res Ipsa Loquitur test: (i) the event ordinarily does not happen without negligence, (ii) the defendant had exclusive control, (iii) the plaintiff did not contribute. All three are satisfied here, so the burden shifts to the factory to disprove negligence.

#### Common traps and how to avoid them

| Trap | Why students fall for it | Correct reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Picking Volenti for any injury-with-consent scenario | Consent to medical surgery is not consent to negligent surgery | Volenti fails where the risk is unknown or the act is negligent |
| Translating "Audi" without linking to Article 14 | Forgetting that India elevates the maxim to a constitutional right | It is a *procedure* due under Article 14, not a mere practice |
| Treating "Bona Fide" as a defence everywhere | "Bona fide" only protects acts done honestly within scope | It does not cure an act that is *mala fide in law* (without authority) |

#### Practice prompts

- A landlord enters the tenant's flat without notice to inspect for damages. Identify the procedural maxim violated and the writ that lies against the landlord's intrusion.
- Ramu sells a car advertised as "accident-free" to Shamu, who later discovers it had been rebuilt after a major crash. Which maxim originally applied, and which 2019 statute displaces it?
