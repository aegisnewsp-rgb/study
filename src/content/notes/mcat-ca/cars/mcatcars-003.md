# Reasoning Beyond the Text — MCAT CARS Skill 3

Reasoning Beyond the Text is CARS Skill 3 in the AAMC CARS framework. It is the ability to apply the passage to new situations: drawing inferences, recognising analogies, identifying implications, and judging how a new scenario would be affected by the author's argument. This skill extends Skill 2 (Reasoning Within the Text) by stepping outside the passage to new contexts.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### What Skill 3 tests

- **Application:** items that ask how the author's argument would apply to a new situation not discussed in the passage.
- **Analogy:** items that ask which new situation is most analogous to the author's argument.
- **Implication:** items that ask what would follow if the author's argument is correct.
- **Extrapolation:** items that ask what the author would predict about a new phenomenon.
- **Counter-argument:** items that ask which new situation would most challenge the author's position.

#### One-pass drill

1. Read the passage once for the main idea (Skill 1) and the argument structure (Skill 2).
2. Identify the central claim and the evidence.
3. For each Skill 3 item, ask: "what would the author say about this new situation?"

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing Skill 2 with Skill 3. Skill 2 asks about the author's argument as presented. Skill 3 asks how the argument applies to new situations.
- Picking an answer that contradicts the author's central claim. The right answer is consistent with the author's view, even if applied to a new situation.
- Using your own view instead of the author's. The MCAT asks what the author would say, not what you think.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Application items

Application items present a new situation and ask which choice best applies the author's argument to it. To answer:

- Identify the central claim of the passage.
- Identify the new situation's key features.
- Apply the author's logic: would the author agree with this application, or disagree?

The right answer is the choice that most consistently extends the author's reasoning.

#### Analogy items

Analogy items present a new situation and ask which choice is most analogous to the passage. To answer:

- Identify the structural pattern of the passage: X leads to Y, with conditions A and B.
- Find the new situation that has the same structural pattern: P leads to Q, with conditions C and D.
- The right answer is the new situation whose structure most closely matches the passage's structure.

#### Implication items

Implication items ask "what would follow if the author's argument is correct?" To answer:

- State the author's claim.
- State the evidence.
- Extend the claim to a new context. The right answer is the choice that follows logically from the author's reasoning.

#### Practice routine

- Take one CARS passage. Identify the central claim and the structure.
- Answer the Skill 3 items. For each item, explain to yourself why the right answer is the most consistent with the author's argument.
- Time yourself: under 8 minutes per passage.
- Take 5 passages in a row. Repeat the routine.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How Skill 3 connects to the other CARS skills

- **Skill 1** identifies what the passage says. Skill 3 extends it to new contexts.
- **Skill 2** evaluates the argument as presented. Skill 3 evaluates how the argument applies elsewhere.
- **Skill 4 (Quantitative Reasoning)** often uses Skill 3 to apply numerical reasoning to new situations.

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"Skill 3 items are subjective."** Skill 3 items have a single correct answer. The right answer is the choice most consistent with the author's argument.
- **"I should use my own view."** CARS items ask about the author's view. Use the passage's claims and evidence as the basis.
- **"Any new situation works."** The right answer is the new situation that most consistently extends the author's reasoning.

#### Exam technique

- For application items, ask: "what would the author say about this new situation?"
- For analogy items, ask: "which new situation has the same structural pattern as the passage?"
- For implication items, ask: "what would follow if the author's argument is correct?"
- Eliminate choices that contradict the author's central claim.
- Use the platform's flag-for-review function for items you cannot answer.
