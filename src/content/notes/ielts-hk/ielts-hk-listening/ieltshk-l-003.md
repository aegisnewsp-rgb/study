# Part 3 — Academic Discussion — IELTS Listening (Hong Kong) Notes

Part 3 of the IELTS Listening test (administered in Hong Kong by the British Council or IDP) is a conversation between two main speakers in an educational or training context — typically university students discussing an assignment with a tutor, two students discussing a research project, or a supervisor and a student discussing feedback. The recording runs about 5-7 minutes and has 10 questions. Tests multiple choice, matching, flow-chart completion, and short-answer questions on argumentative and analytical exchanges.

> Verify the live Listening format on https://ielts.org/ before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Format:** 10 questions.
- **Transcript style:** conversation between two or more speakers in an academic context (e.g. tutor-student, two students, supervisor-researcher).
- **Topics:** assignment requirements, research methodology, course content, academic concepts, feedback on work.
- **Test types:** multiple choice (with 3 or 4 options), matching, flow-chart completion, short-answer questions.
- **Vocabulary:** academic, but the pace is similar to Parts 1 and 2.
- **Argumentation:** speakers may present opposing views or discuss pros and cons.

#### Examiner traps

- Missing the main idea because the speakers are discussing multiple related topics.
- Confusing the speakers' opinions — "I think X is..." vs "John thinks X is...".
- Misunderstanding hedging language ("I sort of think...", "I guess...").

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Common Part 3 contexts

- **Tutor and student:** discussing an assignment, research project, or thesis.
- **Two students:** discussing a group project, lab work, or field study.
- **Supervisor and researcher:** discussing research methodology, findings, or feedback.
- **Student services:** discussing study skills, time management, or academic support.

#### Question types in Part 3

- **Multiple choice (3 or 4 options):** the most common type. The question may ask about the speakers' opinions, the main idea, or a specific detail.
- **Matching:** match speakers to their opinions, or match items to categories.
- **Flow-chart completion:** complete a process diagram with words from the recording.
- **Short-answer questions:** answer open-ended questions about the discussion.

#### Following multi-party discussions

- **Identify the speakers:** typically two or three named speakers (e.g. "John", "Sarah", "the tutor").
- **Track who says what:** pay attention to voice changes and names.
- **Recognise opinion markers:** "I think...", "I believe...", "I would say...", "in my view...".
- **Recognise hedging language:** "I sort of think...", "I guess...", "I'm not sure, but...".
- **Recognise agreement/disagreement:** "exactly", "I agree", "I see your point", "I don't think so", "actually...".

#### Common Part 3 topics

- **Education:** teaching methods, assessment, curriculum design.
- **Research:** methodology, data collection, analysis, findings.
- **Study skills:** time management, note-taking, exam preparation.
- **Workplace:** training, professional development, team dynamics.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-paper links

- **Part 4**: academic lecture; same vocabulary but longer and more complex.
- **Reading**: academic vocabulary and inference.
- **Writing Task 2**: argumentative essays on similar topics.

#### Exam technique

- For multiple choice, eliminate distractors that are mentioned in the recording but do not match the question.
- For matching, track each speaker's opinion carefully.
- For short-answer questions, answer in the words from the recording and within the word limit.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Part 3 is about academic topics, so the vocabulary is too difficult." The vocabulary is academic, but the pace is similar to Parts 1 and 2.
- "Both speakers agree on everything." Not always — Part 3 often includes disagreement or compromise.
