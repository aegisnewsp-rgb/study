# Methods of Teaching

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

A **teaching method** is the overall strategy a teacher plans to deliver a lesson, while a **teaching technique** is the specific activity used inside that strategy. NCE examiners test your ability to classify methods, list their steps, and justify a choice for a given topic.

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Method | Broad, planned strategy covering a whole lesson | Discussion method |
| Technique | Specific classroom activity inside a method | Buzz group, snowballing |
| Strategy | Combination of methods and techniques for an objective | Mixed-mode delivery |
| Approach | Underlying philosophy guiding the teacher | Learner-centred approach |
| Pedagogy | The art and science of teaching as a discipline | Andragogy, heutagogy |

- **Method ≠ Technique**: the method is the roadmap, the technique is the vehicle.
- **Project method** is credited to **Kilpatrick**, while the **activity method** is attributed to **Dewey**.
- Choice of method depends on **O-C-L-T-R-E**: Objectives, Content, Learners, Teacher, Resources, Evaluation.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** "**POLICED Steps**" — for any method, write it as **P**reparation, **O**bjectives, **L**esson delivery, **I**nstructional aids, **C**lass activities, **E**valuation, **D**ismissal. Works for lesson planning AND method description.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Classification of Methods

Methods fall along a spectrum from **teacher-centred** to **learner-centred**. NCE Education frequently asks you to place a named method on this spectrum and to defend the placement.

| Method | Centre of gravity | Key theorist | Best-suited content |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lecture | Teacher | Traditional | Introductory concepts, large audiences |
| Demonstration | Teacher → learner | Hall | Psychomotor skills, science experiments |
| Discussion | Learner | Dewey, Parker | Social studies, literature, ethics |
| Discovery / Inquiry | Learner | Bruner, Suchman | Sciences, problem analysis |
| Project | Learner | Kilpatrick | Real-life integrated tasks |
| Problem-Solving | Learner | Dewey, Polya | Mathematics, sciences |
| Socratic / Heuristic | Learner (question-led) | Socrates | Philosophy, critical reasoning |
| Field trip / Excursion | Environment | Dewey | Geography, biology, environmental studies |

#### Steps Common to Most Methods

1. **Preparation** — set objectives, gather aids, arrange the room.
2. **Introduction / Motivation** — arouse interest or recall prior knowledge.
3. **Presentation / Development** — deliver the main body of the lesson.
4. **Application / Activity** — learners practise or transfer the learning.
5. **Evaluation** — oral questions, tests, observation of performance.
6. **Assignment / Closure** — extend learning beyond the classroom.

> 📌 **Formula Check:** There are no numeric formulas here. The "formula" is the **O-C-L-T-R-E selector**: a defensible method choice must satisfy all six — Objectives, Content, Learners, Teacher competence, Resources, Evaluation style.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** A Primary Education student teacher is asked to teach the topic "Sources of Water in the Local Community" to a Primary 4 class of 40 pupils in a rural school with no laboratory. Using the **O-C-L-T-R-E selector**, justify the most appropriate teaching method, and outline any three steps the teacher must follow.

#### Solution:

1. **Identify the constraints first** — 40 pupils, rural school, no laboratory, Primary 4 (ages 8–10, concrete operational stage).
2. **Apply O-C-L-T-R-E:**
   - **O**bjectives: pupils should identify, locate, and list sources of water in the community.
   - **C**ontent: real, observable features in the pupils' locality (streams, wells, boreholes, rainwater).
   - **L)**earners: concrete thinkers who learn best through direct experience.
   - **T)**eacher: must work within available means; cannot demonstrate chemistry of water purification.
   - **R)**esources: limited; relies on the environment itself.
   - **E)**valuation: requires pupils to report what they observed.
3. **Select the method: Field Trip (Excursion method).** It is learner-centred, uses the environment as the laboratory, suits concrete thinkers, and is low-cost.
4. **State three steps:**
   - **Preparation:** visit the site beforehand, brief pupils on behaviour and safety, prepare a recording sheet.
   - **On-site development:** guide pupils to observe each source, ask probing questions, take notes.
   - **Follow-up:** in class, pupils compare notes, draw a community water map, and answer evaluation questions.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Many candidates pick the **Demonstration method** because it is "practical." Demonstration requires a controlled setting with apparatus — unavailable here. Read the resource list before defaulting to the most popular practical method.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Conceptual Boundaries

| Confusion pair | Real distinction | Why NCE tests it |
|---|---|---|
| Method vs Technique | Method = lesson-wide strategy; Technique = specific activity | A 5-mark question often hinges on this |
| Project vs Activity method | Project = Kilpatrick, ends with a real product; Activity = Dewey, child-initiated learning by doing | Examiners penalise attaching Dewey to the project method |
| Discovery vs Problem-Solving | Discovery = learner finds the rule; Problem-Solving = learner applies a known rule to a new situation | Common MCQ trap |
| Lecture vs Discussion | Lecture = one-way talk ≥ 20 min; Discussion = interactive dialogue | Writing a "lecture" plan under a discussion question loses marks |
| Socratic vs Heuristic | Socratic = general questioning; Heuristic = a specific discovery-questioning sequence (Armstrong) | Socratic is older; Heuristic is the systematic discovery variant |

#### Common Mistakes Examiners Penalise

1. **Citing theorists incorrectly** — Kilpatrick (project), Bruner (discovery), Dewey (activity, problem-solving, discussion), Suchman (inquiry training).
2. **Omitting limitations** — lecture has low retention; field trip is weather-dependent; discussion can be dominated by vocal pupils.
3. **Writing methods without linking to learners' age and stage** — Piaget's stages drive method choice more than the topic name.
4. **Ignoring inclusive education** — current NCE standards require methods that accommodate learners with special needs (UDL: Universal Design for Learning).
5. **Mixing method with approach** — "learner-centred approach" is a philosophy; "discovery method" is a strategy operating under that philosophy.

#### Two Advanced Practice Prompts

1. A JSS2 Social Studies teacher must teach "Effects of Colonial Rule in Nigeria" to 35 learners using only a chalkboard and a textbook. Justify either the **Discussion method** or the **Lecture method** using O-C-L-T-R-E, and explain why the rejected method would fail here.
2. Design a 35-minute lesson on "Simple Equations" for JSS1 using the **Problem-Solving method**. State the objective, list Polya's four steps (**Understand → Devise → Carry out → Review**), and write one evaluation question per step.

> 💡 **High-Yield Revision Hook:** Before naming any method on an NCE paper, run **O-C-L-T-R-E** in your head. If a constraint (no lab, large class, abstract content) blocks the method, the examiner wants you to **switch and justify** — not force the method.
