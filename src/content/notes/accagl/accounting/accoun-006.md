# Final Accounts

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Final Accounts are the end-of-period statements that show **gross profit**, **net profit**, and the **financial position** of a sole trader, partnership, or limited company. The Trading Account derives gross profit, the Profit and Loss Account derives net profit, and the Balance Sheet (Statement of Financial Position) lists assets, equity and liabilities. ACCA FA2 / CA Pakistan Foundation Financial Accounting routinely tests these from a trial balance plus adjustments.

- **Gross Profit** = Net Sales − Cost of Goods Sold (Opening Inventory + Net Purchases + Direct Expenses − Closing Inventory).
- **Net Profit** = Gross Profit + Other Income − Operating Expenses − Finance Costs − Tax.
- **Accounting Equation**: Assets = Equity (Capital + Retained Earnings) + Liabilities.
- **Working Capital** = Current Assets − Current Liabilities.
- Capital items belong in the Balance Sheet; revenue items only enter Trading/P&L.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Purpose and Linkage of the Three Statements
Final Accounts translate a trial balance into decision-useful figures. The Trading Account matches revenue from sales against the cost of those sales to give **gross profit**. The Profit and Loss Account then matches gross profit against operating expenses, finance costs and tax to give **net profit**. The Balance Sheet closes the loop by showing how that profit (plus capital and liabilities) has financed the assets held at the period-end. Under the **accruals (matching) concept**, each item sits in the period it relates to, not the period cash moves.

#### Key Adjustments Tested
Year-end adjustments convert a trial balance into IFRS/CA-Pakistan compliant final accounts. Provision for doubtful debts and depreciation reduce profit and the carrying value of the related asset. Accruals and prepayments re-cut expenses into the correct period. Closing inventory, valued at the **lower of cost and net realisable value** under prudence, reduces COGS and appears under current assets.

| Adjustment | Effect on P&L | Effect on Balance Sheet |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Closing inventory | Reduces COGS, raises gross profit | Debit Current Assets |
| Depreciation | Debit expense | Reduce accumulated depreciation (contra-asset) |
| Accrued expense | Debit expense | Credit Current Liability |
| Prepaid expense | Credit expense | Debit Current Asset |
| Provision for doubtful debts (increase) | Debit expense | Credit allowance under receivables |

#### Capital vs Revenue Distinction
Capital expenditure (machinery, long-term improvements) is **capitalised** to the Balance Sheet and depreciated over its useful life. Revenue expenditure (repairs, consumables) hits the P&L in full. Misclassifying a capital item as revenue **suppresses profit and understates assets** — a common examiner trap.

#### Partnership Addition
Partnership Final Accounts append an **Appropriation Account** after the P&L. Net profit is split: partner salary, interest on capital, interest on drawings (deducted), and the residual shared in the **profit-sharing ratio**.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Manufacturing Account and Cost Build-Up
When a business produces rather than just buys goods, a **Manufacturing Account** sits before the Trading Account. It accumulates raw materials consumed, direct labour and factory overheads to give **Cost of Goods Manufactured**, which then feeds the Trading Account alongside opening and closing finished-goods inventories. CA Pakistan Inter and ACCA FA2 questions often blend raw materials with purchases, so label each line clearly.

#### Common Mistakes and Examiner Traps

1. Entering closing inventory as both a credit in COGS and a debit in current assets without netting — this **double-counts** it.
2. Treating bad debts recovered as a Trading Account credit; recoveries are **operating income** in the P&L.
3. Posting partner drawings against capital in the Appropriation Account instead of deducting **interest on drawings** correctly.
4. Forgetting **partnership salary** is an appropriation, not a charge against gross profit — placing it above net profit inflates working capital wrongly.

#### Mini Worked Example (figures in Rs)
Opening Inventory 40,000; Net Purchases 220,000; Direct Wages 60,000; Carriage Inward 10,000; Closing Inventory 30,000.

| Line | Rs |
| --- | --- |
| Opening Inventory | 40,000 |
| Add: Purchases + Direct Expenses | 290,000 |
| Less: Closing Inventory | (30,000) |
| **COGS** | **300,000** |

Net Sales of 500,000 then yields **Gross Profit = 200,000**, which flows into the P&L.

#### Exam Strategy
Final Accounts questions at FA2 / CAT carry 10–20 marks and sit in Section A. Read every adjustment first, mark accruals (+) and prepayments (−), then build the Trading Account top-down. Always **re-check the Balance Sheet balance** by computing working capital and confirming Assets = Capital + Liabilities to within rounding.

#### Practice Prompts
1. From a given trial balance and a list of eight adjustments, prepare the Trading, P&L and Balance Sheet of a sole trader, then compute working capital.
2. Re-prepare the above as a two-partner partnership with a 3:2 profit-sharing ratio, salary of Rs 120,000 to Partner A, and 10% interest on capital of Rs 400,000.
