# Directive Principles of State Policy

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> The single most-tested fact: DPSP sit in Part IV, run from Article 36 to Article 51, and cannot be enforced in court.

Directive Principles are the Constitution's policy **wishlist** for the State. Part IV lists 16 directives that push India toward a welfare, social, economic, and political democracy. Article 37 calls them **fundamental in governance** and **duty-bound on the State** — yet the same article makes them **non-justiciable**. No citizen can drag the government to court for failing to deliver Article 44 (Uniform Civil Code) or Article 41 (right to work).

The State, as defined in Article 36, mirrors Article 12: the Union, every State government, and every local or other authority within India.

- **Socialist principles** — Articles 38, 39, 39A, 41, 42, 43, 43A, 43B
- **Gandhian principles** — Articles 40, 46, 47, 48
- **Liberal-Intellectual principles** — Articles 44, 45, 48, 48A, 49, 50, 51

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** "**S**ocialist, **G**andhian, **L**iberal — **SGL** like a **signal** the State must obey (in governance, not in court)."

Three judges every Prelims paper touches: **Minerva Mills (1980)**, **Champakam Dorairajan (1951)**, and **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)**.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Build the mechanism first — once the "apply, but cannot sue" logic clicks, article-by-article recall becomes far easier.

#### How DPSP Operate Inside the Constitution

Dr. B.R. Ambedkar described Part IV as a "novel feature" and an "instrument of instructions" borrowed from the Irish Constitution. The Irish influence flows through Article 45 of the Irish Constitution, which inspired Article 39(b) and (c) on wealth distribution.

Article 37 does the heavy lifting. It declares that the provisions in Part IV are **fundamental in the governance of the country** and that it shall be the **duty of the State to apply these principles while making laws**. The catch: a directive "shall not be enforceable by any court". Courts can still read them while deciding Fundamental Rights cases — that is the doctrinal bridge between Part III and Part IV.

#### Three-Class Taxonomy

The 16 articles cluster into three ideological groups, and KAS questions routinely ask which article belongs where.

| Category | Articles | Ideological Anchor | Sample Directive |
|---|---|---|---|
| Socialist | 38, 39, 39A, 41, 42, 43, 43A | Social & economic equality | Equal pay for equal work (39), living wage (43), free legal aid (39A) |
| Gandhian | 40, 46, 47, 48 | Village republic, prohibition, cow protection | Village Panchayats (40), SC/ST education (46), intoxicant ban (47) |
| Liberal-Intellectual | 44, 45, 48, 48A, 49, 50, 51 | Modernist state-building | Uniform Civil Code (44), judiciary-executive separation (50), international peace (51) |

The **42nd Amendment (1976)** inserted Articles 39A, 43B, and 48A. The **44th Amendment (1978)** added Article 38(2).

#### Resolving the FR–DPSP Conflict

| Case | Year | Holding on DPSP vs FR |
|---|---|---|
| Champakam Dorairajan | 1951 | Reserved seats in educational institutions struck down; DPSP yielded to FR |
| Golaknath | 1967 | Parliament cannot amend FR to override DPSP |
| Kesavananda Bharati | 1973 | Harmony between FR and DPSP is part of the basic structure |
| Minerva Mills | 1980 | Harmony and balance between FR and DPSP is a basic feature; struck down parts of 42nd Amendment |

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** A KAS Prelims MCQ asks — *"Which Article of the Indian Constitution provides for the Uniform Civil Code?"* — with options (a) Article 44 (b) Article 45 (c) Article 48 (d) Article 51.

#### Step-by-step resolution:
1. Recall the Liberal-Intellectual cluster: 44 (UCC), 45 (early childhood care), 48 (agriculture/animal husbandry, cow slaughter), 51 (international peace).
2. Match "Uniform Civil Code" — a code of personal laws applicable to all religious communities — directly to Article 44.
3. Reject Article 45 (children below 6), reject Article 48 (cow slaughter, agriculture), reject Article 51 (international peace, ICC). Step 3 eliminates every distractor.
4. **Answer: (a) Article 44.**

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Article 44 (UCC) and Article 51 (international law) are both highly asked single-Article questions and students routinely swap them. Trick questions will phrase Article 51 as "respect for international law and treaty obligations" to lure you away from 44.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Stop memorising lists. Start asking: which directive has actually bitten the State in court, and which one is still a paper tiger?

#### Constitutional Edge Cases

Article 31C, inserted by the **25th Amendment (1971)** and partially saved then clipped by Minerva Mills, gave laws giving effect to Article 39(b) and (c) priority over Articles 14, 19, and 31. Minerva Mills clipped this shield by striking down the second part of Article 31C, so today only Article 39(b) and (c) directives enjoy that immunity — and only against Article 14 and 19 challenges.

Article 38(2), added by the **44th Amendment (1978)**, quietly lowered the welfare bar from "promote" to "minimise inequalities" and explicitly addressed income, status, facilities, and opportunities. This was the Janata government's response to the overreach of the 42nd Amendment.

#### Why DPSP Still Bite — Despite Being Non-Justiciable

Courts have used DPSP to:

- Read the **Right to Education (Article 21-A)** into the **86th Amendment (2002)** and the **RTE Act, 2009**, fulfilling the old Article 45 promise.
- Justify **free legal aid** mandates under Article 39A in *Hussainara Khatoon v. Home Secretary, Bihar* (1979).
- Frame the **right to clean environment** as a duty traceable to Article 48A in *Subhash Kumar v. State of Bihar* (1991).
- Order **separation of judiciary** under Article 50 — still incomplete in many states.

#### Frequent KAS-Specific Traps

1. Treating DPSP as **enforceable**. Article 37 makes the duty binding on the State, but courts cannot issue a writ of mandamus asking the government to "enact a Uniform Civil Code".
2. Forgetting the **42nd Amendment trio**: 39A, 43B, 48A. KAS Prelims has tested these insertions in 2022 and 2024.
3. Confusing **Article 40** (Panchayats) with **Article 50** (Judiciary separation). Both are administrative reforms, but 40 is Gandhian and 50 is Liberal-Intellectual.
4. Labelling Article 41 (right to work) as enforceable. The Supreme Court has rejected this in *Ashok Kumar v. Union of India*.
5. Assuming DPSP apply only to the **Union**. Article 36's definition of "State" drags every Karnataka department, ZP, and municipality inside Part IV.

#### Two Advanced Practice Prompts

1. **Mains-style 10-mark question:** *"Examine the judicial balancing of Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles with reference to Minerva Mills and Kesavananda Bharati."* — Structure: define FR (Part III) and DPSP (Part IV), narrate the tension, cite both cases, conclude with the harmony doctrine.
2. **Prelims-style assertion-reason:** *Assertion* — Article 44 enables Parliament to impose a Uniform Civil Code. *Reason* — DPSP are enforceable in court. Both are individually true, but the Reason is not the correct explanation of the Assertion.
