# Problem Solving and Data Analysis — Statistics and Probability — SAT Math Notes

Statistics and probability are part of the SAT Math Problem Solving and Data Analysis content domain. The SAT tests one-variable and two-variable statistics, scatterplots and line of best fit, conditional probability, independent events, and basic probability rules.

> Verify the live SAT specifications on https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/ before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Mean:** sum / count.
- **Median:** the middle value (or mean of two middle values) when data are ordered.
- **Mode:** the most frequent value.
- **Standard deviation:** measure of spread; larger means more spread.
- **Scatterplot:** a graph of paired data points (x, y).
- **Line of best fit:** the line that best represents the trend in a scatterplot.
- **Probability of an event:** P(A) = (favourable outcomes) / (total outcomes).
- **Independent events:** P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B).

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing "mean" with "median."
- Treating dependent events as independent.

---

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Measures of center and spread

- **Mean:** sensitive to outliers.
- **Median:** robust to outliers.
- **Standard deviation:** quantifies spread; roughly the average distance from the mean.

#### Line of best fit

The line of best fit minimises the sum of squared vertical distances from the points to the line. The slope of the line indicates the direction and strength of the relationship.

#### Probability rules

- **Complement:** P(not A) = 1 - P(A).
- **Independent events:** P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B).
- **Conditional probability:** P(A | B) = P(A and B) / P(B).

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where Statistics and Probability appears across the SAT Math section

Statistics and probability items appear throughout the SAT Math section. They test the candidate's ability to interpret data and apply probability rules.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Mean and median are the same." No — mean is the average; median is the middle value.
- "All events are independent." No — some events depend on each other.

#### Exam technique

- For measures of center, identify which one is required.
- For scatterplot problems, find the line of best fit and use its equation.
- For probability problems, identify the formula (independent, conditional, etc.).

#### Step-by-step worked College Board test example

**Problem**:
Which of the following expressions is equivalent to (x^2 - 9) / (2x^2 + 7x + 3) for all values of x where the denominator is non-zero?

**Solution**:
1. Factor the numerator using the difference of squares:
   x^2 - 9 = (x - 3)(x + 3).
2. Factor the denominator quadratic:
   Look for two numbers that multiply to (2)(3) = 6 and sum to 7: the numbers are 6 and 1.
   Rewrite: 2x^2 + 6x + x + 3 = 2x(x + 3) + 1(x + 3) = (2x + 1)(x + 3).
3. Simplify the rational expression by canceling the common factor (x + 3):
   [(x - 3)(x + 3)] / [(2x + 1)(x + 3)] = (x - 3) / (2x + 1).
4. Note the restriction: x != -3 and x != -1/2. The simplified rational expression is (x - 3) / (2x + 1).
