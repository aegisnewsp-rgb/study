# Ghana Economy and Trade

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

- **Definition:** Ghana Economy and Trade describes the structure of Ghana's production system (agriculture, industry, services), the goods it sells abroad and buys in, and the institutions and blocs that regulate money and commerce.
- **Currency:** Legal tender is the **Ghana cedi (GHS)**, subdivided into 100 pesewas; the cedi trades under a managed-float regime set by the **Bank of Ghana**.
- **Top exports:** **Gold, cocoa beans and cocoa products, crude oil, timber, and cashew**; Ghana historically earns more from cocoa and gold than from oil alone.
- **Top imports:** Machinery, refined petroleum, vehicles, chemicals, rice, and medicines — driving a chronic trade deficit.
- **Key institutions:** **COCOBOD** (cocoa), **GRA** (revenue), **GIPC** (investment), **Bank of Ghana** (monetary policy).
- **Blocs:** Member of **ECOWAS** since 1975 and host of the **AfCFTA Secretariat** in Accra.
- **Headline formula:** GDP = C + I + G + (X − M); a negative (X − M) signals the trade deficit weighing on growth.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Structure of the Economy
Ghana's economy is conventionally split into three sectors. **Agriculture** employs roughly a quarter of the labour force and produces cocoa (the traditional cash crop), cassava, yam, maize, and fish. **Industry** is dominated by mining of gold, manganese, bauxite, and crude oil from the Jubilee and TEN fields, alongside small-scale manufacturing (cement, beverages, textiles). **Services** is now the largest contributor to GDP, driven by banking, telecommunications (MTN, Vodafone Ghana, AirtelTigo), retail, and transport.

#### Exports, Imports, and the Trade Balance
Exports are concentrated in a few primary commodities, exposing Ghana to **commodity price volatility**.

| Category | Main Items | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Exports | Gold, cocoa beans, cocoa paste/butter, crude oil, timber, cashew | Cocoa and gold historically the largest foreign-exchange earners |
| Imports | Machinery, refined petroleum, vehicles, chemicals, rice, medicines | Heavy fuel and capital-goods reliance |
| Balance of Trade | BoT = Exports − Imports | Usually **negative** → trade deficit |

> **Trap:** A persistent deficit is not proof of collapse. It often reflects **import-dependent industrialisation** financed by FDI, remittances, and external borrowing.

#### Institutions and Policy Tools
- **Bank of Ghana** — central bank; issues the cedi, sets the **monetary policy rate**, and manages reserves.
- **Ministry of Finance** — drafts the budget, fiscal policy, and external debt management.
- **Ghana Cocoa Board (COCOBOD)** — sole regulator of cocoa purchasing, quality, and export marketing; pre-finances the crop.
- **Ghana Revenue Authority (GRA)** — collects VAT, income tax, and customs duties.
- **Ghana Investment Promotion Centre (GIPC)** — registers foreign investors and tracks FDI inflows.
- **Ghana Stock Exchange (GSE)** — listings include GCB, AngloGold Ashanti, MTN Ghana, and TotalEnergies Marketing Ghana.

#### Regional Trade Frameworks
Ghana belongs to **ECOWAS** (15-member West African bloc promoting free movement and a common external tariff). In 2021 it hosted the launch of the **African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA)** in Accra — the world's largest free-trade area by membership, complementing ECOWAS at a continental scale.

#### Currency and Price Stability
The cedi has depreciated sharply against the US dollar since 2022, raising import costs and inflation. Inflation is measured by the Ghana Statistical Service's **CPI**, and the BoG uses policy rates and FX interventions to anchor expectations.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Macro Mechanics and the Trade Identity
The expenditure identity **GDP = C + I + G + (X − M)** ties the trade balance directly to growth. When (X − M) is negative and large, GDP growth must lean on consumption, investment, and government spending. The **Real GDP** deflator step — Real GDP = Nominal GDP ÷ (GDP Deflator ÷ 100) — strips out inflation to reveal whether output actually expanded.

#### Edge Cases and Common Errors
> **Distinction examiners exploit:** Balance of Trade covers **goods only**; the **Current Account** adds services, primary income (interest, dividends), and transfers (remittances). Confusing the two costs marks.

- Do not equate **GHS** with the pre-1965 Ghanaian pound or with the **CFA franc** (used by Francophone West Africa, not Ghana).
- AfCFTA **complements** ECOWAS — it does not replace the West African bloc.
- COCOBOD's pre-financing syndicate is why cocoa exports are unusually stable for a primary commodity.
- The **GSE Composite Index** is denominated in GHS; foreign investors face currency risk.

#### Worked Micro-Example
Suppose Ghana records Exports = USD 18 billion and Imports = USD 24 billion in a year.

- **BoT** = 18 − 24 = **−USD 6 billion** → trade deficit.
- **Trade Ratio** = (18 ÷ 24) × 100 = **75%** → exports cover only three-quarters of imports.
- If Nominal GDP = GHS 600 billion and the GDP deflator = 150, then **Real GDP** = 600 ÷ (150 ÷ 100) = **GHS 400 billion**.

#### Exam Strategy (GAT, 3% weight)
Expect one or two MCQs on: top exports (cocoa and gold, not just oil), currency (GHS, 100 pesewas), the central bank (**Bank of Ghana**), COCOBOD's monopoly on cocoa marketing, ECOWAS membership since 1975, and Accra hosting the **AfCFTA Secretariat**. Memorise which body does what — confusing BoG with the Ministry of Finance is a classic wrong-answer lure.

#### Practice Prompts
1. State two reasons why a persistent trade deficit does not automatically signal economic failure in Ghana.
2. Differentiate between the **Balance of Trade** and the **Current Account**, and identify which component remittances belong to.
