# VII. Atomic World (Elective) — HKDSE Physics Notes

This topic covers Elective Topic VII of the HKDSE Physics Elective Part. Candidates sit any two of the four electives on Paper 2. The topic addresses Rutherford's atomic model and the limits of classical physics; the photoelectric effect and Einstein's explanation; Bohr's's atomic model of hydrogen and atomic spectra; wave-particle duality (de Broglie waves, electron diffraction); and probing the nano scale (scanning tunnelling microscope, atomic force microscope).

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Rutherford's nuclear model**: the atom has a small, dense, positively charged nucleus surrounded by mostly empty space (Rutherford scattering experiment, 1911). Electrons orbit the nucleus like planets around the Sun.
- **Limits of classical physics**: classical electromagnetism predicts that an accelerating charge (orbiting electron) should radiate energy, lose energy and spiral into the nucleus. But atoms are stable. Classical physics fails at the atomic scale.
- **Planck's quantum hypothesis (1900)**: energy is emitted and absorbed in discrete packets (quanta), with energy proportional to frequency: E = hf.
- **Photoelectric effect**: light incident on a metal surface causes electrons to be ejected. The kinetic energy of the ejected electrons depends on the frequency of light, not the intensity. There is a threshold frequency below which no electrons are ejected, regardless of intensity.
- **Einstein's explanation (1905)**: light is composed of photons (energy packets). Each photon has energy E = hf. An electron is ejected if the photon energy exceeds the work function φ; the maximum KE of the ejected electron is hf - φ.
- **Bohr's model of the atom (1913)**: electrons orbit the nucleus in fixed, quantised orbits. Electrons can jump between orbits by absorbing or emitting photons of energy ΔE = hf. The angular momentum is quantised: L = nℏ (n = 1, 2, 3, ...).
- **Hydrogen spectrum**: the visible Balmer series (Hα red, Hβ blue-green, etc.), the UV Lyman series, the IR Paschen series. Each series corresponds to transitions to a specific lower orbit.
- **de Broglie's hypothesis (1924)**: every particle has a wavelength λ = h/p, where p is momentum. For electrons with energy in the keV range, the wavelength is ~ 0.01-1 nm, comparable to atomic spacing.
- **Wave-particle duality**: light behaves as both a wave (interference, diffraction) and a particle (photoelectric effect). Matter also has wave-like properties (electron diffraction, double-slit experiment with electrons).
- **Scanning tunnelling microscope (STM)**: probes surfaces with atomic resolution using quantum tunnelling.
- **Atomic force microscope (AFM)**: probes surfaces by measuring the force between a sharp tip and the surface.

#### Examiner traps

- Treating light as purely a wave or purely a particle — light has wave-particle duality.
- Forgetting that the photoelectric threshold frequency is a property of the metal, not of the light.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### The quantum nature of light

**Blackbody radiation**: a perfect absorber (blackbody) emits radiation with a continuous spectrum that depends only on temperature. Classical physics (Rayleigh-Jeans law) predicted infinite intensity at high frequencies (the "ultraviolet catastrophe"). Planck resolved this by quantising the energy of the oscillators: E = hf.

**Photoelectric effect**:

- Threshold frequency f₀: the minimum frequency below which no electrons are ejected. Related to the work function: φ = hf₀.
- Maximum kinetic energy of ejected electrons: KE_max = hf - φ.
- The intensity of the light affects the number of ejected electrons, not their energy.

The photoelectric effect demonstrates the particle nature of light — light energy is delivered in quant packets (photons).

#### Bohr's model of the atom

Bohr postulated:

1. Electrons orbit the nucleus in fixed, quantised orbits.
2. The angular momentum of an electron in an allowed orbit is L = nℏ.
3. Electrons can jump between orbits by absorbing or emitting photons of energy ΔE = hf, where ΔE = |E_initial - E_final|.

For the hydrogen atom: E_n = -13.6 eV/n² (n = 1, 2, 3, ...). The radius of orbit n: r_n = n² × 0.053 nm (Bohr radius).

**Hydrogen spectrum**:

- Lyman series (UV): transitions to n = 1.
- Balmer series (visible): transitions to n = 2.
- Paschen series (IR): transitions to n = 3.

The series limit corresponds to n = ∞ (the ionisation energy of hydrogen is 13.6 eV).

#### Wave-particle duality

**de Broglie wavelength**: λ = h/p = h/(mv). For a macroscopic object (e.g. a baseball), λ is negligibly small. For an electron with kinetic energy 100 eV, λ ≈ 0.123 nm (atomic scale).

**Electron diffraction (Davisson-Germer, 1927)**: electrons directed at a nickel crystal produce a diffraction pattern, demonstrating the wave nature of electrons.

**Double-slit experiment with electrons (Tonomura, 1989)**: even single electrons fired one at a time at a double slit build up an interference pattern, demonstrating wave-particle duality for single particles.

**Heisenberg uncertainty principle**: Δx Δp ≥ ℏ/2. The more precisely the position is known, the less precisely the momentum is known, and vice versa. This is a fundamental property of quantum mechanics, not a measurement limitation.

#### Probing the nano scale

**STM (scanning tunnelling microscope)**: a sharp tip is brought within ~ 1 nm of a conducting surface. A small bias voltage produces a tunnelling current between the tip and the surface, which depends exponentially on the tip-surface distance. Scanning the tip across across the surface produces an atomically resolved image.

**AFM (atomic force microscope)**: a sharp tip on a cantilever is scanned across a surface. The force between the tip and the surface (van der Waals, electrostatic) bends the cantilever; the bending is measured with a laser, producing a topographical image. AFM works on insulating surfaces, unlike STM.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-topic links

- **III. Wave Motion**: interference and diffraction apply to matter waves.
- **V. Radioactivity and Nuclear Energy**: nuclear models and decay.
- **VI. Astronomy and Space Science (Elective)**: stellar spectra reveal element composition.

#### Exam technique

- **For photoelectric questions**: apply KE_max = hf - φ; check that f > f₀.
- **For Bohr model questions**: state the quantisation condition; identify the transition energy.
- **For de Broglie questions**: apply λ = h/p.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Quantum means small." Quantum effects occur at atomic scales; classical physics fails at very small scales or very low energies.
- "Heisenberg's principle is a measurement error." It is a fundamental property of quantum mechanics, not a measurement limitation.
- "Light is either a wave or a particle." Light exhibits wave-particle duality; the description depends on the experiment.
