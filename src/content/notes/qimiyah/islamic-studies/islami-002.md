# Topic 2

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Topic 2** in the Qimiyah General Studies paper bundles three reasoning blocks — quantitative, verbal, and analytical — into a single high-weight section. Five formulas carry roughly 60% of the marks: **Percentage = (Part/Whole) × 100**, **Average = Sum ÷ Count**, **Simple Interest = (P × R × T)/100**, **Distance = Speed × Time**, and **Area of rectangle = L × W**. Treat ratios as ordered pairs (a:b matches c:d) and percentages as multipliers of the base value. For verbal analogy items, map the **relationship** (cause-effect, part-whole, function, degree), not surface synonyms. Inductive reasoning items reward pattern detection over arithmetic tricks — scan for position, sign, and alternation rules. Plan **≤45 seconds per MCQ**; the block is large and time pressure is the silent killer.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Quantitative Reasoning Core
Quantitative items in Topic 2 cluster into four families. **Number operations** test integer, fraction, and decimal manipulation with distractors built from careless sign errors. **Ratio and proportion** requires matching terms in the same order: if a:b = c:d, then ad = bc. Direct proportion scales both sides by the same factor; inverse proportion keeps their product constant (e.g., speed × time = distance for a fixed route). **Percentage change** is computed as (New − Old)/Old × 100, distinct from percentage-point difference, which subtracts two percentages directly. **Profit and loss** uses Cost Price (CP) as the base: Profit% = (SP − CP)/CP × 100.

#### Algebra and Geometry
**Linear equations** of the form ax + b = c collapse to x = (c − b)/a; word problems translate to equations by assigning the unknown to the quantity asked. **Simple Interest** accumulates linearly: A = P(1 + RT/100), where R is annual rate in percent and T in years. For **geometry**, internalise perimeter/area/volume of rectangles, squares, triangles, circles, and cuboids — the volume formula L × W × H traps test-takers who select the surface-area expression 2(LW + LH + WH) for 3D questions.

#### Verbal and Analytical Reasoning
**Vocabulary** items use context to disambiguate; **analogy** items (A:B :: C:?) require identifying the relational operator (synonym, antonym, cause, tool, degree). **Reading comprehension** passages (≈150 words) yield main-idea, inference, and detail questions — read the stem before the passage. **Inductive reasoning** presents number/letter sequences; track single-step deltas, alternating signs, and recursive patterns like ×2 + 1.

#### Key Formulas at a Glance

| Concept | Formula | Variables |
|---|---|---|
| Percentage | (Part/Whole) × 100 | Part, Whole |
| Average | Sum ÷ n | Sum, n = count |
| Simple Interest | PRT/100 | P = principal, R = %/yr, T = years |

Expect single-step arithmetic, one-equation word problems, one short comprehension passage with three linked questions, and a sequence-completion item. The block contributes about **3% of the total paper** but is one of the highest-yield per minute.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases in Quantitative Items
**Weighted vs simple mean** is the classic trap: the average speed of a round trip is *not* the arithmetic mean of the two speeds — it equals 2·S₁·S₂/(S₁+S₂) (harmonic mean) because each leg takes a different share of the total time. **Percentage-point vs percent-change** errors appear in data-interpretation items: a rate rising from 4% to 6% is a **2 percentage-point** increase but a **50% relative change**. **Successive percentages** do not add: a 20% rise followed by 10% rise equals 1.20 × 1.10 = 1.32, i.e. 32% net, not 30%. **Ratio scaling** requires the *smallest integer* representation only when the problem asks for it; otherwise, any proportional pair is acceptable.

#### Edge Cases in Verbal and Analytical Items
Analogy items with **degree relationships** (hot : hotter :: cold : ?) are answered by mapping the grammatical and semantic operator, not by literal translation. Comprehension items frequently include the trap word **"EXCEPT"** or **"NOT"** — underline it in the stem. Inductive sequences may use **two interleaved sub-sequences** (odd-indexed and even-indexed terms follow different rules); test both before committing.

#### Connections to Adjacent Topics
Topic 2 feeds directly into the **science reasoning** items (units, rates) and the **Islamic-studies quantitative passages** (zakat = 2.5%, inheritance fractions). Mastering ratios and percentages here shortens revision time in those cross-topic items.

#### Common Mistakes
- Dividing by the wrong base in profit/loss (use CP, not SP).
- Treating a cuboid's lateral surface as its total surface.
- Answering analogy by synonym lookup; the correct operator is often function, part, or cause.
- Misreading "most likely" comprehension stems as absolute.

#### Worked Example
A trader buys 30 items for SR 450 and sells them at SR 18 each. Find profit percent.
Total SP = 30 × 18 = SR 540. Profit = 540 − 450 = SR 90. Profit% = 90/450 × 100 = **20%**.
