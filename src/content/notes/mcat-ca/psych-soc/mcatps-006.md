# Social Structure, Stratification and Demographics — MCAT Psych/Soc

Social structure, stratification, and demographics form the core of AAMC Foundational Concept 9 of the Psych/Soc section. The MCAT tests the family, education, religion, economy, government, healthcare, social class, stratification, mobility, demographic transition, urbanisation, immigration, and aging. These topics underpin most sociological passages on the exam.

> Verify the live section length, scoring scale, and any in-year content changes on https://students-residents.aamc.org/ before planning around the figures below.

---

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### Core concepts

- **Social institutions:** family, education, religion, economy, government, healthcare. Each fulfils a function for society.
- **Social class:** socioeconomic status based on income, education, occupation. Affects health, education, and life chances.
- **Stratification:** hierarchical arrangement of people based on wealth, power, prestige.
- **Social mobility:** change in social class within a person's lifetime (intragenerational) or across generations (intergenerational).
- **Demographic transition:** the shift from high birth and death rates to low birth and death rates as a society industrialises.
- **Urbanisation:** movement of population from rural to urban areas.
- **Immigration:** movement of people into a country. Affects demographics, culture, economy.
- **Aging:** the growing proportion of older adults in many societies; affects healthcare and economy.

#### One-pass drill

1. Identify the topic: institution, stratification, or demographic.
2. Recall the relevant concept.
3. Apply to the specific scenario.

#### Examiner traps

- Treating social class and income as the same. Class includes education and occupation, not just income.
- Confusing intragenerational and intergenerational mobility.
- Forgetting that the demographic transition involves both birth and death rates.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Social institutions

**Family:** primary agent of socialisation. Forms vary across cultures (nuclear, extended, single-parent, blended, same-sex).

**Education:** transmits knowledge, skills, values, and norms. Sorting function (sorts students into different roles).

**Religion:** provides meaning, social cohesion, and moral guidance. Varies across cultures.

**Economy:** produces and distributes goods and services. Capitalism (market-based) versus socialism (state-based); most modern economies are mixed.

**Government:** makes and enforces laws, provides public goods, redistributes resources.

**Healthcare:** maintains and improves health. Access varies by country (universal, private, mixed).

#### Social stratification

**Class systems:** based on socioeconomic status. Open system: mobility is possible. Class is typically associated with occupation.

**Caste systems:** based on ascribed status (birth). Closed system: little mobility. Associated with traditional societies (e.g., India's caste system).

**Stratification dimensions:** wealth (economic resources), power (ability to influence others), prestige (social respect).

#### Social mobility

**Intragenerational:** change in class within a person's lifetime (e.g., a worker becomes a manager).

**Intergenerational:** change in class between generations (e.g., a plumber's child becomes a doctor).

**Mobility factors:** education, occupation, marriage, inheritance, economic growth.

#### Demographics

**Demographic transition:** as societies industrialise:
1. **Pre-industrial:** high birth rate, high death rate; slow growth.
2. **Early industrial:** death rate falls (better nutrition, sanitation); birth rate stays high; rapid growth.
3. **Mature industrial:** birth rate falls (urbanisation, contraception); growth slows.
4. **Post-industrial:** low birth rate, low death rate; slow growth or decline.

**Urbanisation:** movement from rural to urban areas. Driven by industrialisation and economic opportunity.

**Immigration:** affects population composition and economic growth. Can lead to diversity and cultural change.

**Aging:** the proportion of older adults is rising in many societies, driven by increased life expectancy and lower birth rates. Affects healthcare, pensions, and the workforce.

#### Practice routine

- Take 5 institution items. Identify the institution and its function.
- Take 5 stratification items. Distinguish class from caste; identify the dimension.
- Take 5 mobility items. Distinguish intragenerational from intergenerational.
- Take 5 demographic items. Identify the stage of the demographic transition.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How this topic connects to the other Psych/Soc topics

- **Self-concept** is shaped by socialisation (family, education, religion).
- **Biology of behaviour** interacts with social structure (e.g., stress from low socioeconomic status affects health).
- **Research methods** (Foundational Concept 8) are used to study social structures.

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"Caste systems allow mobility."** Caste systems are closed; mobility is limited.
- **"Urbanisation is the same as suburbanisation."** Urbanisation is movement to cities; suburbanisation is movement to areas just outside cities.
- **"Aging only affects the elderly."** Aging has broad effects on healthcare, pensions, workforce, and family structure.

#### Exam technique

- For institution items, identify the institution and its function.
- For stratification items, distinguish the type of system (class or caste) and the dimension.
- For mobility items, identify the type (intragenerational or intergenerational).
- For demographic items, identify the stage of the transition.
