# Cardiovascular System

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The cardiovascular system is a closed circulatory loop: a four-chambered muscular pump (heart) connected to arteries, capillaries, and veins. NEET PG rewards structural recall — chambers, valves, conducting system, coronary supply, and fetal shunts.

- **Chambers**: 2 atria (receiving) + 2 ventricles (pumping); interatrial and interventricular septa separate sides.
- **Valves**: AV (tricuspid, mitral/bicuspid) anchored by **chordae tendineae** to papillary muscles; semilunar (aortic, pulmonary) have no chordae.
- **Conducting system**: SA node → AV node (0.3 s delay) → Bundle of His → bundle branches → **Purkinje fibres**.
- **Coronary dominance**: Right coronary artery (RCA) is dominant in ~70–80% and supplies the SA node (60%) and AV node (80%).
- **Fetal shunts**: foramen ovale, ductus arteriosus, ductus venosus — all close functionally after birth.

**Formula**: Cardiac Output = Stroke Volume × Heart Rate.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Heart Wall and Pericardium

The heart wall has three layers: **endocardium** (inner, endothelium-lined), **myocardium** (middle, cardiac muscle — thickest in the left ventricle), and **epicardium** (outer, visceral serous pericardium). The pericardium comprises a tough outer **fibrous** layer and an inner **serous** layer split into parietal and visceral (epicardium) parts. Two sinuses matter surgically: the **transverse pericardial sinus** (between aorta/pulmonary trunk anteriorly and SVC/atria posteriorly — clamp site during surgery) and the **oblique sinus** (posterior to the left atrium).

#### Chambers and Valves

The right atrium receives the SVC, IVC, and coronary sinus. The mitral (bicuspid) valve guards the left AV orifice; the tricuspid guards the right. Papillary muscles contract before ventricular systole, tightening chordae tendineae to prevent valve prolapse. The aortic valve has three semilunar cusps with no chordal support.

#### Conducting System and Coronary Supply

| Structure | Function / Detail |
| --- | --- |
| SA node | Pacemaker; in RA near SVC opening; ~70–80 bpm intrinsic rate |
| AV node | Delay (~0.3 s) for atrial emptying; in interatrial septum near coronary sinus |
| Bundle of His | Only electrical bridge between atria and ventricles |
| Purkinje fibres | Modified cardiac muscle (not nerves); fastest conduction |
| Left coronary artery | LAD (anterior 2/3 septum, LV) + circumflex (LA, lateral LV) |
| Right coronary artery | RA, RV, posterior 1/3 septum, SA node (60%), AV node (80%) |

#### Auscultation Sites (often confused with anatomy)

> Auscultation areas do NOT match anatomical valve location — sound travels along flow direction.

- **Aortic**: right 2nd intercostal space (parasternal)
- **Pulmonary**: left 2nd intercostal space
- **Tricuspid**: lower left sternal border, 4th–5th ICS
- **Mitral**: apex, 5th ICS midclavicular line

#### Fetal Shunts

- **Foramen ovale**: right atrium → left atrium across interatrial septum.
- **Ductus arteriosus**: pulmonary trunk → aortic arch (distal to left subclavian).
- **Ductus venosus**: umbilical vein → IVC, bypassing the liver.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Clinical Correlates and Applied Anatomy

Cardiac referred pain travels via sympathetic afferents from **T1–T5**, which is why MI classically presents with left arm, jaw, or epigastric pain. Pericarditis pain worsens on leaning forward because the inflamed parietal pericardium rubs against the visceral layer. Coarctation of the aorta distal to the ductus arteriosus produces rib notching from enlarged intercostal collaterals.

#### Edge Cases and Examiner Traps

| Mistake | Correction |
| --- | --- |
| Calling Purkinje fibres nerves | They are modified cardiac myocytes, not neural tissue |
| Mapping auscultation area to anatomical position | Aortic valve sits on the left but is heard on the right |
| Attributing SA node supply to LAD | SA node is RCA territory in ~60%; AV node in ~80% |
| Mixing ductus arteriosus direction | It shunts PA → aorta (right-to-left), not the reverse |
| Identifying epicardium as separate from pericardium | Epicardium is the visceral serous pericardium |

#### Cross-Topic Links

The conducting system overlaps with **Physiology** (action potentials, refractory periods) and **Pathology** (arrhythmias, ischaemic heart disease). Pericardial sinuses appear in **Surgery** questions on cardiac operative approaches. Fetal shunts reappear in **Paediatrics** for congenital cyanotic heart disease.

#### Practice Prompts

1. Trace blood flow from the SVC through the heart, naming every chamber, valve, and great vessel it crosses.
2. A posterior MI on ECG — name the likely occluded artery, the territory supplied, and why AV nodal block is a recognised complication.

> **Strategy tip**: For image questions on angiograms, identify the vessel branch pattern first (LAD = diagonal + septal; RCA = posterior descending + AV nodal branch) before naming the artery.
