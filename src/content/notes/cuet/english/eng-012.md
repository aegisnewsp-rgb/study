# Direct Indirect Speech

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Direct speech repeats the speaker's exact words inside quotation marks ("He said, 'I am tired.'"), while indirect (reported) speech paraphrases the meaning using a reporting verb and a that-clause ("He said that he was tired"). The core operation is **tense back-shift**: present tenses in direct speech move one step back in indirect speech (Simple Present → Simple Past, Present Perfect → Past Perfect). Use **said** without an object, **told** with an object, and **asked** plus if/whether/Wh-word for questions.

| Change Type | Direct → Indirect |
| --- | --- |
| Pronouns | I → he/she, you → he/she (per reporting subject) |
| Time | today → that day, now → then, tomorrow → the next day |

- Commands shift to **to + V1** (He ordered, "Sit" → He ordered him to sit).
- Wh-questions drop inversion: "Where is she?" → He asked where she was.
- Universal truths stay unchanged: "The earth revolves around the sun" → He said the earth revolves around the sun.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Tense Back-Shift Rules
The reporting verb governs whether tenses shift. When it sits in the **Simple Past**, every present tense in the reported clause moves one step back. A reporting verb in the present, or a reported clause expressing a universal fact, freezes the original tense.

| Direct Tense | Indirect Tense |
| --- | --- |
| Simple Present | Simple Past |
| Present Continuous | Past Continuous |
| Present Perfect | Past Perfect |
| Simple Past | Simple Past (no further shift) |
| Past Perfect | Past Perfect (no shift) |

#### Modal Transformations
Modals follow their own back-shift pattern. **Will → would, can → can, can → can, shall → should**. The modal **must** splits: obligation becomes **had to**, while permanent truth stays **must**. Already-past modals (**would, can, can, should, ought to**) pass through unchanged.

#### Pronoun and Reference Shift
First person becomes the reporting subject's pronoun; second person becomes third person unless the listener is identified; third person stays. Time and place markers translate: **here → there, this → that, now → then, ago → before, last week → the week before**.

#### Questions and Commands
Yes/No questions adopt **if/whether** and statement word order. Wh-questions retain the Wh-word but drop inversion and the question mark. Imperatives convert to **reporting verb + object + to + V1** (ordered, commanded, requested, advised).

- Use **said** without an object; use **told** when the listener is named.
- Insert **that** in long indirect statements to avoid ambiguity between clauses.
- Universal truths and proverbs bypass back-shift entirely.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Tricky Patterns
When the reporting verb is itself present-tense, back-shift is suspended entirely: "She says, 'I am leaving'" → She says (that) she is leaving. Reporting past speech with intervening present narration creates the "historic present" effect, again avoiding shift. Reporting verbs of advice (**suggest, recommend, insist**) trigger a different construction: He suggested going home, not *He suggested to go home*.

#### Exclamations and Reporting-Verb Nuance
Exclamatory sentences carry the emotion into the reporting clause: He exclaimed with joy that he had won / He exclaimed with sorrow that she had failed. The reporting verb itself signals the emotion — **exclaimed, shouted, whispered, murmured, muttered** — and the inner exclamation words ("what", "how") are dropped, replaced by the emotion noun.

#### Worked Micro-Example
Direct: She said, "I have been waiting here since morning, and I will not leave until the manager arrives."
Indirect: She said that she had been waiting there since the morning, and she would not leave until the manager arrived.
Step check: have been waiting → had been waiting (Present Perfect → Past Perfect), here → there, morning (specific time retained) → the morning, will → would, manager arrives (present tense because reporting verb is past but subordinate clause uses natural present) → manager arrived.

| Reporting Verb | Required Object | Typical Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| say | No | Neutral statements |
| tell | Yes (told him) | Neutral statements with listener |
| ask | Yes (asked him) | Questions |
| order / command | Yes | Commands |
| advise / request | Yes | Polite imperatives |

#### Common Traps
1. Leaving the question mark and inversion in indirect Wh-questions.
2. Writing *said me* or *said to him* inconsistently.
3. Back-shifting universal truths and permanent scientific facts.
4. Forgetting the **to + V1** infinitive after orders and requests.

#### CUET-Specific Strategy

Practice the back-shift table until it is automatic; mark pronoun, time-reference and modal changes on a scratch line before committing to an option. The reverse direction (indirect → direct) appears less in many papers but tests the same rules in reverse.
