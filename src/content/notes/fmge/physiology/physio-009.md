# Reproductive and Developmental Physiology

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Reproductive and Developmental Physiology** covers gametogenesis, fertilisation, implantation, pregnancy endocrinology, parturition, and lactation under hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal (HPG) control.

- **Spermatogenesis** (~64 days) in seminiferous tubules: spermatogonia → primary spermatocyte → 2 secondary spermatocytes → 4 spermatids → 4 spermatozoa. **Sertoli cells** (FSH-driven) form the blood–testis barrier; **Leydig cells** (LH-driven) secrete testosterone.
- **Oogenesis** is arrested in prophase I from fetal life; one dominant follicle/month completes meiosis I, with the secondary oocyte arrested in metaphase II until sperm penetration triggers meiosis II completion.
- **Menstrual cycle (28 days):** follicular phase (days 1–14, rising **estrogen**, **LH surge** at day 14 → ovulation) → luteal phase (days 15–28, **corpus luteum** → **progesterone**).
- **Pregnancy hormones:** **hCG** (syncytiotrophoblast) maintains corpus luteum until the luteoplacental shift (~weeks 8–10); **hPL** induces maternal insulin resistance; **progesterone** keeps the endometrium quiescent.
- **Lactation:** **prolactin** = milk synthesis; **oxytocin** = milk ejection (let-down reflex).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### HPG Axis and the Menstrual Cycle

The hypothalamus secretes **GnRH** in pulses, driving anterior pituitary release of **FSH** (follicle growth) and **LH** (ovulation and corpus luteum maintenance). Mid-cycle, sustained high **estrogen** from the dominant Graafian follicle switches feedback to positive, producing the **LH surge** that triggers ovulation ~36 hours later. After ovulation, the ruptured follicle becomes the **corpus luteum**, which secretes progesterone to stabilise the endometrium. If fertilisation does not occur, the corpus luteum regresses by day ~24–26, progesterone falls, and **menses** begins.

#### Gametogenesis Compared

| Feature | Spermatogenesis | Oogenesis |
|---|---|---|
| Site | Seminiferous tubules | Ovarian cortex |
| Duration | ~64 days continuous | Begins in fetus, completes only at fertilisation |
| Cells produced | 4 spermatozoa per meiosis | 1 ovum + 2–3 polar bodies |
| Puberty onset | Continuous after puberty | Fixed pool of oocytes, one dominant/month |

#### Pregnancy Endocrinology

After implantation (~day 6–7) the **syncytiotrophoblast** secretes **hCG**, which rescues the corpus luteum to sustain progesterone output until the **luteoplacental shift** at weeks 8–10, when the placenta takes over steroidogenesis. **hPL** (also placental) induces maternal insulin resistance to shunt glucose to the fetus. **Placental estrogen** is synthesised from **fetal DHEA-S** via placental sulfatase and aromatase — the **fetoplacental unit**.

#### Parturition and Lactation

Rising **estrogen:progesterone ratio**, fetal cortisol, and prostaglandins upregulate **oxytocin receptors** and myometrial gap junctions. Cervical stretch activates the **Ferguson reflex** (positive feedback) on hypothalamic oxytocin release. Post-partum, suckling inhibits hypothalamic dopamine, raising **prolactin** for milk synthesis, while **oxytocin** drives milk ejection.

#### Common Exam Traps

- Source of **hCG** = syncytiotrophoblast, not cytotrophoblast.
- Meiosis II completes only **after fertilisation**, not before.
- Estrogen exerts **positive feedback** at mid-cycle — not solely negative.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Fertilisation and Implantation

Fertilisation typically occurs in the **ampulla of the fallopian tube**. Capacitated sperm undergo the **acrosome reaction**, releasing hyaluronidase and acrosin to penetrate the **zona pellucida**. Sperm–oocyte fusion triggers the **cortical reaction**, modifying the zona to block polyspermy. The zygote undergoes cleavage to the **morula**, then the **blastocyst** (inner cell mass + trophoblast), which implants into the posterior uterine wall ~day 6–7. The trophoblast splits into **syncytiotrophoblast** (invasive, hCG-secreting) and **cytotrophoblast** (inner germinal layer).

#### Fetoplacental Unit and Hormone Shifts

The placenta lacks key enzymes (e.g., 17α-hydroxylase) and depends on the fetal adrenal cortex for **DHEA-S**, which is desulfated by placental sulfatase and aromatised to estrogen. This interdependence explains why fetal demise or anencephaly (low fetal adrenal output) reduces maternal urinary estrogen — a classic FMGE stem.

> **Exam pearl:** The "luteoplacental shift" at ~8–10 weeks means removing the corpus luteum before this point can cause miscarriage, but not after.

#### Parturition Mechanism

Labour initiation integrates fetal maturity signals (rising fetal cortisol) with maternal endocrine priming. Estrogen dominance increases myometrial excitability; prostaglandins (PGE2, PGF2α) drive ripening and contractions. The **Ferguson reflex** sustains contractions via oxytocin positive feedback until delivery.

#### Lactation Physiology

**Colostrum**, secreted in the first 2–3 days, is IgA-rich and lower in fat than mature milk. Suckling afferents suppress hypothalamic **dopamine** (prolactin-inhibiting factor), allowing prolactin pulses for milk synthesis, while a separate oxytocin burst ejects stored milk. Oestrogen and progesterone withdrawal after delivery permits these hypothalamic reflexes to operate — hence combined oral contraceptives can suppress established lactation when given early.

#### Edge Cases and Adjacent Links

- **Menopause**: exhaustion of the follicular pool → low estrogen and inhibin B → loss of negative feedback → elevated FSH/LH (FSH usually rises more than LH).
- **PCOS physiology**: high LH:FSH ratio drives excess thecal androgens with arrested folliculogenesis.
- **Inhibin B** from Sertoli cells specifically provides negative feedback on FSH (not LH), distinguishing feedback selectivity tested in FMGE.

#### Practice Prompts

1. A woman at 7 weeks gestation has her corpus luteum removed surgically. Predict the endocrine outcome and explain why removal at 14 weeks would be tolerated.
2. Contrast the mechanisms by which prolactin and oxytocin are released during suckling, and state which hormone fails first if the mother experiences severe postpartum haemorrhage with pituitary infarction (Sheehan syndrome).
