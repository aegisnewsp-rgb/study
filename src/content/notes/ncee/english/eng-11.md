# Letter Writing Basics

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

A **letter** is a written message sent to a reader at a distance, and NCEE English Paper 2 (Essay & Letter Writing) allocates roughly 20 marks to the letter section, usually one compulsory task out of two choices. Three types appear most often: **formal** (to officials, principals, editors), **informal** (to friends, relatives), and **semi-formal** (to an older neighbour or a teacher you do not know well).

Every formal letter needs seven parts in this exact top-to-bottom order: **sender's address → date → recipient's address → salutation → subject line → body → complimentary close → signature**. Use a **colon** after "Dear Sir" or "Dear Madam" and a **comma** after "Dear Mary." End with **"Yours faithfully"** when the recipient is unknown, **"Yours sincerely"** when the name is known. Date format: **15th March, 2024** (ordinal + month + year, never 15/03/2024).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Parts of a Formal Letter
1. **Sender's address** — top right, no label; usually 1–3 lines.
2. **Date** — directly below the sender's address, written in full.
3. **Recipient's address** ("Inside address") — left-aligned, begins with the recipient's name and designation (e.g., *The Principal, Government Secondary School, Abuja*).
4. **Salutation** — "Dear Sir," / "Dear Madam," / "Dear Sir/Madam," with a **colon**, not a comma.
5. **Subject line** — a single line stating the purpose in capitals or title case, e.g., *SUBJECT: REQUEST FOR SPORTS EQUIPMENT*.
6. **Body** — three paragraphs: opening states the purpose, middle gives details/reasons, closing states the expected action.
7. **Complimentary close** — "Yours faithfully" (unknown recipient) or "Yours sincerely" (known recipient), followed by a comma.
8. **Signature, full name, and designation** — handwritten signature above the typed name.

#### Parts of an Informal Letter
Sender's address and date at the top right, salutation in the centre or left ("Dear Mary," / "My dear John,") with a **comma**, body in conversational paragraphs, then a closing phrase ("Love," "Yours affectionately," "Best wishes,"), and the first name as signature.

#### Tone and Language
Formal letters use **polite, precise, impersonal** English; avoid slang, contractions, and first-person plural. Informal letters allow contractions, exclamations, and warm personal touches.

#### Punctuation Snapshot
| Element | Formal | Informal |
|---|---|---|
| After salutation | **Colon** | **Comma** |
| After complimentary close | **Comma** | **Comma** |
| Date style | 15th March, 2024 | 15th March, 2024 |

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### High-Frequency NCEE Letter Types
The examiners rotate **letters of complaint** (poor service, broken facility), **letters of application/request** (asking the principal for a club, equipment, leave), **letters of invitation** (to a chief or guest speaker), **letters of apology** (for absence or damage), **letters to the editor** (on a community problem), and **thank-you letters** (to a benefactor or teacher). Each follows the same skeleton; only the body paragraphs change.

#### Common Mistakes That Cost Marks
- Writing the **date before the sender's address** — instant loss of 1 mark.
- Using **"Yours sincerely"** for "Dear Sir/Madam" (should be "Yours faithfully").
- Signing with **first name only** in a formal letter; full name and designation are required.
- Leaving out the **subject line**, which carries its own mark.
- Using **"We"** in place of "I" in a personal complaint — the voice must be first-person singular.
- Writing the date as **15/03/2024** instead of **15th March, 2024**.

#### Worked Outline — Letter of Complaint About a Burst Pipe
Sender's address → Date → *The Managing Director, Abuja Water Board, Wuse Zone 5, Abuja* → Salutation *Dear Sir,* → Subject: *REPORT OF A BURST WATER PIPE AT OUR SCHOOL* → Body 1 introduces the problem; Body 2 gives date of discovery, affected blocks, and disruption to lessons; Body 3 requests urgent repair and gives a contact phone number → *Yours faithfully,* → Signature, full name, and designation (e.g., *Senior Prefect*).
