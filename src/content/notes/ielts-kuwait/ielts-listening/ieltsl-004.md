# Part 4 — Academic Lecture — IELTS Listening Notes

Part 4 of the IELTS Listening test is a monologue on an academic subject. Typical contexts: a university lecture, a research summary, an academic talk on a topic like biology, history, or environmental science. The recording runs about 5-7 minutes and has 10 questions. Part 4 is the final and most difficult section of the Listening test.

> Verify the live Listening format on https://ielts.org/ before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Format:** 10 questions. Test types: sentence completion, summary completion, note completion, table completion, flow-chart completion.
- **Transcript style:** one speaker (lecturer or researcher) presenting an academic topic in a structured way.
- **Speed:** academic pace, dense vocabulary, longer sentences than Parts 1-3.
- **Order of answers:** questions follow the recording in order.
- **Predictable content:** definitions, examples, processes, comparisons, evaluations.
- **Common distractor:** the lecturer may use technical vocabulary, then paraphrase or define it. The answer is the paraphrase, not the technical term.

#### Examiner traps

- Writing the technical term instead of the paraphrased definition.
- Missing the answer because it comes after a long sentence with multiple clauses.
- Word count violations on completion items ("NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS").

---

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Question types in Part 4

- **Sentence completion:** complete sentences with words from the recording. Test usually limits the word count.
- **Summary completion:** fill gaps in a summary paragraph. Each gap is a single word or short phrase.
- **Note completion:** fill gaps in informal notes. Often the gaps are key terms.
- **Table completion:** fill in cells of a table. Information is grouped by the recording's structure.
- **Flow-chart completion:** follow a process described in the lecture.

#### Active listening strategy

1. **Anticipate vocabulary:** before listening, predict what vocabulary will appear based on the topic.
2. **Track the structure:** academic lectures usually have an introduction, then sections (definitions, examples, applications, conclusion).
3. **Note key terms:** write down technical terms as they appear; they often appear in the questions.
4. **Listen for transitions:** the lecturer signals structure ("first," "next," "moving on," "to summarise").

#### Building academic vocabulary

Part 4 rewards a wide academic vocabulary. The Cambridge IELTS Academic Word List groups high-frequency words by topic. Practice by listening to BBC Radio 4, TED talks, and university lectures on YouTube; note unfamiliar words and their definitions.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where Part 4 appears across IELTS Listening

Part 4 is always the final section, accounting for 10 of the 40 questions. It is the highest-difficulty section and a strong band discriminator. Candidates who score 7+ in Listening typically score 8-10 in Part 4.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "The technical term is always the answer." No — the lecturer often defines or paraphrases the term in the same sentence; the paraphrase is the answer.
- "I need to understand every word." No — you need to catch the keywords from the questions. Other words can be missed without cost.
- "Part 4 is impossible." It is hard, but it is the most predictable section — academic vocabulary and structure follow standard patterns.

#### Exam technique

- For sentence-completion items, write the exact words from the recording (within the word limit). Paraphrasing loses the mark.
- For summary-completion items, the answer is the next word the lecturer says after the trigger phrase.
- For table-completion items, follow the table's order — the recording matches the table row by row.
