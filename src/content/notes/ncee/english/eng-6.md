# Tenses: Present, Past and Future

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

A **tense** is the verb form that locates an action or state in time relative to the moment of speaking. English has three time frames — Present, Past, Future — and four aspects (Simple, Continuous/Progressive, Perfect, Perfect Continuous), producing **12 tense forms** in total. The auxiliary verb (am/is/are, was/were, have/has, had, will, shall) does the time-marking; the main verb carries the meaning.

- **Simple Present** = habit/truth: *The sun rises in the east.*
- **Past forms** need **V2** (regular) or irregular past forms: *She went / They ate.*
- **Perfect tenses** combine **have/has/had + V3** (past participle): *I have written the letter.*
- **Continuous tenses** add **verb-ing** after the auxiliary: *They were playing when it rained.*

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### The Three Tense Groups and Their Four Aspects

Each of the three time frames (Present, Past, Future) carries four aspects, giving 12 forms. Knowing the structure makes gap-fill and "choose the correct option" items mechanical.

| Aspect | Form | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Simple | base verb / V2 / will + base | Habit, fact, completed action, prediction |
| Continuous | be + verb-ing | Action in progress at a stated time |
| Perfect | have + V3 | Action completed before a reference point |
| Perfect Continuous | have + been + verb-ing | Duration of an action up to a reference point |

#### Time-Marker Cues for NCEE

Examiners test recognition by planting adverbs and adverbials. Identify the marker first, then select the tense.

- **Simple Present:** *always, usually, often, every day, twice a week*
- **Present Continuous:** *now, at the moment, right now, Look!*
- **Present Perfect:** *just, ever, never, already, yet, since, for, recently, so far*
- **Simple Past:** *yesterday, ago, last week/month/year, in 2020, when I was young*
- **Past Continuous:** *while, when + past action, at 8 o'clock yesterday*
- **Past Perfect:** *before, after, by the time, already (in past narrative)*
- **Future forms:** *tomorrow, next week, in 2030, by next year, by the time*

> **Trap alert:** *since* (point of time) pairs with **Perfect Continuous**, but *for* (duration) can pair with both **Perfect** and **Perfect Continuous** depending on whether duration or completion is emphasised.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### High-Yield Distinctions Tested in NCEE Objective Items

- **Simple Past vs. Present Perfect:** *I have seen him yesterday* is wrong because *yesterday* is a finished-time adverb that demands **Simple Past** (*I saw him yesterday*). Conversely, *I have known her for ten years* is correct while *I knew her for ten years* implies the knowing ended.
- **will vs. be going to:** *will* = spontaneous decision, prediction based on opinion (*It will rain tonight*); *be going to* = pre-existing plan or evidence-based prediction (*Look at those clouds — it is going to rain*).
- **Past Simple vs. Past Continuous:** A longer background action takes the **Past Continuous**; the shorter interrupting action takes the **Simple Past** (*I was reading when the phone rang*).

#### Common Errors in Candidate Scripts

| Error | Wrong | Correct |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Past participle confusion | *I have broke it* | *I have broken it* |
| Subject-verb agreement | *He go to school* | *He goes to school* |
| Wrong aspect in past narrative | *While she was cooked, the bell rang* | *While she was cooking, the bell rang* |
| Double auxiliary | *He will can come* | *He will be able to come* |

#### Worked Example

> *By the time the match ______ (start), the crowd ______ (wait) for an hour.*

Answer: ***starts … will have been waiting***. The first blank is a present-tense subordinate clause triggered by *by the time + future reference*; the second is **Future Perfect Continuous** because the waiting extends up to a future point.

#### Practice Prompts

1. Fill in: *She ______ (read) this novel since Monday.* (Expected: *has been reading* — duration marker *since* + ongoing action.)
2. Identify the tense and rewrite in Simple Past: *They have been building the bridge for two years.* (Expected: *They built the bridge / They were building the bridge for two years* — though the continuous nuance is lost.)
