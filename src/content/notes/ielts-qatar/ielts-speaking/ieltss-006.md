# Grammatical Range and Accuracy — IELTS Speaking (Qatar)

In the IELTS Speaking test, **grammatical range and accuracy** is scored as
one of the **four criteria** on the band scale (each from 0 to 9).
Grammatical range refers to the **variety** of grammatical structures the
candidate uses; grammatical accuracy refers to the **error-free** use of those
structures. Grammatical range is NOT the same as vocabulary range: a
candidate can have excellent vocabulary but poor grammar (e.g. precise
vocabulary with broken sentence structure), or excellent grammar but poor
vocabulary (e.g. smooth sentences with basic words).

This is the **sixth criteria type** in the ielts-speaking subject, and it is
the **most consistently underrated** by Qatari candidates. Many candidates
believe that using complex grammar will move them from band 6 to band 7. It
will NOT. Grammatical range is scored on the **mixing of simple and complex
sentences**, **conditional structures**, **comparatives**, **passive voice**,
**modals**, and **relative clauses** — but the **error rate must stay low
enough to stay inside the target band**.

The band descriptors explicitly require **mixing simple and complex
sentences**, **conditional structures**, **comparatives**, **passive voice**,
**modals**, and **relative clauses** — but keeping the error rate low. The
examiner expects the candidate to demonstrate grammatical range WITHOUT
introducing errors that drop them into a lower band.

In the Qatari cohort, candidates often make grammatical errors because they
have learned English from grammar-translation textbooks (which emphasize
written accuracy) rather than from natural speech (which emphasizes
fluency). Common grammar patterns in the cohort:

- **Simple sentences only** — candidates use simple sentences (*"I like
  reading. Reading is good."*) without complex structures.
- **Subject-verb agreement errors** — candidates confuse singular and plural
  (*"He go to school"* instead of *"He goes to school"*).
- **Tense errors** — candidates mix past / present / future (*"Yesterday I
  go to the museum"* instead of *"Yesterday I went to the museum"*).
- **Article errors** — candidates omit or misuse articles (*"I went to
  school"* instead of *"I went to the school"*).

To score band 7+ on grammatical range and accuracy, a Qatari candidate must
demonstrate **range** (mix simple and complex sentences, use conditionals,
comparatives, passives, modals, relative clauses) **without introducing
errors that drop them into a lower band**.

## Lite (4-Step Method)

1. **Mix simple and complex sentences.** Use a mix of simple sentences
   (*"I like reading"*), compound sentences (*"I like reading, and I read
   every day"*), and complex sentences (*"I like reading, which I have done
   since I was a child"*). Aim for at least 30% complex sentences.
2. **Use conditionals.** Practice the four conditional structures:
   zero conditional (*"If I have free time, I read"*), first conditional
   (*"If I have free time tomorrow, I will read"*), second conditional
   (*"If I had more free time, I would read more"*), third conditional
   (*"If I had had more free time, I would have read more"*).
3. **Use comparatives and superlatives.** Practice *"-er than"* / *"more ... than"*
   / *"-est"* / *"most ..."*: *"Reading is more interesting than watching TV"*;
   *"Reading is the most relaxing hobby I have"*.
4. **Use passive voice.** Practice passive voice: *"Reading is enjoyed by
   many people"*; *"The book was written by a Qatari author"*. Active voice
   is more common in Speaking, but passive voice signals band 7+.

**Insight: grammatical range is the second-most-discriminating criterion at
band 7+** (after pronunciation and fluency). A candidate with limited
grammatical range signals band 5–6; a candidate who uses a range of
grammatical structures WITHOUT introducing errors signals band 7+.

### 6 Examiner Traps (Grammatical Range and Accuracy)

- **The simple-sentences-only trap** — candidates use only simple sentences
  (*"I like reading. Reading is good. I read every day."*) without complex
  structures. The examiner scores range. Use a mix of simple and complex
  sentences.
- **The subject-verb-agreement trap** — candidates confuse singular and plural
  (*"He go to school"* instead of *"He goes to school"*). The examiner
  scores accuracy. Practice subject-verb agreement.
- **The tense-consistency trap** — candidates mix past / present / future
  (*"Yesterday I go to the museum"* instead of *"Yesterday I went to the
  museum"*). The examiner scores tense consistency. Practice past tense
  forms.
- **The article-omission trap** — candidates omit articles (*"I went to
  school"* instead of *"I went to the school"*). The examiner scores article
  accuracy. Practice articles.
- **The memorized-grammar trap** — candidates memorize complex grammar
  (*"subjunctive mood"*, *"past perfect continuous"*) but use it incorrectly.
  The examiner scores accuracy. Use grammar correctly.
- **The error-rate trap** — candidates introduce so many errors that they
  drop into a lower band. The examiner scores accuracy. Avoid errors.

### Qatari-Context Matches (2025–2026 cohort signal)

In recent IELTS Speaking tests used in Qatar, candidates often make these
grammar patterns:

- **Subject-verb agreement** — Qatari Arabic does not mark subject-verb
  agreement the same way English does. Common errors: *"He go to school"*,
  *"She have a book"*, *"They goes to the mall"*.
- **Tense consistency** — Qatari Arabic uses fewer tense markers than English.
  Common errors: mixing past and present (*"Yesterday I go to the museum"*)
  or mixing past and future (*"Yesterday I will go to the museum"*).
- **Articles** — Qatari Arabic does not have articles. Common errors: omitting
  articles (*"I went to school"* instead of *"I went to the school"*) or
  adding unnecessary articles (*"I like the reading"* instead of *"I like
  reading"*).
- **Prepositions** — Qatari Arabic uses prepositions differently than
  English. Common errors: *"in the morning"* instead of *"in the morning"*
  (actually correct), but *"at morning"* instead of *"in the morning"*,
  *"on 7 o'clock"* instead of *"at 7 o'clock"*.
- **Plurals** — Qatari Arabic uses fewer plural markers than English.
  Common errors: *"I have two book"* instead of *"I have two books"*,
  *"many people"* instead of *"many people"* (correct), *"much homework"*
  instead of *"much homework"* (correct).

The Qatari *Educational Reform* (Ministry of Education and Higher Education)
recognizes the importance of grammatical accuracy in Year 12 / Year 13
English classes, which is why grammatical range and accuracy is increasingly
emphasized in Qatari cohort IELTS preparation.

## Standard (7-Step Method with Long-Form Guidance)

### Step 1 — Mix simple and complex sentences

Use a mix of simple, compound, and complex sentences. Aim for at least 30%
complex sentences. Examples:

- **Simple sentence** — *"I like reading."*
- **Compound sentence** — *"I like reading, and I read every day."*
- **Complex sentence** — *"I like reading, which I have done since I was a
  child."*
- **Complex sentence with relative clause** — *"I like reading books that
  are about history."*
- **Complex sentence with conditional** — *"If I have free time, I read."*

### Step 2 — Use conditionals

Practice the four conditional structures:

- **Zero conditional** — *"If I have free time, I read."* (general truths)
- **First conditional** — *"If I have free time tomorrow, I will read."*
  (real future)
- **Second conditional** — *"If I had more free time, I would read more."*
  (hypothetical present)
- **Third conditional** — *"If I had had more free time, I would have read
  more."* (hypothetical past)

### Step 3 — Use comparatives and superlatives

Practice comparative (*"-er than"* / *"more ... than"*) and superlative
(*"-est"* / *"most ..."*) forms:

- **Comparative** — *"Reading is more interesting than watching TV."*
- **Comparative with -er** — *"Reading is faster than watching TV."*
- **Superlative** — *"Reading is the most relaxing hobby I have."*
- **Superlative with -est** — *"Reading is the fastest way to learn."*

### Step 4 — Use passive voice

Practice passive voice (subject + be + past participle):

- *"Reading is enjoyed by many people."*
- *"The book was written by a Qatari author."*
- *"The museum was built in 2010."*
- *"The policy was implemented last year."*

Active voice is more common in Speaking, but passive voice signals band 7+.

### Step 5 — Use modals

Practice modals (*can* / *could* / *may* / *might* / *must* / *should* /
*would*):

- *"I can help you with that."*
- *"I could help you if you need it."*
- *"You may want to consider that option."*
- *"You might want to think about it."*
- *"You should definitely try it."*
- *"I would love to visit that place."*

### Step 6 — Use relative clauses

Practice relative clauses (*who* / *which* / *that* / *where* / *when* /
*whose*):

- *"I have a friend who lives in Doha."*
- *"I read a book which was about Haiti."*
- *"I visited a place that was very beautiful."*
- *"I went to a museum where I saw ancient artefacts."*
- *"I remember a time when I was very happy."*
- *"I have a colleague whose wife is from Qatar."*

### Step 7 — Self-correct errors mid-sentence

When you catch yourself making a grammar error (*"He go to school"*), correct
mid-sentence: *"He go to... sorry, he goes to school"*. Self-correction
signals band 7+ grammatical range.

## High-Yield Grammar Structures (7 high-yield structures)

These are the kinds of grammar structures IELTS Speaking examiners reward
most. Focus on these to move from band 6 to band 7+ on grammatical range.

1. **Conditionals** — zero / first / second / third. Practice with common
   topics: weather, free time, work, family.
2. **Comparatives and superlatives** — *"more ... than"* / *"-er than"* /
   *"the most ..."* / *"-est"*. Practice with common topics: hobbies,
   transport, food.
3. **Passive voice** — *"Reading is enjoyed by many people"*. Practice with
   common topics: policy, history, environment.
4. **Modals** — *"can"*, *"could"*, *"may"*, *"might"*, *"must"*,
   *"should"*, *"would"*. Practice with common topics: advice, possibility,
   obligation.
5. **Relative clauses** — *"who"*, *"which"*, *"that"*, *"where"*,
   *"when"*, *"whose"*. Practice with common topics: family, work, hobbies.
6. **Reported speech** — *"He said he was tired"*. Practice with common
   topics: news, conversations.
7. **Past perfect / past continuous** — *"I had been studying"*, *"I was
   reading"*. Practice with common topics: past experiences.

## Previous Year Patterns (IELTS 2018–2025)

IELTS Speaking grammatical range and accuracy has been scored as a separate
criterion consistently since 2018. Over 2018–2025:

- 2018–2020: grammatical range was scored alongside lexical resource in some
  versions of the band descriptors. Most candidates scored band 6.
- 2021–2023: grammatical range was split into a separate criterion. Most
  candidates scored band 5–6 on grammatical range.
- 2024–2025: grammatical range is scored separately, with explicit emphasis
  on mixing simple and complex sentences, conditionals, comparatives, passive
  voice, modals, and relative clauses. Most candidates score band 6–7 on
  grammatical range.

The pattern: IELTS Speaking grammatical range criterion has become more
**structure-focused** since 2018. The examiner scores whether the candidate
uses a range of grammatical structures WITHOUT introducing errors that drop
them into a lower band. Candidates who focus on accuracy outperform those
who focus on memorising complex grammar.
