# Journal Entries

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

A **journal entry** is the original written record of a transaction, kept in the **general journal** in the order of the date it occurred. Every transaction has **two equal effects**: one or more **debits** and one or more **credits**, and the **accounting equation** (Assets = Liabilities + Owner's Equity) must stay balanced after every entry.

#### Must-know rules:

- **Assets and Expenses** → **debit** to increase, credit to decrease.
- **Liabilities, Equity, and Income** → **credit** to increase, debit to decrease.
- Use **source documents** (invoice, receipt, credit note, cheque counterfoil) before journalising.

> WASSCE tip: questions often ask you to *journalise* a list of transactions — watch for the **narration** (a brief explanation) which earns you a mark.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### What a journal entry looks like

| Element | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Date | Day the transaction occurred |
| Accounts to be debited | Listed first, one per line |
| Accounts to be credited | Listed second, indented, one per line |
| Folio (J) | Reference back to the journal page |
| Narration | Brief explanation of the transaction |

#### The double-entry backbone

Each transaction has **equal debit and credit totals**. This is the rule that keeps the **accounting equation** balanced:

> **Assets (GH¢) = Liabilities (GH¢) + Owner's Equity (GH¢)**

Classify every account first — asset, liability, equity, income, or expense — then apply the rule of debit and credit for that class.

#### Types of journal entries

- **Simple entry** — one debit and one credit (e.g., buying stock by cash: Dr Stock, Cr Cash).
- **Compound entry** — two or more debits and one credit, or vice versa (e.g., buying goods partly by cash and partly on credit).
- **Contra entry** — recorded only in the cash book when cash is paid into bank, or bank is used to pay petty cash, so it carries both a debit and a credit but neither affects an outside account.
- **Adjusting entries** — at period end for accrued expenses, prepaid expenses, accrued income, depreciation, and the bad-debt provision.

#### Source documents and the prime books

Before writing any entry, the source document (invoice, debit note, credit note, receipt, paying-in-slip) supplies the figures. Sales and purchase journals feed totals into the general ledger through end-of-period journal entries — never post them one transaction at a time.

#### Common WASSCE question patterns

1. *Journalise* a transaction list for a small business.
2. Identify which **account type** is debited and credited.
3. Distinguish **returns inward** (sales returns, debit) from **returns outward** (purchases returns, credit).
4. Prepare the **two-/three-column cash book**, marking **C** in the folio column for contra entries.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Errors that demand a journal (suspense account)

When a **trial balance** does not balance, the difference is parked in a **Suspense Account**. A correcting journal entry must close it. Examples:

> - Sales of GH¢1,200 to K. Mensah were **omitted**: Dr K. Mensah 1,200 / Cr Sales 1,200.
> - Purchases of GH¢400 from A. Owusu were **credited to A. Owusu** but **omitted from Purchases**: Dr Suspense 400 / Cr Purchases 400 (then settle the suspense when located).
> - Cash received from a debtor of GH¢250 was **posted to the credit side of his account**: reverse with the right side; the suspense absorbs the imbalance.

#### Edge cases that examiners set

- **Capital introduction** — Dr Cash/Bank, Cr Capital (no expense was incurred).
- **Drawings by owner** — Dr Drawings, Cr Cash/Bank/Stock/Asset taken (reduces equity, not an expense).
- **Bad debts** written off — Dr Bad Debts, Cr Debtors. A **provision for doubtful debts** is an adjusting entry using the **Provision for Doubtful Debts Account**.
- **Accrued expense** at year-end — Dr Expense, Cr Accrued Expense (current liability).
- **Prepaid expense** — Dr Prepayment, Cr Expense (reduces the income-statement figure).

#### Connection to adjacent topics

Journal entries feed the **ledger**, whose balances feed the **trial balance**, whose agreed totals feed the **final accounts** (Trading, Profit or Loss, and Balance Sheet). Errors missed here cascade into misstated income statements and balance sheets — examiners test that link by giving you a faulty journal and asking you to *restate the financial position*.

#### Common mistakes

- **Debiting the seller** instead of the affected asset or expense account when goods are bought on credit.
- Recording a transaction only in the **cash book** and forgetting the offsetting entry.
- Forgetting that a **contra entry** still needs a folio mark (C) and a narration; it does **not** appear in the general journal.
- Writing the narration **before** the debits and credits, costing marks for order.

#### Practice prompts

1. On 5 March 2026, Goods costing GH¢3,000 were sold to Y. Boateng for GH¢4,500, half cash and half on credit. *Write the journal entry and the contra entry if any.*
2. The trial balance of K. Asare's business shows a suspense credit of GH¢600. Investigations reveal (i) sales daybook was **undercast** by GH¢400 and (ii) a cheque received from a debtor for GH¢200 was correctly entered in the cash book but **not posted**. *Pass the correcting journal entries.*
