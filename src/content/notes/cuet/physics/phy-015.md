# Electrostatics

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Electrostatics studies **electric charges at rest** — the forces between them, the fields they create, the potentials they produce, and the energy stored in capacitors. Coulomb's inverse-square law governs point charges, while Gauss's law gives a clean shortcut for symmetric charge distributions.

| Quantity | Symbol | SI Unit | Relation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Charge | q | coulomb (C) | q = ne, n integer |
| Electric field | E | N/C or V/m | E = kQ/r² |
| Electric potential | V | volt (J/C) | V = kQ/r |
| Capacitance | C | farad (F) | C = Q/V |
| Permittivity of free space | ε₀ | C²/(N·m²) | ε₀ = 8.854 × 10⁻¹² |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"Field falls faster than potential"** — E ∝ 1/r² (sharp drop) while V ∝ 1/r (gentle drop). For a dipole, E ∝ 1/r³ and V ∝ 1/r². Remember the ladder **Q → 1/r → 1/r² → 1/r³** as you move from potential to field for charge, monopole, dipole.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Laws and Definitions

Coulomb's law states that the electrostatic force between two point charges is **F = k·q₁q₂/r²**, where k = 1/(4πε₀) ≈ 9 × 10⁹ N·m²/C², q in coulombs, and r in metres. The force acts along the line joining the charges — attractive for unlike signs, repulsive for like signs. Charge is **quantised** (q = ne, n ∈ ℤ) and **conserved** in every isolated system.

The **electric field** is defined as the force per unit positive test charge: **E = F/q_test**, in N/C (dimensionally identical to V/m). A test charge is hypothetical and infinitesimally small so it does not disturb the source field. For a point charge, E = kQ/r² radially outward (for +Q) or inward (for −Q).

#### Concept Comparison Matrix

| Concept | Formula | How it Scales | Sign / Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Force between two charges | F = kq₁q₂/r² | 1/r² | Along line joining charges |
| Field of point charge | E = kQ/r² | 1/r² | Radial, outward for +Q |
| Potential of point charge | V = kQ/r | 1/r | Scalar, + for +Q, − for −Q |
| Potential energy of pair | U = kq₁q₂/r | 1/r | + if like, − if unlike |
| Field of dipole on axial line | E_axial = 2kp/r³ | 1/r³ | Along dipole axis |
| Field of dipole on equatorial line | E_eq = kp/r³ | 1/r³ | Anti-parallel to p |

> 📌 **Formula Check:** All formulas above use k = 1/(4πε₀) ≈ 9 × 10⁹ N·m²/C². ε₀ = 8.854 × 10⁻¹² C²/(N·m²). The dimensional formula of E is [M L T⁻³ A⁻¹] and of V is [M L² T⁻³ A⁻¹].

#### Gauss's Law and Capacitance

Gauss's law in integral form: **Φ_E = ∮ E·dA = q_enc/ε₀**, where Φ_E is in N·m²/C. It is most useful for highly symmetric charge distributions — infinite line of charge, infinite plane sheet, and uniformly charged sphere. The **electric flux** through a surface is Φ_E = E·A·cosθ, where θ is the angle between E and the outward area vector.

Capacitance measures the ability to store charge per unit potential: **C = Q/V**, with C in farads. For a parallel-plate capacitor, C = ε₀A/d, where A is plate area in m² and d is separation in m. Inserting a dielectric of constant κ multiplies capacitance by κ and reduces the internal field by 1/κ.

#### Capacitor Combinations and Energy

- **Series:** 1/C_eq = 1/C₁ + 1/C₂ + … → equivalent is **less than** the smallest individual C.
- **Parallel:** C_eq = C₁ + C₂ + … → sum of individual capacitances.

Energy stored in a charged capacitor: **U = ½CV² = ½QV = Q²/(2C)**, in joules. Energy density in the field is u = ½ε₀E².

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** Two point charges q₁ = +4 μC and q₂ = −6 μC are placed 30 cm apart in vacuum. (i) Find the magnitude and nature of the electrostatic force between them. (ii) At what distance from q₁ along the line joining them is the electric field zero?

#### Solution:

(i) Using Coulomb's law:
F = k·q₁·q₂ / r²
F = (9 × 10⁹) · (4 × 10⁻⁶) · (6 × 10⁻⁶) / (0.30)²
F = (9 × 10⁹) · (24 × 10⁻¹²) / 0.09
F = (0.216) / 0.09
F = **2.4 N (attractive)** because the charges have opposite signs.

(ii) Let x be the distance from q₁ where E_net = 0. The zero-field point lies **outside** the segment, on the side of the smaller-magnitude charge (q₁ = 4 μC), since fields must oppose there.

k·q₁ / x² = k·q₂ / (x + 0.30)²

Taking the square root:
√q₁ / x = √q₂ / (x + 0.30)
2 / x = √6 / (x + 0.30)
2(x + 0.30) = x√6
2x + 0.60 = 2.449x
0.60 = 0.449x
x ≈ **1.337 m** from q₁ (on the side opposite q₂).

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students often place the null point between the charges for opposite-sign pairs — but the field is **stronger** near the larger magnitude (q₂ = 6 μC) and **weaker** near the smaller, so the null point cannot lie between them. It always lies on the side of the smaller charge, outside the segment.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Boundary Behaviour

The electric field of a uniformly charged **infinite plane sheet** is E = σ/(2ε₀), independent of distance — a striking boundary case where 1/r² intuition fails. For a conductor in electrostatic equilibrium, the field **inside** is zero and all excess charge resides on the outer surface. At a sharp conducting tip, surface charge density and field magnitude diverge (the principle behind lightning rods and corona discharge).

For an isolated charged conductor of radius R carrying charge Q, the surface potential is V = kQ/R. Two connected conducting spheres share charge in proportion to their radii: **Q₁/Q₂ = R₁/R₂**, because they equilibrate to a common potential.

#### Advanced Traps and Exceptions

| Trap | Why It Fails | Correct Approach |
|---|---|---|
| Using V = kQ/r for a dipole at origin | V is not defined for net-zero charge at a single point | Use V_dip = kp·cosθ/r² for dipole |
| Treating d as constant when battery is disconnected | With V constant (battery connected), Q ∝ C and U ∝ C | With Q constant (disconnected), V ∝ 1/C and U ∝ 1/C |
| Adding capacitances like resistors | Series C is harmonic mean; parallel is arithmetic sum | Memorise: "Series is like parallel-R, parallel is like series-R" |
| Forgetting work done by external agent vs field | W_field = −ΔU; W_external = +ΔU for same displacement | Distinguish sign convention before calculating |
| Applying Gauss's law to non-symmetric surfaces | Flux through irregular Gaussian surface is not simply E·A | Restrict Gauss's law to spherical, cylindrical, planar symmetry |

#### Dielectric Mechanics

When a dielectric slab of constant κ is inserted between capacitor plates (battery still connected), C becomes κC₀, Q becomes κQ₀, V stays fixed at battery EMF, E inside the dielectric becomes E₀/κ, and U becomes κU₀. If the battery is **disconnected first**, Q stays fixed, V drops by factor κ, E drops by factor κ, and U drops by factor κ — the dielectric is pulled into the capacitor.

#### Connections to Adjacent Topics

Electrostatics feeds directly into **Current Electricity** (capacitor discharge through resistors gives exponential decay with τ = RC), **Magnetic Effects of Current** (a moving charge creates both E and B fields, related by the Lorentz force F = qE + qv × B), and **Electromagnetic Waves** (accelerating charges radiate; static fields store energy density ½ε₀E²). The work done against an electrostatic field is path-independent — this defines a **conservative field**, the same class as gravity.

#### Practice Prompts

1. A parallel-plate capacitor with C = 6 μF is charged to 100 V and then disconnected from the battery. A dielectric slab of κ = 4 is slid between the plates. Find the new charge, voltage, capacitance, and energy stored. *(Answer scaffold: Q stays at 6 × 10⁻⁴ C; new C = 24 μF; new V = 25 V; new U = 1.875 J — original was 30 J, so energy decreases by 6×.)*
2. Three charges +q, +q, and −q sit at the vertices of an equilateral triangle of side a. Find the net force on the −q charge due to the other two. *(Answer scaffold: each repulsive force has magnitude F = kq²/a²; the two forces are symmetric at 60° apart and combine to give F_net = √3·kq²/a² directed along the perpendicular bisector toward the midpoint of the +q–+q side.)*
