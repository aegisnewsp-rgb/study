# Mensuration: Areas and Volumes

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Mensuration covers the measurement of **plane figures** (2D area, perimeter) and **solids** (3D volume, curved and total surface area). For NABTEB Mathematics, expect 2–4 questions per paper drawing from triangles, circles, trapeziums, cylinders, cones, spheres, and frustums.

- **Plane figures:** Triangle area = ½ × base × height; Circle area = πr², circumference = 2πr; Trapezium = ½(a + b)h.
- **Solids:** Cylinder V = πr²h, CSA = 2πrh; Cone V = ⅓πr²h, CSA = πrl where l = √(r² + h²); Sphere V = ⁴⁄₃πr³, TSA = 4πr²; Frustum V = ⅓πh(R² + Rr + r²).
- **Always convert units** (cm³ ↔ m³, cm ↔ m) before substitution, and **use radius, not diameter**, in every πr²-type formula.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Plane Figure Formulas

Two-dimensional mensuration asks for area (m²) or perimeter (m). The triangle rule ½ × base × height applies to any triangle once the perpendicular height is identified, not the slant side. A trapezium requires the two parallel sides (a, b) plus perpendicular height h. The circle family introduces arc length s = rθ (θ in radians) and sector area ½r²θ, which NABTEB sets as conversion-style items mixing degrees and radians.

| Shape | Area | Perimeter / Arc |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Triangle | ½ × b × h | b₁ + b₂ + b₃ |
| Trapezium | ½(a + b)h | sum of all four sides |
| Circle | πr² | 2πr |
| Sector | ½r²θ | rθ (θ in rad) |

#### Solid Figure Formulas

For solids, you compute **volume (m³)**, **curved surface area CSA**, and **total surface area TSA = CSA + area of bases**. A cylinder has two circular bases, so TSA = 2πr² + 2πrh = 2πr(r + h). A cone has only one base, so TSA = πr² + πrl = πr(r + l), and its slant height l comes from the Pythagorean relationship l² = r² + h². A sphere has no base — TSA equals its entire surface, 4πr².

#### Typical NABTEB Question Patterns

- A water tank problem mixing litres (1 litre = 1000 cm³) with dimensions in cm or m.
- A cone with given slant height and radius asking for height via Pythagoras.
- A frustum formed by cutting off the top of a cone, where R and r are the two radii.
- A combined-solid problem (e.g., cylinder + hemisphere) requiring addition of volumes.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Common Traps

The highest-scoring NABTEB candidates lose marks on three recurring traps:

1. **Diameter-vs-radius slip:** A question states "diameter = 14 cm" but you substitute 14 into πr² instead of r = 7, inflating the answer 4×. Always halve the diameter first.
2. **CSA vs TSA confusion:** "Find the area of material needed to make a cylindrical pipe open at both ends" wants CSA only (2πrh). "Find the area to paint the outside including the ends" wants TSA (2πr(r + h)). Read for the word *including* or *excluding* the base(s).
3. **Slant height vs perpendicular height:** Cone volume uses h (perpendicular), but CSA and TSA use l (slant). Mixing them produces wrong answers even when the formula looks correct.

#### Worked Micro-Example

A frustum has R = 10 cm, r = 6 cm, h = 12 cm. Find its volume.

- Slant height l = √(h² + (R − r)²) = √(144 + 16) = √160 = 4√10 cm.
- V = ⅓πh(R² + Rr + r²) = ⅓ × π × 12 × (100 + 60 + 36) = ⅓ × π × 12 × 196 = 784π cm³ ≈ 2463 cm³.

#### Practice Prompts

1. A cylindrical drum has radius 0.35 m and height 1.2 m. Find its capacity in litres (1 m³ = 1000 L).
2. A cone of height 9 cm and base radius 12 cm is truncated at height 3 cm from the apex. Compute the volume of the resulting frustum.
