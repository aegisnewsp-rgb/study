# Verbal Analogies (Logical)

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.
A verbal analogy presents two word pairs in the form **A : B :: C : D** and asks you to find the option whose second pair reproduces the **same logical relation** as the first pair, in the **same direction**. The relation can be semantic (synonym, antonym, cause→effect, part→whole, degree, function) or associative (worker→product, tool→user, material→object).
**High-yield steps:** (1) decode the relation in A:B without glancing at C; (2) name the relation type explicitly (e.g., "part-of", "intensifier"); (3) match direction — ascending degree pairs must stay ascending; (4) preserve grammatical category (noun-noun, verb-noun).
**NAT-I/NTS pointers:** expect 5–10 analogy MCQs in Verbal Reasoning; ~40 seconds per item is realistic; traps usually swap antonym for synonym or invert direction.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core structure of an analogy item
Every analogy in NAT-I Verbal Reasoning follows the canonical stem **A : B :: C : D**, read aloud as "A is to B as C is to D". Your task is not to find a synonym for B, nor a word that "goes with" A — you must locate the *abstract relationship* that links A and B, then select a second pair whose members stand in that exact same relationship, oriented the same way.

#### Classification of relation types
Relations fall into two broad families.

**Semantic relations** are meaning-based and dominate the NTS test bank:
- **Synonym:** GLOOM : DESPAIR :: …
- **Antonym:** FRAUD : GENUINE :: …
- **Cause : Effect** (parallel causation in both pairs): DROUGHT : FAMINE :: FLOOD : DESTRUCTION
- **Effect : Cause** (reverse direction — a frequent trap)
- **Part : Whole** or **Whole : Part**
- **Degree / Intensity** (ascending or descending): HOT : SCORCHING :: COLD : FRIGID
- **Function / Purpose : Object** and its inverse
- **Pertainym** (a word that describes a domain): OCEANIC : OCEAN :: MARTIAN : MARS

**Associative (occupational) relations** describe a workplace link:
- Worker : Work-product (POET : ODE)
- Tool : User (STETHOSCOPE : DOCTOR)
- Material : Object (CEDAR : CHEST)
- Means : End (FERTILISER : YIELD)

#### Worked walk-through
Take **THESIS : ANTITHESIS :: … : …**. The relation is "an idea paired with its directly opposing idea" — a paired-opposites pattern. The correct option pair must also be two concepts that exist as conceptual opposites within the same system (e.g., SUMMATION : DISSIPATION, where one denotes accumulation and the other loss of the same quantity). Wrong options typically supply a generic synonym or a single word related to only one stem term.

#### Common traps and how to beat them
- **Thematic decoys:** both pairs in the distractor belong to the same topic (animals, tools) yet share no relational parity. Discard by restating the relation in plain English and checking the option against that restatement.
- **Direction inversion:** A→B is part-to-whole; the option gives whole-to-part. Reject.
- **Category slip:** the stem is noun-noun but the option mixes verb and noun. Reject.
- **Degree reversal:** WARM : HOT :: COOL : COLD preserves ascending warmth; COLD : COOL reverses direction and is wrong.

#### NTS exam pattern
NAT-I Verbal Reasoning places analogies early-to-mid in the section, often intermixed with sentence completion. Items use vocabulary drawn from intermediate-to-advanced English (NTS often tests pertainyms and degree pairs). Allocate ~40 seconds: decode relation in 15 s, scan four options in 20 s, verify in 5 s.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Deeper mechanism: relational abstraction
Analogical reasoning depends on **abstract structural mapping**, not surface resemblance. The brain (and the exam) wants you to extract a relation *template* — a slot-and-filler pattern such as `[intensifier of mild adjective] : [intensifier of moderate adjective] :: [intensifier of weak noun] : [intensifier of strong noun]`. Once the template is fixed, lexical content is interchangeable. Items where both pairs are about "animals" but only one preserves the template (e.g., predator:prey vs. cub:adult) test whether you are mapping structure or merely pattern-matching topics.

#### Edge cases that surface on NTS papers
- **Pertainym chains:** words ending in *-ic, -al, -ous, -ine* that mean "of or relating to". MULTIPLE → MULTIFARIOUS is not a pertainym; OCEAN → OCEANIC is. Memorise the suffix families.
- **Sequential / chronological analogies:** LARVA : PUPA :: TADPOLE : FROG (developmental stage 1 : stage 2). The trap offers stage 2 : stage 1 or a non-sequential organism pair.
- **Function-of vs. used-for:** SCISSORS : CUT (function) versus SCISSORS : SEAMSTRESS (user). The preposition matters; "is used by" is associative, "is used for" is functional.
- **Degree with negation:** BRAVE : COWARDLY (antonym) is NOT the same relation as BRAVE : RECKLESS (escalation). Distinguish opposition from intensification.
- **Triplet-style pseudo-analogies:** some NTS items embed three terms to disguise direction — A : B : C :: D : ?. Treat the operative pair as B:C and find D's match.

#### Two practice prompts
1. **Stem:** AUTHOR : BOOK :: COMPOSER : ?  Identify the relation, then choose the option that fills the blank with a *work-product* (not a tool, not a user).
2. **Stem:** EBB : FLOW :: ? : ?  State whether the relation is oppositional or sequential, then construct a parallel pair such as WAX : WANE or TIDE : RECEDE.

#### Connections and adjacent topics
Verbal analogies share cognitive mechanics with **syllogisms** (deductive mapping), **odd-one-out** (relation isolation), and **sentence completion** (contextual relation). Train yourself to verbalise the relation in one short clause — "B is the intensifier of A", "B is the user of A" — before looking at options; this habit transfers cleanly to NAT-I Antonyms & Synonyms and to NTS GAT Verbal items.
