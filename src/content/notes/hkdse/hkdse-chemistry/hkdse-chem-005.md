# V. Fossil Fuels and Carbon Compounds — HKDSE Chemistry Notes

This topic covers Topic V of the Compulsory Part of HKDSE Chemistry. It addresses fossil fuels (coal, petroleum, natural gas); homologous series, structural formulas and naming of carbon compounds (alkanes, alkenes, alkanols, alkanoic acids, esters); the cracking of petroleum and the reforming of naphtha; and the use of fossil fuels and the resulting environmental issues (air pollution, acid rain).

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Fossil fuels**: coal (formed from plant matter), petroleum (formed from marine organisms), natural gas (mainly methane, formed with petroleum). Non-renewable; major source of energy and raw materials for the chemical industry.
- **Petroleum refining**: fractional distillation separates crude oil into fractions based on boiling point. Lighter fractions (LPG, naphtha, gasoline) have lower boiling points and are more volatile.
- **Cracking**: breaks long-chain alkanes into shorter-chain alkanes and alkenes. Thermal cracking (high temperature) or catalytic cracking (catalyst).
- **Homologous series**: a family of organic compounds with the same general formula and similar chemical properties, differing by -CH₂- units. Examples: alkanes (CₙH₂ₙ₊₂), alkenes (CₙH₂ₙ), alkanols (CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH), alkanoic acids (CₙH₂ₙ₊₁COOH).
- **Naming**: IUPAC nomenclature. Alkanes end in -ane, alkenes in -ene, alkanols in -ol, alkanoic acids in -oic acid. Position number for double bonds and substituents.
- **Functional groups**: alkenes (C=C), alkanols (-OH on a saturated carbon), alkanoic acids (-COOH), esters (-COO-).
- **Alkanes**: saturated hydrocarbons (single bonds only); generally unreactive (substitution reactions with halogens).
- **Alkenes**: unsaturated hydrocarbons (one or more C=C); undergo addition reactions (with H₂, H₂O, HBr, Br₂).
- **Alkanols**: contain -OH group; undergo combustion, substitution, oxidation to alkanoic acids.
- **Alkanoic acids**: contain -COOH group; weak acids; undergo esterification with alkanols.
- **Esters**: contain -COO- group; formed by esterification; many have fruity smells; hydrolysed back to acid and alcohol.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing esterification (acid + alcohol → ester + water) with neutralisation (acid + alkali → salt + water).
- Forgetting that alkenes decolourise bromine water (a positive test for unsaturation).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Fossil fuels

**Coal**: a sedimentary rock formed from plant matter compressed over geological time. Types: peat, lignite, bituminous coal, anthracite (in order of increasing carbon content and energy density). Combustion produces CO₂, SO₂, NOₓ, particulates.

**Petroleum (crude oil)**: a mixture of hydrocarbons formed from marine organisms. Refined by fractional distillation in a fractionating column. Fractions (from top to bottom): refinery gases, gasoline, naphtha, kerosene, diesel oil, fuel oil, lubricating oil, paraffin wax, bitumen.

**Natural gas**: mainly methane (CH₄), with small amounts of ethane, propane and butane. Cleaner-burning than coal or oil; produces less CO₂ per unit of energy.

#### Cracking and reforming

**Cracking**: converts long-chain alkanes to shorter-chain alkanes and alkenes. Example: C₁₀H₂₂ → C₅H₁₂ + C₅H₁₀. Thermal cracking uses high temperature (~700-900°C); catalytic cracking uses a catalyst (e.g. zeolite) at lower temperature.

**Reforming**: converts straight-chain alkanes to branched alkanes and aromatic compounds. Used to produce high-octane gasoline.

#### Homologous series and naming

| Series | General formula | Functional group | Example |
|--------|-----------------|------------------|---------|
| Alkanes | CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ | None (C-C, C-H) | Ethane (C₂H₆) |
| Alkenes | CₙH₂ₙ | C=C | Ethene (C₂H₄) |
| Alkanols | CₙH₂ₙ₊₂O | -OH | Ethanol (C₂H₅OH) |
| Alkanoic acids | CₙH₂ₙO₂ | -COOH | Ethanoic acid (CH₃COOH) |
| Esters | CₙH₂ₙO₂ | -COO- | Ethyl ethanoate (CH₃COOC₂H₅) |

Naming follows IUPAC rules:

- Identify the longest carbon chain containing the functional group.
- Number the chain to give the lowest locant to the functional group.
- Name the substituents and their positions.
- For alkenes, include the position of the double bond (e.g. but-2-ene for CH₃CH=CHCH₃).

#### Reactions of alkanes, alkenes, alkanols, alkanoic acids

**Alkanes**: combustion (alkane + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O), substitution with halogens (CH₄ + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + HCl).

**Alkenes**: addition reactions (alkene + H₂ → alkane; alkene + HBr → bromoalkane; alkene + Br₂ → dibromoalkane; alkene + H₂O → alkanol, catalysed by acid). Test for unsaturation: decolourisation of bromine water (orange to colourless).

**Alkanols**: combustion; substitution (alkanol + HX → haloalkane + H₂O); oxidation (alkanol + [O] → alkanal → alkanoic acid with strong oxidising agents); dehydration (alkanol → alkene + H₂O, with concentrated H₂SO₄ or hot alumina).

**Alkanoic acids**: as weak acids (acid + alkali → salt + water; acid + carbonate → salt + water + CO₂); esterification (alkanoic acid + alkanol → ester + water, catalysed by concentrated H₂SO₄).

**Esters**: hydrolysis (ester + H₂O → alkanoic acid + alkanol, catalysed by acid or base). Used in flavours and fragrances.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-topic links

- **I. Planet Earth**: fossil fuel combustion is the source of greenhouse gases and acid rain.
- **XI. Chemistry of Carbon Compounds**: extends to isomerism, additional functional groups, polymers.
- **XIII. Industrial Chemistry (Elective)**: petrochemicals are the basis of industrial chemistry.

#### Exam technique

- **For naming questions**: identify the longest chain, number to give the lowest locant to the principal functional group, name substituents.
- **For reactions questions**: state the reagents, the conditions (temperature, catalyst), and the products. Use structural formulas to show the mechanism.
- **For "compare alkanes and alkenes" questions**: use a table with columns for saturation, typical reactions, and tests.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Cracking is the same as distillation." Distillation separates a mixture by boiling point; cracking chemically breaks molecules.
- "Esters are acids." Esters are derived from acids and alcohols but are not acidic.
- "All hydrocarbons are gases." Hydrocarbons range from gases (C1-C4) through liquids (C5-C17) to solids (C18+).
