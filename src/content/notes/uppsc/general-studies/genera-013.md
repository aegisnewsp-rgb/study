# Topic 13

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Polity and Governance for UPPSC RO/ARO covers the **Constitution of India**, the **Parliamentary System**, **Fundamental Rights (Articles 12–35)**, **Directive Principles (Articles 36–51)**, and **Fundamental Duties (Article 51A)**. Master the **Preamble's** three key words — *Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic* (Socialist and Secular were added by the **42nd Amendment, 1976**). Learn the five **writs**: Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, and Quo Warranto under **Article 32** (Supreme Court) and **Article 226** (High Court). Distinguish a **Money Bill** (certified by the Speaker, Rajya Sabha can only recommend) from an **Ordinary Bill**. Remember the **73rd and 74th Amendments** for Panchayati Raj and Municipalities. UPPSC RO/ARO typically asks 3–5 polity MCQs from this cluster.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Preamble and Source
The **Preamble** declares India a Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic with justice, liberty, equality, and fraternity. The phrase "We, the people of India" reflects **popular sovereignty**. The Preamble is **not a part of the Constitution originally** but the **Berubari Union (1960)** case held it was; **LIC of India (1995)** overruled that, declaring it is **not a law**, yet the **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)** ruling treats it as part of the **basic structure**.

#### Fundamental Rights vs Directive Principles
**Fundamental Rights (Part III, Articles 12–35)** are **justiciable** — enforceable by courts. They include Right to Equality (14–18), Right to Freedom (19–22), Right against Exploitation (23–24), Right to Religion (25–28), Cultural and Educational Rights (29–30), and Right to Constitutional Remedies (32). **DPSPs (Part IV, Articles 36–51)** are **non-justiciable** — they guide the State in policy-making. The Supreme Court applied DPSPs in **Minerva Mills (1980)** to strike down parts of the **42nd Amendment** that placed FRs subordinate to DPSPs.

#### Parliamentary System
The **President** is the nominal head; real executive power rests with the **Prime Minister** and the **Council of Ministers** (Article 74). A **Money Bill** can be introduced **only in Lok Sabha** with the **President's prior recommendation**, certified by the **Speaker**; Rajya Sabha has **14 days** to return it with recommendations. A **Joint Sitting** of Parliament (Article 108) is presided over by the **Speaker of Lok Sabha**.

#### Key Constitutional Bodies
The **Election Commission** (Article 324), **CAG** (Article 148), **UPSC/SPSC** (Articles 315–323), and **Finance Commission** (Article 280) are constitutional. The **Finance Commission** is constituted every **five years** (or earlier) by the President. The **Attorney General** (Article 76) is the government's chief legal officer and has the right of audience in any court.

#### Common Exam Traps
A Money Bill is defined in **Article 110**. The **22nd Amendment (1969)** added **Mizoram** to the First Schedule as a UT. The **Panchayati Raj** has **three tiers** — Gram, Block, Zila — with elections conducted by the **State Election Commission** (not the Election Commission of India).

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Federalism and Centre–State Relations
The **Seventh Schedule** distributes legislative power via the **Union List (97 subjects)**, **State List (66 subjects)**, and **Concurrent List (47 subjects)**. Residuary powers lie with the Centre under **Article 248**. In case of conflict on a Concurrent subject, **Article 254** gives the **Central law supremacy**. **Article 356** (President's Rule) is imposed on the report of the **Governor** when constitutional machinery breaks down — this is the most-tested emergency provision in UPPSC. **Article 352** declares National Emergency on the basis of **war, external aggression, or armed rebellion** ("armed rebellion" replaced "internal disturbance" via the **44th Amendment, 1978**).

#### Judicial Review and Basic Structure
**Judicial Review** is implicit in the Constitution and was firmly established in **Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973)**, where the 13-judge bench laid down the **Basic Structure Doctrine** — Parliament cannot destroy the Constitution's basic features (federalism, secularism, judicial review, democracy, etc.) even through a constitutional amendment under **Article 368**.

#### Constitutional and Non-Constitutional Bodies
The **NITI Aayog** (replaced the **Planning Commission** in **2015**) and **Lokpal** (under the Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, **2013**) are **non-constitutional** bodies. The **NHRC** (National Human Rights Commission) was created by the **Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993**. Distinguish them from constitutional bodies like the **CAG** (whose reports are examined by the **Public Accounts Committee** of Parliament).

#### Practice Prompts
1. The words "Socialist" and "Secular" were added to the Preamble by which Constitutional Amendment? Which case held the Preamble is part of the basic structure?
2. Differentiate between a **Money Bill** and a **Financial Bill**. Can a Money Bill be introduced in Rajya Sabha? What is the time limit for Rajya Sabha to act on it?
