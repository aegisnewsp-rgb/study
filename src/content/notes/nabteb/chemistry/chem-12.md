# Amines and Polymers

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

An **amine** is an organic derivative of ammonia (NH₃) in which one or more hydrogen atoms are replaced by **alkyl (R–)** or **aryl (Ar–)** groups. Amines are classified by how many carbons sit directly on nitrogen: **primary (1°, R–NH₂)**, **secondary (2°, R₂NH)**, **tertiary (3°, R₃N)**, and **quaternary ammonium (R₄N⁺X⁻)**. The nitrogen carries a **lone pair**, making amines **basic** — alkyl groups donate electrons and *raise* basicity, while an aryl group like phenyl withdraws electrons by resonance and *lowers* basicity. Reactions to remember: **acylation** (acyl chloride + amine → amide), **Hofmann bromamide** (amide + Br₂/NaOH → 1° amine with one less carbon), and **diazotisation** of aromatic 1° amines with **HNO₂/HCl at 0–5 °C** to give **diazonium salts**, which couple with phenols/aromatic amines to form **azo dyes**. **Polymerisation** joins small **monomers** into long chains — **addition** (C=C, no by-product, e.g. poly(ethene), PVC, PTFE) or **condensation** (two functional groups eliminate **water/HCl**, e.g. nylon, Terylene). NABTEB favourites: classify an unknown amine, predict basicity order, draw the monomer of a named polymer, and write the repeat unit of nylon-6,6.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Classification and Nomenclature
Amines are subdivided by counting the alkyl/aryl groups bonded directly to nitrogen:

| Class | General form | Example | IUPAC name |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary | R–NH₂ | CH₃NH₂ | methanamine |
| Secondary | R–NH–R' | (CH₃)₂NH | N-methylmethanamine |
| Tertiary | R₃N | (CH₃)₃N | N,N-dimethylmethanamine |
| Quaternary | R₄N⁺ X⁻ | (CH₃)₄N⁺Cl⁻ | tetramethylammonium chloride |

Aromatic amines such as **aniline (C₆H₅NH₂)** are treated separately; their nitrogen lone pair is delocalised into the benzene ring, reducing availability for protonation.

#### Basicity of Amines
The lone pair on nitrogen accepts a proton, so amines act as **Brønsted–Lowry bases**. In aqueous solution, basicity depends on two opposing effects:

- **Inductive electron donation by alkyl groups** stabilises the conjugate acid R–NH₃⁺ (raises pKb above ~3.4). Order in aliphatic series: (CH₃)₂NH > CH₃NH₂ > (CH₃)₃N > NH₃, with the **dimethyl secondary amine** being most basic among small alkyl amines because three methyl groups sterically hinder solvation of the trimethylammonium ion.
- **Resonance withdrawal by an aryl group** in aniline spreads the lone pair into the ring (see resonance structures with the C=N⁺ double bond), making aniline a *much* weaker base than cyclohexylamine.

In water, a typical aliphatic amine has **Kb ≈ 10⁻³–10⁻⁴ (pKb 3–4)**, while aniline has **Kb ≈ 4 × 10⁻¹⁰ (pKb ~ 9.4)**.

#### Key Reactions
- **Reduction of nitro compounds**: Ar–NO₂ + 6[H] (Sn/HCl or Fe/HCl) → Ar–NH₂ + 2H₂O.
- **Hofmann bromamide**: R–CO–NH₂ + Br₂ + 4NaOH → R–NH₂ + Na₂CO₃ + 2NaBr + 2H₂O. *Carbon count decreases by one* — a frequent NABTEB trap.
- **Acylation**: R–NH₂ + R'–COCl → R'–CO–NHR + HCl.
- **Diazotisation**: C₆H₅NH₂ + NaNO₂ + 2HCl at 0–5 °C → C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ + NaCl + 2H₂O. The diazonium salt **couples** with phenol (pH 9–10) or with an aromatic amine (pH 4–5) to give a coloured azo dye (–N=N–).

#### Polymerisation
**Addition polymerisation** requires a C=C bond in the monomer (ethene, propene, vinyl chloride, tetrafluoroethene) and is initiated by **free radicals**, **Ziegler–Natta catalysts**, or UV light. Nothing leaves the reaction — *the polymer is the only organic product*. **Condensation polymerisation** requires **two functional groups per monomer** (or two complementary difunctional monomers), and each bond-forming step eliminates **water, HCl, or methanol**. Examples:

- **Nylon-6,6**: H₂N–(CH₂)₆–NH₂ + HOOC–(CH₂)₄–COOH → polyamide with repeat unit –NH–(CH₂)₆–NH–CO–(CH₂)₄–CO– + n H₂O.
- **Terylene (PET)**: HO–CH₂–CH₂–OH + HOOC–C₆H₄–COOH → polyester repeat unit –O–CH₂–CH₂–O–CO–C₆H₄–CO– + n H₂O.
- **Proteins** and **starch/cellulose** are natural polymers — proteins are **polyamides of α-amino acids**, polysaccharides are polyacetals of glucose.

A summary table useful for NABTEB:

| Feature | Addition | Condensation |
|---|---|---|
| Monomer requirement | C=C or C≡C | Two functional groups |
| By-product | None | Small molecule (H₂O, HCl) |
| Initiator | Radical/Ziegler–Natta | Heat or catalyst |
| Examples | poly(ethene), PVC, PTFE | nylon, Terylene, urea–formaldehyde |

**Thermoplastics** (poly(ethene), PVC) soften on reheating; **thermosets** (Bakelite, melamine) set permanently after first heating and cannot be remoulded. NABTEB tests both identification (name the polymer from monomer or vice versa) and the *type* of polymerisation.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases in Amine Chemistry
The distinction between 2° and 3° amines by **Hinsberg's test** exploits reactivity with benzenesulphonyl chloride: 1° amines form soluble sulfonamides that dissolve in KOH after deprotonation; 2° amines form insoluble sulfonamides; 3° amines do not react. Quaternary ammonium salts have **no lone pair on nitrogen** and therefore **cannot act as bases** — a frequent distractor in NABTEB MCQs where a "tertiary" and a "quaternary" compound are both offered as answers.

Diazonium salt stability is **strongly temperature-dependent**: aromatic diazonium salts are stable only **below 5 °C**; warming with water gives phenols (Ar–OH + N₂), warming with CuCl or CuBr gives **Sandmeyer** products (Ar–Cl, Ar–Br), and warming with CuCN gives Ar–CN. Aliphatic 1° amines with HNO₂ give **unstable diazo compounds that immediately liberate N₂**, producing alcohols, alkenes and alkyl halides — *not* useful azo dyes.

#### Mechanism Spotlight: Coupling of a Diazonium Salt
The diazonium ion Ar–N≡N⁺ acts as a **weak electrophile**. It attacks the activated ring of a phenol (or a tertiary aromatic amine) in an **electrophilic aromatic substitution**, restoring aromaticity by losing H⁺. The azo linkage –N=N– is responsible for the intense colour because of extended conjugation across both aromatic rings — this is the basis of indicator dyes (methyl orange, methyl red, congo red) and many food colourants tested in NABTEB practical integrated papers.

#### Polymer Mechanics and Failure Modes
Addition polymerisation proceeds by **initiation, propagation and termination**. A radical initiator (e.g. dibenzoyl peroxide) fragments under heat to give R•, which adds to the C=C bond to form a new radical that attacks the next monomer. Termination occurs by **coupling** (R–R) or **disproportionation**. **Ziegler–Natta catalysts** (TiCl₄/Al(C₂H₅)₃) allow stereoregular polymerisation, producing isotactic or syndiotactic polymers with higher crystallinity and density than the atactic form — relevant to the distinction between **HDPE and LDPE**.

Condensation polymerisation is **step-growth**: every monomer-end can react with every other end, so molecular weight rises slowly until late in the reaction. The **Carothers equation** relates degree of polymerisation *X̄ₙ* to the extent of reaction *p*:
#### X̄ₙ = 1 / (1 − p)
At *p* = 0.99, *X̄ₙ* = 100 — a high conversion is essential to obtain usable polymers, which is why condensation polymers require exact stoichiometry and removal of the small-molecule by-product (often under reduced pressure).

#### Worked Example
**Q:** Arrange in increasing order of basicity: NH₃, C₆H₅NH₂ (aniline), (CH₃)₂NH, (C₂H₅)₂NH.
**Method:** Aniline is resonance-stabilised → *weakest*. NH₃ is a reference. Both dialkylamines benefit from two electron-donating alkyl groups. Diethylamine is slightly more basic than dimethylamine because longer alkyl chains give stronger +I induction.
**Answer:** C₆H₅NH₂ < NH₃ < (CH₃)₂NH < (C₂H₅)₂NH.

#### Common Exam Traps
- Confusing **amide** (R–CO–NH₂) with **amine** (R–NH₂) — amides are *not* basic because the lone pair is delocalised into the carbonyl.
- Forgetting that **Hofmann bromamide** *reduces* the carbon chain by one.
- Writing **poly(ethene)** as "(C₂H₄)ₙ" — the repeat unit is **(–CH₂–CH₂–)** and the monomer is ethene C₂H₄; bracket style matters in NABTEB marking.
- Calling nylon a polyester — it is a **polyamide**.
