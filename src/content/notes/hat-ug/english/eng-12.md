# Direct and Indirect Speech

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Direct speech** reproduces the speaker's exact words inside quotation marks: *He said, "I am tired."* **Indirect speech** (reported speech) reports the same meaning inside the reporting clause, dropping the quotation marks and shifting tense, pronouns, and time/place markers: *He said (that) he was tired.*

Core shifts to memorise:
- **Tense back-shift** when the reporting verb is past: Present → Past (am/is/are → was/were; have/has → had; will → would; can → could; may → might).
- **Pronoun shift:** first person → the reporter; second person → the listener; third person unchanged.
- **Time/place shift:** now → then, today → that day, yesterday → the day before, tomorrow → the next day, here → there, this → that.
- **Imperative** → *to*-infinitive: *"Sit down."* → He told me to sit down.
- **Question** → statement order with **if/whether** (yes/no) or the wh-word (wh-questions): *"Are you coming?"* → He asked if I was coming.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Definition and the Reporting Structure
Every reported-speech sentence has two parts: a **reporting verb** (say, tell, ask, exclaim, warn…) and a **reported clause** carrying the original message. In direct speech the reported clause is verbatim and enclosed in inverted commas. In indirect speech the same message is fused into the main clause, so quotation marks disappear and the grammar of the reported clause bends to fit the reporting verb's tense.

#### Tense Back-Shift Rule
When the reporting verb is in the past tense, the verb inside the reported clause must shift one step into the past. The standard ladder is: Present Simple → Past Simple; Present Continuous → Past Continuous; Present Perfect → Past Perfect; Past Simple → Past Perfect; "will" → "would". Modal verbs convert as follows: can → could, may → might, shall → should, must → had to (for obligation) or stays "must" (for deduction). Back-shift is **not** applied when the reporting verb is present, when the statement is a permanent truth (*The teacher said that the earth revolves around the sun*), or when the information is still current at the moment of reporting.

#### Pronoun, Time, and Place Shifts
The reporter becomes the first-person reference, the listener becomes the second-person reference, and the third person stays the same. Time and place adverbials migrate to past-corresponding forms.

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| now | then |
| today | that day |
| yesterday | the day before |
| tomorrow | the next day / the following day |
| ago | before |
| here | there |
| this / these | that / those |

#### Reporting Verbs and Sentence-Type Conversions
- **Statements** use *say* (no indirect object) or *tell* (requires indirect object): *He said that…* / *He told me that…*
- **Yes/No questions** drop the auxiliary, add **if/whether**, and end with a full stop: *"Is he home?"* → She asked if he was home.
- **Wh-questions** keep the wh-word but use statement word order: *"Where do you live?"* → He asked me where I lived.
- **Commands and requests** become a **to-infinitive** clause: *"Open the door."* → He ordered/asked/told me to open the door.
- **Exclamations** use verbs like *exclaimed, cried, shouted, sighed, wondered* plus a *that*-clause: *"What a beautiful day!"* → She exclaimed that it was a very beautiful day.

#### Exam-Specific Pattern
In HAT-UG English, questions usually present a single sentence in direct speech and ask the student to rewrite it in indirect form, selecting the correct verb form, pronoun, and time-marker. Options typically test the modal shift, the back-shift of present perfect to past perfect, and the correct time adverbial (e.g., "the day before" vs "yesterday").

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Subjunctive Constructions
Some reporting verbs govern a special pattern. *Suggest, recommend, advise, demand, insist, request* can take (a) a **that-clause with the base form of the verb** (subjunctive): *He suggested that she **apply** early*, or (b) a **gerund** with a preposition: *He suggested applying early*. Verbs such as *warn, remind, congratulate, accuse, blame* are followed by a preposition + object + gerund: *She warned him against going out alone.* Mastering these is essential for HAT-UG error-spotting items that put a bare infinitive after *suggest* (correct) instead of a present participle (wrong).

#### When Back-Shift Does Not Apply
Three situations release the reported clause from back-shift:
1. The reporting verb is in the **present** (*He says he is busy*).
2. The information is a **universal/habitual truth** (*Newton said that gravity pulls objects toward the earth*).
3. The reported event is **still true at reporting time** (*She told me her brother lives in Lahore*).
Examiners exploit this by offering distractors with shifted tenses where no shift is required.

#### Common Mistakes
- Forgetting that **indirect questions invert to statement order**: *He asked where was she going* (wrong) vs *He asked where she was going* (right).
- Writing *He said me that…* instead of *He told me that…* — **say** never takes an indirect object directly; **tell** does.
- Leaving "now" or "here" in the reported clause when the new context is elsewhere in time/space.
- Converting *"must not"* (prohibition) wrongly to "would not" instead of "must not" / "was not to".
- Using "yesterday" in indirect speech — the correct form is "the day before" or "the previous day".

#### Worked Example
Direct: *She said, "I met him yesterday at the station, and I will call him tomorrow."*
Step 1 — reporting verb stays past ("said").
Step 2 — back-shift: *met* → *had met*; *will call* → *would call*.
Step 3 — pronouns unchanged (third person).
Step 4 — time: *yesterday* → *the day before*; *tomorrow* → *the next day*; *here* not present, so unchanged.
Indirect: *She said (that) she had met him the day before at the station and (that) she would call him the next day.*

#### Practice Prompts
1. Rewrite in indirect speech: *The teacher said to the students, "Complete your assignments before the bell rings."*
2. Convert: *"Where have you been all morning?" the officer asked the clerk.* Provide both a yes/no-style and a wh-question conversion approach, then justify whether back-shift is required if the clerk is still missing at the moment of reporting.
