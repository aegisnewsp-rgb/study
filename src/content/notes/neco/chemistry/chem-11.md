# Organic Chemistry: Alkanes and Alkenes

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Alkanes** are **saturated hydrocarbons** with only single C–C bonds; their general formula is **C_nH_(2n+2)** (e.g. methane CH₄, ethane C₂H₆, propane C₃H₈). Carbons are **sp³ hybridised** with bond angles ≈109.5°. **Alkenes** are **unsaturated hydrocarbons** containing at least one **C=C double bond**; general formula **C_nH_(2n)** (ethene C₂H₄, propene C₃H₆). Carbons of the double bond are **sp² hybridised**. Each series is a **homologous series** differing by –CH₂–. Alkanes undergo **free radical substitution** (e.g. CH₄ + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl under UV); alkenes undergo **electrophilic addition** across the C=C (e.g. C₂H₄ + Br₂ → CH₂BrCH₂Br). NECO hot points: distinguish saturated vs unsaturated by formula and by the **bromine water decolourisation test**.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Structure and Bonding
Alkanes have every carbon bonded to four other atoms using single **sigma (σ) bonds**, giving tetrahedral geometry. Alkenes contain one **double bond made of one σ + one pi (π) bond**; the π bond is formed by sideways overlap of p-orbitals and lies above and below the C–C plane, making the π electrons exposed to attacking reagents. This is why alkenes are more reactive than alkanes despite both being non-polar molecules.

#### Homologous Series and Isomerism
Both families show **homologous series** behaviour: melting point, boiling point and density rise gradually with molecular mass because larger molecules experience stronger **van der Waals (London dispersion) forces**. **Branched isomers** boil lower than straight-chain isomers (e.g. isobutane < n-butane) due to reduced surface contact. Alkanes show only **structural (chain) isomerism** from C₄ upwards; alkenes show chain, positional (C=C location) and **geometric (cis–trans) isomerism** because the C=C restricts rotation.

#### Chemical Reactions
**Alkanes:** combustion (CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O), free-radical substitution with halogens, and cracking to produce smaller alkanes + alkenes. **Alkenes:** **electrophilic addition** of H₂, HX, H₂O and X₂ across C=C; addition of HX follows **Markovnikov's rule** — H attaches to the carbon already carrying more hydrogens.

#### Tests for Unsaturation
- **Bromine in CCl₄ or bromine water:** decolourised instantly by alkenes, no change with alkanes.
- **Acidified KMnO₄ (Baeyer's test):** purple colour disappears with alkenes, persists with alkanes.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Preparation Methods
Alkanes are sourced from **petroleum** via **fractional distillation**; longer alkanes are broken down by **thermal or catalytic cracking** (e.g. C₁₀H₂₂ → C₈H₁₈ + C₂H₄), giving alkenes as by-products. In the lab, ethene is made by **dehydration of ethanol** with concentrated H₂SO₄ at **170 °C** (at 140 °C diethyl ether forms instead), or by **dehydrohalogenation** of an alkyl halide with alcoholic KOH.

#### Mechanism and Markovnikov Logic
In HX addition, the **H⁺ electrophile** attacks the π bond, generating a carbocation intermediate; Markovnikov's rule predicts that the **more stable (more substituted) carbocation forms**, because alkyl groups donate electron density inductively. So propene + HBr → 2-bromopropane (major) rather than 1-bromopropane. Hydration of ethene (C₂H₄ + H₂O → C₂H₅OH) is the industrial route to ethanol.

#### Worked Example
Name the product of the reaction between propene (CH₃–CH=CH₂) and HCl. Apply Markovnikov: H adds to CH₂ (already has 2 H), Cl to the middle carbon → **2-chloropropane, CH₃–CHCl–CH₃**.

#### Common Mistakes
Writing **C_nH_(2n) for alkanes** or **C_nH_(2n+2) for alkenes** reverses saturation logic. Cycloalkanes (e.g. cyclohexane C₆H₁₂) also follow C_nH_(2n) — do not confuse them with alkenes. A negative bromine-water test does **not** prove a compound is an alkane; it only proves absence of C=C unsaturation.

#### Practice Prompts
1. An unknown hydrocarbon has formula C₄H₈. State one chemical test that confirms it is unsaturated and write the observation.
2. Ethanol was heated with excess concentrated H₂SO₄ at 170 °C. Write the equation, name the product, and state the role of the acid.
