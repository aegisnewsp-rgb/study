# Cardiovascular System

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The cardiovascular system is a **closed circulatory network** built around a four-chambered muscular pump (two atria + two ventricles) and a branching vessel tree (arteries → arterioles → capillaries → venules → veins). The core structural pillars for NEET PG Anatomy are: chamber and septal anatomy, the four valves with their chordae/papillary supports, the conducting system, coronary supply, and the fetal shunts that close at birth.

- **Conducting system order:** SA node → AV node (0.3 s delay) → Bundle of His → right & left bundle branches → Purkinje fibres.
- **Coronary dominance** (~80% of people are right-dominant): RCA → posterior interventricular artery; SA node supplied by RCA in ~60%, AV node in ~80%.
- **Fetal shunts:** foramen ovale (atrial), ductus arteriosus (pulmonary trunk → aortic arch), ductus venosus (umbilical vein → IVC).
- **CO = SV × HR**; SV = EDV − ESV; EF (%) = (SV / EDV) × 100.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Chambers and Septa

The heart sits in the middle mediastinum enclosed by the pericardium. The right atrium receives the **SVC, IVC, and coronary sinus**; the left atrium receives four pulmonary veins (two from each lung). The interventricular septum has a thin **membranous part** (just below the aortic valve, common site of VSDs) and a thick muscular part. The left ventricular wall is roughly three times thicker than the right because it pumps against systemic pressure.

#### Valves and their Supports

Atrioventricular valves (**tricuspid** right, **mitral/bicuspid** left) are anchored by **chordae tendineae** to **papillary muscles**, preventing prolapse during systole. Semilunar valves (pulmonary and aortic) have **no chordae** — their cusps are kept in shape by their own crescentic form. The **aortic valve** has three semilunar cusps with sinuses that feed the **right and left coronary arteries**.

| Valve | Type | Position | Closure sound |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tricuspid | AV (with chordae) | Right AV orifice | S1 |
| Mitral | AV (with chordae) | Left AV orifice | S1 |
| Pulmonary | Semilunar (no chordae) | RV outflow | S2 |
| Aortic | Semilunar (no chordae) | LV outflow | S2 |

#### Conducting System and Innervation

The **SA node** in the upper right atrium sets the rate (pacemaker). Impulses travel to the **AV node** where a 0.3-second delay allows atrial emptying before ventricular contraction. The **Bundle of His** divides into right and left bundle branches running beneath the endocardium, ending in **Purkinje fibres** that depolarise the ventricular myocardium from apex upwards. **Sympathetic** fibres from T1–T5 accelerate the heart; **parasympathetic** vagal (CN X) fibres slow the SA node. Cardiac referred pain travels via T1–T5, explaining left-arm/ jaw pain in MI.

#### Pericardium and Sinuses

The fibrous pericardium is tough and anchors the heart. Inside, the serous pericardium has parietal and visceral (**epicardium**) layers. The **transverse pericardial sinus** lies between the aorta/pulmonary trunk (anterior) and SVC/atria (posterior) — surgeons pass a clamp through it to occlude both great vessels. The **oblique sinus** sits posterior to the left atrium.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Coronary Circulation and Clinical Correlates

The **left coronary artery** arises from the left aortic sinus and bifurcates into the **LAD** (anterior 2/3 of interventricular septum, anterior LV, apex) and the **circumflex** (left atrium, lateral LV). The **right coronary artery** runs in the right AV groove and supplies the right atrium, right ventricle, posterior 1/3 of the septum, and — in roughly **70–80% of people (right-dominant)** — gives off the posterior interventricular artery. Occlusion of the LAD produces **anteroseptal MI**; RCA occlusion produces **inferior MI** often with bradycardia (SA/AV nodal ischaemia). The great cardiac vein accompanies the LAD; the **coronary sinus** drains into the right atrium.

#### Fetal Circulation and Postnatal Closure

In utero, the placenta does the gas exchange. Oxygenated blood from the placenta travels via the **umbilical vein** → **ductus venosus** (bypassing the liver) → IVC → right atrium. Most is shunted across the **foramen ovale** into the left atrium; the rest goes to the right ventricle and is shunted from the pulmonary artery to the aortic arch via the **ductus arteriosus**, bypassing the non-functional lungs. At birth, lung expansion raises left atrial pressure, functionally closing the foramen ovale (anatomical closure → **fossa ovalis**). Rising O₂ tension closes the ductus arteriosus → **ligamentum arteriosum**; the ductus venosus → **ligamentum venosum**. Patency beyond 48 hours suggests a **patent ductus arteriosus (PDA)**, heard as a continuous machinery murmur.

| Shunt | Connects | Adult remnant |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Foramen ovale | Right atrium → Left atrium | Fossa ovalis |
| Ductus arteriosus | Pulmonary trunk → Aortic arch | Ligamentum arteriosum |
| Ductus venosus | Umbilical vein → IVC | Ligamentum venosum |

#### Common Mistakes and Image-Based Traps

- Mixing **auscultation sites** with anatomical position: the aortic valve is left-sided but heard at the **right 2nd intercostal space**; the pulmonary valve at the **left 2nd ICS**; tricuspid at the lower left sternal border; mitral at the **apex (5th ICS, midclavicular)**.
- Treating **Purkinje fibres as nerves** — they are modified cardiac muscle, hence the term *nodal tissue* applies only to the SA and AV nodes.
- Saying the **epicardium is the pericardium** — it is only the visceral layer of the serous pericardium and the outermost layer of the heart wall itself.

**Worked example:** A patient with heart rate 75 bpm and stroke volume 70 mL has **CO = 70 × 75 = 5250 mL/min (≈5.25 L/min)**. With EDV 120 mL, EF = (70/120) × 100 = **≈58%** (normal 55–70%). If SBP = 120 and DBP = 80 mmHg, MAP = 80 + ⅓(40) = **≈93 mmHg**.
