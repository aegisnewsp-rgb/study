# Reading Items in UNDANA's Language Section Present a

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Topic 3** of the UNDANA Admission Language paper tests two skill clusters: reading comprehension of short English passages and production of short functional texts (announcement, invitation, procedure). At 3% of the total score, expect roughly 3–5 multiple-choice items rather than long essays.

- **Reading focus**: locate the **main idea** (in many papers the topic sentence, sometimes the concluding sentence), distinguish **explicit information** from **implicit information (inference)**, and resolve **pronoun or lexical reference** (it, they, this, such).
- **Writing focus**: reproduce the **generic structure** and language features of **procedure** (goal → materials/ingredients → steps), **announcement** (opening → content → closing), and **invitation** (inviter → occasion → time/place → closing).
- **High-yield pointers**: procedures favour **Simple Present Passive** ("The water is boiled..."), announcements use **formal register** without contractions, and invitations state date, time, and venue explicitly.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Reading comprehension sub-skills

Reading items in UNDANA's Language section present a 100–200-word passage followed by questions that probe three cognitive levels. Knowing the level tells you whether to scan for a literal word or reason one step further.

| Sub-skill | What the question asks | Strategy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main idea | The author's central point | Read first and last paragraph; pick the option that is broad enough to cover all paragraphs |
| Explicit information | A fact directly stated | Scan for the keyword or synonym; the answer is in most keys paraphrased, not copied |
| Implicit information | A conclusion not written outright | Eliminate options literally in the text; choose the one that *must* be true given the stated facts |
| Reference | What a pronoun/lexical item refers to | Substitute the candidate noun back into the sentence; keep number and gender agreement |

#### Functional text production

Short functional texts follow predictable **generic structures** and **language features**. Examiners reward structure accuracy more than creativity.

- **Procedure text** (How to / How is … made?): goal → materials → steps in chronological order; uses **imperatives** or **Simple Present Passive**; adverbs of sequence (first, then, finally).
- **Announcement text**: opening stating the issuing body ("Attention, all students…") → content (what, when, where, who) → closing remark or signature; formal, no contractions.
- **Invitation text**: invitation line → occasion → specific date, time, place → RSVP request; semi-formal register allowed for social events.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge cases and frequent traps

Two traps appear repeatedly in UNDANA-style reading items. First, the **opening sentence is a hook**, not the topic sentence, so main-idea choices that quote it verbatim are wrong. Second, **"it" or "they"** can refer to a whole preceding clause rather than a single noun — substituting a single noun then breaks the sentence's grammar, exposing the trick.

| Common mistake | Why it loses marks | Correct approach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Picking a detail sentence as the main idea | Too narrow; does not cover all paragraphs | Choose the option that subsumes every paragraph |
| Selecting an explicit answer for an inference question | Repeats the text instead of reasoning | Eliminate literal matches; keep only the *must-be-true* option |
| Writing "How to make tea" actively | Wrong generic structure and voice for a procedure | Use "How is tea made?" and Simple Present Passive throughout |
| Using "we'll" or "gonna" in an announcement | Informal register contradicts the text type | Keep full forms ("we will") and third-person issuing body |

#### Worked micro-example

Passage excerpt: *"Kupang in many papers suffers water shortages during the dry season. The local government has built new reservoirs. Residents are urged to use water wisely."*

Question: *"What is the main idea of the passage?"* — Correct answer: *"Kupang is responding to dry-season water shortages."* The hook sentence is about the problem; the topic sentence names the *response*, which is what every paragraph supports.

#### Practice prompts

1. In a 150-word passage about coral bleaching near Kupang, identify which sentence is the topic sentence and justify your choice in one sentence.
2. Rewrite the following informal invitation as a semi-formal invitation following the standard generic structure: *"Hey friends, come to my birthday Saturday at my house around 7. It'll be fun!"*
