# Venn Diagrams

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

A **Venn diagram** pictures sets as overlapping circles drawn inside a rectangle (the **Universal Set U**). Each circle is a set, and the overlap shows elements common to both. NAT-I (NTS) tests this topic through numeric problems on people/things in overlapping groups, worth about 4% of the Analytical Reasoning paper (in most keys 1–2 MCQs, 1 mark each).

- **n(A ∪ B) = n(A) + n(B) − n(A ∩ B)** — the inclusion–exclusion rule for two sets (unitless count).
- **n(A ∪ B ∪ C)** uses the three-set version with a +n(A∩B∩C) correction.
- **Region labels to memorise:** *Only A*, *Only B*, *A∩B only*, *A∩B∩C*, *None* (outside all circles, inside U).

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"Add all, Subtract pairs, Add triple"** — the three-set inclusion–exclusion sign pattern is **+ − − +**. Forget the final **+** and the answer runs short.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Notation and Region Logic

Every region inside the rectangle has a precise meaning. *"Only A"* means an element belongs to A and to **neither** B nor C. *"A ∩ B"* means in A **and** B, also C. Mis-reading these is the single biggest score-killer in NTS-style questions.

#### Set Operators Compared

| Operator | Symbol | English meaning | Region in diagram |
|---|---|---|---|
| Union | A ∪ B | A **or** B (or both) | Everything inside the two circles |
| Intersection | A ∩ B | A **and** B together | The lens-shaped overlap |
| Complement | A′ | Everything in U **but not** A | Outside circle A, inside rectangle |
| Symmetric Difference | A △ B | In A or B but **not both** | The two crescent parts |
| Disjoint sets | A ∩ B = ∅ | No shared elements | Circles do not touch |

#### Key Formulas with Variables Defined

- **n(A ∪ B)** = n(A) + n(B) − n(A ∩ B), where **n(X)** is the cardinality (count of elements), a unitless integer ≥ 0.
- **n(A ∪ B ∪ C)** = n(A) + n(B) + n(C) − n(A∩B) − n(B∩C) − n(A∩C) + n(A∩B∩C).
- **n(Only A)** = n(A) − n(A∩B) − n(A∩C) + n(A∩B∩C).
- **n(Exactly two sets)** = n(A∩B) + n(B∩C) + n(A∩C) − 3·n(A∩B∩C).
- **n(At least one)** = Total surveyed − n(None).

#### Three-Step Method for NTS Questions

1. Sketch three circles inside the rectangle and label all eight interior regions.
2. Translate each English phrase into a region: *"only doctors"* → Only Doctor; *"both doctor and teacher"* → Doctor ∩ Teacher.
3. Write one equation per given statement, then solve for the unknown using inclusion–exclusion.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** In a survey of 120 people, 60 like Tea (T), 50 like Coffee (C), and 30 like both. How many like **exactly one** of the two drinks, and how many like **neither**? 
(A) 50, 20 
(B) 80, 40 
(C) 70, 50 
(D) 60, 30

#### Solution:
Step 1 — Find the union: n(T ∪ C) = 60 + 50 − 30 = **80** people. 
Step 2 — "Exactly one" = n(T) + n(C) − 2·n(T∩C) = 60 + 50 − 2(30) = 80 − 60 = **50** people. Equivalently, Only T = 60 − 30 = 30 and Only C = 50 − 30 = 20, total 50. 
Step 3 — "Neither" = 120 − 80 = **40** people.

Answer: **(B) 80, 40** for union/exactly-one breakdown; the "exactly one" count is **50** and the "neither" count is **40**.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Many students subtract 30 only once and report "exactly one = 80" — they have actually computed the union, not "exactly one." Remember: *exactly one* = union − intersection = 80 − 30 = 50.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Boundary Conditions

- **"Some" is not a fraction.** In NTS wording, *"some A are B"* means at least one element lies in A ∩ B; it does **not** mean 50%. Treat it as ≥1.
- **Minimum-value problems.** When a region is described only by *"can be"*, assume the smallest valid count (in most keys 0) to minimise the answer; assume the largest valid count (equal to the smaller circle) to maximise it.
- **Three-set sign trap.** Skipping the final +n(A∩B∩C) under-counts the union by exactly the size of the triple overlap. Always circle the sign pattern **+ − − +** before writing the formula.
- **Scale is irrelevant.** NTS diagrams are logic diagrams; circle sizes carry no quantitative meaning. Two correct diagrams of different shapes are both valid.
- **De Morgan link.** (A ∪ B)′ = A′ ∩ B′. Useful when a question asks "how many like *neither* A nor B?" — compute the complement of the union.
- **Syllogism bridge.** "All A are B; no B is C" maps to circle A fully inside circle B, with circle C disjoint from B — hence no element can be in A∩C.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. *Minimum problem:* In a class of 50, every Maths student also studies Physics, and 20 study both. 10 study neither. Find the minimum number who study Physics only.
2. *Three-set problem:* 100 students, 40 in A, 35 in B, 30 in C. A∩B = 10, B∩C = 8, A∩C = 5, A∩B∩C = 3. Verify the union and then compute "exactly two" and "none".
