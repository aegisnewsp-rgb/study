# The Seventh Schedule of the Constitution Divides Legislative

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The **Seventh Schedule** of the Constitution divides legislative power among the **Union List** (97 entries — defence, railways, atomic energy), **State List** (66 entries — police, public order, agriculture), and **Concurrent List** (47 entries — criminal law, marriage, electricity). **Residuary powers** rest with Parliament under **Article 248** read with Entry 97 of the Union List.

During a **National Emergency (Article 352)**, the Union List becomes paramount and Parliament may legislate on State List subjects (**Article 250**). **Article 356** (President's Rule) is distinct — it is invoked on the breakdown of constitutional machinery in a state, not on external aggression.

- **Governor**: appointed by the President under **Article 153**, holds office during presidential pleasure (**Article 156**).
- **Inter-State Council**: statutory-advisory body under **Article 263** for Centre–State coordination.
- **Sarkaria Commission (1983)** and **Punchhi Commission (2007)** shape modern Centre–State relations.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Three Lists under Schedule VII

Articles 245 and 246 carve India's legislative competence into three exclusive or overlapping domains. Parliament alone legislates on the Union List; state legislatures alone handle State List subjects in normal times; both can legislate on the Concurrent List, with Union law prevailing in conflict under **Article 254**.

| List | Entries | Examples | Override Rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Union | 97 | Defence, atomic energy, currency, foreign affairs | Supremacy during Emergency (Art. 250) |
| State | 66 | Police, public order, agriculture, prisons | Subject to Union supremacy in concurrent conflict |
| Concurrent | 47 | Criminal law (IPC), marriage, electricity, drugs | Union law prevails if repugnant (Art. 254) |

#### Governor and Article 356

The **Governor** acts as the constitutional head of the state, an agent of the Centre, and a link between state and Union executives. Beyond routine assent, the Governor exercises **discretionary powers** in specific situations such as recommending President's Rule, reserving a bill for presidential consideration under **Article 200**, and deciding on a hung assembly.

Article 356 lapses after **two months** unless approved by both Houses of Parliament. Continuation beyond six months requires an emergency already in operation. The Sarkaria Commission recommended a **floor test on the floor of the House** before imposing President's Rule to prevent misuse.

#### Coordination Mechanisms

- **Article 263**: Inter-State Council chaired by the Prime Minister.
- **Article 261**: Full faith and credit clause between states.
- **Articles 301–307**: Freedom of trade, commerce and intercourse, subject to reasonable restrictions in public interest.
- **Zonal Councils** (statutory, 1956) differ constitutionally from the Inter-State Council.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Doctrine of Harmonious Construction

Under **Article 254**, when a Union law and a State law on a Concurrent List subject clash, the Union law prevails and the State law becomes void *to the extent of repugnancy*. However, if the State law was reserved for presidential assent and received it, the State law prevails in that territory. The doctrine of **harmonious construction** requires courts to interpret both laws to give effect to each where possible, rather than declaring either void wholesale.

#### Common Traps and Practice Prompts

A regular confusion is between **Article 352 (National Emergency)** and **Article 356 (President's Rule)**. Article 352 is proclaimed on grounds of war, external aggression, or armed rebellion and extends Parliament's power over State List subjects. Article 356 deals with internal state-level breakdown — different ground, different duration rules, different parliamentary approvals.

1. **Practice prompt 1**: "Residuary powers of legislation under the Indian Constitution vest in the Parliament under which article?" — Answer: Article 248 read with Entry 97, Union List.
2. **Practice prompt 2**: Identify two distinctions between the Inter-State Council and Zonal Councils — constitutional basis vs. statutory basis, and all-India membership vs. zonal membership.
