# International Organisations & Summits

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> One-screen cheat sheet: pair the body with its headquarters and founding year.

Most MAT questions on this topic collapse into a three-column match: **organisation, headquarters, founding year**. The United Nations (founded 24 October 1945, New York) anchors almost every other question because most specialised agencies — WHO (Geneva, 1948), UNESCO (Paris, 1945), UNICEF (New York, 1946), FAO (Rome, 1945) — sit under it. Bretton Woods twins IMF and World Bank both work out of Washington, D.C. but have different mandates: IMF guards balance-of-payments, World Bank lends for development.

| Body | HQ city | Born |
|---|---|---|
| United Nations | New York | 1945 |
| WHO | Geneva | 1948 |
| NATO | Brussels | 1949 |
| ASEAN | Jakarta | 1967 |
| SAARC | Kathmandu | 1985 |
| SCO | Beijing | 2001 |

> 💡 **Memory Hook (UN agencies):** "**U**NICEF lives in **N**ew York, **W**HO works in **G**eneva, **U**NESCO paints in **P**aris, **F**AO eats in **R**ome" — read the capital letter of each city to recall the body.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Working layer: how the bodies interconnect, where they sit in the global order, and how MAT frames its distractors.

#### The UN family tree

The **United Nations** has six principal organs — General Assembly, Security Council, ECOSOC, International Court of Justice, Secretariat, and the now-suspended Trusteeship Council. The Security Council carries the only veto: five permanent members (USA, UK, France, Russia, China — the P5) plus ten non-permanent members elected for two-year terms. MAT loves testing whether a student knows that non-permanent members cannot veto.

Specialised agencies are functionally autonomous but linked to the UN through ECOSOC cooperation agreements. Confusing agency with parent body is a regular trap — UNESCO is *not* the UN, and OPEC is intergovernmental but never joined the UN system.

| Body | Parent? | Mandate in one phrase |
|---|---|---|
| UNICEF | UN agency | Child survival and education |
| WHO | UN agency | Global public health |
| IMF | Bretton Woods (independent of UN) | Exchange-rate and BoP stability |
| World Bank | Bretton Woods (independent of UN) | Development lending to low-income states |
| OPEC | Intergovernmental (not UN) | Coordination of petroleum exports |
| NATO | Military alliance (not UN) | Collective defence under Article 5 |

#### Regional and political groupings

Three layers matter for MAT: economic forums (G7, G20), regional cooperation blocs (EU, ASEAN, SAARC, African Union, SCO), and security alliances (NATO, Quad). G20 started in 1999 after the Asian financial crisis; it now has 19 countries plus the European Union and, since the 2023 New Delhi Summit, the African Union. G7 is a Western subset that predates G20. BRICS expanded in 2024 to include Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, UAE and Saudi Arabia; the membership list rotates, so the current composition must be verified against the official communique.

> 📌 **Recall anchor:** Summits rotate presidency. India held the G20 presidency in 2023, Brazil in 2024, South Africa in 2025 — the order is alphabetical by host country name and follows the troika pattern (past, current, next host).

#### COP and WEF

COP summits are the Conference of Parties to UNFCCC. Paris (COP21, 2015) produced the Paris Agreement; Dubai hosted COP28 (2023), Baku hosted COP29 (2024), and Belem, Brazil is scheduled for COP30 (2025). The World Economic Forum meets at Davos every January, founded by Klaus Schwab in 1971.

#### Resolving a representative question

A typical four-option MCQ reads: *"The headquarters of the International Atomic Energy Agency is located at:"* with options New York, Geneva, Vienna, Paris.

Step 1 — Eliminate by family: IAEA is a UN-system body but operates independently from the New York cluster.
Step 2 — Eliminate UNESCO: UNESCO sits in Paris; IAEA is not a cultural body.
Step 3 — Eliminate WHO: WHO occupies Geneva; IAEA deals with atomic energy, not health.
Step 4 — Match by function: Vienna hosts IAEA — and also OPEC, which is a recurring distractor for the same city.

Answer: **Vienna**.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Options A (New York), B (Geneva) and D (Paris) all host well-known UN bodies, which is exactly why the question is framed this way. Students who recall only "IAEA is part of the UN family" pick New York. The correct tie is *function → city*, not *family → city*.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Edge cases, mechanism quirks, and the distinctions MAT stretches into negative-marking territory.

#### Distinctions MAT regularly blurs

| Frequently confused pair | Actual difference |
|---|---|
| IMF vs World Bank | Both in Washington, D.C.; IMF = short-term liquidity, BoP crises; World Bank = long-term project loans, poverty reduction |
| G7 vs G20 | G7 = 7 advanced economies + EU; G20 = 19 economies + EU + AU (since 2023); G20 is the broader forum |
| Shanghai Five vs SCO | Shanghai Five (1996) was a security grouping of China, Russia, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan; SCO (2001) added Uzbekistan |
| NATO as offensive vs defensive | Article 5 is a defensive trigger; the treaty is collective-defence, not collective-offence |
| BRICS vs BRICS+ | BRICS = Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa (2009 first summit); BRICS+ expanded in 2024 |
| UNESCO vs UN | UNESCO is one agency of the UN, headquartered in Paris, with its own Director-General |

#### Edge cases examiners exploit

1. **Veto arithmetic.** The P5 are the *only* members with veto power. Non-permanent members can vote on substantive matters but cannot block a resolution. Abstention is *not* a veto.
2. **Founding year trap.** ASEAN's Bangkok Declaration is 8 August 1967; SAARC's founding charter is signed in Dhaka in 1985. Both are mid-20th-century regional pacts and easy to misdate.
3. **EU vs Council of Europe vs Eurozone.** EU is the political-economic union headquartered in Brussels; the Council of Europe (Strasbourg) is a human-rights body covering more states; the Eurozone is a currency subset of EU members. MAT sometimes substitutes one for another.
4. **Observer status.** The Vatican has UNGA observer status but is not a member. Palestine also holds observer status. MAT tests this by asking which entity "is a member of the UN" — neither qualifies as a full member.
5. **Troika succession.** G20 presidencies run in alphabetical order through host nations; the previous, current and incoming presidents form a troika that guides the agenda for three years.

> 📌 **Sharp edge:** Headquarters questions are the highest-yield format because each city is overloaded with bodies — Geneva alone hosts WHO, WTO, ILO and UNHCR. Always match by *function* (health → WHO; trade → WTO; labour → ILO; refugees → UNHCR) rather than by the loose "UN city" association.

#### Practice prompts

1. *"The only UN Security Council members with veto power are the five permanent members. Identify which of the following temporary members served a two-year term and correctly state whether a non-permanent member can block a substantive resolution."* — answer must note non-permanent members can vote but **cannot veto**; abstention ≠ veto.
2. *"Place the following in the order of their founding: SAARC, ASEAN, NATO, SCO, UN."* — expected sequence: UN (1945) → NATO (1949) → ASEAN (1967) → SAARC (1985) → SCO (2001). Students who reverse SAARC and NATO usually forget NATO predates SAARC by 36 years.
