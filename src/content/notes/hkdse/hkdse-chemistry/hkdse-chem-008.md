# VIII. Chemical Reactions and Energy — HKDSE Chemistry Notes

This topic covers Topic VIII of the Compulsory Part of HKDSE Chemistry. It addresses enthalpy change of reactions (exothermic and endothermic); standard enthalpy changes of formation, combustion and neutralisation; Hess's law; and fuel cells and energy storage.

> Verify the live HKDSE Chemistry Assessment Framework on https://www.hkeaa.edu.hk/ before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Enthalpy change (ΔH)**: the heat change at constant pressure. Units: kJ or kJ/mol.
- **Exothermic reaction**: ΔH < 0; releases heat to the surroundings. Examples: combustion, neutralisation, respiration.
- **Endothermic reaction**: ΔH > 0; absorbs heat from the surroundings. Examples: photosynthesis, thermal decomposition, dissolving ammonium nitrate.
- **Standard enthalpy change (ΔH°)**: enthalpy change measured under standard conditions (298 K, 1 atm, 1 mol/dm³ for solutions).
- **Standard enthalpy of formation (ΔH°f)**: the enthalpy change when 1 mole of a compound is formed from its elements in their standard states.
- **Standard enthalpy of combustion (ΔH°c)**: the enthalpy change when 1 mole of a substance burns completely in oxygen under standard conditions.
- **Standard enthalpy of neutralisation (ΔH°n)**: the enthalpy change when 1 mole of H⁺ reacts with 1 mole of OH⁻ to form water. For strong acid + strong base: ΔH°n ≈ -57 kJ/mol.
- **Hess's law**: the total enthalpy change is independent of the pathway taken (provided the initial and final conditions are the same).
- **Bond energies**: the energy required to break 1 mole of bonds in gaseous molecules. ΔH = Σ(bond energies of bonds broken) - Σ(bond energies of bonds formed).
- **Fuel cells**: devices that convert chemical energy directly to electrical energy (continuous, as long as fuel is supplied). The hydrogen fuel cell: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O, with H₂ oxidised at the anode and O₂ reduced at the cathode.
- **Energy storage**: batteries (galvanic cells), fuel cells, supercapacitors, pumped hydro storage.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing exothermic with endothermic — exothermic releases heat (ΔH < 0); endothermic absorbs heat (ΔH > 0).
- Treating ΔH°f as the enthalpy of formation of any compound, not from its elements in their standard states.
- Forgetting that bond breaking is endothermic, bond forming is exothermic.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Enthalpy changes

**Standard enthalpy of formation (ΔH°f)**: defined for 1 mole of compound formed from elements in their standard states. ΔH°f of an element in its standard state is 0.

**Standard enthalpy of combustion (ΔH°c)**: defined for 1 mole of substance completely burnt in excess oxygen.

**Standard enthalpy of neutralisation (ΔH°n)**: the heat released when 1 mole of H⁺ reacts with 1 mole of OH⁻. For strong acid + strong base: -57 kJ/mol. For weak acids/bases, the magnitude is smaller because energy is absorbed in the dissociation of the weak acid/base.

#### Hess's law applications

**Calculating ΔH°r from ΔH°f**: ΔH°r = Σ(ΔH°f of products) - Σ(ΔH°f of reactants).

Example: CH₄(g) + 2O₂(g) → CO₂(g) + 2H₂O(l). ΔH°r = ΔH°f(CO₂) + 2·ΔH°f(H₂O) - ΔH°f(CH₄) - 2·ΔH°f(O₂). Since O₂ is an element in its standard state, ΔH°f(O₂) = 0.

**Calculating ΔH°r from ΔH°c**: ΔH°r = Σ(ΔH°c of reactants) - Σ(ΔH°c of products).

**Constructing enthalpy cycles**: Hess's law problems often involve constructing a Hess cycle diagram. The two routes (direct and indirect) must give the same ΔH.

**Calculating ΔH from bond energies**: ΔH = Σ(bonds broken) - Σ(bonds formed). Bonds broken (reactant bonds) require energy; bonds formed (product bonds) release energy.

#### Fuel cells and energy storage

**Hydrogen-oxygen fuel cell**:

- Anode (negative): 2H₂ → 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ (hydrogen is oxidised).
- Cathode (positive): O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ → 2H₂O (oxygen is reduced).
- Overall: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O.

The fuel cell produces electricity continuously as long as H₂ and O₂ are supplied. The only product is water. Fuel cells are more efficient than combustion engines and produce no pollutants at the point of use.

**Other fuel cells**: methanol fuel cell, ethanol fuel cell, solid oxide fuel cell.

**Energy storage**: batteries (galvanic cells — lead-acid, lithium-ion, nickel-metal hydride), fuel cells, supercapacitors, pumped hydro storage.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-topic links

- **V. Fossil Fuels and Carbon Compounds**: combustion of hydrocarbons is the main source of energy.
- **IX. Rate of Reaction**: reaction rate affects the rate of heat release.
- **X. Chemical Equilibrium**: equilibrium constants depend on temperature (van't Hoff equation), which is related to ΔH.

#### Exam technique

- **For enthalpy calculation questions**: write the balanced equation, identify the relevant ΔH° values, and apply Hess's law (with correct signs).
- **For bond energy questions**: identify the bonds in reactants (broken) and products (formed); apply ΔH = Σ(broken) - Σ(formed).
- **For fuel cell questions**: write the half-equations at each electrode and the overall equation.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "ΔH°f of any compound is positive." Most compounds have negative ΔH°f (energy released when forming from elements). ΔH°f is positive only for endothermic compounds (e.g. NO, C₂H₂).
- "All reactions produce heat." Many reactions absorb heat from the surroundings (endothermic).
- "Fuel cells store energy." Fuel cells are energy conversion devices, not storage. The fuel itself stores the energy.
