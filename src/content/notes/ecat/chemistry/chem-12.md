# Transition Metals and Coordination Chemistry

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Transition metals are **d-block elements** forming one or more ions with a partially filled d-subshell. Their hallmark behaviour — **variable oxidation states**, coloured compounds, and **catalytic activity** — arises because the (n−1)d and ns orbitals are close in energy. A **coordination complex** has a central metal ion bonded to surrounding **ligands** through **coordinate (dative) covalent bonds**, in which the ligand donates both electrons of the shared pair. Two must-know formulas:

- **Charge on complex** = oxidation state of metal + sum of ligand charges
- **Spin-only magnetic moment**: μ = √(n(n+2)) BM, where n = number of unpaired electrons

ECAT pointers: (1) Identify geometry from **coordination number** (4 → tetrahedral/square planar; 6 → octahedral). (2) Memorise the **spectrochemical series** to predict high-spin vs low-spin. (3) Practice IUPAC naming of at least one cation and one anion complex.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Electronic Configuration and Variable Valency
The 3d series (Sc–Zn) shows anomalies at **Cr (3d⁵4s¹)** and **Cu (3d¹⁰4s¹)** because a half-filled or fully filled d-subshell lowers energy. Because (n−1)d and ns orbitals differ by only a few hundred kJ mol⁻¹, 3d metals exhibit multiple oxidation states — Mn shows +2, +3, +4, +6, and +7.

#### Werner's Theory
Werner proposed two kinds of valency: **primary (ionisable)** satisfied by anions outside the coordination sphere, and **secondary (non-ionisable)** satisfied by ligands directly attached to the metal. For example, in [Co(NH₃)₆]Cl₃ the three Cl⁻ are ionisable, while the six NH₃ are non-ionisable.

#### Crystal Field Theory
In an **octahedral field**, the five d-orbitals split into a lower **t₂g** set (dxy, dyz, dzx) and a higher **eg** set (dx²−y², dz²) separated by **Δo (crystal field splitting energy)**. CFSE = (−0.4 × n_t₂g + 0.6 × n_eg) × Δo, where n_t₂g and n_eg are the electron counts in each set. Tetrahedral splitting Δt ≈ 4/9 Δo, which is too small to force pairing, so tetrahedral complexes are almost always **high-spin**.

#### Spectrochemical Series (weak → strong field)
I⁻ < Br⁻ < F⁻ < H₂O < NH₃ < en < CN⁻ < CO
Strong-field ligands → large Δo → **low-spin**; weak-field ligands → small Δo → **high-spin**.

#### Isomerism
Square planar [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] shows **cis–trans (geometrical)** isomerism. Octahedral [Co(en)₃]³⁺ shows **optical isomerism** because it is non-superimposable on its mirror image.

#### Nomenclature Example
[Co(NH₃)₅Cl]SO₄ → *pentaamminechloridocobalt(III) sulphate*. Cation first, ligands alphabetical, anionic ligand gets the **-o** suffix; metal oxidation state in Roman numerals.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Deeper Mechanisms
- **d¹ and d² in octahedral fields** have CFSE = −0.4Δo and −0.8Δo respectively, yet remain paramagnetic because pairing energy P > Δo at low d-counts.
- **d⁸ ions (Ni²⁺, Pd²⁺, Pt²⁺, Au³⁺)** prefer **square planar** geometry because the extra stabilisation of a fully filled t₂g set outweighs the pairing cost — a classic ECAT trap where students default to "4 = tetrahedral".
- **Chelate effect**: ethylenediamine (en, bidentate) and EDTA (hexadentate) form complexes with dramatically larger **stability constants K** than equivalent monodentate ligands, due to both entropy gain (more released particles on formation) and a more favourable enthalpy from the chelate ring.
- **Inner vs outer orbital complexes**: [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺ uses 3d orbitals (d²sp³, inner, low-spin, diamagnetic), while [CoF₆]³⁻ uses 4d orbitals (sp³d², outer, high-spin, paramagnetic with 4 unpaired electrons).

#### Worked Example
Find μ, CFSE, and spin state of [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻.
Fe²⁺ is d⁶. CN⁻ is a strong-field ligand, so all six electrons pair in t₂g. n = 0 unpaired, μ = 0 BM (diamagnetic). CFSE = (−0.4 × 6)Δo = −2.4Δo. The complex is **low-spin inner-orbital d²sp³**, diamagnetic.

#### Common Mistakes
- Writing μ using **total** d-electrons instead of unpaired ones.
- Confusing **coordination number** (count of σ-donor atoms, e.g. 6 in [Co(NH₃)₅Cl]²⁺ because Cl⁻ is still bonded to Co even though it sits in the inner sphere) with oxidation state.
- Forgetting that **transitions within d-d splits cause colour**; d⁰ (Sc³⁺, Ti⁴⁺) and d¹⁰ (Zn²⁺) ions are therefore **colourless**.

#### Practice Prompts
1. Name [Cr(NH₃)₄Cl₂]Cl and state the number of geometrical isomers.
2. For [Mn(H₂O)₆]²⁺ (high-spin, d⁵), calculate CFSE in units of Δo and the magnetic moment μ in BM.
