# Audit Engagement & Planning

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Audit engagement & planning is the opening phase of an audit under **ISA 300** (Planning) and **ISA 210** (Terms of Audit Engagements). The auditor agrees the terms, checks **preconditions**, sets **materiality**, and builds an **overall audit strategy** plus a detailed **audit plan** that directs the team to sufficient appropriate audit evidence.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ISA 210 | Agrees the terms and preconditions for the audit |
| ISA 220 | Quality control for an audit engagement (leadership, ethics, review) |
| ISA 230 | Audit documentation requirements |
| ISA 300 | Overall audit strategy and audit plan |
| ISA 315 | Identifying and assessing risks of material misstatement |
| ISA 320 | Materiality in planning and performing an audit |
| ISA 570 | Going concern evaluation (≥12 months from FS date) |
| TCWG | Those charged with governance |
| Engagement Letter | Written agreement of audit terms with management/TCWG |

- **Overall Materiality (OM)** = 0.5%–1% of an appropriate benchmark (profit, revenue, assets).
- **Performance Materiality (PM)** ≈ OM × 0.50, used to aggregate uncorrected misstatements.
- **Clearly Trivial Threshold (CTT)** ≈ PM × 0.05; below this, misstatements need not be accumulated.
- **Audit Risk (AR)** = Inherent Risk (IR) × Control Risk (CR) × Detection Risk (DR); DR is derived, AR is set low.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"A-R-I-D"** for the audit-risk equation — **A**udit risk = **R**isk (inherent) × **R**isk (control) × **D**etection. And for materiality, remember **"PM = half of OM"** — PM is always lower, OM × 0.50.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Preconditions & Engagement Letter (ISA 210)
Before accepting or continuing an engagement, the auditor must confirm two **preconditions**: (i) the **financial-reporting framework** used by management is acceptable, and (ii) management/TCWG agree to the **premise** of the audit (acknowledgement of their responsibilities, providing access to information, and written confirmations).

The **engagement letter** records the agreed terms. Mandatory contents include the **objective and scope** of the audit, **responsibilities of the auditor** (ISA 200) and **management**, the applicable **FRF**, the expected **form of any reports**, **ethical requirements** including independence, identification of **TCWG**, the **fee arrangement**, any **limitations of liability**, and an indicative **communication timetable**. A letter omitting TCWG or the FRF fails the precondition test and the engagement should not be accepted.

#### Strategy vs Plan (ISA 300)
The **overall audit strategy** sets the **scope, timing, direction and resources** of the audit and is finalised before detailed work begins. The **audit plan** converts the strategy into specific **nature, timing and extent** of further audit procedures. Treat them as two separate documents: the strategy answers *what, when, where, who*; the plan answers *how*.

#### Risk Assessment & Materiality (ISA 315 / ISA 320)
Risk-assessment procedures include **inquiries** of management and TCWG, **analytical procedures** at the planning stage, and **observation and inspection** to understand the entity, its environment, and internal control. These feed the assessed risks of material misstatement at FS level and assertion level.

Materiality is a matter of **professional judgement** based on users' information needs. The auditor selects a benchmark (profit before tax is common; revenue or total assets when profit is volatile), applies a percentage (typically 0.5%–1% of PBT), and allocates PM to balances, transactions and disclosures.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix

| Concept | Strategy | Audit Plan |
|---|---|---|
| Level | High-level | Detailed |
| Question answered | Scope, timing, direction, resources | Nature, timing, extent of procedures |
| Timing | Set first | Developed after strategy |
| Auditor focus | Deployment of team & reporting deadlines | Specific responses to assessed risks |
| Document | Overall strategy memo | Audit programme / plan |

| Concept | Overall Materiality | Performance Materiality | Clearly Trivial |
|---|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Decide what misstatements matter | Aggregate uncorrected misstatements | Filter out de minimis items |
| Sizing | 0.5%–1% of benchmark | ≈ OM × 0.50 | ≈ PM × 0.05 |
| Set when | At planning | At planning | At planning |

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem
**Question:** Aaroog & Co. audits Lexon Ltd, a listed entity with profit before tax of PKR 2,400 million. The engagement partner sets overall materiality at 1% of PBT and performance materiality at 50% of OM. Calculate OM, PM, and the clearly trivial threshold (at 5% of PM). If the auditor wants low audit risk at 5% and assesses inherent risk as 70% and control risk as 50%, compute the detection risk to be set.

#### Solution:
- OM = 1% × PKR 2,400 m = **PKR 24 m**.
- PM = 50% × 24 m = **PKR 12 m**.
- CTT = 5% × 12 m = **PKR 0.6 m**.
- DR = AR ÷ (IR × CR) = 0.05 ÷ (0.70 × 0.50) = 0.05 ÷ 0.35 = **0.1429 (≈ 14.3%)**.

The auditor plans substantive procedures to keep detection risk around 14%, meaning substantially more evidence is needed than if inherent/control risk were lower.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students frequently use **revenue** as the benchmark for a manufacturer whose profit is volatile but stable in revenue. ACCA/CA Pakistan examiners reward matching the benchmark to user needs — pick PBT for profit-oriented entities; switch to revenue or total assets only when profit is abnormal or immaterial.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Going Concern (ISA 570) and Fraud (ISA 240) Edges
The **going-concern evaluation period** must cover **at least twelve months from the date of the financial statements**, not from the date of the audit report. The auditor reaches a **dual conclusion**: whether the going-concern basis is appropriate and whether material uncertainty exists requiring a separate section in the auditor's report.

Under **ISA 240**, **revenue recognition** is a **presumed fraud risk** that can only be rebutted with documented, entity-specific reasoning; the override-of-controls risk is always presumed. Communication with TCWG of fraud risks identified and any actual fraud detected is mandatory.

#### Acceptance/Continuance Traps (ISA 210 / IFAC Code)
Common advanced traps and exceptions:

1. Confusing OM with PM — PM is always **lower** than OM and used to **aggregate** misstatements; OM drives the audit opinion.
2. **Stale materiality** — preliminary figures change; update materiality during the audit when actual results differ materially.
3. **Volatile benchmarks** — when PBT is near zero or distorted, switch to revenue (0.5%–1%) or total assets (1%–2%).
4. **Fee-dependence threat** — recurring fees **>15%** from one listed-audit client raise self-interest and self-review threats under the **IFAC Code of Ethics**.
5. **Engagement-quality reviewer (EQR)** — required for **listed entities** and other high-risk audits under **ISA 220**; independence of the EQR is essential.
6. **Documentation (ISA 230)** — must include strategy, plan, materiality, assessed risks, and significant judgments, sufficient for an **experienced auditor** (having no prior knowledge) to understand the conclusions.
7. **Communication (ISA 260)** — TCWG must be told about planned scope/timing, significant findings, and the auditor's independence.

#### Adjacent Topics
This topic pairs tightly with **Audit Risk & Response (ISA 330)**, **Quality Control (ISQM 1 / ISA 220)**, **Audit Sampling**, and **Group Audits (ISA 600)** — group PM is commonly **OM × 0.20 to 0.50** allocated to components.

- A client wants the audit completed three weeks earlier than last year with the same fee. Outline the **acceptance/continuance** considerations.
- A component auditor in another jurisdiction refuses ISA-compliant reporting. Discuss the **group auditor's options** under ISA 600 and the impact on the group audit opinion.
