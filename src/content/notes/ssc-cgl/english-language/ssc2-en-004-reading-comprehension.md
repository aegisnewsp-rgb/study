# Reading Comprehension (Passage + Questions)

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Reading Comprehension in **SSC CGL Tier 2 English** tests how accurately you extract meaning from a 500–800 word passage and answer 4–6 questions on **literal meaning, inference, vocabulary-in-context, tone, and author's purpose**. The paper contributes roughly 8–12 questions in the comprehension section of the 200-mark Tier 2 paper, making it a reliable scoring zone for prepared candidates.

- **Skim first, question second, re-read third** — read the passage for structure (topic sentence, paragraph role), glance at questions, then return to the relevant section for a focused read.
- **Vocabulary-in-context** means choosing the option that fits the surrounding sentence, not the option that sounds most familiar in isolation.
- **Inference** questions require combining two or more textual clues — eliminate options that are true in real life but unsupported by the passage.

| Passage Type | Signal Words to Watch |
| --- | --- |
| Factual / Expository | "states", "according to", "data shows" |
| Argumentative | "however", "therefore", "consequently" |
| Literary / Narrative | "yet", "meanwhile", "nonetheless" |
| Scientific | "experiment", "hypothesis", "results indicate" |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Skimming, Scanning, and the Three-Pass Method
Effective Tier 2 handling depends on **structured reading, not speed-reading**. Skim the passage in 60–90 seconds: identify the **central theme** (usually in the first or last paragraph), note the function of each paragraph (introduction, evidence, counter-argument, conclusion), and mark transition words. Then read the questions and underline the *type* — literal, inferential, vocabulary, or tone. Finally, re-read only the relevant 2–4 sentences with full attention.

#### Question Types You Will Meet
SSC CGL Tier 2 comprehension clusters around five recurring question types. Recognising the type controls your strategy.

| Question Type | Strategy | Trap to Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main idea / central theme | Look at first sentence + last paragraph | Choosing the topic instead of the author's claim about the topic |
| Vocabulary-in-context | Substitute each option in the sentence | Picking a dictionary synonym that breaks sentence grammar |
| Inference (implicit) | Combine two textual clues | Choosing an option true in real life but absent from the passage |
| Tone / attitude | Read diction and rhetorical devices | Confusing narrator's tone with quoted character's tone |
| Fact vs opinion | Check for verifiable claim | Treating a strong opinion as a fact |

#### Tracking Reference and Logic
Pronoun antecedents ("it", "they", "this") and **logical connectors** (because, although, however, moreover, consequently, on the other hand) are load-bearing — they hold the argument's chain together. Misreading "however" as "therefore" flips the author's stance and produces a wrong inference answer.

- Always read the sentence **before and after** the connector before answering stance questions.
- Circle every pronoun the first time you skim; resolve it before answering reference-based questions.
- For "NOT / EXCEPT" questions, actively look for the **false** statement — re-read each option against the passage.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Examiner Traps
The hardest Tier 2 RC questions exploit **qualifying language** and **distractor expansion**. Words like *some, often, mainly, frequently, tend to* soften claims, while *always, never, all, every, completely* overgeneralise. Inference options that drop these qualifiers become factually wrong even when the core claim is true.

| Distractor Pattern | What It Looks Like | How to Defeat It |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Real-world true, passage-unsupported | Plausible fact outside the text | Verify every claim against a passage sentence |
| Extreme language shift | "always" replacing "often" | Match the qualifier exactly |
| Off-topic expansion | Adds unrelated detail to a true core | Check whether the *added* part appears in the passage |
| Tone mismatch | Uses positive words for a critical passage | Re-evaluate overall diction, not isolated words |

#### Worked Micro-Example
Passage sentence: *"The policy, **albeit** well-intentioned, has produced **perverse** incentives that discourage long-term investment."*

Question: *The word "perverse" most nearly means —*
- (A) generous
- (B) contrary to intent
- (C) financially rewarding
- (D) legally invalid

Substituting each option: (A) breaks the contrast signalled by "albeit"; (C) contradicts "discourage"; (D) is unsupported. **(B)** fits the syntax ("incentives that are contrary to intent") and the passage theme of unintended negative outcomes.

#### Exam Strategy for SSC CGL Tier 2
Comprehension sits inside the English section of the 200-mark, 2-hour paper, alongside active-passive, error-spotting, and cloze test. Budget **8–10 minutes per comprehension set**: 2 minutes skimming, 1 minute reading questions, 5–6 minutes answering, 1 minute review.

1. Practice one passage daily from previous SSC CGL Tier 2 papers — the diction and idiom mirror real Tier 2 passages.
2. Maintain an error log: write the question type, your wrong answer, the correct answer, and the *specific* reason (qualifier missed, pronoun unresolved, real-world intrusion).
3. For vocabulary-in-context drills, build sentences using the *wrong* options to see why they break — this builds faster elimination reflexes than reading the right answer alone.
