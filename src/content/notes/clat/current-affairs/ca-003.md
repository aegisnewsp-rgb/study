# Legal News

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Legal News for CLAT covers recent developments in Indian law — Supreme Court and High Court rulings, parliamentary statutes, ordinances, and constitutional amendments reported in the 12 months before the exam. The area carries roughly **5%** of the total marks under the Current Affairs & GK section (which itself is ~25% of CLAT). Three codes replaced the IPC, CrPC and Indian Evidence Act from **1 July 2024**: the **BNS**, **BNSS** and **BSA**.

- Read at least one national daily (*The Hindu* or *Indian Express*) plus one law outlet (*LiveLaw*, *Bar & Bench*).
- Note judge names, bench strength, citation and date whenever you log a judgment.
- Map each item to a head: criminal, constitutional, commercial, environmental, family, tax, cyber.

| Head | What to log |
| --- | --- |
| Judgment | Bench, citation, ratio, relief |
| Statute | Section numbers, date of assent |
| Ordinance | Article 123 promulgation date |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### What CLAT actually tests

CLAT questions on Legal News fall into three formats: a 350-word passage followed by comprehension MCQs, a standalone factual MCQ on a recent Act or judgment, or a principle-application set where the principle is drawn from a reported case. The Consortium of NLUs restricts the syllabus to events in the **trailing 12-month window**, so anything older is a guaranteed waste of revision time.

#### Reading the law in a news item

Every news item has a *political slug* and a *legal core*. The slug is the headline ("SC pulls up Centre"); the legal core names the statute interpreted, the article of the Constitution invoked, the relief granted and the binding reasoning. CLAT rewards the legal core, not the slug.

#### The new criminal codes

From **1 July 2024**, the **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS)** replaced the IPC, the **Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS)** replaced the CrPC, and the **Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA)** replaced the Indian Evidence Act. Old IPC section numbers no longer map cleanly — for example, murder shifted from **IPC 302** to **BNS 302**, rape from **IPC 376** to **BNS 63**, and cheating from **IPC 420** to **BNS 318**.

#### Common traps examiners exploit

| Trap | What happens | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quoting IPC sections post-July 2024 | Wrong section number, wrong court history | Always cite the BNS/BNSS/BSA equivalent |
| Obiter treated as ratio | "Law declared" line is actually a passing remark | Identify the binding issue framed by the bench |
| High Court mistaken for SC | Inflated importance, wrong citation | Log the court along with the bench |

- Always log: date, court, bench, judges, citation, statutory provisions, relief.
- Prefer primary sources (sci.gov.in) over secondary summaries.
- Treat Law Commission Reports as recommendations, not enacted law.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Mapping sources to marks

The most efficient pipeline is one national daily for political framing plus one law outlet for accuracy. *LiveLaw* and *Bar & Bench* publish cause lists, daily orders and verifiable citations within hours. *Supreme Court Observer* provides editorial analysis of constitutional benches. Maintain a monthly logbook tagged by subject head — criminal, constitutional, commercial, environmental, family, labour, tax, cyber — and revisit it weekly.

#### Ratio decidendi vs obiter dicta

A SC judgment contains a binding **ratio decidendi** (the rule on the point decided) and non-binding **obiter dicta** (observations by the way). CLAT passages deliberately lift an obiter as the "principle" — students who copy the highlighted sentence lose marks. The test: was the issue framed by the bench, and did the answer to it form the basis of the order? If not, it is obiter.

#### Constitutional pointers that recur

- **Article 32** and **Article 226** — writ jurisdictions of SC and High Courts respectively.
- **Article 142** — SC's complete justice power, often invoked for monetary compensation in human-rights matters.
- **Article 123** — route for presidential ordinances when Parliament is not in session.
- **Basic structure doctrine** — revisited in every major constitutional bench ruling.

#### Worked micro-example

A 2025 passage quotes: *"The Court observed that live-in relationships are recognised under the Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005, though the personal laws may differ."* The ratio is the statutory recognition under the **PWDVA 2005**. The obiter is the comparative remark on personal laws. A CLAT question will ask which Act governs protection — answer is PWDVA, not a personal-law statute.

#### Common mistakes

- Citing IPC sections after 1 July 2024 without BNS conversion.
- Reporting only the headline, missing the relief and provisions interpreted.
- Conflating PILs admitted with PILs disposed of, inflating the importance of interim stays.
- Ignoring the 12-month window and logging older judgments.

#### Practice prompts

1. A passage discusses the SC's use of **Article 142** to grant compensation in a custodial-death case. Identify whether the operative paragraph is ratio or obiter, and explain using a 60-word answer.
2. Compare the **BNS Section 302** and the erstwhile **IPC Section 302**. Note one drafting change and one procedural consequence under the **BNSS**.
