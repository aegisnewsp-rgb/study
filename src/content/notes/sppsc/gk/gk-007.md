# Pakistan's Economy & Industry

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Pakistan's economy is measured at **market prices** through Gross Domestic Product (GDP), composed of agriculture, industry, and services. Provincial candidates for SPSC must recognise the core formulas, the apex institutions, and Sindh's industrial geography.

- **GDP (basic identity):** $GDP = C + I + G + (X - M)$, where $C$ = private consumption, $I$ = investment, $G$ = government spending, $X$ = exports, $M$ = imports (all in PKR at current or constant prices).
- **Per Capita Income:** $PCI = \dfrac{GDP}{\text{Mid-year population}}$, expressed in PKR/person; the State Bank of Pakistan (SBP) also publishes a USD conversion.
- **Inflation (CPI):** $\pi = \dfrac{CPI_n - CPI_{n-1}}{CPI_{n-1}} \times 100$, a dimensionless percentage published monthly by the Pakistan Bureau of Statistics (PBS).
- **Central bank:** State Bank of Pakistan (SBP), headquartered in Karachi — NOT the National Bank of Pakistan.
- **Apex tax body:** Federal Board of Revenue (FBR) at federal level; Sindh Revenue Board (SRB) collects provincial services sales tax.

| Term | Meaning | Unit / Dimension |
|---|---|---|
| GDP | Total monetary value of goods & services produced domestically | PKR [M L² T⁻²] |
| GNP | GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA) | PKR |
| Current Account Balance | Trade in goods, services + net primary & secondary income | PKR |
| Fiscal Deficit | Total Expenditure − Total Revenue | PKR |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"A-I-S 22-20-53"** — Agriculture ≈ 22%, Industry ≈ 20%, Services ≈ 53% of GDP (PBS national accounts). Picture Pakistan's economy as a **services-skewed pyramid** sitting on a narrow agricultural base, with textiles as the industrial keystone.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Sectoral Architecture

Pakistan's national accounts classify output into three broad sectors. Services dominate because of wholesale-retail trade, finance, transport, and public administration. Industry is led by **Large-Scale Manufacturing (LSM)**, a subset tracked by the PBS LSM Index covering textiles, cement, fertilizers, automobiles, and pharmaceuticals. Agriculture remains the largest employer though not the largest contributor — cotton (silver fibre), wheat, rice (Basmati + IRRI), sugarcane, and maize drive crop output, while livestock contributes the single biggest agricultural sub-share.

Textiles account for roughly **60% of Pakistan's export earnings**, making the sector a frequent MCQ target. Distractors commonly use "rice", "IT services", or "sports goods" — none match the textile share.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix

| Concept | Distinguishing Feature | Common Confusion |
|---|---|---|
| GDP vs GNP | GNP = GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad | Forgetting NFIA (workers' remittances component) |
| Fiscal Deficit vs Public Debt | Deficit = annual flow; Debt = cumulative stock | Treating them as interchangeable annual figures |
| FBR vs SRB | FBR = federal income/sales/customs/excise; SRB = Sindh services sales tax | Calling provincial collection "FBR Sindh" |
| PSDP vs Sindh ADP | PSDP = federal public investment; ADP = Sindh Annual Development Programme | Citing federal allocations as provincial |
| Managed Float vs Free Float | PKR is managed/crawl-like since 2019 IMF reforms | Saying rupee is "fully floating" |
| CPI vs WPI | CPI = consumer retail prices; WPI = wholesale/producer prices | Using WPI growth as "inflation" in MCQs |

#### Fiscal & External Aggregates

- **Fiscal Deficit-to-GDP:** $\dfrac{\text{Total Expenditure} - \text{Total Revenue}}{GDP} \times 100$ — a flow measure, quoted as a % of GDP.
- **Current Account Balance:** $(X - M)_{goods+services} + \text{Net Primary Income} + \text{Net Secondary Income}$, all in PKR; chronic deficit is funded by IMF programmes, FDI, and remittances.
- **Remittances** (recorded under secondary income) flow largely from Gulf states, the UK, and the US — a stabiliser of the external account.
- **Foreign Exchange Reserves** are held by SBP and reported weekly; the usable portion excludes encumbered deposits.
- **External debt composition:** multilateral (IMF, World Bank, ADB), bilateral (China, Saudi Arabia, UAE), commercial Eurobonds, plus IMF EFF/ECF tranches.

#### Monetary & Trade Institutions

The SBP sets the **policy rate**, regulates banks, and manages the managed-float exchange rate. The FBR administers direct and indirect federal taxes. Provincial revenue-raising in Sindh rests with the SRB for services and the Excise & Taxation Department for motor vehicles, property, and professional taxes.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** In a recent fiscal year, Pakistan's GDP at current prices is reported as PKR 100 trillion, mid-year population is 240 million, and GDP comprises: Consumption PKR 65 t, Investment PKR 14 t, Government Spending PKR 13 t, Exports PKR 10 t, Imports PKR 2 t (figures simplified). Calculate (a) GDP using the expenditure identity, (b) Per Capita Income in PKR, and (c) comment on the structural interpretation of the trade balance.

#### Solution:

(a) Apply $GDP = C + I + G + (X - M)$:
$GDP = 65 + 14 + 13 + (10 - 2) = 65 + 14 + 13 + 8 = \textbf{PKR 100 trillion}$ ✓ (identity reconciles).

(b) $PCI = \dfrac{GDP}{\text{Population}} = \dfrac{100 \times 10^{12}}{240 \times 10^{6}} = \dfrac{10^{14}}{2.4 \times 10^{8}} \approx \textbf{PKR 416,667 per person}$.

(c) Since $X > M$, the trade balance is **positive** (surplus), contributing positively to GDP via net exports. In Pakistan's actual data, imports typically exceed exports, producing a **trade deficit** that is partly offset by remittances in the broader current account.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students often compute Per Capita Income using GNP instead of GDP, or divide by **total** population instead of **mid-year** population — the PBS denominator is mid-year. Also, a positive net-export figure ($X - M > 0$) is unusual for Pakistan; if the question shows imports larger than exports, do NOT force a surplus.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Sindh's Industrial & Fiscal Geography

Karachi is the financial and industrial nerve — the **Pakistan Stock Exchange (PSK 100 index)**, the SBP head office, the headquarters of major banks, and the Sindh Industrial Trading Estate (SITE) cluster. Cotton belts in Sanghar, Sukkur, and Ghotki feed the ginning-pressing-spinning pipeline that ends in Faisalabad's looms. Thar coalfields (Block-II, Sino coal mining under CPEC) supply lignite for power plants, while coastal districts support fisheries and a developing blue-economy zone.

**Fiscal federalism:** Sindh's share in the divisible pool is determined by the **NFC Award** (latest 7th Award). SPSC questions frequently quote the population criterion weight, the province's percentage share, or specific federal PSDP allocations routed through Sindh. Always cite the Award edition in force rather than a memorised single number — the formula/weights change with each Award.

#### CPEC & Structural Change

CPEC's three pillars — **Gwadar Port**, transport (Karakoram Highway Phase-II, ML-1 rail upgradation, Hakla–D.I. Khan motorway), and **energy projects** — are financed through concessional and commercial loans from Chinese state-owned banks, primarily the EXIM Bank. Special Economic Zones (SEZs) under CPEC, including Rashakai (KP), Dhabeji (Sindh), and Allama Iqbal Industrial City (Punjab), offer tax holidays and one-window facilitation. For SPSC, the trap is treating CPEC as a grant — the loan-repayment obligation flows into external debt servicing.

#### Inflation & Base-Year Methodology

Pakistan rebased its national accounts to **FY2015-16** prices. Quoting older share percentages (agriculture at 30%+) is the most frequent distractor. CPI base year is **2015-16 = 100**; the PBS publishes national, urban, and rural CPIs, plus a Sensitive Price Indicator (SPI) for weekly essentials.

#### Advanced Traps & Edge Cases

1. **GNP > GDP** when NFIA is positive (remittance inflows, foreign income of overseas Pakistanis). For most recent years, GNP ≈ GDP + a small positive NFIA component.
2. **Real vs Nominal GDP** — deflate using a GDP deflator or compare constant-price series; MCQs sometimes ask which is the "growth indicator" (answer: real/constant-price GDP).
3. **Privatisation proceeds** are non-tax receipts that reduce the fiscal deficit only on the revenue side if counted; the cash is a financing item under "disinvestment proceeds".
4. **FDI vs Portfolio Investment** — FDI confers management control (≥10% equity under SBP definition); portfolio flows are passive. Exam MCQs use this 10% threshold as a distractor.
5. **Sindh ADP vs Federal PSDP** — provincial ADPs are financed by the provincial government's own resources plus federal transfers; mixing the two is a classic trap.

#### Practice Prompts (Self-Test)

1. Distinguish between the policy rate set by SBP, the interbank offered rate (KIBOR), and the yield on a 10-year Pakistan Investment Bond. Which directly anchors retail lending rates?
2. Construct a one-page note contrasting LSM Index growth (PBS) with overall industrial GDP growth, and explain why the two diverge during a textile-export slowdown.
