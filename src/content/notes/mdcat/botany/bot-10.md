# Plant Nutrition

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Plant nutrition covers how green plants acquire **essential elements**, assimilate them, and use them in metabolism. Eighteen elements are universally recognised as essential: nine **macronutrients** (C, H, O, N, P, K, Ca, Mg, S) required in larger amounts, and nine **micronutrients** (B, Cu, Fe, Mn, Zn, Mo, Cl, Ni, plus beneficial Na, Si in some species). An element qualifies as essential under the **Arnon and Stout criteria** — required to complete the life cycle, directly involved in metabolism, and not replaceable. Carbon enters as **CO₂** through stomata; H and O come from **water absorbed by roots**; the rest are taken up as mineral ions from soil. Absorption occurs through **root hair cells** by **active** (ATP-driven, against gradient) and **passive** (along electrochemical gradient) mechanisms. Remember: **nitrogen deficiency** causes chlorosis in older leaves first because N is mobile, while **iron and calcium deficiency** shows in young leaves because both are immobile.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Classification of Essential Elements

Plants need 18 essential elements, divided by quantity required:

| Class | Elements | Typical source |
|-------|----------|----------------|
| Macronutrients | C, H, O, N, P, K, Ca, Mg, S | Air, water, soil minerals |
| Micronutrients | B, Cu, Fe, Mn, Zn, Mo, Cl, Ni | Soil minerals (trace) |

C, H, O are **non-mineral** (from CO₂ and H₂O); the other 15 are **mineral nutrients** absorbed as ions. Nitrogen is the most demanded mineral, absorbed predominantly as **NO₃⁻** and to a lesser extent NH₄⁺.

#### Criteria of Essentiality (Arnon & Stout, 1939)

1. The element must be **absolutely required** for the plant to complete its life cycle (vegetative + reproductive).
2. Its function must be **direct**, not indirect (e.g., correcting a microbial effect does not count).
3. No other element can **substitute** for it fully.

A classic MDCAT trap lists "beneficial" elements (Na, Si, Co) as essential — they are not, even though they aid some species.

#### Mechanism of Mineral Absorption

Two pathways exist across the root cortex:

- **Apoplastic pathway** — water and ions move through cell walls and intercellular spaces without crossing any membrane, blocked at the **Casparian strip** of the endodermis.
- **Symplastic pathway** — ions cross the plasma membrane into the cytoplasm and travel via **plasmodesmata** from cell to cell, reaching the **xylem** for upward transport driven by **transpiration pull**.

#### Active vs Passive Absorption

- **Active absorption** uses **ATP** and carrier proteins to move ions **against** the electrochemical gradient (e.g., K⁺ uptake via K⁺-specific carriers).
- **Passive absorption** follows the gradient; ion exchange at root surface obeys the **cation exchange capacity (CEC)** of soil colloids (humus and clay hold K⁺, Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺).

#### Nitrogen Fixation

Atmospheric N₂ (~78%) is unusable directly. **Rhizobium** (symbiotic, in legume root nodules) and **Azotobacter** (free-living, aerobic soil) reduce N₂ → NH₃ using the **nitrogenase** enzyme complex, which is highly O₂-sensitive (leghemoglobin maintains low O₂ in nodules). NH₃ is then **nitrified**: NH₄⁺ → NO₂⁻ (Nitrosomonas) → NO₃⁻ (Nitrobacter) for plant uptake.

#### Mobility and Deficiency Symptoms

Mobile elements (N, P, K, Mg) are translocated from older to younger leaves under deficiency → **older leaves show symptoms first**. Immobile elements (Ca, Fe, B, S) cannot be remobilised → **young leaves show symptoms first**. MDCAT MCQs frequently test this contrast.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

#### Mycorrhizal Associations and Soil Fertility

Roughly 90% of vascular plants form **mycorrhiza** — mutualistic symbioses where fungal hyphae extend the effective root surface area. **Ectomycorrhizae** (in pines, oaks) form a Hartig net between cortical cells; **endomycorrhizae / arbuscular mycorrhizae** penetrate cell walls and form arbuscules for nutrient exchange. The fungus delivers P, Zn, and Cu; the plant supplies carbohydrates. MDCAT may ask why forest seedlings fail in sterilised soil — absence of mycorrhizal inoculum limits P uptake.

#### Hydroponics and Soilless Culture

**Hydroponics** is the technique of growing plants in nutrient solutions without soil, popularised by Julius von Sachs and Knop (1865). A balanced Knop's solution contains KNO₃, Ca(NO₃)₂, MgSO₄, KH₂PO₄, and Fe-EDTA in defined molar ratios. It is the experimental proof that plants need only inorganic ions, supporting the **mineral nutrition theory** that overturned the humus theory of earlier botanists.

#### Cation Exchange Capacity (CEC) in Detail

Soil particles carry negative charges (humus, clay). They adsorb **cations** (K⁺, Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, NH₄⁺) loosely, releasing them into soil solution as H⁺ from root respiration displaces them. The **CEC** value (cmol(+)/kg) predicts **soil fertility** — high CEC = high nutrient-holding capacity. Sandy soils have low CEC and need frequent fertilisation; clayey/loamy soils retain nutrients better.

#### Munch Pressure-Flow Hypothesis (Phloem Transport)

Organic food (sucrose) synthesised at the **source** (leaves) is loaded into phloem sieve tubes, raising osmotic pressure → water enters from xylem → turgor pressure builds. At the **sink** (roots, fruits, young leaves), sucrose is unloaded, water leaves back to xylem, and pressure drops. The resulting pressure gradient drives bulk flow. This explains why girdling a stem above the ground kills the root.

#### Common MDCAT Pitfalls

- Confusing **macronutrients** with "more important" — both macro and micro are equally essential; the distinction is **quantity**, not biological priority.
- Treating **passive absorption** as ATP-dependent — only active absorption costs energy.
- Assuming Fe deficiency appears in old leaves — Fe is **immobile**, so chlorosis appears in **young** leaves (interveinal chlorosis).
- Thinking **Azotobacter** forms root nodules — only **Rhizobium** (and related genera like Bradyrhizobium) nodulate legumes; Azotobacter is free-living.

#### Practice Prompts

1. A dicot seedling supplied with Knop's solution lacking magnesium develops interveinal chlorosis in older leaves first. Identify the element deficient, classify it (macro/micro, mobile/immobile), and state its role in plants.
2. Explain why a legume crop improves soil nitrogen content but a wheat crop does not, naming the bacterium, the enzyme, and the protective role of leghemoglobin.
