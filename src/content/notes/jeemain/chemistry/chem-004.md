# Classification

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Classification** in JEE Main Chemistry bundles two related ideas: organising elements into the **Modern Periodic Table** (periods and groups, blocks s/p/d/f) and sorting compounds by the **type of chemical bond** they contain — **ionic, covalent (polar or nonpolar), and coordinate (dative)**. The table itself is built on the **Modern Periodic Law**: physical and chemical properties are periodic functions of **atomic number (Z)**, not atomic mass. A **period** equals the highest principal quantum number *n* of the element, while a **group (1–18)** is decided by the valence-shell configuration. For compounds, an **ionic bond** is electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions (NaCl), a **covalent bond** is sharing of electrons (H₂, CH₄, Cl₂), and a **coordinate bond** is a shared pair donated entirely by one atom (H₃N→BF₃). **High-yield points**: (1) **Dipole moment μ = q × d** — non-zero only for polar covalent bonds. (2) **Isoelectronic species** share electron count and configuration (Na⁺, Mg²⁺, Al³⁺, Ne all have 10 e⁻). (3) **Diagonal relationship** of Li–Mg, Be–Al, B–Si comes from similar charge/size ratio.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### The Modern Periodic Table

The current table rests on **Moseley's Modern Periodic Law (1913)**: properties are a periodic function of **atomic number Z**. A **period** is the horizontal row whose number equals the highest principal quantum number *n* in the valence shell (period 2 = elements with n = 2 as the highest occupied shell). A **group** (1–18) is a vertical column whose members share similar valence-electron configuration and therefore similar chemistry.

Elements split into four blocks based on the sub-shell being filled:

- **s-block**: groups 1, 2 — configuration ns¹–² (Li, Na, K… Be, Mg, Ca…). All are metals; form basic oxides.
- **p-block**: groups 13–18 — ns² np¹–⁶ (Al, Si, P, S, Cl, Ar…). Contains metals, metalloids, and nonmetals.
- **d-block**: groups 3–12 — (n−1)d¹–¹⁰ ns⁰–² (Sc, Ti… Cu, Zn…). **Transition metals** show **variable valency** because (n−1)d and ns electrons both participate in bonding.
- **f-block**: lanthanoids (4f) and actinoids (5f) — placed separately below the main table.

#### Periodic Trends

Across a period (left → right), **Z** increases while electrons enter the same shell, so effective nuclear charge rises. The consequences:

| Property | Across a period (L→R) | Down a group (T→B) |
|---|---|---|
| Atomic radius | **Decreases** | Increases |
| Ionisation enthalpy | **Increases** (with dips at half-filled / fully-filled stability) | Decreases |
| Electronegativity | **Increases** | Decreases |
| Metallic character | Decreases | Increases |
| Oxidising power | Increases (for nonmetals) | Decreases |

#### Anomalous Behaviour of Second-Period Elements

Li, Be, B, C, N, O, and F differ from their own-group members because of (i) very **small atomic size**, (ii) **high charge density**, and (iii) **absence of d-orbitals** (so they cannot expand their octet). This produces the **diagonal relationship**: Li resembles Mg, Be resembles Al, and B resembles Si in properties such as oxide nature and chloride bridge formation.

#### Classification of Compounds by Bond Type

- **Ionic bond** — complete transfer of electrons; held by Coulombic attraction (Na⁺Cl⁻, MgO). Best between low-EN metals and high-EN nonmetals.
- **Covalent bond** — sharing of an electron pair. **Polar** when ΔEN > 0 (HCl, H₂O, NH₃) — gives a measurable **dipole moment μ = q × d**, measured in Debye (D). **Nonpolar** when ΔEN = 0 (H₂, O₂, N₂, Cl₂, CH₄).
- **Coordinate (dative) bond** — a special covalent bond in which **both electrons come from one atom** (the donor) onto an electron-deficient acceptor. The arrow is drawn **from donor lone pair → acceptor** (e.g., H₃N:→BF₃ forming H₃N–BF₃; O←→H₃O⁺). Once formed, a coordinate bond is indistinguishable from a normal covalent bond.

#### Isoelectronic Species

Species with the **same number of electrons** and identical configuration are *isoelectronic*: Na⁺, Mg²⁺, Al³⁺, Ne, F⁻, O²⁻, N³⁻ all have 10 electrons. Among them, ionic radius decreases as nuclear charge increases.

#### Exam Patterns

JEE Main tests this topic through: (a) block/group identification from configuration, (b) one-liners on trend ordering (EN, IE, radius), (c) dipole-moment / polar-nonpolar MCQs, and (d) coordinate-bond arrow representation.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases in Period and Block Identification

A period is **not** the count of shells an atom has; it is the highest *n* in use. Potassium (Z = 19) has configuration [Ar] 4s¹, so the highest *n* is 4 → **period 4**, even though the atom has electrons in n = 1, 2, 3, and 4 (four shells). Conversely, chromium (Z = 24) is [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹ and copper (Z = 29) is [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹ — these are **expected configurations** that break the Aufbau sequence because half-filled (d⁵) and fully-filled (d¹⁰) sub-shells gain extra stability. A common MCQ trap asks the "anomalous" element's correct configuration.

**Hydrogen's placement** is genuinely ambiguous: ns¹ puts it in group 1, but it also needs only one electron to complete its 1s shell like halogens. JEE accepts group 1 placement but tests the duality (H forms H⁺ with metals, H⁻ with active metals such as NaH, CaH₂).

**d-block elements** in their group + oxidation-state relations show that the **group number equals the number of valence electrons** (ns + (n−1)d) for groups 3–12, except that group 12 (Zn, Cd, Hg) does **not** show variable valency above +2 because the d-sub-shell is fully filled and does not participate.

#### Coordinate-Bond Mechanics and Reversibility

The donor in a coordinate bond must have a **lone pair** (NH₃, H₂O, F⁻, CO, CN⁻) and the acceptor must be **electron-deficient** (BF₃, AlCl₃, H⁺, transition-metal ions with empty d-orbitals such as Ag⁺ in [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺). Once formed, the bond is covalent — but it can revert if the acceptor's affinity is satisfied elsewhere. The **Curved-arrow notation** must always go **donor → acceptor**, never the reverse; a frequent JEE image-based question flips this.

#### Worked Micro-Example

Classify each species and identify the bond type:
(a) H₂O — **covalent, polar** (ΔEN(O − H) ≈ 1.4).
(b) CaCl₂ — **ionic** (Ca²⁺ with two Cl⁻).
(c) [Cu(NH₃)₄]²⁺ — four **coordinate bonds** from NH₃ nitrogen to Cu²⁺.
(d) NH₄⁺ — three covalent N–H bonds plus one **coordinate bond** from N's lone pair to H⁺.
(e) CO — a polar covalent bond with a small **coordinate contribution** from the oxygen lone pair into empty C anti-bonding/acceptor orbital; formal charge analysis gives a triple bond (one σ + two π) with a small dative π component.

Isoelectronic check: P³⁻, S²⁻, Cl⁻, Ar, K⁺, Ca²⁺ all have **18 electrons** — same isoelectronic series.

#### Common Mistakes

- Saying "period number = number of shells" — wrong; it equals the **highest n** used.
- Labelling all p-block elements as nonmetals; p-block contains metals, metalloids, and nonmetals.
- Drawing the dative arrow from acceptor back to donor.
- Forgetting that **variable valency is largely a d-block phenomenon**, not a p-block one.
- Treating H as a strict alkali metal — it shares properties of both group 1 and group 17.

#### Practice Prompts

1. An element has configuration [Kr] 4d¹⁰ 5s² 5p⁴. State its **period, group, block**, and predict its **electronegativity trend** relative to the element directly above it in the same group.
2. Among BF₃, NH₃, H₂O, and AlCl₃, identify which pairs can combine through a **coordinate bond** and draw the correct donor → acceptor arrow for each adduct.
