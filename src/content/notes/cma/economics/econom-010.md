# Money and Banking

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Money** is anything generally accepted as a medium of exchange, unit of account, store of value, and standard of deferred payment. The **Reserve Bank of India (RBI)** is the apex monetary authority that issues currency, regulates banks, and controls money supply through instruments like **CRR, SLR, Repo Rate, Reverse Repo Rate, Bank Rate, and Open Market Operations (OMO)**.

RBI defines four monetary aggregates — **M1** (narrow money), **M2**, **M3** (broad money), and **M4** — each adding a specific layer of deposits to the previous one.

- **M1** = Currency with public + Demand deposits + Other deposits with RBI
- **M3** = M1 + Time deposits with banks (used as broad money for policy)
- **Credit multiplier** m = 1 / r, where r = total reserve ratio

> Tip: In CMA Foundation MCQs, M1 vs M3 is the most-tested monetary aggregate distinction.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Functions and Qualities of Money
Money performs four primary functions: medium of exchange (eliminates the double-coincidence problem of barter), unit of account (common measure of value), store of value (wealth held over time), and standard of deferred payment (settles future obligations like loans). Good money must be durable, portable, divisible, homogeneous, stable in value, scarce, and acceptable.

#### Indian Monetary Aggregates
RBI classifies money into four measures. M1 is the narrowest, most liquid, and is the closest proxy for transactions money. M3 adds time deposits and is the operational target for monetary policy.

| Aggregate | Components | Liquidity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| M1 | Currency with public + Demand deposits + Other deposits with RBI | Highest |
| M2 | M1 + Savings deposits of post offices | High |
| M3 | M1 + Time deposits with banks (broad money) | Medium |
| M4 | M3 + All deposits with post offices (excl. NSC) | Lower |

#### Banking Structure in India
**RBI** stands at the apex. Below it sit **Scheduled Commercial Banks** (those listed in the Second Schedule of the RBI Act, 1934 with paid-up capital ≥ ₹5 lakh), which include Public Sector, Private Sector, Foreign, and Regional Rural Banks. Cooperative banks form a parallel tier.

#### Monetary Policy Instruments
Quantitative tools control the **quantity** of money; qualitative tools control the **flow** of credit to specific sectors.

- **CRR** — cash balance banks must keep with RBI (no interest paid).
- **SLR** — gold, government securities, or cash banks hold themselves.
- **Bank Rate** — long-term lending rate from RBI to banks.
- **Repo Rate** — short-term (overnight) rate under the Liquidity Adjustment Facility.
- **Reverse Repo Rate** — rate at which RBI absorbs funds from banks.
- **OMO** — buying/selling government securities to inject or absorb liquidity.

> Scoring tip: A one-line question on "Bank Rate vs Repo Rate" is a free mark if you remember Bank Rate = long-term, Repo Rate = overnight under LAF.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Credit Creation by Commercial Banks
A single bank cannot create unlimited credit; the **banking system as a whole** expands deposits through the multiplier. If initial deposit ΔR = ₹1,000 and reserve ratio r = 0.20, total deposit creation = ΔR / r = ₹5,000. The simple formula assumes no cash leakage and no excess reserves.

A more realistic framework uses **high-powered money (H)** = Currency with public (C) + Cash reserves of banks with RBI (R). The money multiplier becomes k = (1 + c) / (r + e + c), where c = currency-deposit ratio, r = reserve-deposit ratio, e = excess reserves ratio.

#### CRR vs SLR — Common Trap
Both ratios bind bank balance sheets but operate differently. CRR is maintained **with the RBI** in cash form, earns no interest, and directly affects lending capacity. SLR is maintained **by the bank itself** in the form of gold, government securities, approved liquid assets, or cash, and earns returns on the gilt portion.

#### Negotiable Instruments
A **cheque** is an unconditional order in writing to a bank to pay a stated sum. **Endorsement** transfers the owner's rights to a third party through a signature on the back. **Crossing** of a cheque (two parallel lines with "A/c Payee" or "Not Negotiable") adds security by restricting payment to a bank account only.

#### Inflation and Deflation
**Inflation** is a sustained rise in the general price level; **deflation** is a sustained fall. Two mechanisms: **demand-pull** (excess aggregate demand chases limited output) and **cost-push** (rising input costs like wages or oil shift the supply curve left). Measured as Inflation Rate = [(CPIₜ − CPIₜ₋₁) / CPIₜ₋₁] × 100.

1. Scheduled bank status requires listing in the Second Schedule of the RBI Act, 1934, not nationalisation.
2. M3 = M1 + Time deposits, and M3 is treated as broad money for policy targeting.
3. Practice computing ΔD = ΔR/r when r is given as a percentage (convert to decimal first).
