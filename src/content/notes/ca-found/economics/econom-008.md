# Factor Markets

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> The single most-tested idea: factor demand is *derived*, not direct.

**Factor markets** are where the services of land, labour, capital, and entrepreneurship are traded. Households supply the factors; firms demand them. The price paid for each factor service is its **factor income**.

| Factor | Payment | Who supplies | Who demands |
|---|---|---|---|
| Land | Rent | Households (landowners) | Firms, farmers |
| Labour | Wages | Households (workers) | Firms |
| Capital | Interest | Households (savers) | Firms (borrowers) |
| Entrepreneurship | Profit | Households (organisers) | Firms/owners |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook (Memory Hook): "Rent Wages Interest Profit = Land Labour Capital Entrepreneur" — read the L's down each column. RWIP on the left, LLCE on the right.**

The hiring rule a firm follows is **MRP = MFC**, where MRP = MPP × MR. Under perfect competition MR = P, so MRP = MPP × P.

---

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Most marks drop on the difference between *derived* demand and *direct* demand.

#### Why factor demand is "derived"

A firm never wants labour or a tractor for its own sake. It wants the **output** that labour and the tractor can produce. Hence demand for a factor springs from demand for the final good — economists call this **derived demand**. A fall in the price of smartphones shifts demand for assembly-line workers; a rise in the price of wheat shifts demand for tractors and farm labour. This single idea answers most "why does factor X fall/rise" questions in the exam.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix

| Concept | What it measures | Formula | When it equals MRP |
|---|---|---|---|
| MRP (Marginal Revenue Product) | Extra revenue from one more unit of factor | MPP × MR | Always the hiring benchmark |
| VMP (Value of Marginal Product) | Extra *value* of output from one more unit of factor | MPP × P | When firm is a price-taker (MR = P), VMP = MRP |
| MFC (Marginal Factor Cost) | Extra cost of hiring one more unit of factor | ΔTotal Factor Cost / ΔFactor Units | Equals factor price for a competitive firm |

Under perfect competition in the product market, **P = MR**, so VMP and MRP coincide. Under monopoly or monopolistic competition, P > MR, so VMP > MRP — examiners use this gap to test whether you know which market structure is assumed.

#### Profit-maximising factor employment

A firm hires units of a factor until the extra revenue from the last unit just matches the extra cost of that unit:

#### MRP = MFC

- For a perfectly competitive factor market, the firm's MFC is a horizontal line at the market wage W. Hiring stops where W = MRP_L = MPP_L × P.
- For a monopsony buyer of a factor, the MFC curve lies **above** the factor supply curve, so the firm hires less and pays a lower wage than a competitive buyer — a frequent CA Foundation trap.

#### Worked example you can redo in two minutes

A bakery sells cakes at P = ₹200 each in a perfectly competitive market. The 5th worker produces an extra 8 cakes per day, and the 6th worker produces an extra 6 cakes per day. The market wage is ₹1,400/day.

#### Step 1 — MRP for the 5th worker:
MRP = MPP × P = 8 × 200 = ₹1,600.

#### Step 2 — MRP for the 6th worker:
MRP = 6 × 200 = ₹1,200.

#### Step 3 — Compare with wage W = ₹1,400:
Hire the 5th worker (MRP ₹1,600 > W ₹1,400, surplus ₹200). Reject the 6th (MRP ₹1,200 < W ₹1,400, loss ₹200).

**Step 4 — Decision:** employ **5 workers**. The 6th unit is the one whose MRP first falls below the wage.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students equate MRP with the *average* product or write MRP = AFC. Always use the **marginal** physical product, never the average, and remember the marginal wage rule is MRP = MFC, not MRP = AFC.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Land behaves unlike every other factor in the short run; capital behaves unlike every other factor in the long run.

#### Edge cases that change the answer

- **Rent under Ricardian theory.** Land's supply is fixed, so rent is a **differential surplus**, not a cost of production. The formula is *Rent = Market price − Cost of production on the marginal (no-rent) land*. A student who treats rent as the landlord's cost price loses the mark.
- **Quasi-rent.** A factor whose supply is inelastic only in the short run (e.g., a specialised machine) earns a surplus above transfer earnings. Once the long run frees supply, that surplus disappears — Marshall's point.
- **Joint supply of land.** One acre can simultaneously yield crops, grazing, and mineral rights. Imputing a single rent to a single use is a textbook simplification the exam quietly exploits.
- **Supply of labour is not just about wage.** It also depends on population, migration, skill composition, and reservation wage. A higher wage can *reduce* hours worked beyond a point (backward-bending labour supply curve) — pick this up only if a question shows an unusual wage–employment graph.

#### Formulae with units and dimensions

| Symbol | Meaning | Standard unit | Dimensional formula |
|---|---|---|---|
| MRP | Marginal revenue product | ₹ per unit of factor per period | [M L² T⁻²] / [T] = [M L² T⁻³] (per period) |
| MPP | Marginal physical product | Units of output per unit of factor | dimensionless ratio |
| MR | Marginal revenue | ₹ per unit of output | [M L² T⁻³] per unit |
| P | Price of output | ₹ per unit | [M L² T⁻³] per unit |
| MFC | Marginal factor cost | ₹ per unit of factor per period | [M L² T⁻³] per unit |
| W | Wage rate | ₹ per hour | [M T⁻²] × [T] = [M L T⁻²] simplified |

#### Common traps — ranked by how often they appear

1. Saying factor demand is "direct" — it is **derived**, always.
2. Writing VMP = MRP without checking whether the product market is competitive.
3. Confusing rent (payment to land) with house rent in everyday speech.
4. Using average product instead of marginal product in the MRP formula.
5. Treating transfer payments (subsidy, pension) as factor income — they are not.

#### Two advanced prompts to test yourself

1. A textile mill operates under monopsony in the local labour market and perfect competition in cloth. Draw the supply of labour to the firm, the MFC curve, the MRP_L curve, and label the wage paid and the wage that *would* clear the market under perfect competition. Which is lower, and why?
2. A factory's demand for electricity is derived. The factory's output price falls by 20% while MPP of electricity stays constant. State, with the formula, the exact percentage change in MRP_E and explain why electricity consumption may not fall by 20%.
