# Legal News

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Legal News in CLAT tests your awareness of Indian judicial, legislative and constitutional developments from the 12-month window before the exam. Marks sit inside the Current Affairs & GK section, contributing roughly 5% of the paper (about 2–3 questions out of the 50 in that section).

- **Core sources:** The Hindu, Indian Express, LiveLaw, Bar & Bench, Supreme Court Observer.
- **Three new criminal codes** replaced the IPC, CrPC and Indian Evidence Act on **1 July 2024**: BNS, BNSS and BSA — old section numbers no longer apply.
- **High-yield items:** SC and HC landmark rulings, ordinances under **Article 123**, constitutional amendments, PIL outcomes and statutory notifications from the Ministry of Law and Justice.

#### What examiners test

CLAT frames Legal News as comprehension passages or factual MCQs drawn from Supreme Court and High Court rulings, parliamentary enactments, and ordinances. Roughly 5% of the total paper (≈2–3 questions) comes from this sub-area, embedded inside the 25-mark Current Affairs section. Each question rewards a student who can identify the court, bench strength, statutory provision, and the precise relief granted.

#### Mapping a news item correctly

Read every item through five filters: court and bench, judges, citation/date, statutory provisions interpreted, and relief granted. Without these five, you risk the most common trap — writing the political slug instead of the legal issue. Always cite the **BNS section number** (for example Section 302 BNS for murder, not IPC 302) in any answer written after July 2024.

#### Key vocabulary

- **Ratio decidendi** — the binding legal principle; only this is the "law declared" under Article 141.
- **Obiter dicta** — persuasive observations by the bench, not binding on lower courts.
- **PIL (Public Interest Litigation)** — filed under Article 32 (SC) or Article 226 (HC).
- **Article 142** — SC's complete power to pass orders necessary for "complete justice".

#### Edge cases that change your answer

#### Common traps and corrections

| Trap | Why it loses marks | Correct approach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quoting IPC sections after 1 July 2024 | Code repealed and replaced by BNS | Use BNS/BNSS/BSA section numbers only |
| Citing only the headline | Loses ratio and relief | Write issue, provision interpreted, relief granted |
| Treating obiter as binding | Article 141 binds only ratio decidendi | Separate ratio from observations in any answer |
| Confusing HC division bench with SC | Two courts, two precedential values | Check sci.gov.in for SC, then hcservices.ecourts.gov.in for HC |
| Citing a Law Commission Report as law | Reports are recommendations, not statutes | Note "recommended by Law Commission, not yet enacted" |

#### Connections to adjacent topics

Legal News overlaps heavily with **Constitutional Law** (basic structure doctrine, Article 370 abrogation aftermath, Article 21 privacy rulings), **Criminal Law** (BNS, BNSS bail provisions, PMLA amendments), and **Polity** (ordinances under Article 123, President and Governor assent flows). Linking a news item to its parent doctrine, rather than reading it in isolation, lifts comprehension-passage scores.

#### Worked micro-example

A 2025 question reads: *"The Supreme Court recently held that the right to clean air is enforceable under Article 21. Which writ did the court issue?"* The news item — *In re: Right to Clean Air* — disposed a PIL under **Article 32**, and the bench issued a **mandamus** to states. The ratio is that non-feasance on pollution control violates Article 21, and the writ flows from Article 32. Writing only "the court slammed the government" loses marks because it skips writ, article, and relief.

#### Practice prompts

1. List five SC judgments from the last 12 months and identify the ratio decidendi in each.
2. Take any three ordinances promulgated under Article 123; state whether each has lapsed, been re-promulgated, or replaced by an Act.
