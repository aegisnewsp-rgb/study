# The Scientific Method in Practice

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The **scientific method** is a structured, evidence-based procedure that turns observations into reliable knowledge through **testable hypotheses** and **controlled experiments**. It is iterative: results feed back into a refined hypothesis rather than ending the inquiry.

- **Step sequence:** Observation → Question → Hypothesis (If…then…) → Prediction → Experiment (with control group) → Data analysis → Conclusion → Peer review and replication.
- **Variables trio:** *independent* (what you change), *dependent* (what you measure), *controlled* (what you keep constant).
- **Hypothesis rule:** must be **falsifiable** — phrased as a statement predicting an outcome, never as a question.
- **Examiner trap:** the method is cyclical, not linear; a single experiment never "proves" a hypothesis.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core flow of the method

A researcher begins with an **observation** that prompts a focused, answerable **question**. The question is reformulated as a **hypothesis** — a falsifiable statement of the form "If [independent variable changes], then [dependent variable] will [predicted outcome]." From the hypothesis, a **prediction** is deduced using prior theory, then tested through a **controlled experiment**.

#### Variables and the control group

Experimental design hinges on isolating cause and effect. The **independent variable** is manipulated, the **dependent variable** is measured, and **controlled variables** are held constant. A **control group** receives no treatment, providing the baseline against which the experimental group is compared.

- A hypothesis written as "Does light affect plant growth?" is invalid — it is a question, not a prediction.
- A single trial cannot confirm a claim; replication across conditions and researchers is required.
- Peer review exposes methodological flaws before results enter the accepted body of knowledge.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Why the method is cyclical

Philosophers of science (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy) describe the method as iterative: a **conclusion** that contradicts the hypothesis triggers a revised hypothesis, not abandonment of science. Negative results are publishable and informative — they narrow the hypothesis space and prevent wasted replication.

#### Bias, reproducibility, and peer review

A robust study controls for **observer bias** (double-blind designs), **sampling bias** (randomisation), and **confirmation bias** (pre-registration of methods). **Reproducibility** — independent teams obtaining the same result — is the ultimate credibility test. Peer review filters method and statistical errors before publication.

#### Common mistakes flagged by CAT examiners

1. Listing the method as a strict linear sequence instead of a feedback loop.
2. Confusing independent and dependent variables, which inverts the cause-effect claim.
3. Omitting the control group so that "no change detected" is uninterpretable.
4. Treating a supported hypothesis as "proved" rather than "not falsified by current evidence."

#### Micro-worked example

**Observation:** A leaf appears greener after watering with milk. **Hypothesis:** *If* diluted milk (independent: milk concentration in mL/L) *is applied to soil*, *then* leaf chlorophyll concentration (dependent: mg/g) *will increase* compared with water alone. **Control:** plants watered with equal volumes of plain water. **Controlled variables:** light hours, temperature, pot size, plant species. **Analysis:** a t-test compares means; **conclusion** accepts or rejects the hypothesis and guides the next iteration.

#### Practice prompts

1. A student writes: "Does temperature change the rate of dissolving?" — rewrite it as a valid hypothesis.
2. In an experiment on sleep and memory, identify which variable is dependent and justify the need for a control group.
