# Pakistan's Economy & Industry

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your SPSC paper.

Pakistan runs a **mixed developing economy** measured annually in the *Pakistan Economic Survey 2024-25* by the Ministry of Finance. The expenditure formula you must memorise is:

> **GDP = C + I + G + (X − M)**

where C is household **consumption**, I is **investment**, G is **government spending**, X is **exports**, and M is **imports** — all valued in PKR.

Three high-yield facts for the MCQ paper:

- **Sectors (approximate shares):** Agriculture ~22–24%, Industry ~20–22%, Services ~53–55%.
- **Sindh contributes ~47% of national GDP** on only ~24% of the population, driven by Karachi's port trade, cotton, rice, and large-scale manufacturing at SITE Karachi.
- **Textiles account for ~60% of Pakistan's exports** — the single most-tested industrial fact.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months of preparation.

Pakistan's economy is conventionally split into three productive sectors whose shares are quoted yearly in the Economic Survey. **Agriculture** (~22–24% of GDP, ~37–40% of employment) produces wheat, rice, cotton, sugarcane, and maize; Sindh leads in rice and cotton. **Industry** (~20–22%) includes Large-Scale Manufacturing (LSM), mining, construction, and energy distribution; textiles dominate exports while cement, fertilisers, and automotive assembly anchor domestic industry. **Services** (~53–55%) cover finance, transport, retail, IT, and public administration.

#### Key Macroeconomic Measures

| Indicator | Formula (PKR where relevant) | What it captures |
|---|---|---|
| GDP | C + I + G + (X − M) | Output produced *inside* Pakistan |
| GNP | GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad | Output earned by *Pakistani nationals* |
| Real GDP | (Nominal GDP ÷ GDP Deflator) × 100 | Output at base-year prices (strips out inflation) |
| Per Capita Income | GDP ÷ Total Population | Average income per person |
| Inflation Rate | [(CPI_t − CPI_{t−1}) ÷ CPI_{t−1}] × 100 | Annual price change |

#### Institutions You Must Name

- **State Bank of Pakistan (SBP)** — monetary policy, foreign-exchange reserves, banking regulation.
- **Planning Commission** — Five-Year Plans and the annual Public Sector Development Programme (PSDP).
- **Privatisation Commission** — divestment of state-owned enterprises.
- **Ministry of Finance** — publishes the *Pakistan Economic Survey* every fiscal year.

> **Trap:** Fiscal deficit and current account deficit look similar but differ — *fiscal* is government revenue vs. expenditure, while *current account* is trade + services + remittances combined.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

Sindh is Pakistan's economic engine: **Karachi Port and Port Qasim** handle roughly 60% of national port throughput, while the SITE industrial area hosts textiles, pharmaceuticals, automotive assembly (Suzuki, Indus Motor), and engineering units. Coastal districts contribute a **fisheries** subsector, and Hyderabad-Sukkur corridor produces cement (Thatta, Dadu) and sugar.

#### Common Mistakes Examiners Exploit

1. **GDP vs. GNP** — GDP counts output produced within borders; GNP adds income Pakistanis earn abroad (remittances, overseas labour) and subtracts what foreigners earn inside.
2. **Nominal vs. real growth** — a 28% nominal GDP jump with 25% inflation is barely 2–3% real growth. Always use the deflator.
3. **Sector share vs. employment share** — agriculture's ~22% GDP share hides its ~38% employment share, indicating productivity gaps that move-ups relax.
4. **EPZ vs. SEZ** — Export Processing Zones (older, export-only) differ from Special Economic Zones built under **CPEC** (broader tax/customs incentives, longer lease terms).
5. **Privatisation vs. denationalisation** — privatisation transfers State units to private buyers; denationalisation reverses earlier nationalisation (e.g. 1970s units returned post-1990s).

#### Practice Prompts

- *"Which province contributes the largest share to Pakistan's GDP?"* — answer: **Sindh (~47%)**.
- *"Calculate real GDP growth if nominal GDP rose 25% and the deflator rose 18%."* — answer: ~**5.9% real growth**.
