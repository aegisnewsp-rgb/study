# General Science in UPPSC RO/ARO Prelims

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

General Science in UPPSC RO/ARO Prelims covers Physics, Chemistry and Biology at the Class 10–12 NCERT level, contributing 8–12 questions out of 150. The section rewards recall of definitions, SI units, formulas and disease facts rather than advanced derivations.

- **Key formulas:** F = m·a, W = F·d, V = I·R, P = V·I, v = u + at, 1/f = 1/v + 1/u.
- **SI base units:** metre (m), kilogram (kg), second (s), ampere (A), kelvin (K), mole (mol), candela (cd).
- **High-yield facts:** g = 9.8 m/s², pH < 7 acidic, Vitamin C is water-soluble, malaria vector is *Anopheles* mosquito.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Physics Fundamentals

Mechanics questions test Newton's three laws and the universal law of gravitation. Weight W = m·g differs from mass because gravity varies, while mass remains constant. Work–energy problems use W = F·d (cosθ) and the kinetic energy relation KE = ½mv²; power P = W/t measured in Watts.

Light and optics questions rely on the mirror equation 1/f = 1/v + 1/u, with sign convention for concave mirrors (focal length negative) versus convex mirrors (positive). Current electricity circuits repeat Ohm's law V = IR and the rules for series (R = R₁+R₂) and parallel (1/R = 1/R₁+1/R₂) resistors.

#### Chemistry Basics

Chemical equations must be balanced: H₂ + Cl₂ → 2HCl. The pH scale runs 0–14, where values below 7 indicate acidic solutions, 7 is neutral, and above 7 is basic/alkaline. Periodic table trends relevant to Prelims are atomic number, period (row) and group (column).

#### Biology and Health

Cell biology basics include the nucleus, mitochondria, ribosomes and cell membrane. Common diseases asked include tuberculosis (bacterial, *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*), malaria (protozoan, vector *Anopheles*), dengue (virus, vector *Aedes aegypti*) and COVID-19 (SARS-CoV-2 coronavirus).

| Vitamin | Solubility | Deficiency Disease |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vitamin A | Fat-soluble | Night blindness |
| Vitamin B-complex | Water-soluble | Beriberi, pellagra |
| Vitamin C | Water-soluble | Scurvy |
| Vitamin D | Fat-soluble | Rickets |
| Vitamin K | Fat-soluble | Slow blood clotting |

#### Common Exam Traps

- Writing "degree Kelvin" — correct symbol is K with no degree sign.
- Confusing Vitamin A deficiency (night blindness) with Vitamin D (rickets).
- In vacuum, all objects fall at the same g, regardless of mass.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Worked Numerical

**Problem:** A 2 kg block is pushed with 10 N across a frictionless surface for 3 s starting from rest. Find (a) acceleration, (b) final velocity, (c) work done.

#### Solution:
- a = F/m = 10/2 = 5 m/s²
- v = u + at = 0 + 5×3 = 15 m/s
- W = F·d = F·(½at²) = 10·(½·5·9) = 225 J

#### Edge Cases and Common Mistakes

| Concept | Wrong Belief | Correct Statement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mass vs weight | "Weight is in kg" | Mass in kg; weight W = mg in N |
| pH paper | "pH 8 is acidic" | pH < 7 is acidic |
| Chemical equilibrium | "Reactions stop" | Dynamic — forward and reverse rates equal |
| Light in vacuum | "Heavy objects fall faster" | All fall at g = 9.8 m/s² |
| Vitamin D | Causes night blindness | Rickets and osteomalacia |

#### Everyday Science Connections

Soaps are sodium salts of long-chain fatty acids; detergents are sulphonate-based and work in hard water. Polymers like PVC (polyvinyl chloride) and polyethylene appear in packaging questions. Fertilizers (NPK) and pesticides are tested for their primary nutrient or chemical class.

#### Practice Prompts

1. A resistor of 12 Ω and 6 Ω are connected in parallel across a 12 V battery. Find the total current and power dissipated.
2. Identify the vitamin deficiency for each: (a) scurvy, (b) beriberi, (c) night blindness, (d) rickets. State solubility in each case.

#### Exam Strategy

UPPSC RO/ARO questions are statement-based MCQs worth 1 mark each with negative marking of 0.33. Skip vitamin-solubility pairings if unsure — they carry heavy negative-marking risk. Prioritize chapters on diseases, SI units, basic electricity and everyday chemistry; these four areas together yield roughly 60% of the section's questions.

> **Strategic tip:** Read the Class 6–10 NCERT Science textbook summary boxes before the exam — UPPSC RO/ARO tends to source 5–6 questions directly from those boxed facts.
