# Uganda's Legal System and Court Structure

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Uganda operates a **pluralistic common-law legal system** inherited from British colonial rule. The 1995 Constitution of Uganda is the supreme law, binding every organ of the State (Article 2). Below it sit Acts of Parliament, delegated legislation, customary law, and the received common law and doctrines of equity (Article 122). Uganda has **no separate Constitutional Court** — constitutional matters are heard by the High Court sitting with a bench of three or more justices.

- **Apex court:** Supreme Court (final appellate jurisdiction, 5–7 justices for constitutional questions).
- **Civil monetary limits (Magistrates Courts Act):** Magistrate Grade II → small claims; Grade I → up to UGX 20 million; Chief Magistrate → up to UGX 50 million.
- **Doctrine of binding precedent (stare decisis):** ratio decidendi of a higher court binds lower courts in the hierarchy.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Sources of Ugandan Law

The Constitution (1995, last amended 2017) sits at the apex. Acts of Parliament enacted by the unicameral Parliament follow. Delegated legislation (statutory instruments, rules, bylaws) made under enabling Acts comes third. Customary law applies **only where the parties are subject to it** and only to the extent it is not repugnant to natural justice, equity, and good conscience, nor inconsistent with any written law. The Judicature Act Cap 13 directs courts to apply English common law and doctrines of equity as received, subject to local circumstances.

#### Court Hierarchy and Jurisdiction

The judicial structure descends from the Supreme Court to the Court of Appeal, the High Court (with Constitutional, Commercial, Execution and International Crimes Divisions), the Industrial Court, the Chief Magistrate's Court, Magistrates Grade I and II, and at the base Local Council II courts handling only customary civil matters.

| Court | Key Jurisdiction Point |
| --- | --- |
| Supreme Court | Final appellate court; 5–7 justices decide constitutional and interpretation matters |
| Court of Appeal | Hears appeals from High Court and subordinate courts/tribunals |
| High Court | Unlimited original civil/criminal jurisdiction; supervisory review of subordinate courts |
| Industrial Court | Labour, employment, and related disputes under Article 137 |
| Magistrates' Courts | Schedule-based criminal jurisdiction; civil claims by monetary limit |
| Local Council II | Customary civil matters only, minor monetary value |

#### Judicial Precedent

Higher courts bind lower courts within the hierarchy. The Supreme Court's decisions on points of law are binding on all other courts. The Court of Appeal binds the High Court and subordinate courts except where the Supreme Court has since ruled otherwise. Obiter dicta are persuasive, not binding.

- Always check the **year of the decision** — older English common-law authorities may be displaced by Ugandan statutes.
- Article 50 of the Constitution permits any aggrieved person to bring a public interest suit, expanding access to the High Court.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Common Misconceptions

A persistent trap is the belief that Uganda has a free-standing Constitutional Court comparable to South Africa or Kenya. The High Court sitting with a bench of three or more justices performs that constitutional function (Article 137(1)). Tribunal decisions of bodies such as the Equal Opportunities Commission or the Uganda Revenue Authority are **subordinate to the High Court** and are subject to judicial review on points of law, jurisdiction, and procedural fairness.

Customary law is further constrained by the Land Act Cap 227 and the Constitution's equality clauses; customary rules that discriminate on grounds of sex, ethnicity, or religion are void. Local Council II courts are **not courts of record** and their decisions are revisable by the Chief Magistrate's Court.

#### Connections to Other LDC Papers

Mastery of forum selection underpins Civil Procedure (CPR), Criminal Procedure, and the Land Law papers. The hierarchy rules determine whether an appeal lies as of right or requires leave — Supreme Court appeals generally require leave, while Court of Appeal appeals from the High Court in civil matters over UGX 50 million lie as of right.

#### Common Mistakes

- Treating Local Council II courts as full courts of record.
- Assuming customary law binds non-customary communities automatically.
- Citing the Court of Appeal as Uganda's apex court.
- Ignoring the Industrial Court's statutory origin under Article 137.
