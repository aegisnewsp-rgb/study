# Kingdom Monera

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Kingdom Monera** contains all **prokaryotes** — unicellular organisms whose circular, double-stranded DNA lies free in the cytoplasm as a **nucleoid**, with no nuclear membrane and no membrane-bound organelles (no mitochondria, ER, Golgi, lysosomes). The cell wall, where present, contains **peptidoglycan (murein)** in eubacteria and **pseudopeptidoglycan / S-layer protein** in archaebacteria. Nutrition spans autotrophic (photo- and chemo-) and heterotrophic modes; reproduction is principally **binary fission**.

| Group | Defining Trait | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Archaebacteria | Wall lacks peptidoglycan; ether-linked branched lipids; extremophiles | *Methanobacterium*, *Halobacterium* |
| Eubacteria | True peptidoglycan wall; ester-linked lipids | *E. coli*, *Streptococcus* |
| Cyanobacteria (BGA) | Photosynthetic, chlorophyll-*a* + phycobilins; N₂-fixing **heterocyst** | *Nostoc*, *Anabaena* |
| Mycoplasma (PPLO) | **Wall-less**, pleomorphic; penicillin-resistant | *M. pneumoniae* |
| Rickettsiae | Obligate intracellular parasites; arthropod-borne | *R. prowazekii* |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"AMCREAM"** — **A**rchaebacteria, **M**ycoplasma, **C**yanobacteria, **R**ickettsiae, **E**ubacteria, **A**ctinomycetes, **M**ethanogens are the Monera sub-groups NEET loves to test.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Cell Architecture of a Prokaryote

A bacterial cell carries a single circular chromosome (~1 mm long when uncoiled, packed into the **nucleoid**) plus smaller **plasmids**. The plasma membrane invaginates to form **mesosomes**, which host respiratory enzymes, DNA replication machinery, and cell-wall synthesis — they functionally replace mitochondria. Surface appendages include a single polar or peritrichous **flagellum** (locomotion), **pili/fimbriae** (conjugation, attachment), and a polysaccharide **capsule/slime layer** (protection, virulence).

#### Archaebacteria vs Eubacteria — Concept Matrix

| Feature | Archaebacteria | Eubacteria |
|---|---|---|
| Cell wall | Pseudopeptidoglycan / S-layer protein; **no true peptidoglycan** | Peptidoglycan (murein) |
| Membrane lipid | Branched isoprenoids, **ether** linkages | Straight fatty acids, **ester** linkages |
| Habitat | Extreme — high salt, high temp, low pH, anaerobic guts | Ubiquitous — soil, water, living hosts |
| Example niches | *Halobacterium* (salt lakes), *Sulfolobus* (hot springs), *Methanobacterium* (rumen, sludge) | *E. coli*, *Rhizobium*, *Lactobacillus* |

#### Nutrition Modes in Bacteria

1. **Photoautotrophs** — use bacteriochlorophyll / chlorophyll-*a* (e.g., purple sulphur bacteria *Chromatium*).
2. **Chemoautotrophs** — derive energy from oxidation of inorganic substrates: *Nitrosomonas* (NH₃ → NO₂⁻), *Nitrobacter* (NO₂⁻ → NO₃⁻), sulphur bacteria (H₂S → S).
3. **Heterotrophs** — saprophytic decomposers, parasites, or symbionts (*Rhizobium* in legume nodules).

> ⚠️ **Note:** Methanogens are *not* photosynthetic and do not release O₂; they are strict anaerobes producing CH₄ (biogas).

#### Gram Staining (Christian Gram, 1884)

| Step | Reagent | Gram-positive result | Gram-negative result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary stain | Crystal violet | Purple | Purple |
| Mordant | Iodine | Purple (CV–I complex trapped) | Purple |
| Decolouriser | Alcohol/acetone | **Retains** purple (thick peptidoglycan) | **Loses** purple (thin wall + LPS) |
| Counterstain | Safranin | Purple | **Pink/Red** |

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** A soil sample yields bacteria that retain crystal violet after decolourisation, possess a thick peptidoglycan layer with **teichoic acids**, and lack lipopolysaccharide (LPS). Identify the group, and state why the antibiotic **penicillin** is effective against them but **not** against *Mycoplasma pneumoniae*.

#### Solution:
1. Retention of crystal violet → **Gram-positive**.
2. Thick peptidoglycan + teichoic acids + no LPS confirms Gram-positive classification (e.g., *Streptococcus*, *Staphylococcus*, *Bacillus subtilis*).
3. Penicillin inhibits **transpeptidase** (DD-transpeptidase / PBP), blocking cross-linking of peptidoglycan strands → the wall weakens, the cell lyses.
4. *Mycoplasma pneumoniae* **lacks a cell wall entirely** (only a sterol-containing plasma membrane) → penicillin has **no target**; treatment uses tetracyclines or macrolides (e.g., erythromycin) instead.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students often claim "penicillin kills all bacteria." It cannot work on **wall-less Mycoplasma** or on intracellular **Rickettsiae** (poor drug penetration into host cells) — drug choice depends on the bacterium's structure and habitat.

#### Bacterial Shapes — Identification Table

| Shape | Name | Example | Disease (if any) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spherical | Coccus (mono-/diplo-/strepto-/staphylo-) | *Streptococcus pneumoniae* | Pneumonia |
| Rod | Bacillus | *Clostridium tetani*, *E. coli* | Tetanus, diarrhoea |
| Comma | Vibrio | *Vibrio cholerae* | Cholera |
| Spiral | Spirillum / Spirochete | *Treponema pallidum* | Syphilis |
| Filamentous | Actinomycetes | *Streptomyces* (source of streptomycin) | Tuberculosis drug source |

#### Advanced Traps and Exceptions

1. **Mycoplasma sterol requirement** — Although wall-less, Mycoplasma incorporates **cholesterol / sterols** from the host into its plasma membrane to stabilise it; this is unique among prokaryotes.
2. **Heterocyst ≠ spore** — A **heterocyst** (in *Nostoc*, *Anabaena*) is a thick-walled, nitrogenase-containing cell for **N₂ fixation** under aerobic conditions; it is *not* a reproductive structure. Vegetative cells perform photosynthesis but cannot fix nitrogen because O₂ inactivates nitrogenase.
3. **Cyanobacteria are NOT algae** — They are prokaryotes placed in Monera despite being photosynthetic; this violates the "all photosynthetic organisms are plants" misconception and is a classic NEET distractor (e.g., option: "Nostoc is a green alga" — wrong).
4. **Rickettsiae are bacteria, not viruses** — They have a cell wall, ribosomes (70S), and DNA/RNA; but they are **obligate intracellular parasites** because they rely on host ATP. Vector-borne: louse (*R. prowazekii* → epidemic typhus), tick (*R. rickettsii* → Rocky Mountain spotted fever), mite (*Orientia tsutsugamushi* → scrub typhus).
5. **Bacterial growth mathematics** — From a single cell, after *n* generations: **Nₜ = N₀ × 2ⁿ**, where N₀ = initial cells, Nₜ = cells after time *t*, *n* = *t* / *t_g*, and *t_g* (generation time, min) = **ln 2 / k** ≈ **0.693 / k**. *E. coli* under optimum conditions has *t_g* ≈ 20 min.
6. **Resolving power matters** — Resolving power of a compound microscope follows the **Abbe limit**: d = (0.61 · λ) / (n · sin θ), where d = smallest resolvable distance (m), λ = wavelength of light (m), n · sin θ = numerical aperture (typically 1.25 with oil immersion). Because Mycoplasma ≈ 0.1–0.3 µm, oil-immersion objectives (~1000× total magnification) are required to visualise them.
7. **Genetic recombination, not true sexual reproduction** — Conjugation (F-pilus / Hfr transfer), transformation (uptake of free DNA — Griffith, 1928), and transduction (phage-mediated) allow gene flow without meiosis or gametes.
8. **Beneficial bacteria** — *Rhizobium* (N₂ fixation in legumes), *Lactobacillus* (curd/yoghurt), *Streptomyces* (≈70% of clinical antibiotics), cyanobacteria as **biofertilisers** in paddy fields (*Anabaena* in *Azolla*).

#### Practice Prompts

1. A researcher isolates a microbe from a hot sulphur spring (pH 2, 80 °C). Predict its kingdom, lipid linkage type, cell-wall composition, and a likely genus.
2. Compare the cell envelopes of *Escherichia coli* (Gram-negative) and *Bacillus subtilis* (Gram-positive), and explain why each reacts differently to lysozyme and penicillin.
