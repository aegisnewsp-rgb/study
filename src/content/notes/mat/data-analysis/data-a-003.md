# Pie Charts & Circle Diagrams

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your MAT Data Analysis slot.

A pie chart divides a circle into wedges where each sector's **central angle** is proportional to its share of the total. The full circle equals **360° = 100%**, so **1% corresponds to 3.6°**.

- **Central Angle (°) = (Component Value / Total Value) × 360°**
- **Percentage Share (%) = (Component Value / Total Value) × 100**
- **Component Value = (Sector Angle / 360°) × Total Value**

MAT questions on this topic in most keys ask for percentage distribution, the ratio of two categories, or the absolute spend/value of a sector when the total revenue, expenditure, or population is given. Always divide by the stated total first — the "total" is sometimes a sub-group, not the grand sum.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months before MAT.

#### Reading the Chart
MAT pie diagrams in standard papers show 4–8 categories, and sectors are read clockwise from the **12 o'clock position**. The largest wedge represents the biggest contributor and the smallest represents the least. When a chart shows monthly expenditure on rent, food, transport, and savings, the **Total Expenditure** (not income) is the base for every percentage.

#### Conversion Steps
1. Sum the given component values to get the total.
2. Compute each category's fraction of the total.
3. Multiply that fraction by 360° to get the central angle.
4. Verify: all angles should add back to 360°.

#### Quick Reference Table

| Quantity | Formula | Units |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Central Angle | (Component / Total) × 360 | degrees (°) |
| Percentage Share | (Component / Total) × 100 | percent (%) |
| Component Value | (Sector Angle / 360°) × Total | same as total units |
| Arc Length | (Sector Angle / 360°) × 2πr | same as r |
| Degrees per 1% | 360 / 100 = 3.6 | ° per % |

- If a sector measures 90°, its share is 90/360 = 25%.
- If rent is 25% of an expenditure of ₹40,000, rent equals ₹10,000.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Worked Example
A firm's monthly expenditure of ₹6,00,000 is split across four heads: Salaries ₹3,00,000, Rent ₹1,20,000, Raw Material ₹1,20,000, and Other ₹60,000. Compute each central angle.

- Salaries: (3,00,000 / 6,00,000) × 360 = **180°**
- Rent: (1,20,000 / 6,00,000) × 360 = **72°**
- Raw Material: **72°**
- Other: (60,000 / 6,00,000) × 360 = **36°**

Sum check: 180 + 72 + 72 + 36 = 360°. If rent rises by ₹30,000, new rent angle = (1,50,000 / 6,30,000) × 360 ≈ 85.7°, and Salaries share drops to ~171.4°.

#### Edge Cases and Connections
Multi-pie or **pie-of-pie** charts appear in MAT when a single category is exploded into sub-shares; treat each ring as an independent pie with its own 360°. Comparisons between two pie charts (e.g., 2024 vs 2025 revenue mix) require converting each wedge to a percentage before comparing, because the totals can differ. This connects directly to **Bar Charts** and **Line Graphs**, where absolute values dominate.

#### Common Mistakes
- Using the decimal share directly as the angle (e.g., writing 0.25 instead of 90°).
- Misreading the **3 o'clock starting point** assumption; MAT charts begin at 12.
- Dividing by grand total when the question specifies a **sub-total**.
- Sign errors when the question asks for the **difference** between two sectors.

#### Practice Prompts
1. In a pie chart showing ₹5,00,000 expenditure, a sector subtends 54°. Find the sector value.
2. Transport (20%) and Food (35%) of a ₹2,40,000 budget — what is the angle difference between the two sectors in degrees?
