# Wave Optics

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Wave Optics treats light as an electromagnetic wave whose **superposition** produces interference, diffraction, and polarisation — three effects geometric optics cannot predict. Two coherent sources (same frequency, fixed phase relation) give a stable fringe pattern whose spacing depends only on wavelength, slit separation, and screen distance.

| Quantity | Formula | Variables & SI units |
|---|---|---|
| Fringe width (YDSE) | β = λD/d | λ wavelength (m), D screen distance (m), d slit separation (m) |
| Path difference | Δx = d sinθ ≈ dy/D | y fringe position from centre (m) |
| Resultant intensity | I = I₁ + I₂ + 2√(I₁I₂) cos Δφ | Δφ phase difference (rad); intensity in W·m⁻² |
| Brewster angle | tan i_B = μ | μ refractive index of denser medium |
| Malus's law | I = I₀ cos²θ | θ angle between polariser transmission axes (rad) |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"YDS = λD/d"** — *Young's Double Slit Fringe width = lambda × Distance / separation*. Pair it with the phase rule: **"Path λ → bright, Path λ/2 → dark"** to handle YDSE in under a minute.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Mechanisms
Huygens' principle states that every point on a wavefront acts as a source of secondary wavelets; the new wavefront is the envelope of these wavelets. The principle yields the laws of reflection and refraction and explains why light bends around edges (diffraction). For sustained interference the two sources must be **coherent** — derived from the same wavefront (e.g., the two slits in YDSE illuminated by one source). Intensity, not amplitude, combines because detectors respond to the time-averaged energy flux.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix

| Phenomenon | Cause | Condition for maxima | Condition for minima | Distinctive feature |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Interference (YDSE) | Two coherent sources | Δx = nλ | Δx = (n + ½)λ | Equal-width bright and dark fringes |
| Diffraction (single slit) | Bounded wavefront | Central broad maximum | a sinθ = nλ, n ≠ 0 | Central fringe is twice as wide as others |
| Thin-film (reflected) | Phase change at denser boundary | 2μt cos r = (n + ½)λ | 2μt cos r = nλ | Extra π shift on reflection from denser medium |
| Diffraction grating | N slits interfere | (d + a) sinθ = nλ | Adjacent minima overlap | Sharp principal maxima |

#### Key Steps for YDSE Numericals
1. Identify d (slit separation) and D (screen-to-slit distance); never confuse d with slit width a.
2. Compute fringe width β = λD/d.
3. If a slab of thickness t and refractive index μ is inserted, the pattern shifts by **S = (μ−1)tD/d**; the shift is towards the slab side.
4. Convert all angles to radians before applying path-difference or phase formulas.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem
**Question:** In a YDSE setup, the slit separation is d = 0.5 mm and the screen is D = 1 m away. A thin film of refractive index μ = 1.5 and thickness t = 4 µm is placed in front of one slit. If the incident light has wavelength λ = 500 nm, how many fringes will shift across the central maximum?

#### Solution:
Fringe shift formula: S = (μ − 1)tD/d.
Substituting: S = (1.5 − 1) × (4 × 10⁻⁶ m) × (1 m) / (0.5 × 10⁻³ m) = 0.5 × 4 × 10⁻⁶ / 5 × 10⁻⁴ = 8 × 10⁻³ m.
Fringe width: β = λD/d = (500 × 10⁻⁹ m)(1 m) / (0.5 × 10⁻³ m) = 1 × 10⁻³ m.
Number of fringes shifted = S/β = 8 × 10⁻³ / 1 × 10⁻³ = **8 fringes**.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students commonly compute the optical path change as 2μt instead of (μ−1)t, because the unshifted air path is already present. Use (μ−1)t for the *net* shift of the pattern, not 2μt (the latter belongs to thin-film interference, not YDSE slab problems).

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Advanced Traps

1. **White-light YDSE:** The central fringe is white; coloured fringes appear only on either side because β ∝ λ. The dispersion of the pattern gives a near-linear colour spread.
2. **Newton's rings:** Radii satisfy r_n² = nλR for dark rings (reflected light) or r_n² = (n − ½)λR for bright rings when a plano-convex lens sits on a glass plate; R is the lens radius of curvature (m).
3. **Wedge film:** Fringes are straight, parallel, equally spaced; fringe spacing s = λ/(2μθ) where θ is the wedge angle (rad). Engineers use this to measure thin-film thickness or flatness.
4. **Resolving power of grating:** R = nN, where N is the total number of illuminated lines and n is the order. For n = 2 and N = 10⁴, R = 2 × 10⁴, meaning Δλ ≈ 0.05 nm at λ = 1000 nm.
5. **Polarisation by reflection:** Complete polarisation occurs *only* at Brewster's angle; at all other angles the reflected light is partially polarised. At i_B the reflected and refracted rays are exactly 90° apart — a useful geometric check.
6. **Doppler-free spectroscopy limit:** R increases with order, but high orders have weaker intensity and overlapping spectra. Practical gratings rarely exceed n = 5 without an Echelle configuration.

#### Common Mistakes in JEE Advanced Numerical Sets

| Mistake | Correct treatment |
|---|---|
| Using λ in nm in β = λD/d without converting | Always convert to metres: 1 nm = 10⁻⁹ m |
| Confusing diffraction grating element (d+a) with YDSE d | Grating uses the period (d+a); YDSE d is the slit-to-slit distance |
| Applying Malus's law with θ measured from the wrong axis | θ is between polariser and analyser transmission axes |
| Forgetting that interference term averages to zero for incoherent sources | Incoherent I = I₁ + I₂ — the cross-term vanishes |
| Treating Brewster's angle and critical angle as interchangeable | tan i_B = μ (polarisation); sin i_C = 1/μ (total internal reflection) |

#### Advanced Practice Prompts
1. A diffraction grating with 5000 lines/cm is illuminated at normal incidence by light containing wavelengths 589.0 nm and 589.6 nm. Find the smallest order at which the sodium doublet is just resolved.
2. A wedge-shaped air film of angle 0.5° is formed between two glass plates. If 12 fringes are observed per cm using λ = 600 nm, compute the wedge angle and verify with the formula s = λ/(2θ).
