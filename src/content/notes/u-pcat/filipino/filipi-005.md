# Topic 5

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

**Pangungusap** (Sentences) is a group of words containing a **simuno** (subject) and a **panag-uri** (predicate) that expresses a complete thought. For UPCAT Filipino, you must recognise four sentence types by their function and ending punctuation:

| Sentence Type | Filipino Name | Function | Ends With |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deklaratibo | **Padamog** | States facts, opinions, or promises | Period (.) |
| Imperatibo | **Pautos** | Issues commands or requests | Period (.) or command verb |
| Patanong | **Patanong** | Asks questions | Question mark (?) |
| Pagkahagulgol | **Pagkahagulgol** | Expresses strong emotion | Exclamation mark (!) |

**Three structural variants** also appear on the exam: **Pangungusap na Tudling** (compound sentence with two equivalent parts joined by a comma or conjunction) and **Pangungusap na Panudla** (imperative with no stated subject — used in oaths and calls). The **simuno** names the person or thing being discussed; the **panag-uri** describes or states the action of the simuno. A sentence is only complete when both are present.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Defining the Pangungusap

A **pangungusap** is a grammatically complete unit of thought formed by a **simuno** (subject) and a **panag-uri** (predicate). The simuno answers *who* or *what* is being discussed; the panag-uri tells what the simuno does, is, or is like. Example: In *"Ang mag-aaral ay nagbabasa,"* **ang mag-aaral** is the simuno and **ay nagbabasa** is the panag-uri.

#### Classification by Function (Uri ayon sa Gamit)

1. **Pangungusap na Padamog (Deklaratibo)** — states a fact, opinion, or declaration. *"Masaya ang Pilipinas sa tag-ulan."* Ends with a period.
2. **Pangungusap na Pautos (Imperatibo)** — gives a command or request. *"Kumuha ka ng payong."* Ends with a period or a direct command verb.
3. **Pangungusap na Patanong** — asks a question. *"Kailan ang graduation?"* Ends with a question mark.
4. **Pangungusap na Pagkahagulgol** — expresses intense emotion (surprise, anger, joy). *"Ang gandang tumakbo ng hangin!"* Ends with an exclamation mark.

#### Classification by Structure (Uri ayon sa Estruktura)

- **Pantay o Tudling** — a compound sentence with two clauses of equal grammatical rank, joined by a comma or conjunction (*"Hindi ako pupunta, ngunit manonood ako sa telebisyon"*).
- **Walang Simuno o Pandiwa (Panudla)** — a sentence lacking an explicit subject, commonly used in prayers, pledges, and direct addresses. *"Pagpalain mo po kami."*

#### Common Exam Traps

The most frequent UPCAT trap presents a sentence like *"Nasaan ka na?"* and asks students to classify it. The answer is **Patanong**, not Padamog — despite its declarative-sounding structure, the question mark and interrogative function are definitive. Another trap separates Tudling sentences from simple Padamog sentences by inserting a comma without a conjunction; both clauses must have equal structural weight to qualify as Tudling.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

#### Mechanism: Simuno and Panag-uri in Detail

The simuno (subject) can be **payak** (simple: *"Si Maria ay umalis"*), **tambalan** (compound: *"Si Juan at si Ana ay nagtawag"*), or **lipon** (collective: *"Ang klase ay pumasok na"*). The panag-uri always contains a **pandiwa** (verb), which carries information about **aspekto** (aspect: completed vs. ongoing) and **pokus** (voice: actor, beneficiary, etc.). A common mistake is misidentifying a Panudla sentence as incomplete — it is grammatically valid because the subject is understood (*ikaw*, *tayo*). For UPCAT questions on sentence correction, ensure the pandiwa matches the simuno in number and aspect.

#### Uri ayon sa Pokus ng Pandiwa

Students must distinguish sentences by verb focus because this affects how the simuno relates to the action:

- **Pokus sa Actor (Aktor):** *"Sumulat siya ng liham."* The subject performs the action.
- **Pokus sa Layon (Target):** *"Niluto ng nanay ang pagkain."* The subject is acted upon.
- **Pokus sa Gamit:** *"Ginamit niya ang kutsara."* The subject uses something.

Understanding pokus is critical when correcting **di-makatwirang pangungusap** (illogical sentences) on the UPCAT — the test frequently inserts subject-verb mismatches based on wrong pokus selection.

#### Connecting Tudling to Logical Paraphrasing

A Tudling sentence (*"Umalis siya, ngunit nanatili ang problema"*) expresses contrast or addition between two equivalent clauses. This directly connects to UPCAT reading-comprehension questions where you must identify the author's main argument: if one clause is a fact and the other is an opinion, the sentence is structurally Tudling but functionally mixed. Spotting this distinction helps in answering *"Ano ang ipinapahayag ng may-akda?"* style questions.

#### Practice Prompts

1. Classify this sentence and identify all components: *"O, diyos ko, dinggin mo ang aming dalangin!"* — State whether it is Pagkahagulgol, Panudla, or Tudling. Name the simuno and panag-uri.
2. A sentence reads: *"Ang mga ibon ay niluwaan ng hangin"* — Is this logically sound? If not, rewrite it with correct pokus so that the meaning is preserved.
