# Blood Relations

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Blood Relations** problems give verbal clues (e.g., "A is the son of B", "C is the mother of D") and ask you to identify how two people — often mentioned only indirectly — are related. Two relation families matter: **blood relations** (parent, sibling, child, cousin, uncle/aunt, nephew/niece, grandparent, grandchild) and **affinal/marriage relations** (husband, wife, son-in-law, daughter-in-law, brother-in-law, sister-in-law, father-in-law, mother-in-law). Always **fix the gender** of every person from a clue word ("mother", "brother", "wife") before drawing the tree, then place them on a vertical **family tree** with a centre couple on the middle line. For **MDCAT** Logical Reasoning, expect 1 MCQ worth roughly 3% weight, usually a short 3–4 statement chain resolved in under 90 seconds.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core relation vocabulary

Relations split along two axes: **blood (consanguine)** versus **marriage (affinal)**, and **same generation** versus **different generation**. Memorise these anchors:

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Father / Mother | Direct parents (one level above) |
| Son / Daughter | Direct children (one level below) |
| Brother / Sister | Same-generation siblings |
| Grandparent | Two levels above |
| Uncle / Aunt | Parent's sibling |
| Nephew / Niece | Sibling's child |
| Cousin | Children of uncle/aunt |
| In-laws | Spouse + spouse's relatives + siblings' spouses |

#### Drawing the family tree

Use a vertical diagram: grandparents on the top line, parents on the middle line connected by a horizontal sibling line, and children on the bottom. **Spouses** sit at the same horizontal level (father is drawn beside mother, not below her). Each person appears only once. The diagram forces you to track gender and generation simultaneously.

#### Coded and "pointing to photograph" variants

In coded puzzles, the key is given in the question (e.g., **A + B → "A is the father of B"**, **A × B → "A is the sister of B"**, **A − B → "A is the wife of B"**). Decode every symbol *first*, then place each person on the tree. In photograph problems, the spoken sentence is always from **the speaker's viewpoint** — "He is the son of my father's only son" said by a male means the person in the photo is his brother.

#### Typical MDCAT pattern
A short 3-line statement chain (e.g., "P is the daughter of Q. R is the brother of Q. S is the husband of P.") followed by "How is S related to R?" Draw the tree, label every gender, then read the answer.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge cases that trip candidates

- **"Brother of my father" vs "father of my brother"**: the first is your uncle; the second is your father. The Hindi/Urdu phrasing trap transfers directly into English MDCAT items.
- **"Only son" vs "only child"**: "only son" allows daughters; "only child" excludes siblings entirely.
- **Maternal vs paternal uncle/aunt**: never assume. A statement like "my uncle" with no qualifier forces you to wait for a second clue, often delivered via age ordering.
- **In-laws are not blood relatives**: the sister of your spouse is your sister-in-law, *not* your sister. The son of your sibling is your nephew, never your son.
- **Spouse in a chain does not change generation**: your mother's husband and your father are the same person; treating them as two different nodes double-counts the relationship.
- **Generation gaps**: a "cousin's son" is two generations below you, making him a second cousin once removed, not a nephew. MDCAT rarely goes this deep, but Paper A aspirants for re-tests should know it.

#### Adjacent topics

Blood Relations overlaps with **Direction Sense** (combined "facing north/south" family-tree items) and **Coding-Decoding** (the coded-relation variant). Practice them together to avoid context-switch errors.

#### Common mistakes

- Forgetting to **lock gender** before drawing — leads to impossible pairings later.
- Treating "+", "−", "×" symbols as universal — they are re-defined in every question.
- Reading "no daughter" as "no children" — a household can have only sons.
- Assuming a person's spouse is opposite-gendered in coded puzzles without checking the key.

#### Worked micro-example

Statements: (1) A is the son of B. (2) C is the daughter of B. (3) D is the husband of C. Question: How is D related to A?
Tree: B (parent) → A (son), C (daughter); D marries C. From A's perspective, D is the **brother-in-law** of A. Answer: **Brother-in-law**.

#### Practice prompts

1. P is the mother of Q. R is the brother of P. S is the son of R. How is S related to Q?
2. Pointing to a boy, Riya says, "He is the son of the only brother of my mother's husband." How is the boy related to Riya?
