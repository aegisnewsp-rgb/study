# Work Energy Power

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Scan this first if the bell rings in an hour.

Work is a scalar. Its sign comes from the angle between **force** and **displacement**: positive when they line up, zero when perpendicular (centripetal force, normal reaction), negative when opposed (kinetic friction, retarding tension). The SI unit of work, kinetic energy and every form of potential energy is the **joule (J)**, dimension `[M L² T⁻²]`. Power is the time-rate of doing work, measured in **watt (W) = J s⁻¹`, dimension `[M L² T⁻³]`.

| Quantity | Formula | SI unit | Dimensions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Work by constant force | `W = F d cosθ` | J | `[M L² T⁻²]` |
| Kinetic energy | `KE = ½ m v²` | J | `[M L² T⁻²]` |
| Gravitational PE | `PE = m g h` (g ≈ 10 m s⁻² in NEET) | J | `[M L² T⁻²]` |
| Spring PE | `PE = ½ k x²` | J | `[M L² T⁻²]` |
| Average / instantaneous power | `P = W/t` and `P = F v cosθ` | W | `[M L² T⁻³]` |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook — "WFK" ladder:** **W**ork changes **K**inetic energy, **F**riction eats energy, springs store ½ k x². For unit conversion traps, remember **746 W = 1 horsepower**, not 736 or 756.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Use this as your main reference for the next two months of prep.

#### Core Principles and the Work–Energy Theorem

The **work–energy theorem** is the single most powerful statement in this chapter: net work done on a body equals its change in kinetic energy, `W_net = ΔKE`. It holds for constant forces, variable forces and curved paths alike. For a variable force, work is the line integral `W = ∫ F · dx`, which for a spring obeying Hooke's law `F = −k x` integrated from `x_i` to `x_f` gives `W = ½ k (x_f² − x_i²)`. The minus sign in Hooke's law makes spring force a **conservative** force, and conservative forces are exactly those for which a scalar potential energy `U` can be defined such that `F = −dU/dx`.

#### Conservative vs Non-Conservative Forces

A force is conservative when the work it does around a closed loop is zero, equivalently when it depends only on position (not velocity or time). Gravity, the spring force and the electrostatic Coulomb force are conservative. Kinetic friction, air drag and the viscous force on a falling raindrop are non-conservative. Total mechanical energy `E = KE + U` is conserved only when no non-conservative force does net work on the system.

#### Collisions — When KE Survives

In every collision in an isolated system, **linear momentum is conserved**. Kinetic energy is conserved only in **elastic** collisions. For a head-on elastic collision between masses `m_1` (initial velocity `v_1`) and `m_2` (initial velocity `v_2`), the post-collision velocity of the first body is

```
v_1' = ((m_1 − m_2)/(m_1 + m_2)) v_1 + (2 m_2/(m_1 + m_2)) v_2
```

The **coefficient of restitution** `e = (relative speed of separation)/(relative speed of approach)` distinguishes the cases: `e = 1` elastic, `0 < e < 1` inelastic, `e = 0` perfectly inelastic (bodies stick). A 1 kg ball hitting an identical stationary ball elastically transfers 100 % of its velocity; the same collision with putty yields `e = 0` and a common final speed of half the incoming speed.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix

| Property | Elastic collision | Inelastic collision | Perfectly inelastic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Momentum conserved | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Kinetic energy conserved | Yes | No | No |
| Coefficient of restitution `e` | `e = 1` | `0 < e < 1` | `e = 0` |
| Bodies stick after impact | No | No | Yes |
| Typical NEET marker | Two unknowns, two equations | Energy loss appears as heat/deformation | `v_f = (m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2)/(m_1 + m_2)` |

#### Power and the Velocity Relationship

Average power is total work divided by total time. Instantaneous power is `P = F · v cosθ` — direction matters only for the dot product, the output itself is a scalar. For a vehicle whose driving force balances a resistive force `F_r = b v`, the engine power delivered is `P = F_r v = b v²`; conversely, to maintain a steady speed the engine must supply exactly `P = F_drag · v`. Two recurring NEET framings: (i) the engine delivers maximum power at the start of acceleration and at maximum velocity, with a minimum near `v = v_max/2` for relations of the form `P = P_0 (v − v²/v_max)`; (ii) on a banked road or in uniform circular motion, instantaneous power is zero because `F ⊥ v` at every instant.

#### Worked Problem

A 2 kg block slides down a rough incline of angle 30° and length 5 m. The coefficient of kinetic friction is 0.2. The block starts from rest. Find the kinetic energy at the bottom of the incline and the speed there.

Given: `m = 2 kg`, `θ = 30°`, `L = 5 m`, `μ_k = 0.2`, `g = 10 m s⁻²`, `v_i = 0`. The forces along the incline are gravity component `m g sinθ` (down the slope) and friction `μ_k m g cosθ` (up the slope, opposing motion).

Net work done:

```
W_net = m g L (sinθ − μ_k cosθ)
      = 2 × 10 × 5 × (0.5 − 0.2 × 0.866)
      = 100 × (0.5 − 0.1732)
      = 100 × 0.3268
      ≈ 32.7 J
```

By the work–energy theorem, `KE_bottom = W_net ≈ 32.7 J`. Hence `v_f = √(2 KE/m) = √(2 × 32.7/2) = √32.7 ≈ 5.72 m s⁻¹`.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** friction opposes motion along the surface, so it acts up the slope when the block slides down. Students who place friction down the slope flip the sign of `μ_k cosθ` and get `W ≈ 65.4 J` and `v ≈ 8.1 m s⁻¹` — an option that is always planted as a distractor.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> For students building intuition at the limits.

#### Variable Forces, Path Dependence and Limits

The defining property that separates conservative from non-conservative forces is path independence of work: `∮ F · dl = 0`. Three mathematical limits worth carrying forward:

1. As `k → ∞` (ideal rigid spring), the work to compress by any finite `x` becomes infinite — useful when modelling inelastic collisions where bodies do not rebound.
2. For a body on a smooth hemisphere of radius `R` sliding off the top, conservation of energy gives `v² = 2 g R (1 − cosθ)`; at the bottom `v² = 2 g R`, identical to free fall through height `R`. The contact force is `N = m g (3 − 2 cosθ)`, so the block leaves the surface when `cosθ = 2/3`.
3. In simple harmonic motion, `KE` and `PE` are individually sinusoidal at twice the oscillator frequency, while their sum is exactly constant — a direct visualisation of mechanical-energy conservation.

#### Advanced Traps and Exceptions

1. Negative work by friction can become positive when the body the friction acts on moves in the friction's direction — e.g. friction from the road accelerates a walking pedestrian's foot; do not assert "friction always opposes motion of the body it acts on" without checking frame of reference.
2. Work done by the **normal force** is zero only when the contact surface is rigid and there is no displacement into or along the surface; in problems involving a block pressed against a moving wall (like a conveyor pushing a package), the normal does positive work on the package.
3. Power is **frame-dependent** even though energy is not. The power delivered by an engine to a car is `P = F · v_car` in the ground frame; in a frame moving with the car it is zero. NEET numericals assume the ground frame unless stated.
4. In collisions, momentum conservation requires an **isolated** system (no net external impulse). A ball bouncing off a moving train floor does not conserve momentum in the ground frame; always identify the system before writing the conservation equation.
5. Spring PE `½ k x²` is measured from the natural length; adding a constant to `U` does not change physics. NEET occasionally tests whether you recognise that only differences in PE matter.
6. Use `g = 10 m s⁻²` in NEET numericals unless the question explicitly states otherwise; using `9.8` can push your final answer into a wrong option.

#### Two Advanced Practice Prompts

1. A chain of length `L` and mass `M` is held vertically with its lower end just touching a scale. It is released. Find the reading on the scale as a function of the length that has landed, and explain why the reading momentarily exceeds `2 M g`.
2. A spring of constant `k` is compressed by `x` and used to launch a block of mass `m` up an incline of angle `θ` with kinetic friction coefficient `μ_k`. Derive the maximum distance the block travels up the incline, and find the condition on `x` for the block to return to its starting point.
