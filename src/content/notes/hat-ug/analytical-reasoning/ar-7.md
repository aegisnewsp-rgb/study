# Statement and Assumptions

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

A **Statement and Assumptions** item gives a short passage (statement) plus 4–5 candidate assumptions. Your job is to pick the one that is **implicit** — something the writer takes for granted but does not actually say.

- **Implicit assumption** = unstated but logically *required* for the statement to make sense.
- **Explicit assumption** = already visible inside the statement.
- **Not implied** = plausible in real life but not *necessary* given the statement.

Apply the **negation test**: if removing the assumption breaks the statement's logic or purpose, it is implicit. In HAT-UG, this question type in most keys contributes 1–2 questions (≈4% of the Analytical Reasoning block).

| Term | One-line meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Statement | The passage whose hidden belief you must expose |
| Implicit assumption | Unstated but necessary precondition |
| Negation test | If Q is false, the statement collapses — then Q is implicit |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Definition and scope

An **assumption** is a background belief the speaker treats as given. In a Statement and Assumptions item, only the option that is *logically required* — not merely *useful* or *likely* — counts as the correct implicit assumption. The statement functions like the conclusion of a mini-syllogism; the assumption is the missing premise that makes the conclusion reasonable or actionable.

#### How to test each option

Use the negation test systematically. Read each option, mentally replace it with its opposite, and ask whether the statement still makes sense. If the opposite destroys the statement's purpose, the original option is implicit. If the opposite still leaves a sensible statement, the option is not implied.

Also separate **assumption** from **inference**. An inference is what *follows* from the statement; an assumption is what must *already be true* for the statement to work. Many wrong options are inferences in disguise.

#### Typical HAT-UG question pattern

HAT-UG Analytical Reasoning in most keys presents a one-sentence advisory or warning followed by four or five candidate assumptions. One is implicit, the rest are explicit, not implied, or absurd. Each item carries equal marks; speed matters because the broader AR section covers roughly 4% of the test.

| Question element | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| Statement type (advice / warning / request) | The hidden **motive** behind the action |
| Candidate assumption | Does the statement collapse if it is false? |
| Wrong-option type | Inference, mere justification, or generic truth |
| Correct answer | Logically indispensable, not merely desirable |

- Always scan for the speaker's **intent** first — it in many papers reveals the assumption.
- Beware options that *strengthen* the statement; strengthening is not presupposing.
- Discard assumptions that are ** true; the test demands *necessity*.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge cases and the "desirable but not necessary" trap

Examiners commonly insert options that *sound* reasonable in everyday life but are not strictly required by the statement. Example: a statement urges citizens to vote. A desirable but not necessary assumption can be "voting is a civic duty" — true in many cultures, yet the statement can still function without it. The correct implicit assumption would be "eligible citizens currently have the option to vote," because removing that makes the appeal meaningless.

#### Common mistakes

- Selecting options that only *support* the statement rather than *presuppose* it.
- Marking assumptions that are ** true as implicit; the test needs *necessary* truth.
- Ignoring the speaker's motive behind warnings, requests, and recommendations.
- Confusing inference (what follows) with assumption (what must already hold).

#### Practice prompts

1. Statement: "The company issued a warning against sharing login credentials." Which assumption is implicit — that credentials exist, that employees currently share them, that sharing causes harm, or that warnings reduce risk?
2. Statement: "Please complete the feedback form before leaving." Identify the implicit assumption, then explain why "feedback improves service" is not the correct answer.

#### Exam strategy for HAT-UG

| Strategy | Why it works |
| --- | --- |
| Apply negation test to every option | Filters out non-essential assumptions |
| Identify motive of the statement first | Surfaces the hidden precondition |
| Watch for "always/never/some" qualifiers | Reveals over-generalised wrong options |
| Time-box ~60 seconds per AR item | Protects time for higher-weight sections |
