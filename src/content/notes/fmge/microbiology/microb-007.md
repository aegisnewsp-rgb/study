# Antimicrobial Chemotherapy

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your FMGE screening examination.

Antimicrobial chemotherapy exploits biochemical and structural differences between bacterial pathogens and human host cells (selective toxicity). Bacterial targets include peptidoglycan cell walls, 70S ribosomes (composed of 30S and 50S subunits), bacterial topoisomerases, and de novo folic acid biosynthetic enzymes.

Antimicrobial agents are broadly divided into **bactericidal** agents (which reduce viable bacterial counts by $\ge 99.9\%$ or $3\log_{10}$ within 18 to 24 hours) and **bacteriostatic** agents (which arrest bacterial replication, relying on host cellular immunity to clear the infection).

#### Core Classification and Functional Profiling of Antimicrobial Agents

| Antibacterial Class | Representative Agents | Primary Molecular Target | Bactericidal vs Bacteriostatic | Primary Clinical Applications |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Penicillins & Cephalosporins** | Amoxicillin, Ceftriaxone, Cefepime | Penicillin-Binding Proteins (transpeptidases) | Bactericidal | Streptococcal pharyngitis, meningitis, pneumonia, gonorrhea |
| **Carbapenems & Monobactams** | Meropenem, Imipenem-Cilastatin, Aztreonam | PBPs (PBP-2 and PBP-3); highly stable against beta-lactamases | Bactericidal | Multidrug-resistant Gram-negative sepsis, ESBL infections; Aztreonam for penicillin-allergic patients |
| **Glycopeptides & Lipopeptides** | Vancomycin, Teicoplanin, Daptomycin | D-Ala-D-Ala terminus of peptidoglycan (Vancomycin); membrane depolarization (Daptomycin) | Bactericidal | Methicillin-Resistant *Staphylococcus aureus* (MRSA), *Enterococcus faecalis* |
| **Aminoglycosides** | Gentamicin, Amikacin, Tobramycin | 16S rRNA of 30S ribosomal subunit (causes codon misreading) | Bactericidal (concentration-dependent) | Severe aerobic Gram-negative bacilli infections, synergy in enterococcal endocarditis |
| **Tetracyclines & Glycylcyclines** | Doxycycline, Minocycline, Tigecycline | 30S ribosomal subunit (blocks aminoacyl-tRNA binding at A-site) | Bacteriostatic | Rickettsial diseases (Scrub typhus), Chlamydia, Cholera, Mycoplasma pneumonia |
| **Macrolides & Lincosamides** | Azithromycin, Clarithromycin, Clindamycin | 23S rRNA of 50S ribosomal subunit (blocks peptide translocation) | Bacteriostatic | Community-acquired pneumonia, atypical pathogens, anaerobic soft-tissue infections |
| **Fluoroquinolones** | Ciprofloxacin, Levofloxacin, Moxifloxacin | DNA gyrase (topoisomerase II) and Topoisomerase IV | Bactericidal | Urinary tract infections, typhoid fever (Salmonella), pseudomonal infections |
| **Folate Antagonists** | Sulfamethoxazole + Trimethoprim (Cotrimoxazole) | Dihydropteroate synthase and Dihydrofolate reductase (sequential inhibition) | Bactericidal in combination (Bacteriostatic individually) | *Pneumocystis jirovecii* pneumonia, *Nocardia*, Stenotrophomonas, uncomplicated cystitis |

⚡ **High-Yield FMGE Rule:** Bactericidal antimicrobials are strictly mandatory in four life-threatening clinical states where host phagocytic defense is compromised or anatomically shielded: infective endocarditis, bacterial meningitis, febrile neutropenia, and severe osteomyelitis.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Comprehensive study guide for the National Board of Examinations screening test.

#### 1. Detailed Mechanisms of Action by Cellular Target

1. **Inhibition of Peptidoglycan Cell Wall Synthesis:**
   - **Beta-Lactam Antibiotics:** Possess a four-membered beta-lactam ring resembling the D-alanyl-D-alanine moiety. They bind to membrane-associated Penicillin-Binding Proteins (PBPs), irreversibly inactivating the transpeptidase enzyme responsible for cross-linking the peptidoglycan glycan strands. This triggers bacterial autolysins, leading to osmotic lysis.
   - **Glycopeptides (Vancomycin, Teicoplanin):** Large hydrophilic molecules that bind hydrogen atoms on the terminal D-Ala-D-Ala dipeptide of cell wall pentapeptide precursors. This blocks transglycosylase (chain elongation) and transpeptidase (cross-linking). Because vancomycin cannot pass through outer membrane porins, it possesses zero activity against Gram-negative bacteria.
   - **Fosfomycin:** Structurally analogizes phosphoenolpyruvate, irreversibly inactivating the enolpyruvate transferase enzyme (MurA). This blocks the condensation of uridine diphosphate-N-acetylglucosamine with phosphoenolpyruvate, the first committed step in bacterial peptidoglycan biosynthesis.
2. **Inhibition of Ribosomal Protein Synthesis:**
   - **30S Subunit Target:**
     - *Aminoglycosides:* Actively transported into bacteria via an oxygen-dependent, electron transport-mediated active process (hence totally ineffective against obligate anaerobes like *Bacteroides*). They bind the 30S subunit, inducing mRNA misreading and abnormal cell membrane insertion, resulting in rapid bactericidal death.
     - *Tetracyclines:* Enter via passive diffusion and active transport. Reversibly bind to the 30S subunit, sterically obstructing the arrival of charged aminoacyl-tRNA to the ribosomal acceptor (A) site.
   - **50S Subunit Target:**
     - *Macrolides (Azithromycin, Clarithromycin):* Reversibly bind domain V of 23S rRNA in the 50S subunit, inhibiting peptidyltransferase-mediated translocation and causing premature dissociation of peptidyl-tRNA.
     - *Chloramphenicol:* Binds the peptidyl transferase center of 50S subunit, preventing peptide bond synthesis. Can inhibit mammalian mitochondrial 70S ribosomes, causing bone marrow aplasia.
     - *Linezolid (Oxazolidinones):* Binds the P-site of the 50S subunit, preventing the formation of the functional 70S initiation complex. Highly effective against Vancomycin-Resistant Enterococci (VRE) and MRSA.
3. **Inhibition of Nucleic Acid Synthesis:**
   - **DNA Gyrase and Topoisomerase IV Blockade:** Fluoroquinolones stabilize broken DNA double strands during replication, converting essential topoisomerases into cellular toxins that trigger apoptotic-like SOS responses.
   - **RNA Polymerase Blockade:** Rifampicin binds the $\beta$-subunit of bacterial DNA-dependent RNA polymerase (*rpoB* gene), physically blocking the elongation of nascent RNA transcripts.

#### 2. Molecular Mechanisms of Bacterial Resistance

| Biochemical Resistance Mechanism | Genetic Basis | Target Antimicrobial Class | Classic Clinical Pathogens |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Beta-Lactamase Hydrolysis (ESBLs: CTX-M, TEM, SHV)** | Plasmid-encoded beta-lactamases hydrolyzing penicillins and cephalosporins | Cephalosporins, monobactams, penicillins | *Escherichia coli*, *Klebsiella pneumoniae* |
| **Carbapenemase Production (NDM-1, KPC, OXA-48)** | Metallo-beta-lactamases (NDM requires zinc) or serine carbapenemases | Carbapenems (Meropenem, Imipenem) | Multidrug-resistant *Klebsiella*, *Acinetobacter baumannii* |
| **Altered Penicillin-Binding Protein (PBP-2a)** | Chromosomal acquisition of *mecA* or *mecC* gene on SCCmec element | All beta-lactams (except anti-MRSA 5th-gen cephalosporins ceftaroline) | Methicillin-Resistant *Staphylococcus aureus* (MRSA) |
| **Target Dipeptide Alteration (D-Ala-D-Lac)** | Transposon-mediated acquisition of *vanA* or *vanB* gene cluster | Glycopeptides (Vancomycin, Teicoplanin) | Vancomycin-Resistant Enterococci (*E. faecium*, *E. faecalis*) |
| **Active Efflux Pump Overexpression (MexAB-OprM, NorA)** | Upregulation of ATP-binding cassette or RND efflux pumps | Fluoroquinolones, Tetracyclines, Chloramphenicol | *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, *Staphylococcus aureus* |
| **Methylation of 23S Ribosomal RNA (*erm* genes)** | Plasmid-mediated ribosomal methylase (inducible MLSb resistance) | Macrolides, Lincosamides (Clindamycin), Streptogramin B | *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Staphylococcus aureus* (positive D-test) |

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Quantitative pharmacokinetics/pharmacodynamics (PK/PD), consensus guidelines, and clinical cases.

#### 1. Pharmacokinetic / Pharmacodynamic (PK/PD) Indices

Antimicrobial efficacy in vivo correlates with specific quantitative PK/PD parameters determined by the Minimum Inhibitory Concentration (MIC):

1. **Time-Dependent Killing ($T > \text{MIC}$):**
   - Applies to: Beta-lactams (Penicillins, Cephalosporins, Carbapenems).
   - The bactericidal kill rate reaches a maximum at 4 to 5 times the MIC; escalating concentrations higher yields minimal additional killing.
   - Efficacy requires the free (unbound) drug concentration to remain above the MIC for at least 40% to 50% of the dosing interval for penicillins and 60% to 70% for cephalosporins.
   - Dosing strategy: Frequent intermittent dosing, prolonged 3- to 4-hour extended infusions, or continuous IV infusions.
2. **Concentration-Dependent Killing ($C_{\max}/\text{MIC}$):**
   - Applies to: Aminoglycosides, Daptomycin.
   - The higher the peak serum concentration relative to the organism's MIC, the faster and more extensive the bacterial eradication.
   - Requires a peak-to-MIC ratio of $C_{\max}/\text{MIC} \ge 8\text{ to }10$.
   - Associated with a prolonged Post-Antibiotic Effect (PAE), where bacterial growth remains suppressed for hours after serum drug concentrations fall below the MIC.
   - Dosing strategy: Once-daily high-dose consolidation (e.g., Gentamicin 5 to 7 mg/kg single daily dose).
3. **Exposure-Dependent Killing ($\text{AUC}_{24}/\text{MIC}$):**
   - Applies to: Vancomycin, Fluoroquinolones, Tigecycline, Linezolid.
   - Efficacy depends on total cumulative 24-hour drug exposure relative to the MIC.
   - Target for Vancomycin in serious MRSA infection: $\text{AUC}_{24}/\text{MIC} = 400\text{ to }600$.

#### 2. Quantitative PK/PD Case Calculation

**Clinical Case Application:**  
A 58-year-old intensive care patient weighing 70 kg has confirmed *Staphylococcus aureus* bacteremia with a vancomycin MIC of $1.0\text{ mg/L}$.

Vancomycin therapeutic drug monitoring is conducted. Serum concentrations measured at steady state during an every-12-hour regimen ($1,250\text{ mg}$ IV every 12 hours) yield:
- Peak concentration ($C_{\text{peak}}$): $36.0\text{ mg/L}$
- Trough concentration ($C_{\text{trough}}$): $14.0\text{ mg/L}$
- Area under the 12-hour concentration-time curve ($\text{AUC}_{12}$): $260\text{ mg}\cdot\text{h/L}$

Calculate the total 24-hour area under the curve ($\text{AUC}_{24}$) and determine if the PK/PD index clears the target:

$$\text{AUC}_{24} = 2 \times \text{AUC}_{12} = 2 \times 260\text{ mg}\cdot\text{h/L} = 520\text{ mg}\cdot\text{h/L}$$

Calculate the $\text{AUC}_{24}/\text{MIC}$ ratio:

$$\frac{\text{AUC}_{24}}{\text{MIC}} = \frac{520\text{ mg}\cdot\text{h/L}}{1.0\text{ mg/L}} = 520$$

**Clinical Interpretation:**  
The calculated $\text{AUC}_{24}/\text{MIC}$ ratio of 520 falls within the recommended therapeutic target range of 400 to 600. Ratios below 400 correlate with clinical treatment failure and emergence of vancomycin-intermediate *S. aureus* (VISA), whereas values exceeding 600 markedly elevate the risk of acute nephrotoxicity without improving clinical cure rates. The regimen is maintained with regular serum creatinine and trough monitoring.

#### 3. High-Frequency FMGE Clinical Vignettes

1. **The D-Zone Test for Inducible Clindamycin Resistance:** An erythromycin-resistant, clindamycin-susceptible isolate of *Staphylococcus aureus* undergoes disk diffusion testing. Placing erythromycin and clindamycin disks 15 mm apart reveals a blunting of the clindamycin inhibition zone adjacent to the erythromycin disk, forming the shape of the letter 'D'. This indicates the presence of an inducible erythromycin ribosome methylase (*erm*) gene. Clindamycin therapy is contraindicated in this patient because exposure to clindamycin in vivo triggers mutation to constitutive resistance, resulting in clinical failure.
2. **Red Man Syndrome vs. Anaphylaxis:** Rapid intravenous infusion of vancomycin (< 60 minutes) directly stimulates mast cell degranulation via MRGPRX2 receptor activation, causing histamine release resulting in flushing, pruritus, and erythematous rash over the face, neck, and upper torso. This is an adverse non-IgE-mediated pseudoallergic infusion reaction. Treatment involves slowing the infusion rate to over 120 minutes and pre-treating with oral antihistamines. It does not constitute an allergic contraindication to future vancomycin therapy.
