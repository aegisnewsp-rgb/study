# A Complete Ecosystem Includes the Biotic Community Producers

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Living Things and Their Environment** is the UPCAT Science topic that examines how organisms interact with each other and with non-living components of their surroundings. An **ecosystem** combines **biotic factors** (all living organisms — plants, animals, microbes) and **abiotic factors** (light, temperature, water, soil, air). Energy enters through producers and moves upward, with only about **10% transferred between trophic levels** (Lindeman's 10% Rule), while the remaining ~90% is lost as heat.

- **Population density** = number of individuals ÷ area sampled (ind/km² or ind/m²).
- **Population growth rate** = (birth rate − death rate) + (immigration − emigration), expressed per 1000 individuals per year.
- Populations cannot grow forever; they level off at the **carrying capacity (K)** set by limiting resources.
- Food **webs** (not single chains) better capture the interconnected feeding relationships in a real community.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Ecosystem Structure
A complete ecosystem includes the biotic community (producers, consumers, decomposers) plus the abiotic environment. Decomposers such as fungi and bacteria close the nutrient loop by breaking down organic waste into reusable minerals. Misclassifying decomposers as "abiotic" or excluding them from the food web is a frequent UPCAT trap.

#### Energy Flow and the 10% Rule
Sunlight is captured by **autotrophs (producers)** during photosynthesis. Herbivores (**primary consumers**) eat producers; carnivores (**secondary and tertiary consumers**) eat other consumers. Lindeman's 10% Rule states that roughly 10% of the energy stored at one trophic level is converted to biomass at the next, which is why food chains rarely exceed 4–5 levels and why top predators are scarce.

| Concept | Key point |
| --- | --- |
| Producer | Autotroph that makes its own food via photosynthesis |
| Primary consumer | Herbivore feeding directly on producers |
| Decomposer | Bacteria/fungi recycling dead matter into nutrients |
| 10% Rule | Only ~10% of energy passes to the next trophic level |

#### Population Characteristics and Dynamics
Population **size** is the raw count, while **density** requires dividing by area. Three distribution patterns exist: **clumped** (most common, e.g., herds), **uniform** (evenly spaced, e.g., territorial birds), and **random** (rare, requires uniform resources). Growth follows a **J-shaped curve** when resources are unlimited, but real populations follow an **S-shaped logistic curve** that plateaus at carrying capacity **K**.

#### Human Impact and Conservation
Deforestation, pollution, overfishing, and fossil-fuel combustion reduce **biodiversity** and disrupt ecosystem balance. UPCAT items often pair a human activity with the conservation method that counters it.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Mechanism Depth
**Ecological succession** describes the predictable species turnover after disturbance. **Primary succession** begins on bare rock or lava with pioneer species such as lichens, then progresses to mosses, grasses, shrubs, and finally a climax community — this can take centuries. **Secondary succession** skips the pioneer stage because soil already exists, so recovery is faster (years to decades), as seen in abandoned farmland or areas after Typhoon Odette.

Population growth rate calculations in UPCAT sometimes include migration. Remember: **growth rate = (births − deaths) + (immigrants − emigrants)**, all per 1000 individuals per year. A negative value signals decline, which is the demographic reality behind many Philippine endangered-species programs.

#### Common Mistakes to Avoid
- Confusing **percent cover** (used for plants that don't occur as discrete individuals, like grass) with **population density** (used for countable animals).
- Forgetting that energy *decreases* by ~90% per level, not 50% — many students guess 50% from a vague memory of "half".
- Treating a food chain as more accurate than a food web; in nature, most consumers eat several species.

| Term | Units / Formula |
| --- | --- |
| Population density | individuals ÷ area (ind/m² or ind/km²) |
| Birth rate | (births ÷ population) × 1000 per year |
| Percent cover | (area covered ÷ total area) × 100 % |
| Carrying capacity (K) | Maximum sustainable population size |

> **Exam tip:** UPCAT Science allots only ~3% of items to this topic — usually 1–2 questions. Memorize the 10% Rule, the biotic/abiotic distinction, and the logistic S-curve to bank these quick points.

**Practice prompts:** (1) A grassland loses 75% of its plant biomass to a fire. Identify the succession type and the first colonizers. (2) If a producer community stores 20,000 kcal/m², how much reaches the tertiary consumers? (Answer: ~20 kcal/m².)
