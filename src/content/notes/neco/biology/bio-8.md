# Excretory Systems and Osmoregulation

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Excretion** removes nitrogenous metabolic waste from body cells, while **osmoregulation** keeps water and salt balance stable. The mammalian kidney contains roughly 1 million **nephrons**, each performing ultrafiltration, selective reabsorption, tubular secretion and concentration of urine.

- **Ammonia** — excreted by aquatic animals; highly toxic, requires large water volume.
- **Urea** — excreted by mammals; produced via the **ornithine (urea) cycle** in the liver.
- **Uric acid** — excreted by birds, reptiles, insects; minimally watery, paste-like.
- **ADH** increases water reabsorption in the collecting duct; **aldosterone** increases Na⁺ (and water) reabsorption in the distal tubule.

| Waste type | Toxicity | Water needed | Example organism |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ammonia | Very high | Large | Tilapia (fish) |
| Urea | Moderate | Moderate | Human |
| Uric acid | Low | Minimal | Domestic fowl |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Urine formation in the nephron

A nephron has a renal corpuscle (glomerulus + Bowman's capsule), a proximal convoluted tubule, loop of Henle, distal convoluted tubule and collecting duct. Blood enters the glomerulus under high pressure; **ultrafiltration** forces water, glucose, urea, Na⁺ and amino acids into the capsule, leaving proteins and cells behind.

**Net filtration pressure** drives this step:

> NFP = P_GC − P_BS − π_GC
> where P_GC = glomerular hydrostatic pressure (≈ 55 mmHg), P_BS = Bowman's capsule pressure (≈ 15 mmHg), π_GC = plasma oncotic pressure (≈ 30 mmHg). NFP ≈ 10 mmHg.

In the proximal tubule, roughly 99% of glucose, amino acids and useful ions are **selectively reabsorbed** into peritubular capillaries. **Tubular secretion** in the distal tubule adds H⁺, K⁺ and drugs into the filtrate.

#### Hormonal control of water and salt balance

When the body dehydrates, osmoreceptors in the hypothalamus trigger **antidiuretic hormone (ADH/vasopressin)** release from the posterior pituitary. ADH inserts **aquaporin-2 channels** into the collecting duct, letting water leave the filtrate and re-enter the hyperosmotic medulla. **Aldosterone** from the adrenal cortex increases Na⁺ reabsorption in the distal tubule, pulling water with it.

- Dehydration → raised blood osmolality → ADH release → concentrated urine.
- High blood pressure/volume → suppressed ADH → dilute urine.
- Low Na⁺ → aldosterone release → more Na⁺ retained.

#### Liver's role and accessory organs

The liver **deaminates** excess amino acids, releasing ammonia that the **ornithine cycle** converts to urea. Alongside the kidneys, the lungs (CO₂, water vapour) and skin (sweat) act as **accessory excretory organs**.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Comparative excretory systems

Different phyla solve nitrogenous-waste disposal with distinct structures, and the choice of waste is tightly linked to habitat water availability.

| Organism | Excretory structure | Main waste | Habitat clue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Amoeba | Contractile vacuole | Ammonia | Freshwater — pumps out excess water |
| Flatworm | Flame cell (protonephridium) | Ammonia | Aquatic |
| Earthworm | Nephridium | Urea | Moist soil |
| Insect | Malpighian tubule | Uric acid | Terrestrial — conserves water |
| Fish (most) | Kidney | Ammonia | Aquatic |
| Mammal | Nephron | Urea | Terrestrial, water-moderate |

Sharks and a few bony fish are an exception: they **retain urea** in the blood (alongside trimethylamine N-oxide) to raise plasma osmolality and match seawater, reducing water loss.

#### Edge cases and common mistakes

- **Excretion ≠ egestion.** Faeces removal is egestion, not excretion, because it never entered body cells.
- The **Malpighian body** is only the glomerulus + Bowman's capsule, not the whole nephron.
- **ADH does not "add" water to blood** — it only changes membrane permeability; water moves passively down the medullary osmotic gradient built by the **counter-current multiplier** in the loop of Henle (cortex ≈ 300 mOsm/L → medulla ≈ 1200 mOsm/L).
- **Negative feedback loop:** low blood volume/pressure → juxtaglomerular cells release **renin** → angiotensin II → ADH and aldosterone surge → restored volume.

#### Worked micro-example

A dehydrated student has plasma osmolality of 305 mOsm/kg. Osmoreceptors fire, ADH rises, aquaporin-2 channels open in the collecting duct, and water exits into the 1200 mOsm/L medullary interstitium. Urine output falls to ~0.5 L/day, concentrating waste; plasma osmolality returns to ~290 mOsm/kg.
