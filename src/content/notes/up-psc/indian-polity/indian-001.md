# Preamble and Salient Features of Constitution

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

- The **Preamble** is the introductory declaration of the Constitution of India, opening with the words **"We, the people of India"**, declaring the nation a **Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic** committed to securing **Justice (social, economic, political)**, **Liberty**, **Equality**, and **Fraternity**.
- Drafted by **Jawaharlal Nehru** in the form of his **Objective Resolution** (moved 13 December 1946, adopted 22 January 1947), and adopted on **26 November 1949**; it came into force on **26 January 1950**.
- The words **"Socialist"**, **"Secular"** and **"Integrity"** were **inserted by the 42nd Amendment, 1976** — they are not original features.
- The Preamble is **non-justiciable** (LIC of India case, 1995) but is a part of the **basic structure** (Kesavananda Bharati, 1973).
- "**Republic**" means the **President** (head of state) is an **elected**, not hereditary, office.
- For UPPSC PCS Polity (3% weightage), one question is consistently asked on the **amendment history** of the Preamble and the **Berubari / Kesavananda** distinction.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Origin and Drafting
The Preamble emerged from the **Objective Resolution** moved by **Jawaharlal Nehru** in the **Constituent Assembly** on **13 December 1946** and adopted on **22 January 1947**. Although the **Drafting Committee** under **B.R. Ambedkar** prepared the bulk of the Constitution, the Preamble itself was Nehru's handiwork — a distinction UPPSC frequently tests. The Constituent Assembly adopted the Constitution on **26 November 1949**; the Preamble and the bulk of provisions came into force on **26 January 1950**, deliberately chosen to commemorate the **Purna Swaraj** declaration of 1930.

#### Salient Features
- **Sovereignty**: India possesses supreme internal authority and is free from any external dominion status — a reaffirmation after the **India Independence Act, 1947**.
- **Socialist** and **Secular**: These are **additions by the 42nd Amendment, 1976**. "Socialist" implies a mixed economy; "Secular" means the state has no official religion.
- **Democratic Republic**: Government rests on the will of the people, and the **President is elected** (Article 54) rather than being a hereditary monarch.
- **Justice, Liberty, Equality, Fraternity**: The four-fold objectives covering the **socialist, economic, political** dimensions of justice, freedoms of thought/expression/belief/cult, equality of status and opportunity, and the assurance of individual **dignity** with national **unity and integrity**.

#### Legal Status
In **Berubari Union (1960)**, the Supreme Court held the Preamble is **not a part of the Constitution**. This position was reversed in **Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973)**, where the 13-judge Bench ruled the Preamble **is a part of the Constitution and a component of the basic structure**, which Parliament cannot abridge even under Article 368. In the **LIC of India case (1995)**, the Court further clarified that the Preamble is **non-justiciable** — its values are not directly enforceable in court.

#### Amendability
Article 368 enables Parliament to amend the Preamble, exercised only once — the **42nd Amendment, 1976** — without altering its "basic features" test.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Adjudication History
The Preamble's legal status has shifted across four decades of case law. **Berubari Union No. 1 (1960)** treated it as a mere political declaration, not law. **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)** overruled this and embedded the Preamble within the **basic structure doctrine**, holding it amendable in form but unalterable in identity — Justice **H.R. Khanna**'s opinion is the operative one. **LIC of India v. Consumer Education and Research Centre (1995)** and **S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994)** together established that while courts cannot strike down legislation solely for violating Preamble ideals, those ideals (especially **secularism, socialism, democracy**) function as interpretive guides for **judicial review** and **federalism disputes**.

#### Common Mistakes
- Attributing the Preamble's drafting to the **Drafting Committee** — wrong; Nehru drafted it.
- Quoting **26 January 1950** as the date of **adoption** — the adoption date is **26 November 1949**.
- Treating **"Socialist"** and **"Secular"** as original — they were inserted by the **42nd Amendment, 1976**, and "**Integrity**" was added to "Unity" in the same amendment.
- Assuming the Preamble is **justiciable** — it is non-justiciable but doctrinally protected as basic structure.

#### Connections to Adjacent Topics
- Links directly to **Fundamental Rights (Part III)** and **Directive Principles (Part IV)**, which operationalise Justice, Equality and Liberty.
- Connects to **Citizenship (Part II)**, **Amendment Procedure (Article 368)**, and **Basic Structure Doctrine**.
- The "**We, the people**" opening sources the Constitution's authority in popular sovereignty — the same principle invoked in **federal disputes** under Article 356 (Bommai).
