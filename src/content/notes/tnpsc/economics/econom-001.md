# Introduction to Economics

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> One-screen summary for the night before Prelims — read this twice.

Economics studies how societies ration scarce resources against unlimited wants. That scarcity is the entire reason the subject exists; without it, no choice, no trade-off, no price.

#### The three definitions TNPSC repeats:

| Economist | Definition | Exam Cue |
|---|---|---|
| Adam Smith (1776) | "Study of wealth" | Classical / wealth definition |
| Alfred Marshall (1890) | "Study of mankind in the ordinary business of life" | Welfare / material definition |
| Lionel Robbins (1932) | "Science which studies human behaviour as a relationship between ends and scarce means with alternative uses" | Scarcity definition (most-cited) |

**Central problems every economy must answer:** What to produce, How to produce, For whom to produce.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **WHF** = **W**hat, **H**ow, **F**or whom — the three questions every textbook chapter keeps circling back to. Pair it with **Scarcity → Choice → Opportunity Cost** (S-C-O) to lock the basic logic chain in 5 seconds.

One formula worth memorising: `Opportunity Cost = (units of next-best alternative given up) / (units of chosen good gained)`. It is a ratio, no units.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Build working understanding of definitions, branches, systems, and the national-income arithmetic TNPSC loves.

#### Branches and Methodologies

Economics splits into **Microeconomics** (individual households, firms, single markets) and **Macroeconomics** (whole-economy aggregates like GDP, inflation, unemployment). A second cut separates **Positive Economics** ("what is", fact-based, testable) from **Normative Economics** ("what ought to be", value-laden, prescriptive). TNPSC routinely frames options where a normative statement is camouflaged as a positive one.

#### Comparison Matrix — Easy-to-Mix Pairs

| Pair | Distinction | Common Confusion |
|---|---|---|
| Micro vs Macro | Unit of analysis: single agent vs aggregate | Calling GDP "micro" or calling a firm's cost curve "macro" |
| Positive vs Normative | Fact vs value judgement | Marking "Rent control is unfair" as positive |
| GDP at MP vs GDP at FC | MP includes Net Indirect Taxes (NIT); FC excludes it | Treating the two as equal |
| Capitalist vs Mixed | Pure private ownership vs public + private coexistence | Calling India capitalist |
| Liberalisation vs Globalisation | Removing controls/licences (1991) vs integrating with world markets | Using the words interchangeably |
| GDP vs GNP | GDP = produced inside borders; GNP = produced by residents anywhere | Adding net factor income from abroad |
| WPI vs CPI | Wholesale vs Consumer price index | Swapping the baskets |

#### Central Problems and the PPC

Every economy, regardless of ideology, must solve **What, How, For whom**, plus a growth problem over time. The **Production Possibility Curve (PPC)** is the textbook device: any point on the curve is productively efficient, points inside show unemployment of resources, points outside are currently unattainable. Movement along the curve illustrates opportunity cost — producing more guns means fewer butter, and the slope of the PPC at any point is the **Marginal Rate of Transformation (MRT)**.

#### Factors of Production and Their Remuneration

| Factor | Remuneration | Paid by |
|---|---|---|
| Land | Rent | Landlord / owner of natural resources |
| Labour | Wages | Employer |
| Capital | Interest | Financial institutions, bondholders |
| Entrepreneurship | Profit | The firm, after all other costs |

#### Indian Economy — Identifying Features

India is classified as a **developing / mixed economy**. Public sector dominates strategic sectors (defence, railways, atomic energy); private sector drives consumer goods, IT services, and most manufacturing since 1991. The **Planning Commission** (1950–2014) drafted Five-Year Plans; **NITI Aayog** replaced it as a policy think-tank without the fund-allocation power.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

#### Question (TNPSC Group 1 mains style):
"The national income of a country, computed using the expenditure method, is the sum of household consumption (₹60,000 cr), gross investment (₹20,000 cr), government spending (₹15,000 cr), and net exports. Exports equal ₹10,000 cr and imports equal ₹12,000 cr. Compute GDP at market price."

#### Resolution, step by step:

1. Net Exports = Exports − Imports = 10,000 − 12,000 = **−₹2,000 cr**.
2. Apply the expenditure identity: GDP = C + I + G + (X − M).
3. Substitute: GDP = 60,000 + 20,000 + 15,000 + (−2,000) = **₹93,000 cr**.

Units throughout are ₹ crore, i.e. monetary units; the underlying dimension reduces to [M L² T⁻²] only in a dimensional-analysis sense, since national-income aggregates are bookkeeping totals in money.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Many aspirants add exports and imports as separate positive terms and never apply the **net** sign — that single mistake flips the answer by ₹22,000 cr. Also, expenditure-method GDP is **at market price**; converting to **factor cost** requires subtracting **Net Indirect Taxes (NIT = indirect taxes − subsidies)**.

> 📌 **Formula Check:** GDP = C + I + G + (X − M), where C, I, G, X, M are all in monetary units (₹) for the same accounting period (usually one financial year). Do not mix current and constant prices.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Edge cases, definition traps, and adjacent-topic links for three-month-plus prep.

#### Definition Genealogy That TNPSC Tests

| Year | Thinker | Frame | One-line tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1776 | Adam Smith | Wealth | Classical founder |
| 1890 | Marshall | Material welfare | Marginalist revolution |
| 1932 | Robbins | Scarcity + choice | Modern definition |
| 1948 | Samuelson | Welfare/society-wide | Neoclassical synthesis |

A statement such as "Economics is the study of wealth" is **Adam Smith**, not Robbins. Robbins shifted the subject away from wealth toward **choice under scarcity** — that re-framing is what makes him the answer for any TNPSC question containing the phrase "scarce means with alternative uses".

#### Inflation Measurement — Two Indexes, Two Baskets

- **WPI (Wholesale Price Index)** tracks prices at the wholesale / bulk stage; published by the Office of the Economic Adviser, DPIIT. Used historically as India's headline inflation gauge.
- **CPI (Consumer Price Index)** tracks retail prices paid by households; CPI-Combined is what the RBI now references for policy rates.

A common extended-trap question: "Which index is published by RBI?" — RBI does not publish either; it **uses** CPI. The Ministry of Commerce publishes WPI; the Ministry of Statistics (MoSPI) publishes CPI.

#### LPG 1991 — Three Distinct Reforms

| Reform | Meaning | One-policy marker |
|---|---|---|
| Liberalisation | Removal of industrial licensing, end of MRTP monopoly controls | Delicensing of industries |
| Privatisation | Disinvestment and reduction of public-sector monopoly | Disinvestment of PSUs |
| Globalisation | Integration with world markets, FDI inflows | Foreign-exchange decontrol, FDI welcome |

These three are **bundled in 1991** under the Narasimham–Rao–Manmohan reforms, but they are conceptually separate. A question asking which reform specifically "permitted automatic FDI approval up to 51% in high-priority industries" tests **Liberalisation + Globalisation together**, not Privatisation.

#### Adjacent-Topic Links

- **Indian Economy →** Move next to Planning (Five-Year Plans, NITI Aayog role).
- **Macro aggregates →** Pairs directly with Money and Banking (RBI, repo rate) and Fiscal Policy (tax–expenditure stance of the Union Budget).

#### Two Advanced Practice Prompts

1. A country records GDP (MP) of ₹200 lakh cr and Net Indirect Taxes of ₹20 lakh cr. Compute GDP at factor cost and explain why the two differ. *(Answer: ₹180 lakh cr; the gap is NIT, the indirect-tax wedge between what buyers pay and what producers receive.)*
2. State whether "A minimum wage of ₹18,000 should be mandated nationally" is a positive or normative statement, and rewrite it as one of the opposite type. *(Original is normative; a positive rewrite: "Five states currently mandate a minimum wage above ₹18,000.")*

#### Common Extended-Tier Mistakes

1. Quoting **per capita income** as National Income ÷ Population without naming **NNP at FC** (the UN-specified denominator for India) — a one-symbol omission that costs the mark.
2. Saying India is a **socialist** economy because the Constitution originally used the word — the 1991 reforms and the formal post-1991 policy stance classify India as **mixed**, not socialist.
3. Treating **NITI Aayog** as a successor with identical powers to the Planning Commission — NITI Aayog is an advisory body; it does **not** allocate plan funds.
