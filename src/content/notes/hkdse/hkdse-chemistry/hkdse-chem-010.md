# X. Chemical Equilibrium — HKDSE Chemistry Notes

This topic covers Topic X of the Compulsory Part of HKDSE Chemistry. It addresses reversible reactions and dynamic equilibrium; the equilibrium constant Kc; Le Chatelier's principle (concentration, pressure, temperature changes); and industrial processes (e.g. the Haber process, the Contact process).

> Verify the live HKDSE Chemistry Assessment Framework on https://www.hkeaa.edu.hk/ before planning revision.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Reversible reaction**: a reaction that can proceed in both forward and reverse directions. Denoted by ⇌.
- **Dynamic equilibrium**: the state in which the forward and reverse reaction rates are equal, so concentrations remain constant (but reactions are still occurring in both directions).
- **Equilibrium constant Kc**: for aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD, Kc = [C]^c[D]^d / [A]^a[B]^b. Kc is constant at a given temperature.
- **Le Chatelier's principle**: if a system at equilibrium is disturbed, the position of equilibrium shifts to counteract the disturbance.
- **Effect of concentration**: adding reactant shifts equilibrium right; removing reactant shifts equilibrium left.
- **Effect of pressure (gaseous systems only)**: increasing pressure shifts equilibrium toward the side with fewer moles of gas; decreasing pressure shifts toward the side with more moles.
- **Effect of temperature**: for an exothermic forward reaction (ΔH < 0), increasing T shifts equilibrium left (favours endothermic direction); decreasing T shifts right. For an endothermic forward reaction, the opposite.
- **Catalyst**: does NOT change the position of equilibrium; speeds up both forward and reverse reactions equally, so equilibrium is reached faster.
- **Industrial applications**: Haber process (N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃, exothermic, fewer moles of gas on product side); Contact process (2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃, exothermic, fewer moles).

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing Kc with the rate of reaction — Kc is a thermodynamic quantity about the position of equilibrium; rate is a kinetic quantity.
- Applying pressure effects to liquid-phase equilibria — pressure effects only matter when there are gases involved.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Equilibrium constant Kc

For a reversible reaction aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD at equilibrium:

**Kc = [C]^c × [D]^d / [A]^a × [B]^b**

Where [X] is the equilibrium concentration in mol/dm³.

Kc is constant at a given temperature. A large Kc (Kc > 1) means the equilibrium lies to the right (products favoured). A small Kc (Kc < 1) means the equilibrium lies to the left (reactants favoured).

#### Le Chatelier's principle

**Concentration**:

- Adding more reactant: Q < Kc → reaction shifts right → more product formed.
- Removing product: Q > Kc → reaction shifts right → more product formed.
- Removing reactant: Q > Kc → reaction shifts left → more reactant reformed.

**Pressure (gases only)**:

- Increasing P shifts toward the side with fewer moles of gas.
- Decreasing P shifts toward the side with more moles.
- If moles are equal on both sides, pressure has no effect on position.

**Temperature**:

- Increasing T shifts toward the endothermic direction.
- Decreasing T shifts toward the exothermic direction.

**Catalyst**: a catalyst provides an alternative reaction pathway with lower Ea. It speeds up both forward and reverse reactions equally, so equilibrium is reached faster but the position of equilibrium is unchanged.

#### Industrial processes — compromise conditions

Industrial processes balance yield, rate and economics:

**Haber process (N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃, ΔH < 0)**:

- Low temperature favours yield (exothermic).
- High pressure favours yield (fewer moles on product side).
- But low T gives slow rate; high P requires expensive equipment.
- **Compromise conditions**: ~450°C (rate), ~200 atm (yield and equipment), Fe catalyst.

**Contact process (2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃, ΔH < 0)**:

- Low temperature favours yield.
- But low T gives slow rate.
- **Compromise conditions**: ~450°C, 1-2 atm (high P unnecessary because gases are already at high concentration), V₂O₅ catalyst.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-topic links

- **IX. Rate of Reaction**: equilibrium is established when forward and reverse rates are equal.
- **VIII. Chemical Reactions and Energy**: ΔH is the driving force; activation energy is the kinetic barrier.
- **XIII. Industrial Chemistry (Elective)**: industrial chemistry is the application of equilibrium and rate principles.

#### Exam technique

- **For Kc questions**: set up an ICE table; express equilibrium concentrations in terms of x; substitute into the Kc expression; solve.
- **For Le Chatelier questions**: identify the type of disturbance (concentration, pressure, temperature); apply the appropriate shift.
- **For industrial process questions**: explain why the chosen conditions are a compromise between yield, rate and economics.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Equilibrium means the reaction has stopped." Equilibrium is dynamic — reactions continue in both directions at equal rates.
- "Adding more reactant always increases the amount of product." Yes, in a closed system at equilibrium, more reactant does shift the equilibrium right. But it does not change Kc.
- "Catalysts affect equilibrium constants." No — catalysts only affect the rate of reaching equilibrium, not the equilibrium position.
