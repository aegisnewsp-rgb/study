# Hydrocarbons

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Hydrocarbons contain **only carbon and hydrogen**, classified into **saturated alkanes (CₙH₂ₙ₊₂)**, **unsaturated alkenes (CₙH₂ₙ)**, **alkynes (CₙH₂ₙ₋₂)**, and **aromatics like benzene (C₆H₆)**. JEE Advanced tests whether you can read these general formulas, rank carbocation stability, and predict Markovnikov versus anti-Markovnikov regiochemistry in a single step.

| Family | General Formula | Hybridisation | Bond Angle | Characteristic Reaction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Alkane | CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ | sp³ | ~109.5° | Free-radical halogenation |
| Alkene | CₙH₂ₙ | sp² | ~120° | Electrophilic addition |
| Alkyne | CₙH₂ₙ₋₂ | sp | 180° | Addition, then ozonolysis |
| Arene | CₙH₂ₙ₋₆ (benzene series) | sp² | 120° | Electrophilic aromatic substitution |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook — "MAPS-OH":** **M**ark **A**nchor **P**riority to **S**ubstituent / **O**utcome **H**ierarchy. For HX to an alkene, H goes to the carbon with **more H's already** (Markovnikov); for HBr + peroxide, swap to the carbon with **fewer H's** (anti-Markovnikov, peroxide effect). The acronym also reminds you that carbocations go **3° → 2° → 1° → methyl** in stability.

The degree of unsaturation formula **DoU = (2C + 2 + N − H − X)/2** (dimensionless) lets you decode any mystery molecular formula into rings + π-bonds in seconds.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Working understanding of mechanisms, traps, and exam patterns.

#### Hybridisation and Geometry

Carbon hybridisation governs every structural and reactivity feature. sp³ alkanes are tetrahedral (~109.5°), sp² alkenes and arenes are trigonal planar (~120°) with a π-cloud above and below, and sp alkynes are linear (180°). Rotation about a C=C or C≡C bond is restricted, which is the physical basis for geometric (cis-trans, E/Z) isomerism in alkenes. Two distinct substituents on *each* double-bond carbon are required; otherwise cis-trans fails even though E/Z may still apply through CIP priority rules.

#### Carbocation Stability and the Markovnikov Outcome

Carbocation stability follows the order **tertiary > secondary > primary > methyl**, driven by hyperconjugation (σ C–H overlap into the empty p-orbital) and inductive donation from alkyl groups. When HX adds to an unsymmetrical alkene, the proton attaches to the carbon that yields the more stable carbocation, placing X on the more substituted carbon — the **Markovnikov** outcome. **Anti-Markovnikov** addition occurs *only* with HBr in the presence of peroxides (the **Kharasch effect**), via a radical chain. HCl and HI do **not** show this peroxide reversal.

#### Comparison Matrix: Alkane vs Alkene vs Alkyne vs Benzene

| Feature | Alkane | Alkene | Alkyne | Benzene |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| General formula | CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ | CₙH₂ₙ | CₙH₂ₙ₋₂ | CₙH₂ₙ₋₆ (C₆H₆ for n=6) |
| σ bonds per C | 4 | 3 | 2 | 3 |
| π bonds per C | 0 | 1 | 2 | 0.5 (delocalised) |
| Preferred reaction | Substitution (radical) | Electrophilic addition | Addition (twice possible) | Electrophilic substitution |
| Reagent that reacts | Cl₂/hν | Br₂/CCl₄ | Br₂, then excess Br₂ | Br₂/FeBr₃, HNO₃/H₂SO₄ |
| Hybridisation | sp³ | sp² | sp | sp² |

#### Ozonolysis as a Structure-Deducing Tool

Ozonolysis cleaves every C=C into two carbonyl fragments: **R₂C=** gives a **ketone**, **RHC=** gives an **aldehyde**, **H₂C=** gives **formaldehyde (HCHO)**, and **=CH–CH=** termini yield **glyoxal (OHC–CHO)**. Reductive workup (Zn/H₂O or DMS) preserves aldehydes; oxidative workup (H₂O₂) converts any –CHO into –COOH. Counting the carbons of the carbonyl products and stitching them back reconstructs the original alkene.

#### Aromatic Electrophilic Substitution

Benzene resists addition because it would destroy aromaticity. Instead, it undergoes **EAS**: nitration (HNO₃/H₂SO₄), halogenation (X₂/FeX₃ or AlCl₃), Friedel–Crafts alkylation/acylation (RX/AlCl₃, RCOCl/AlCl₃), and sulphonation (oleum). The key directing groups:

- **–OH, –NH₂, –NHR, –OR, –R, –X** → activating, *ortho/para*-directing (lone pair or hyperconjugation donation)
- **–NO₂, –CN, –COOH, –CHO, –COR, –SO₃H** → deactivating, *meta*-directing (resonance withdrawal)

#### Worked Solution — Identifying an Alkene from Ozonolysis Products

A hydrocarbon C₆H₁₂ yields, on reductive ozonolysis, equimolar amounts of **acetone [(CH₃)₂C=O]** and **propanal [CH₃CH₂CHO]**. Identify the alkene.

**Working.** Each ozonolysis product contains the carbonyl carbon that was originally part of the C=C. Acetone contributes (CH₃)₂C= and propanal contributes CH₃CH₂CH=. Joining these two fragments gives **(CH₃)₂C=CH–CH₂–CH₃**, i.e. **2-methylpent-2-ene**. Total carbons: 5 + 1 = 6 ✓ matches C₆H₁₂ ✓ (DoU = 1, one C=C, no ring).

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students often join the carbons *between* the two carbonyl carbons, producing the impossible 3-methylpent-3-ene with a quaternary double bond. Always draw C=C → C=O + C=O with the **same** carbon count on each side as the alkene supplied.

> 📌 **Formula Check:** Combustion: CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ + (3n+1)/2 O₂ → n CO₂ + (n+1) H₂O; ΔH_comb in kJ mol⁻¹. For C₅H₁₂: 8 moles O₂ per mole of pentane; useful for stoichiometry MCQs.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Edge cases, anti-aromaticity limits, and JEE-Advanced-level traps.

#### Aromaticity Boundaries (Hückel's 4n+2 Rule)

A cyclic, planar, fully-conjugated system is aromatic only when it holds **4n+2 π electrons** (n = 0, 1, 2…). Benzene (6 π e⁻, n=1), the **cyclopentadienyl anion** (6 π e⁻ from 4 π + lone pair, aromatic), and the **tropylium cation** (C₇H₇⁺, 6 π e⁻, aromatic) all qualify. Cyclopentadiene itself (4 π e⁻) is **not** aromatic — only the anion with the extra lone pair is. Cyclobutadiene (4 π e⁻) is anti-aromatic and distorts to a rectangular geometry to escape destabilisation; cyclopropenyl **cation** (2 π e⁻) is aromatic, while the **anion** (4 π e⁻) is anti-aromatic.

#### Eliminations: Saytzeff vs Hofmann

**Saytzeff (Zaitsev):** the more substituted alkene forms in E1/E2 eliminations because alkyl groups stabilise the transition state and the alkene through hyperconjugation. **Hofmann:** a bulky base acting on a quaternary ammonium salt removes the *less hindered* β-H, yielding the less substituted alkene; the leaving group bulk dominates over product stability.

#### Edge-Case List — Reaction Selectivity

1. **HBr + peroxide** is the *only* HX that flips regiochemistry to anti-Markovnikov; HCl and HI do not.
2. **Carbocation rearrangement** can hijack Markovnikov predictions: a 2° cation next to a tertiary site migrates an alkyl/H before capture by X⁻.
3. **Benzyne mechanism:** chlorobenzene + NaNH₂/liquid NH₃ gives a roughly 50:50 mixture of *meta* and *para* products via a triple-bond benzyne intermediate; do not confuse with ordinary EAS.
4. **Friedel–Crafts fails** on strongly deactivated rings (e.g., nitrobenzene) and with primary alkyl halides that rearrange.
5. **Cis-trans vs E/Z:** identical when each carbon bears two unambiguous substituents; CIP priority decides the *E*/*Z* label whenever groups differ in atomic number at the first point of difference.

#### Boundary Conditions on General Formulas

CₙH₂ₙ fits both monoalkenes **and** cycloalkanes — never conclude "alkene" from a formula alone; you need a reaction (Br₂/CCl₄ decolorisation) or a DoU analysis (cyclohexane DoU = 1, but it is not an alkene). Likewise, CₙH₂ₙ₋₂ includes alkynes **and** cycloalkadienes (e.g., cyclopentadiene, C₅H₆).

#### Two Advanced Practice Prompts

1. **Aromaticity reasoning:** Cyclooctatetraene (COT, C₈H₈, 8 π e⁻) is non-aromatic in its ground state because it puckers into a tub shape to avoid the 4n anti-aromatic destabilisation. Reduce COT with potassium to the dianion [COT]²⁻; predict the geometry change and the new π-electron count. *Expect: planar, 10 π e⁻, aromatic.*

2. **Ozonolysis reconstruction:** A C₇H₁₂ alkene gives, on reductive ozonolysis, **only acetone** as the carbonyl product. What is the structure? *Answer: 2,4-dimethylpent-2-ene, (CH₃)₂C=CH–CH(CH₃)₂ — three fragments of acetone stitched at the central carbon would exceed C₇, so symmetry must collapse the four terminal carbons into two equivalent isopropylidene units.*

> 📌 **Aromaticity Diagnostic:** Cyclic + planar + fully conjugated + 4n+2 π electrons. Miss any one, and the system falls back to non-aromatic, even if the electron count appears correct.

#### Exam Strategy for the 5% Hydrocarbon Slice

JEE Advanced typically asks 1 MCQ plus 1 numerical/integer per paper on this unit. The **fastest 3 marks** come from ozonolysis carbon-counting and Markovnikov direction; the **slowest 4 marks** come from aromatic EAS with competing directing groups. Spend the first 30 seconds of each hydrocarbon question writing the general formula next to the given molecular formula to immediately fix the family.
