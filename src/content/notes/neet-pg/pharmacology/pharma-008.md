# Drug Interactions

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your NEET PG examination.

Drug interactions occur when the pharmacological or toxicological action of a drug is altered by another drug, food item, or environmental agent. These interactions are classified into pharmacokinetic interactions (affecting absorption, distribution, metabolism, or excretion) and pharmacodynamic interactions (affecting receptor binding, signal transduction, or physiological responses).

In the NEET PG examination, questions test the distinction between microsomal enzyme inducers and inhibitors, dangerous drug combinations leading to arrhythmia or bleeding, and transport-protein competition involving P-glycoprotein.

#### Critical Lethal Drug Combinations in Medical Practice

| Drug Combination | Interacting Mechanism | Clinical Consequence | Emergency Management |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Warfarin + Fluconazole / Metronidazole** | CYP2C9 inhibition impairs clearance of active (S)-warfarin | Marked elevation of INR, gastrointestinal or intracranial hemorrhage | Discontinue warfarin, administer vitamin K1 and four-factor prothrombin complex concentrate |
| **Methotrexate + NSAIDs / Penicillins** | Competition for organic anion transporter (OAT1/3) and reduced renal blood flow | Severe pancytopenia, mucositis, acute renal failure | Leucovorin (folinic acid) rescue, urinary alkalinization with sodium bicarbonate |
| **Sildenafil + Nitroglycerin / Isosorbide** | Synergistic cGMP accumulation causing profound vascular smooth muscle relaxation | Refractory vasodilatory shock, coronary hypoperfusion, cardiac arrest | Aggressive intravenous fluid resuscitation, alpha-1 agonists (norepinephrine); avoid nitrates |
| **Lithium + Thiazides / ACE Inhibitors** | Proximal tubule sodium depletion increases compensatory lithium reabsorption | Coarse tremors, ataxia, delirium, seizures, nephrotoxicity | Discontinue lithium, hydrate with isotonic normal saline; hemodialysis if serum level > 4.0 mEq/L |
| **Digoxin + Amiodarone / Verapamil** | P-glycoprotein inhibition at intestinal and renal tubular membranes | Sinus bradycardia, heart blocks, ventricular bigeminy, xanthopsia | Atropine for bradycardia, Digoxin-specific Fab antibodies (Digibind) |

⚡ **High-Yield Examination Rule:** All azole antifungals, macrolides (except azithromycin), and protease inhibitors inhibit cytochrome P450 enzymes. Conversely, rifampicin, carbamazepine, phenytoin, and barbiturates act as potent microsomal enzyme inducers.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Systematic preparation for postgraduate medical entrance tests.

#### 1. Pharmacokinetic Mechanisms of Drug Interactions

Pharmacokinetic interactions modify the concentration of the active drug reaching its target site over time:

1. **Absorption Level:**
   - **Chelation and Insoluble Complexation:** Fluoroquinolones (ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin) and tetracyclines bind polyvalent metallic cations ($Fe^{2+}$, $Ca^{2+}$, $Mg^{2+}$, $Al^{3+}$) in oral antacids or iron supplements. This reduces bioavailability by up to 80%. Dose separation by at least 2 to 4 hours is necessary.
   - **Gastric pH Alteration:** Antacids, H2 receptor antagonists, and proton pump inhibitors elevate intragastric pH above 4.0, impairing dissolution and absorption of weakly basic antifungal drugs like ketoconazole and itraconazole.
   - **Gastrointestinal Motility Alteration:** Metoclopramide accelerates gastric emptying, increasing the absorption rate of paracetamol, while atropine and anticholinergic agents delay gastric emptying, slowing absorption.

2. **Distribution and Plasma Protein Binding:**
   - Acidic drugs bind predominantly to plasma albumin, while basic drugs bind to alpha-1-acid glycoprotein. Displacement of one drug from albumin by another (such as displacement of warfarin or tolbutamide by salicylates, sulfonamides, or phenylbutazone) transiently elevates free unbound drug fraction.
   - For drugs with low volume of distribution ($V_d < 0.15\text{ L/kg}$), transient toxicity is pronounced until enhanced clearance restores steady-state unbound concentration.

3. **Metabolism Level (The Cytochrome P450 System):**
   - The hepatic microsomal mixed-function oxidase system accounts for phase I oxidative metabolism. Competitive or non-competitive inhibition of specific CYP isozymes raises parent drug concentrations within 24 to 48 hours. Enzyme induction requires de novo protein synthesis and reaches maximal effect over 1 to 2 weeks.

| Cytochrome P450 Isozyme | Major Substrates | Clinically Potent Inducers | Clinically Potent Inhibitors |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **CYP3A4 / CYP3A5** (metabolizes ~50% of therapeutic drugs) | Cyclosporine, tacrolimus, atorvastatin, simvastatin, midazolam, oral contraceptives, calcium channel blockers | Rifampicin, carbamazepine, phenytoin, phenobarbital, St. John's wort | Ketoconazole, itraconazole, clarithromycin, erythromycin, ritonavir, grapefruit juice |
| **CYP2D6** (genetically polymorphic) | Metoprolol, timolol, carvedilol, flecainide, codeine (prodrug), tramadol, amitriptyline | No clinically significant inducers known | Quinidine, fluoxetine, paroxetine, bupropion |
| **CYP2C9** | (S)-Warfarin, phenytoin, celecoxib, glipizide, losartan | Rifampicin, barbiturates | Fluconazole, metronidazole, amiodarone, sulfamethoxazole, miconazole |
| **CYP2C19** | Clopidogrel (prodrug), omeprazole, diazepam, voriconazole | Rifampicin | Omeprazole, esomeprazole, fluconazole, ticlopidine |
| **CYP1A2** | Theophylline, caffeine, clozapine, olanzapine, ropinirole | Cigarette smoke (polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons), charbroiled food | Ciprofloxacin, fluvoxamine, cimetidine |

4. **Excretion Level:**
   - **Renal Tubular Secretion Competition:** Probenecid competes with acidic drugs for the Organic Anion Transporter (OAT1/OAT3) in the proximal convoluted tubule. Probenecid prolongs the half-life of penicillin, cephalosporins, and methotrexate.
   - **Urinary pH Manipulation:** Alkalinization of urine ($pH > 7.5$) with intravenous sodium bicarbonate increases ionization of weak acids (salicylates, phenobarbital), preventing tubular reabsorption and promoting clearance. Acidification with ammonium chloride or ascorbic acid accelerates excretion of weak bases (amphetamines, phencyclidine).

| Excretion Transporter / Mechanism | Affected Substrate | Interacting Agent | Mechanism of Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **P-glycoprotein (MDR1 / ABCB1)** | Digoxin, dabigatran, cyclosporine | Verapamil, amiodarone, quinidine, clarithromycin | Efflux transporter inhibition in gut enterocytes and renal tubules increases systemic bioavailability |
| **Organic Anion Transporter (OAT1/3)** | Methotrexate, penicillin, indomethacin | Probenecid, high-dose aspirin | Competitive inhibition of proximal tubular active secretion raises serum methotrexate levels |
| **Organic Cation Transporter (OCT2 / MATE)** | Metformin, dofetilide | Cimetidine, trimethoprim, dolutegravir | Inhibition of tubular secretion leads to metformin accumulation and lactic acidosis |
| **Tubular Water & Solute Clearance** | Lithium | Hydrochlorothiazide, furosemide, enalapril | Thiazide-induced hypovolemia increases proximal tubule sodium and lithium reabsorption |

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive reference for medical residents, clinical pharmacology vivas, and residency entrance exams.

#### 1. Severe Pharmacodynamic Syndromes in Acute Care

Pharmacodynamic interactions occur without alteration in plasma drug concentration, arising from agonism, antagonism, or synergistic modulation at common receptor or post-receptor pathways.

| Clinical Syndrome | Precipitating Drug Combinations | Pathophysiological Cascade | Classic Physical Findings & Antidote |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Serotonin Syndrome** | SSRI (sertraline) + MAOI (phenelzine, linezolid, moclobemide), or Tramadol + Fentanyl + Triptans | Hyperstimulation of 5-HT1A and 5-HT2A receptors in central and peripheral nervous systems | Hyperthermia, neuromuscular clonus (spontaneous, inducible, or ocular), hyperreflexia, diaphoresis; Antidote: Cyproheptadine |
| **Disulfiram-Like Reaction** | Ethanol + Metronidazole, Cefoperazone, Cefotetan, Griseofulvin, Chlorpropamide | Inhibition of aldehyde dehydrogenase causes acetaldehyde accumulation | Facial flushing, throbbing headache, diaphoresis, tachycardia, hypotension, nausea and vomiting; Management: IV crystalloids, antiemetics |
| **Acquired Torsades de Pointes (Long QT)** | Macrolides + Fluoroquinolones + Ondansetron + Terfenadine + Haloperidol + Class IA / III antiarrhythmics | Blockade of rapid delayed rectifier potassium current ($I_{Kr}$ encoded by *hERG* gene) delays ventricular repolarization | Prolonged QTc (> 500 ms), polymorphic ventricular tachycardia twisting around isoelectric line; Antidote: IV Magnesium Sulfate (1-2 g) |
| **Malignant Hyperthermia** | Volatile anesthetics (Halothane, Isoflurane) + Succinylcholine | Mutation in Ryanodine receptor (*RYR1*) leads to uncontrolled sarcoplasmic reticulum calcium release | Masseter muscle spasm, rapid hyperthermia, metabolic acidosis, rhabdomyolysis; Antidote: IV Dantrolene sodium |

#### 2. Quantitative Pharmacokinetics of Drug Interactions

In clinical therapeutics, alterations in hepatic clearance directly impact steady-state serum concentrations:

$$\text{Total Clearance } (Cl) = \frac{\text{Rate of Elimination}}{C_p} = \frac{V_d \cdot 0.693}{t_{1/2}}$$

At steady state under regular maintenance dosing, the average plasma concentration ($C_{ss}$) is given by:

$$C_{ss} = \frac{F \cdot \text{Dose}}{Cl \cdot \tau}$$

where $F$ is oral bioavailability, $\tau$ is the dosing interval, and $Cl$ is systemic clearance.

##### Worked Clinical Calculation

**Clinical Scenario:**  
A 54-year-old asthmatic patient receives oral theophylline at 400 mg every 12 hours ($\tau = 12\text{ h}$, $F = 1.0$). The patient's baseline theophylline clearance is $3.0\text{ L/h}$, providing a stable plasma concentration:

$$C_{ss} = \frac{1.0 \times 400\text{ mg}}{3.0\text{ L/h} \times 12\text{ h}} = \frac{400}{36} = 11.1\text{ mg/L}$$

This concentration lies safely within the therapeutic window (10 to 20 mg/L).

The patient develops acute pyelonephritis and is started on oral ciprofloxacin (500 mg BID). Ciprofloxacin inhibits CYP1A2, reducing the patient's theophylline clearance by $50\%$ to $1.5\text{ L/h}$.

Calculate the new steady-state concentration:

$$C_{ss\text{ (new)}} = \frac{1.0 \times 400\text{ mg}}{1.5\text{ L/h} \times 12\text{ h}} = \frac{400}{18} = 22.2\text{ mg/L}$$

**Clinical Interpretation:**  
The plasma level exceeds the 20 mg/L toxicity threshold, putting the patient at acute risk of theophylline toxicity characterized by intractable vomiting, multifocal atrial tachycardia, and life-threatening generalized tonic-clonic seizures. The theophylline dose must be reduced by $50\%$ immediately when prescribing ciprofloxacin.

#### 3. Common Exam Traps in NEET PG

1. **The Clopidogrel-Omeprazole Dilemma:** Clopidogrel is an inactive thienopyridine prodrug requiring two-step hepatic bioactivation, with CYP2C19 generating the active thiol metabolite. Concurrent administration of omeprazole or esomeprazole inhibits CYP2C19, reducing antiplatelet aggregation and precipitating recurrent myocardial infarction or stent thrombosis. Pantoprazole exhibits minimal CYP2C19 inhibition and represents the preferred gastroprotective agent.
2. **Azithromycin vs. Erythromycin:** Erythromycin and clarithromycin contain a 14-membered lactone ring that forms an inactive nitrosoalkane complex with CYP3A4, acting as powerful inhibitors. Azithromycin contains a 15-membered azalide ring that does not form this complex and does not inhibit CYP3A4.
3. **Statins and Grapefruit Juice:** Lovastatin, simvastatin, and atorvastatin undergo intestinal CYP3A4 first-pass metabolism. Furanocoumarins in grapefruit juice irreversibly inactivate intestinal CYP3A4, causing up to a fifteen-fold surge in systemic statin exposure and precipitating severe rhabdomyolysis. Rosuvastatin (CYP2C9/OATP1B1) and pravastatin (sulfation) avoid this interaction.
