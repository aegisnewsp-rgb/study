# The Constitution of 1956 and the Republican Era

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The Constitution of 1956 was Pakistan's first **republican, domestically framed** constitution, enacted on **23 February 1956** and brought into force on **23 March 1956**. It ended the dominion status inherited from 1947 and replaced the **Governor-General** with an elected **President** as ceremonial head of state, while real executive power rested with the **Prime Minister** and Cabinet responsible to the National Assembly.

- **Form of government**: Federal parliamentary republic with Islamic character.
- **First President**: Iskander Mirza (23 March 1956); **PM under whom enacted**: Chaudhry Muhammad Ali.
- **Abrogated**: 7 October 1958 by President Iskander Mirza, lasted only ~2 years 7 months.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Background and Framing

Pakistan became independent on 14 August 1947 under the Indian Independence Act, 1947 as a dominion with the Governor-General representing the British Crown. The Constituent Assembly, which doubled as the federal legislature, appointed the **Basic Principles Committee** (initially chaired by Prime Minister Muhammad Ali Bogra, later by Chaudhry Muhammad Ali) to draft guiding principles. After prolonged debate over federalism, parity between the two wings, and the role of Islam, the final report was approved in 1954 and the constitution passed on 23 February 1956.

#### Salient Features

The 1956 Constitution declared Pakistan an **"Islamic Republic"**, drawing ideological grounding from the Objectives Resolution (Sukkur Resolution) of March 1949. It adopted a **federal parliamentary** system, recognised **Urdu and Bengali** as official languages, and provided for **fundamental rights**, **directive principles of state policy**, and an **independent judiciary** with the Supreme Court at the apex. The **Repugnancy Clause** required that no law repugnant to the teachings of Islam, as laid down in the Quran and Sunnah, could be enacted.

#### The Republican Transition

On 23 March 1956 the dominion era formally ended. **Iskander Mirza**, the last Governor-General, was sworn in as Pakistan's first President. Power shifted visibly from the Crown's representative to an elected Pakistani head of state, although the office remained largely ceremonial under the new constitution.

| --- | --- |
| Form of state | Federal parliamentary republic |
| Legislature | National Assembly (unicameral in practice) |
| Head of State | President, elected by Assembly |
| Head of Government | Prime Minister, leader of majority party |
| Judiciary | Independent Supreme Court as guardian |
| Islamic Character | Repugnancy Clause, Advisory Council of Islamic Ideology |

#### Reasons for Abrogation

The constitution was abrogated on **7 October 1958** by President Iskander Mirza, citing political instability, weak centre–province relations, the controversial **One Unit** scheme, frequent dissolutions, and the parliamentary system's inability to deliver governance. Power was handed to General Ayub Khan the same month.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Comparative Edge Cases

The 1956 Constitution is best understood by contrast with its successors. Unlike the **1962 Constitution** (presidential, indirect election, Basic Democracies), the 1956 document was **parliamentary**, with the President elected by the National Assembly and the Prime Minister commanding its confidence. Compared with the **1973 Constitution**, the 1956 framework lacked a **bicameral legislature**, did not name provinces (because One Unit merged West Pakistan's four provinces), and provided weaker federal guarantees. The Islamic provisions of 1956, however, directly influenced the 1973 Articles 2-A and the Council of Islamic Ideology.

#### Common Mistakes in PPSC Answers

1. Calling Jinnah "first President" — he died as Governor-General in 1948.
2. Saying the system was "presidential" — it was parliamentary.
3. Citing 1958 as a long-lived constitution — it lasted only ~31 months.
4. Confusing One Unit with East Pakistan — One Unit merged **West** Punjab, Sindh, NWFP, and Baluchistan.
5. Crediting Ayub Khan with abrogation — it was Mirza who struck first, then handed power to Ayub.
