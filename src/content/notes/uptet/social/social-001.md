# Ancient Indian History: Indus Valley Civilization

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> What to fix in your head in the first hour: sites, dates, and the one planning feature examiners love.

The **Indus Valley Civilization** (c. 3300–1300 BCE) sat along the Indus and Ghaggar-Hakara river systems and left behind two flagship cities: **Mohenjo-daro** (Sindh) and **Harappa** (Punjab, Pakistan). Rakhigarhi in Haryana is now the largest known Harappan site.

#### The 5 facts that win marks
- **Planning:** grid-iron streets, **baked-brick** houses on raised mud platforms, separate **Citadel** and **Lower Town**, covered drains with manholes.
- **Iconic structures:** **Great Bath** at Mohenjo-daro, **Granary** at Mohenjo-daro, dockyard-like tank at **Lothal**.
- **Discoverers:** **Daya Ram Sahni** → Harappa (1921); **R. D. Banerjee** → Mohenjo-daro (1922).
- **Script:** **Indus Script** is still undeciphered.
- **Material:** Bronze Age culture — no iron tools.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook (Lite):** "**SHaM-Do-L**" — **S**ahni, **H**arappa, **1921**; **M**ohenjo-daro, **D**r. Banerjee, **O**lder twin cities; **L**othal is in **L**ittle Gujarat. Read it as "**SHAM + DOL**" = the two-city lie.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Build the working model: chronology, town plan, economy, religion, and how UPTET frames the questions.

#### Chronology in three slices

The civilization did not arrive finished; it grew. NCERT divides it into **Early Harappan** (c. 3300–2600 BCE), **Mature Harappan** (c. 2600–1900 BCE) — the planned-city peak — and **Late Harappan** (c. 1900–1300 BCE), when sites shrink, trade fades, and many settlements are abandoned. The Mature phase is what 80% of UPTET questions test.

#### Town planning: the reason it tops the syllabus

Harappan cities show **grid-iron streets** running N–S and E–W, houses aligned to the cardinal directions, and a standard **brick ratio of 1 : 2 : 4**. Two zones dominate every major site: a raised **Citadel** (ritual/admin) and a **Lower Town** (residential/industrial). The **Great Bath** at Mohenjo-daro measures about **12 m × 7 m**, roughly **2.4 m deep**, waterproofed with **bitumen** between two layers of finely fitted bricks — designed for ritual immersion, not household bathing.

Drainage is the headline achievement: covered brick drains with **manholes** at junctions, soak pits, and house-level chutes feeding into street lines. Water flowed from north to south because of a deliberate slope.

#### Economy, weights, and trade

Agriculture combined **wheat, barley, rice (at Lothal)**, dates, and cotton. Herds included **humped cattle, buffalo, goats, and sheep**. Craft specialisation was sharpest at **Chanhudaro** (beads), **Nageshwar** (shell), and **Lothal** (microbeads, ivory). **Standardised cuboidal stone weights** ran in a **binary–decimal ratio series (1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100, 200, 500, 1000…)**; an ivory scale from Lothal defines the Harappan **cubit = 1.32 inches (3.35 cm)**.

Long-distance trade reached **Mesopotamia**: Harappan seals turn up at **Ur and Susa**, and Mesopotamian texts call the Indus land **"Meluha."** Carnelian, lapis, copper, and gold came in; finished goods went out.

#### Religion and iconography

The **Pashupati seal** (a horned figure cross-legged on a dais, surrounded by animals) is read as a proto-Shiva or "Lord of Beasts." **Mother Goddess** terracotta figurines, the **pipal motif**, and the **humped bull** dominate seal art. **Fire altars** at Kalibangan — laid out in a row — hint at early Vedic-style rites. Burials are mainly **extended inhumation** with grave goods; the **Cemetery H** at Harappa is unusual for its **urn burials**.

#### How the examiner frames it

Most UPTET items are **1–2 mark MCQs**: site–state matching, "which is NOT Harappan," "the Indus Script is —," "the Great Bath is at —," and match-the-following between artefacts and sites. **Assertion–reason** pairs commonly test the **undeciphered script** and the **Bronze Age (no iron)** fact.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Three traps repeat every year: (1) calling the **Indus Script Dravidian** — it is undeciphered; (2) calling Lothal a confirmed **dockyard** — current ASI reading calls it a **basin/tank**; (3) putting **Rakhigarhi in Punjab** instead of Haryana. Read options closely before ticking.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Push past memorisation: contested points, edge cases, and how to argue a difference in a 2-mark item.

#### What historians still argue about

No single collapse story survives scrutiny. Four hypotheses stay alive: **Aryan migration** (Ridge-furrow sites stop around 1900 BCE), **climate-driven drying of the Ghaggar-Hakara** (Lakshmi at Kalibangan and Bhirrana show early rice, then shift to drought-tolerant millet), **tectonic uplift and flooding** of the Indus channel (Mohenjo-daro shows flood-debris layers), and **decline of Mesopotamian trade** (Ur's fall roughly tracks the Late Harappan decline). UPTET answers should mention **two** causes, not one — the official NCERT line accepts multi-causal decline.

#### Edge cases worth memorising

- **Lothal "dockyard":** the structure is about **214 m × 36 m**, with a 12 m wide inlet channel; ASI's post-2014 reading is **tidal tank / basin**, not a true harbour. Mark it as **"probable dockyard"** to be safe.
- **Pashupati seal:** the figure has **three faces, horned headdress, surrounded by a tiger, elephant, rhino, and buffalo** with two deer below. Mythological reading is contested; the safer exam phrase is "**proto-Shiva or Lord of Animals**."
- **Dancing Girl (Mohenjo-daro):** a 10.5 cm **bronze** (lost-wax casting) figure, dated c. 2500 BCE — **not** terracotta, not a man.
- **Rakhigarhi:** Haryana, mound HR-1 to HR-7; **largest Harappan site in India**, and one of the few with a **twin Citadel-Lower Town layout** confirmed on Indian soil.
- **Cemetery H at Harappa:** urn burials with grave goods, later date, possibly a distinct post-urban phase.

#### Advanced practice prompts

1. *"The Indus Script has been read as Dravidian."* Evaluate in four lines using what you know about script corpus size (≈ 4,000 inscribed objects, mostly 1–5 signs) and the absence of bilingual inscriptions like the Rosetta Stone.
2. *"Harappan town planning was uniform across all sites."* Give **two pieces of evidence against** this claim — for example, **Dholavira's** stepped reservoir system differs from Mohenjo-daro's grid; **Sothi-Siswal** sites lack the Citadel-Lower Town split.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap (Extended):** A statement claiming the script is "Dravidian" or "proto-Brahmi" is **false** by NCERT standard; so is "Harappans knew iron." If the assertion–reason stem uses either, the answer is **(c) Assertion is wrong, Reason is wrong**.
