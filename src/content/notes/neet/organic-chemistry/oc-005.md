# Carboxylic Acids

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

A **carboxylic acid** carries the **–COOH** functional group: a carbonyl (C=O) and a hydroxyl (–OH) bound to the same sp²-hybridised carbon. General formula R–COOH, where R = H, alkyl, or aryl. The –COOH carbon is **C1** in IUPAC naming; the alkane "-e" becomes "-oic acid" (methanoic, ethanoic, benzoic acid) and ring-bound acids use the suffix "-carboxylic acid".

The two facts NEET tests most often are **acidity order** and the **H-bonded dimer**. Carboxylic acids (pKa ≈ 4–5) are far more acidic than water (pKa 15.7), alcohols (pKa ≈ 16–18), or phenols (pKa ≈ 10) because the **conjugate base (RCOO⁻) is resonance-stabilised over two equivalent oxygens**. Boiling points are anomalously high because two molecules pair through **two O–H···O=C hydrogen bonds**, forming a cyclic dimer even in vapour.

- **Acidity ladder:** HCOOH (3.75) > CH₃COOH (4.76) > C₂H₅COOH (4.87) > C₆H₅COOH (4.20) > phenol (10) > ROH (≈17) > H₂O (15.7).
- **Identification:** effervescence with NaHCO₃ releasing CO₂ — distinguishes –COOH from phenol, which does not react.
- **Named reactions to memorise:** HVZ (α-bromination, Br₂/red P), Rosenmund reduction (RCOCl → RCHO), Kolbe's electrolysis (2 RCOO⁻ → R–R + 2 CO₂), soda-lime decarboxylation (RCOONa + NaOH/CaO → R–H), Schmidt reaction (RCOOH + HN₃/H⁺ → RNH₂).

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"CARDS-HEP"** for the reaction map — **C**OCl (SOCl₂), **A**mide (NH₃, heat), **R**eduction (LiAlH₄ → RCH₂OH; Rosenmund → RCHO), **D**ecarboxylation (soda-lime), **S**aponification/ester, **H**VZ (α-Br), **E**lectrolysis (Kolbe), **P**erkin/Schmidt. And for acidity: **"Fluoro > Formic > Acetic > Phenol > Alcohol"** — remember fluorine substitution gives fluoroacetic acid pKa 2.6, the strongest simple carboxylic acid.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Functional Group and Hybridisation

The carboxyl carbon is **sp²**, giving a **planar** –COOH with bond angles near 120°. The C=O (≈1.21 Å, stronger) and C–O (≈1.36 Å, weaker) bonds are *not* equivalent; the C=O stretches near **1700 cm⁻¹** in IR, and the broad O–H stretch appears between **2500–3300 cm⁻¹**, useful in qualitative analysis.

#### Why Carboxylic Acids Are Acidic

Loss of the O–H proton gives the **carboxylate anion**, in which the negative charge is delocalised equally over **two oxygen atoms** (resonance structures of identical energy). This makes the conjugate base far more stable than an alkoxide (charge localised on one O) or a phenoxide (charge delocalised over the ring but with less electronegative atoms). 

> 📌 **Formula Check:** pKa = –log₁₀ Ka (dimensionless). For acetic acid, Ka = 1.8 × 10⁻⁵, so pKa = 4.76. Henderson–Hasselbalch: **pH = pKa + log₁₀([A⁻]/[HA])** with [A⁻] and [HA] in mol·L⁻¹.

Electron-**withdrawing** groups (–F, –Cl, –NO₂, –CN) raise acidity via the **–I (inductive) effect**, which stabilises the anion. Electron-**donating** alkyl groups (+I) destabilise the anion, so acidity falls along HCOOH > CH₃COOH > C₂H₅COOH > …

#### Concept Comparison Matrix

| Feature | Carboxylic acid (RCOOH) | Phenol (ArOH) | Alcohol (ROH) |
|---|---|---|---|
| pKa range | 3.7 – 5 | 8 – 10 | 16 – 18 |
| Reaction with NaHCO₃ | Brisk CO₂ effervescence | No reaction | No reaction |
| Reaction with NaOH | Salt formed | Salt formed | No reaction |
| Conjugate-base stabilisation | Resonance over 2 equivalent O | Resonance into ring | Localised on O |
| Effect of –I group on α-C | Strongly increases acidity | Weak effect | Weak effect |
| Boiling point | Very high (H-bonded dimer) | High (H-bonding) | High (H-bonding) |

#### Preparation Methods

1. **Oxidation** of primary alcohols or aldehydes (KMnO₄, K₂Cr₂O₇/H⁺).
2. **Nitrile hydrolysis:** R–CN + H⁺/H₂O → R–COOH (adds one carbon).
3. **Grignard + CO₂:** RMgX + CO₂ → R–COOMgX → H₃O⁺ → R–COOH.
4. **Side-chain oxidation** of toluene → benzoic acid with KMnO₄/heat.

#### Reaction Map at a Glance

- NaHCO₃ → RCOONa + CO₂↑ + H₂O (identification).
- PCl₅ or SOCl₂ → RCOCl (acyl chloride; SOCl₂ preferred because by-products are gases).
- NH₃ → RCOONH₄ → on heating, RCONH₂ (amide).
- R'OH + conc. H₂SO₄ → RCOOR' + H₂O (Fischer esterification, reversible).
- LiAlH₄ → RCH₂OH (primary alcohol); Rosenmund (H₂/Pd-BaSO₄) → RCHO.
- Br₂/red P → α-bromo acid (Hell–Volhard–Zelinsky; needs red P + Br₂, *not* just Br₂ alone).
- Soda-lime: RCOONa + NaOH/CaO (dry) → R–H + Na₂CO₃.
- Kolbe's electrolysis: 2 RCOO⁻ K⁺ → R–R + 2 CO₂ + 2 K⁺ + 2 e⁻.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** Arrange the following in **increasing order of acidity** and justify:  
(a) CH₃COOH, (b) HCOOH, (c) C₆H₅COOH (benzoic acid), (d) C₆H₅OH (phenol), (e) CH₃CH₂OH (ethanol). Identify which one will liberate CO₂ with aqueous NaHCO₃.

#### Solution:

Step 1 — Apply the inductive effect to formic vs acetic acid:  
HCOOH has H attached to the carboxyl carbon (no +I donor), so the anion is not destabilised. CH₃COOH has a methyl group exerting +I, destabilising CH₃COO⁻. So **HCOOH (pKa 3.75) > CH₃COOH (4.76)** in acidity.

Step 2 — Compare benzoic acid vs acetic acid: the **phenyl ring is electron-withdrawing by the –I effect** of the sp² carbon relative to an sp³ CH₃ group, so C₆H₅COOH (pKa 4.20) is slightly stronger than CH₃COOH. So order is **HCOOH > C₆H₅COOH > CH₃COOH**.

Step 3 — Compare carboxylic acids with phenol and alcohol: resonance-stabilised carboxylate over two equivalent oxygens > phenoxide (charge into ring, but O is less stabilising) > ethoxide (charge localised). pKa values: phenol ≈ 10, ethanol ≈ 16. So **C₆H₅OH > CH₃CH₂OH** in acidity.

Final order (increasing acidity, weakest → strongest):

#### CH₃CH₂OH (16) < C₆H₅OH (10) < CH₃COOH (4.76) < C₆H₅COOH (4.20) < HCOOH (3.75).

Only (a), (b), and (c) — all carboxylic acids — liberate CO₂ with NaHCO₃; phenol (d) does **not**; ethanol (e) does **not**.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students frequently place benzoic acid *below* acetic acid by wrongly claiming "the phenyl ring donates electrons by resonance." The ring is attached through an **sp² carbon that is inductively electron-withdrawing**, but the resonance donation from a directly attached –COOH does not exist (no lone pair on the carboxyl C). The +R effect of –COOH *into* the ring applies only when the ring is the *substrate*, not when –COOH is on the ring. Stick to the **measured pKa values**.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Resonance, Hybridisation, and Bonding Details

The –COOH carbon is **sp²**; the molecule is planar around it. The O–H proton is the acidic one (not the C–H). The C–O single bond has partial double-bond character (~1.36 Å, vs 1.43 Å for a pure C–O single bond in alcohols) due to resonance. In the conjugate base, the two C–O bonds become **identical, ~1.27 Å**, intermediate between single and double bond — classic proof of delocalisation. NMR: the acidic proton appears far downfield (δ 10–13 ppm) because of strong deshielding by two electronegative oxygens.

#### Dimerisation and Physical Properties

In non-polar solvents and in the gas phase, RCOOH exists as a **cyclic dimer** held by two simultaneous O–H···O=C hydrogen bonds (~30 kJ·mol⁻¹ each). This doubles the effective molecular mass for boiling-point purposes, so acetic acid (Mr 60) boils at 118 °C — comparable to a much heavier alcohol. Only the **first three members** (HCOOH, CH₃COOH, C₂H₅COOH) are fully miscible with water; from butanoic acid onward, water miscibility drops sharply. **Palmitic (C16) and stearic (C18) acids** are waxy solids obtained by saponification of fats.

#### Edge Cases and Exceptions

| Reaction | Condition that works | Condition that fails |
|---|---|---|
| Soda-lime decarboxylation | Sodium salt + dry NaOH/CaO, heat | Free acid alone (no decarboxylation) |
| Thermal decarboxylation | β-keto acids, malonic acid (lose CO₂ on gentle heating) | Simple aliphatic acids (need soda-lime) |
| Fischer esterification | Tertiary ROH gives elimination, not ester | Tertiary alcohols → alkene |
| HVZ bromination | Needs catalytic red P + Br₂ | Br₂ alone (no reaction) — common wrong answer |
| Rosenmund reduction | Acid chloride + H₂/Pd-BaSO₄ (poisoned Pd) | Free acid or anhydride (does not work) |
| Schmidt reaction | RCOOH + HN₃/conc. H₂SO₄ → RNH₂ | Without acid catalyst, no reaction |

#### Substituent Effects on Acidity (Quantified)

- α-Fluoroacetic acid, FCH₂COOH: pKa = **2.59** (one –F on the α-C strongly withdraws).
- α-Chloroacetic acid, ClCH₂COOH: pKa = **2.87**.
- Trichloroacetic acid, CCl₃COOH: pKa = **0.65** — comparable to a mineral acid.
- Acetic acid: 4.76.
- Benzoic acid: 4.20 (ring –I outweighs any +R since –COOH has no lone pair on C).

> 💡 **High-Yield Revision Hook:** As a rule of thumb, each halogen on the α-carbon **drops the pKa by roughly 1 unit**; three halogens cumulatively drop it by ~4 units, pushing trichloroacetic acid into strong-acid territory.

#### Conversion Chain to Memorise

RCH₂OH ⇌ RCHO ⇌ RCOOH ⇌ RCOCl ⇌ RCONH₂ ⇌ RNH₂  
(step-by-step reagents: PCC/anh.; KMnO₄; SOCl₂; NH₃/heat; Br₂/NaOH/Hofmann, *or* LiAlH₄ then Hofmann.)

This single chain covers ~40% of NEET conversion questions linking alcohols, aldehydes, acids, halides, amides, and amines.
