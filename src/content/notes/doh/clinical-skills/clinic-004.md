# Medication Administration and Safety

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your DOH OSCE.

The **10 Rights of Medication Administration** are the spine of every DOH medication station: right patient, drug, dose, route, time, documentation, reason, response, refusal, and assessment. Two patient identifiers — full name plus date of birth or medical record number — must be confirmed before a tablet, syringe, or IV line is opened. Paediatric and high-alert drugs require an **independent double-check** by a second licensed nurse, and the chart is updated immediately after the dose is given, never before.

#### Must-Know Dosing Formula

| Formula | Variables and Units | When Used |
|---|---|---|
| Dose (mg) = Weight (kg) × Dose-per-kg (mg/kg) | W in kilograms, prescribed dose in mg/kg | Paediatric weight-based dosing |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook (Memory Hook): "Patients Don't Dare Refuse The Right Drug Rightly"** — Patient, Drug, Dose, Route, Time, Documentation, Reason, Response, Refusal, Assessment. Mnemonic covers all 10 Rights in canonical OSCE order.

A second nurse's co-signature is mandatory for insulin, heparin, opioids, concentrated electrolytes, and paediatric chemotherapy. Routes travel with fixed needle gauges and angles: ID 25–27 G at 10–15°, SC 25–30 G at 45–90°, IM 20–23 G at 90° (Z-track for iron).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### The Rights in Practice

Each Right has a concrete procedural anchor in the DOH Medication Safety Policy. *Right patient* is enforced through two identifiers matched against the wristband, not the bed number. *Right drug* demands a three-check rule: once when removing from storage, once during preparation, and once at the bedside. *Right documentation* requires charting after the dose is given — a pre-charted entry is treated as a falsified record and a sentinel event.

> 💡 **Examiner Tip:** DOH stations commonly test whether you can name which Right was violated, not whether you remember the list. Practise mapping scenarios to a single failing Right.

#### Comparison Matrix: Error Categories vs Reporting Path

| Error Type | Definition | Reporting Pathway |
|---|---|---|
| Medication Error | Any preventable event that may cause or lead to inappropriate medication use | Incident report within 24 h, root-cause analysis |
| Adverse Drug Event (ADE) | Any harm experienced by a patient related to drug therapy | Documented in chart + pharmacovigilance form |
| Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR) | A noxious, unintended response at normal doses | Yellow Card / DOH pharmacovigilance system |
| Near Miss | Error caught before reaching the patient | Anonymous reporting encouraged, no blame |
| LASA Confusion | Look-Alike, Sound-Alike drug mix-up | Tall-man lettering on MAR, separated storage |

#### Routes, Needles, and Volumes

| Route | Gauge | Angle | Site Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intradermal (ID) | 25–27 G | 10–15° | Forearm, upper back (TB, allergy) |
| Subcutaneous (SC) | 25–30 G | 45–90° | Abdomen, thigh, upper arm (insulin, heparin) |
| Intramuscular (IM) | 20–23 G | 90° | Vastus lateralis, ventrogluteal, deltoid |
| Z-track IM | 20–23 G | 90° | Iron dextran, irritating solutions |
| IV bolus / infusion | 18–22 G | — | Peripheral or central line, aseptic non-touch technique |

#### Paediatric Calculation Walk-Through

A 14 kg child is prescribed paracetamol at 15 mg/kg orally. Stock suspension is 120 mg/5 mL. Dose needed = 14 × 15 = 210 mg. Volume = (210 × 5) / 120 = 8.75 mL. The prescription is checked against the paediatric reference range (10–15 mg/kg per dose), and a second nurse independently confirms the volume before administration. Aseptic non-touch technique applies to oral syringes used in paediatrics, particularly for nasogastric routes.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Confusing mg with mL is the highest-yield failure mode in DOH numeracy stations. Always cross-check the stock concentration (mg per mL) and write the final volume on the MAR, not the dose alone.

#### IV Infusion Rate Worked Problem

A patient is to receive 500 mL of normal saline over 4 hours using a giving set with a drop factor of 20 gtt/mL. Total minutes = 240. Drops per minute = (500 × 20) / 240 = 41.67, rounded to 42 gtt/min. Convert to mL/h for a volumetric pump: 500 / 4 = 125 mL/h. Document the calculation, infusion start time, and check the site every hour for phlebitis, extravasation, and fluid balance.

For titrated vasoactive infusions, the standard relation is:

| Climb | Support |
|---|---|
| mL/h = Ordered dose (mcg/kg/min) × Weight (kg) × 60 / Concentration (mcg/mL) | Dopamine, heparin, nitroglycerin titrations |

Renal dose adjustment uses Cockcroft–Gault creatinine clearance: [(140 − Age in years) × Weight in kg] / [72 × Serum creatinine in mg/dL], multiplied by 0.85 for female patients, giving clearance in mL/min.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

Edge cases dominate advanced DOH stations. A patient on **narrow therapeutic index** drugs — digoxin, lithium, warfarin, theophylline, gentamicin — requires trough and peak level monitoring alongside clinical assessment for toxicity signs: nausea, visual halos, tinnitus, bleeding, ototoxicity. **Polypharmacy** in elderly patients (≥ 5 chronic medications) raises the ADE risk geometrically, and a **Best Possible Medication History (BPMH)** at every transition of care is the DOH-recommended reconciliation step.

#### Advanced Traps and Exceptions

1. **Look-Alike Sound-Alike (LASA) drugs** must use tall-man lettering on the MAR and physically separated storage. Examples routinely tested: **DOPamine** vs **DOBUTamine**, **hydrALAZINE** vs **hydrOXYzine**, **clonazePAM** vs **cloNIDine**.
2. **High-alert double-checks** apply to insulin (units only, never "U"), heparin flushes, IV opioids, concentrated potassium, and paediatric chemotherapy — a second licensed nurse's co-signature is non-negotiable.
3. **PRN documentation** must record the indication, pre-administration assessment, time given, and post-administration response within 30 minutes — failure to document the response is the most common PRN audit finding.
4. **STAT and single-dose orders** auto-expire: STAT within 5–15 minutes, single-dose within 24 hours. Carrying a STAT order to the next shift without re-ordering is a system error.
5. **Refusal of medication** is a patient right. Document the refusal, the information provided, and notify the prescriber — coercive re-administration is an ethical violation.
6. **Controlled substances** in the UAE require a separate register, witness countersignature on wastage, and secure double-locked storage per DOH controlled-drug regulations.
7. **Bar-Code Medication Administration (BCMA)** is the technology layer that catches most Right-Patient and Right-Drug failures; bypassing the scanner voids the safety net.

#### Adjacent Topics Worth Linking

Medication safety overlaps with **anaphylaxis management** (IM adrenaline 0.5 mg adults, 0.01 mg/kg paediatric), **hypoglycaemia reversal** (15 g rapid carbohydrate, recheck in 15 min), **opioid-induced respiratory depression** (naloxone titration), and **anticoagulation monitoring** (INR for warfarin, aPTT for unfractionated heparin). Renal dosing connects directly to fluid and electrolyte management; pharmacovigilance connects to incident-reporting and root-cause analysis.

#### Practice Prompts

1. A 22 kg child is prescribed vancomycin 15 mg/kg IV every 8 hours. Stock is 500 mg in 10 mL. Calculate the volume per dose, the dilution volume needed for a 5 mg/mL final concentration, and the infusion duration required to avoid red-man syndrome.
2. A patient with a creatinine clearance of 28 mL/min is admitted on metformin 1 g BD. Identify the prescribing concern, the recommended action, and the alternative monitoring you would suggest to the prescriber before administration.
