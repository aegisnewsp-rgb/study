# s-Block

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The **s-block** houses Group 1 (alkali metals, Li→Fr) and Group 2 (alkaline earth metals, Be→Ra), whose valence electrons fill the outermost **ns¹** or **ns²** orbital. Low ionisation enthalpy, strong electropositivity, and a steady **decrease in ionisation enthalpy, electronegativity, and melting point down each group** define the family.

| Property | Group 1 (ns¹) | Group 2 (ns²) |
|---|---|---|
| Valence configuration | ns¹ | ns² |
| Common oxidation state | +1 | +2 |
| Flame colour | Na-yellow, K-violet, Li-crimson | Ca-brick red, Sr-crimson, Ba-apple green |
| Typical oxide | O²⁻ / O₂²⁻ / O₂⁻ | O²⁻ (Ba also forms BaO₂) |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** "**LiNa K Ruby C*s F*renzy**" → Group 1 order Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs, Fr. For flame colours remember **"Li-Crimson, Na-Yellow, K-Violet (see through Blue glass), Rb-Red, Cs-Blue"**, and for Group 2 **"Ca-Brick, Sr-Crimson, Ba-Apple green"**.

NEET typically asks 1–2 MCQs from this block, mostly on anomalous behaviour of Li/Be and flame colours.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Electronic Configuration and Periodic Trends

Group 1 elements adopt **[Noble gas] ns¹** and Group 2 adopt **[Noble gas] ns²**. Because the differentiating electron enters an s-orbital that is poorly shielded, the trends are sharp: atomic and ionic radii **increase down the group**, while **first ionisation enthalpy (IE₁, kJ mol⁻¹) decreases** and so does electronegativity (Pauling scale).

A useful relation governing salt solubility is the **thermodynamic cycle**:

> ΔH_solution = ΔH_lattice + ΔH_hydration

where ΔH is in kJ mol⁻¹. A salt dissolves freely only when the **exothermic hydration enthalpy outweighs the endothermic lattice enthalpy**.

#### Anomalous Behaviour and Diagonal Relationship

**Li** and **Be** show properties deviant from their own groups because of their small radii, high charge density, and high polarising power. The diagonal relationship arises because polarising power of Li⁺ ≈ that of Mg²⁺, and of Be²⁺ ≈ that of Al³⁺.

| Anomalous property | Li (vs Na) | Be (vs Mg) |
|---|---|---|
| Nature of oxide/hydroxide | Covalent Li₂O; amphoteric LiOH | Covalent BeO; amphoteric Be(OH)₂ |
| Reaction with N₂ | Forms Li₃N | Forms Be₃N₂ |
| Carbonate stability | Li₂CO₃ decomposes on heating | BeCO₃ decomposes easily |
| Diagonal partner | Mg | Al |

#### Oxides, Peroxides, Superoxides

- **Na** burns in excess O₂ to give the peroxide **Na₂O₂** (O₂²⁻).
- **K, Rb, Cs** form superoxides **MO₂** (O₂⁻), e.g. KO₂.
- **Ba** forms peroxide **BaO₂**.
- Normal oxides **M₂O / MO** are strongly basic; basicity **decreases** from Group 1 to Group 2 (LiOH < NaOH for basic strength in solution, but Be(OH)₂ is amphoteric).

> 📌 **Formula Check:** Hydration enthalpy ΔH_hyd (kJ mol⁻¹) decreases down each group: Li⁺ ≈ −519, Na⁺ ≈ −406, K⁺ ≈ −322. Hence Li salts with small anions (F⁻, CO₃²⁻) are sparingly soluble.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** Among the following, the salt that is **sparingly soluble in water** despite belonging to Group 1 is:
(a) NaCl (b) KBr (c) Li₂CO₃ (d) CsNO₃

#### Solution:
Step 1 — Identify the rule: Most Group 1 salts are freely soluble because their hydration enthalpy exceeds the lattice enthalpy.
Step 2 — Apply the exception: For **Li⁺**, the small ionic radius (~76 pm) gives an exceptionally **high ΔH_hyd ≈ −519 kJ mol⁻¹**, but with a small, high-charge-density anion like **CO₃²⁻**, the **lattice enthalpy of Li₂CO₃ is even higher** (>2200 kJ mol⁻¹).
Step 3 — Apply ΔH_solution = ΔH_lattice + ΔH_hyd; the sum is **positive**, so dissolution is non-spontaneous.
**Answer: (c) Li₂CO₃**.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students often assume *all* alkali metal salts are soluble. The rule has three named exceptions — **LiF, Li₂CO₃, Li₃PO₄** — and these appear almost every year in assertion-reason items.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Solubility Contrasts within Group 2

Group 2 violates the "down-the-group similarity" pattern sharply:

| Salt | Be salt | Mg salt | Ca salt | Sr salt | Ba salt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hydroxide M(OH)₂ | Amphoteric, insoluble | Insoluble | Sparingly soluble | Soluble | Strongly soluble |
| Sulphate MSO₄ | Soluble | Soluble | Sparingly soluble | Insoluble | Insoluble |
| Carbonate MCO₃ | Decomposes <100 °C | Decomposes ~540 °C | Stable to ~825 °C | Stable to ~1100 °C | Stable >1300 °C |

The reversal is thermodynamic: for **hydroxides**, ΔH_hyd dominates (decreasing down the group less sharply than lattice enthalpy for small anions), so solubility **increases** down the group. For **sulphates**, the large SO₄²⁻ anion's lattice enthalpy decreases down the group faster than ΔH_hyd, so solubility **decreases**.

#### Thermal Stability of Nitrates vs Carbonates

A frequently missed point:

1. Group 2 **carbonates**: stability ↑ down the group (BeCO₃ < MgCO₃ < CaCO₃ < SrCO₃ < BaCO₃).
2. Group 2 **nitrates**: stability **↓** down the group because the larger cation cannot stabilise the large NO₃⁻ as effectively against thermal decomposition.

#### Flame Test Mechanics

When heated in a Bunsen flame, valence electrons of s-block atoms are excited to higher orbitals. On returning, they emit photons whose wavelength lies in the visible region. Filter the K flame through **cobalt blue glass** to cut out the yellow Na contamination that obscures the violet K line at 766.5 nm.

#### Common Pitfalls in NEET Items

1. Assuming **Be(OH)₂ is basic** — it is **amphoteric**, reacting with excess NaOH to give **[Be(OH)₄]²⁻**.
2. Confusing **peroxide (O₂²⁻)** with **superoxide (O₂⁻)** — check the alkali metal: Na → peroxide, K/Rb/Cs → superoxide.
3. Stating that Li resembles Na — for covalent oxide, nitride, and complex hydride behaviour, Li resembles **Mg** (diagonal relationship), not Na.
4. Forgetting the **biological angle**: Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase maintains the membrane potential; Ca²⁺ forms **hydroxyapatite** in bones; Mg²⁺ is the central ion of **chlorophyll a/b**.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. **Assertion–Reason:** "Assertion: BeCO₃ decomposes at a lower temperature than BaCO₃. Reason: Be²⁺ has higher polarising power than Ba²⁺." Decide whether both statements are true and whether the reason correctly explains the assertion. *(Answer: both true; reason is correct — high polarisation by Be²⁺ destabilises the carbonate.)*
2. **Numerical:** Calculate the molarity of a solution prepared by dissolving **4.0 g of NaOH** (M = 40 g mol⁻¹) in **250 mL** of water.
*M = (4.0 × 1000)/(40 × 250) = **0.4 mol L⁻¹**.*

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** NEET regularly frames oxide identification as a two-step question — first identify whether the compound is a peroxide or superoxide (by checking the alkali metal), then balance the equation. Confusing **2 Na₂O₂ + 2 CO₂ → 2 Na₂CO₃ + O₂** (peroxide) with **4 KO₂ + 2 CO₂ → 2 K₂CO₃ + 3 O₂** (superoxide) costs marks every year.
