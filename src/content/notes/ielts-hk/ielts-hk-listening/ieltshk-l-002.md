# Part 2 — Social Monologue — IELTS Listening (Hong Kong) Notes

Part 2 of the IELTS Listening test (administered in Hong Kong by the British Council or IDP) is a monologue set in an everyday social context — speech about local facilities, a guided tour, arrangements for an event, or descriptions of places and procedures. The recording runs about 5-7 minutes and has 10 questions. Tests plan/map/diagram labelling, summary completion, sentence completion, multiple choice.

> Verify the live Listening format on https://ielts.org/ before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Format:** 10 questions.
- **Transcript style:** one speaker, monologue (not a conversation). Common contexts: a tour guide describing a place, a manager explaining facilities, an event organiser describing arrangements.
- **Test types:** plan/map/diagram labelling, summary completion, sentence completion, multiple choice, matching.
- **Order:** questions follow the recording in order.
- **Predictable content:** locations, features, schedules, prices, facilities.
- **Signposting language:** the speaker uses phrases like "first of all", "next", "moving on", "finally" to structure the monologue.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing the order of items in a list (the speaker may mention an item out of order).
- Missing a key word in a fast-spoken phrase.
- Forgetting to follow the word limit on completion items.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Common Part 2 contexts

- **A tour or visit:** a tour guide describes the history, layout, and features of a place (museum, university campus, historic site).
- **A facility description:** a manager explains the facilities, opening hours, and rules of a community centre, sports club, or library.
- **An event announcement:** an organiser describes the schedule, location, and arrangements for a festival, conference, or community event.
- **A process description:** a presenter explains how a service works (e.g. recycling collection, public transport).

#### Question types in Part 2

- **Plan/map/diagram labelling:** label a plan, map, or diagram with words from the recording. Test your spatial reasoning and ability to follow location descriptions.
- **Summary completion:** complete a summary of the recording using words from the recording within a stated word limit.
- **Sentence completion:** complete sentences with words from the recording.
- **Multiple choice:** choose one of three options.
- **Matching:** match items (e.g. features to locations) from a list.

#### Preparation strategy

1. **Predict:** read the questions carefully. For map labelling, study the map first; for completion items, identify the type of information needed.
2. **Listen for signposting language:** the speaker uses transition words to indicate the order of items.
3. **Visualise:** for map/diagram items, mentally trace the speaker's description.
4. **Check:** after the recording ends, check spelling and word limits.

#### Signposting language to listen for

- **Order/sequence:** first of all, then, next, after that, finally, lastly.
- **Location:** on the left, on the right, opposite, next to, behind, in front of, at the end of.
- **Addition:** also, in addition, furthermore, what is more.
- **Contrast:** however, although, on the other hand, but.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-paper links

- **Part 1**: transactional conversation in an everyday context.
- **Part 3**: academic discussion; higher vocabulary and complexity.
- **Speaking Part 2**: a 1-2 minute monologue on a given topic.

#### Exam technique

- For map/diagram labelling, label the items in the order you hear them; this minimises the risk of confusion.
- For summary completion, identify the keywords (e.g. "the main advantage is...") and listen for the answer.
- For multiple choice, eliminate distractors that are mentioned in the recording but do not match the question.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "I can use synonyms in completion items." No — you must use words from the recording. The exact word is required.
- "Maps are labelled left-to-right." No — maps are labelled in the order the speaker describes the items.
