# Arithmetic Operations

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Arithmetic operations are the four basic procedures — **addition (+)**, **subtraction (−)**, **multiplication (×)**, and **division (÷)** — used to combine, compare, partition, or scale numerical quantities in the TPA section.

- **Order of operations (PEMDAS/BODMAS):** Parentheses → Exponents → Multiplication/Division (left to right) → Addition/Subtraction (left to right).
- **Commutative property** holds for + and × only; (8 − 3) ≠ (3 − 8).
- **Sign rule for integers:** like signs yield positive, unlike signs yield negative.

| Operation | Inverse | Sign rule (integers) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Addition (+) | Subtraction | Keep sign of larger absolute value |
| Subtraction (−) | Addition | Convert: a − b = a + (−b) |
| Multiplication (×) | Division | (+)(+) = +; (−)(−) = +; (+)(−) = − |
| Division (÷) | Multiplication | Same sign rule as multiplication |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Definitions

Each of the four operations has named parts. In **a + b = c**, *a* and *b* are addends and *c* is the sum. In **a − b = c**, *a* is the minuend, *b* the subtrahend, and *c* the difference. In **a × b = c**, both *a* and *b* are factors and *c* is the product. In **a ÷ b = c**, *a* is the dividend, *b* the divisor (with b ≠ 0), and *c* the quotient. These labels matter because UI Entrance word problems frequently use phrases such as "kurangi dari" (subtract from), which determines which value is the minuend.

#### Arithmetic Properties

Three properties govern how operations combine. The **commutative property** states a + b = b + a and a × b = b × a — valid for addition and multiplication only. The **associative property** lets you regroup without changing the result: (a + b) + c = a + (b + c). The **distributive property** ties multiplication to addition: a × (b + c) = a × b + a × c. Recognising these lets you simplify mental arithmetic quickly.

#### Order of Operations

When an expression mixes operations, the universal rule PEMDAS applies: resolve **P**arentheses first, then **E**xponents, then **M**ultiplication and **D**ivision from left to right, then **A**ddition and **S**ubtraction from left to right.

- Expression: 6 + 2 × 3 = 6 + 6 = 12 (not 24).
- Nested case: (8 − 3) × 4 + 2² ÷ 2 = 5 × 4 + 4 ÷ 2 = 20 + 2 = 22.
- Fraction case: ½ + ⅓ = (1·3 + 1·2) / (2·3) = 5/6 (common denominator 6).

#### Percentage Conversion

A percentage is simply a fraction with denominator 100, so a% = a/100. To compute a percentage change, the formula is **(new − old) / old × 100%**, always using the original value as the base.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Integer Arithmetic and Sign Rules

Integer multiplication and division obey a strict sign rule: a positive times a positive is positive, a negative times a negative is positive, and unlike signs give a negative. This rule cascades through chained operations, so even numbers of negative factors yield a positive result. Subtraction of a negative is best rewritten as addition: 7 − (−3) = 7 + 3 = 10.

#### Fraction and Decimal Mechanics

To add or subtract fractions a/b and c/d, first find a **common denominator** (typically bd or the LCD), then apply (ad ± bc) / bd. Multiplication is direct: a/b × c/d = ac / bd. Division inverts the divisor: a/b ÷ c/d = a/b × d/c = ad / bc. Converting decimals to fractions uses place value: 0.35 = 35/100 = 7/20.

#### Percentage Change — Worked Example

A laptop's price rises from Rp 4,000,000 to Rp 4,600,000. Compute the increase.

> Percentage change = (new − old) / old × 100%

Plugging in: (4,600,000 − 4,000,000) / 4,000,000 × 100% = 600,000 / 4,000,000 × 100% = 15%. The 15% increase is measured against the original Rp 4,000,000, not the new price.

#### Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
| --- | --- |
| Evaluating 6 + 2 × 3 as 24 | Apply PEMDAS: multiply before adding → 12 |
| Forgetting b ≠ 0 in a ÷ b | Division by zero is undefined; reject any answer with b = 0 |
| Treating −3 × −4 as −12 | Two negatives multiply to +12 |
| Computing % change against new value | Always use the original as denominator |
| Adding fractions without common denominator | Convert to a/b = (ka)/(kb) first, then add numerators |

#### Exam Strategy for TPA

The UI Entrance Academic Potential section carries this topic at roughly **3%** weight, but arithmetic is the engine behind word problems on ratios, averages, profit/loss, and simple algebra. Allocate about 45 seconds per numeric item; if a calculation stalls, mark and move on, since later questions are often easier.

1. Evaluate (18 − 6 ÷ 2) × 3 + 4² without using a calculator, applying PEMDAS step by step.
