# What the 2010 Constitution Does

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The Constitution of Kenya 2010 is the supreme law, promulgated on **27 August 2010** after the 4 August 2010 referendum. It replaced the 1963 independence Constitution and reorganised Kenya from a parliamentary system into a **presidential, bicameral, devolved State** built on a justiciable Bill of Rights and Chapter Six integrity rules.

- **Supremacy (Article 2):** Any law or act inconsistent with the Constitution is void.
- **Bill of Rights (Chapter Four):** Enforceable through the High Court under Article 22.
- **Devolution (Chapter Eleven):** 47 counties receive powers, functions and revenue.
- **Bicameral Parliament (Chapter Eight):** National Assembly + Senate; only the National Assembly passes money Bills.
- **Chapter Six:** Leadership and integrity test binding all State officers.

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Supremacy | Constitution overrides every other law |
| Devolution | Transfer of power to 47 elected county governments |
| Bicameral | Two-House Parliament: National Assembly + Senate |
| Chapter Six | Mandatory integrity and ethics code for State officers |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"S-B-D-C-S-I"** — *Supreme law, Bill of Rights, Devolved counties, Chapter Six integrity, Separation of powers, Independent commissions.* Six functions, one Constitution.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Structural Shifts From 1963

The 2010 Constitution re-engineers the State on four axes: leadership form, rights enforcement, territorial governance and integrity standards. Kenya moved from a Westminster-style parliamentary system with strong Prime-Ministerial powers to a presidential system with a directly elected Executive President (Chapter Fifteen). A unicameral Parliament became **bicameral**, with the National Assembly representing constituencies and the Senate protecting county interests. Eight provinces were abolished and replaced by 47 counties running their own governments under Article 176.

#### Articles That Anchor the Framework

| Article / Chapter | Function | Consequence if Ignored |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Article 2 | Supremacy + international-law incorporation | Unconstitutional statutes stay on the statute book |
| Article 22 | Bill of Rights access via High Court | Rights violations have no remedy |
| Article 24 | Limitation test on rights | Blanket restrictions become legal |
| Chapter 11 (Art. 174–200) | Devolution to 47 counties | Service delivery collapses to the centre |
| Article 114 | Senate barred from money Bills | Senate wrongly "passes" the Appropriations Act |
| Chapter Six (Art. 73–80) | Integrity of State officers | Corrupt officials remain in office |

#### Key Independent Commissions Established

- **IEBC** — Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (Article 88).
- **JSC** — Judicial Service Commission (Article 171).
- **SRC** — Salaries and Remuneration Commission (Article 230).
- **KNHRC** — Kenya National Human Rights and Equality Commission (Article 59).
- **CAJ** — Commission on Administrative Justice (Ombudsman, Article 59).
- **NCIC** — National Cohesion and Integration Commission (Article 59).

> 📌 **Formula Check:** Article 2(5) auto-incorporates general rules of international law; Article 2(6) requires any treaty to be ratified by Parliament before domestic binding effect.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** A student claims that the Senate of Kenya can pass the Appropriations Act because both Houses debate national revenue. Which constitutional provision defeats this claim?

#### Solution:
- The 2010 Constitution assigns money-Bill monopoly to the **National Assembly** under Article 114.
- The Senate can only debate, amend or recommend within **14 days**, after which the National Assembly can proceed.
- Senate power over county revenue (Article 217 Division of Revenue Act) is a separate function, not a national money Bill.
- Therefore, the student's claim is defeated by **Article 114(1)**, read with the Senate's role under Article 217.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Examiners test whether you confuse the Senate's *county revenue* role with the National Assembly's *national money-Bill* monopoly. Always name **Article 114** when the issue is the Appropriations Act or a Finance Bill.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Boundary Conditions

1. **Citizenship anomaly:** The 2010 Constitution (Article 14) recognises **dual citizenship**, a sharp break from the 1963 single-citizenship rule. Kenyans by birth who acquired foreign citizenship before 2010 had five years to declare.
2. **Article 24 limitation test:** A right can be limited only if the limit is *reasonable and justifiable* in an open, democratic society based on human dignity, equality and freedom — the test courts apply in *R v Cabinet Secretary, Ministry of Health ex parte Karanja & 2 others*.
3. **Schedule Six transitional clauses:** Judges, magistrates and State officers appointed under the old Constitution were vetted under the *Judicial Service Act, 2011* and Chapter Six before confirmation.
4. **Article 261 enforcement orders:** Where Parliament fails to enact legislation required by the Constitution, courts can issue orders — the *Intersex Persons Act, 2018* is a direct output of such an order.
5. **Article 81 electoral system:** Combines simple majority (single-member constituencies) with proportional representation (party lists), producing the *2/3 gender rule* test under Article 81(b).
6. **Devolution revenue floor:** Article 203 requires national revenue shared to counties to be **at least 15%**, escalating to **20%** by 2030 under the *Public Finance Management (PFM) Act, 2012* formula.

#### Common Traps Candidates Sit

| Misconception | Correct Position |
| --- | --- |
| The President appoints judges alone | Article 166 requires JSC recommendation and National Assembly approval |
| The Senate controls all public funds | Senate controls only the Division of Revenue Act; National Assembly controls national money Bills |
| Devolution created provinces | Devolution created 47 counties; provinces were abolished |
| International law needs an Act of Parliament | Only *treaties* do; general international law enters automatically under Article 2(5) |
| Chapter Six applies only to politicians | Chapter Six binds every State officer, including public servants and judges |

1. Trace how Article 2 supremacy, Article 22 enforcement and Article 24 limitations combine to make the Bill of Rights justiciable in the High Court.
2. Using Article 174, list four objectives of devolution and match each to a specific function in Schedule Four.
