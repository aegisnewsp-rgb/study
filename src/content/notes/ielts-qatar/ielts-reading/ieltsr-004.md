# Identifying Writer's Views/Claims (Yes/No/Not Given)

> Verify the live Yes/No/Not Given question format and the distinction from True/False/Not Given on https://ielts.org/ Identifying Writer's Views/Claims question type guide and the British Council / IDP preparation pages before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Format:** a set of statements about the writer's views or claims; the candidate decides for each statement whether the writer holds this view (Yes), holds the opposite view (No), or expresses no view at all (Not Given).
- **Where it appears:** typically on Passage 2 or Passage 3 (the harder passages, often academic or argumentative in tone). Usually 4-6 statements per test (~10-15% of the 40-question total).
- **Order:** statements follow the passage in order. Statement 1 covers early content, Statement 6 covers late content.
- **Source of truth:** the WRITER'S VIEW, not the real world, not other sources, not facts stated in the passage. The candidate asks: **does the writer believe, claim, or assert this?**
- **Marking:** +1 correct, 0 incorrect (no negative marking). Spelling errors are accepted if the meaning is unchanged.

#### The three labels — what each really means

| Label | Meaning | Test |
|---|---|---|
| **Yes** | The WRITER CLAIMS this view. The passage states or implies that the writer believes/holds/asserts the statement. | Does the writer explicitly or implicitly agree? |
| **No** | The WRITER DOES NOT CLAIM this view. The writer holds the OPPOSITE view. | Does the writer explicitly or implicitly disagree? |
| **Not Given** | The WRITER EXPRESSES NO VIEW. The passage does not state whether the writer believes or disbelieves the statement. | Does the writer say nothing about this? |

#### The critical distinction: Yes/No/Not Given vs True/False/Not Given

| Question type | Tests | Source of truth |
|---|---|---|
| **True/False/Not Given** (ieltsr-003) | FACT (does the statement agree with the passage?) | The PASSAGE — what it says |
| **Yes/No/Not Given** (ieltsr-004) | VIEW (does the writer believe this?) | The WRITER — what they believe |

| Statement | True/False/Not Given | Yes/No/Not Given |
|---|---|---|
| "Doha is the capital of Qatar." | True (passage says so) | Not Given (passage may report fact; writer may not have a view) |
| "Education City has improved Qatar's research output." | True if passage says it | Yes if writer believes it; No if writer thinks otherwise; Not Given if writer expresses no view |
| "Carbon emissions will rise over the next decade." | True if passage states this | Yes if writer believes it; No if writer thinks otherwise; Not Given if writer expresses no view |

The crucial insight: **Yes/No/Not Given tests the writer's OPINION, not the passage's facts.** A statement can be factually TRUE (the passage says it) but the writer may not hold it as a view.

---

#### Examiner traps

- **Real-world knowledge contamination.** A statement can be factually TRUE in the real world (e.g. "Education City hosts US branch campuses") but the writer may not explicitly hold this as a VIEW. The label could be Not Given if the passage reports it neutrally.
- **The writer reports a fact ≠ the writer believes a fact.** Many passages present factual claims without endorsing them. The candidate who marks Yes because the fact appears in the passage fails the question.
- **Quotation trap.** The writer may quote a researcher who disagrees with the writer's view. The candidate must answer based on the writer's view, not the quoted researcher's view.
- **Hedge words.** "However," "although," "but," "yet" signal that the writer holds a view OPPOSITE to the previous claim. The label is No.
- **Modal verbs.** "Could," "might," "may," "perhaps" signal that the writer expresses no firm view. The label is Not Given.
- **Confusion with True/False.** Yes/No is about VIEW; True/False is about FACT. Confusing the two is the most common error.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### The 7-step strategy for Yes/No/Not Given

A measured 7-step approach to answering Yes/No/Not Given statements in 30-45 seconds each:

1. **Read the statement stem (5 sec).** Identify the SUBJECT (what the statement is about) and the VIEW being claimed (what the writer is alleged to believe).
2. **Identify the keywords (5 sec).** Underline (in your mind) the content words in the statement: nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs that capture the alleged view.
3. **Locate the relevant paragraph (10 sec).** Use the statement's order to find the paragraph that contains the writer's view. Statement 1 is early; Statement 6 is later.
4. **Identify the writer's tone (15 sec).** Read the paragraph carefully for opinion signals. Is the writer asserting, agreeing, disagreeing, or remaining neutral?
5. **Compare the statement to the writer's view (10 sec).** Match the statement's view to the writer's actual view. Identify agreements, oppositions, and silences.
6. **Choose the label (5 sec).**
   - **Yes** if the writer agrees (explicitly or implicitly).
   - **No** if the writer disagrees (explicitly or implicitly).
   - **Not Given** if the writer expresses no view.
7. **Mark and move on (5 sec).** Mark the answer, then move to the next statement. Do not second-guess once you have decided.

#### The opinion-signal lexicon

The candidate who reads for facts instead of views loses. Train with a daily drill: read a paragraph and write down every opinion signal.

| Signal type | Words/phrases |
|---|---|
| **Strong assertion** | "I believe," "I am convinced," "in my view," "clearly," "undoubtedly," "without doubt," "must be," "absolutely" |
| **Soft assertion** | "I think," "I would argue," "I would suggest," "it seems to me," "in my opinion" |
| **Hedge** | "perhaps," "maybe," "could," "might," "may," "it is possible" |
| **Disagreement markers** | "however," "but," "although," "yet," "nevertheless," "nonetheless," "on the contrary," "conversely" |
| **Strong disagreement** | "I disagree," "I reject," "this is wrong," "this is incorrect," "fails to acknowledge," "overlooks" |
| **Reporting (not endorsing)** | "according to," "X argues," "X claims," "X believes," "X states" — note that these report someone else's view; the writer's view may differ |

#### Distinguishing writer-view from reported-fact

IELTS passages often mix facts and views. The candidate must distinguish between the writer's view and reported facts:

| Passage says... | Writer's view? |
|---|---|
| "Carbon emissions rose by 5% in 2024." | Report a fact. The writer's view is on whether this is GOOD or BAD. |
| "Carbon emissions rose by 5% in 2024, and this trend is concerning." | The writer believes the trend is CONCERNING. |
| "Some researchers argue that emissions will continue to rise." | Report others' view. The writer's view is what THEY believe, not what the researchers believe. |
| "Emissions will continue to rise, but the writer notes that..." | The writer DISAGREES with the prediction. |
| "Education City has improved Qatar's research output." | Report a fact. The writer's view is on whether this is GOOD. |
| "Education City has improved Qatar's research output, which is welcome." | The writer believes the improvement is WELCOME. |
| "Education City has improved Qatar's research output, yet the situation is not yet solved." | The writer believes the situation is incomplete. |

The crucial insight: **the writer's view is what they EXPRESS, not what they REPORT.**

### 🔴 Deep — Mastery (2mo+)

#### The cognitive architecture of Yes/No/Not Given

Yes/No/Not Given is the question type that tests the candidate's ability to read for VIEW rather than FACT. Most candidates confuse Yes/No with True/False, and within Yes/No, confuse No with Not Given.

A candidate scoring below 7 on Yes/No/Not Given typically has a writer-view-recognition gap, a hedge-word-recognition gap, or a quoted-view-separation gap. The deeper intervention is to read for OPINION SIGNALS first, then for the writer's view, and to recognise that reported facts do NOT constitute the writer's view.

#### Practice strategy for Yes/No/Not Given

1. **Use the British Council and IDP official IELTS practice tests.** The question styles and difficulty are calibrated against the live test.
2. **Practise with a stopwatch.** A 6-statement Yes/No/Not Given set should take 3-4 minutes. Without practice, candidates take 5-7 minutes.
3. **Read for opinion signals first.** Build the habit of underlining (in your mind) the opinion-signal words (clearly, undoubtedly, however, although, but, I believe, I disagree).
4. **Distinguish writer-view from reported-fact.** A statement that reports a fact reported in the passage is Not Given, not Yes — unless the writer endorses the fact as their view.
5. **Distinguish Yes/No from True/False.** Yes/No is about VIEW; True/False is about FACT. Confusing the two is the most common error.
6. **Distinguish No from Not Given.** No = the writer holds the OPPOSITE view; Not Given = the writer expresses NO view.
7. **Practise with Qatari-context passages.** Al Jazeera English editorials, Gulf Times opinion pieces, QU research commentary, QF program descriptions, Education City faculty opinion pieces build the local opinion-signal patterns you will encounter on test day.

#### When Yes/No/Not Given marks reflect a deeper issue

- **Persistent Yes/No confusion with True/False:** the indicator is view-vs-fact discrimination. Train with a daily 5-minute "is this a view or a fact?" drill.
- **Persistent No/Not Given confusion:** the indicator is disagreement-vs-silence recognition. Train with a daily 5-minute "does the writer DISAGREE or remain SILENT?" drill.
- **Persistent real-world-knowledge errors:** the indicator is the candidate's over-reliance on prior knowledge. Train with a daily drill: read statements about a passage and explicitly say "I must answer based on the writer's view, not real-world knowledge."
- **Persistent reported-fact errors:** the indicator is reporting-vs-endorsing discrimination. Train with a daily drill: read a passage and identify which sentences report facts (no writer view) vs. which express the writer's view.
- **Persistent quotation errors:** the indicator is writer-vs-quoted-source separation. Train with a daily drill: read a passage and identify which sentences express the writer's view vs. which quote another source.

#### Edge cases and known traps

1. **Reported facts are not views.** If the passage reports a fact but the writer does not endorse it, the label is Not Given.
2. **Quoted views are not the writer's view.** If the writer quotes a researcher who holds a view, but the writer's view is different, the label is based on the writer's view.
3. **Hedge words indicate no firm view.** "Perhaps," "maybe," "could," "might," "may" indicate the writer is not committing to the view. The label is often Not Given.
4. **Disagreement markers indicate opposite views.** "However," "but," "although," "yet" indicate the writer holds a view OPPOSITE to the previous claim. The label is No.
5. **Modal verbs may indicate possibility.** "Could," "might" may indicate the writer believes the statement is possible. The label could be Yes, No, or Not Given depending on context.
6. **Reported speech.** "X argues that..." reports X's view, not the writer's view. The writer's view is separate.
7. **The writer's view may evolve through the passage.** Statement 1 may reflect the writer's initial view; Statement 6 may reflect a later, more considered view. Use the order.
8. **No negative marking.** Guess if you do not know the answer. A blank answer is guaranteed wrong; a guessed answer is 33% likely right.
9. **Spelling is forgiving.** A spelling error that does not change the meaning is accepted. A missing letter is not.
10. **American vs British spelling.** IELTS accepts either American or British spelling as long as it is consistent within a single answer.

### Pro Tips — Identifying Writer's Views/Claims (Yes/No/Not Given)

1. **Compare the statement to the writer's view, not to your knowledge.** IELTS Reading tests the candidate's ability to read the writer's view — not to validate real-world facts.
2. **Read for opinion signals first.** Underline (in your mind) the opinion-signal words (clearly, undoubtedly, however, although, but, I believe, I disagree). These signal where the writer's view is.
3. **Distinguish writer-view from reported-fact.** A reported fact is not a writer-view. The label is Not Given if the passage reports the fact but the writer does not endorse it.
4. **Distinguish Yes/No from True/False.** Yes/No is about VIEW; True/False is about FACT. Confusing the two is the most common error.
5. **Distinguish No from Not Given.** No = the writer DISAGREES (holds the opposite view); Not Given = the writer EXPRESSES NO VIEW.
6. **Watch for hedge words ("perhaps," "maybe," "could," "might," "may").** These signal the writer is not committing to the view. The label is often Not Given.
7. **Watch for disagreement markers ("however," "but," "although," "yet").** These signal the writer DISAGREES. The label is No.
8. **Use the order.** Statement 1 covers early in the passage; Statement 6 covers later.
9. **Don't second-guess.** Mark the answer and move on. Over-thinking causes more errors than under-thinking.
10. **Practise with Qatari-context passages.** Al Jazeera English editorials, Gulf Times opinion pieces, QU research commentary, QF program descriptions, Education City faculty opinion pieces cut cognitive load on test day.
