# News Report Selections — OSSLT Reading

News Report is one of the four reading selection types on the OSSLT. The EQAO Framework describes it as inverted-pyramid news writing on a current event, with the most important information at the top. News Report selections are paired with multiple-select items that target the five Ws (who/what/when/where/why), the lead, supporting facts, and accompanying graphic elements.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### What a News Report selection looks like

- A short news article written in inverted-pyramid style — most important information first, background next, supporting details last.
- A headline and a byline (sometimes with a dateline).
- A clear lead paragraph that answers who, what, when, where, why (and sometimes how).
- Supporting paragraphs with quotes, statistics, or background.
- Often paired with a captioned photo, a sidebar, a small table, or a chart.

#### How News Report items are organised

- Reading 1 items: identify the lead, locate a stated detail (who/what/when/where), recall a quoted fact, read a graphic element.
- Reading 2 items: infer the writer's purpose (to inform, to alert, to advocate), infer the meaning of a quoted phrase, infer why the writer placed a detail where it did.
- Reading 3 items: connect the news event to a real-world situation or to another selection on the test.
- One conventions item: a sentence-level edit, often a punctuation or subject-verb agreement fix.

#### Examiner traps

- Treating the headline as a peripheral detail. The headline is the most concise statement of the lead. Many Reading 1 items can be answered from the headline alone.
- Confusing fact with comment. News Report selections usually separate stated facts from the writer's comments. Items ask "according to the selection" — only stated facts are eligible.
- Missing the graphic element. News Report items often reference a captioned photo, a sidebar, or a small chart. Read the graphic element before answering.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Strategy for News Report selections

**Read the headline first, then the lead.** The headline plus the lead paragraph contain the five Ws. Reading 1 items targeting who/what/when/where can usually be answered from these two lines alone.

**Skim the supporting paragraphs for quotes and statistics.** Most News Report selections contain at least one direct quote and one statistic. Reading 1 items often target these.

**Note the graphic element.** A captioned photo, sidebar, or chart is part of the selection. Read it before answering the items — many items depend on it.

**Identify the writer's purpose.** News Report writers usually write to inform. Sometimes they write to alert (an urgent event) or to advocate (an opinion-bearing report on a community issue). Reading 2 items ask the student to infer the purpose from the verbs and adjectives.

#### Practice routine for News Report

- Take one EQAO-style News Report selection. Read the headline and the lead first.
- Write down who, what, when, where, why from the lead. Compare your list to the Reading 1 items.
- Identify the supporting paragraphs' content: quotes, statistics, background. Compare your list to the remaining Reading 1 items.
- Read the graphic element. Note the caption or label.
- Time yourself. Target: under 7 minutes per News Report selection (read + answer items).

#### How News Report differs from the other selection types

- **Real-Life Narrative** is personal and reflective; News Report is factual and event-focused. RLN items ask about character and feeling; News Report items ask about facts and purpose.
- **Information Paragraph** explains a concept; News Report reports an event. Information Paragraph items ask about definition; News Report items ask about the five Ws.
- **Dialogue** is conversation; News Report is exposition. Dialogue items ask about speaker intent; News Report items ask about writer purpose.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How News Report connects to the other OSSLT items

- **The long-writing tasks** depend on reading expository source material. The Series-of-Paragraphs task uses a source the student must interpret to support an opinion. News Report practice strengthens the source-reading skill.
- **Reading 3** in News Report selections often asks the student to connect the event to a real-world situation. This is the same skill the open-response reading item tests.

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"I need to read every paragraph in detail."** The lead has the five Ws. Read it first, then skim the supporting paragraphs.
- **"The graphic element is decoration."** The graphic element is part of the selection. Items depend on it.
- **"The writer is being objective."** News Report writers usually are objective, but the choice of which facts to include and which to omit is itself a choice. Reading 2 items may ask what the inclusion of a specific fact suggests.

#### Exam technique

- Read the headline first, then the lead. Then read the supporting paragraphs in order.
- Note the graphic element. Read the caption or label.
- For Reading 1 items, ask: "where in the selection is this stated?" Find the line.
- For Reading 2 items, ask: "what is the writer's purpose, and what does this detail suggest?"
- Use the platform's flag-for-review function. Do not leave blanks on selected-response items.
