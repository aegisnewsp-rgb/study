# Governance

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Governance** is the set of processes, institutions and mechanisms through which authority is exercised in managing a country's economic, political and social affairs. It is broader than **Government**, since it includes civil society, markets and citizens alongside the state.

- **World Bank/UNDP attributes**: accountability, transparency, rule of law, responsiveness, participation, equity, effectiveness, consensus-orientation, strategic vision.
- **Backbone statutes**: RTI Act 2005, Lokpal Act 2013, Whistleblowers Protection Act 2014, Citizen's Charter (1997), Companies Act 2013, LARR 2013, PESA 1996.
- **Anchor reports**: 2nd **ARC Report on Ethics in Governance (2008)**; Supreme Court's *Prakash Singh v. UoI* (2006) for police reforms.
- **High-yield pointers**: 73rd Amendment = rural PRIs (11th Schedule, 29 subjects); 74th = urban ULBs (12th Schedule, 18 subjects). NITI Aayog replaced Planning Commission in 2015 but has no fund-allocation power.

> Memory anchor: "Government is the actor; Governance is the process."

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Meaning and Scope
Governance, as understood in **UPSC CSE GS2**, is not synonymous with government. Government denotes the formal institutional apparatus (executive, legislature, judiciary); governance denotes the wider web of actors — state, market and civil society — that shape how decisions are made and implemented. UNDP frames it around eight attributes, of which **accountability, transparency and participation** are most frequently examined.

#### Theoretical Underpinnings
- **Principal-Agent framework**: citizens/state are principals; bureaucracy is the agent. Information asymmetry explains rent-seeking and the case for RTI.
- **New Public Management (Hood, 1991)**: market-like mechanisms, performance contracts, results-based budgeting.
- **New Public Governance**: networked, pluralistic delivery through partnerships with NGOs and the private sector.
- **Stakeholder theory (Freeman, 1984)** underpins inclusive policy formulation.

#### Accountability Mechanisms

| Type | Instruments |
|---|---|
| Political | Elections, parliamentary committees, no-confidence motion |
| Legal | Courts, tribunals, judicial review |
| Administrative | **CAG** (Art. 148), **CVC** (CVC Act 2003), departmental vigilance |
| Social | RTI, media, civil society, social audit under MGNREGA |
| Professional | Codes of conduct, service rules |

#### Transparency and Right to Information
**RTI Act 2005** is the cornerstone. Section 4 imposes proactive disclosure obligations, Section 6 governs applications, Section 7 sets a 30-day disposal timeline, and Section 8 lists nine exemptions including sovereignty, scientific/economic interests and fiduciary data. Section 20 provides penalties of ₹250–₹25,000 on Public Information Officers.

#### e-Governance and Citizen's Charter
The **Sevottam Model** (2006) integrates service delivery standards, grievance redress and a charter mark. MeitY's maturity model progresses from emerging → connected governance, reflected in **UMANG, DigiLocker, e-Sign, GeM and e-Courts**.

#### Constitutional Decentralisation
The **73rd Amendment (1992)** and **74th Amendment (1992)** are non-justiciable at the state level but backed by Article 243 and Schedule entries. **PESA 1996** extends panchayats to Scheduled Areas with rights over minor forest produce.

> Exam tip: a single mains question on "strengthening democratic governance" expects you to cite ARC-2 (2008), RTI, Lokpal Act 2013, and at least one SC judgment.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Conceptual Traps
Conflating **Lokpal** (central, statutory under the 2013 Act) with **Lokayukta** (state-level, scope varies — Karnataka has functional teeth, others are advisory) is a high-frequency error. Similarly, **CAG audits accounts** but does not probe propriety in the way CVC does — yet CAG's *Performance Audits* increasingly overlap with vigilance functions. Candidates often misattribute RTI to Article 21; the Act is statutory, though read alongside **Article 19(1)(a)**.

#### Corporate Governance Layer
**Cadbury Report (1992)** shaped disclosure norms globally; India's **Kumar Mangalam Birla Committee (SEBI, 2000)** introduced Clause 49 of the Listing Agreement, refined under the **Companies Act 2013** (Sections 149 independent directors, 166 duties, 177 audit committee, 178 nomination & remuneration). SEBI's **LODR Regulations 2015** now govern listed entities.

#### Cooperative Federalism in Practice
GST implementation, **Finance Commission** recommendations (currently the 16th FC for 2026–31) and concurrent list management demonstrate cooperative federalism, distinct from competitive federalism where states vie for investment. **Article 253** enables treaty implementation; **Article 356** remains contested as central overreach.

#### Public Policy Cycle
Howlett and Ramesh's stages — **agenda setting, formulation, adoption, implementation, evaluation, termination** — offer a diagnostic frame for mains answers on flagship schemes (e.g., why MGNREGA succeeded in some states and stagnated in others despite uniform design).

#### Common Mistakes to Avoid
- Treating Police Reforms as a "state subject only" — Public Order is List II, but *Prakash Singh* (2006) imposes binding directives.
- Assuming RTI applies uniformly; intelligence organisations listed in the **Second Schedule** are exempted, with limited judicial review.
- Believing NITI Aayog allocates funds — it is an advisory body; allocation rests with the Finance Ministry and FC.

#### Practice Prompts
1. *"Lokpal is a toothless tiger without a strong Lokayukta ecosystem." Discuss with reference to state-level variations.* (Anchor: Lokpal Act 2013, ARC-4 Report on State Accountability.)
2. *"e-Governance is a means, not an end, to inclusive service delivery." Critically examine in the context of Digital India.* (Anchor: MeitY maturity model, UN e-Government Survey 2024, rural digital divide.)
