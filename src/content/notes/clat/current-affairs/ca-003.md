# Legal News

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Legal News in CLAT tests your awareness of the Indian legal developments reported in the **12 months** before the exam — Supreme Court and High Court judgments, Parliamentary bills, presidential ordinances, and amendments to the three new criminal codes that took effect on **1 July 2024**. Most questions are passage-based, so reading comprehension matters as much as factual recall.

- **BNS replaces IPC**, **BNSS replaces CrPC**, and **BSA replaces the Indian Evidence Act** — old section numbers (302, 376, 420 IPC) are wrong answers in 2024-onwards questions; new BNS sections are 302, 63, 318.
- Know at least three landmark rulings from the past year (bench strength, judge names, ratio), plus any ordinance under **Article 123**.
- Read **The Hindu** or **Indian Express** daily and one law outlet (LiveLaw, Bar & Bench, Supreme Court Observer).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### What CLAT Tests Under "Legal News"

CLAT's Current Affairs & GK section (about 25% of the paper) contains roughly 2–3 Legal News items per paper. Items arrive as factual MCQs, principle-application sets, or comprehension passages drawn from mainstream dailies and law journals. The **12-month rolling window** is strict: judgments or bills older than that are irrelevant, even if famous.

#### The Three New Criminal Codes

The Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), and Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA) replaced the IPC, CrPC, and Indian Evidence Act from **1 July 2024**. Section numbers were renumbered and some offences redefined — for example, the new Section 63 BNS reframes sexual penetration rather than the old Section 375 IPC's "penetrative sexual assault" language.

#### Key Doctrinal Vocabulary

| Concept | What it means in a news item |
| --- | --- |
| Ratio Decidendi | The binding legal principle a judge decides — only this is "law declared" under Article 141 |
| Obiter Dicta | Judge's incidental observations — persuasive, not binding |
| PIL | Public Interest Litigation filed under Article 32 (SC) or Article 226 (HC) |
| Article 142 | SC's complete justice power — used for sweeping orders like Babri, same-sex marriage reliefs |
| Ordinance | Executive legislation under Article 123, valid for 6 weeks max per House session |

#### Common Examiner Traps

- Quoting **IPC 302** instead of **BNS 302** after July 2024 — same number, different statute.
- Treating an interim stay as a final judgment.
- Citing a Law Commission Report as enacted law — it is only a recommendation.
- Reporting only the political slug ("SC slams Centre") without the legal issue, relief, and provisions interpreted.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Mapping Cases to Subject Heads

A reliable revision method is to bucket each ruling by subject — criminal, constitutional, commercial, environmental, family, labour, tax, cyber. This lets you answer principle-application questions that rephrase a known case into a fictional fact pattern. Keep a one-line note per case: *parties → issue → statutory provision → ratio → relief*.

#### Reading Primary Sources

The Supreme Court of India website (`main.sci.gov.in`) publishes judgments and cause lists the same day. For orders and oral observations, LiveLaw and Bar & Bench give same-day coverage with verbatim quotes. Always note **bench strength** (Coram), **judge names**, **citation** (e.g. (2025) 1 SCC 142), and **date of judgment** — examiners credit these details in comprehension answers.

#### Constitutional Bench Watchlist

Track references to **basic structure doctrine** (Kesavananda Bharati lineage), **Article 32** maintainability, **Article 226** scope, **Article 142** complete justice, and **Article 370** abrogation aftermath. Notification-driven news — NALSA schemes, ECI orders, Ministry of Law & Justice gazettes — also appears in CLAT passages.

#### Practice Prompts

1. *A passage describes a 2025 SC ruling granting relief under Article 142 in an environmental dispute.* Identify which of three given case-summaries matches: (a) Tata Sons vs Cyrus Mistry-type commercial, (b) Tamil Nadu silt-engulfment case, (c) Article 370 review.
2. *A factual MCQ asks which statute now governs "admissibility of electronic records" after 1 July 2024.* Answer: **BSA 2023** (Sections 57–61), not the old Evidence Act.
