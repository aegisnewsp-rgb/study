# Biomolecules: Amino Acids, Proteins and Enzymes — MCAT Bio/Biochem

Amino acids, proteins, and enzymes form the first part of AAMC Foundational Concept 1 of the Bio/Biochem section. The MCAT tests amino acid structure and properties, peptide bonds, protein primary through quaternary structure, and enzyme kinetics (Michaelis-Menten, Lineweaver-Burk) and regulation. These topics are foundational for nearly every other biochemistry passage on the exam.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### Core concepts

- **Amino acid structure:** a central carbon (α-carbon) bonded to an amino group (NH₂), a carboxyl group (COOH), a hydrogen, and a side chain (R group). The R group defines the amino acid.
- **Peptide bonds:** covalent bonds between the carboxyl group of one amino acid and the amino group of the next. Formed by dehydration (loss of water).
- **Protein structure:** primary (amino acid sequence), secondary (α-helix, β-sheet, stabilised by hydrogen bonds), tertiary (3D folding, stabilised by interactions between R groups), quaternary (multiple polypeptide subunits).
- **Enzyme kinetics:** Michaelis-Menten equation: v = (V_max × [S]) / (K_m + [S]). K_m is the substrate concentration at half V_max.
- **Enzyme regulation:** competitive inhibition (increases apparent K_m, no change in V_max); non-competitive inhibition (no change in K_m, decreases V_max).

#### One-pass drill

1. Identify the topic: amino acid, peptide bond, structure level, or kinetics.
2. Recall the relevant rule or equation.
3. Apply it to the specific molecule or scenario.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing competitive and non-competitive inhibition. Competitive increases K_m (more substrate needed); non-competitive decreases V_max (max rate lowered).
- Forgetting that peptide bonds are planar (rigid) due to partial double-bond character.
- Treating all amino acids as equivalent. R groups differ; hydrophobicity, charge, and size vary widely.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Amino acids in detail

There are 20 standard amino acids. The MCAT tests:

- **Side chain properties:** hydrophobic (alanine, valine, leucine, isoleucine, methionine, phenylalanine, tryptophan, proline); polar (serine, threonine, asparagine, glutamine, tyrosine, cysteine); acidic (aspartate, glutamate); basic (lysine, arginine, histidine).
- **Special amino acids:** proline (cyclic, disrupts α-helix); cysteine (forms disulfide bonds); glycine (smallest, no chiral centre).
- **Essential amino acids:** the nine amino acids the human body cannot synthesise (histidine, isoleucine, leucine, lysine, methionine, phenylalanine, threonine, tryptophan, valine).

#### Protein structure

- **Primary:** the linear sequence of amino acids, determined by the gene.
- **Secondary:** regular patterns stabilised by hydrogen bonds. α-helix (right-handed, 3.6 residues per turn); β-sheet (parallel or antiparallel strands).
- **Tertiary:** the 3D folding of a single polypeptide. Stabilised by hydrophobic interactions, hydrogen bonds, ionic bonds, disulfide bridges, and van der Waals forces.
- **Quaternary:** the assembly of multiple polypeptide subunits (e.g., haemoglobin has four subunits).

**Denaturation:** disruption of secondary, tertiary, and quaternary structure by heat, pH, salt, or chemicals. Primary structure is preserved.

#### Enzyme kinetics

**Michaelis-Menten:** v = (V_max × [S]) / (K_m + [S]). At low [S], v is proportional to [S]. At high [S], v approaches V_max.

**K_m:** the substrate concentration at half V_max. A low K_m means high affinity; a high K_m means low affinity.

**Lineweaver-Burk:** double-reciprocal plot of 1/v against 1/[S]. The y-intercept is 1/V_max; the x-intercept is -1/K_m. The slope is K_m / V_max.

**Inhibition:**
- Competitive: inhibitor binds to the active site; competes with substrate. Increases K_m, no change in V_max. Overcome by high [S].
- Non-competitive: inhibitor binds to an allosteric site. No change in K_m, decreases V_max. Not overcome by [S].
- Mixed: combines features of competitive and non-competitive.

#### Practice routine

- Take 5 amino acid items. Identify the side chain property and the resulting behaviour.
- Take 5 protein structure items. Identify the level of structure and the stabilising interactions.
- Take 5 Michaelis-Menten items. Compute v given V_max, K_m, and [S].
- Take 5 inhibition items. Identify the type and predict the effect on K_m and V_max.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How this topic connects to the other Bio/Biochem topics

- **Carbohydrates and lipids** rely on the same structural principles (monomer-polymer, dehydration synthesis, hydrolysis).
- **Metabolism** depends on enzyme catalysis: glycolysis, the Krebs cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation are enzyme-catalysed pathways.
- **Genetics** depends on protein structure: transcription factors, polymerases, and ribosomes are proteins.

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"Proline is a helix-former."** Proline is a helix-breaker — its cyclic structure disrupts the α-helix.
- **"Competitive inhibition lowers V_max."** Competitive inhibition does not lower V_max at high [S]. It only increases the apparent K_m.
- **"The primary structure is destroyed by denaturation."** Denaturation disrupts secondary, tertiary, and quaternary structure but not primary. Renaturation can restore function.

#### Exam technique

- For amino acid items, focus on the side chain: hydrophobic, polar, acidic, basic.
- For structure items, identify the level (primary through quaternary) before choosing the stabilising interaction.
- For kinetics items, plot on a Michaelis-Menten curve or a Lineweaver-Burk plot before computing.
- For inhibition items, identify the inhibitor's binding site (active site or allosteric site).
- Use the platform's calculator.
