# Growth and Development

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Growth** is an irreversible, measurable increase in plant size — in length, area, fresh/dry mass, or cell number — produced by **cell division**, **cell enlargement**, and **cell differentiation** at meristems. **Development** is the qualitative progression of structural, physiological, and biochemical changes from germination to senescence, directed by **intrinsic factors** (genetic programme, plant growth regulators) and **extrinsic factors** (light, temperature, water, nutrients). The two are inseparable but distinguishable: growth is *quantitative*, development is *qualitative*. Growth plotted against time gives a **sigmoid (S-shaped) curve** with lag, exponential (log), stationary, and senescence phases. Must-know growth metrics: **AGR = (W₂ − W₁)/(t₂ − t₁)** and **RGR = (ln W₂ − ln W₁)/(t₂ − t₁)**, with the identity **RGR = NAR × LAR**. MDCAT traps: confusing growth with development, treating ABA as a promoter, and swapping photoperiodism with vernalization.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Defining Growth vs Development
Growth is the permanent, quantitative gain in a plant's parameters — protoplasmic mass, cell number, linear dimension, fresh or dry weight. Development is the broader, qualitative series of events: germination, differentiation, organogenesis, flowering, fruiting, senescence, and abscission. A seed germinating, becoming a seedling, then an adult plant illustrates both: each phase has a unique structural and biochemical identity that growth alone cannot explain.

#### Sites of Growth — Meristems
All new cells originate from **meristematic tissue**, whose cells are thin-walled, dense-cytoplasm, and divide continuously. Three types matter for MDCAT:

| Meristem | Location | Product |
|---|---|---|
| **Apical** | Root and shoot tips | Primary growth (length) |
| **Lateral** (vascular & cork cambium) | Vascular cylinder, pericycle region | Secondary growth (girth) |
| **Intercalary** | Base of internodes, leaf sheaths | Regrowth after grazing (e.g. grasses) |

Cell division happens *inside* the meristem; cells pushed behind it enter the **elongation zone** (auxin-driven turgor-driven expansion), then the **differentiation zone** where xylem, phloem, and epidermis specialise.

#### Growth Curve
A typical growth curve is **sigmoid (S-shaped)**: lag phase (slow division as enzymes activate), log/exponential phase (maximum AGR, redifferentiation active), stationary phase (RGR falls as resources limit division), and senescence phase (dry weight may fall). The asymptotic plateau reflects shifting balance between cell production and cell death.

#### Growth Metrics
- **AGR** (Absolute Growth Rate): fresh weight gained per unit time — used in agriculture to compare varieties.
- **RGR** (Relative Growth Rate): per-unit biomass increment per unit time — better for comparing seedlings vs mature plants because it normalises for size.
- **LAR** (Leaf Area Ratio) and **NAR** (Net Assimilation Rate) decompose RGR into photosynthetic capacity vs efficiency: **RGR = NAR × LAR**.

#### Plant Growth Regulators (PGRs)
Five classical PGRs recur in MCQs:
- **Auxin (IAA)** — apical dominance, phototropism, cell elongation.
- **Gibberellin (GA₃)** — internode elongation (bolting), α-amylase in barley aleurone.
- **Cytokinin** — cell division, delay of senescence.
- **ABA** — growth inhibitor, stomatal closure, seed dormancy.
- **Ethylene** — fruit ripening, abscission, often inhibitory to elongation.

#### Photoperiodism vs Vernalization
**Photoperiodism** is the response to *day length*, mediated by phytochrome and the CONSTANS/FT module, classifying plants as short-day, long-day, or day-neutral. **Vernalization** is the requirement of *prolonged cold* to flower (e.g. winter wheat, *Arabidopsis*). MDCAT MCQs frequently pair these in "difference-between" stems.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Connections
**Indeterminate vs determinate growth:** apical meristems confer *indeterminate* primary growth — woody perennials add leaves for centuries. *Determinate* growth terminates in a floral meristem converting to a flower; whole shoots (e.g. sunflower capitulum) then stop elongating. Monocarpic plants flower once and die, polycarpic plants iterate.

**Secondary growth is not optional in dicots:** the vascular cambium produces secondary xylem inward and secondary phloem outward; the cork cambium replaces the epidermis with periderm. MDCAT sometimes asks why monocot "stems" lack a ring of secondary growth — answer: scattered vascular bundles lack a continuous cambium.

**Hormonal antagonism and synergism:** auxin:cytokinin ratio dictates *in vitro* organogenesis — high cytokinin favours shoot buds, high auxin favours roots. ABA antagonises GA during germination; ethylene synergises ABA in stress responses.

**Photoperiodism details:** the critical day/night length triggers flowering; *night length* is what phytochrome measures. A flash of red light *during* a long inductive night converts Pfr back, short-day plants perceive as no induction. Far-red reverses red — a classic MDCAT assertion-reason trap.

**Vernalization specifics:** effective temperatures are typically 1–7 °C for 4–8 weeks; vernalized plants acquire epigenetic silencing of *FLC* so *FT* can express. Vernalization is *not* the same as stratification (cold treatment to break seed dormancy).

#### Common Mistakes
1. Equating "growth in plants" with increase in height only — ignore girth, root length, leaf area, fresh vs dry mass.
2. Stating that all PGRs "promote growth" — ABA and (often) ethylene inhibit.
3. Confusing the *exponential phase* with linear growth — RGR, not AGR, is constant in this phase.
4. Mixing photoperiodism (light *duration*) with photonasty (light *intensity*-triggered movement).

#### Practice Prompts
1. A maize seedling gains 2 g dry mass between day 10 and day 20. Its initial dry mass is 5 g. Calculate RGR per day and AGR per day. *(Answer: AGR = 0.2 g day⁻¹; RGR = ln(7/5)/10 ≈ 0.0336 day⁻¹.)*
2. Explain why removing the apical bud releases lateral buds from dormancy, naming the PGR involved and the phenomenon.
