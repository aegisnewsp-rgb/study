# Cost Accounting Basics

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your ICAN exams.

**Cost accounting** records, classifies and analyses the cost of materials, labour and overheads so management can plan, control and price products. The four-stage cost build-up below is the most-tested structure in ICAN Foundation and Intermediate papers.

- **Prime Cost** = Direct Materials + Direct Labour + Direct Expenses
- **Works Cost** = Prime Cost + Factory/Production Overheads
- **Cost of Production** = Works Cost + Opening WIP − Closing WIP
- **Cost of Sales** = Cost of Production + Opening Finished Goods − Closing Finished Goods

| Element | Included items |
| --- | --- |
| Direct | Materials issued to production, wages of shop-floor workers, hire of special machinery |
| Indirect | Factory rent, depreciation of plant, supervisor's salary, indirect materials |

> Exam tip: questions usually give a factory overhead figure and ask for "Works Cost" — do not stop at Prime Cost.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students working through the ICAN Foundation and Intermediate syllabuses.

#### Classification of Costs

Costs are grouped four ways in ICAN's syllabus: **by element** (material, labour, expense), **by function** (production, administration, selling, finance), **by behaviour** (fixed, variable, semi-variable), and **by traceability** (direct vs indirect). A semi-variable cost such as electricity has a fixed standing charge plus a variable usage charge; the High-Low method separates the two using:

> Variable cost per unit = (Highest activity cost − Lowest activity cost) ÷ (Highest activity units − Lowest activity units)

#### Stock Valuation Methods

Issues of materials are priced using **FIFO** (oldest price issued first), **LIFO** (most recent price issued first), or **Weighted Average (AVCO)** (recomputed after each receipt). In a rising-price environment FIFO reports lower material cost and higher profit, LIFO gives the opposite, and AVCO sits between the two.

#### Labour Remuneration Systems

| System | Pay formula |
| --- | --- |
| Time rate | Hours worked × hourly rate |
| Piece rate | Units produced × rate per unit |
| Halsey premium | Time-rate pay + 50% × (time saved × rate) |
| Rowan premium | Time-rate pay × (time worked ÷ time allowed) |

#### Overhead Absorption

Production overheads are recovered into product cost using a pre-determined **absorption rate** based on direct labour hours, machine hours, % of prime cost, % of direct wages, or % of direct material. The choice of base affects reported product cost even when total overhead is unchanged.

- **Job Costing** — distinct cost per order or contract.
- **Batch Costing** — cost per batch of identical items.
- **Process Costing** — average cost per unit over a continuous process.
- **Service Costing** — cost per unit of service (e.g. per passenger-km).

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for deeper mastery and revision before Part II.

#### Marginal Costing and CVP

Under marginal costing only **variable cost** is charged to the product; fixed overheads are written off in full to the period. **Contribution** = Sales − Variable Cost, and **Break-even units** = Fixed Cost ÷ Contribution per unit. The margin of safety equals budgeted sales minus break-even sales, expressed in naira or units.

> Trap: do not absorb fixed overhead into unit cost under marginal costing — examiners specifically mark this as wrong.

#### Standard Costing Variances

Material variances split into **price** (standard price − actual price) × actual quantity and **usage** (standard quantity − actual quantity) × standard price. Labour variances mirror this with **rate** and **efficiency** sub-variances. Fixed overhead variances split into expenditure, volume, capacity and efficiency components.

#### Material Control Levels

| Level | Formula |
| --- | --- |
| Re-order level | Maximum usage × Maximum lead time |
| Minimum level | Re-order level − (Average usage × Average lead time) |
| Maximum level | Re-order level + Re-order quantity − (Minimum usage × Minimum lead time) |
| Average level | Minimum level + ½ Re-order quantity |
| EOQ | √(2AO ÷ C) where A = annual demand, O = order cost, C = carrying cost per unit/year |

#### Common ICAN Traps

- Forgetting to subtract closing WIP before adding opening WIP in the Cost of Production formula.
- Computing EOQ without the square root or omitting annual carrying cost per unit.
- Mixing Halsey (50% sharing) with Rowan (proportional) premium formulas.
- Using LIFO when the question specifies FIFO — examiners explicitly test consistent method application.

#### Practice Prompts

1. A factory incurred Direct Materials ₦450,000, Direct Labour ₦280,000, Direct Expenses ₦70,000 and Production Overheads ₦320,000. Opening WIP was ₦40,000 and Closing WIP ₦25,000. Compute Prime Cost, Works Cost, Cost of Production and Cost of Sales.
2. Annual demand is 24,000 units, ordering cost ₦1,500 per order and carrying cost ₦6 per unit per year. Calculate EOQ and the number of orders per year.
