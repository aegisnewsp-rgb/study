# Blood Relations

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Blood Relations** problems give you a chain of verbal statements describing who is married to whom, who is whose parent or sibling, and expect you to deduce an unknown relation. Two relation families must stay distinct: **blood (consanguinity)** — father, mother, brother, sister, son, daughter, uncle, aunt, nephew, niece, cousin — and **affinity (in-law/marriage)** — husband, wife, father-in-law, mother-in-law, brother-in-law, sister-in-law, son-in-law, daughter-in-law. The fastest solver technique is to **draw a generation chart** as you read: G1 (grandparents) on top, G2 (parents + their spouses), G3 (the speaker + siblings + cousins). Master the three highest-yield traps: (1) *"my mother's only son"* refers to the speaker himself if he is male, not his brother; (2) in **pointing problems**, the speaker is the subject of the sentence, the pointed-at person is the object; (3) **coded symbols** typically use `+` for male, `-` for female, and `A/B/C` for generations. Always re-check the final answer against every given statement.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core relation vocabulary

A reliable mental table keeps the answer choices straight:

| Blood (consanguinity) | Affinity (in-law / marriage) |
|---|---|
| Father, Mother | Father-in-law, Mother-in-law |
| Brother, Sister | Brother-in-law, Sister-in-law |
| Son, Daughter | Son-in-law, Daughter-in-law |
| Uncle, Aunt, Nephew, Niece, Cousin | Husband, Wife |

**Brother-in-law** has three distinct origins — spouse's brother, sibling's husband, or spouse's sibling's husband. The same is true of **sister-in-law** and **uncle/aunt** (can be blood or marital). Never lock in a relation until the family tree shows which origin the statement creates.

#### Coded-symbol convention

NTS test items often use shorthand: `+` for male, `-` for female, and letters `A`, `B`, `C` for generations 1, 2, 3. So `A+M` means a male of generation 1 (grandfather tier) and `C-F` means a female of generation 3 (the speaker's tier). Substitute these into actual family members when you redraw the tree.

#### Pointing-type problems

In a pointing statement, the speaker is the subject and the person being pointed at is referenced in the object. Example: *"Pointing to a lady, A says, 'She is the daughter of the woman who is the mother of the husband of my mother.'"* Strip the chain from the inside out: my mother's husband = my father; my father's mother = my father's mother (paternal grandmother); the daughter of that woman = my father's sister = my **aunt**. So the lady is A's aunt, and the question "How is the lady related to A?" is answered **Aunt**.

#### Single vs multi-generation mapping

For a two-generation map, you need 6–8 nodes (G2 parents, their siblings, G3 children). For three generations, add grandparents and great-aunts/uncles. Always place **spouses at the same horizontal level** as their blood relatives, and connect them with a single horizontal marriage line, then draw vertical descent lines for children.

#### Standard exam patterns

NTS items arrive in three shapes: (a) direct "How is X related to Y?" with a 3–4 statement chain, (b) pointing/photograph problems where the speaker references themselves in third person, and (c) coded-notation diagrams where you must fill in the relation symbol for a labelled box. The correct option is the one consistent with **every** statement — eliminate any choice that contradicts even one clause.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge cases that break careless solvers

- **"Only son" vs "only brother":** *"My mother's only son"* is the speaker if the speaker is male; it is his brother only if the speaker is female. Always determine the speaker's gender first.
- **Cousin direction:** Children of two brothers are **parallel cousins**; children of a brother and a sister are **cross cousins**. When a problem involves a cousin's marriage, the resulting in-law chain differs — a parallel-cousin's spouse is a cousin-in-law, not a sibling-in-law.
- **Generation shifts via marriage:** Marrying upward (e.g., to an uncle's widow) does **not** change your own generation; you remain G3. The spouse simply joins your generation node.
- **Negation clues:** Phrases like *"is not the mother of"*, *"is not the brother of"* are designed to eliminate options — treat them as constraints, not decoration.

#### Worked micro-example

> *A is the son of B. C is the daughter of B. D is the husband of C. E is the brother of A.*

Family tree: G2 has B (parent) and B's spouse; G3 has A (son), C (daughter), E (brother of A → another son of B), and D married into the family via C. Question: *How is D related to E?* D is C's husband, and C is E's sister, so D is E's **brother-in-law** (specifically, the sister's-husband variety).

#### Common mistakes

- Drawing siblings at the parental level because a statement mentions them before a parent.
- Reading *my uncle's only daughter* as my cousin without checking whether the daughter is on the paternal or maternal side.
- Selecting the first relation that fits one statement instead of verifying it against all statements.
- Forgetting that a person may appear under two roles (e.g., someone is both your cousin and your brother-in-law) — NTS items often exploit this dual relation.

#### Practice prompts

1. *Pointing to a man in a photograph, Saima says, "His mother is the only daughter of my mother's mother." How is the man related to Saima?*
2. *P is the brother of Q. R is the daughter of Q. S is married to R. T is the son of S. How is T related to P?*

#### Exam strategy for NAT-I (NTS)

Blood Relations carries roughly **2–4 questions** in the Analytical Reasoning section (~4% weight). Allocate **under 90 seconds per item** — read once, sketch the tree, read again to confirm, then pick the option that survives all constraints. Skip and return if the chain has more than four nested clauses; these are best solved last when your reading speed is calibrated.
