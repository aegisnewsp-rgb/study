# Cell Membrane Physiology and Transport

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The plasma membrane is a **phospholipid bilayer** with embedded **integral proteins**, **peripheral proteins**, and **cholesterol** that selectively regulate solute, ion, and water movement. Transport follows the **fluid mosaic model**, with lipid-soluble molecules (O₂, CO₂, steroids) crossing by simple diffusion and polar/charged species requiring **channels or carriers**.

The **Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase** extrudes **3 Na⁺ out for 2 K⁺ in per ATP**, generating a net outward current that contributes roughly **−5 to −10 mV** to the resting potential of about **−70 to −90 mV**. The **Nernst equation at 37 °C** simplifies to **E (mV) ≈ 61/z · log₁₀([ion]out/[ion]in)**, while the **GHK equation** accounts for simultaneous Na⁺, K⁺, and Cl⁻ permeabilities.

- **Osmolarity vs tonicity**: urea raises osmolarity but not tonicity; only effective osmoles shift cell volume.
- **Pump stoichiometry**: 3 Na⁺ out / 2 K⁺ in — electrogenic, not 1:1.
- **Clinical anchors**: CFTR defect (cystic fibrosis), NCC (Gitelman), NKCC2 (Bartter), ENaC (Liddle).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Membrane Architecture and Selective Permeability

The bilayer's **amphipathic phospholipids** orient hydrophilic heads outward and hydrophobic tails inward, creating a barrier to ions and polar molecules. **Cholesterol** inserts between fatty-acid tails, reducing fluidity at 37 °C and decreasing permeability to small water-soluble solutes. **Glycoproteins and glycolipids** form the glycocalyx, functioning in cell recognition and receptor anchoring.

Transport across this barrier divides into **passive** (down electrochemical gradient, no ATP) and **active** (against gradient, requires energy). Passive includes **simple diffusion**, **facilitated diffusion** (carrier- or channel-mediated, saturable, stereospecific), and **osmosis**. Active includes **primary** (direct ATP use) and **secondary** (driven by an ion gradient, typically Na⁺).

#### Stoichiometry and Electrochemical Logic

| Transporter | Mechanism | Key substrate / ion | Stoichiometry / direction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase | Primary active | Na⁺, K⁺ | 3 Na⁺ out / 2 K⁺ in per ATP |
| Ca²⁺ ATPase (PMCA/SERCA) | Primary active | Ca²⁺ | 1 Ca²⁺ out / 2 H⁺ in (SERCA) |
| SGLT1 | Secondary active, symport | Na⁺, glucose | 2 Na⁺ : 1 glucose |
| Na⁺/Ca²⁺ exchanger (NCX) | Secondary active, antiport | Na⁺, Ca²⁺ | 3 Na⁺ in / 1 Ca²⁺ out |
| CFTR | Channel (ATP-regulated) | Cl⁻ | Down electrochemical gradient |

#### Gibbs-Donnan and Tonicity

Impermeant intracellular anions (proteins, organic phosphates) attract permeant cations and repel permeant anions, producing the **Gibbs-Donnan effect**. This raises intracellular osmolarity and contributes a small Donnan potential to Vm. **Tonicity** describes only effective osmoles: a **0.9% NaCl** solution is isotonic (≈ 308 mOsm/L), hypotonic solutions cause **swelling/lysis**, and hypertonic solutions cause **crenation**.

- **Clinical correlate**: IV fluid selection (NS, RL, D5W) depends on tonicity and the patient's volume/osmolar status.
- **Receptor-mediated endocytosis** is clathrin-dependent, specific, and saturable (LDL, transferrin, B₁₂-intrinsic factor).
- **Aquaporins (AQP1–AQP12)** mediate water flux; AQP2 in renal collecting duct is regulated by ADH via cAMP.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Fick, Nernst, and Starling — Numeric Anchors

**Fick's law** governs small-molecule flux: J = −D · (dC/dx). Doubling the diffusion coefficient (D) or the concentration gradient doubles flux; increasing membrane thickness (e.g., multilamellar barriers) reduces it. **Osmotic pressure** follows the van't Hoff relation **π = nRTC**, where n is the number of dissociable particles (i for NaCl ≈ 2; i for glucose ≈ 1).

The **Goldman-Hodgkin-Katz equation** predicts Vm when multiple ions contribute: Vm = (RT/F) · ln((P_K[K⁺]out + P_Na[Na⁺]out + P_Cl[Cl⁻]in) / (P_K[K⁺]in + P_Na[Na⁺]in + P_Cl[Cl⁻]out)). At rest, P_K dominates, driving Vm toward E_K.

#### Starling Forces and Edema

Capillary fluid exchange obeys **Jv = Lp · S · [(Pc − Pi) − σ(πc − πi)]**. A net outward force at the arteriolar end filters fluid; reabsorption occurs venously; the lymphatics return the remainder. Pathologic shifts: ↑Pc (heart failure), ↓πc (nephrotic syndrome, ↓ albumin), ↑σ (burns), or lymphatic obstruction cause **edema**.

#### Common Mistakes on INI CET

| Mistake | Correction |
| --- | --- |
| Stating Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase is 1:1 | It is 3 Na⁺ out / 2 K⁺ in — electrogenic |
| Using Nernst with base-e log at 37 °C | Use 61/z · log₁₀; 58/z at 20 °C |
| Calling pinocytosis receptor-mediated | Pinocytosis is non-specific fluid uptake; RME is clathrin-dependent |
| Treating facilitated diffusion as ATP-dependent | It is passive and saturable; ATP is not consumed |
| Confusing osmolarity with tonicity | Urea raises osmolarity but freely crosses — it does not shift cell volume |

2. A patient receives 5% dextrose; once dextrose is metabolized, the solution behaves as **free water** — predict the effect on a hyponatremic patient.
