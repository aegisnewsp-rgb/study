# Legal News

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Legal News** in CLAT refers to reported developments in Indian law over the 12-month window before the exam — Supreme Court and High Court judgments, new statutes, ordinances, and amendments to the three criminal codes that replaced the IPC, CrPC and Indian Evidence Act from 1 July 2024.

- **BNS / BNSS / BSA**: Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita and Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam now govern criminal law, procedure and evidence. Old IPC section citations are outdated.
- **Ratio decidendi** is the binding legal principle of a case; **obiter dicta** are remarks that are persuasive, not binding. Examiners exploit this distinction in principle-application sets.
- **Article 142** (complete justice), **Article 32** and **Article 226** (writ jurisdiction) are the most-tested constitutional provisions in legal-news MCQs.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Sources and Reference Window
CLAT questions are drawn from developments reported in the **12 months preceding the exam date**. The Consortium of NLUs recommends reading at least one national daily (*The Hindu* or *Indian Express*) and one legal outlet such as **LiveLaw**, **Bar & Bench** or **Supreme Court Observer**. Ministry of Law and Justice notifications, Law Commission reports and NALSA circulars also qualify as primary material.

#### The Three New Codes (Effective 1 July 2024)
The colonial-era criminal framework was replaced by three parliamentary enactments. Mapping old to new sections is a high-yield exercise.

| Old Section (IPC) | New Section (BNS) | Offence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 302 IPC | 302 BNS | Murder |
| 376 IPC | 63 BNS | Penetrative sexual assault |
| 420 IPC | 318 BNS | Cheating |
| 304A IPC | 106 BNS | Causing death by negligence |
| CrPC, 1973 | BNSS, 2023 | Criminal procedure |
| Indian Evidence Act, 1872 | BSA, 2023 | Law of evidence |

#### Reading a Reported Judgment
A passage-based CLAT item will quote a paragraph from a Supreme Court ruling. Read for **which bench** (division or Constitution Bench), **who wrote the majority opinion**, and **which statutory provision was interpreted**. Distinguish **ratio decidendi** (the rule on which the decision rests) from **obiter dicta** (incidental observations). Citing obiter as the binding rule is the most common trap in principle-application questions.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Case-Mapping Heuristic
Sort every reported development into one of ten subject heads — criminal, constitutional, commercial, environmental, family, labour, tax, cyber, IPR and ADR. This index lets you retrieve a case quickly when a passage appears. A typical CLAT passage of 300–400 words is followed by four MCQs testing (a) the **legal issue**, (b) the **statute interpreted**, (c) the **relief granted**, and (d) a **distractor** that swaps the bench or year.

#### High-Yield Traps
- **Bench strength confusion**: A division-bench High Court ruling is not binding on the Supreme Court; a SC three-judge ruling can be reviewed only by a larger bench.
- **Ordinance vs Law Commission Report**: An ordinance under **Article 123** has force of law for six weeks maximum unless ratified; a Law Commission report is only a recommendation.
- **PIL status**: Admission of a PIL is procedural — it carries no verdict on merits. Interim orders are temporary and rarely the "final" law.
- **Article 370 abrogation** and **same-sex marriage** rulings are repeatedly tested; know the date, bench and operative order.

#### Worked Practice Prompts
1. A 2024 Supreme Court ruling holds that the State cannot attach property without following the procedure under Section 83 BNSS. Identify whether this is ratio or obiter if the same ruling also remarks on bail jurisprudence in a separate paragraph.
2. A news item reports that "the Law Commission recommended" decriminalising a section. State whether the recommendation is binding and the procedural step required before the section ceases to operate.
