# Environment and Ecology

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> High-yield summary of ecosystem energetics, biodiversity conservation, Ramsar wetlands in Tamil Nadu, and ecological legislation for TNPSC Group 1.

**Ecology & Environmental Biology — High-Yield Essentials**

1. **Ecosystem Energetics & Trophic Dynamics:**
   - **Trophic Hierarchy:** Primary producers (autotrophs) → Primary consumers (herbivores) → Secondary consumers (carnivores) → Tertiary consumers (top carnivores).
   - **Lindeman's 10% Energy Law (1942):** Only approximately 10% of the energy stored as biomass at any given trophic level is transferred to the succeeding trophic level; the remaining 90% is dissipated as metabolic respiration and heat.
   - **Ecological Pyramids:**
     - *Pyramid of Energy:* **Always upright** in all natural ecosystems without exception, conforming to the second law of thermodynamics.
     - *Pyramid of Biomass:* Upright in terrestrial forest/grassland ecosystems; **inverted** in aquatic (lake, ocean) ecosystems where phytoplankton biomass is lower than zooplankton/fish biomass at any single moment.
     - *Pyramid of Numbers:* Upright in grassland; **inverted** in a parasitic ecosystem (single tree supporting hundreds of birds harboring thousands of parasites).
2. **Pollution and Ecotoxicology:**
   - **Biomagnification:** Progressive amplification of persistent, non-biodegradable toxic substances (such as DDT, mercury, and polychlorinated biphenyls) across successive trophic tiers in a food chain.
   - **Eutrophication:** Nutrient enrichment (nitrates and phosphates from agricultural runoff and domestic detergents) causing massive algal blooms, subsequent microbial decomposition, biochemical oxygen demand (BOD) surge, and severe aquatic asphyxiation.
3. **Ramsar Wetland Sites in Tamil Nadu (India's Leading State):**
   - Tamil Nadu hosts the highest number of Ramsar sites in India (16+ designated wetlands).
   - Key Ramsar sites: Point Calimere Wildlife and Bird Sanctuary, Pichavaram Mangrove (one of the largest mangrove systems in India), Vedanthangal Bird Sanctuary (oldest waterbird sanctuary in India), Karikili Bird Sanctuary, Gulf of Mannar Marine Biosphere Reserve, Koonthankulam Bird Sanctuary, Vembannur Wetland Complex.

⚡ **TNPSC Group 1 Exam Trap:** Candidates often believe all ecological pyramids can be inverted. Remember that the **pyramid of energy is ALWAYS upright** in every ecosystem because energy cannot be recycled and is irrevocably lost as metabolic heat at each successive trophic step.

## 1. Biogeochemical Cycles: Gaseous vs Sedimentary

Nutrients circulate continuously between biotic living organisms and abiotic geochemical reservoirs:

| Cycle Type | Reservoir Pool | Key Transformations & Bacterial Agents | Environmental Impact of Anthropogenic Disruption |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nitrogen Cycle (Gaseous)** | Atmosphere (78% N2) | **Fixation:** *Rhizobium*, *Azotobacter*, *Anabaena*; **Nitrification:** *Nitrosomonas* (NH3 to NO2-), *Nitrobacter* (NO2- to NO3-); **Denitrification:** *Pseudomonas denitrificans*, *Thiobacillus* | Excess synthetic fertilizers drive groundwater nitrate contamination and aquatic eutrophication |
| **Carbon Cycle (Gaseous)** | Atmosphere & Oceanic Carbonate | Photosynthetic fixation by plants and oceanic phytoplankton; respiratory and combustion release | Elevated atmospheric CO2 concentrations drive global greenhouse warming and ocean acidification |
| **Phosphorus Cycle (Sedimentary)** | Lithosphere (phosphate rocks and deposits) | Weathering of phosphate minerals releases PO4(3-); taken up by plant roots; zero gaseous atmospheric reservoir phase | Mining and synthetic detergents cause accelerated runoff and aquatic algal blooms |
| **Sulphur Cycle (Sedimentary & Gaseous)** | Earth's crust (gypsum, pyrite) & volcanic H2S/SO2 | Oxidation by *Thiobacillus*; reduction by *Desulfovibrio* | Industrial coal combustion emits SO2, forming sulphuric acid ($) precipitation (acid rain, pH < 5.6) |

## 2. In-Situ vs Ex-Situ Biodiversity Conservation in Tamil Nadu

Conservation approaches protect biological taxa either within their natural habitats or in controlled artificial environments:
- **In-Situ Conservation (On-Site):**
  - **Biosphere Reserves:** Gulf of Mannar Marine Biosphere Reserve (coastal marine biodiversity, dugong / sea cow habitat), Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve (India's first biosphere reserve, UNESCO MAB program), Agasthyamalai Biosphere Reserve.
  - **National Parks in Tamil Nadu:** Guindy National Park (Chennai), Mudumalai National Park (Nilgiris), Mukurthi National Park (endemic Nilgiri tahr habitat), Anamalai Tiger Reserve / Indira Gandhi National Park, Gulf of Mannar Marine National Park.
  - **Tiger Reserves in Tamil Nadu:** Kalakkad Mundanthurai, Mudumalai, Anamalai, Sathyamangalam (TX2 Award winner), Srivilliputhur Megamalai.
- **Ex-Situ Conservation (Off-Site):** Zoological gardens (Arignar Anna Zoological Park, Vandalur), botanical gardens, cryopreservation seed banks, in-vitro germplasm repositories.

## 3. Landmark Environmental Statutes and Global Treaties

TNPSC civil service examinations frequently assess national legislative frameworks and multilateral environmental agreements (MEAs):
- **Wildlife Protection Act, 1972:** Established national parks, wildlife sanctuaries, and Schedule I–VI wildlife classifications (amended in 2022 to conform with CITES).
- **Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974:** Established Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) and State Pollution Control Boards (TNPCB).
- **Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980:** Mandates prior central government approval for diversion of reserved forest lands for non-forestry purposes.
- **Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981:** Regulates gaseous emissions; amended in 1987 to explicitly categorize noise as an atmospheric pollutant.
- **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986:** Umbrella legislation enacted under Article 253 in the wake of the 1984 Bhopal Gas Tragedy; empowers the Union government to set nationwide environmental quality standards.

## 4. TNPSC Group 1 Previous Year Questions (PYQs)

**Question 1 (TNPSC Group 1 Prelims):** Which state in India currently possesses the largest number of designated Ramsar wetland sites?  
*(A) Uttar Pradesh (B) Tamil Nadu (C) Kerala (D) West Bengal*  
**Answer:** (B) Tamil Nadu (Explanation: Tamil Nadu leads all Indian states with 16 designated Ramsar wetland sites, including Point Calimere, Vedanthangal, Pichavaram, and the Gulf of Mannar).

**Question 2 (TNPSC Group 1 Prelims):** In which year was the first Biosphere Reserve in India—the Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve—established?  
*(A) 1986 (B) 1992 (C) 1972 (D) 2000*  
**Answer:** (A) 1986 (Explanation: The Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve was designated in 1986 as India's first biosphere reserve under the UNESCO Man and the Biosphere (MAB) program).

**Question 3 (TNPSC Group 1 Prelims):** The ecological phenomenon wherein non-biodegradable toxins accumulate in progressively higher concentrations at each successive trophic level is known as:  
*(A) Bioaccumulation (B) Biomagnification (C) Bioremediation (D) Eutrophication*  
**Answer:** (B) Biomagnification (Explanation: Biomagnification refers to the progressive increase in toxicant concentration per unit biomass from lower to higher trophic tiers).
