# Plant Life and Reproduction

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Plant reproduction** occurs in two modes: **asexual (vegetative propagation)** from stems, roots, or leaves without seeds, and **sexual reproduction** through flowers that produce seeds and fruits. A typical **flower** has four whorls — sepals (calyx), petals (corolla), stamens (androecium), and pistil/carpel (gynoecium). **Pollination** is the transfer of pollen from anther to stigma; **fertilisation** is the fusion of male and female gametes inside the ovule.

**Double fertilisation** is unique to angiosperms: one male gamete + egg cell → zygote (2n); the second male gamete + two polar nuclei → endosperm (3n).

- **Vegetative organs used:** stem tuber (potato), runner/stolon (strawberry), rhizome (ginger), bulb (onion), leaf buds (Bryophyllum).
- **Agents of pollination:** wind (grasses, wheat), insects (sunflower, jasmine), water, birds, and bats.
- **Germination needs:** water, oxygen, and suitable temperature — not water alone.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Flower as the Reproductive Organ
A flower is the reproductive shoot of an angiosperm, organised in **four whorls** on a swollen tip called the thalamus. From outside inward: **sepals** (calyx, protect the bud), **petals** (corolla, attract pollinators with colour and scent), **androecium** (male whorl, made of stamens — each with an anther and filament), and **gynoecium** (female whorl, made of carpels/pistils — each with stigma, style, and ovary containing ovules). A **bisexual (hermaphrodite) flower** has both whorls functional (Hibiscus, mustard); a **unisexual flower** has only one (papaya, maize).

#### Sexual Reproduction Pathway
Pollen grains released from the anther land on a compatible stigma → pollen tube grows down the style → male gametes enter the ovule → **syngamy** (gamete + egg = diploid zygote) and **triple fusion** (gamete + two polar nuclei = triploid endosperm) occur together as **double fertilisation**. The zygote becomes the embryo; the endosperm stores starch, proteins, or oils to nourish it. The ovule matures into the **seed**, and the ovary wall becomes the **fruit**.

#### Vegetative (Asexual) Reproduction
New plants grow from somatic parts without gamete fusion, producing **genetically identical offspring (clones)**. Common natural methods are listed below.

| Organ modified | Example plant | Structure |
|---|---|---|
| Stem tuber | Potato | Swollen underground stem tip with eyes |
| Runner/Stolon | Strawberry, grass | Horizontal stem above ground |
| Rhizome | Ginger, turmeric | Horizontal underground stem |
| Bulb | Onion, garlic | Short stem with fleshy leaves |
| Leaf buds | Bryophyllum | Plantlets on leaf margins |
| Budding | Yeast | Outgrowth detaches as new cell |

#### Pollination Types and Agents
**Self-pollination** transfers pollen within the same flower or plant (rice, wheat); **cross-pollination** moves pollen between different plants of the same species, increasing genetic variation. Flower features match the pollinating agent.

| Agent | Flower features | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Wind (anemophily) | Small, dull, no nectar, light feathery pollen, exposed stamens | Maize, wheat, grass |
| Insects (entomophily) | Bright, scented, nectar, sticky/spiky pollen | Sunflower, jasmine, Salvia |
| Water (hydrophily) | Ribbon-like pollen, submerged flowers | Vallisneria, Hydrilla |
| Birds / bats | Tubular, dull-red, copious dilute nectar | Sunbird-pollinated Butea, banana |

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Monoecious vs Dioecious Plants
**Monoecious** plants (maize, coconut, cucumber) bear separate male and female flowers on the **same** plant, so cross-pollination can still occur between flowers. **Dioecious** plants (papaya, date palm, mulberry) bear male and female flowers on **different** plants — making them strictly cross-pollinated and dependent on agents. This distinction frequently appears in CTET as a "classify the plant" question.

#### Seed Dispersal and Germination
Seeds travel away from the parent plant to reduce competition and colonise new areas.

- **Wind:** winged seeds (maple samara), hairy pappus (dandelion, sunflower).
- **Water:** fibrous, waterproof coat (coconut, lotus).
- **Animals:** hooks stick to fur (Xanthium/cleavers); edible fruits eaten, seeds excreted (berry, mango, tomato).
- **Explosion (mechanical):** drying pods split and fling seeds (pea, balsam, mustard).

**Germination** activates the embryo once it absorbs water (imbibition). The radicle emerges first to form roots, then the plumule forms shoots. **Dormancy** is a resting stage in which mature seeds stay metabolically inactive until conditions (moisture, oxygen, suitable 20–30 °C temperature, and sometimes light) are met. Some seeds (orchid) need light; others (onion, lily) germinate only in darkness.

#### Common Traps in CTET Questions
- **Pollination ≠ fertilisation:** pollination ends at the stigma; fertilisation ends inside the ovule.
- **Endosperm ploidy:** in angiosperms it is **triploid (3n)** — haploid is a common wrong answer.
- **Fruit origin:** botanically, the fruit develops only from the ovary; sepals and petals usually wither after fertilisation.
- **Vegetative propagation is asexual** — no gametes fuse, so offspring are clones of the parent.

#### Practice Prompts
1. A flower has bright red petals, strong fragrance, and sticky pollen. Identify the most likely pollinating agent and justify with two floral features.
2. Trace the path of a pollen grain from the anther to the formation of a seed, naming each structure and the ploidy of the cells involved (pollen grain n, zygote 2n, endosperm 3n).
