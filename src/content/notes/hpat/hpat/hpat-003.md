# Non-Verbal Reasoning

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute HPAT-Ireland revision before the ACER sitting.

**Non-Verbal Reasoning** — Quick Facts for Irish medical applicants (RCSI, UCD, TCD, UL)

**What is Section 3?**
Section 3 is the third of three separately-timed HPAT-Ireland sections for standard applicants. It carries 32 multiple-choice questions in 40 minutes (about 75 seconds per question). The section does not draw on any academic curriculum - it assesses abstract pattern recognition across shape-and-pattern sequences, mirror images, figure rotations, folding, embedded figures and logical set combinations, all assessed entirely through diagrams. It is administered by ACER (Australian Council for Educational Research) on behalf of the participating universities.

**At a glance:**
- **32 multiple-choice questions** with four or five response alternatives and a single correct answer
- **40 minutes** strictly timed (about 75 seconds per question)
- **Visual-spatial reasoning skills** - pattern recognition, mirror images, rotations, folding, embedded figures, logical set combinations
- **No curriculum content** - revision is about repeated timed diagram-drill, not memorisation
- **Most-improved section under timed practice** - Section 3 rewards volume more than any other HPAT section

**HPAT Section timing reference (standard applicants):**
| Section | Skill | Questions | Duration | Duration | Mins/Q |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Logical Reasoning & Problem Solving | 44 | 65 min | ~88 s |
| 2 | Interpersonal Understanding | 36 | 45 min | ~75 s |
| 3 | Non-Verbal Reasoning | 32 | 40 min | ~75 s |
| 4 | Written English | 2 tasks | n/a | short written pieces (UL only) |

**Time budget rule:** Plan 40 minutes of uninterrupted, timed practice for Section 3 alone before each full-length mock. Section 3 carries about 29% of total HPAT questions across Sections 1-3 (32 of 112) and the smallest question count - but the skills (visual pattern recognition, spatial reasoning) improve most strongly under repeated timed exposure, so under-revising it is the most common score-leak pattern.

### 🟡 Standard — Detailed Review (1d–2w)
> A working understanding for steady preparation across a few weeks.

**What Section 3 actually measures**
Section 3 assesses abstract pattern recognition entirely through diagrams - no text, no numbers, no outside knowledge. The productive way to think about the section is as **six overlapping sub-skills** blended into a single 40-minute block:

1. **Shape-and-pattern sequences** - a sequence of 3-5 figures where each step applies a transformation (rotation, scaling, addition/removal of a feature). The candidate identifies the rule and applies it to the next step.
2. **Mirror images** - a figure and its mirror-image counterpart (left-right, vertical, diagonal). The candidate identifies the correct orientation or matches a target to its reflection.
3. **Figure rotations** - a figure rotated by some angle. The candidate identifies the rotated version from alternatives, or recognises that two figures are the same figure at different rotations.
4. **Folding and unfolding** - a flat 2D shape that folds into a 3D solid, or a 3D solid that unfolds into a flat 2D net. The candidate identifies the correct fold or the correct net.
5. **Embedded figures** - a small shape hidden inside a larger, more complex figure. The candidate identifies whether the small shape is present in the larger.
6. **Logical set combinations (matrix-and-set conditions)** - a 3x3 grid where each row and column applies a transformation. The candidate identifies the rule (often a 2D operation: rotation + colour, scale + count) and selects the missing cell.

**Why curriculum revision does not help (and what does)**
HPAT-Ireland Section 3 does not draw on any academic curriculum - the items test visual-spatial reasoning that is independent of study. ACER's official Information Booklet states that preparation should not assume any particular academic background. The pattern that improves Section 3 scores, in order of leverage:

1. **Timed full-section runs** - 40-minute practice sets with strict stop, scored afterwards. Section 3 is the section that improves most under repeated timed exposure, and it rewards volume more than any other HPAT section. **The single largest improvement lever is volume of timed practice.**
2. **Official ACER Practice Tests** - the only published real past questions; they show the ACER style of diagram and the wrong-answer conventions.
3. **Section-specific drill** - sub-skills where you keep missing (e.g. folding questions, mirror-image questions) for 20-30 minute focused blocks, then return to full-section runs.
4. **Error-log review** - one-line note per missed practice question: type, reason (misread the rotation, picked the wrong net, missed the embedded figure), and the correction. Patterns identify the actual weak sub-skill.
5. **Spatial-reasoning warm-ups** - 5-10 minutes of diagram-puzzle practice (e.g. tangram, jigsaw, mental rotation exercises) before each Section 3 practice set primes the skill for the section.

### High-Yield Topics

**Topics to drill down in detail, in priority order:**

1. **3x3 logical-set matrix items** - the largest single item-type cluster in published ACER materials. The skill is to identify the rule that operates across each row (and column, when both apply). Common rule families: rotation by a fixed angle; scale by a fixed factor; colour-inversion; addition or removal of a feature; combination of two transformations.

2. **Shape-and-pattern sequences** - one row of 3-5 figures where each step applies a transformation. The skill is to compare figures side-by-side, identify the transformation, and apply it to the next step. Common wrong answers add a feature that wasn't in the sequence, or omit a feature that was.

4. **Mirror-image orientation** - figures and their mirror reflections. The skill is to mentally flip the target and compare to alternatives. Common wrong answers rotate the target by 90 degrees (rather than reflecting it), or apply the wrong axis of reflection.

5. **Figure rotations** - figures rotated by 90/180/270 degrees. The skill is to mentally rotate the target and compare to alternatives. Common wrong answers reflect rather than rotate, or apply a different rotation angle.

6. **Folding and unfolding (3D solids)** - a flat 2D net that folds into a 3D solid, or vice versa. The skill is to mentally fold or unfold the figure. Common wrong answers apply the wrong colour to a face, or select a net that folds to a different solid.

7. **Embedded figures** - a small shape hidden inside a larger, more complex figure. The skill is to scan systematically; common wrong answers are false positives (the small shape appears similar but a feature is missing or a position is wrong).

8. **Pacing and time management** - 75 seconds per question is tight. Section 3 diagrams take longer to interpret than Section 1 passages or Section 2 scenarios. The productive pattern is to skip-and-flag a hard question within 30 seconds, attempt all remaining questions in order, then return to flagged questions in any remaining time. A blank answer scores zero; an attempted answer has a 25-33% chance of being correct by elimination.

### Previous Year Patterns

**Recurring Section 3 patterns in ACER practice materials (qualitative, not quantitative):**

1. **3x3 matrix with one cell missing** - one 3x3 matrix where each row (and often each column) applies a transformation. The candidate selects the missing cell from 4-5 alternatives. Items test the candidate's ability to identify the rule and apply it. Plan to spend ~90 seconds per item.

2. **Sequence of 3-5 figures with one missing** - one short sequence where each step applies a transformation. The candidate selects the next figure. Plan to spend ~75 seconds per item.

3. **Mirror-image pair** - one target figure with 4-5 alternative orientations. The candidate selects the correct mirror reflection. Plan to spend ~60 seconds per item.

4. **Figure rotation with 4-5 alternatives** - one target figure rotated by some angle. The candidate selects the correct rotation. Plan to spend ~60 seconds per item.

6. **Net matching (3D solids)** - one 3D solid and 4-5 alternative 2D nets. The candidate selects the correct net that folds into the solid. Plan to spend ~90 seconds per item.

7. **Mixed-format sets** - some sets combine a sequence with a mirror-image or rotation test. Plan to spend ~90 seconds per item.

**Pattern to watch:** Section 3 items are *single-diagram* (one figure per question, four or five alternatives) — they are not long-form passages with multiple sub-items. Plan to spend 75-90 seconds per item, including a quick first scan and a slower verification pass.

### Pro Tips

1. **Use the official ACER Practice Tests first.** They are the only published real past questions and the only authoritative source of section style. Work through them after the practice-question and practice-placement stages described on the ACER preparation page.

2. **Volume of timed practice is the single largest improvement lever.** Section 3 is the section that improves most under repeated timed exposure. Run all Section 3 practice sets under strict 40-minute timing; never extend the timer because the section is finite in 100 minutes.

3. **Skip hard items within 30 seconds.** Section 3 items take longer to interpret than Section 1 or Section 2 items. If you can't parse the diagram in 30 seconds, flag-and-skip and attempt the easy ones first. Return to flagged items in any remaining time.

4. **Don't bring outside visual patterns or tricks to items.** Section 3 items invite you to use prior experience with diagram puzzles ("of course the right answer is the one with the rotational symmetry"). The right answer is the one that *follows the rule*. Strip the item to what the diagram actually shows.

5. **Section 3 rewards volume.** It has 32 questions - the smallest of the three HPAT sections. Under-revising it is the most common score-leak pattern because candidates think "fewer items = less to study". The opposite is true: Section 3 needs more timed practice volume than Sections 1 or 2 because each item requires interpretation of a diagram rather than reading text.

6. **Practice spatial reasoning warm-ups before sessions.** 5-10 minutes of diagram-puzzle practice (e.g. tangram, jigsaw, mental rotation exercises) before each Section 3 practice set primes the skill and improves first-half accuracy.

8. **Don't confuse mirror with rotation.** Section 3 deliberately mixes mirror-image and rotation items in the same block. The two operations look similar at a glance. Pause for half a second on each item to identify whether the right answer is a mirror or a rotation.

9. **Confirm Section 3 in the live Information Booklet before each cycle.** ACER updates the booklet annually. Verify the live question count, time allocation, response format and the participating universities' score-combination rules on hpat-ireland.acer.org before committing a revision plan to a student.
