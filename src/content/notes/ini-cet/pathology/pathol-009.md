# Environmental Pathology

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Environmental pathology covers diseases caused by **chemical, physical, and biological agents** encountered in air, water, soil, food, and the workplace. Master the agent-to-disease pairings: **asbestos → mesothelioma**, **vinyl chloride → hepatic angiosarcoma**, **aflatoxin B1 → hepatocellular carcinoma (TP53 codon 249 mutation)**, **benzene → AML/aplastic anaemia**, **lead → microcytic anaemia with basophilic stippling**, and **methylmercury → Minamata disease**.

- Cigarette smoke is the single most preventable environmental cause of COPD, lung cancer, atherosclerosis, and bladder cancer.
- Pneumoconioses show distinct histology: **coal macules** (CWP), **whorled silicotic nodules** (silicosis), **ferruginous bodies** (asbestosis).
- Toxicokinetics follows absorption → distribution → Phase I CYP bioactivation/detoxication → Phase II conjugation → excretion.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Pollutant classification

Environmental agents fall into **chemical** (heavy metals, pesticides, CO, SO₂, NO₂, ozone), **physical** (ionizing/non-ionizing radiation, asbestos fibres), and **biological** (moulds, endotoxins) categories. Mechanistically they injure tissue via direct damage, oxidative stress, genotoxicity, immune dysregulation, or endocrine disruption.

#### Occupational carcinogens — the classic pairings

| Agent | Setting | Hallmark malignancy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Asbestos | Insulation, shipyard | Mesothelioma, bronchogenic carcinoma |
| Vinyl chloride | PVC manufacture | Hepatic angiosarcoma |
| Aflatoxin B1 | Contaminated grain/peanut | Hepatocellular carcinoma (TP53 249) |
| Benzene | Petrochemical, solvent | AML, aplastic anaemia |
| Arsenic | Pesticide, smelting | Skin, lung cancers |
| Naphthalene | Chemical industry | Lung, laryngeal cancers |

#### Heavy-metal toxicology

| Metal | Pathognomonic clue |
| --- | --- |
| Lead | Microcytic hypochromic anaemia with **basophilic stippling**; cognitive impairment in children; Burtonian line on gingiva |
| Mercury (methyl) | Minamata disease — cerebellar/brain neurotoxicity |
| Cadmium | Itai-itai disease — renal tubular damage, osteomalacia |
| Arsenic | Hyperpigmentation, palmo-plantar keratoses, peripheral neuropathy |

Smoking does **not** cause mesothelioma but synergises with asbestos to multiply bronchogenic-carcinoma risk.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Pneumoconioses — distinguish the histology

- **Coal workers'**: carbon-laden macrophages in upper-zone coal macules, can progress to progressive massive fibrosis.
- **Silicosis**: whorled hyalinised silicotic nodules with birefringent silica crystals; markedly raises TB risk.
- **Asbestosis**: lower-lobe fibrosis with **ferruginous (asbestos) bodies** — beaded, dumbbell-shaped fibres coated by haemosiderin-laden macrophages.

#### Radiation injury

Ionizing radiation generates ion pairs and free radicals causing DNA double-strand breaks. Acute radiation syndrome progresses through **haematopoietic, GI, and CNS phases** at increasing doses; chronic effects include fibrosis and radiation-induced leukaemia, thyroid, and breast cancers.

#### Toxicokinetics framework

1. **Absorption** via lung, GI tract, or skin.
2. **Distribution** governed by lipid solubility and protein binding.
3. **Phase I** CYP reactions can bioactivate (e.g. aflatoxin B1 → epoxide) or detoxify.
4. **Phase II** conjugation (GSH, glucuronide) in most keys detoxifies.
5. **Excretion** through kidney or bile determines tissue dose.

#### Air-pollution mechanisms

PM2.5/PM10 and NO₂/SO₂ drive COPD exacerbations, asthma, lung cancer, and cardiovascular events. **Ground-level ozone**, not stratospheric ozone, is the principal oxidant of photochemical smog and causes direct pulmonary oxidant injury.

#### Practice prompts

1. A 55-year-old PVC-plant worker presents with RUQ mass and haemorrhagic ascites; biopsy shows irregular vascular channels lined by atypical endothelium. Identify the carcinogen and the molecular hallmark.
2. A schoolchild with pica has developmental delay, colicky abdominal pain, and a peripheral smear showing microcytic cells with coarse basophilic granules. Name the toxin, three confirmatory lab findings, and the marrow enzyme it inhibits (δ-ALA dehydratase, ferrochelatase).
