# JUPEB Science Combination (PCM)

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid review of the Physics, Chemistry, and Mathematics (PCM) subject combination, faculty eligibility, grading points, and Direct Entry (DE) cutoffs for JUPEB.

**PCM Science Combination — High-Yield Essentials**

1. **Constituent Subjects:** **Physics**, **Chemistry**, and **Mathematics** (Pure and Applied A-Level components), supplemented by General Studies.
2. **Primary Faculty Pathways:**
   - **Faculty of Engineering & Technology:** Electrical/Electronics, Mechanical, Civil, Chemical, Petroleum, Mechatronics, Computer Engineering.
   - **Faculty of Physical Sciences:** Computer Science, Mathematics, Statistics, Physics, Industrial Chemistry, Geology, Geophysics.
   - **Faculty of Environmental Sciences:** Architecture, Building Technology, Surveying and Geoinformatics, Urban and Regional Planning.
3. **JUPEB Point Calculation Matrix:**
   - Grades scale: **A = 5 points, B = 4 points, C = 3 points, D = 2 points, E = 1 point, F = 0 points**.
   - **Bonus Point Rule:** Candidates who pass all three registered subjects without an 'F' receive **+1 bonus point**.
   - Maximum achievable aggregate: $5 + 5 + 5 + 1 = \mathbf{16\text{ points}}$. Minimum pass threshold for DE consideration: $\mathbf{5\text{ points}}$ (e.g., E-E-E + 1).

⚡ **JUPEB Exam Trap:** Students aiming for Chemical Engineering frequently choose PBC (Physics, Biology, Chemistry). However, virtually all federal universities (UNILAG, OAU, UNIBEN, UI) mandate **Mathematics** over Biology for all engineering disciplines. Selecting PBC permanently invalidates engineering direct entry eligibility!

## 1. Syllabus Modules Across the PCM Triad

The JUPEB PCM syllabus is an intensive two-semester curriculum equivalent to the Cambridge A-Level standard:

| Subject Component | Semester 1 Modules | Semester 2 Modules | Laboratory Practical Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Physics (PHY)** | Mechanics, vectors, gravitation, circular motion, properties of matter, fluid dynamics | Thermal physics, wave optics, electromagnetism, atomic physics, nuclear decay | Ray tracing with glass prisms, simple harmonic pendulum, potentiometer resistivity |
| **Chemistry (CHM)** | Stoichiometry, atomic orbitals, chemical bonding, gas laws, periodic trends | Chemical energetics, reaction kinetics, chemical equilibria, organic functional groups | Acid-base volumetric titration, qualitative salt cation/anion analysis, redox titrations |
| **Mathematics (MTH)** | Sets, algebraic sequences, trigonometry, differential calculus, coordinate geometry | Integral calculus, matrices, vectors, differential equations, probability and statistics | Pure problem-solving, numerical integration (Trapezoidal rule), statistical hypothesis tests |

## 3. Examination Structure and Scoring Tactics

The national JUPEB examination administered by the University of Lagos consists of three distinct papers per subject:
1. **Paper I (Objective MCQs):** 50 multiple-choice questions testing rapid conceptual recall and numerical fluency (1 hour).
2. **Paper II (Theory / Essay):** 4 to 6 structured mathematical problem-solving problems requiring step-by-step working, derivations, and clear physical explanations (2.5 hours).
3. **Paper III (Practical Laboratory Exam):** Independent laboratory experiment (Physics bench apparatus, Chemistry titration / salt identification) graded on accuracy, graph plotting, error gradient calculation, and significant figure conventions.

## 4. JUPEB PCM Practice Questions

**Question 1 (JUPEB Physics):** A stone of mass 0.5 kg attached to a string of length 1.2 m rotates in a horizontal circle at a constant speed of 6 m/s. The centripetal force acting on the stone is:  
*(A) 10 N  
(B) 15 N  
(C) 18 N  
(D) 24 N*  
**Answer:** (B) 15 N (Explanation: $F_c = \frac{m v^2}{r} = \frac{0.5 \times 6^2}{1.2} = \frac{0.5 \times 36}{1.2} = \frac{18}{1.2} = 15\text{ N}$).

**Question 2 (JUPEB Chemistry):** In a standard acid-base titration, 25.0 cm³ of 0.10 mol/dm³ sodium hydroxide solution completely neutralizes 20.0 cm³ of hydrochloric acid solution. What is the concentration of the acid?  
*(A) 0.08 mol/dm³  
(B) 0.125 mol/dm³  
(C) 0.150 mol/dm³  
(D) 0.200 mol/dm³*  
**Answer:** (B) 0.125 mol/dm³ (Explanation: Reaction stoichiometry is 1:1. $\frac{C_a V_a}{C_b V_b} = \frac{1}{1} \implies C_a \times 20.0 = 0.10 \times 25.0 \implies C_a = \frac{2.5}{20.0} = 0.125\text{ mol/dm}^3$).

**Question 3 (JUPEB Mathematics):** Find the derivative of $f(x) = x^3 \ln(x)$ with respect to $x$:  
*(A) $3x^2 \ln(x) + x^2$  
(B) $3x^2 / x$  
(C) $x^2 \ln(x) + 3x$  
(D) $3x^2 \ln(x) + 3x^2$*  
**Answer:** (A) $3x^2 \ln(x) + x^2$ (Explanation: Apply the product rule: $\frac{d}{dx}[u \cdot v] = u'v + uv'$. Here $u = x^3 \implies u' = 3x^2$, and $v = \ln(x) \implies v' = 1/x$. Thus, $f'(x) = 3x^2 \ln(x) + x^3(1/x) = 3x^2 \ln(x) + x^2$).
