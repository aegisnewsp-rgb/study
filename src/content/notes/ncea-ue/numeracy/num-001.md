# Use mathematics and statistics to meet the numeracy demands of a range of everyday situations (US32406) — NCEA UE Numeracy Notes

Unit Standard 32406 (US32406) is the dedicated co-requisite unit standard for the University Entrance numeracy requirement. It is internally assessed by the school (or by an approved provider) and carries 5 credits at Level 1 or above. The standard tests the ability to apply mathematics and statistics to real-life contexts, including formulating approaches, using mathematical and statistical methods, and explaining the reasonableness of results.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **5 credits**, internally assessed, NZQA moderated.
- **Level 1 or above**, can be used for the NCEA Level 1 certificate AND for UE numeracy.
- Tests the ability to formulate, apply, and explain mathematical and statistical methods in real-life situations.
- The "Y" column in the UE numeracy standards spreadsheet confirms this standard counts toward UE numeracy.
- The standard is the dedicated co-requisite numeracy standard, so it provides 5 of the 10 required UE numeracy credits.

#### Examiner traps

- Treating it as a "free" numeracy standard — the marking schedule requires application in context, not just calculation.
- Skipping the "explain reasonableness" component — NZQA markers look for evidence that the student interpreted the result, not just calculated it.
- Confusing US32406 (numeracy) with US32412 (Te Pāngarau numeracy) — they are separate standards, although both count toward UE numeracy.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### What the standard tests

US32406 tests three components, per the NZQA Purpose Statement:

1. **Formulate** mathematical and statistical approaches to solving problems in a range of meaningful situations.
2. **Use** mathematics and statistics to meet the numeracy demands of a range of meaningful situations.
3. **Explain** the reasonableness of mathematical and statistical responses to situations.

The standard is often delivered as a Common Assessment Activity (CAA), which is a national numeracy task set by NZQA. The school then marks the student's work against the published schedule.

#### The marking schedule

US32406 uses a four-grade scale (Not Achieved, Achieved, Merit, Excellence). The grade descriptors typically assess:

| Grade | What it requires |
|---|---|
| Achieved | Formulate, use, and explain mathematical and statistical approaches in everyday situations. |
| Merit | Formulate, use, and clearly explain mathematical and statistical approaches in everyday situations. |
| Excellence | Formulate, use, and explain mathematical and statistical approaches in a range of meaningful situations, with reflection on reasonableness. |

#### How it fits into UE

UE numeracy requires 10 credits at Level 1 or above. The dedicated co-requisite standard US32406 provides 5 of those credits. The other 5 credits can come from:

- US32412 (Te Pāngarau, the Māori-medium equivalent) — 5 credits.
- NCEA Level 1 Mathematics external standards 91946 or 91947 (when used in conjunction with US32406).
- "Numeracy-rich" achievement standards in Mathematics, Science, Geography and other subjects (downloadable spreadsheet on the NZQA UE numeracy page).

#### Common contexts

The CAA typically presents contexts such as:

- Personal finance (budgets, interest rates, loans).
- Health (medication dosages, BMI, nutrition).
- Work (wages, GST, invoices).
- Travel (currency, fuel consumption, speed/distance/time).
- Household (cooking measurements, electricity bills).

Each context requires mathematical and statistical reasoning, not just calculation.

#### Preparation strategy

1. **Practice real-life numeracy** — calculate bills, work out budgets, convert units.
2. **Practice interpreting results** — is the answer reasonable? Does it make sense?
3. **Use the CAA practice materials** — NZQA publishes practice assessments and exemplars.
4. **Read the standard carefully** — know what is being assessed.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### The Excellence move

The biggest gap between Merit and Excellence on US32406 is the depth of interpretation.

For example, on a context about household electricity bills:

- Merit: Calculate the cost of electricity used, identify the most expensive appliance.
- Excellence: Calculate the cost, identify the most expensive appliance, AND compare to last month's bill, AND explain the implications for the household budget, AND suggest changes to reduce consumption.

The Excellence response engages with the result, not just the calculation.

#### Connecting to UE readiness

US32406 is one of the dedicated co-requisite unit standards. Together with US32412 (Te Pāngarau) or selected achievement standards, it satisfies the full 10-credit UE numeracy requirement.

For students aiming for university, achieving US32406 at Level 1 (Year 11) means they have the numeracy half of UE secured with one less thing to worry about in Years 12 and 13.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "US32406 is just maths." No — it is application of maths and statistics in real-life contexts.
- "Calculations are enough." No — the standard requires interpretation and explanation of reasonableness.
- "It's like a maths test." No — it is more applied and contextualised than a traditional maths test.
- "Once you achieve it, you're done." With UE numeracy, yes — but the skill is reusable throughout life.
