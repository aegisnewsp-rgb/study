# Biology: Cell and Genetics

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

- **Cell theory** states that all organisms are made of cells, the cell is the basic unit of life, and every cell originates from a pre-existing cell (Rudolf Virchow, 1855).
- **Prokaryotic** cells (bacteria, archaea) lack a true nucleus and membrane-bound organelles; **eukaryotic** cells (animals, plants, fungi, protists) possess both.
- **Mitosis** = one division, two genetically identical diploid (2n) daughter cells. **Meiosis** = two divisions, four genetically distinct haploid (n) gametes.
- **Mendel's Law of Segregation**: the two alleles of a gene separate into different gametes. **Independent Assortment**: genes on different chromosomes are distributed to gametes independently.
- High-yield ratios: monohybrid 3:1 (phenotypic) and 1:2:1 (genotypic); dihybrid 9:3:3:1; test cross 1:1; co-/incomplete dominance 1:2:1.
- **DNA structure**: antiparallel double helix, A–T paired by 2 hydrogen bonds, G–C paired by 3 hydrogen bonds (Chargaff's rule: %A = %T, %G = %C).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Cell Structure and Organelles
The **plasma membrane** is a phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins that controls what enters and exits the cell. The **nucleus** houses DNA wrapped around histones and directs cellular activities. **Mitochondria** generate ATP through aerobic respiration (oxidative phosphorylation on the inner membrane); **chloroplasts** (plant cells) carry out photosynthesis using thylakoid-stacked grana. The **endoplasmic reticulum (ER)** comes in two forms: rough ER (ribosome-studded) synthesises membrane and secretory proteins, while smooth ER synthesises lipids and detoxifies. The **Golgi apparatus** modifies, sorts and packages these products. **Lysosomes** contain hydrolytic enzymes (acid pH ~4.5) for intracellular digestion; plant cells substitute **vacuoles** for this role. **Ribosomes** (80S in eukaryotes, 70S in prokaryotes) translate mRNA. The **cell wall** of plants is made of cellulose; fungi use chitin; bacteria use peptidoglycan.

#### Cell Cycle and Division
The cycle has four phases — **G1** (growth), **S** (DNA replication), **G2** (preparation for mitosis) and **M** (mitosis + cytokinesis). Checkpoints at G1, G2 and metaphase prevent damaged DNA from being passed on. **Mitosis stages** (PMAT) — Prophase, Metaphase, Anaphase, Telophase — produce two diploid, genetically identical cells for growth and repair. **Meiosis** has Meiosis I (homologous chromosomes separate, reducing chromosome number from 2n to n) and Meiosis II (sister chromatids separate), generating four haploid gametes and increasing genetic variation through crossing over (Prophase I) and independent assortment.

#### Mendelian Genetics
A **gene** is a DNA segment coding for a trait; an **allele** is a variant form. Individuals can be **homozygous** (AA or aa) or **heterozygous** (Aa). A **dominant** allele masks a **recessive** one in the heterozygote's **phenotype**. **Law of Segregation**: alleles separate during meiosis so each gamete carries one allele. **Law of Independent Assortment**: alleles of genes on different chromosomes segregate independently. A **Punnett square** predicts offspring genotype/phenotype ratios — monohybrid crosses give 3:1 phenotypic and 1:2:1 genotypic ratios, while dihybrid crosses give 9:3:3:1 phenotypic and 1:2:1:2:4:2:1:2:1 genotypic ratios.

#### Molecular Genetics
DNA replication is **semi-conservative** (Meselson–Stahl, 1958): each parental strand templates a new strand, producing two molecules each containing one old and one new strand. **Transcription** (nucleus) copies a gene into mRNA using RNA polymerase; the **genetic code** is read in **codons** (three-nucleotide units on mRNA), with 64 codons coding for 20 amino acids plus stop signals. **Translation** occurs at ribosomes with **tRNA** delivering amino acids (anticodon-codon pairing) and **rRNA** catalysing peptide bond formation. **Mutations** include point (silent, missense, nonsense), frameshift (insertion/deletion), and chromosomal (deletion, duplication, inversion, translocation).

| Cross type | Phenotypic ratio | Genotypic ratio |
|---|---|---|
| Monohybrid (Aa × Aa) | 3 : 1 | 1 : 2 : 1 |
| Dihybrid (AaBb × AaBb) | 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 | 1:2:1:2:4:2:1:2:1 |
| Test cross (Aa × aa) | 1 : 1 | 1 : 1 |
| Co-/incomplete dominance | 1 : 2 : 1 | 1 : 2 : 1 |

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Non-Mendelian Patterns
**Incomplete dominance** (snapdragon, Mirabilis jalapa) produces a blended phenotype in heterozygotes — a red × white cross yields pink F1, and the F2 ratio is 1 red : 2 pink : 1 white. **Co-dominance** (ABO blood groups) shows both alleles fully expressed — the A and B alleles are both dominant over O but co-dominant with each other, producing AB phenotype. **Sex-linked inheritance** follows the X chromosome: a carrier mother (X^H X^h) passes the recessive allele (e.g., haemophilia, colour blindness) to 50% of sons. **Polygenic inheritance** (skin colour, height) and **pleiotropy** (one gene → multiple traits, as in sickle-cell anaemia, where the β-globin mutation affects oxygen transport, spleen function, and malaria resistance) extend classical Mendelian analysis.

#### Pedigree and Karyotype Analysis
A **pedigree** uses standardised symbols — squares (males), circles (females), filled shapes (affected), half-filled (carrier), horizontal line (mating), vertical line (offspring) — to trace trait inheritance across generations. **Autosomal dominant** traits appear in every generation and affect both sexes equally; **autosomal recessive** traits skip generations and require two carrier parents. A **karyotype** is the organised display of an individual's chromosomes, ordered by size and centromere position (autosomes 1–22, then sex chromosomes XX/XY); humans normally have 46 chromosomes. Common abnormalities visible on karyotype include **Down syndrome** (trisomy 21), **Turner syndrome** (XO), and **Klinefelter syndrome** (XXY).

#### Common Mistakes
Confusing **allele** with **gene**, or **genotype** with **phenotype**. Forgetting that Mendel's 9:3:3:1 ratio only applies when both parents are double heterozygotes and genes assort independently. Mixing up mitosis and meiosis: mitosis has one division and produces identical cells; meiosis has two divisions and produces four non-identical cells. Believing the lagging strand is synthesised continuously — it is built as Okazaki fragments by DNA polymerase, then joined by DNA ligase. Treating Chargaff's rule (%A = %T) as species-specific ratios rather than the broader fact of complementary base pairing.

#### Worked Example
A pea plant heterozygous for height (Tt) and seed shape (Rr) is test-crossed with a homozygous recessive (ttrr). Expected offspring: TtRr, Ttrr, ttRr, ttrr — each in a 1:1:1:1 ratio (1/4 = 25% each). Genotype and phenotype ratios coincide because the recessive parent only contributes recessive alleles.

#### Practice Prompts
1. In a cross between a red-coat bull (RR) and a white-coat cow (WW) showing incomplete dominance, what fraction of F2 offspring are expected to show roan (mixed red-white) coat when F1 individuals are interbred?
2. A woman whose father was colour-blind marries a colour-blind man. If they have a son, what is the probability the son is colour-blind? (Recall the father must pass his Y, not his X, to a son.)
