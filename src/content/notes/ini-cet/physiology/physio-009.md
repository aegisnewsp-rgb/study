# Gastrointestinal Physiology: Motility

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your INI CET examination.

Gastrointestinal motility coordinates the mechanical breakdown, propulsive transit, and fluid absorption of ingested nutrients throughout the alimentary canal. Smooth muscle contraction in the gut is governed by intrinsic electrical rhythms generated by specialized pacemaker cells called the Interstitial Cells of Cajal (ICC), regulated by the intrinsic enteric nervous system (ENS) and modulated by extrinsic autonomic innervation.

The basic electrical rhythm (BER), or slow waves, consists of rhythmic, undulating fluctuations in smooth muscle membrane potential between -65 mV and -45 mV. Slow waves do not cause contractions on their own. When the slow wave depolarizes above the mechanical threshold (approximately -40 mV), voltage-gated L-type calcium channels open, generating spike potentials that elicit smooth muscle contraction.

#### Pacemaker Frequencies and Functional Motility Patterns

| Segment of Gut | Pacemaker (ICC) Frequency | Dominant Motility Pattern | Primary Physiological Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Esophagus** | No intrinsic slow waves (striated upper, smooth lower) | Primary and secondary peristalsis | Propulsion of bolus from pharynx into gastric reservoir |
| **Stomach (Antrum)** | 3 cycles per minute | Receptive relaxation, antral systole, retropulsion | Reservoir accommodation, mechanical grinding (< 2 mm), controlled emptying |
| **Duodenum** | 12 cycles per minute (highest in gut) | Non-propulsive segmentation | Chyme emulsification, mixing with bile and pancreatic enzymes |
| **Jejunum** | 10 to 11 cycles per minute | Segmentation and short-distance peristalsis | Nutrient digestion and mucosal absorption |
| **Terminal Ileum** | 8 to 9 cycles per minute | Segmentation, migrating motor complex | B12 and bile salt absorption; ileocecal sphincter barrier |
| **Colon (Cecum / Sigmoid)** | 3 to 6 / min (cecum) and 9 to 12 / min (rectosigmoid) | Haustral churning, mass peristalsis (1-3 times daily) | Water and electrolyte reabsorption, fecal storage and expulsion |

⚡ **High-Yield INI CET Principle:** The frequency of slow waves is set entirely by the Interstitial Cells of Cajal and remains constant regardless of neural or hormonal stimulation. Hormones and neurotransmitters modulate the number of spike potentials and the amplitude of contractions, but they cannot alter the slow wave frequency.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Systematic preparation for AIIMS, JIPMER, PGIMER, and NIMHANS entrance examinations.

#### 1. Electrophysiological Pacemakers: Interstitial Cells of Cajal (ICC)

The Interstitial Cells of Cajal originate from mesenchymal precursors and form an electrically coupled syncytium with gastrointestinal smooth muscle cells through gap junctions (connexin 43). They express the receptor tyrosine kinase KIT (CD117) and the calcium-activated chloride channel anoctamin-1 (ANO1 / TMEM16A). 

Gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GIST) arise from neoplastic transformation of these pacemaker cells, displaying diagnostic CD117 and DOG-1 positivity and responding to tyrosine kinase inhibitors like imatinib.

#### 2. Pharyngeal and Esophageal Motility

Swallowing (deglutition) proceeds across three synchronized phases:
1. **Oral Phase (Voluntary):** The tongue forces the masticated bolus postero-superiorly against the hard palate and into the oropharynx, innervated by cranial nerves V3, VII, and XII.
2. **Pharyngeal Phase (Involuntary):** Entry of the bolus into the posterior pharynx stimulates sensory receptors innervated by cranial nerves IX and X. The deglutition center in the medulla coordinates respiratory inhibition (deglutition apnea), superior elevation of the larynx, retroversion of the epiglottis, approximation of the vocal cords, and transient relaxation of the Upper Esophageal Sphincter (cricopharyngeus muscle).
3. **Esophageal Phase:**
   - **Primary Peristalsis:** Elicited directly by pharyngeal swallowing. A caudally propagating contraction wave travels down the esophagus at 3 to 5 cm/s. The vagus nerve controls both striated muscle (upper third) and smooth muscle (lower two thirds).
   - **Secondary Peristalsis:** Triggered by localized distension from a retained food bolus or acid reflux. Mediated by intrinsic enteric sensory neurons, clearing the esophageal lumen without involving pharyngeal swallowing.
   - **Lower Esophageal Sphincter (LES):** Maintains a resting pressure of 15 to 25 mmHg above intragastric pressure, sustained by intrinsic myogenic tone and cholinergic tone. On swallowing, vagal inhibitory fibers release Nitric Oxide (NO) and Vasoactive Intestinal Polypeptide (VIP), producing complete LES relaxation lasting 6 to 8 seconds.

| Neurotransmitter / Mediator | Anatomical Source | Target Gut Muscle Action | Clinical Drug Correlation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Acetylcholine (ACh)** | Postganglionic parasympathetic and enteric motor neurons | Muscarinic M3 activation: stimulates contraction and secretions | Bethanechol stimulates motility; Atropine and Dicyclomine inhibit motility |
| **Substance P / Neurokinin A** | Enteric excitatory motor neurons | Tachykinin NK1 receptor activation: strong smooth muscle contraction | Aprepitant blocks NK1 receptors in the CTZ and gut to prevent chemotherapy vomiting |
| **Nitric Oxide (NO)** | Non-adrenergic, non-cholinergic (NANC) inhibitory neurons | Guanylyl cyclase activation: elevates cGMP, producing profound relaxation | Nitrates and Sildenafil relax the lower esophageal sphincter in achalasia |
| **Vasoactive Intestinal Polypeptide (VIP)** | Enteric inhibitory motor neurons | Relaxes sphincters (LES, Oddi, internal anal); stimulates watery secretion | VIPoma (WDHA syndrome): watery diarrhea, hypokalemia, achlorhydria |
| **Motilin** | Endocrine M cells of duodenal and jejunal mucosa | Stimulates Phase III of the Migrating Motor Complex in the fasting state | Erythromycin acts as a direct motilin receptor agonist, used in gastroparesis |

#### 3. Gastric Reservoir and Emptying Mechanics

1. **Receptive Relaxation and Gastric Accommodation:**
   - When food enters the esophagus and stomach, mechanoreceptors elicit a vago-vagal inhibitory reflex. Nitric oxide and VIP relax the fundus and corpus, allowing the stomach to accommodate volumes up to 1.5 liters with negligible increases in intragastric pressure.
   - Surgical vagotomy (truncal or selective) abolishes this receptive relaxation, causing rapid pressure spikes, early postprandial satiety, and accelerated emptying of liquids.
2. **Antral Systole and Retropulsion:**
   - Peristaltic waves originate in the mid-corpus pacemaker zone and propagate toward the pylorus. As the contraction reaches the antrum, the terminal antral contraction occurs simultaneously with pyloric sphincter closure.
   - Solid food particles are compressed against the closed pylorus and forcefully propelled backward into the antrum (retropulsion), shearing particles until they are reduced to a suspension measuring under 1 to 2 mm in diameter.
3. **The Ileal Brake:**
   - Infusion of unabsorbed lipids and carbohydrates into the distal ileum triggers the release of Peptide YY (PYY) and Glucagon-like Peptide 1 (GLP-1) from enteroendocrine L cells. These peptides act centrally and peripherally to delay gastric emptying and prolong intestinal transit time, optimizing absorption.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Advanced clinical pathophysiology, high-resolution manometry, and surgical physiology.

#### 1. The Migrating Motor Complex (MMC)

In the interdigestive (fasting) state, the stomach and small intestine clear non-digestible residual solids (such as bone fragments, fiber, and desquamated cells) through the Migrating Motor Complex, a cyclic propagating motor pattern recurring every 90 to 120 minutes. Feeding immediately disrupts the MMC, replacing it with irregular fed-state mixing contractions.

The MMC consists of four distinct consecutive phases:
1. **Phase I (Quiescence):** Lasts 45 to 60 minutes. Complete electrical and motor silence, accounting for roughly 50% of the cycle.
2. **Phase II (Irregular Contractions):** Lasts 30 to 45 minutes. Intermittent, non-propulsive contractions of variable amplitude; bile secretion increases.
3. **Phase III (The Activity Front):** Lasts 5 to 10 minutes. Regular, high-amplitude, uninterrupted peristaltic contractions occurring at the maximum slow wave frequency (3/min in stomach, 12/min in duodenum). Contractions propagate from the gastric antrum to the distal ileum at 2 to 5 cm/min. The pylorus remains widely open, sweeping large undigested debris into the duodenum. Driven by surges of plasma **motilin**.
4. **Phase IV (Transition):** Brief period of 2 to 5 minutes of decreasing mechanical activity transitioning back into Phase I.

#### 2. Clinical Motility Disorders and High-Resolution Manometry

| Motility Disorder | Anatomical Site & Pathophysiology | Key Diagnostic Sign on Investigation | Definitive Clinical Management |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Achalasia Cardia** | Degeneration of inhibitory ganglion cells (NO/VIP) in myenteric plexus; unopposed ACh tone | High-resolution manometry: Integrated Relaxation Pressure (IRP > 15 mmHg) + aperistalsis. Barium swallow: "Bird's beak" appearance | Pneumatic balloon dilation, Laparoscopic Heller myotomy, or Peroral Endoscopic Myotomy (POEM) |
| **Hirschsprung Disease** | Congenital failure of neural crest migration to distal bowel; absence of both Auerbach and Meissner plexuses | Rectal suction biopsy: absence of ganglion cells and hypertrophied nerve trunks staining positive for Acetylcholinesterase | Surgical pull-through procedure (Swenson, Duhamel, or Soave technique) |
| **Diabetic Gastroparesis** | Autonomic neuropathy involving vagus nerve plus loss of Interstitial Cells of Cajal | Gastric emptying scintigraphy: > 10% meal retention at 4 hours | Prokinetics (metoclopramide, domperidone, oral erythromycin), tight glycemic control |
| **Systemic Sclerosis (Scleroderma)** | Fibrous replacement of muscularis propria and vascular ischemia of vasa nervorum | Manometry: absent peristalsis in lower two thirds of esophagus with hypotensive, incompetent LES | High-dose proton pump inhibitors to prevent ulceration; prokinetics |

#### 3. High-Resolution Esophageal Manometry Calculations

In the Chicago Classification (v4.0) of esophageal motility disorders, two quantitative parameters are clinically decisive:

1. **Integrated Relaxation Pressure (IRP):** The mean lowest residual pressure of the Lower Esophageal Sphincter during 4 contiguous or non-contiguous seconds within a 10-second window starting at upper sphincter opening. An IRP exceeding 15 mmHg indicates impaired esophagogastric junction outflow (classic for Achalasia or EGJ outflow obstruction).
2. **Distal Contractile Integral (DCI):** Quantifies the contractile vigor of smooth muscle peristalsis across the distal esophagus:

$$DCI = \sum (\text{Contractile Amplitude } [\text{mmHg}] \times \text{Duration } [\text{s}] \times \text{Length } [\text{cm}])$$

Expressed in units of $\text{mmHg}\cdot\text{s}\cdot\text{cm}$.

##### Worked Manometric Example

**Clinical Presentation:**  
A 38-year-old female presents with severe retrosternal chest pain and episodic solid and liquid dysphagia. High-resolution esophageal manometry is performed with ten test swallows of 5 mL water.

During a test swallow, esophageal peristalsis displays the following profile across the distal 10 cm segment:
- Mean contraction amplitude: $85\text{ mmHg}$ above baseline
- Mean contraction duration: $6.0\text{ seconds}$
- Segment length evaluated: $10\text{ cm}$

Calculate the Distal Contractile Integral ($DCI$):

$$DCI = 85\text{ mmHg} \times 6.0\text{ s} \times 10\text{ cm} = 5,100\text{ mmHg}\cdot\text{s}\cdot\text{cm}$$

**Diagnostic Classification Based on Chicago Criteria:**
- Failed peristalsis: $DCI < 100\text{ mmHg}\cdot\text{s}\cdot\text{cm}$
- Ineffective motility: $DCI < 450\text{ mmHg}\cdot\text{s}\cdot\text{cm}$
- Normal peristaltic vigor: $DCI = 450 \text{ to } 5,000\text{ mmHg}\cdot\text{s}\cdot\text{cm}$
- Hypercontractile (Jackhammer) esophagus: $DCI > 8,000\text{ mmHg}\cdot\text{s}\cdot\text{cm}$

With a calculated value of $5,100\text{ mmHg}\cdot\text{s}\cdot\text{cm}$, the contractile amplitude lies at the transition between vigorous normal peristalsis and hypercontractile motility. Combined with a measured normal median IRP of $9.2\text{ mmHg}$ ($< 15\text{ mmHg}$), outflow obstruction and achalasia are formally ruled out, pointing toward distal esophageal spasm or hypercontractile peristalsis.
