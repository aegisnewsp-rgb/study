# Kinetic Theory

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Kinetic theory models a gas as a huge number of identical, perfectly elastic, point-sized molecules moving randomly and obeying Newton's laws, with negligible intermolecular forces except during brief collisions. From this microscopic picture, all macroscopic gas laws (Boyle's, Charles's, Gay-Lussac's, Avogadro's) collapse into one equation, and pressure, temperature, and internal energy emerge as averages over molecular speeds.

| Quantity | Formula | SI unit |
|---|---|---|
| Ideal gas equation | $PV = nRT$ | P: Pa, V: m³, n: mol, T: K |
| Pressure from kinetic theory | $P = \frac{1}{3}\frac{N}{m}\overline{v^2}$ | Pa |
| RMS speed | $v_{rms} = \sqrt{\dfrac{3RT}{M}}$ | m s⁻¹ |
| Mean free path | $\lambda = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}\,\pi d^2 n}$ | m |
| Equipartition energy / DOF | $E_{avg} = \frac{1}{2}k_BT$ | J |

- **Degrees of freedom (NEET level):** monoatomic f = 3, diatomic f = 5, polyatomic f = 6 or 7.
- **γ = Cp/Cv = 1 + 2/f**, so γ = 5/3 for monoatomic, 7/5 for diatomic gases.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"PMR Vs"** — **P**ressure from momentum transfer, **M**ean free path is microscopic **V**s **s**peed; remember the speed order **v_p < v̄ < v_rms** with factors √2, √(8/π), √3 — "p less, mean more, RMS rules".

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Assumptions of Kinetic Theory

The postulates, when combined, let you derive every gas law from mechanics alone:

1. Gas consists of a very large number of identical molecules in continuous random motion.
2. Molecules are point particles — their actual volume is negligible compared with the container.
3. Molecules exert no force on one another except during collisions.
4. All collisions (molecule–molecule and molecule–wall) are perfectly elastic.
5. Collision time is negligible compared with the time between collisions.
6. Newton's laws hold for each molecule.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"Six S's"** for the postulates — **S**mall size, **S**mall collision time, **S**mooth (no force), **S**pringy (elastic), **S**huffled motion (random), **S**tandard Newton laws.

#### Derivation Outline — Pressure from Molecular Impacts

Consider N molecules, each of mass m, in a cube of side L moving with speeds averaged as $\overline{v^2}$. The change in momentum per wall hit is $2mv_x$, and the rate of hits is $v_x/(2L)$. Summing over all molecules and applying the equilibrium condition $\overline{v_x^2} = \overline{v_y^2} = \overline{v_z^2} = \overline{v^2}/3$ gives the key relation $PV = \frac{1}{3}Nm\overline{v^2}$. Dividing by V yields $P = \frac{1}{3}\rho\overline{v^2}$, where $\rho = Nm/V$ is mass density.

#### Equipartition and Internal Energy

Each degree of freedom contributes $\frac{1}{2}k_BT$ to the average molecular energy.

| Gas type | Translational DOF | Rotational DOF | Vibrational DOF | Total f (NEET) | γ = 1 + 2/f |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Monoatomic (He, Ar) | 3 | 0 | 0 | 3 | 1.67 |
| Diatomic (N₂, O₂) | 3 | 2 | 0 (frozen at room T) | 5 | 1.40 |
| Polyatomic (CO₂, H₂O) | 3 | 3 | 0 (frozen at NEET T) | 6 or 7 | 1.33 or 1.29 |

Internal energy depends only on T: $U = \frac{f}{2}nRT$ for an ideal gas.

#### Concept Comparison Matrix — Three Characteristic Speeds

| Speed | Formula | Relation to v_rms | When it appears |
|---|---|---|---|
| Most probable, $v_p$ | $\sqrt{2k_BT/m}$ | $v_p = \sqrt{2/3}\,v_{rms}$ | Peak of Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution |
| Mean (average), $\bar{v}$ | $\sqrt{8k_BT/\pi m}$ | $\bar{v} = \sqrt{8/(3\pi)}\,v_{rms}$ | Used in effusion (Graham's law) |
| RMS, $v_{rms}$ | $\sqrt{3k_BT/m} = \sqrt{3RT/M}$ | baseline | Appears in pressure & kinetic-energy formulas |

> 📌 **Formula Check:** Use $R = 8.314$ J·mol⁻¹·K⁻¹ with M in kg·mol⁻¹; switch to $R = 0.0821$ L·atm·mol⁻¹·K⁻¹ only if P is in atm and V in litres.

#### Mean Free Path

$\lambda = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}\,\pi d^2 n}$, where d is the effective molecular diameter (≈ 3 × 10⁻¹⁰ m for air) and n is number density. This explains why a gas smells travel metres, not the kilometres that the molecular speed (≈ 500 m/s) would suggest.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** At what temperature will the RMS speed of O₂ molecules equal the RMS speed of H₂ at 27 °C? (Molar masses: H₂ = 2 g·mol⁻¹, O₂ = 32 g·mol⁻¹.)

#### Solution:

Equating $v_{rms}$ for the two gases and cancelling $\sqrt{3R}$:

$$\sqrt{T_{O_2}/M_{O_2}} = \sqrt{T_{H_2}/M_{H_2}}$$

$$T_{O_2} = T_{H_2} \cdot \frac{M_{O_2}}{M_{H_2}} = (300\,\text{K}) \cdot \frac{32}{2} = 4800\,\text{K}$$

Step-by-step:

1. Convert 27 °C → 300 K (NEET answers need Kelvin).
2. Insert M in kg·mol⁻¹ (or keep g·mol⁻¹ — the ratio is dimensionless, so units cancel).
3. Solve: $T_{O_2} = 300 \times 16 = 4800$ K.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Forgetting to convert °C to K. Since $v_{rms} \propto \sqrt{T}$, plugging 27 instead of 300 gives an answer sixteen times too small — a classic one-step trap. Also, do NOT convert M to kg; the ratio 32/2 is the same in any mass unit.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Advanced Traps and Edge Cases

| Trap | Why it happens | Correct handling |
|---|---|---|
| Treating diatomic f as 7 always | Vibrational modes (kT ≈ ℏω) are frozen below a few thousand K | Use f = 5 for N₂, O₂, H₂ at NEET temperatures |
| Writing Cp − Cv = R for unit mass | The molar identity is Cp − Cv = R; per kg it is R/M | Divide by molar mass when using specific heats (c_p, c_v) |
| Using $P = nk_BT$ with mixed units | NEET numericals may give V in litres | Convert V → m³ (1 L = 10⁻³ m³) before using SI |
| Saying pressure comes from repulsion | Pressure arises from momentum transfer during elastic wall collisions | Apply Newton's 2nd/3rd law to wall impact |
| Believing gas constants are universal | R (per mole) vs k_B (per molecule) differ by N_A | R = N_A · k_B = 8.314 J·mol⁻¹·K⁻¹ |

#### Real Gases — Van der Waals Extension

Real gases deviate when density rises or T falls. The Van der Waals equation patches two failures of the ideal model:

$$\left(P + \frac{an^2}{V^2}\right)(V - nb) = nRT$$

- The $a/V^2$ term corrects **attractive** intermolecular forces (reduces P below ideal).
- The $nb$ term excludes the **finite volume** of molecules (reduces available V).
- At low pressure and high temperature, both a and b corrections shrink, recovering PV = nRT.

#### Brownian Motion and Experimental Evidence

Robert Brown (1827) and later Einstein (1905) showed that pollen grains suspended in water jitter because of unbalanced molecular impacts. The jitter's mean-square displacement is proportional to T and inversely to viscosity, providing direct experimental proof that molecules exist and move randomly.

#### Speed Distribution — Maxwell–Boltzmann

The fraction of molecules with speed between v and v+dv is $f(v)\,dv = 4\pi\!\left(\frac{m}{2\pi k_BT}\right)^{3/2}\!v^2 e^{-mv^2/2k_BT}\,dv$. From this distribution you can derive all three characteristic speeds and verify the equipartition theorem by averaging $v^2$.

#### Connection to Adjacent Topics

- **Thermodynamics:** $U = \frac{f}{2}nRT$ seeds the first law ΔU = nC_vΔT.
- **Waves & Sound:** RMS speed of air molecules sets the scale for sound propagation.
- **Heat Transfer:** Mean free path governs thermal conductivity κ ∝ ρ c_v v̄ λ of a gas.

#### Two Advanced Practice Prompts

1. A cylinder contains He at 27 °C and 1 atm. Find (a) the RMS speed of He atoms and (b) the mean kinetic energy of translation per atom. (M_He = 4 g·mol⁻¹.)
2. Two identical bulbs contain O₂ and N₂ at the same temperature. Compare their (i) RMS speeds, (ii) mean free paths if both have the same number density. (M_O₂ = 32, M_N₂ = 28 g·mol⁻¹.)

> 📌 **Formula Check:** For question 1, $v_{rms} = \sqrt{3RT/M}$; for the mean translational energy, use $\bar{E} = \frac{3}{2}k_BT$ — both are direct consequences of kinetic theory's three translational DOF.
