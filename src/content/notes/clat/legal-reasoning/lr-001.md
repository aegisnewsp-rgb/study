# Legal Maxims

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

A **legal maxim** is a short Latin or English phrase that states a settled rule of law. CLAT tests maxims through (1) meaning recall, (2) fact-pattern identification, and (3) consequence inference, usually 4–6 MCQs per paper.

- **Mens Rea** = guilty mind; **Actus Reus** = guilty act. Both needed for most crimes (Section 6 IPC).
- **Ignorantia Juris Non Excusat** = ignorance of law is no excuse.
- **Audi Alteram Partem** = hear the other side; pairs with **Nemo Judex In Causa Sua** (no one should be judge in their own cause).
- **Volenti Non Fit Injuria** = to a willing person, no injury is done (consent to risk).
- **Caveat Emptor** = let the buyer beware, now limited by Section 14, Sale of Goods Act, 1930 and the Consumer Protection Act, 2019.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Classification by Function

Legal maxims divide into three functional families. Knowing the family helps you eliminate distractors quickly in MCQs.

| Family | Purpose | Representative Maxims |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Substantive law | Creates or defines a right, duty, or liability | Mens Rea, Actus Reus, Volenti Non Fit Injuria, Caveat Emptor, Pacta Sunt Servanda |
| Procedural law | Governs how courts hear disputes | Audi Alteram Partem, Nemo Judex In Causa Sua, Habeas Corpus, Res Judicata |
| Evidence / interpretation | Guides proof and reading of documents | Res Ipsa Loquitur, Ejusdem Generis, Noscitur a Sociis, Expressio Unius Est Exclusio Alterius |

#### Maxims Governing Contractual Interpretation

Courts use four Latin canons when a contract's wording is ambiguous. Each produces a different inference, so do not mix them.

1. **Ejusdem Generis** — general words following specific words are restricted to the same class (e.g., "horses, cattle, or other animals" limits "animals" to four-legged livestock).
2. **Noscitur a Sociis** — a word takes its meaning from accompanying words.
3. **Expressio Unius Est Exclusio Alterius** — mentioning one thing excludes the other.
4. **Pacta Sunt Servanda** — agreements must be honoured, codified in Section 10, Indian Contract Act, 1872 as the basis of free consent.

#### Maxims Governing Criminal Liability

For a CLAT fact pattern, ask whether the accused performed the physical element (Actus Reus) and possessed the mental element (Mens Rea). Section 6 IPC and Sections 76–106 IPC codify this framework. **In pari delicto** (equal fault) blocks recovery when both parties are equally wrong.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Mapping Maxims to Indian Statutes

Exam questions frequently link a maxim to the section where it is enacted. Memorising these anchors turns a recall question into a 10-second pick.

| Maxim | Statutory Anchor | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mens Rea | Section 6, IPC 1860 | "Nothing is an offence done without a guilty mind" |
| Free consent / Pacta Sunt Servanda | Section 10–14, Indian Contract Act 1872 | Makes agreements enforceable |
| Caveat Emptor (modified) | Section 14, Sale of Goods Act 1930 | Implied conditions override the maxim in merchantable quality disputes |
| Audi Alteram Partem | Article 14, Constitution of India; principles of natural justice | Quasi-judicial orders without a hearing are void |
| Res Ipsa Loquitur | Common-law evidence doctrine | Permits negligence inference when the instrument causing harm is exclusively in the defendant's control |

#### Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

- Confusing **Audi Alteram Partem** (right to be heard) with **Nemo Judex In Causa Sua** (no bias). CLAT often pairs them in separate options for the same fact pattern.
- Applying **Volenti Non Fit Injuria** to negligence where the plaintiff's consent was partial or obtained under duress. Indian courts (e.g., *Hall v. Hebert*, 1993 SCC 12) restrict it to knowing assumption of obvious risk.
- Reading **Res Ipsa Loquitur** as conclusive proof of negligence. It only allows an *inference*, rebuttable by the defendant.
- Selecting **Caveat Emptor** for goods sold online or for services, which now fall under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 — a frequent 2024–2025 paper trap.

#### Practice Prompts

1. A driver parks on a hill without applying the handbrake; the car rolls and injures a pedestrian. Identify the maxim that lets the court presume negligence without further proof of how the brakes failed.
2. A tenant signs a lease containing a clause prohibiting "cattle, horses, sheep, or other livestock". The tenant later brings in pigs. Applying a canon of construction, decide whether pigs are permitted and state the reasoning.
