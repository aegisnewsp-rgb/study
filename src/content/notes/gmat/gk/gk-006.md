# Ghana's Cities, Districts and Population

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Ghana is divided into **16 regions**, each split into **metropolitan areas, municipalities, and ordinary districts** administered by Metropolitan, Municipal and District Assemblies (MMDAs). **Accra** (capital: Greater Accra Region) is the national capital and largest city; **Kumasi** (Ashanti Region) is the second largest.

| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Number of regions | 16 (six created in 2019) |
| Most recent census | 2021 Population & Housing Census |
| National population (2021) | 30,832,019 |
| Headline agency | Ghana Statistical Service (GSS) |

- Largest city: **Accra**; second largest: **Kumasi**; other major cities include Tamale, Sekondi-Takoradi, Cape Coast, Sunyani, Ho, Koforidua, Bolgatanga.
- Constitutional basis for districts: **1992 Constitution, Chapter 20** plus the **Local Governance Act, 2016 (Act 936)**.

#### The 16 Regions and Their Capitals

The six new regions created in 2019 — **Western North, Ahafo, Bono East, Oti, Savannah and North East** — were carved out of existing regions, raising the total from 10 to 16. Each region has a **regional capital** that is usually its largest settlement but is not always the national capital.

#### District Classification and Local Governance

Districts are **statutory creations** under Chapter 20 of the 1992 Constitution. They fall into three classes:

- **Metropolitan**: population ≥ 250,000 (in practice often ≥ 1 million).
- **Municipal**: population ≥ 95,000.
- **Ordinary District**: all remaining rural-leaning areas.

Each is governed by an **MMDA** with a Presiding Member, a Chief Executive appointed by the President, and elected assembly members. About **261 MMDAs** are currently recognised after successive splits.

#### Population Distribution and the 2021 Census

The **Ghana Statistical Service (GSS)** conducts a decennial census. The 2021 figure of **30,832,019** supersedes the 2010 count of about 24.6 million. **Greater Accra** is the most densely populated region because it concentrates government, port and financial activity; the **Northern, Upper East, Upper West, Savannah and North East** regions remain sparsely populated despite covering a large land area.

> Exam tip: GAT almost always frames a regional question as "which region is the most densely populated?" — answer **Greater Accra**, not Ashanti, even though Ashanti has the second-largest population.

#### Population Formulas Students Must Recognise

Several formulas appear in GAT General Knowledge numerical items and demographic reasoning passages:

- **Population Density** D = P ÷ A, where P is total population (persons) and A is land area in km², giving **persons/km²**.
- **Annual Growth Rate** r = [(P₂/P₁)^(1/n) − 1] × 100. Between 2010 (~24.66 million) and 2021 (30,832,019), n = 11 years, yielding roughly **2.05%** per year — useful for projection MCQs.
- **Sex Ratio** = (Males ÷ Females) × 100. Ghana's 2021 sex ratio is approximately **97 males per 100 females**, slightly skewed by male out-migration from northern regions.
- **Urbanisation Rate** = (Urban Population ÷ Total Population) × 100. Ghana crossed the **50% urban threshold** around the 2010s.
- **Dependency Ratio** = [(Population 0–14 + Population 65+) ÷ Population 15–64] × 100. A youthful structure keeps this ratio high, framing the **demographic dividend** policy debate.

#### Edge Cases and Common Traps

- **Confusing creation date**: the six new regions were created by **Constitutional Instruments in 2019**, not 2000 or 2010.
- **Conflating regional and national capital**: only **Accra** is both; Kumasi, Cape Coast, Tamale, Ho and Sunyani are regional capitals only.
- **Outdated district counts**: ignore figures like 110, 138 or 170 — the current number is around **261 MMDAs**.
- **Census year**: use **2021**, not 2020, for the latest headline; the fieldwork spanned late 2021 into 2022 but the reference date and reporting year is 2021.
- **Land area**: Ghana's total area is roughly **238,533 km²**, so average national density sits near **129 persons/km²**, but Greater Accra exceeds **1,200 persons/km²** while Northern savannah regions fall below **50**.

#### Worked Reasoning Prompt

> Practice: If Greater Accra had 5,455,692 people in 2021 across about 3,245 km², compute its population density.
> D = 5,455,692 ÷ 3,245 ≈ **1,681 persons/km²** — roughly 13 times the national average. This justifies the "most densely populated region" claim.

#### Practice Prompts

1. List the six regions created in 2019 and their respective capitals.
2. If Ghana's population grows at 2.05% annually from 30,832,019 in 2021, estimate the 2031 population. (Answer ≈ **37.7 million**, applying the growth-rate formula above.)
