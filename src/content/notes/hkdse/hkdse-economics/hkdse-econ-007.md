# G. National Income Determination and Price Level — HKDSE Economics Notes

This topic covers Topic G of the Compulsory Part of HKDSE Economics. It addresses the simple Keynesian model (aggregate demand, equilibrium national income, the multiplier); the AD-AS model (aggregate demand, short-run aggregate supply, long-run aggregate supply); fiscal and monetary policy in the AD-AS framework; and the relationship between inflation and unemployment (the Phillips curve).

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Aggregate demand (AD)**: total planned expenditure on domestic goods and services at each price level. AD = C + I + G + (X - M). Slopes downward.
- **Aggregate supply (AS)**: total planned output at each price level.
- **Short-run aggregate supply (SRAS)**: slopes upward. Firms adjust output in response to demand changes in the short run.
- **Long-run aggregate supply (LRAS)**: vertical at the natural level of output. The economy returns to potential in the long run.
- **Equilibrium**: where AD = AS.
- **Multiplier**: a change in autonomous spending causes a larger change in equilibrium income. Multiplier = 1 / (1 - MPC), where MPC is the marginal propensity to consume.
- **Fiscal policy**: government spending (G) and taxation (T). Expansionary: increase G or decrease T (shift AD right). Contractionary: decrease G or increase T (shift AD left).
- **Monetary policy**: central bank's actions on interest rates and money supply. Expansionary: lower interest rates or increase money supply (shift AD right). Contractionary: raise interest rates or decrease money supply (shift AD left).
- **Inflation**: a sustained rise in the general price level.
- **Unemployment**: people willing and able to work but not employed.
- **Phillips curve**: shows the inverse relationship between inflation and unemployment. In the short run: lower inflation → higher unemployment; in the long run, the Phillips curve is vertical at the natural rate of unemployment.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing SRAS with LRAS — SRAS slopes upward; LRAS is vertical.
- Treating the multiplier as infinite — the multiplier assumes idle resources and constant prices; in reality, full-employment ceilings and rising prices limit the effect.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Simple Keynesian model

The simple Keynesian model assumes:

- Prices are fixed (at least in the short run).
- Output is determined by aggregate demand.
- Investment is autonomous (independent of income).

Equilibrium income Y is where AD = Y:

Y = C + I + G + (X - M) = (C₀ + c·Y) + I + G + (X - M)

Solving for Y: Y = (1/(1-c)) · (C₀ + I + G + X - M)

The multiplier (1/(1-c)) amplifies the effect of changes in autonomous spending.

**Marginal propensity to consume (MPC)**: the fraction of an extra dollar of disposable income spent. 0 < MPC < 1.

**Marginal propensity to save (MPS)**: 1 - MPC.

The multiplier = 1 / (1 - MPC) = 1 / MPS.

**Example**: MPC = 0.8, ΔI = $100 million → ΔY = $100 × (1/0.2) = $500 million.

#### AD-AS model

**Aggregate demand (AD)**: total planned expenditure at each price level. Slopes downward because:

- **Wealth effect**: higher P → lower real wealth → lower C.
- **Interest rate effect**: higher P → higher interest rates → lower I.
- **Exchange rate effect**: higher P → weaker currency → more M, less X.

**Short-run aggregate supply (SRAS)**: slopes upward because of sticky wages, sticky prices, and misperceptions.

**Long-run aggregate supply (LRAS)**: vertical at the natural level of output (potential GDP). Determined by technology, capital stock, labour force, and institutions — not by the price level.

**Equilibrium**:

- AD = SRAS at the short-run equilibrium.
- AD = LRAS at the long-run equilibrium.
- If short-run equilibrium is to the left of LRAS, output is below potential (recessionary gap).
- If short-run equilibrium is to the right of LRAS, output is above potential (inflationary gap).

#### Fiscal and monetary policy

**Fiscal policy** (government actions on G and T):

- **Expansionary fiscal policy**: increase G or decrease T → AD shifts right → output rises, unemployment falls, inflation rises.
- **Contractionary fiscal policy**: decrease G or increase T → AD shifts left → output falls, unemployment rises, inflation falls.
- **Automatic stabilisers**: built-in stabilisers (e.g. progressive income tax, unemployment benefits) that dampen fluctuations without explicit action.

**Monetary policy** (central bank actions on interest rates and money supply):

- **Expansionary monetary policy**: lower interest rates or buy assets → AD shifts right → output rises.
- **Contractionary monetary policy**: raise interest rates or sell assets → AD shifts left → output falls.

**Time lags**: fiscal policy (long lags); monetary policy (shorter lags but still significant).

#### Phillips curve

**Short-run Phillips curve**: inverse relationship between inflation and unemployment. Lower unemployment → higher inflation.

**Long-run Phillips curve**: vertical at the natural rate of unemployment. Monetary policy cannot permanently reduce unemployment below the natural rate; it can only cause higher inflation.

**Expectations-augmented Phillips curve**: inflation depends on expected inflation, the unemployment gap, and supply shocks.

#### Inflation and unemployment

**Inflation types**:

- **Demand-pull**: AD shifts right beyond potential output.
- **Cost-push**: SRAS shifts left (e.g. oil price shock).

**Unemployment types**:

- **Frictional**: workers transitioning between jobs.
- **Structural**: mismatch between workers' skills and jobs' requirements.
- **Cyclical**: due to a recessionary gap (below potential).
- **Natural rate**: the rate of unemployment consistent with potential output.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Exam technique

- **For AD-AS questions**: identify the shift (AD or AS); determine the effect on output and price level.
- **For multiplier questions**: calculate the multiplier; apply to a change in autonomous spending.
- **For Phillips curve questions**: distinguish short run from long run; identify supply shocks.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "The multiplier is always positive." A negative MPC would give a negative multiplier; in reality, 0 < MPC < 1.
- "The Phillips curve is a permanent trade-off." The long-run Phillips curve is vertical; only short-run trade-offs exist.
- "Lower inflation always reduces welfare." Moderate inflation can be a sign of a healthy economy; hyperinflation is the concern.
