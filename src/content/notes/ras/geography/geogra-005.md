# Natural Vegetation of Rajasthan

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Rajasthan's natural vegetation is shaped by an arid-to-sub-humid rainfall gradient across the Aravallis and the Thar. Five **Champion & Seth (1968)** forest types occur: Desert Thorn (5/DS1), Dry Deciduous *Anogeissus–Acacia* (5/DSA1), Dry Teak (5A/C1, in southern Banswara), Dry Bamboo (5/E5), and Desert Euphorbia Scrub (5/DS2). Forest cover is about **9.57 %** of the state's 3,42,239 km² (ISFR 2021) — well below the 33 % National Forest Policy target.

- **State Tree:** Khejri (*Prosopis cineraria*) — native, nitrogen-fixing, deep taproot.
- **State Flower:** Rohida (*Tecomella undulata*) — threatened, Burseraceae-linked timber.
- **State Bird:** Great Indian Bustard — Desert National Park, Jaisalmer/Barmer.
- **Largest sal forests:** Southern Aravallis (Udaipur–Chittorgarh–Banswara).
- **Ramsar wetland (often confused as forest):** Keoladeo Ghana, Bharatpur.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"K-K-R-G-B"** = **K**hejri (tree), **K**umath, **R**ohida (flower), **G**reat Indian Bustard (bird), **B**anni Grasslands (restoration) — five symbols RPSC repeats every year.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Rainfall–Vegetation Zones

Rajasthan's flora maps cleanly onto four rainfall isohyets. Below 250 mm, only xeric ephemerals and *Calligonum polygonoides* (Phog) survive on sand dunes. The 250–500 mm belt produces **Tropical Thorn Forest** dominated by *Acacia senegal*, *Capparis decidua* (Ker), and *Prosopis cineraria*. Between 500–750 mm, **Tropical Dry Deciduous** stands of *Anogeissus pendula* (Dhok) take over; above 750 mm, on the southern Aravalli slopes and Mount Abu, sub-tropical broad-leaved hill forest appears.

- **< 250 mm:** Phog, Aak (*Calotropis*), thorn scrub.
- **250–500 mm:** Khejri, Kumath, Babool (native), Ker.
- **500–750 mm:** Dhok, Salar (*Boswellia serrata*), Khair.
- **> 750 mm:** Teak pockets, Bamboo, *Sterculia*, *Lannea* — restricted to Banswara, Dungarpur, Mount Abu.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** The forest cover of Rajasthan is approximately 9.57 % of its geographical area. If the state's total geographical area is 3,42,239 km², calculate (a) the actual forest area in km², and (b) the percentage shortfall against the National Forest Policy target of 33 %.

#### Solution:
- (a) **FC = (9.57 / 100) × 3,42,239 = 32,752.3 km²**
  - Formula used: **FC % = (F_A / T_A) × 100** ⟹ F_A = (FC % × T_A) / 100
- (b) **Shortfall = 33 − 9.57 = 23.43 percentage points**
  - Equivalent missing area = (23.43 / 100) × 3,42,239 ≈ **80,193 km²** still to be afforested to meet the 33 % benchmark.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students often write "Rajasthan has the highest forest cover in India." It does not — Madhya Pradesh and Arunachal Pradesh lead. Rajasthan is among the **top five by absolute area** but ranks **near the bottom by percentage cover**. Quote ISFR 2021 specifically to score full marks.

#### Conservation Hotspots

- **Ranthambore & Sariska:** Dry deciduous, tiger reserve.
- **Desert National Park:** Great Indian Bustard breeding habitat.
- **Keoladeo Ghana:** UNESCO + Ramsar, wintering Siberian cranes.
- **Mount Abu WLS:** Only sub-tropical hill forest in Rajasthan.
- **Banni Grasslands:** Community-led sewan grass restoration, Kutch-border districts.

#### Quantitative Ecology of Arid Forests

Field studies in Jaisalmer and Jodhpur use the **Shannon–Wiener index H′ = −Σ(pᵢ · ln pᵢ)** to compare disturbed vs. protected thorn stands. Mature Khejri–Ker woodlands record **H′ ≈ 2.1–2.6**; *Prosopis juliflora*-invaded IG Canal sites drop to **H′ ≈ 0.8–1.2**, signalling collapse of native species evenness. Biomass estimation uses **B (t/ha) = 0.25 · Σ(Dᵢ² · Hᵢ · Nᵢ) / 10,000**, where D is diameter at breast height (cm), H is height (m), N is stems per hectare — useful for Mains data-interpretation questions.

- **D = diameter at breast height (cm)**, **H = height (m)**, **N = stem count (per ha)**, **B = woody biomass (kg/ha)**.
- CDl (Canopy Density Index) is computed as **CDI = Σ(Aᵢ · dᵢ) / A_total**, with crown density factor dᵢ between 0.1 (open scrub) and 1.0 (closed canopy).

#### Edge Cases and Frequent Traps

1. *Prosopis juliflora* spreads through the **Indira Gandhi Canal Command Area (Ganganagar, Hanumangarh)**; declared a noxious weed under Rajasthan Forest Act, 1953 (amended 2017).
2. **Chambal ravines of Dholpur-Karauli** host semi-evergreen elements (*Butea*, *Diospyros*) atypical of the broader arid matrix.
3. **Rohida (Tecomella undulata)** is monotypic in its genus — confusing it with *Tectona* (teak) loses marks in mains.
4. **Salar (Boswellia serrata)** yields **guggul/lac** and is restricted to southern Aravallis; it is **not** the same as Sal tree of central India.
5. **Mining threat:** Aravalli ranges near Udaipur face limestone quarrying, fragmenting *Anogeissus pendula* corridors.
6. **Great Indian Bustard** population fell below **< 150** (2023 estimate) — the "Project Bustard" breeding programme at Sam (Jaisalmer) is a probable mains topic.
7. **Banni Grasslands** restoration is in **Pali–Barmer border districts**, not Gujarat's Banni — examiners exploit this routinely.
8. **Dholpur–Bundi stone** holds a GI tag but is mineral, not vegetation; it appears in mixed-bag RPSC questions.

#### Exam Strategy

- Prelims typically tests **3–4 MCQs** on: state symbols, ISFR percentages, Champion–Seth codes, protected-area districts.
- Mains GS-I expects a **150-word short note** on "Indira Gandhi Canal and ecological change" or "Conservation challenges of Aravalli forests."
- Memorise: forest cover % (9.57), geographical area (3,42,239 km²), state symbols trio, and five Champion–Seth codes — these four items cover ≈ 80 % of past questions.
- Always pair a species with its district cluster, not its botanical family alone, to demonstrate applied geography.
