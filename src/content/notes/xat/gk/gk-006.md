# General Science and Technology

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your XAT.

**General Science and Technology** in XAT GK tests two layers: (1) static fundamentals from Physics, Chemistry and Biology at the class 9–10 NCERT level, and (2) current S&T developments from the last 12–18 months. The GK section carries roughly **3% weightage** (~8 questions), has **no negative marking**, and no sectional cut-off — so attempt every question.

> Exam tip: Educated guessing is "free" here because a wrong answer costs you zero.

#### Must-know formulas and units

| Concept | Formula | Variables & units |
|---|---|---|
| Newton's 2nd law | F = m·a | F in newton (N), m in kg, a in m/s² |
| Ohm's law | V = I·R | V in volts, I in amperes, R in ohms |
| Mass–energy | E = m·c² | E in joules, c ≈ 3 × 10⁸ m/s |
| Ideal gas | PV = nRT | R = 8.314 J/(mol·K), T in kelvin |

**High-yield pointers:** remember SI base units (m, kg, s, A, K, mol, cd), pH < 7 = acid, > 7 = base, deficiency of **vitamin C = scurvy**, **vitamin D = rickets**, mitosis = 2 daughter cells, meiosis = 4. Sound needs a medium; light does not.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Build working fluency across Physics, Chemistry, Biology and current S&T.

#### Physics fundamentals XAT expects

XAT rarely tests derivation — it tests recall of the *right* law attached to the *right* phenomenon. The pattern is a one-line stem ("A body at rest stays at rest unless…") followed by four close options. Memorise Newton's three laws, the gravitation formula **F = G·m₁m₂/r²** with G = 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg², and the mirror equation **1/f = 1/v + 1/u** for lens/mirror questions.

#### Chemistry block — periodic table, bonding, pH

The periodic table gives XAT its most common chemistry stems: identify the group of an element from atomic number, or pick the lightest noble gas (helium). Bonding types — **ionic (metal + non-metal, electron transfer), covalent (non-metals, electron sharing), metallic (delocalised electron sea)** — appear as single MCQs.

> Trap alert: Students often mark acids as basic because of the word "hydrogen". Recall: pH < 7 acidic, = 7 neutral, > 7 basic.

The pH formula **pH = −log₁₀[H⁺]** has shown up indirectly (e.g., "which solution has the highest H⁺ concentration?").

#### Biology — cells, vitamins, disease, genetics

Cell organelles (nucleus = control, mitochondria = powerhouse, ribosomes = protein synthesis) and the mitosis-vs-meiosis contrast are perennial favourites. Vitamin-deficiency matching is asked almost every year:

| Vitamin | Deficiency disease |
|---|---|
| A | Night blindness |
| B1 | Beriberi |
| C | Scurvy |
| D | Rickets |
| K | Slow blood clotting |

For current S&T, track **Chandrayaan-3** (lunar south-soft landing, Aug 2023), **Aditya-L1** (India's first solar observatory, launched Sept 2023), **Gaganyaan** (uncrewed test flights in 2024), and **DRDO missiles** — Agni (long-range ballistic), BrahMos (supersonic cruise), Akash (medium-range SAM), Prithvi (short-range tactical).

#### Computer basics and IT

Questions test definitions: hardware vs software, RAM vs ROM, input/output devices, **HTTP/HTTPS**, cloud computing, IoT, and a working definition of AI/ML. AI/ML is the most-tested modern IT topic.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Edge cases, mechanism-level traps, and exam strategy.

#### Mechanism traps examiners exploit

1. **SI vs CGS confusion** — Force: 1 N = 10⁵ dyne; Energy: 1 J = 10⁷ erg. Examiners offer both units in options; pick the SI one unless asked otherwise.
2. **Sound vs light** — Sound needs a *medium* and travels fastest in solids; light needs *no* medium and is fastest in vacuum. Human hearing range is **20 Hz–20 kHz**; below 20 Hz is infrasonic, above is ultrasonic.
3. **Ohm's law sign trap** — V = IR is valid only for ohmic conductors at constant temperature; filament bulbs deviate as temperature rises.
4. **Radioactivity** — α particles (He nuclei, +2 charge), β particles (electrons, −1 charge), γ rays (no charge, no mass). Penetrating power: γ > β > α.
5. **Mitosis vs meiosis** — Mitosis: somatic cells, 2 diploid daughters; Meiosis: germ cells, 4 haploid daughters. Crossing-over occurs only in meiosis I.

#### Connections to adjacent topics

GK overlaps with **Decision Making** when XAT embeds an ethical S&T dilemma (e.g., CRISPR gene editing in humans). It also touches **Essay Writing** (topics like "AI and the future of work" recur). Treat General Science as a *current-affairs feeder* — Nobel Prizes in Physics/Chemistry 2024, AI-related breakthroughs, and any new ISRO milestone (e.g., **Gaganyaan's first crewed flight schedule**) become essay fuel too.

#### Worked micro-example

A 2 kg block is pushed with 10 N on a frictionless surface. Using F = m·a, acceleration a = F/m = 10/2 = **5 m/s²**. After 4 s, velocity v = u + at = 0 + 5×4 = 20 m/s. Kinetic energy KE = ½mv² = ½×2×20² = **400 J**.

> Common mistake: Forgetting to square velocity — students often write KE = mv = 40 J and lose the mark.

#### Strategy for XAT GK

The 3% weightage means **time efficiency beats depth**. Spend ≤ 5 minutes on the full GK section. Read one good S&T current-affairs capsule (last 18 months) and revise NCERT class 9–10 science summaries twice. No negative marking ⇒ mark every question.

#### Practice prompts

1. A solution has [H⁺] = 10⁻⁹ M. What is its pH, and is it acidic or basic?
2. Match each missile — Agni, BrahMos, Akash, Prithvi — to its primary role (intercontinental / cruise / surface-to-air / short-range tactical).
