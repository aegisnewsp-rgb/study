# Mathematics and Logical Reasoning

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The quantitative and logical reasoning section tests your ability to handle numbers, interpret data, and think systematically. Focus on arithmetic shortcuts, basic algebra, percentage calculations, averages, ratios, and common logical reasoning patterns. These skills are used in everyday professional life as well as in exam contexts.

**High-Yield Facts for Qimiyah:**
- **Percentage:** To find X% of Y: (X/100) × Y. To express A as % of B: (A/B) × 100
- **Average:** Sum of values / number of values — even if the distribution is unequal, the average lies between min and max
- **Ratio:** If A:B = 3:4 and B = 20, then A = 15 (3/4 × 20)
- **Simple interest:** I = PRT/100 (P = principal, R = rate, T = time in years)
- **Probability:** P(event) = Number of favorable outcomes / Total number of possible outcomes
- ⚡ Exam tip: When solving percentage problems, use the fraction method — 25% = 1/4, 20% = 1/5, 10% = 1/10 — this is faster than decimal multiplication

## Arithmetic — Core Skills

### Percentages

**Finding percentages:**
- 10% of 80 = 8 (simply move the decimal point: 10% × 80 = 0.10 × 80 = 8)
- 15% of 200 = 30 (10% = 20, 5% = 10, so 15% = 30)
- 7.5% of 400 = 30 (7.5% = 75/1000; 75/1000 × 400 = 30)

**Percentage increase/decrease:**
- Original price = 80; new price = 100 → Increase = (100-80)/80 × 100 = 25%
- Original = 200; new = 160 → Decrease = (200-160)/200 × 100 = 20%

**Percentage shortcuts:**
- X% of Y = Y% of X ( commutative property of multiplication): 8% of 25 = 25% of 8 = 2
- To increase by X%: multiply by (100+X)%; to decrease by X%: multiply by (100–X)%
- A is X% more than B → A = B × (1 + X/100)

**Compound percentage:**
- A value increases by 10% each year: multiply by 1.10 each year
- Starting value = 1000; after 3 years at 10% compound: 1000 × 1.1³ = 1331

### Averages

**Formula:** Average = (Sum of all values) / (Number of values)

**Example:** Test scores: 70, 80, 85, 90, 75
- Sum = 400; Number = 5 → Average = 80

**Weighted average:**
- If group A has average 60 with 10 people; group B has average 80 with 5 people
- Combined average = (60×10 + 80×5) / 15 = (600 + 400) / 15 = 1000/15 ≈ 66.7

**Common trap:** The average of averages is NOT the overall average unless all groups have the same size.

### Ratios and Proportions

**Ratio A:B** expresses the relative size of A to B.
- If A:B = 3:4 and A + B = 140 → A = 3/7 × 140 = 60; B = 4/7 × 140 = 80

**Direct proportion:** When one value increases, the other increases at the same rate (A ∝ B)
**Inverse proportion:** When one value increases, the other decreases (A ∝ 1/B)

**Sharing in a ratio:** If money is divided in ratio A:B:C = 2:3:5 and total = 1000:
- Total parts = 2+3+5 = 10; A = 2/10 × 1000 = 200; B = 300; C = 500

### Profit and Loss

**Profit % = (Profit / Cost Price) × 100**
**Loss % = (Loss / Cost Price) × 100**
**Selling Price (SP) = Cost Price (CP) + Profit = CP – Loss**

**Example:** CP = 80, SP = 100 → Profit = 20 → Profit % = 20/80 × 100 = 25%
**Example:** SP = 90, profit = 10 → CP = SP – profit = 80 → Profit % = 10/80 × 100 = 12.5%

**Discount:** Marked Price (MP) → Discount% → Selling Price
- MP = 200; discount 15% → SP = 200 × 0.85 = 170

### Simple and Compound Interest

**Simple Interest:** I = P × R × T / 100
- P = 1000, R = 5%, T = 3 years → I = 1000 × 5 × 3 / 100 = 150
- Total amount = P + I = 1150

**Compound Interest:** Amount = P × (1 + R/100)^T
- P = 1000, R = 5%, T = 2 years → Amount = 1000 × 1.05 × 1.05 = 1102.5
- CI = 1102.5 – 1000 = 102.5 (vs simple interest of 100)

## Algebra — Basic Operations

### Linear Equations
- Solve for x: 3x + 7 = 22 → 3x = 15 → x = 5
- Word problem: "A number plus 5 equals 12" → x + 5 = 12 → x = 7

### Quadratic Equations
Standard form: ax² + bx + c = 0
**Quadratic formula:** x = [–b ± √(b² – 4ac)] / 2a

**Example:** x² – 5x + 6 = 0
- Factor: (x – 2)(x – 3) = 0 → x = 2 or x = 3
- Or: a=1, b=–5, c=6 → discriminant = 25 – 24 = 1 → x = (5 ± 1)/2 → x = 3 or 2

### Simultaneous Equations
Solve: 2x + y = 10 and x + y = 6
- Subtract second from first: x = 4 → substitute: 4 + y = 6 → y = 2

### Exponents and Powers
- aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ
- aᵐ / aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ
- (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ
- a⁰ = 1 (for a ≠ 0)
- a⁻¹ = 1/a

## Geometry — Essential Facts

### Angles
- Sum of angles in a triangle = **180°**
- Sum of angles in a quadrilateral = **360°**
- Straight angle = **180°**
- Angles around a point = **360°**
- Complementary angles sum to 90°; supplementary angles sum to 180°

### Triangle Types
- **Equilateral:** All sides equal (60° each)
- **Isosceles:** Two sides equal; base angles equal
- **Right triangle:** One angle = 90°; Pythagorean theorem applies

### Pythagorean Theorem
- For a right triangle with legs a and b, hypotenuse c: **a² + b² = c²**
- Classic 3-4-5 triangle: 3² + 4² = 5² → 9 + 16 = 25
- Other common Pythagorean triples: 5-12-13, 8-15-17, 7-24-25

### Circles
- **Circumference:** C = 2πr = πd
- **Area:** A = πr²
- **Arc length:** (θ/360) × 2πr where θ is the angle in degrees
- **Sector area:** (θ/360) × πr²

### Areas and Volumes

| Shape | Area Formula | Volume Formula |
|---|---|---|
| Rectangle | l × w | — |
| Triangle | ½ × base × height | — |
| Circle | πr² | — |
| Cube | 6a² | a³ |
| Cylinder | 2πr(r+h) | πr²h |
| Sphere | 4πr² | ⁴⁄₃πr³ |

## Probability and Statistics

### Probability
- **P(event)** = Favorable outcomes / Total outcomes
- **P(always true)** = 1; **P(impossible)** = 0
- **P(not A)** = 1 – P(A) (complement rule)
- **P(A or B)** = P(A) + P(B) – P(A and B) (addition rule — only if events are not mutually exclusive)

**Independent events:** P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B)
- Example: Probability of getting two heads in two coin tosses = ½ × ½ = ¼

**Mutually exclusive events:** Cannot happen at the same time → P(A and B) = 0

### Statistics

**Mean (Average):** Sum / Count
**Median:** The middle value when data is arranged in order (if two middle values, average them)
**Mode:** Most frequently occurring value (can be no mode, one mode, or multiple modes)
**Range:** Maximum value – minimum value

**Standard deviation:** Measures spread from the mean — higher SD means more spread

## Logical Reasoning

### Number Series
Find the pattern:
- 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 → Pattern: multiply by 2 (powers of 2)
- 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8 → Pattern: Fibonacci (sum of previous two)
- 3, 6, 11, 18, 27 → Pattern: differences are 3, 5, 7, 9 (odd numbers)

### Coding and Decoding
- If "APPLE" is coded as "BQQMF" → each letter shifted by +1 in the alphabet
- "BANANA" in this code would be "CBOBOF"
- Reverse alphabet coding: A=26, B=25... Z=1 → "APPLE" would be... (A=26, P=11...)

### Blood Relations
- "A is B's brother" → A and B share at least one parent; A is male
- "A is B's sister" → A and B share at least one parent; A is female
- "A is B's mother" → A is female; one generation above B
- "A is B's daughter" → A is female; one generation below B
- **Logical deduction:** If all X are Y, and Z is X → Z is Y. If some X are Y, and Z is X → Z *might* be Y.

### Syllogisms
**Structure:** Premise 1 + Premise 2 → Conclusion

**Example:**
- Premise 1: All mammals are warm-blooded
- Premise 2: All whales are mammals
- Conclusion: All whales are warm-blooded ✓

**Invalid example:**
- Premise 1: All A are B
- Premise 2: All C are B
- Conclusion: All A are C ✗ (just because A and C are both B doesn't mean A = C)

### Data Interpretation
- Read tables, charts, and graphs carefully
- Identify the trend (increasing, decreasing, constant)
- Calculate percentages or ratios as needed
- Check units — ensure consistency

## Common Exam Traps

1. **Misreading the decimal point** in percentage calculations — always double-check
2. **Forgetting that average of averages** is not the overall average (need weighted average)
3. **Compound interest vs simple interest** — compound grows faster over time; make sure you use the right formula
4. **Assuming a pattern in number series continues the same way** — always verify by checking all given terms
5. **Confusing median and mode** — median is the middle value; mode is the most frequent value
6. **Forgetting to square the difference** in the quadratic formula — the discriminant is b² – 4ac (not b – 4ac)

⚡ **Exam tip:** In the heat of the exam, many students forget that "percentage points" and "percentage change" are different. A change from 20% to 25% is a 5 percentage point increase AND a 25% increase relative to the original (5/20 × 100 = 25%). Watch for this trick.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

#### Successive percentage changes compound, they do not add

If a value first rises by 20% and then falls by 20%, the answer is not the original value. Multipliers, not percentages, govern the result: 1.20 × 0.80 = 0.96, so the value ends 4% below where it started. The same holds in the other order — 0.80 × 1.20 = 0.96 — so the order of two percentage changes does not matter, only the set of multipliers does.

That identity generalises: a rise of a% followed by a fall of a% always returns you to (1 − a²/100) of the original, so the net loss is always less than most candidates guess and never negative.

| Operation | Multiplier |
|---|---|
| Increase by 25% | 1.25 |
| Decrease by 25% | 0.75 |
| Increase by 100% | 2.00 |
| Decrease by 100% | 0.00 |

#### Reversing a percentage statement

Three phrasings of the same arithmetic trip up most candidates, and the exam rotates between them deliberately.

| What the question says | What to divide | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 40 is 20% of what? | 20% | 200 |
| 120 is what percent of 200? | 200 | 60% |
| A rises from 80 to 100; percent change? | 80, the base | 25% |

The rule that carries all three: the percentage is always calculated **on the base**, and the base is the earlier value in time. Whenever "percent" appears and you are unsure which way to divide, name the base out loud first.

#### Assumed-mean shortcut for averages

Given five values averaging 26 with one value unknown, do not reconstruct the missing entry arithmetically. Shift every value by a round number so that most become zero or near-zero.

Take 25 as the assumed mean. Suppose the four known values are 18, 24, 32, 22. Their deviations from 25 are −7, −1, +7, −3, which sum to −4. If the total deviation is zero, the unknown value deviates by +4, so the unknown value is 29. Verification: 18 + 24 + 32 + 22 + 29 = 125, and 125 ÷ 5 = 26. This turns a six-line computation into one line whenever the known values cluster.

#### Average of averages needs weights

A class of 30 boys averaging 70 and a class of 20 girls averaging 80 do not average to 75. That figure is the unweighted mean of two means and it is almost always a distractor. The weighted result is (30 × 70 + 20 × 80) ÷ 50 = 3700 ÷ 50 = 74. Always reconstruct the raw totals before dividing.

#### Successive discounts are not additive

Two discounts of 20% then 10% leave 0.80 × 0.90 = 0.72 of the price, an effective discount of 28%, not 30%. The shortcut worth memorising: for two discounts, effective % off = a + b − ab/100.

| Pair | Additive (wrong) | Multiplied (correct) |
|---|---|---|
| 20% + 10% | 30% | 28% |
| 25% + 25% | 50% | 43.75% |
| 40% + 10% | 50% | 46% |

For an equivalent single discount matching a sequence, divide the remaining fraction by itself: 0.72 × 0.72 = 0.5184, so the square root gives an equivalent single discount of about 28.02% — barely different from 28% here, but the gap widens as the discounts grow.

#### Profit percentage is defined against the cost price

A seller buys at ₹80 and sells at ₹100. The gain is ₹20 on a cost of ₹80, so the profit is 25% — not 20%, which would be the gain measured against the selling price. Exam options regularly include the selling-price version precisely because students divide by the wrong base.

When an article is sold at a profit of 25% and the seller still makes a profit of 10% overall, the second 10% is on the new selling price, not the cost. Find the composite by multiplying: 1.25 × 1.10 = 1.375, i.e. 37.5% on cost. An equivalent loss works the same way, with multipliers below 1.

#### Simple versus compound interest

Simple interest pays a flat rate on the original principal every period. Compound interest credits each period's interest back into the balance, so the interest itself earns interest. The gap between them is what makes repeated percentages behave differently from repeated sums.

If the interest under compound rates doubles the amount, then 2 = (1 + r/100)ⁿ, so 1 + r/100 = 2^(1/n). For n periods: 3 years gives about 25.99%, 10 years gives about 7.18%, 20 years gives about 3.53%. Memorising these three covers most compounding questions faster than any formula.

#### Quadratic roots without solving

For ax² + bx + c = 0, the discriminant D = b² − 4ac decides the nature of the roots, and the sum and product of the roots are −b/a and c/a.

| D | Nature of roots |
|---|---|
| D < 0 | No real roots; the graph cuts neither axis |
| D = 0 | Two equal real roots; the graph touches the axis |
| D > 0 | Two distinct real roots |

Read the sign straight off the graph instead of calculating: a parabola opening upward with its vertex below the axis crosses twice; with the vertex on the axis it touches once; with the vertex above it, never crosses.

#### Number series: find the rule of the differences first

Write the first differences under the sequence. If they are constant, the sequence is quadratic in n and the second differences will be constant. If the ratios are constant, it is geometric.

| Sequence | First differences | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 2, 5, 10, 17, 26 | 3, 5, 7, 9 | n² + 1 |
| 3, 6, 12, 24, 48 | 3, 6, 12, 24 | doubling |
| 1, 4, 9, 16, 25 | 3, 5, 7, 9 | perfect squares |

With mixed or alternating series, split by position. Separate the odd-positioned terms and the even-positioned terms and look for a rule in each; 2, 3, 5, 4, 7, 9 splits into 2, 5, 7 and 3, 4, 9, each independently sensible.

#### Syllogisms: the honest answer is usually "no definite conclusion"

A conclusion follows only when it is true in every arrangement consistent with the statements. If the premises leave the terms in more than one possible arrangement, the conclusion is not established.

Given "All A are B" and "All B are C": a conclusion that all A are C follows, because the chains compose. "Some C are A" follows too. But "All C are A" reverses the chain and does not follow — C may contain members that are not A. Whenever reversing a chain appears in an option, mark it as the intended wrong answer.

The containment test to apply: the middle term of a valid syllogism must link the other two in a way the premises support, and the conclusion must never be more specific than the premises allow.

#### Coding–decoding: track positions, not meanings

In letter-shift questions write the alphabet twice and count. If A → K, every letter moves ten places forward, so S maps to C (S is 19, +10 = 29, wrap to 3 = C). In reverse-shifts subtract and wrap the same way.

For opposite-letter pairs, the complement of each letter against a 26-letter alphabet is a fixed rule: A↔Z, B↔Y, C↔X, and so on. These pairings are arbitrary and must be memorised, not derived — they are the one part of the topic with no shortcut.

For number coding, substitute letters for digits in the given relationship and check which assignment satisfies it for every element. A single element that contradicts the rule eliminates the whole option.

#### Blood relations: fix a reference point first

Direction problems fail when positions are not fixed. Anchor the person named in the query, draw that person in the centre, and place everyone else relative to them using only the stated relations.

"A is the father of B, B is the sister of C, C is the son of D" — placing C first, B sits beside C as a sister, A is above that pair as B's father, and D sits below as C's father. Note that D is then also B's father by implication, which makes C the son of both A and D, a contradiction the question intends you to catch.

The recurring traps: gender-specific words like "wife", "son" and "daughter" must be honoured, "brother of my father's wife" is my uncle rather than my father, and a relation stated for two people does not transfer to a third unless the chain explicitly connects them.

#### Data interpretation: solve for the missing value

In a table where one entry is unknown and every row and column total is given, cross-multiply the row and column the missing cell sits in and subtract from the grand total. This resolves the missing value without computing any other cell.

If the totals do not reconcile, you have misread a row — check the units before reworking the arithmetic. Mixed units (thousands against lakhs, or rupees against percentages of something else) account for most data-interpolation errors.

> **Examiner trap:** the fastest wrong answers in this topic come from skipping the units and the base. Before dividing, name out loud what the percentage is of and what the question's unit is. Three of the six "common exam traps" listed in the standard tier above are this single mistake wearing different clothes.
