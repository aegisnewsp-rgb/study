# Pakistan Movement & Independence (1857–1947): Ideological Evolution, Political Chronology & Constitutional Milestones

The Pakistan Movement (*Tehreek-e-Pakistan*) was a transformative constitutional and political struggle spanning nine decades from the War of Independence in 1857 to the emergence of the sovereign state of Pakistan on 14 August 1947 (27 Ramadan 1366 AH). For candidates preparing for the National Law Test (NLT), Law Admission Test (LAT), and higher civil service examinations in Pakistan, Pakistan Studies and General Knowledge sections test the political philosophy of the Two-Nation Theory, the educational reforms of Sir Syed Ahmad Khan, the constitutional diplomacy of Quaid-e-Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah, the ideological framework articulated by Allama Muhammad Iqbal, and the sequence of British colonial constitutional missions.

## Complete 3-Tier Study Guide

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Revision (1h–1d sprint)

For high-yield recall before examination morning, memorize these essential constitutional dates, landmark treaties, and founding personalities:

- **Chronological Anchors of the Pakistan Movement**:
  - **1857**: War of Independence; culmination of British East India Company rule; Queen Victoria's Proclamation of 1858 transferring direct sovereignty to the British Crown.
  - **1875**: Sir Syed Ahmad Khan founds Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental (MAO) High School at Aligarh (upgraded to College in 1877; chartered as Aligarh Muslim University in 1920).
  - **1 October 1906**: **Simla Deputation** led by Sir Aga Khan III meets Viceroy Lord Minto, successfully demanding separate electorates for Muslims.
  - **30 December 1906**: Foundation of the **All-India Muslim League (AIML)** at Dhaka during the 20th session of the Muhammadan Educational Conference, chaired by Nawab Viqar-ul-Mulk. Nawab Salimullah Khan of Dhaka proposed the foundation resolution.
  - **1909**: **Morley-Minto Reforms** (Indian Councils Act 1909) officially grant **Separate Electorates** to Muslims.
  - **December 1916**: **Lucknow Pact**; joint session of Congress and Muslim League. Congress accepted separate electorates; Quaid-e-Azam hailed as "Ambassador of Hindu-Muslim Unity".
  - **1928**: **Nehru Report** rejected separate electorates and federal residue powers to provinces. Quaid-e-Azam famously declared: *"This is the parting of ways."*
  - **March 1929**: Quaid-e-Azam articulates his **Fourteen Points** at the Delhi session of AIML, establishing the non-negotiable charter of Muslim constitutional demands.
  - **29 December 1930**: **Allahabad Address**; Allama Muhammad Iqbal presents his presidential vision for a consolidated North-Western Muslim state.
  - **28 January 1933**: Choudhry Rahmat Ali coins the name **"PAKISTAN"** in his historic pamphlet *Now or Never; Are We to Live or Perish Forever?*
  - **23 March 1940**: **Lahore Resolution** (Pakistan Resolution) passed at Minto Park (now Iqbal Park), presented by A.K. Fazlul Huq (Sher-e-Bangla) and presided over by Quaid-e-Azam.
  - **1945–1946**: General Elections; AIML wins **100% of Muslim seats in Central Legislative Assembly** (30/30) and 87% of Muslim seats in Provincial Assemblies, establishing its sole representative mandate.
  - **3 June 1947**: Mountbatten Plan partitions British India into two independent dominions.
  - **14 August 1947**: Pakistan attains sovereign statehood; Quaid-e-Azam sworn in as First Governor-General.

### 🟡 Standard — Core Exam Concepts (2d–2mo preparation)

In this standard preparation tier, we examine the three intellectual pillars of the Pakistan Movement: Sir Syed's Modernism, Iqbal's Philosophical Blueprint, and Jinnah's Constitutional Leadership.

#### 1. Sir Syed Ahmad Khan & The Aligarh Renaissance

Following the catastrophe of 1857, British colonial administrators held Muslims primarily responsible for the revolt, resulting in widespread disenfranchisement, confiscation of jagirs, and exclusion from the administrative services. Sir Syed Ahmad Khan (1817–1898) initiated the **Aligarh Movement** based on a pragmatic multi-pronged strategy:
1. **Reconciliation with the British**:
   - Authored *Asbab-e-Baghawat-e-Hind* (The Causes of the Indian Revolt, 1859), demonstrating that British administrative failures, cultural insensitivity, and lack of Indian representation in legislative councils caused the upheaval.
   - Published *Loyal Mohammedans of India* to dismantle colonial prejudices against Muslim loyalty.
2. **Modern Scientific & Western Education**:
   - Founded the **Scientific Society** at Ghazipur in 1864 (later shifted to Aligarh), translating advanced English works on mathematics, history, and natural sciences into Urdu.
   - Visited England in 1869, studying the residential collegiate systems of Oxford and Cambridge, which directly inspired the establishment of MAO College in 1877.
3. **Intellectual & Social Awakening**:
   - Issued the renowned journal *Tahzib-ul-Akhlaq* (Mohammedan Social Reformer) in 1870 to eradicate obscurantism and promote rationalism.
   - Established the **All-India Muhammadan Educational Conference** in 1886, providing a pan-Indian deliberative forum that eventually served as the launching platform for the All-India Muslim League in 1906.
4. **Seed of the Two-Nation Theory**:
   - Triggered by the **Hindi-Urdu Controversy in Banaras (1867)**, where Hindu leaders demanded replacing Urdu written in Persian script with Hindi written in Devanagari as the official court language.
   - Sir Syed prophetically told Mr. Shakespeare (Divisional Commissioner of Banaras): *"Now I am convinced that these two nations will not join whole-heartedly in anything. At present there is no open hostility between them, but on account of the so-called educated people, it will increase immensely in future."*

#### 2. Allama Muhammad Iqbal & The Allahabad Vision (1930)

Philosopher-poet Allama Muhammad Iqbal provided the philosophical and spiritual foundation of Pakistani nationhood:
- **Critique of Western Territorial Nationalism**:
  - Iqbal argued that European territorial nationalism subordinated spiritual and ethical values to geographical chauvinism. For Muslims, Islam is not merely a private theological belief but an organic social, legal, and cultural commonwealth.
- **The Historic Presidential Address at Allahabad (29 December 1930)**:
  - Presiding over the 21st annual session of the All-India Muslim League, Iqbal delivered his epoch-making address:
    > *"I would like to see the Punjab, North-West Frontier Province, Sindh and Baluchistan amalgamated into a single State. Self-government within the British Empire, or without the British Empire, the formation of a consolidated North-West Indian Muslim State appears to me to be the final destiny of the Muslims, at least of North-West India."*
  - Iqbal was the first Muslim leader to articulate a clear territorial formula for Muslim self-determination from a major political platform.
- **Iqbal-Jinnah Letters (1936–1937)**:
  - In a series of confidential letters, Iqbal persuaded Quaid-e-Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah to return from self-imposed exile in London and assume active leadership of Indian Muslims, convincing him that the Muslim League must become a mass-based political movement.

#### 3. Quaid-e-Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah: The Constitutional Transformation

Jinnah's political career evolved through three distinct, principled phases:
1. **The Nationalist Ambassador of Unity (1906–1920)**:
   - Joined Congress in 1906 and Muslim League in 1913 on the express condition that his loyalty to the League would not compromise his dedication to national self-rule.
   - Architect of the **Lucknow Pact (1916)**, demonstrating that constitutional progress required inter-communal power sharing.
   - Resigned from Congress in 1920 during the Nagpur session, objecting to Gandhi's unconstitutional methods of civil disobedience and religious mobilization during the Non-Cooperation Movement.
2. **The Disillusioned Constitutionalist (1920–1934)**:
   - Formulated the **Delhi Muslim Proposals (1927)** and proposed amendments to the Nehru Report (1928), offering to forfeit separate electorates if joint electorates with statutory reservation of seats and provincial autonomy were guaranteed.
   - The categorical rejection of these safeguards by Congress prompted his famous **Fourteen Points (1929)**.
   - Attended the First and Second Round Table Conferences in London (1930–1931), but finding both Congress intransigent and Muslim factions disunited, temporarily settled in London to practice law at the Privy Council.
3. **The Sole Representative Leader of the Muslim Nation (1934–1947)**:
   - Returned to India in 1934 to reorganize the All-India Muslim League into a disciplined mass political organization.
   - Championed the **Two-Nation Theory** in his Presidential Address at Lahore on 22 March 1940:
     > *"The Hindus and Muslims belong to two different religious philosophies, social customs, literatures. They neither intermarry nor interdine together and, indeed, they belong to two different civilizations which are based mainly on conflicting ideas and conceptions... To yoke together two such nations under a single state, one as a numerical minority and the other as a majority, must lead to growing discontent and final destruction of any fabric that may be so built up for the government of such a state."*

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Analytical Study (3mo+ mastery)

In this advanced tier, we analyze British constitutional missions, the 1937–1939 Congress Rule crisis, the 1946 Cabinet Mission Plan, and the boundary demarcation dilemmas.

#### 1. The Congress Ministries (1937–1939) & The Day of Deliverance

Under the Government of India Act 1935, provincial elections were held in early 1937. Congress formed ministries in 7 out of 11 provinces, leading to deep alienation among Muslim communities:
- **Grievances Documented by Fact-Finding Committees**:
  - *Pirpur Report (1938)*: Commissioned by AIML under Raja Syed Muhammad Mehdi of Pirpur; documented systemic marginalization of Muslims in Bihar, UP, and CP.
  - *Shareef Report (1939)*: Documented atrocities in Bihar.
  - *Fazlul Huq's "Muslim Sufferings Under Congress Rule" (1939)*.
- **Specific Catalysts of Estrangement**:
  - Enforcement of *Bande Mataram* as the official national anthem in schools and legislative assemblies.
  - Implementation of the *Wardha Educational Scheme* and *Vidya Mandir Scheme*, emphasizing Hindu mythological imagery and spinning wheel ritualism.
  - Compulsory salutation of Congress tricolor flags on public buildings.
  - Restrictions on cow slaughter and interruptions to Azan.
- **Day of Deliverance (*Youm-e-Nijat*)**:
  - When Britain declared war on Germany in September 1939, Viceroy Lord Linlithgow declared India a belligerent without consulting provincial ministries.
  - In protest, Congress provincial ministries resigned *en masse* in October–November 1939.
  - Quaid-e-Azam appealed to Muslims across India to observe Friday, **22 December 1939**, as a **"Day of Deliverance and Thanksgiving"** from the tyranny, oppression, and injustice of Congress rule.

#### 2. The Cabinet Mission Plan (1946) & The Grouping Clause

In March 1946, Prime Minister Clement Attlee dispatched a three-member Cabinet Mission (Lord Pethick-Lawrence, Sir Stafford Cripps, and A.V. Alexander) to negotiate transfer of power:

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|               CABINET MISSION THREE-TIER CONSTITUTIONAL PLAN (1946)               |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| CENTRAL TIER:   • Minimal Union Government controlling Foreign Affairs, Defence, |
|                 and Communications with powers to raise necessary finances.       |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| PROVINCIAL TIER:• All residual powers vested in autonomous Provinces.             |
|                 • Mandatory grouping into three sections:                         |
|                   - Section A (Hindu-Majority): Madras, Bombay, UP, CP, Bihar,    |
|                     Orissa.                                                       |
|                   - Section B (North-West Muslim-Majority): Punjab, NWFP, Sindh.  |
|                   - Section C (North-East Muslim-Majority): Bengal, Assam.        |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| SECTION LEVEL:  • Each section drafts provincial constitutions; option to opt out |
|                 of a group after first general elections under new constitution.   |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

- **Jinnah's Statesmanship**: To avoid civil strife and preserve economic cohesion, Quaid-e-Azam accepted the Cabinet Mission Plan on 6 June 1946, as the compulsory grouping clause conceded the substance of Pakistan.
- **Congress Sabotage & Direct Action Day**:
  - Jawaharlal Nehru, on assuming Congress presidency in July 1946, declared at a press conference in Bombay that Congress would enter the Constituent Assembly "completely unfettered by agreements" and would modify the grouping clause at will.
  - Realizing that Congress would use its raw numerical majority to crush Muslim provincial autonomy once the British departed, the Muslim League withdrew its acceptance on 29 July 1946 and declared **16 August 1946** as **Direct Action Day** to demonstrate Muslim resolve.

## Worked NLT Practice Questions

### Question 1 (NLT General Knowledge Section)

**Question:** Which of the following leaders presided over the historic Dhaka session on 30 December 1906 where the All-India Muslim League was officially founded?
- (A) Sir Aga Khan III
- (B) Nawab Salimullah Khan of Dhaka
- (C) Nawab Viqar-ul-Mulk
- (D) Quaid-e-Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah

**Detailed Solution:**
- The historic meeting was convened at Dhaka immediately following the 20th session of the Muhammadan Educational Conference.
- The session was formally presided over by **Nawab Viqar-ul-Mulk** (Mushtaq Hussain).
- **Nawab Salimullah Khan of Dhaka** proposed the resolution establishing the All-India Muslim League, which was seconded by Hakim Ajmal Khan, Maulana Zafar Ali Khan, and others.
- **Sir Aga Khan III** was subsequently elected as the first permanent honorary president of the League, while Quaid-e-Azam joined the Muslim League several years later in 1913.
- *Correct Answer:* **(C) Nawab Viqar-ul-Mulk**.

### Question 2 (Analytical Constitutional History)

**Question:** Consider the following historical statements regarding the Cabinet Mission Plan of 1946:
1. It recommended an immediate partition of British India into two fully sovereign, independent states.
2. It proposed a three-tier constitutional structure with a weak federal centre and autonomous provinces.
3. The provinces were categorized into three sections (Groups A, B, and C) with compulsory grouping.
4. The All-India Muslim League initially accepted the Cabinet Mission Plan.

Which of the statements given above are correct?
- (A) 1 and 2 only
- (B) 2, 3 and 4 only
- (C) 3 and 4 only
- (D) 1, 3 and 4 only

**Detailed Solution:**
- **Statement 1 is incorrect**: The Cabinet Mission Plan explicitly rejected the immediate partition and creation of an independent Pakistan. Instead, it proposed an undivided Indian Union with a weak centre.
- **Statement 2 is correct**: The plan created a three-tier system: the Central Union (handling only Defence, Foreign Affairs, and Communications), Sections/Groups of provinces, and individual autonomous Provinces holding all residual powers.
- **Statement 3 is correct**: The provinces were divided into Section A (Hindu-majority), Section B (North-Western Muslim-majority: Punjab, NWFP, Sindh), and Section C (North-Eastern Muslim-majority: Bengal, Assam). Grouping at the section level was mandatory for drafting provincial constitutions.
- **Statement 4 is correct**: Quaid-e-Azam and the Muslim League Council accepted the Cabinet Mission Plan on 6 June 1946 because the compulsory grouping clause conceded the practical territorial reality of Pakistan without armed conflict.
- *Correct Answer:* **(B) 2, 3 and 4 only**.

## Common Preparation Traps for NLT Candidates

- **Trap 1: Founding President vs. Session President**: Do not confuse the person who **presided over the inaugural Dhaka session** (Nawab Viqar-ul-Mulk) with the person elected as the **first permanent president** of the Muslim League (Sir Aga Khan III), or the host who **moved the resolution** (Nawab Salimullah Khan).
- **Trap 2: Lahore Resolution Eponyms**: The Lahore Resolution of 23 March 1940 did **not** contain the word "Pakistan" anywhere in its official text! The Hindu press and newspapers mocked it as the "Pakistan Resolution", a name which Quaid-e-Azam and the Muslim League proudly adopted.
- **Trap 3: Choudhry Rahmat Ali vs. Allama Iqbal**: Allama Iqbal proposed the North-West consolidated state in his 1930 Allahabad Address, but he did **not** coin the word "Pakistan". Choudhry Rahmat Ali coined the acronym in January 1933 at Cambridge in his pamphlet *Now or Never*.
- **Trap 4: Separate Electorates Timeline**: Separate electorates were demanded by the Simla Deputation in **1906**, granted by the British in **1909** (Morley-Minto), and conceded by the Indian National Congress in **1916** (Lucknow Pact).
