# Critical Study of Literature (Year 12) — HSC English Advanced (NESA 2024) Notes

Critical Study of Literature is the Year 12 Module B of the HSC English Advanced syllabus. It is examined in HSC Paper 2 Section II. Students undertake an in-depth critical study of a substantial literary text, examining how meaning is shaped through textual features, form and context.

> Re-check the live specification details, prescribed text list and any in-year changes on https://curriculum.nsw.edu.au/learning-areas/english/english-advanced-11-12-2024/overview/course before planning revision around these figures.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Module focus.** In-depth critical study of ONE substantial literary text. Form, language, context and meaning are studied in detail.
- **Text.** One prescribed text from the NESA list. The text rotates on a five-year cycle.
- **Outcomes.** Students demonstrate deep understanding of how the composer constructs meaning; analyse textual features; compose critical responses.
- **Examination.** HSC Paper 2, Section II. Extended response on the prescribed text.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing this module with the Year 11 Critical Study of Literature — Year 12 expects deeper engagement with a single text.
- Treating the prescribed text as a story to retell — the marking scheme rewards analysis of HOW the composer constructs meaning.

---

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### The single-text focus

Year 12 Module B is the only module where students study one text in depth. The focus is on the composer's craft — how the choice of form, structure and language creates meaning. Markers reward depth over breadth; a response that quotes a single passage closely and analyses it thoroughly beats a response that quotes ten passages superficially.

#### Analytical depth

A strong analytical response:
1. Quotes a specific passage (3-8 lines of prose, 1-3 stanzas of poetry).
2. Identifies specific language features (diction, imagery, syntax, sound, structure).
3. Explains the effect of each feature on the responder.
4. Connects the passage to the broader themes and context of the text.
5. Justifies a thesis about the composer's purpose.

#### Context

Context (time, place, culture, history) shapes the text and shapes how responders read it. A response should engage with context where relevant, but context is not the main focus — textual analysis is.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Why Year 12 Module B rewards the deepest reading

- **The text is the centre of attention.** There is no comparative stimulus and no unseen. The student's job is to know the prescribed text deeply and respond in detail.
- **The marking scheme scores depth of analysis.** A 5-mark analytical question on a passage rewards close engagement; a 15-mark extended response rewards sustained thesis-driven argument.
- **The text rotates.** NESA publishes a five-year prescribed text list. The current cycle's text is the only one examined; teachers and students should focus on it.

#### Year 12 planning pattern

- **Term 1.** Read the prescribed text in full, twice. Build a chapter-by-chapter or section-by-section summary.
- **Term 2.** Identify 3-5 key passages per section for the quote bank. Practice timed analytical responses on short passages.
- **Term 3.** Full Paper 2 practice with both Section II (this module) and Section I (Textual Conversations). Mark against the published HSC marking guidelines.
- **Term 4 (HSC).** Final revision of the quote bank and the analytical framework.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "The whole text must be analysed." No — pick 3-5 passages in the response and analyse them deeply. Coverage is not rewarded; depth is.
- "Context is the main thing." Context is supporting evidence; textual analysis is the centre.
- "Longer is better." A focused 4-paragraph response beats a sprawling 8-paragraph response that repeats the same point.

#### Specification reference

This module is examined in HSC Paper 2 Section II. The prescribed text list is published annually by NESA. Confirm the live prescribed text and any NESA updates on curriculum.nsw.edu.au for the current specification.
