# Four-Part Word Structure

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Medical terminology is the standardized Greek- and Latin-based vocabulary clinicians use to describe anatomy, disease, and procedures with one precise meaning per term.

Every term follows a four-part pattern: **prefix** (location/quality) + **word root** (core body part) + **combining vowel** (usually **o**) + **suffix** (condition or procedure).

| Word Part | Role | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Prefix | Modifies meaning | hyper-, hypo-, tachy-, brady- |
| Root | Body structure | cardi/o, gastr/o, hepat/o |
| Combining vowel | Joins parts | -o- (drop before vowel-starting suffix) |
| Suffix | Condition or procedure | -itis, -algia, -ectomy, -ology |

For the Saudi GP Board, expect short matching items that ask you to decode a term like *tachycardia* (fast heart) or pick the suffix that means *inflammation*.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Four-Part Word Structure

Medical terms are decoded from right to left. The **suffix** carries the core clinical meaning (a disease, procedure, or condition), the **root** names the anatomical structure, the **prefix** alters location, time, or quantity, and the **combining vowel** (almost always *o*) only appears when joining two roots or a root to a consonant-starting suffix. Drop the *o* when the next suffix starts with a vowel — *gastritis*, not *gastroitis*.

#### Prefixes and Suffixes You Must Recognise

| Category | Items | Decoded Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Quantity/quality prefixes | a-, an-, hyper-, hypo- | without, without, excessive, deficient |
| Speed prefixes | tachy-, brady- | fast, slow |
| Directional prefixes | endo-, peri-, sub-, supra- | within, around, below, above |
| Condition suffixes | -itis, -algia, -pathy, -emia | inflammation, pain, disease, blood condition |
| Procedure suffixes | -ectomy, -ostomy, -scopy, -plasty | removal, opening, viewing, repair |
| Discipline suffixes | -ology, -ologist | study of, one who studies |

#### Directional Terms

Pairs you will chart daily: **superior/inferior** (above/below), **anterior/posterior** (front/back), **medial/lateral** (toward midline/away), **proximal/distal** (near/far from trunk).

#### Standard Exam Question Patterns

Saudi GP Board items typically present a clinical phrase ("inflammation of the stomach lining") and ask which term fits, or show a term such as *subcutaneous* and ask for its meaning. Saudi Health Data Dictionary documentation reinforces identical spelling, so a single misread vowel can change the charted diagnosis.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Eponyms, Acronyms, and Plurals

Beyond the four-part structure, charting relies on **eponyms** (named discoveries such as *Parkinson disease*) and **acronyms** (MI, COPD, CVA). Saudi facilities follow the Saudi Health Data Dictionary v2, so acronyms used in discharge summaries must match the registered dictionary form.

Plurals are not English-style. Common Greek/Latin forms:

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| nucleus | nuclei |
| diagnosis | diagnoses |
| vertebra | vertebrae |
| appendix | appendices |
| bacterium | bacteria |

Writing *appendixes* or *diagnosis* in a referral letter is flagged as a documentation error in Saudi primary-care audits.

#### Common Mistakes Trapped on the Exam

- **Opposite prefixes:** *hyperthyroidism* (overactive) vs *hypothyroidism* (underactive) — one letter shifts the entire management.
- **-ology vs -pathy:** *cardiology* is the specialty, *cardiopathy* is the disease.
- **-emia decoding:** the prefix specifies the substance — *glycemia* is sugar in blood, not generic blood condition.
- **Combining vowel misuse:** *gastralgia* (stomach pain), not *gastroalgia*; *gastritis*, not *gastroitis*.

#### Adjacent Topics Worth Linking

This terminology foundation feeds directly into **anatomy**, **pharmacology nomenclature** (suffixes -pril, -sartan, -statin), **diagnostic coding** (ICD-10 entries used in Saudi MoH reporting), and **clinical documentation integrity** standards audited by the Saudi Health Council.

#### Practice Prompts

1. Decode *endocarditis*: identify the prefix, root, combining vowel, and suffix, then state the condition in plain English.
2. A referral letter reads *"patient has multiple appendices seen on imaging."* Is the plural correct? Rewrite using the appropriate medical term for the inflammation of that structure.
