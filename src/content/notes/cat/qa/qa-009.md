# Triangles

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

A **triangle** is a three-sided polygon whose interior angles sum to **180°**; CAT QA tests both direct triangle properties and their use inside mensuration, coordinate, and trigonometry questions.

- **Area** = ½ × base × height, or **Heron's formula** Area = √[s(s−a)(s−b)(s−c)], where s = (a+b+c)/2.
- **Sine rule**: a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C = 2R (R = circumradius). **Cosine rule**: a² = b² + c² − 2bc·cos A.
- For a **right triangle**: r = (a+b−c)/2, R = c/2, where c is the hypotenuse.

> **Tip:** Memorise the equilateral shortcuts — Area = (√3/4)a², R = a/√3, r = a/(2√3), altitude = (√3/2)a.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Properties and Similarity

Two triangles are **similar** when AA, SSS, or SAS criteria hold; the **ratio of areas equals the square** of the corresponding-side ratio. **Congruence** requires SSS, SAS, ASA, or RHS — note that SSA is the ambiguous case and does **not** guarantee a unique triangle. In CAT word problems, a typical trap is forcing the SSA pair into a single configuration.

#### Centres of a Triangle

| Centre | Construction | Key ratio/formula |
|---|---|---|
| **Centroid** | Intersection of medians | Divides each median 2:1 from vertex |
| **Incentre** | Intersection of angle bisectors | r = Area / s |
| **Circumcentre** | Intersection of perpendicular bisectors | R = abc / (4·Area) |
| **Orthocentre** | Intersection of altitudes | Distance from vertex A = 2R·cos A |

> All four centres **coincide** only in an **equilateral triangle** — a frequent assertion-reason trap.

#### The Three Trigonometric Rules

Use the **sine rule** to flip between side and angle, the **cosine rule** to find an unknown side given two sides and the included angle (or to check obtuseness via the sign of cos A), and the **tangent rule** (a−b)/(a+b) = tan((A−B)/2) / tan((A+B)/2) for difference-of-angles shortcuts.

#### CAT Question Patterns

Expect 1–2 triangle-based questions (5% weight) of three flavours: direct area/perimeter calculation using Heron's formula; similar-triangle **ratio scaling** embedded in a diagram; or a sine/cosine rule application on a word problem involving heights, distances, or shadows. CAT stays in **degree mode** — keep angles in degrees and prefer factorable integer values.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and the Equilateral Identity

In an **equilateral triangle** of side a: Area = (√3/4)a², R = a/√3, r = a/(2√3), altitude h = (√3/2)a, and R = 2r. The identity **R ≥ 2r** (Euler's inequality) holds for every triangle, with equality only at the equilateral case — a quick sanity check on answer choices.

#### Worked Example

A triangle has sides 13, 14, 15. Find its area, inradius, circumradius.

- s = (13+14+15)/2 = 21.
- Area = √[21·8·7·6] = √7056 = **84 m²**.
- r = Area/s = 84/21 = **4 m**.
- R = abc/(4·Area) = (13·14·15)/(4·84) = 2730/336 = **8.125 m**.

#### Common Mistakes

- Plugging the **perimeter** instead of the **semi-perimeter** into Heron's formula — always divide by 2 first.
- Mixing **median** (vertex→midpoint), **altitude** (vertex⊥opposite side), and **angle bisector**; they coincide **only** in equilateral triangles.
- Using **SSA** to claim congruence and producing a second valid configuration — check the ambiguous case.
- Computing area as base·height without confirming the height is **perpendicular** to that base (slanted-height trap).

#### Connections to Adjacent Topics

Triangle geometry feeds **coordinate geometry** (distance formula ↔ cosine rule), **mensuration** (3-D solids decompose into triangular faces), and **trigonometry** (sine/cosine identities). The relation **1/r = 1/r₁ + 1/r₂ + 1/r₃** linking inradius and exradii appears occasionally in tougher sets.

#### Practice Prompts

1. In a triangle with sides 7, 9, 12, find the area using Heron's formula and then compute R.
2. A 10 m ladder leans against a wall; the foot is 6 m from the wall. Using the cosine rule, find the angle the ladder makes with the ground.
