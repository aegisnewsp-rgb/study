# Mixed Graphs & Data Fusion

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

A **mixed graph** in MAT Data-Analysis presents two linked visual representations in one question — for example, a bar chart for absolute sales paired with a line plot for percentage growth, or a pie for category share alongside a table for region-wise totals. **Data fusion** is the step where you merge values from these heterogeneous sources into a single computed answer (ratio, share, average, or growth rate). The key skill is identifying the **common axis** (year, category, region) that links the two charts so values can be paired correctly.

- Locate the **shared label** (year/region/category) before reading any number.
- Check **units and scale** on each chart — %, lakhs, crores, or index.
- Extract raw values, align by category, then apply the requested operation.
- Expect **1–2 questions** in the DI–DS section, mid-to-high difficulty.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### What is data fusion in DI?

Data fusion means combining two or more data representations — chart-plus-table, bar-plus-line, pie-plus-caselet — into one inference. The test-taker must read each source, identify the linking variable, and cross-compute. A typical MAT stem reads: *"From the bar chart showing Company revenue and the line graph showing profit margin, find Company-A's profit in 2022."*

#### The five-step fusion procedure

1. Identify the **linking variable** (year, region, product) common to both visuals.
2. Read **scale and units** on each axis; note whether values are absolute or percentage.
3. Extract the **required raw values** from each chart at the aligned category.
4. Decide the **operation**: share = part ÷ total × 100, or profit = revenue × margin.
5. Sanity-check the **magnitude** against the chart to catch decimal-place errors.

#### Common MAT traps

| Trap type | What examiners do | Quick fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scale mix-up | Line shows %, bar shows counts | Read axis label first, not the legend |
| Mismatched sort | Bars sorted by value, table alphabetical | Use the label, not position |
| Unit drift | One chart in lakhs, another in crores | Convert to a common unit before compute |
| Time anchor | Bar at year-end, line at mid-year | Match the time-point, not the period |

#### Typical question pattern

A MAT item usually asks for a *fused* insight: *"What % of total Category-A revenue came from Region-X, given Category-A's share in the pie and Region-X's count in the table?"* The solve is `(Region-X count ÷ Category-A total) × 100`, where Category-A total itself comes from fusing the pie's % with the overall sales figure.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge cases examiners exploit

**Stacked bars fused with a separate total.** When a stacked column shows sub-category contributions and a line or table gives the grand total, students often double-count. Rule: take the *segment height* directly; never re-sum and then compare to the printed total unless the question asks for verification.

**Dual-axis charts.** MAT occasionally uses a single canvas with a left axis (rupees in crores) and a right axis (units in thousands). The trap: pairing revenue and volume at the same x-position yields a misleading ratio. Always confirm both axes share the same category at that x-tick.

**Caselet-plus-chart fusion.** A short paragraph introduces a condition (e.g., "only 60% of dealers reported") that must be applied to a chart value. Skip the multiplier and the answer is off by 40%.

#### Worked micro-example

A bar chart shows Company revenue (₹ crore): P = 200, Q = 150, R = 250. A separate pie shows profit share: P = 30%, Q = 45%, R = 25%. Question: *Company Q's profit, given total profit ₹400 crore.*
- Link: Company Q is the common label.
- Pie share × total profit: 0.45 × 400 = **₹180 crore**.

#### Exam-specific strategy

- MAT Data-Analysis carries **~3% weight**, but mixed-graph items are **scoring-deciders** in DI–DS.
- Budget **2–3 minutes per item**; if alignment is unclear, mark and move — these questions punish rushed reading.
- Practice **5–7 fusion sets** before the exam; speed comes from pattern recognition of the linking variable.

#### Practice prompts

1. A line graph gives 2019–2023 growth rates and a table gives base-year revenue; compute 2023 revenue for one firm.
2. A pie shows segment % and a bar shows region totals; find the region contributing the highest absolute segment value.
