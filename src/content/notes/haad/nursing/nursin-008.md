# Nursing Ethics and Professional Practice

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

> Scan this block the morning of the exam — it carries the four principles and the licensure anchor.

Ethical conduct in UAE nursing rests on four principles that almost every HAAD scenario stem tests: **autonomy** (patient's right to decide), **beneficence** (actively doing good), **non-maleficence** (above all, do no harm), and **justice** (fair, non-discriminatory care). These map onto the ICN Code's three domains — *Nurse and People*, *Nurse and Practice*, *Nurse and the Profession* — and onto the ANA Code's nine provisions. Operationally, a nurse in Abu Dhabi is licensed under the DoH/HAAD Professional Qualification Requirements (PQR), legally bound by UAE Federal Law No. 4 of 1983 on Medical Liability, and answerable for every documented action or omission.

- **Memory Hook — "A-B-N-J-V-F-A"** for the principles: **A**utonomy, **B**eneficence, **N**on-maleficence, **J**ustice, then the duties **V**eracity, **F**idelity, **A**ccountability.
- **Quick rule for DNR stems:** comfort care and analgesia continue; DNR means no *resuscitation*, not no *treatment*.
- **Consent rule:** consent is a process, must be in their language, and must be documented — never a one-off signature.
- **Whistleblowing:** mandatory, not optional, when patient safety is at risk (ANA Provision 6; HAAD reporting duty).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

> Treat the principles as a hierarchy — autonomy sets the direction, non-maleficence draws the floor.

#### The four-principle framework and what each one actually requires

| Principle | What it obliges the nurse to do | Typical exam trigger |
|---|---|---|
| **Autonomy** | Disclose information, obtain informed consent, respect refusal | Family asks to withhold diagnosis from a competent adult |
| **Beneficence** | Actively promote the patient's wellbeing | Choice of intervention that benefits vs merely relieves |
| **Non-maleficence** | Avoid harm; weigh risk against benefit | Medication error, falls risk, restraint use |
| **Justice** | Allocate care fairly; non-discrimination | Triage, ICU bed allocation, gender-concordant requests |

#### Codes of ethics in clinical use

The **ICN Code (2012)** organises conduct into three relationships. *Nurse and People* covers the four fundamental responsibilities — promote health, prevent illness, restore health, alleviate suffering — and grounds informed consent and confidentiality. *Nurse and Practice* mandates evidence-based care, delegation, and CPD. *Nurse and the Profession* addresses collaboration, advocacy, and protection of the profession's integrity. The **ANA Code** layers nine provisions on top, and Provisions 1, 3, and 6 (compassion, health promotion, integrity/whistleblowing) appear most often in MCQs.

#### ETHNIC model — a bedside decision-making sequence

Use **ETHNIC** when a stem gives an unresolved dilemma: **E**xplain what is happening medically; lay out the **T**reatment plan and prognosis; **H**ear the patient's (and family's) values; **N**egotiate a workable plan; deliver the **I**ntervention; arrange **C**losure with follow-up and documentation. The model forces the nurse to surface autonomy concerns before defaulting to beneficence.

#### Scenario work-through

A 62-year-old Emirati man with newly diagnosed colon cancer is admitted. His adult son asks the nurse not to tell the father "because it will upset him." The patient is alert, oriented, and speaks Arabic; a professional interpreter is available. The most appropriate nursing action is to **acknowledge the son's concern, then disclose the diagnosis to the patient in Arabic with the interpreter, document the conversation, and offer the son support** — because the patient's right to information overrides family wishes when the patient is a competent adult.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** picking "honour the son's request to protect the family" selects family-centred beneficence over patient autonomy. In UAE adult practice, autonomy wins for competent patients; only when the patient lacks capacity does the family step in as decision-maker.

#### Regulatory pillars for the UAE context

| Pillar | Source | Nurse implication |
|---|---|---|
| Licensure | DoH/HAAD PQR | Title, scope, and CPD hours defined |
| Civil liability | UAE Federal Law No. 4 of 1983 | Omissions = negligence; documentation is legal record |
| Fitness to practise | DoH/HAAD conduct rules | Mandatory reporting of impairment or unsafe practice |
| Patient rights | UAE Patient's Bill of Rights | Information, consent, privacy, second opinion |

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

> Edge cases are where HAAD marks separate a pass from a fail — read each one as a stem in waiting.

#### Cases that look like one principle but reward another

| Stem signal | Correct principle | Why students slip |
|---|---|---|
| Family requests "do not tell mother her cancer" | Autonomy | Choosing beneficence by assuming protection |
| Nurse notices a chemotherapy dose 10× the ordered amount | Non-maleficence | Beneficence ("it'll help the patient") mis-applied |
| Two patients need the last ICU bed; one is VIP | Justice | Loyalty to a familiar patient overrides fairness |
| Colleague arrives at work smelling of alcohol | Non-maleficence + reporting duty | Treated as a confidentiality issue, not a safety one |

#### Cultural and Islamic-ethics considerations specific to the Gulf

Modesty preferences (same-gender providers where feasible), gender-concordant care when requested and clinically equivalent, dietary respect during Ramadan for conscious inpatients, and end-of-life rulings that distinguish withdrawal of futile treatment from active euthanasia are routinely tested. DNR in the UAE aligns with the Patient's Bill of Rights and DoH end-of-life policy — comfort care, analgesia, hygiene, and family presence continue; only resuscitation is withheld. Capacity is presumed; a two-physician assessment is needed before a patient is declared incapable and a family surrogate activated.

#### Scope-of-practice traps that cost marks

- Administering a medication without a valid, legible order or beyond your licensure tier counts as **professional misconduct**, not merely an error.
- Delegating a task to unlicensed staff that requires nursing judgement (e.g., initial assessment) breaches scope and accountability.
- Posting any patient detail on personal social media — even a "vague" description — breaches confidentiality; UAE facilities treat it as reportable misconduct.
- Carrying over an expired DoH licence, or practising without CPD hours documented, blocks renewal and may invalidate indemnity.

#### Accountability vs responsibility — wording that matters

**Accountability** is legal answerability for your actions and omissions; **responsibility** is the role obligation to perform a duty. When a stem asks "who is *legally* responsible for the medication error," the correct answer names the nurse who administered the dose (accountability), not the unit manager (responsibility for supervision).

#### Advanced practice prompts

1. A patient with capacity refuses a life-saving blood transfusion on religious grounds. Walk through ETHNIC, identify which principle dominates, and outline the documentation chain required in a UAE facility.
2. You witness a senior nurse falsifying a vital-signs entry on a deteriorating patient. Apply ANA Provisions 3 and 6, list the internal reporting steps under DoH/HAAD, and describe the documentation that protects you as the reporter.
