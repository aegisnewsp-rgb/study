# Consumer Behaviour

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Consumer Behaviour** studies how individuals select, buy, use, and dispose of goods to satisfy needs and wants, blending psychological, social, cultural, and economic influences. The CMA Foundation Economics paper tests the cardinal–ordinal distinction, equilibrium conditions, and elasticity concepts directly.

#### Must-know formulas

| Concept | Formula | Variables |
|---|---|---|
| Marginal Utility | MU = ΔTU / ΔQ | utils per unit |
| One-good equilibrium | MUx / Px = MUm | utils per rupee |
| Two-good equilibrium | MUx / Px = MUy / Py = MUm | utils per rupee |
| Price Elasticity | Ep = (%ΔQd) / (%ΔP) | dimensionless |
| Budget Line | Px·X + Py·Y = M | slope = –Px/Py |

**High-yield pointers:** state the Law of Equi-Marginal Utility, name the four properties of indifference curves, and remember that consumer surplus equals the area under the demand curve above the market price.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Need, Want and Demand

A **need** is a basic biological requirement (food, shelter); a **want** is the culturally shaped form a need takes (biryani, a flat); **demand** is want backed by purchasing power and willingness to pay. The CMA Foundation paper frequently frames MCQs around this hierarchy to test whether students confuse biological urgency with market behaviour.

#### Cardinal Utility Analysis

Cardinal utility measures satisfaction in **utils**. **Total Utility (TU)** rises with consumption, while **Marginal Utility (MU)** falls after a point — the Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility. A consumer allocating a fixed money income M maximises satisfaction when the last rupee spent on every good yields the same MU, expressed as MUx/Px = MUy/Py = MUm. The classical Diamond–Water Paradox is resolved here: water carries high TU but low MU (and low price), while diamonds carry high MU (and high price).

#### Ordinal Utility and Indifference Curves

Ordinal utility only ranks bundles. An **indifference curve** U = f(X, Y) = constant joins bundles giving equal satisfaction. Four properties matter for exams:

#### Four key properties of indifference curves

- Downward sloping (more of X means less of Y at constant utility)
- Convex to the origin (diminishing MRS)
- Higher curve represents higher utility
- Curves never intersect

The **Marginal Rate of Substitution** MRSxy = MUx/MUy measures the rate at which a consumer willingly trades Y for X.

#### Budget Line and Equilibrium

The budget line Px·X + Py·Y = M has slope –Px/Py. Equilibrium occurs at the tangency where **MRSxy = Px/Py**, and the chosen bundle lies on the highest attainable indifference curve. Always plot the budget line first when solving graphical questions.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Price, Income and Substitution Effects

A fall in Px of good X triggers two effects. The **Substitution Effect** (Hicksian holds utility constant; Slutsky holds the original bundle affordable) shifts demand toward the now-cheaper X. The **Income Effect** raises real income, increasing X if it is normal and decreasing X if it is inferior. A **Giffen good** is an inferior good whose income effect outweighs its substitution effect, producing an upward-sloping demand curve — a classic CMA trap.

#### Elasticity Linkages

Price Elasticity of Demand (Ep) is governed by five determinants: availability of substitutes, income share, necessity-versus-luxury status, time horizon, and addicitivity. **Engel's Law** states that the food budget share falls as income rises, formalised by **Engel Elasticity** Ee = (%ΔQx)/(%ΔM). Cross Elasticity Exy tells whether goods are substitutes (positive) or complements (negative).

#### Revealed Preference and Consumer Surplus

Samuelson's **Revealed Preference Theory** infers preferences from observed choices: if a consumer picks bundle A over affordable bundle B, then A is revealed preferred to B. **Consumer Surplus**, the triangular area under the demand curve above price P, captures the gap between what buyers would pay and what they actually pay, and is the foundation of welfare economics.

#### Common mistakes students make

- Confusing TU (utils) with MU (utils per unit) in numeric questions
- Assuming MU is always positive; it can be zero or negative once TU falls
- Treating MRS and price ratio as the same thing — they coincide only at equilibrium
- Forgetting that Giffen goods are inferior goods, not luxury goods

#### Practice prompts

1. A consumer spends ₹600 on tea (Px = ₹20) and biscuits (Py = ₹30). If MUx = 40 utils and MUy = 50 utils, is the consumer in equilibrium? Show the reallocation.
2. Draw an indifference map and budget line showing the tangency condition, then illustrate the substitution and income effects of a price fall in good X.
