# Te Pāngarau numeracy standard (US32412) — NCEA UE Numeracy Notes

Unit Standard 32412 (US32412) is the Te Pāngarau (Māori-medium) equivalent of US32406. It carries 5 credits at Level 1 or above and contributes to UE numeracy for students on a Māori-medium pathway. The standard tests the application of mathematics and statistics in everyday situations through te reo Māori, with the same three-component structure as US32406.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **5 credits**, internally assessed by the school or approved provider.
- Te Pāngarau (Māori-medium) equivalent of US32406, suitable for students on a Māori-medium pathway.
- Tests the same three components as US32406: formulate, use, explain.
- The standard is part of the dedicated co-requisite framework introduced from 2024 onwards (transition period 2024-2027).
- A student can use US32412 instead of US32406 to satisfy the dedicated co-requisite numeracy half of UE.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing US32412 with Te Reo Māori subject standards — US32412 is a numeracy standard delivered in te reo Māori; it is not a language standard.
- Assuming the standard is only for fluent speakers — it is for students on a Māori-medium pathway, with appropriate support.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### What the standard tests

US32412 tests the same three components as US32406, but delivered in te reo Māori:

1. **Whakatakoto tikanga** (formulate mathematical and statistical approaches).
2. **Whakamahi tikanga** (use mathematics and statistics in everyday situations).
3. **Whakamārama i te tika** (explain the reasonableness of results).

The contexts are everyday situations, but the language of delivery is te reo Māori.

#### How it fits into UE

US32412 contributes 5 credits toward UE numeracy, in the same way as US32406. A student on a Māori-medium pathway can achieve UE numeracy through:

- US32412 (5 credits) + US32406 (5 credits) = 10 credits.
- US32412 (5 credits) + numeracy-rich achievement standards (5 credits) = 10 credits.

The standards can be combined as long as the total meets the 10-credit requirement.

#### Cultural context

The Te Pāngarau framework was developed with input from iwi and Māori-medium education experts. It recognises the importance of culture and language in mathematics education — for example, traditional Māori measurement systems, navigation, and weaving involve mathematical concepts.

For Excellence, students engage with the cultural context of mathematics, including tikanga Māori perspectives on number, measurement, and statistical reasoning.

#### Internal assessment logistics

The standard is internally assessed by the school or approved provider. Materials are in te reo Māori. Marking is moderated by NZQA, with cultural advisors typically involved.

#### Preparation strategy

For students on a Māori-medium pathway:

1. **Practice real-life numeracy in te reo Māori** — budgets, measurements, calculations in everyday contexts.
2. **Practice interpreting results in te reo Māori** — explaining reasonableness.
3. **Engage with cultural context** — traditional Māori mathematics (e.g., raranga patterns, wayfinding).
4. **Use whānau and kaiako support** — the Māori-medium pathway is supported by the school and community.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Why Te Pāngarau matters

Te Pāngarau recognises te reo Māori as a valid medium for mathematical reasoning. For many Māori-medium students, this is essential for fair assessment — being assessed in English would not reflect their actual numeracy skills.

The framework also supports the revitalisation of te reo Māori by providing a pathway for students to achieve NCEA and UE through te reo Māori.

#### Excellence in Te Pāngarau

The Excellence grade requires perceptive engagement with mathematical and statistical reasoning in te reo Māori, including:

- Formulating sophisticated approaches to complex contexts.
- Using mathematical and statistical methods with control.
- Explaining reasonableness with cultural responsiveness.

For Excellence, the student demonstrates not just numerical competence but also the integration of mathematics with te ao Māori.

#### Combining with US32406

A bilingual student might choose to attempt both US32406 and US32412. The combination provides:

- 10 credits toward UE numeracy.
- Evidence of competence in both English-medium and Māori-medium numeracy contexts.
- A richer preparation for university study, which may involve both English-medium and te reo Māori contexts (especially for study at a wānanga).

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Te Pāngarau is only for fluent speakers." No — it is for students on a Māori-medium pathway, with appropriate support.
- "Te Pāngarau is easier than US32406." It is different, not easier or harder.
- "Cultural context doesn't matter." It does — the standards are grounded in te ao Māori.
- "I can use Te Pāngarau in English-medium contexts." No — it is designed for te reo Māori contexts.
