# Legal News

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Legal News in CLAT refers to recent developments in Indian law reported in mainstream dailies and law portals during the 12-month reference window before the exam. The section carries roughly **5% weightage** inside the 50-question Current Affairs & GK component (about **25%** of the CLAT paper). You must recognise **landmark Supreme Court and High Court judgments**, **statutory amendments**, and **presidential ordinances**.

- Track the three new criminal codes — **BNS, BNSS, BSA** — which replaced the IPC, CrPC and Indian Evidence Act from **1 July 2024**.
- Distinguish **ratio decidendi** (binding reasoning) from **obiter dicta** (incidental remarks).
- Note constitutional developments such as **Article 370 abrogation aftermath**, **Article 142** completions, and **PILs** admitted under **Article 32**.

| Code (Old) | Code (New) | Effective |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IPC | Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS) | 1 Jul 2024 |
| CrPC | Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS) | 1 Jul 2024 |
| Indian Evidence Act | Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA) | 1 Jul 2024 |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### What CLAT Tests Under "Legal News"

The Consortium of NLUs frames Legal News as a reading-comprehension and factual-recall sub-skill inside Current Affairs. A typical CLAT item presents a 200–250 word news passage drawn from a recent Supreme Court ruling, an ordinance, or a statute amendment, then asks 3–5 linked questions on bench strength, issue, relief, and provisions interpreted. Roughly **5 of the 50 Current Affairs questions** in a recent paper touched a legal-news passage, matching the 5% indicative weight.

#### Tracking Sources You Must Read

You cannot revise this section from a single textbook. The Consortium expects familiarity with the way law is actually reported.

| Source | What it covers | Frequency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Hindu / Indian Express (national daily) | Supreme Court reportable items, ordinances, bills | Daily |
| LiveLaw, Bar & Bench, Supreme Court Observer | Case summaries, bench notes, oral-order highlights | Daily |
| Press Information Bureau releases | Ministry of Law & Justice notifications, gazette text | As issued |
| NALSA / Election Commission bulletins | ADR, electoral law updates | Monthly |

#### Building a Filing System

Map every item to a subject head so revision stays systematic.

- **Constitutional:** Article 370, basic-structure references, Article 142 completions.
- **Criminal:** BNS, BNSS, BSA sections replacing IPC, CrPC, IEA provisions.
- **Commercial & Tax:** IBC, GST Council, Competition Act amendments.
- **Civil & Family:** live-in relationships, same-sex marriage, matrimonial law.
- **Environment, Labour, Cyber, IPR:** tribunal rulings, MFN clauses, digital laws.

> Tip: keep a one-line note per item — case name, court, date, key section, and relief — instead of full summaries.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases That Change Marks

Legal News items look factual, but examiners exploit precise distinctions. Memorising the political headline ("SC pulls up Centre") without the underlying issue invites a wrong option. **Always record the issue, the provision interpreted, and the relief granted**, not the moral of the story.

| Trap | Why it loses marks | Correct approach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quoting IPC section 302 after 1 Jul 2024 | The provision was renumbered to BNS 302 with changed punishment clauses | Quote BNS section and note commencement date |
| Treating an obiter as the ratio | Only ratio decidendi binds lower courts | Identify which paragraph the bench marked as the "law declared" |
| Citing a 5-judge ruling as a 3-judge ruling | Larger benches carry greater precedential weight | Note exact bench strength and constituent judges |
| Mixing a Law Commission recommendation with an enacted law | Reports are advisory; only gazetted text is law | Verify whether a Bill has received Presidential assent |
| Citing a case older than the reference window | The 12-month window is binding for CLAT | Filter notes strictly by date of judgment |

#### Worked Example: Identifying the Legal Issue

Suppose the passage reads: *"The Supreme Court refused to stay the notification linking Aadhaar with electoral rolls, observing that privacy interests were balanced against preventing impersonation."* A strong answer notes the **PIL under Article 32**, the **bench strength**, the **Aadhaar Act, 2016** provisions discussed, and frames the relief as refusal of stay rather than a final ruling. A weak answer simply calls it "an Aadhaar case".

#### Practice Prompts

1. Take a recent LiveLaw report on a Supreme Court PIL; write the ratio in two sentences and cite the specific Article under which relief was granted.
2. Pick one BNS section replacing an old IPC provision; list the change in punishment clause and explain why Parliament redrafted it.

> Exam strategy: spend **45–60 seconds** per legal-news question, flag passage-based sets early, and return to them only after clearing factual MCQs to preserve time for the linked sub-questions.
