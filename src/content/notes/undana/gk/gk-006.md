### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### What UNDANA means by Topic 6
The PMB General Knowledge section groups civic-literacy items under Topic 6. UNDANA — *Universitas Nusa Cendana* in Kupang — uses a 3% slice of total questions to probe whether incoming freshmen already understand the Indonesian constitutional framework. It is small in weight but predictable: every PMB cycle over the last several years has included at least one Pancasila order, one UUD 1945 article, and one state-institution question.

#### Pancasila — the five principles in exact order
1. **Ketuhanan Yang Maha Esa**
2. **Kemanusiaan yang Adil dan Beradab**
3. **Persatuan Indonesia**
4. **Kerakyatan yang Dipimpin oleh Hikmat Kebijaksanaan dalam Permusyawaratan/Perwakilan**
5. **Keadilan Sosial bagi Seluruh Rakyat Indonesia**

The order is fixed and frequently tested in MCQs.

#### UUD 1945 — what to memorise
The **Pembukaan (Preamble)** contains the four paragraphs that encode the philosophical basis of the state. Chapter I on *Bentuk dan Kedaulatan* establishes that sovereignty resides in the people and is exercised according to the Constitution. Article 4(1) vests executive power in the President.

> Examiner tip: questions phrased "Pasal yang mengatur…" almost always point to Articles 1, 4, or 27 — learn those three cold.

#### State institutions — the clean distinction
- **Lembaga negara (state organs)**: MPR, DPR, DPD, Presiden, MA, MK, BPK, KY.
- **Lembaga pemerintahan (government organs)**: line ministries under the President (kementerian), plus local governments.

UNDANA candidates confuse MA and MK often. **MA** = highest judicial authority in ordinary, military, and religious courts. **MK** = sole interpreter of the Constitution with nine justices appointed by the President and confirmed by DPR.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge cases the PMB loves
The "trick" MCQs usually hinge on two traps: (1) the **opening phrase "Bahwa sesungguhnya kemerdekaan itu ialah hak segala bangsa"** is the Preamble's *first* paragraph — many students assume it is the Pledge or the state principle list. (2) The **DPD** has members elected from each province but **has no power to enact laws** — only DPR does. DPD issues *consideration* on regional issues.

#### Connections to adjacent UNDANA topics
Topic 6 pairs naturally with **Topic 1 (national history)**, particularly the *Soempah Pemoeda* oath and the Proclamation of 17 August 1945, because both anchor UUD 1945's legitimacy. It also overlaps with **Topic 4 (geography of NTT)** where questions ask which province your campus sits in — Nusa Tenggara Timur.

#### Common mistakes
- Swapping Pancasila principles #2 and #3, or misremembering principle #4's full phrasing.
- Calling the **BPK** a "lembaga pemerintahan" — it is a *lembaga negara* per Article 3 of UUD 1945.
- Forgetting that **KY (Komisi Yudisial)** oversees judges but cannot overturn court decisions.

> Scoring tip: secure all 1–2 Topic 6 questions first; the 3% weight means you only need accuracy, not volume. A confident 90-second answer here frees minutes for heavier-weighted sections.

#### Worked prompt
A 2023-style PMB item reads: *"Lembaga yang berwenang mengadili perkara pada tingkat kasasi adalah…"* Correct answer: **Mahkamah Agung (MA)**, *bukan* MK, because cassation belongs to MA's jurisdiction.

#### Practice prompts
1. List the five Pancasila principles in correct order and write one sentence explaining each.
2. Identify which institution — DPR, DPD, MPR, or MA — has authority to (a) pass laws, (b) interpret the Constitution, (c) conduct cassational review.
