# Alcohols Phenol Ether

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Alcohols** carry a hydroxyl (–OH) group on an sp³ aliphatic carbon (R–OH). **Phenols** carry –OH directly on an sp² aromatic carbon of a benzene ring (Ar–OH). **Ethers** link two alkyl or aryl groups through oxygen (R–O–R′) with no O–H bond. The three families share the general molecular formula **CₙH₂ₙ₊₂O** for saturated acyclic mono-oxygenated compounds (n ≥ 1) and are therefore constitutional isomers.

| Property | Alcohols (R–OH) | Phenols (Ar–OH) | Ethers (R–O–R′) |
|---|---|---|---|
| –OH on carbon type | sp³ aliphatic | sp² aromatic | None (C–O–C) |
| Acidity (pKa, dimensionless) | 16–18 (ethanol ≈ 16) | ≈ 10.0 | Practically neutral |
| Boiling point (C₁ analogue, °C) | Ethanol 78.37 | Phenol 182 | Methoxymethane −24.8 |
| H-bonding | Strong intermolecular | Inter + intra (ortho) | None (no O–H) |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** "**HBA-AEE**" — **H**ydrogen-bonding, **B**oiling point, **A**cidity order (carboxylic > phenol > alcohol) → three traps. Then recall "**C-O-H**" appears in alcohols, "**Ar-O-H**" in phenols, and "**C-O-C**" (no H) in ethers — the H is the whole game.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Acid Strength and Why
Phenol's pKa ≈ 10.0 vs ethanol's ≈ 16 means phenol is roughly **10⁶ times more acidic**. The phenoxide ion (C₆H₅O⁻) is resonance-stabilised across the ortho and para positions of the ring, while an alkoxide (RO⁻) has no such delocalisation. Electron-withdrawing groups (–NO₂ at ortho/para) further stabilise phenoxide; electron-donating groups (–CH₃, –OCH₃) destabilise it and lower acidity. The full order is: **carboxylic acids (pKa 4–5) > phenols (≈10) > alcohols (≈16–18) ≈ water (15.7)**.

#### Preparation Routes at a Glance
- **Alcohols**: acid-catalysed hydration of alkenes; hydroboration–oxidation (anti-Markovnikov); oxymercuration–demercuration (Markovnikov, no rearrangement); NaBH₄ or LiAlH₄ reduction of carbonyls; Grignard addition to aldehydes/ketones.
- **Phenols**: cumene–H₂O₂ industrial route; alkali fusion of benzene sulphonate; hydrolysis of diazonium salts (ArN₂⁺ + H₂O, warm).
- **Ethers**: Williamson synthesis (preferred laboratory method); acid-catalysed dehydration of alcohols (gives symmetrical ethers as side product to alkene).

#### Concept Comparison Matrix

| Reaction | Reagent & Conditions | Substrate Requirement | Major Product |
|---|---|---|---|
| Williamson synthesis | R–O⁻Na⁺ + R′–X (Sₙ2) | R′–X **must be 1°** (3° → E2 elimination) | R–O–R′ (unsymmetrical ether) |
| Lucas test | ZnCl₂ + conc. HCl | C₁–C₆ alcohols only | Turbidity = R–Cl (rate: 3° > 2° > 1°) |
| Acid-catalysed dehydration | conc. H₂SO₄, 413–440 K | 2°/3° alcohols (Zaitsev alkene) | Alkene + H₂O |
| Kolbe's reaction | Na phenoxide + CO₂, 400 K, 4–7 atm | Phenoxide ion | Salicylic acid (o-hydroxybenzoic acid) |
| Reimer–Tiemann | CHCl₃ + aq. NaOH, 340 K | Phenol | Salicylaldehyde (CHO at ortho) |
| Fries rearrangement | Phenyl ester + anh. AlCl₃ | Phenolic ester | o/p-hydroxyaryl ketone |
| Ether cleavage by HI (excess) | 2 HI, heat | Any dialkyl ether | R–I + R′–I + H₂O |

> 📌 **Formula Check:** Williamson — R–ONa + R′–X → R–O–R′ + NaX. Always pick the **simpler alkyl group** for the alkoxide and the **primary alkyl halide** for R′–X.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem
**Question:** An organic compound "X" has molecular formula C₇H₈O. It is insoluble in NaHCO₃, soluble in NaOH, gives a violet colour with neutral FeCl₃, and reacts with Br₂/water to give a white precipitate of formula C₇H₅Br₃O. Identify X and write the reaction with Br₂.

#### Solution:
1. Degree of unsaturation = (2×7 + 2 − 8)/2 = 4 → one benzene ring (3 double bonds + 1 ring).
2. Soluble in NaOH but not NaHCO₃ ⇒ phenolic –OH (pKa ≈ 10), not a carboxylic acid (pKa ≈ 5).
3. FeCl₃ violet colour + Br₂/H₂O giving a tribromo product ⇒ phenol ring with –OH activating all ortho/para positions.
4. Molecular formula C₇H₈O with one ring + one –OH leaves a –CH₃ group on the ring.
5. Therefore **X is *o*-cresol (2-methylphenol)**, also matching C₇H₈O.
6. Reaction: C₇H₈O + 3 Br₂ → C₇H₅Br₃O↓ + 3 HBr. The white precipitate is **2,4,6-tribromo-2-methylphenol** (molar mass ≈ 345.8 g mol⁻¹).

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students often pick benzyl alcohol (C₆H₅–CH₂OH) because it also has C₇H₈O. But benzyl alcohol's –OH is on an sp³ benzylic carbon — it does **not** give FeCl₃ colour and does **not** decolorise Br₂/water to a tribromo precipitate. Phenol-specific tests kill that option.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Intramolecular H-bonding and Steam Volatility
*o*-Nitrophenol shows intramolecular H-bonding between the phenolic –OH and the *ortho*-NO₂ group, forming a six-membered chelate ring. This frees the molecule from intermolecular H-bonding, lowering its b.p. (≈ 100 °C with steam) compared to the *m*- and *p*-isomers (m.p. 96 °C and 114 °C respectively). The consequence: only the *ortho* isomer is **steam-volatile**, a classical NCERT separation trick.

#### Ether Cleavage — Bond-Selective Mechanism
With **HI**, dialkyl ethers are cleaved by Sₙ2 at the **less hindered** alkyl carbon first, giving R–I + R′–OH. With **excess HI**, the alcohol is further converted: R′–OH + HI → R′–I + H₂O. For **methyl aryl ethers** (e.g. anisole, C₆H₅OCH₃): HI attacks the methyl (Sₙ2), giving C₆H₅OH + CH₃I. With HBr under forcing conditions, demethylation still gives phenol + CH₃Br; the aryl C–O bond remains intact because Sₙ2 on an sp² aromatic carbon is forbidden.

#### Epoxide Ring-Opening Selectivity
- **Acidic conditions (H⁺ catalyst, weak nucleophile):** nucleophile attacks the **more substituted** carbon (more carbocation character, Markovnikov-like).
- **Basic conditions (strong nucleophile like OH⁻, RO⁻, RMgX):** nucleophile attacks the **less substituted** carbon via Sₙ2.
- Both pathways give **anti addition** (inversion at attacked carbon) because the C–O bond breaks from the back side of the incoming nucleophile.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts
1. **Numerical pKa problem:** The pKa of 2,4-dinitrophenol is 4.11, of phenol 10.0, and of 2,4,6-trinitrophenol (picric acid) 0.38. Calculate how many times more acidic picric acid is than phenol, and explain using resonance.
2. **Assertion–Reason item:** "Assertion (A): Phenol is more acidic than ethanol. Reason (R): The phenoxide ion is stabilised by resonance while ethoxide is not." Both statements are true and R is the correct explanation of A — practise writing this exact phrasing for NEET A–R stems.

| Trap | Reality |
|---|---|
| Vinyl alcohol (CH₂=CH–OH) as a stable alcohol | Tautomerises to CH₃CHO (keto form dominates) |
| 3° alkyl halide in Williamson | E2 elimination dominates, ether yield negligible |
| Ether cleavage giving only 1 RI | With excess HI, both R groups become RI |
| Phenol more acidic than CH₃COOH | Wrong: pKa(phenol) ≈ 10 > pKa(acetic acid) ≈ 4.76 |
| Lucas test on C₈+ alcohol | Insoluble in reagent → no turbidity, false-negative |

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** NEET frequently asks the order **acidity > boiling point > H-bonding** for ROH / ArOH / ROR. Memorise C₁ b.p. values: ethanol 78.37, methoxymethane −24.8, phenol 182 °C — these exact numbers appear in numerical-match questions.
