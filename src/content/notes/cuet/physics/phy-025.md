# Dual Nature

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Radiation and matter both behave as waves **and** as particles. Light shows its particle side through the **photoelectric effect**, and matter shows its wave side through the **de Broglie wavelength**, confirmed experimentally by **Davisson and Germer**.

| Quantity | Formula | Key idea |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Photon energy | E = hν | h = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s |
| Work function | φ = hν₀ | ν₀ = threshold frequency |
| Max KE of photoelectron | K_max = hν − φ | Independent of intensity |
| Stopping potential | eVₛ = hν − φ | Vₛ depends only on ν |
| de Broglie wavelength | λ = h / mv = h / p | Momentum p in kg·m/s |

- **Threshold frequency** is the minimum ν below which no electron is emitted, no matter how bright the source.
- A graph of K_max vs ν is a straight line with slope **h** (Planck's constant) and x-intercept at ν₀.
- de Broglie wavelength of an electron accelerated through 150 V is ≈ 0.1 nm (≈ atomic spacing), which is why crystals diffract electrons.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Photoelectric effect — particle signature of light

Hertz observed that ultraviolet light ejects electrons from a metal surface. Classical wave theory failed: it predicted electron emission at any frequency if intensity were high enough, and a measurable time delay. Einstein (1905) resolved this by treating light as discrete quanta called **photons**, each carrying energy E = hν.

A photon is absorbed entirely by one electron. The electron uses part of that energy to escape the metal (the **work function** φ) and the rest becomes K_max:

K_max = hν − φ (valid for ν ≥ ν₀)

Intensity simply means more photons per second, so saturation current rises with intensity, but each photoelectron's energy does not.

| Observation | Wave theory says | Photon theory says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Emission below ν₀ | Possible at high intensity | Impossible — each photon lacks energy hν₀ |
| KE vs intensity | KE should rise with intensity | KE is independent of intensity |
| Emission timing | Time delay expected | Instantaneous (< 10⁻⁹ s) |

#### de Broglie hypothesis and matter waves

de Broglie (1924) proposed that any particle of momentum p has an associated wavelength λ = h / p. For electrons accelerated through potential V:

λ = h / √(2meV) = 1.227 / √V  nm (V in volts)

Matter waves are **not** electromagnetic; Born interpreted them as probability amplitudes — |ψ|² gives the likelihood of finding the particle at a location. Davisson and Germer (1927) diffracted electrons off a nickel crystal, confirming the wavelength quantitatively.

#### Heisenberg uncertainty

The wave nature of matter forces Δx · Δp ≥ h / (4π). An electron confined to an atom (~10⁻¹⁰ m) therefore has Δp ≥ ~10⁻²⁴ kg·m/s — non-negligible, which is why classical orbits fail inside atoms.

- **Numericals to practise:** find φ from ν₀, find Vₛ from λ, find λ of a 100 eV electron, and find the slope/intercept of the K_max vs ν graph.
- **Trap:** do not convert φ from eV to J before substituting into K_max = hν − φ; 1 eV = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Worked numerical — stopping potential from wavelength

Monochromatic light of wavelength 400 nm falls on a metal of work function 2.0 eV. Find the stopping potential.

- Photon energy: E = hc/λ = (1240 eV·nm) / 400 nm = 3.10 eV
- K_max = E − φ = 3.10 − 2.0 = 1.10 eV
- eVₛ = K_max → Vₛ = **1.10 V**

The stopping potential depends only on λ and φ, not on how many photons arrive.

#### Edge cases and adjacent links

- **Relativistic electrons:** when V exceeds ~10⁴ V, use p = γm₀v and λ = h/γm₀v; the non-relativistic formula overestimates λ.
- **Photon momentum and radiation pressure:** even though photons are massless, p = h/λ, used in Compton scattering (next chapter) and solar-sail calculations.
- **Connection to atoms:** Bohr's quantisation condition 2πr = nλ works because the electron's matter wave must form a standing wave around the nucleus.
- **Why matter waves aren't "sound":** sound needs a medium; matter waves describe probability in abstract configuration space, not mechanical displacement of any substance.

#### Common mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
| --- | --- |
| Using E = hc/λ for electrons | Use λ = h/√(2meV) — electron KE ≠ photon energy |
| Saying intensity increases K_max | Intensity changes **number** of photoelectrons, not their energy |
| Treating ν₀ as an x-intercept of K vs I graph | ν₀ is the x-intercept of the **K_max vs ν** graph |
| Dropping units in de Broglie problems | λ comes out in metres only if p is in kg·m/s |
