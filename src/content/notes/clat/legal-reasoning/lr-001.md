# Legal Maxims

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Legal maxims** are short Latin (occasionally English) phrases that capture a rule, doctrine, or presumption used in courts. CLAT tests them through passage-based MCQs that ask either the meaning, the correct maxim for a fact pattern, or the legal consequence flowing from a maxim.

Most-tested maxims in recent CLAT papers cluster in three buckets: criminal liability, contract interpretation, and procedural fairness.

- **Criminal liability:** *Mens Rea* (guilty mind), *Actus Reus* (guilty act), *Ignorantia Juris Non Excusat* (ignorance of law is no excuse).
- **Contracts:** *Volenti Non Fit Injuria* (consent to risk), *Caveat Emptor* (let the buyer beware), *Pacta Sunt Servanda* (agreements must be kept).
- **Procedure / fairness:** *Audi Alteram Partem* (hear the other side), *Nemo Judex In Causa Sua* (no one judge in own cause), *Res Ipsa Loquitur* (the thing speaks for itself).

Memorise the Latin, the English meaning, and one statutory anchor — usually Section 6 IPC for *Mens Rea*, Section 10 Indian Contract Act for free consent, Section 14 Sale of Goods Act for *Caveat Emptor* exceptions.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### What a "maxim" actually is

A legal maxim is a pithy formulation of a rule that judges have repeatedly applied, not a statute in itself. Indian courts draw maxims from English common law, Roman law, and equity. They function as **interpretive aids**: when a statute is silent or when a judge fills gaps in common-law reasoning, the maxim supplies a default rule. For example, *Ubi Jus Ibi Remedium* ("where there is a right, there is a remedy") underpins the writ jurisdiction under Articles 32 and 226 of the Constitution.

#### Categories tested in CLAT

The 25–30 high-frequency maxims divide cleanly into four functional categories.

| Category | Function | Representative maxims |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Substantive criminal law | Fix liability | *Mens Rea*, *Actus Reus*, *Ignorantia Juris Non Excusat* |
| Contract & tort | Allocate risk | *Volenti Non Fit Injuria*, *Caveat Emptor*, *In Pari Delicto* |
| Evidence & procedure | Govern proof and fairness | *Res Ipsa Loquitur*, *Audi Alteram Partem*, *Nemo Judex In Causa Sua*, *Habeas Corpus* |
| Interpretation | Read statutes and contracts | *Expressio Unius Est Exclusio Alterius*, *Ejusdem Generis*, *Noscitur a Sociis*, *Pacta Sunt Servanda* |

#### How examiners test application

A typical CLAT question frames a 100–150 word passage — a buyer injured by a defective appliance, a worker who fell off an unguarded machine after signing a risk waiver, a public hearing cancelled without notice — and asks which maxim applies. The skill tested is **matching the operative legal idea**, not translating Latin. Many wrong options are deliberately close (e.g., *Audi Alteram Partem* vs *Nemo Judex In Causa Sua*), so read the passage for the specific procedural defect.

- Translate the Latin to English first.
- Identify the *category* of legal problem (criminal / contract / procedural / interpretive).
- Eliminate maxims belonging to the wrong category before picking.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Statutory anchors you must know

CLAT frequently embeds a maxim inside a passage that also names a section. Linking maxim to section converts a recall question into a recognition question.

| Maxim | Statutory / constitutional anchor | What it operationally means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Mens Rea* | Section 6 IPC, General Exceptions Chapter | A culpable mental state is required for most offences; strict-liability statutes carve this out |
| *Actus Reus* | Sections 33–34 IPC | A voluntary act or unlawful omission causing harm |
| *Ignorantia Juris Non Excusat* | Section 76 IPC (limited exception) | Mistake of law does not excuse; mistake of fact may |
| *Volenti Non Fit Injuria* | Contract Act general principles on consent | Defence only when consent to risk was free, informed, and not against public policy |
| *Audi Alteram Partem* | Article 14 read with natural justice | Right to be heard before an adverse order |
| *Nemo Judex In Causa Sua* | Article 14, administrative law | Bias rule; decision-maker must be impartial |
| *Caveat Emptor* | Section 14, Sale of Goods Act, 1930, plus Consumer Protection Act, 2019 | Buyer must examine; exceptions cover merchantable quality, fitness for purpose, and consumer guarantees |

#### Edge cases and traps

- **Volenti vs. negligence:** *Volenti* requires *full knowledge* of the risk and *voluntary acceptance*; signing a generic safety waiver after a worksite briefing does not satisfy it. Courts (e.g., *Hall v. Hebert*, *Smith v. Baker*) restrict the defence tightly.
- **Caveat Emptor vs. CPA 2019:** The maxim survives in B2B sales but is largely displaced for consumers; choosing it for a retail e-commerce scenario loses marks.
- **Mens Rea vs. strict liability:** Public-welfare offences (food adulteration under the older Prevention of Food Adulteration Act, motor vehicle violations) often drop *Mens Rea* — the wrong default answer.
- **Audi Alteram Partem vs. Nemo Judex In Causa Sua:** The first is about *not hearing*; the second is about *biased hearing*. CLAT options regularly swap them.

#### Worked micro-example

Fact: A municipal authority cancels a shopkeeper's licence without giving prior notice or an opportunity to be heard. Authority cites financial irregularity.
Reasoning: The defect is **procedural**, not about bias. The correct maxim is *Audi Alteram Partem*. *Nemo Judex In Causa Sua* would apply only if the deciding officer had a personal interest in the outcome.

#### Practice prompts

1. A factory worker signs an acknowledgement that he "understood the risks" of operating unguarded machinery. He is injured. Does *Volenti Non Fit Injuria* apply? Why or why not?
2. A statute lists "motor vehicles, aircraft, and vessels" as regulated conveyances. A court interprets "vessels" to include hovercraft using *Ejusdem Generis*. Identify the trigger word and the class.
