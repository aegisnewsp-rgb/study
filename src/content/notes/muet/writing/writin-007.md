# Cohesion and Coherence

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Cohesion** is the surface-level glue that ties sentences together grammatically and lexically. **Coherence** is the logical unity that ties ideas together across a paragraph or essay. Both are assessed under the *Organisation and Development* criterion of MUET Paper 4, and strong use of both typically raises the writing band by 1–2 levels.

- **Cohesion (surface ties)** — built with cohesive devices: *reference* (pronouns, this, these), *conjunctions* (however, therefore, moreover), *lexical ties* (synonyms, repetition, collocation), and *substitution/ellipsis*.
- **Coherence (logical unity)** — built with a clear *topic sentence*, *paragraph unity*, *thematic progression*, and *logical sequencing* (chronological, cause-effect, general-to-specific).
- **Unity rule** — every sentence must develop the single idea announced in the topic sentence; anything off-topic breaks coherence.

| Term | What it does | Example device |
|---|---|---|
| Cohesion | Links sentences grammatically/lexically | pronouns, conjunctions, synonyms |
| Coherence | Links ideas logically | topic sentence, logical order, unity |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"C-Glue vs I-Glue"** — **C**ohesion glues **sentences** (grammar/words); **C**oherence glues **ideas** (logic). Remember: many *however/therefore* ≠ coherent if the ideas themselves jump around.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Defining the two principles

Halliday and Hasan frame cohesion as the **grammatical and lexical ties between sentences** — the linguistic "stitches" a reader can point to. Coherence is the **logical relationship between ideas** that the reader perceives through meaning flow. A paragraph can be richly cohesive (full of pronouns and connectors) yet incoherent if the ideas drift; another can be coherent in thought but flat in cohesion if every link is missing.

#### Halliday & Hasan's five cohesive devices

| Device | Function | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Reference | Pronouns/demonstratives point back or forward | *The students* failed because **they** did not revise. |
| Substitution | One item replaces another | *I prefer the former, not the latter.* |
| Ellipsis | Words are omitted because they are recoverable | *She will sing and he will dance.* |
| Conjunction | Logical relationship between clauses | *However*, *therefore*, *meanwhile* |
| Lexical cohesion | Vocabulary ties via repetition/synonymy/collocation | *child … kid … infant*; *doctor … hospital … patient* |

#### Logical connectors by function

Writers who reach Band 5–6 vary their connectors. The table below maps function to typical signals you should rotate in MUET essays.

| Function | Connectors to rotate |
|---|---|
| Addition | furthermore, moreover, in addition, similarly |
| Contrast | however, nevertheless, on the other hand, conversely |
| Cause–effect | therefore, consequently, as a result, thus |
| Sequence | firstly, subsequently, eventually, finally |
| Illustration | for example, for instance, such as, namely |

#### Paragraph structure for coherence

A coherent paragraph follows a predictable pattern that examiners recognise:

1. **Topic sentence** — announces the single controlling idea.
2. **Supporting sentences** — extend, evidence, exemplify the idea.
3. **Concluding/linking sentence** — closes the paragraph and bridges to the next.

> 📌 **Formula Check:** Unity = 1 topic sentence per paragraph + every supporting sentence referring back to it. A paragraph with two unrelated ideas scores as two weak paragraphs.

#### Logical sequencing patterns

Choose the sequence that fits your purpose: *chronological* (narratives), *cause–effect* (problem-solution essays), *general-to-specific* (introductions narrowing to thesis), *comparison–contrast*, or *classification* (categorising points into groups).

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students sprinkle *however, therefore, moreover* into every sentence and assume cohesion is solved. The MUET marker is checking whether the **ideas themselves** progress logically. A paragraph full of connectors but jumping from *pollution* to *family values* to *tourism* scores low on coherence even if "linked" grammatically.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** A student writes the paragraph below for MUET Writing Task 2 (essay on technology in education). Identify **two cohesion errors** and **one coherence error**, then rewrite a corrected version in 8–10 sentences.

> *"Technology has changed education. Students use laptops in class. However, it is expensive. My grandmother never used it. Furthermore, students can learn faster. Teachers worry because of screens. For example, laptops are heavy. However, my brother loves his tablet."*

#### Solution:

- **Cohesion error 1** — "it" in sentence 3 has no clear antecedent (laptops? technology? education?). Replace with **"laptop ownership"** or **"this technology"** with a prior referent.
- **Cohesion error 2** — "it" in sentence 4 misreferences "technology" while the surrounding sentences shift to grandmother and tablet. Rewrite as **"My grandmother never used laptops in her classroom."**
- **Coherence error** — sentence 4 (grandmother) drifts from the controlling idea of technology's effect on *current students*. Delete or move it to a separate paragraph on generational comparison.

#### Corrected rewrite (9 sentences):

> *"Technology has transformed modern classrooms. Today, students use laptops and tablets for daily lessons. This shift has accelerated learning, since digital resources allow instant access to information. Moreover, students can collaborate on shared documents from home. However, this technology is expensive, and many families struggle to afford devices. Furthermore, teachers worry about screen time and reduced face-to-face interaction. For example, prolonged laptop use may cause eye strain and shorter attention spans. Nevertheless, schools can introduce screen-break policies to address these concerns. In conclusion, technology benefits education when its costs and health effects are managed carefully."*

Notice: clear topic sentence, logical progression (benefits → problems → solution → close), varied connectors, and consistent reference chains (*this shift, this technology, these concerns*).

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Do not chain *however* and *furthermore* back-to-back — it signals mechanical, low-band writing. Vary connectors across **contrast / addition / cause / sequence** functions.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Cohesion–coherence at micro and macro levels

Micro-structure operates **sentence-to-sentence** (pronoun chains, conjunctions, lexical ties). Macro-structure operates **paragraph-to-paragraph** (logical division of the essay into introduction, body, conclusion; thematic progression where each new paragraph advances the thesis). MUET markers reward macro-level coherence through clear paragraphing and a recognisable essay architecture — not just sentence-level stitching.

#### Thematic progression patterns

| Pattern | How it works | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| Constant theme | Same theme maintained; rheme varies | Argumentative essays with one thesis |
| Linear progression | Rheme of sentence N becomes theme of N + 1 | Problem-solution chains |
| Derived theme | New theme derived from a hypernym | Classification essays |
| Split rheme | One rheme splits into multiple parallel themes | Listing advantages/disadvantages |

> 💡 **High-Yield Revision Hook:** **"T-Rhyme"** — every sentence has a **T**heme (old/given info) and a **Rh**eme (new claim). Linking sentence N's rheme to sentence N+1's theme is the engine of coherence.

#### Edge cases and advanced traps

1. **Cataphoric reference** — a pronoun refers *forward* to a later noun ("This is what I mean: honesty matters"). Use sparingly; MUET markers prefer anaphoric reference (looking backward) because it aids clarity.
2. **Ellipsis across paragraphs** — dropping repeated nouns across paragraph breaks is acceptable only if the preceding paragraph's topic sentence provides a recoverable anchor. Otherwise the reader loses the thread.
3. **Mixed sequencing signals** — combining *firstly* with *in conclusion* inside the same body paragraph signals incoherent architecture.
4. **Logical connectives as false friends** — *since* can mean "because" or "from that time"; misplacement causes coherence collapse in cause-effect essays.
5. **Cohesive density overload** — repeating the same noun phrase instead of using pronouns (or vice versa, replacing every noun with a pronoun) both hurt readability.

#### Connections to adjacent topics

- **Task Response / Content** (Writing criterion): coherence of *argument* depends on idea development; cohesion only dresses the surface.
- **Grammar range** (CEFR-aligned marking): complex sentence structures with relative clauses and participle phrases raise cohesion quality.
- **Vocabulary range**: lexical cohesion through synonyms and collocations shows Band 5–6 vocabulary control.

#### Two advanced practice prompts

1. **Diagnostic rewrite** — Take any 150-word paragraph from a past MUET Writing sample. Label each sentence with **T**heme and **R**heme, then redraw arrows from each Rheme to the next sentence's Theme. Breaks in the chain expose coherence weaknesses.
2. **Cohesion audit** — Colour-highlight five device types (reference, conjunction, lexical, substitution, ellipsis) in a Band 6 model essay. If any colour covers less than 5% of sentences, the writer is under-using that device — diversify.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Candidates often believe that *more connectors = higher score*. The MUET marking scheme explicitly rewards **appropriate, varied, and accurate** cohesion, not sheer quantity. Three well-placed connectors outperform ten mechanical ones.
