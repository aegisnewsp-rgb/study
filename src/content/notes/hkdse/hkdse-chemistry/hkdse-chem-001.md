# I. Planet Earth — HKDSE Chemistry Notes

This topic covers Topic I of the Compulsory Part of HKDSE Chemistry. It addresses the atmosphere, hydrosphere and lithosphere; the origin of the atmosphere and oceans; the impact of human activities on the environment (acid rain, ozone depletion, greenhouse effect, climate change); water treatment and sewage treatment; and the chemistry of metals and non-metals in the Earth's crust. This is one of twelve Compulsory Part topics and is tested on both Paper 1 Section A (MCQ) and Section B (structured/essay).

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Earth's three spheres**: atmosphere (gas envelope), hydrosphere (water — oceans, rivers, lakes, groundwater, ice), lithosphere (solid Earth — crust, mantle, core).
- **Origin of the atmosphere**: volcanic outgassing produced the early atmosphere (water vapour, CO₂, nitrogen, ammonia, sulfur compounds). Photosynthesis by cyanobacteria produced oxygen, which accumulated over geological time.
- **Origin of the oceans**: water vapour from volcanic outgassing condensed as the Earth cooled, forming the oceans.
- **Human impact**: acid rain (SO₂ and NOₓ from fossil fuel combustion), ozone depletion (CFCs), greenhouse effect and climate change (CO₂ and other greenhouse gases), eutrophication (excess nutrients in water).
- **Water treatment**: filtration, sedimentation, chlorination, fluoridation, pH adjustment.
- **Sewage treatment**: primary (physical — screening, sedimentation), secondary (biological — activated sludge), tertiary (chemical — nutrient removal).

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing the ozone layer with the greenhouse effect — the ozone layer absorbs UV-B; greenhouse gases absorb infrared radiation.
- Treating the early atmosphere as similar to today's — the early atmosphere had little or no oxygen.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### The atmosphere

The atmosphere is approximately 78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen, 1% argon, plus trace gases (CO₂, methane, ozone, water vapour). The atmosphere has several layers (troposphere, stratosphere, mesosphere, thermosphere, exosphere).

The ozone layer (in the stratosphere) absorbs most of the Sun's UV-B radiation. Ozone depletion by CFCs (chlorofluorocarbons) was identified in the 1980s; the Montreal Protocol (1987) phased out CFC production. The ozone layer is expected to recover by mid-century.

#### The hydrosphere

Water covers ~71% of Earth's surface. ~97% is saltwater (oceans); ~2% is frozen (ice caps, glaciers); ~1% is freshwater (rivers, lakes, groundwater).

Water is a polar molecule with hydrogen bonding, giving it unique properties: high boiling point, high specific heat capacity, cohesion, adhesion, surface tension. Water is the universal solvent.

Water treatment (drinking water):

- **Screening**: removes large debris.
- **Coagulation/flocculation**: alum or ferric chloride aggregates fine particles.
- **Sedimentation**: particles settle out.
- **Filtration**: through sand, gravel, or membrane filters.
- **Disinfection**: chlorination (or UV, ozone).
- **Fluoridation**: adds fluoride to prevent tooth decay.
- **pH adjustment**: to prevent corrosion of pipes.

Sewage treatment:

- **Primary**: screening, grit removal, sedimentation (primary sludge).
- **Secondary**: biological treatment (activated sludge or trickling filter); aerobic bacteria break down organic matter.
- **Tertiary**: nutrient removal (nitrogen, phosphorus), disinfection.
- **Sludge treatment**: anaerobic digestion (produces biogas), dewatering, landfill or incineration.

#### The lithosphere

The lithosphere is the rigid outer layer of the Earth, including the crust and the uppermost mantle. The crust is composed of oxygen (46%), silicon (28%), aluminium (8%), iron (5%), calcium (4%), sodium (3%), potassium (3%), magnesium (2%), plus trace elements.

Common minerals: silicates (quartz, feldspar, mica), carbonates (calcite, dolomite), oxides (hematite, magnetite), sulfides (galena, pyrite).

Common metals in the Earth's crust: aluminium (most abundant metal), iron, calcium, sodium, potassium, magnesium. Common non-metals: oxygen, silicon, hydrogen, nitrogen, sulfur, phosphorus.

#### Human impact on the environment

**Acid rain**: SO₂ and NOₓ from fossil fuel combustion react with water in the atmosphere to form sulfuric and nitric acids, lowering the pH of precipitation. Effects: damage to forests, lakes, buildings; mobilisation of toxic metals. Control: flue gas desulfurisation, low-NOx burners, cleaner fuels.

**Ozone depletion**: CFCs release chlorine atoms in the stratosphere, which catalytically destroy ozone. Effects: increased UV-B, increased skin cancer and cataract risk. Control: Montreal Protocol (1987) phasing out CFCs; recovery is underway.

**Greenhouse effect and climate change**: CO₂, CH₄, N₂O and halocarbons trap heat in the atmosphere. Effects: rising global temperatures, sea-level rise, ocean acidification, more frequent extreme weather events. Control: emissions reductions, renewable energy, energy efficiency, carbon capture and storage.

**Eutrophication**: excess nutrients (nitrogen, phosphorus) from agricultural runoff or sewage cause algal blooms, which block light and deplete dissolved oxygen when they decompose. Effects: fish kills, loss of biodiversity. Control: nutrient removal in sewage treatment, reduced fertiliser use, riparian buffer zones.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-topic links

- **X. Chemical Equilibrium**: atmospheric CO₂ is part of the global carbon cycle; ocean acidification is the equilibrium between atmospheric CO₂ and dissolved CO₂/HCO₃⁻/CO₃²⁻.
- **VIII. Chemical Reactions and Energy**: combustion of fossil fuels is the source of greenhouse gas emissions and the source of the enthalpy change topics.
- **XIII. Industrial Chemistry (Elective)**: industrial chemistry connects to environmental issues (acid rain, eutrophication).

#### Exam technique

- **For environmental questions**: identify the pollutant, the source, the effect, and the control measure. Use specific examples.
- **For water treatment questions**: trace the process step by step (screening → coagulation → sedimentation → filtration → disinfection).
- **For atmospheric chemistry questions**: identify the gas, the reaction, and the environmental impact.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "The ozone hole is a hole in the ozone layer." The ozone "hole" is a region of significantly depleted stratospheric ozone over Antarctica in the Southern Hemisphere spring.
- "Acid rain is a localised problem." Acid rain is a transboundary problem — emissions in one country can cause acid rain in another.
- "Climate change is the same as weather." Climate is the long-term average of weather; climate change refers to long-term shifts.
