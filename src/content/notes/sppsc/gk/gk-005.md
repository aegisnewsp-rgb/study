# Current Affairs (National & International)

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Current Affairs in the SPSC (Sindh) Combined Competitive Examination tests your awareness of **recent events** in Pakistan (with a Sindh focus) and the wider world over the **last 6–12 months**. Questions appear as MCQs inside the General Knowledge paper, alongside Pakistan Studies, Islamiat, and static GK. A candidate must track **appointments**, **schemes**, **summits**, **awards**, and **Sindh-specific issues** like Thar coal and the Indus river system.

| Domain | Must-Track Items (Last 6–12 Months) |
|---|---|
| **Pakistan Federal** | President, Prime Minister, COAS, Chief Justice, Senate Chairman, National Assembly Speaker, new federal secretaries, IMF/EAD policy actions. |
| **Sindh Province** | Governor Sindh, Chief Minister Sindh, Karachi/Hyderabad/Sukkur developments, Thar coalfield milestones, Sindh Solid Waste Management, provincial cabinet reshuffles. |
| **International** | UNGA sessions, G20 Summit host & theme, COP climate summit outcomes, Nobel Prize winners, BRICS/SCO developments, Pakistan's diplomatic wins. |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **PAINS** — **P**akistan Federal posts, **A**wards (Nobel/Pride of Performance), **I**nternational orgs (UN, OIC, SAARC, SCO), **N**eighbours & borders (India, China, Afghanistan, Iran), **S**indh-specific schemes. Read one newspaper column daily and group every fact under PAINS.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### What SPSC Actually Tests

The SPSC General Knowledge section carries roughly **3% weight** in the screening MCQs, but Current Affairs is the highest-scoring sub-topic because it rewards consistent newspaper reading rather than rote memorisation. Paper setters draw from **Dawn**, **The News**, **Express Tribune**, **Pakistan Today**, and the **PID (Press Information Department)** releases. Expect 5–8 direct MCQs from current events plus 3–4 indirectly linked ones (e.g., "Which organisation published the X report?"). Permanent factual GK (constitution, geography) overlaps heavily, so revise them together.

#### Core Areas You Must Cover

1. **Pakistan's constitutional offices** — President, Prime Minister, Speaker, Chairman Senate, Chief Justice, Attorney General, Auditor General.
2. **Sindh-specific governance** — Governor (ceremonial head), Chief Minister (executive head), Provincial Assembly of Sindh (legislature), Sindh High Court (judiciary).
3. **International organisations and Pakistan's role** — UN (founded 1945, Pakistan member since 1947), OIC (1969, Pakistan a founding member), SAARC (1985), ECO (1985), SCO (Pakistan joined as full member in 2017), WTO, IMF, World Bank, WHO.
4. **Recent appointments, resignations, oath-taking ceremonies** at federal and Sindh cabinet level.
5. **Awards** — Nobel Prize (six fields: Physics, Chemistry, Medicine, Literature, Peace, Economics), Pulitzer Prize (US journalism/literature), Pakistan civilian honours Hilal-i-Imtiaz, Sitara-i-Imtiaz, Tamgha-i-Imtiaz, Pride of Performance.
6. **Summits and conferences** — UNGA (annual, September, New York), G20 (rotating presidency), COP (UNFCCC climate), BRICS, ECO summits.

#### Commonly Confused Terms

| Confused Pair | Key Distinction |
|---|---|
| **Governor of Sindh vs Chief Minister of Sindh** | Governor is the **ceremonial** head appointed by the President on PM's advice; CM is the **elected executive** head who commands the provincial assembly's majority. |
| **SAARC vs ECO vs SCO** | SAARC = South Asian regional body (1985, HQ Kathmandu). ECO = Economic Cooperation Organisation (Iran-Turkey-Pakistan driven, HQ Tehran). SCO = Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (Eurasian security bloc, Pakistan joined as full member in 2017). |
| **WHO vs WTO vs IMF vs World Bank** | WHO = health (UN specialised agency, Geneva). WTO = trade (Geneva). IMF = short-term balance-of-payments support (Washington). World Bank = long-term development loans (Washington). |
| **Hilal-i-Imtiaz vs Sitara-i-Imtiaz vs Tamgha-i-Imtiaz** | All are Pakistani civilian honours; Hilal is highest among the three, then Sitara, then Tamgha. Pride of Performance is for arts/sports/literature. |
| **Nobel Prize Economics vs Nobel Prize Literature** | Economics = official "Sveriges Riksbank Prize in Economic Sciences" added in 1968. Literature is the older literary award. Don't mix winners. |

> 📌 **Formula Check:** No numerical formulas — but recall that **Pakistan joined SCO in 2017** and **SAARC in 1985**; these dates are the most-quoted year-fact traps in SPSC papers.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** Pakistan became a full member of which international organisation in 2017?
(a) SAARC  (b) ECO  (c) SCO  (d) NATO

#### Solution:
1. Identify the year → **2017**.
2. Match with Pakistan's accession history:
   - SAARC → Pakistan is a **founding member (1985)**. Not 2017.
   - ECO → Pakistan is a **founding member (1985)**. Not 2017.
   - SCO → Pakistan's full membership became effective on **9 June 2017**. ✔
   - NATO → Pakistan is **not** a member.
3. Correct answer: **(c) SCO**.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Candidates confuse ECO (1985) with SCO (2017) because both have three letters and a "Cooperation" theme. Remember the anchor: **SCO = Shanghai = Security (2017)**, **ECO = Economic (1985)**.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Sindh-Focused Edge Cases

SPSC papers disproportionately reward Sindh-specific awareness because the provincial public service commission prioritises local governance. The following six themes appear almost every cycle:

| Sindh Theme | Specific Facts to Master |
|---|---|
| **Thar Coalfield** | One of the world's largest coal reserves; Engro Powergen Thar (EPTL) Block-II; Thar Coal Power Plant connected to national grid; lignite-based generation capacity expansions. |
| **Indus River System** | Six rivers: Indus, Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, Sutlej; **Indus Waters Treaty 1960** between India and Pakistan (brokered by World Bank); Tarbela and Mangla dams. |
| **Karachi Development** | K-IV (Greater Karachi Water Supply), K-Septic, BRT Yellow/Red/Green lines, Sindh Solid Waste Management Board, Karachi Circular Railway revival. |
| **Provincial Ministries** | Sindh cabinet portfolios — Health, Education, Irrigation, Local Government, Planning & Development. |
| **High Court of Sindh** | Located in Karachi; Chief Justice of Sindh High Court is a constitutional post under Article 200 of the 1973 Constitution. |
| **Sindh Cultural Heritage** | Shah Abdul Latif Bhittai, Sachal Sarmast, Sufi shrines (Sehwan Sharif), Mohenjo-Daro (UNESCO World Heritage, Indus Valley). |

#### International Bodies — Membership Mechanics

1. **UN General Assembly (UNGA)** — All 193 member states; Pakistan member since 30 September 1947; **OIC** was founded in 1969 at the Rabat Summit (Pakistan key architect).
2. **G20** — 19 countries + EU + AU; presidency rotates annually; Indonesia 2022, India 2023, Brazil 2024, South Africa 2025.
3. **BRICS** — Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa (original five); expanded 2024 to include Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, UAE, Saudi Arabia (Argentina declined).
4. **COP summits** — COP26 Glasgow 2021, COP27 Sharm el-Sheikh 2022, COP28 Dubai 2023, COP29 Baku 2024 (Loss & Damage Fund operationalised).
5. **Nobel Prize** — Awarded annually since 1901 (Peace since 1901; Economics since 1969); announced in October each year.
6. **Pakistan's multilateral posture** — Always a UN peacekeeping contributor; observer/member of various WTO and climate blocs; recently upgraded diplomatic ties with regional neighbours.

#### Two Advanced Practice Prompts

1. **Constitutional Trap:** A 2024-model SPSC MCQ asks, *"Who appoints the Governor of Sindh?"* — Answer: **the President of Pakistan** (on the advice of the Prime Minister), under Article 101 of the 1973 Constitution. Do **not** confuse this with the **Chief Minister**, who is elected by the Provincial Assembly of Sindh.

2. **Economic Indicator Trap:** A question lists *"foreign exchange reserves, inflation rate, GDP growth, remittances"* — only ask which institution publishes the official figure. Answer: **State Bank of Pakistan (SBP)** for reserves and monetary stats; **Pakistan Bureau of Statistics (PBS)** for inflation (CPI) and GDP. Mixing SBP with PBS is the standard trap.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** SPSC setters test **currency-of-knowledge** — outdated facts (e.g., saying Pakistan joined SCO "recently" without naming 2017, or quoting a previous year's G20 host) are penalised. Anchor every fact to its **year of occurrence** and **issuing authority** (UN, IMF, PID, SBP) before walking into the exam hall.
