# Understanding Explicitly Stated Information — OSSLT Reading 1

Reading 1 is the largest cluster of items on the OSSLT. The EQAO Framework describes it as locating directly stated ideas and supporting details in a selection. Every Real-Life Narrative, Information Paragraph, News Report, and Dialogue selection begins with a stretch of Reading 1 items, and the OSSLT rubric scores accuracy on these items first.

> Verify the live item count, session timing and any in-year item-type changes on https://www.eqao.com/the-assessments/osslt/ before planning around the figures below.

---

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### What Reading 1 actually tests

- **Identifying who, what, when, where.** The who/what/when/where are stated in plain language; the item is "according to the selection, when did X happen?" and the answer is on the page.
- **Direct recall of stated details.** Names, dates, quantities, places, and definitions that the writer has placed inside the text.
- **Explicit cause and effect.** The selection uses signal words ("because," "as a result," "this led to") to draw the link; the item asks for the consequence or the reason, both of which are visible in the selection.
- **Sequence and order.** The selection uses time markers ("first," "next," "after," "finally") and the item asks what came first, what came next, or what happened just before something else.

#### One-pass drill

1. Read the selection once for sense.
2. Skim the item stems and underline the question word (who, what, when, where, why, how).
3. Scan the selection for the exact detail that matches the stem; do not rely on memory.
4. Match the detail to the answer choice. Eliminate choices that add information not in the selection.
5. If two choices look plausible, the right answer is the one with detail word-for-word from the text.

#### Examiner traps on Reading 1

- A detail is *plausible* but not in the selection. Reading 1 requires the answer to be stated in the text, not implied from prior knowledge.
- Two answer choices use different units (m vs km, weeks vs months). The question stem asks for one specific unit; the matching detail is the one in the same unit.
- The item stem paraphrases the selection. The right answer is the option that best matches the *meaning* of the original wording, not a literal substring.

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Strategy for Reading 1 passages

**Read the stem before the selection when the platform allows it.** On the OSSLT computer-based platform, the items sit beside the selection; scan the stems first to mark what is being asked, then locate the matching detail. This is faster than reading the entire selection and going back.

**Underline signal words in the selection.** "Because," "so," "as a result of," "this led to," "therefore" mark explicit cause-effect. "First," "next," "after that," "finally," "in 2019," "two weeks later" mark sequence. Underlining these keeps the eye locked on the explicit answer rather than drifting to inference.

**Use a single-pass method for each item:**

1. Decide what kind of detail is being asked (name, date, place, quantity, reason, sequence).
2. Find the line or sentence that contains that detail.
3. Compare the answer choices to the wording of the line.
4. The right answer is the choice that matches the detail as written.

#### Practice routine for Reading 1

- Take one EQAO-style Real-Life Narrative selection. Time yourself on the seven Reading 1 items only. Target: under 30 seconds per item.
- Take one News Report selection. Focus on the lead and first two paragraphs — most Reading 1 items in news writing live there.
- Take one Dialogue selection. Reading 1 in Dialogue is mostly about who-said-what: match the speaker label to the line.
- Take one Information Paragraph selection. Reading 1 in information text is mostly about definition recall.

#### Cross-check with Reading 2 and Reading 3

A Reading 1 answer is *always* explicitly stated. If the answer requires combining two ideas, or guessing what the writer meant, it is a Reading 2 (inference) item, not Reading 1. Many students lose marks on Reading 1 by reading too carefully — over-interpreting simple statements. Read the selection at face value for Reading 1 items.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How Reading 1 connects to the other OSSLT items

- **Reading 2 (inference)** uses the same selection. The Reading 1 items give you the explicit facts; the Reading 2 items then ask you to combine or extend those facts.
- **Reading 3 (making connections)** uses the same selection plus your own knowledge. Reading 1 supplies the explicit anchors for Reading 3 to build on.
- **The open-response reading item** is built on Reading 3 but uses Reading 1 detail as supporting evidence. If Reading 1 is solid, the open-response paragraph is easier to write.

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"This is in the selection somewhere."** Reading 1 items are about *which stated detail* matches the stem. If two choices both appear in the selection, the right answer is the one the stem is asking about.
- **"The selection said X, so the answer is X."** The selection may say several things in the same paragraph. The stem is asking for one specific detail. Re-read the stem after locating each candidate detail.
- **"I know this from real life."** Reading 1 is about the selection, not the world. If the answer is not in the selection, it is wrong even if it is true.

#### Exam technique

- Read the item stem first. The stem tells you what kind of detail to look for (name, date, quantity, reason, sequence, definition).
- Mark the relevant line mentally before reading the choices. The right answer will match the wording of that line.
- If you cannot find the detail, skip the item and return. Do not let a hard Reading 1 item slow down the easy ones behind it.
- Use the platform's flag-for-review function if you are unsure. Do not leave blanks on selected-response items.
