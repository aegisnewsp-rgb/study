# Maurya Empire

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> The shortest path through Mauryan India: one founder, one philosopher, one war, one conversion.

- **Founded c. 322 BCE** by **Chandragupta Maurya** with **Chanakya (Kautilya)** as the strategist who authored the *Arthashastra*.
- **Ashoka (r. c. 268–232 BCE)** fought the **Kalinga War (c. 261 BCE)**, after which he embraced **Dhamma** — a code of ethics distinct from monastic Buddhism.
- Capital: **Pataliputra** (modern Patna), at the **confluence of the Ganga, Son, Gandak and Punpun**.
- Greek source to remember: **Megasthenes' *Indica*** sent by Seleucus, describing 7 castes and Pataliputra's 570 towers, 64 gates.
- Last ruler pushed aside: **Brihadratha (185 BCE)**, overthrown by Pushyamitra Shunga.

> 💡 **Memory Hook:** **"C-C-A-A-B-B"** — *Chandragupta, Chanakya, Arthashastra* (one bloc) → *Ashoka, Ashoka's Dhamma after Kalinga* (second bloc) → *Brihadratha, 185 BCE* (collapse). The double letters mark the philosopher-author pair and the war-conversion pair.

| Ruler | Span (BCE) | One-line anchor |
|---|---|---|
| Chandragupta Maurya | 322–298 | Defeated Nandas, treaty with Seleucus |
| Bindusara | 298–272 | "Amitraghata" — slayer of foes |
| Ashoka | 268–232 | Kalinga War, Dhamma, edicts |
| Later Mauryas | 232–185 | Decline, ended by Pushyamitra |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Sources and the Foundational Pair

Two threads run through every Mauryan answer: the **Greek accounts** (Megasthenes, Strabo, Justin, Pliny, Arrian) and the **Indian treatise** *Arthashastra*. Treat them as complementary — Greek sources give external observation, Kautilya gives internal mechanics. The chronicler Justin's *Epitome* names the founder as **Sandrokottos**, which examiners love to test against Chandragupta.

#### Kautilya's Statecraft — Two Lists, Often Confused

Kautilya frames foreign policy through **Shadgunya** (six methods) and state through the **Saptanga** (seven limbs). TNPSC questions often swap these terms.

| Concept | Number | Six/Seven Elements | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shadgunya | 6 | Sandhi, Vigraha, Yana, Asana, Sanshraya, Dvaidhibhava | Foreign-policy choices |
| Saptanga | 7 | Swami, Amatya, Janapada, Durga, Kosa, Dand, Mitra | Constituents of the state |

> 📌 **Formula Check:** *Bhaga* = 1/6 of produce (the canonical Mauryan land-revenue share), distinct from *Bali* (voluntary gift), *Kara* (periodic cess) and *Shulka* (customs duty). Use the right word — each denotes a different levy.

#### Administration and Espionage

The hierarchy runs **King → Amatyas → Sachivas → Sthanikas (provincial officers)**. Below sit the **Rajukas** (land/land-revenue officers, mentioned in Ashoka's edicts) and the spy network **Samstha** with three categories: **Sanjivakas**, **Samahartas**, **Saramaparikas**. Brahmacharis too appear in the *Arthashastra* as a category of wandering informants.

#### Ashoka's Edicts — Site, Script, Substance

| Edict Type | Sample Sites | Scripts Used | Topic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rock Edicts | Girnar, Dhauli, Jaugada, Shahbazgarhi, Mansehra, Yerragudi, Sopara | Brahmi, Kharosthi, Greek, Aramaic | Dhamma, moral law |
| Pillar Edicts | Delhi-Topra, Allahabad-Kosam, Lauriya-Araraj, Lauriya-Nandangarh, Rampurva, Nigali Sagar, Brahmagiri | Brahmi | Administration, welfare |

Pillar Edict **VII** records Ashoka's withdrawal from Lumbini and his Dhamma diplomacy with Hellenistic kings (Antiochus, Ptolemy, Antigonus, Magas).

#### Worked Example: Chronology Question

> A TNPSC prelims MCQ asks: **"Arrange the following in chronological order: (a) Ashoka's accession, (b) Kalinga War, (c) Conversion to Dhamma, (d) Dispatch of Dhamma emissaries to Hellenistic kingdoms."**

#### Resolution:

1. Ashoka's accession — **c. 268 BCE** (Bindusara died c. 272, four-year interregnum, then Ashoka).
2. Kalinga War — **c. 261 BCE** (rock edict XIII states the conquest).
3. Conversion to Dhamma — **c. 261 BCE onward**, immediately after Kalinga (edicts XIII, V, IV).
4. Dhamma emissaries to the five Hellenistic kings — **c. 257 BCE** (edict XIII names the rulers).

Correct order: **a → b → c → d**.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Candidates flip the Kalinga War and the conversion, treating the war as Ashoka's *motive* rather than his *trigger*. Conversion follows the war, not the other way round; pre-Kalinga Ashoka was described in his own edicts as *priyadarsi* but not yet as a Dhamma sovereign.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

#### Dhamma versus Buddhism — The Distinction That Loses Marks

Ashoka's edicts speak of **Dhamma**, not Buddhism. Dhamma is a lay-ethical programme: restraint, respect for elders, abstention from animal slaughter on specific days, tolerance of sects, and welfare of all beings. Buddhism is the monastic path he eventually patronised by building **viharas** and convening the **Third Buddhist Council at Pataliputra (c. 250 BCE)**. A question phrased as "Ashoka's religion" expects Buddhism; phrased as "Ashoka's policy" expects Dhamma.

#### Advanced Traps and Exceptions

1. **Vishnugupta ≠ a different author.** Vishnugupta is Kautilya's *gotra* name. The *Arthashastra* is attributed to Kautilya, Vishnugupta and Chanakya — three names, one person. Inventing a separate Vishnugupta is the most common error.
2. **Sanchi Stupa** was commissioned by Ashoka, not Chandragupta, and later enlarged under the Shungas and Satavahanas.
3. **Languages of the rock edicts** are not uniform. Shahbazgarhi and Mansehra are **Kharosthi**; the Kandahar bilingual inscription of Ashoka is in **Greek and Aramaic**; all others are predominantly **Brahmi**.
4. **Brihadratha's fall** in 185 BCE is the standard terminus. Pushyamitra Shunga, a *senapati* (general), killed him during a parade. Do not cite 180 BCE or 170 BCE — these are rounded off by some popular sources and lose a mark.
5. **Ashoka's missionaries** — Mahinda (son) to Sri Lanka, Mahinda's sister **Sanghamitta** carrying a sapling of the Bodhi tree. The Mahavamsa records the mission; the rock edicts confirm the broader Dhamma mission.
6. **Megasthenes' embassy** is dated **c. 302 BCE** under Seleucus I Nicator, well before Ashoka. Indica was lost; fragments survive via Strabo, Arrian, Pliny, Diodorus and Athenaeus.

#### Comparative Frame: Mauryan vs. Gupta Administration

| Feature | Maurya | Gupta |
|---|---|---|
| Title of king | *Raja*, *Samrat* (Ashoka) | *Maharajadhiraja*, *Parameshvara* |
| Provincial unit | *Sthanas* under *Sthanikas* | *Bhuktis* under *Uparikas* |
| Espionage | Formal Samstha network | Less central; feudatories relied on |
| Land revenue | *Bhaga* (1/6 produce) | *Bhaga* continued; *Uparikara* for officials |
| Foreign contact | Hellenistic kings (Antiochus, etc.) | Sassanid, Roman trade via ports |

#### Two Practice Prompts

1. **Map-based question (Prelims style):** Match these edict sites to their modern state — Girnar (Gujarat), Dhauli (Odisha), Shahbazgarhi (Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan), Yerragudi (Andhra Pradesh), Sopara (Maharashtra), Brahmagiri (Karnataka).
2. **Assertion-reason (Mains style):** "Assertion: Megasthenes is the most reliable Greek source on the Mauryas. Reason: He resided in Pataliputra at the court of Chandragupta Maurya." Examine whether the reason correctly supports the assertion — note that his embassy predates Bindusara's birth and that his account survives only through later authors, so 'most reliable' must be qualified.
