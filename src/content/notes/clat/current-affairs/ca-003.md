# Legal News

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Legal News in CLAT tests your awareness of Indian judicial, legislative and executive developments reported within the **12-month window** before the exam date. It sits inside the **Current Affairs & General Knowledge** section, which carries roughly 25% of CLAT marks, with Legal News contributing about **5%**. Expect passage-based comprehension, factual MCQs or principle-application sets drawn from recent Supreme Court and High Court rulings, ordinances under Article 123, and parliamentary statutes.

- **Three new criminal codes** — BNS, BNSS and BSA replaced the IPC, CrPC and Indian Evidence Act from **1 July 2024**.
- **Ratio decidendi** is the binding legal principle; **obiter dicta** are persuasive observations.
- **Article 32** (Supreme Court) and **Article 226** (High Courts) enforce fundamental rights through writs.
- Always cite the **bench strength, judge name, date and citation** of any judgment you quote.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### What Examiners Actually Test

CLAT papers do not reward political slug writing. A passage on a recent ruling will ask you to identify the legal issue, the constitutional/statutory basis, the relief granted and the precedent value. Track at least one national daily (The Hindu, Indian Express) and one law outlet (LiveLaw, Bar & Bench, Supreme Court Observer) every week to stay inside the qualifying window.

#### Mapping News to Legal Categories

| Category | What to Track | Example Authority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Constitutional | Article 32/142/226 rulings, basic structure references | Supreme Court Constitution Benches |
| Criminal | BNS sections replacing IPC sections, BNSS procedural changes | Ministry of Home Affairs |
| Commercial / IPR | Patent, trademark, arbitration rulings | Commercial Division of HC |
| Family | Live-in relationships, same-sex marriage jurisprudence | Supreme Court |
| Regulatory | PMLA, RTI, Election Commission notifications | NALSA, ECI, MoLJ |

#### How to Read a Reported Judgment

1. Note the **citation** (e.g. *AIR 2024 SC 1234*) and **bench strength**.
2. Separate the **ratio decidendi** from **obiter dicta**.
3. Identify the **statutes interpreted** — old IPC/CrPC references must be migrated to BNS/BNSS equivalents.
4. Read the **press release of the Supreme Court** on sci.gov.in before trusting newspaper summaries.

The most common slip is quoting old section numbers — IPC 302 is now BNS 302, IPC 376 is BNS 63, IPC 420 is BNS 318.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Examiner Traps

- **Interim orders vs final judgments**: A stay or notice is not the "law declared". Only a final ratio is binding precedent under Article 141.
- **Ordinance vs Act**: An ordinance under Article 123 lapses in six weeks unless Parliament ratifies it. Many "bills" you read are still ordinances when reported.
- **Law Commission Reports** are recommendations, not law. They appear in news but cannot be cited as enacted statutes.
- **PIL status confusion**: Admission of a PIL is procedural; only the disposal order settles the substantive question.
- **Article 370 abrogation** news must be tagged with the 2019 Constitutional (Downgrade) Order and subsequent Supreme Court ruling of December 2023.

#### Worked Example — Migrating an IPC Reference

| Old Provision | New Code | New Section | Subject |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| IPC 302 | BNS | 302 | Murder |
| IPC 376 | BNS | 63 | Sexual offences |
| IPC 420 | BNS | 318 | Cheating |
| CrPC 125 | BNSS | 144 | Maintenance |
| IEA 65B | BSA | 63 | Electronic evidence |

If a CLAT passage still says "Section 376 IPC", the correct migration in your answer key is **Section 63 BNS**.

#### Practice Prompts

1. Read the latest Constitution Bench ruling on Article 370 and write a 100-word note separating ratio from obiter.
2. Pick one BNS provision notified in 2024–25 and explain which IPC section it replaced, citing the commencement date of 1 July 2024.

#### Exam Strategy

Allocate **8–10 minutes** to the Current Affairs section overall, with about **2 minutes** for Legal News items. Skim the passage for the court name, statute and relief before reading options.
