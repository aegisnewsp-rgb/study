# Trial Balance

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

A **Trial Balance (TB)** is an internal listing of all ledger account balances on a specific date, used to verify that **total debits equal total credits** under the double-entry system.

#### Must-Know Facts

- **Verification equation:** Total Debits = Total Credits (PKR/USD).
- **Not a financial statement** — it is an arithmetical check only.
- **Three preparation methods:** total method, balance method, total-and-balance method.

| Balance Side | Account Types |
|---|---|
| **Debit balances** | Assets, Expenses, Drawings, Losses |
| **Credit balances** | Liabilities, Capital, Income, Gains |

> **Exam trap:** A balanced trial balance does **not** prove the books are error-free. Errors of principle, complete omission, and compensating errors leave the TB in agreement.

---

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Purpose and Scope

The trial balance is drawn up after posting all journal entries to the ledger and before preparing financial statements under **IAS 1 – Presentation of Financial Statements**. Its sole purpose is to confirm the **arithmetical accuracy** of double-entry bookkeeping. It is an internal working paper, not distributed to external users.

#### Methods of Preparation

#### Total Method

Each ledger account is totalled separately on both debit and credit sides, and the net figure is entered in the trial balance on the heavier side.

#### Balance Method

Only the closing balance of each ledger account is extracted (the side with the larger total). This is the most common method in ACCA FA and ICAP CAF-1 papers.

#### Total-and-Balance Method

Both totals and balances are shown side by side — useful for audit working papers.

#### Errors Revealed and Concealed

| Disclosed by disagreement | NOT disclosed (TB still balances) |
|---|---|
| Casting (adding) errors | Error of principle |
| Posting to the wrong side | Complete omission of a transaction |
| Partial omission of a double-entry | Original (prime) entry errors |
| Transposition in ledger posting | Compensating errors |

#### The Suspense Account

When the trial balance does not balance, the difference is posted to a **suspense account** so the document can be completed. The suspense balance is cleared once the underlying error is identified and corrected via a journal entry.

> **Worked relationship:** Suspense Account balance = |Total Debits − Total Credits|

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Adjusted and Post-Closing Variants

Three versions of the trial balance appear across the accounting cycle:

1. **Unadjusted TB** — extracted after nominal and personal accounts are balanced but **before** year-end adjustments (accruals, prepayments, depreciation, bad debts).
2. **Adjusted TB** — incorporates adjusting entries and forms the **direct basis** for drafting the statement of profit or loss and statement of financial position under IFRS for SMEs or full IFRS.
3. **Post-closing TB** — prepared after closing entries transfer revenue and expense balances to capital; it contains only **permanent (real) accounts**: assets, liabilities, and equity.

#### Common Mistakes in ACCA FA / ICAP CAF-1

- Recording **opening balances as current-period transactions** — they are not part of this period's TB.
- Confusing **discounts allowed** (expense, debit balance) with **discounts received** (income, credit balance).
- Forgetting **contra entries** such as sales returns posted to the purchases returns account.
- Treating the suspense account as an asset or liability rather than a temporary clearing balance.
- Assuming that because IAS 1 does not require a TB to be published, the working paper itself is optional for exam questions — it is mandatory for FA/FR computation tasks.

#### Worked Example (Mechanism)

Suppose total debits in an unadjusted TB are PKR 1,250,000 and total credits are PKR 1,247,800. The PKR 2,200 difference is placed on the **credit side** of the suspense account (since credits are short). Once a PKR 2,200 sales invoice posted to the debit of the wrong customer is corrected, suspense is cleared by debiting suspense and crediting the corrected receivable.

> **Strategy note:** Trial balance questions typically carry **2–4 marks** in ACCA FA and **5–8 marks** in ICAP CAF-1. Allocate roughly **1.8 minutes per mark** and always show the suspense working — even when it nets to zero.

#### Practice Prompts

1. From a given list of ledger balances (some debit, some credit, with one omission of PKR 4,500), prepare a trial balance and identify the suspense entry required.
2. Explain why an error of principle does **not** cause the trial balance to disagree, citing one numerical illustration involving purchases of machinery debited to the purchases account.
