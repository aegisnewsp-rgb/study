# Pakistan's Political System

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Pakistan operates as a **federal parliamentary democratic republic** under the **Constitution of 1973**, which declares the country an **Islamic Republic**. Power flows from a **bicameral legislature (Majlis-e-Shoora)** through the **Prime Minister** (head of government, leader of the National Assembly majority) to the **President** (ceremonial head of state, indirectly elected by an Electoral College).

- **National Assembly**: 342 seats total — 272 general (FPTP, direct vote) + 60 women + 10 non-Muslim minorities (proportional, party-list).
- **Senate**: 96 members elected by provincial assemblies via proportional representation, giving **22 seats per province** plus 8 from former FATA/GB and 4 from Islamabad.
- **Federalism**: Powers split via the Federal, Concurrent, and Provincial Lists; the **18th Amendment (2010)** abolished the President's dissolution power and devolved many subjects to provinces.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Constitutional Basis and Structure

The **Constitution of Pakistan, 1973** is the supreme law. Article 1 defines the territories; Article 6 forbids subversion. Pakistan is structured on three pillars — **executive, legislature, judiciary** — with a fourth pillar of constitutional bodies such as the **Election Commission of Pakistan (Article 218)** and the **Federal Shariat Court (Articles 203-A to 203-J)**.

#### Executive and Legislative Roles

The **Prime Minister**, elected by National Assembly members, leads the Cabinet and commands the confidence of the lower house. The **President**, elected by an Electoral College (both houses of Parliament + four provincial assemblies) through proportional representation, acts largely on PM advice after the **18th Amendment**. Federal ministries answer to the PM; provinces have their own **Governor** (federal nominee) and **Chief Minister** (provincial assembly leader).

#### Federalism and Power Distribution

The Sixth Schedule divides legislative subjects into three lists: Federal (defence, foreign affairs, currency), Concurrent (criminal law, education), and Provincial (local government, agriculture). The **NFC Award** governs fiscal transfers, with provinces receiving a formula-based share of federal divisible pool taxes.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases, Civil-Military Relations, and Practice Prompts

Pakistan's system has oscillated between civilian and military rule. **Direct military governments** ruled from 1958–1969, 1977–1988, and 1999–2008. The **8th Amendment (1985)** entrenched presidential powers, which the **18th Amendment (2010)** later rolled back, restoring parliamentary supremacy. **Article 232** permits emergency declarations; **Article 224** authorises **caretaker governments** between assemblies.

Political parties register under the **Political Parties Act 2002** with the ECP; major federal players include **PTI, PML-N, PPP**, alongside strong provincial parties such as **MQM, JUIF, ANP, BAP, BNM**. The **judiciary** is independent: the **Supreme Court** sits at the apex (1 Chief Justice + puisne judges), followed by **High Courts** in each province and subordinate district/sessions courts.

For PPSC, expect 1–2 MCQs per paper on this topic. Focus on **article numbers** (6, 48, 58, 69, 218, 224, 232, 203-A), **seat counts** (342 / 96 / 22), and **post-18th Amendment** changes.
