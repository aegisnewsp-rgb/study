# Cardiovascular System

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The **cardiovascular system** is a closed circulatory loop: a four-chambered muscular pump (heart) plus arteries, capillaries, and veins transporting blood, gases, hormones, and wastes. NEET PG Anatomy tests structural organisation — chambers, valves, conducting system, coronary supply, pericardium, and fetal shunts.

- **Cardiac Output (CO)** = Stroke Volume × Heart Rate (L/min)
- **Ejection Fraction (EF)** = (SV / EDV) × 100; normal ≈ 55–70 %
- **Stroke Volume** = EDV − ESV (≈ 70 mL at rest)
- **MAP** = Diastolic BP + ⅓ (Systolic − Diastolic)

| Chamber | Receives | Pumps to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Right atrium | SVC, IVC, coronary sinus | Right ventricle (via tricuspid) |
| Left atrium | 4 pulmonary veins | Left ventricle (via mitral) |
| Right ventricle | Right atrium | Pulmonary trunk |
| Left ventricle | Left atrium | Aorta |

**Conducting order:** SA node → AV node → Bundle of His → bundle branches → **Purkinje fibres**.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Chambers and Septa

The heart sits in the middle mediastinum enclosed by the **pericardium** (fibrous outer + serous inner with parietal and visceral/epicardial leaves). Right atrium receives the **SVC, IVC**, and **coronary sinus**; the left atrium admits four pulmonary veins. The **interatrial septum** carries the fossa ovalis (remnant of the foramen ovale). The **interventricular septum** has a small membranous part (just below the aortic valve — common site of VSD) and a thick muscular part.

#### Valves

Atrioventricular valves (**tricuspid** right, **mitral/bicuspid** left) are anchored by **chordae tendineae** to **papillary muscles**, preventing prolapse during ventricular systole. Semilunar valves (**pulmonary, aortic**) have no chordae — their cusps close by back-pressure.

#### Conducting System

The **SA node** (pacemaker, in the right atrial wall near the SVC opening) initiates each beat. The impulse slows at the **AV node** (≈ 0.1 s delay for ventricular filling), then travels down the **Bundle of His** which divides into right and left bundle branches ramifying as **Purkinje fibres** beneath the endocardium. Only SA and AV nodes are true nodal tissue; Purkinje cells are modified cardiac muscle, not nerves.

#### Coronary Circulation

The **left coronary artery** branches into LAD (anterior 2/3 septum, anterior LV) and circumflex (left atrium, lateral LV). The **right coronary artery** supplies the right atrium, right ventricle, posterior 1/3 septum, and — in ~70–80 % (right-dominant) — the SA and AV nodes via the posterior interventricular artery.

| Valve | Anatomical site | Auscultation area |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Aortic | Left, behind sternum | Right 2nd intercostal space |
| Pulmonary | Anterior, left of sternum | Left 2nd intercostal space |
| Tricuspid | Right, lower sternal border | Lower left sternal border (5th ICS) |
| Mitral | Left, posterior | Apex (5th ICS, mid-clavicular line) |

- **Sympathetic supply:** T1–T5 accelerator nerves → ↑ HR, contractility.
- **Parasympathetic:** vagus (CN X) → ↓ SA node firing.
- **Referred pain:** T1–T5 dermatomes — explains left-arm pain in MI.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Fetal Circulation and Shunts

In utero, the placenta handles gas exchange, so the fetal lung is bypassed through three shunts: **foramen ovale** (right atrium → left atrium across the interatrial septum), **ductus arteriosus** (pulmonary trunk → aortic arch, distal to left subclavian), and **ductus venosus** (umbilical vein → IVC, bypassing the liver). Functional closure occurs within hours to days of birth; anatomical fibrosis yields the **ligamentum teres, ligamentum arteriosum**, and **ligamentum venosum**.

#### Pericardial Sinuses

The **transverse pericardial sinus** lies between the arterial outflow (aorta + pulmonary trunk) anteriorly and the atria/SVC posteriorly — surgeons pass a clamp through it to occlude both great vessels during coronary surgery. The **oblique sinus** sits behind the left atrium.

#### Common Mistakes

- Mixing auscultation site with anatomical position (aortic valve sits left but is heard at the right 2nd ICS).
- Assuming Purkinje fibres are nerves — they are modified cardiomyocytes.
- Forgetting that chordae tendineae are exclusive to AV valves.
- Stating ductus arteriosus connects aorta to pulmonary vein — it links pulmonary trunk to aortic arch.

#### Worked Example

A resting adult has HR = 72 bpm, EDV = 120 mL, ESV = 50 mL.  
SV = 120 − 50 = 70 mL. CO = 70 × 72 = 5040 mL/min ≈ 5 L/min.  
EF = (70 / 120) × 100 ≈ 58 % (normal). MAP at BP 120/80 = 80 + ⅓(40) ≈ 93 mmHg.

#### Practice Prompts

1. A newborn becomes cyanotic on crying; a right-to-left shunt is suspected across the interatrial septum. Which embryonic structure failed to close, and what remnant normally persists?
2. During cardiac surgery, the surgeon passes an index finger behind the aorta and pulmonary trunk to clamp both. Name the sinus entered and the structures forming its anterior and posterior walls.
