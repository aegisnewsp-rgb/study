# Physical Features of India

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

India has **six physiographic divisions**: the Himalayas, the Northern Plains, the Peninsular Plateau, the Indian Desert, the Coastal Plains, and the Islands. The **Himalayas** are young fold mountains formed by the collision of the Indian Plate with the Eurasian Plate, divided into Greater (Himadri), Lesser (Himachal), and Shiwaliks. The **Northern Plains** are alluvial deposits of the Indus–Ganga–Brahmaputra river system, carrying **Khadar** (new alluvium) and **Bhangar** (old alluvium with kankar).

- **Highest peak in India (territory)**: Kangchenjunga, **8,598 m** (Sikkim); K2 at 8,611 m lies in Pakistan-administered Kashmir.
- **Andaman & Nicobar** = volcanic arc islands (Bay of Bengal); **Lakshadweep** = coral atolls (Arabian Sea).
- CTET Paper-2 tests 1–2 MCQs on divisions, peaks, Khadar/Bhangar, and key passes.

#### The Six Physiographic Divisions

| # | Division | Key Feature | Example / Location |
|---|----------|-------------|--------------------|
| 1 | **Himalayas** | Young fold mountains, still rising ~1 cm/year | Himadri, Himachal, Shiwaliks |
| 2 | **Northern Plains** | Alluvial, flat, fertile | Punjab, UP, Bihar, Assam plains |
| 3 | **Peninsular Plateau** | Oldest landmass (Gondwana), tabular | Deccan Plateau, Central Highlands |
| 4 | **Indian Desert** | Arid, <25 cm rainfall | Thar (western Rajasthan, parts of Gujarat) |
| 5 | **Coastal Plains** | Narrow strips east and west | Konkan, Malabar, Coromandel, Northern Circars |
| 6 | **Islands** | Two groups, different origins | Andaman & Nicobar; Lakshadweep |

#### Alluvial Soil and River Plains

The Indo-Gangetic trough was filled by deposits from the Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra, and tributaries, producing two distinct layers. **Khadar** is the new, fertile flood-plain alluvium renewed almost every year; **Bhangar** is the older terrace alluvium above the flood level, often laced with **kankar** (calcareous nodules) and less fertile. This distinction appears frequently as a direct MCQ.

> **Exam tip:** If a question mentions "new alluvium near the river, renewed by floods" — pick **Khadar**. If it says "older terrace with kankar" — pick **Bhangar**.

#### The Peninsular Plateau and Its Edges

The plateau is bounded by the **Western Ghats** (continuous, higher, ~900–1600 m) and the **Eastern Ghats** (discontinuous, broken by rivers like Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri). It holds rich **black soil (regur)** suited to cotton and large reserves of iron, coal, and manganese.

#### Common Confusions to Avoid

- Western Ghats are continuous; Eastern Ghats are discontinuous — examiners exploit this.
- Lakshadweep is **coral**, not volcanic; Andaman & Nicobar is **volcanic/arc**, not coral.
- The Peninsular Plateau is the **oldest** landmass; the Himalayas are the **youngest**.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Mountain Passes: Linking Trade and Strategy

Passes through the Himalayas are not just geographic features — they are gateways that historically controlled Central Asian trade and today hold military value. CTET often pairs a pass with its state and the river or border it connects.

| Pass | State / Region | Connects / Significance |
|------|----------------|-------------------------|
| Shipki La | Himachal Pradesh | Sutlej gorge, India–Tibet route |
| Nathu La | Sikkim | India–China, ancient Silk Route link |
| Bom Di La | Arunachal Pradesh | India–Tibet, highest pass in this region |
| Khardung La | Ladakh | Gateway to Siachen and Nubra valley |
| Pir Panjal | Jammu & Kashmir | Links Kashmir valley with outer hills |
| Karakoram | Ladakh | Links Ladakh with Central Asia |

#### Western vs Eastern Ghats: A Closer Look

The Western Ghats form a **continuous barrier** running parallel to the Arabian Sea, blocking southwest monsoon winds and creating heavy rainfall on the windward side. The Eastern Ghats run parallel to the Bay of Bengal but are **broken** into disconnected hill ranges by east-flowing rivers. Because the Western Ghats intercept moisture, the **Deccan Plateau's eastern side is drier** than its western side — a classic assertion-reason trap.

#### Origin of the Two Island Groups

**Lakshadweep** sits on the **Laccadive-Chagos submarine ridge** and consists of coral atolls built on submarine volcanic peaks. **Andaman & Nicobar** form the emergent peaks of a volcanic arc associated with the subduction zone near the Arakan Yoma (Myanmar) — making them geologically and strategically distinct.

> **Strategy note (CTET Paper-2):** Of the ~60 Social Studies MCQs, **1–2** come from Physical Features of India. High-yield items are: (a) matching divisions with features, (b) Khadar vs Bhangar, (c) identifying passes, (d) Western vs Eastern Ghats, (e) island origin. Aim to solve each in under 60 seconds.

#### Practice Prompts

1. A student claims K2 (8,611 m) is India's highest peak. Identify the error and state the correct peak within Indian territory, with its elevation.
2. Match each — Shipki La, Nathu La, Bom Di La, Khardung La — to its correct state and explain why passes matter strategically beyond geography.
