# Cardiovascular System

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The cardiovascular system is a **closed circulatory network** comprising a four-chambered muscular pump (heart) and a branching vascular tree (arteries → arterioles → capillaries → venules → veins). It delivers oxygen, nutrients, hormones, and immune cells, while removing CO₂ and metabolic waste.

- **Heart chambers:** 2 atria (receiving) + 2 ventricles (pumping), separated by the **interatrial** and **interventricular septa**.
- **Valves:** AV valves (tricuspid right, mitral/bicuspid left) anchored by **chordae tendineae** to papillary muscles; semilunar valves (aortic, pulmonary) have no chordae.
- **Conducting system:** SA node → AV node → Bundle of His → bundle branches → **Purkinje fibres**.
- **Fetal shunts:** foramen ovale, ductus arteriosus, ductus venosus — all close functionally after birth.
- **High-yield formulas:** CO = SV × HR; SV = EDV − ESV; EF = (SV/EDV) × 100; MAP = DBP + ⅓(SBP − DBP).

| Valve | Type | Auscultation site |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Aortic | Semilunar | Right 2nd ICS, sternal border |
| Pulmonary | Semilunar | Left 2nd ICS, sternal border |
| Tricuspid | AV | Lower left sternal border (5th ICS) |
| Mitral | AV | Apex, 5th ICS mid-clavicular line |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Chambers and septa
The right atrium receives the SVC, IVC, and coronary sinus. The left atrium has **four pulmonary vein openings** (two on each side). The ventricles are separated by an interventricular septum divided into a small upper **membranous part** (just below the aortic valve — common site of VSDs) and a larger lower **muscular part**.

#### Valves and supporting apparatus
The AV valves (tricuspid — 3 cusps; mitral — 2 cusps) are anchored to papillary muscles via chordae tendineae, preventing prolapse during systole. Semilunar valves (aortic, pulmonary) have **three semilunar cusps with no chordae**; their closure produces the second heart sound. The right atrium contains the **fossa ovalis** — the embryological remnant of the foramen ovale.

#### Conducting system and cardiac cycle
The SA node in the right atrial wall near the SVC opening sets the rate (~70 bpm). The AV node delays the impulse by ~0.1 s to allow ventricular filling, then conducts via the Bundle of His → right and left bundle branches → Purkinje fibres.

> **Mnemonic for conduction order:** "**S**almon **A**re **A**bsolutely **D**elicious **B**etween **H**is **B**ranches **P**rotein" → SA → AV → Bundle of His → Bundle branches → Purkinje.

Cycle duration (s) = 60 ÷ HR. At 75 bpm, one cycle ≈ 0.8 s.

#### Coronary circulation
- **Left coronary artery (LCA):** divides into LAD (anterior 2/3 of septum, anterior LV) and circumflex (left atrium, lateral/posterior LV).
- **Right coronary artery (RCA):** supplies RA, RV, posterior 1/3 of septum, and (in ~70–80% of people — "right-dominant") the SA and AV nodes.

| Artery | Main territory | Nodal supply |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LAD | Anterior LV, anterior 2/3 septum | — |
| Circumflex | Lateral/posterior LV, LA | — |
| RCA | RA, RV, posterior 1/3 septum | SA (60%), AV (80%) |

#### Pericardium and applied surface anatomy
The fibrous pericardium is tough and outer; the serous layer has parietal and visceral (epicardium) parts. The **transverse pericardial sinus** lies behind the aorta and pulmonary trunk — surgeons clamp both great vessels through it.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Pericardial sinuses and surgical anatomy
- **Transverse sinus:** between aorta/pulmonary trunk anteriorly and SVC/atria posteriorly — landmark for clamping during cardiopulmonary bypass.
- **Oblique sinus:** posterior to the left atrium, bounded by pulmonary veins — recess of pericardial cavity.

#### Fetal circulation and shunt closure
Oxygenated placental blood enters via the umbilical vein, bypasses the liver through the **ductus venosus** into the IVC, and enters the right atrium. Most is shunted via the **foramen ovale** to the left atrium; the rest reaches the aorta via the **ductus arteriosus** (pulmonary trunk → aortic arch).

| Shunt | Connects | Closes after birth as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Foramen ovale | RA → LA (atrial) | Fossa ovalis (anatomical) |
| Ductus arteriosus | Pulmonary trunk → aortic arch | Ligamentum arteriosum |
| Ductus venosus | Umbilical vein → IVC | Ligamentum venosum |

> **Common trap:** ductus arteriosus shunts right-to-left *in utero* (bypassing non-functional lungs) but left-to-right *after birth* if it remains patent (PDA), causing machinery-like murmur.

#### Nerve supply and referred pain
Sympathetic fibres from **T1–T5** (cervical and upper thoracic sympathetic chain) accelerate the heart; vagus (CN X) slows SA node firing. Cardiac pain fibres travel via the same T1–T5 dorsal roots — explaining **referred pain to the left arm and jaw in myocardial infarction**.

#### Edge cases and exam traps
1. **LCA dominance confusion:** do not assume LAD supplies nodal tissue — RCA does in ~70–80%.
2. **Valve location vs auscultation site:** the aortic valve is left-sided anatomically but heard at the **right** 2nd intercostal space.
3. **Purkinje fibres are modified myocytes**, not nerves — only SA and AV nodes are true nodal tissue.
4. **Epicardium = visceral serous pericardium = outermost heart wall layer**, distinct from the fibrous pericardium.

#### Worked example
At HR = 70 bpm with EDV = 120 mL and ESV = 50 mL:
- SV = 120 − 50 = 70 mL
- CO = 70 × 70 = 4 900 mL/min ≈ 4.9 L/min
- EF = (70/120) × 100 ≈ **58%** (normal: 55–70%)

#### Practice prompts
1. Identify the structure crossed by a surgeon passing a finger behind the pulmonary trunk and aorta — and name the two great vessels it isolates.
2. A newborn with cyanosis improving on crying likely has transposition; in a PDA, why does indomethacin help, and what does it mechanistically target?
