# Rotational Motion

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

- **Rotational motion** describes a rigid body turning about a fixed axis; every particle traces a circle of radius *r* with the same angular velocity **ω** (rad/s) and angular acceleration **α** (rad/s²).
- The rotational analogue of Newton's second law: **τ_net = I α**, where **τ** is torque (N·m) and **I** is the **moment of inertia** (kg·m²) — the rotational measure of mass.
- **Angular momentum** **L = I ω** is conserved when the net external torque about an axis is zero (skater pulling in arms, spinning top in frictionless pivot).
- Rotational kinetic energy is **K_rot = ½ I ω²**; for a body in **pure rolling**, total kinetic energy is **K = ½ M v_cm² + ½ I_cm ω²**, with the no-slip condition **v_cm = R ω**.
- Key MI values to memorise: solid sphere **⅖ MR²**, hollow sphere **⅔ MR²**, solid cylinder/disc **½ MR²**, ring **MR²**, thin rod about centre **ML²/12**.
- JEE Main fact: expect **1–2 questions (≈3% weightage)**, often a numerical on moment of inertia using the parallel-axis theorem or a torque-angular acceleration problem.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Torque and Angular Momentum
**Torque** about a point is defined as **τ = r × F**, with magnitude τ = r F sin θ; it is the rotational counterpart of force. **Angular momentum** about the same point is **L = r × p = I ω** for a rigid body rotating about a symmetry axis. Newton's second law extends to rotation as **τ_net = dL/dt = I α**, valid when I is constant.

#### Moment of Inertia
The moment of inertia of a system of point masses is **I = Σ mᵢ rᵢ²**, and for a continuous body it is obtained by integration. It depends on the distribution of mass about the chosen axis, not just total mass. The **radius of gyration k** is defined through **I = M k²**, giving the equivalent perpendicular distance at which the entire mass could be concentrated.

#### Two Fundamental Theorems

| Theorem | Formula | When to apply |
|---|---|---|
| **Parallel-axis** | I = I_cm + M d² | Shifting a known I_cm to a parallel axis at distance d |
| **Perpendicular-axis** | I_z = I_x + I_y | Planar laminae (2-D) only; never 3-D solids |

The perpendicular-axis theorem fails for 3-D bodies because I_z for a solid cylinder is **½ MR²**, not MR² + ½ MR².

#### Rolling Without Slipping
Pure rolling joins translation of the centre of mass and rotation about it through the constraint **v_cm = R ω** and **a_cm = R α**. Total kinetic energy is **K = ½ M v_cm² + ½ I_cm ω²**. The friction in pure rolling is *static* and does no work — a frequent conceptual trap.

#### Standard Bodies — MI About Symmetry Axis

| Body | I (about symmetry axis) |
|---|---|
| Solid sphere | (2/5) M R² |
| Hollow sphere (thin) | (2/3) M R² |
| Solid cylinder / disc | (1/2) M R² |
| Ring / thin hollow cylinder | M R² |
| Thin rod, through centre, ⟂ to length | M L² / 12 |
| Thin rod, through end | M L² / 3 |

#### Static Equilibrium
A rigid body at rest requires both **ΣF = 0** and **Στ = 0** about *any* point. The choice of pivot is arbitrary because torque balance is independent of the reference point when forces sum to zero.

#### Typical JEE Main Question Types
- Compute I of a composite body by adding part-wise moments about a common axis.
- Apply τ = I α to find angular acceleration of a pulley / rod with mass.
- Angular momentum conservation in a person–turntable or two-disc collision problem.
- Rolling body on an incline: equate loss in PE **M g h** to **K_total**; result for solid sphere is **a = (5/7) g sin θ**.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Subtleties
- **Sign convention for torque**: use **τ = r × F** consistently; choosing an axis and projecting forces that produce positive vs. negative angular acceleration must follow the right-hand rule. A force whose line of action passes through the pivot contributes zero torque — common in door–hinge problems where the hinge reaction drops out.
- **Variable moment of inertia**: when I changes (e.g., a figure skater pulling arms in), τ_net = dL/dt still holds, but τ = I α is no longer valid; use **L = constant** for conservation cases.
- **Rolling friction vs. kinetic friction**: a wheel rolling on a rough surface with slipping has *kinetic* friction doing negative work; once slipping ceases, only static friction remains and does no work. The transition point is often asked.
- **Angular impulse**: **∫ τ dt = ΔL** — useful for impulsive collisions where forces act over short times but torques are large.

#### Connections to Adjacent Topics
- **Work–energy theorem for rotation**: **W = ∫ τ dθ = Δ(½ I ω²)**, mirroring W = ∫ F dx = Δ(½ m v²) in linear motion.
- **Angular SHM**: a physical pendulum obeys **d²θ/dt² = −(M g d / I) θ** where d is the distance from pivot to centre of mass, giving ω² = M g d / I.
- **Toppling vs. sliding**: a block on a surface topples when the line of action of weight falls outside the base; threshold condition involves the moment of inertia and base width.

#### Common Mistakes
1. **Mixing linear and rotational KE**: a rolling solid sphere of mass M and speed v has K = ½ M v² + ½ (⅖ M R²)(v/R)² = **(7/10) M v²**, not ½ M v².
2. **Perpendicular-axis misapplication**: writing I_z = I_x + I_y for a 3-D solid leads to wrong answers (it is only for laminar shapes).
3. **Ignoring the hinge reaction's lever arm**: for a rod hinged at one end, the hinge force generally has zero torque *about the hinge* — but its value is found from ΣF, not Στ.
4. **Treating angular momentum as a scalar when it isn't**: L is a vector along the rotation axis; collisions can change its *direction* (gyroscope precession).

#### Worked Micro-Example
A solid cylinder (M = 4 kg, R = 0.3 m) rolls without slipping down a 5 m incline of angle 30°. Find its speed at the bottom.
- Energy balance: M g h = ½ M v² + ½ I ω² = ½ M v² + ½ (½ M R²)(v/R)² = **(3/4) M v²**.
- h = 5 sin 30° = 2.5 m → v² = (4/3) g h = (4/3)(9.8)(2.5) = 32.67 → **v ≈ 5.71 m/s**.

#### Practice Prompts
1. Two discs (M, 2M; radii R, R/2) rotating independently at ω, 2ω are suddenly coupled to share a common axis. Find the common angular velocity using L conservation.
2. A uniform rod of length L hinged at one end is released from horizontal. Using τ = I α about the hinge, find α just after release and the hinge force at that instant.
