# Speeches and Article Writing

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Speeches and Article Writing are two of the four compulsory **essay-type options** in WAEC WASSCE English Language Paper 1 (Section B), tested through **Question 4 (Speech)** and **Question 5 (Article)**. A speech is delivered live to a seated audience and uses direct address; an article is written for a newspaper readership. Each option attracts about **12–13 marks** under the WAEC Essay scheme (Content 24, Organisation 15, Expression 11, shared across one chosen question).

- **Speech cues:** "as the head boy/girl", "addressing your school", "delivering a welcome address".
- **Article cues:** "as a newspaper article", "for publication in a national daily".
- **Target length:** around **450–550 words**, equivalent to one legible handwritten page.
- **Marks lost fastest:** wrong format, missing salutation, no paragraph breaks, ignoring the given role.

| Feature | Speech | Article |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Salutation | "Mr Chairman, distinguished guests…" | None — supply a title |
| Voice | First-person plural "we/us" | Detached or columnist voice |
| Closing device | Slogan + thank-you | Recommendation or verdict |
| Register | Persuasive, oral | Informative/analytical, written |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Identifying the Required Format

The WAEC question stem gives explicit cues. Phrases like *"deliver a speech as the Senior Prefect"* or *"addressing the graduating set"* signal **Question 4**, demanding a speech. Phrases like *"write an article suitable for publication in The Guardian"* signal **Question 5**, demanding a feature article. Mixing the two formats — opening an article with *"Dear Editor"* or ending a speech with a recommendation paragraph — is the single most penalised error.

#### Anatomy of a WAEC Speech

A speech follows a fixed rhetorical skeleton. Begin with a **salutation** naming the audience (chairman, principal, guests, colleagues), then state the **occasion and purpose** in the introduction. The body should carry **three to four points**, each with rhetorical devices such as **repetition, rhetorical questions, parallelism, and emotive appeals**. Close with a memorable **slogan or call to action**, and end with *"Thank you for listening."*

#### Anatomy of a WAEC Article

An article opens with a **catchy title** (e.g. *The Menace of Exam Malpractice*), followed by an **introductory paragraph** that frames the issue. Subsequent paragraphs each develop **one main idea with a topic sentence**, supported by examples, statistics, or quoted authority. The final paragraph offers a **recommendation, prediction, or reflective verdict** consistent with the title's stance.

#### The WAEC Marking Lens

| Criterion | Marks | What the examiner scans for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Content | 24 | Relevance, depth, and balanced treatment of the topic |
| Organisation | 15 | Paragraphing, logical flow, intro–body–conclusion |
| Expression & Mechanics | 11 | Grammar, spelling, punctuation, appropriate vocabulary |

- Use **register matching** the audience (formal for school assembly, semi-formal for a youth column).
- Keep a **consistent tone** — persuasive for speeches, analytical or persuasive for articles.
- Maintain the stipulated **role**: writing as yourself when told to write as the Head Boy breaks the voice contract.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Examiner Traps

WAEC rewards candidates who obey the **role, audience, and purpose triangle**. A question asking you to *"address new students on the dangers of drug abuse"* requires three identifications: you are the **Head Boy**, the audience is **freshers**, and the purpose is to **warn and persuade**. Slip on any one and the answer reads as a generic essay. Another trap is the **argumentative article** that demands a balanced view — WAEC penalises one-sided rants unless the question explicitly says "support" or "against".

#### Connecting to Adjacent Skills

Speech writing overlaps with **debate and oratory** tested in oral English, while article writing feeds into **comprehension summary and letter-to-the-editor** tasks. The paragraphing discipline used here is identical to the organisation scheme in **Narrative/Descriptive essays (Question 7)**.

#### Common Mistakes to Pre-empt

1. Writing **"Dear Sir"** inside an article body — that belongs to Question 6 (Letter Writing).
2. Omitting the **slogan line** in a speech, which leaves the ending flat and forfeits rhetorical marks.
3. Producing **under 400 words** so ideas appear skeletal and Content marks collapse below 12.
4. Padding with **repetition of the same point in different words**, which Organisation marks down.

#### Worked Micro-Plan: Question 5 — *"Write an article for publication in a national newspaper on the rising cost of living and its effects on students."*

| Section | What to write |
| --- | --- |
| Title | *When Textbooks Become a Luxury* |
| Para 1 | Hook — a student skipping meals to buy handouts |
| Para 2 | Causes — fuel price hikes, naira devaluation, school fees |
| Para 3 | Effects — dropout risk, poor concentration, mental strain |
| Para 4 | Government and school-level recommendations |
| Closing | Forward-looking verdict: education must not become a casualty |

#### Practice Prompts

1. As the **Head Girl**, deliver a speech at your school's prize-giving day urging students to embrace reading culture.
2. Write an article titled *"Social Media: A Blessing or a Curse?"* for publication in your school magazine.

Target **45 minutes** per essay in the exam hall — 5 minutes planning, 35 minutes writing, 5 minutes proofreading for spelling and paragraph breaks.
