# Demonstrate mathematical reasoning (91947) — NCEA Level 1 Mathematics and Statistics Notes

Achievement Standard 91947 is the second of two NCEA Level 1 Mathematics and Statistics externals and the sister standard to 91946. It carries 5 credits and is assessed by an end-of-year NZQA examination in November/December. Unlike 91946 (which is "interpret and apply in context"), 91947 is "demonstrate mathematical reasoning" — the emphasis is on showing the working, justifying each step, and using relational or extended abstract thinking. 91947 is where Merit and Excellence grades are most often lost or won.

> Verify the live assessment specification and any in-year changes on https://www.nzqa.govt.nz/nqfdocs/ncea-resource/specifications/2026/91947-spc-2026.pdf before planning revision around the figures below.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **5 credits**, external NZQA examination at the end of Year 11.
- Emphasis on **mathematical reasoning** — showing every step, justifying each decision.
- Questions cover **number, algebra, geometry, measurement, statistics, probability** with reasoning-led exposition.
- The exam runs for **three hours** with multiple questions.
- **Merit** (relational thinking) requires choosing methods that connect concepts (e.g., using a pattern in number to simplify an algebra problem).
- **Excellence** (extended abstract thinking) requires generalising or reflecting on why the result holds.

#### Examiner traps

- Writing only the final answer — NZQA markers need to see the reasoning that connects the start to the end.
- Skipping the justification of a method choice — Merit and Excellence are about method, not just answer.
- Confusing "explain" with "show" — "explain" requires a sentence about why, not just the algebraic manipulation.
- Using a calculator on a non-calculator question (some questions are explicitly non-calculator).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Exam structure

The 91947 paper has four or five questions with a total of around 40-50 marks. The questions are reasoning-led: each asks the student to set up a mathematical argument, carry out a multi-step derivation, and justify the conclusion. The exam is **three hours** long and **calculator-active** for the relevant questions (the paper specifies which questions are non-calculator).

#### The four-grade scale in action

NZQA's grade descriptors for 91947 give the clearest signal about what each grade looks like.

| Grade | What it requires |
|---|---|
| Achieved | Solve problems using number, algebra, geometry, measurement, statistics or probability. Show the working. |
| Merit (relational thinking) | Use the methods from Achieved, plus connect concepts (e.g., use the structure of an equation to choose the right method). |
| Excellence (extended abstract thinking) | Use the methods from Merit, plus generalise, reflect, or justify the model in some way. |

#### A worked example: solving a quadratic by completing the square

Question: "Solve x² + 6x + 5 = 0 by completing the square."

**Achieved-grade working:**

x² + 6x + 5 = 0

Add and subtract (6/2)² = 9:

x² + 6x + 9 − 9 + 5 = 0

(x + 3)² − 4 = 0

(x + 3)² = 4

x + 3 = ±2

x = −1 or x = −5

This gets Achieved because it shows the working.

**Merit-grade working:**

Above, plus: "I chose to complete the square because the equation is a quadratic in standard form, and the constant term is small enough that the method produces a small number under the square root. The discriminant check (b² − 4ac = 36 − 20 = 16) confirms two real solutions."

This gets Merit because it connects the method to the structure of the equation and checks the result.

**Excellence-grade working:**

Above, plus: "Completing the square also gives the vertex of the parabola directly: the vertex is at (−3, −4), which is the minimum value of x² + 6x + 5. This connects to the geometric meaning of the quadratic and explains why the equation has two real roots that are symmetric about x = −3."

This gets Excellence because it generalises to the geometric meaning and reflects on why the result holds.

#### Reasoning patterns to practice

- **Algebraic proof.** "Show that (x + 3)² − 4 = (x + 5)(x + 1)." Each step needs an explicit reason (expand the square, factor, group terms).
- **Geometric proof.** "Show that the angles in a triangle sum to 180°." Use parallel lines and alternate angles.
- **Statistical reasoning.** "Explain why the median is a better summary than the mean for this data." Reasons: skewed data, outliers, ordinal vs interval measurement.
- **Probability reasoning.** "Explain why these two events are independent / not independent." Reasons: the result of one event affects or doesn't affect the sample space of the other.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### The single biggest Merit-to-Excellence lever

The most common Merit-to-Excellence gap is the absence of a single sentence that reflects on the result. Adding one sentence — "this connects to the geometric meaning of …" or "this would not have worked if the data had been …" — moves a paper from Merit to Excellence. Practice writing that one reflective sentence for every problem.

#### Working backwards from the answer

For Excellence practice, work backwards from the answer:

- Why does the discriminant tell us the number of roots? Because it is the part under the square root in the quadratic formula, and a negative value under the square root gives complex roots.
- Why does the IQR measure spread better than the range? Because it ignores outliers.
- Why does probability in a uniform sample space equal the favourable outcomes over total outcomes? Because each outcome is equally likely by assumption, so the probability of any subset is proportional to its size.

Writing the "why" is the move that NZQA's marking schedule rewards.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "I don't need to write the question back." Yes you do — NZQA markers need to see what your reasoning refers to.
- "Merit is just Achieved with more steps." No — Merit is Achieved with method connection.
- "Excellence is just Merit with more pages." No — Excellence is Merit with reflection.
- "I can leave the final step implicit." No — the final step is the only step the marker sees, so it must be visible.
