# Karnataka Medieval History: Kalachakra, Jain/Buddhist Centres, Hoysala, and Vijayanagara

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The empire that lasts longest in this syllabus is Vijayanagara (1336–1646 CE) — four dynasties, 310 years, and the largest South Indian political footprint before the Sultanates finished it off. **Harihara I** and **Bukka I** (Sangama brothers) founded it in 1336 with the spiritual backing of **Vidyaranya** of Sringeri Math; **Krishna Deva Raya** (1509–1529, Tuluva) was its military and literary peak; defeat came at **Rakshasa-Tangadi (Talikota), 1565** under regent Rama Raya against the Deccan Sultanate coalition.

- **Hoysalas** (c. 1026–1343): capital shifted **Sosevur → Belur → Halebidu**; signature = **soapstone (chloritic schist)**, **stellate plan**, **lathe-turned pillars**, emblem = **Sala slaying tiger**.
- **Key temples**: **Chennakeshava, Belur** (1117, Vishnuvardhana) · **Hoysaleswara, Halebidu** (1121, Narasimha I) · **Keshava, Somanathapura** (1268, Soma Dandanayaka).
- **Jain centres**: **Shravanabelagola** (Chavundaraya's 57-ft Bahubali, 983 CE) · **Kambadahalli** · **Mudabidri** · **Karkala** (42-ft Gommateshwara, 1432).
- **Kalachakra** = Buddhist tantric *Wheel of Time* cycle (12-year calendar) — **NOT** the same as **Kalachuri dynasty** of Kalyani (1156–1183, Bijjala).

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** "**S**ala **S**laying **T**iger = **S**oapstone **S**tellate" — Hoysala in one image. For Vijayanagara think "**HB** for victory, **TR** for ruin": Harihara–Bukka founded, Talikota–Rakshasa destroyed.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Working understanding for the regular study window — read once, recall for months.

#### Religious Foundations: Kalachakra, Jain Bastis, and the Buddhist Trail

The **Kalachakra** ("Wheel of Time") is a Vajrayana Buddhist tantric system centred on a 12-year calendrical ritual; in Karnataka its material footprint is the rock-cut caves at **Kanheri**, **Badami** (6th-century Buddhist viharas cut into the same sandstone cliffs the Chalukyas later used for Hindu caves), and the Jain *basti* complexes that grew under Gangas and Hoysalas. **Shravanabelagola** has two Gommateshwara statues often confused in prelims: Chavundaraya's 57-ft monolith of 983 CE versus the 42-ft Karkala statue of 1432 CE commissioned by **Veera Pandya Bhairava**. Both are Jain, both are digambara, both are *ekakuta* — and that is the end of their similarity. **Kambadahalli Panchakuta Basadi**, **Chandragiri Bastis**, and **Lakshmeshwara** round out the Hoysala-era Jain triangle.

#### Hoysala Political Arc

Sala (legendary founder, tiger-hunt origin myth) → **Nripa Kama** → **Vishnuvardhana** (r. 1117, converted from Jainism to Sri Vaishnavism under **Ramanuja**, built Chennakeshava at Belur) → **Narasimha I** (Hoysaleswara, Halebidu, 1121) → **Veera Ballala II** (peak, 1173–1220) → terminal decline under Khalji invasions — **Malik Kafur's Madurai campaign (1311)** and later Bahmani pressure ended the line by 1343.

#### Vijayanagara State and Patronage

Four dynasties: **Sangama** (1336–1485), **Saluva** (1485–1505), **Tuluva** (1505–1570, peak), **Araveedu** (1570–1646, post-Talikota). Administrative vocabulary the examiner will test: **Ashtadiggajas** (Krishna Deva Raya's eight court poets — Peddana, Tenali Ramakrishna foremost), **Amara-Nayaka** system, **Sutradhari** (imperial priest/mentor), **Rajya-pradhani** (chief minister), **Mahanadu** (royal council), land revenue termed **Siddhaya**, irrigation by **anicuts** on the Tungabhadra.

#### Worked Solution: Identifying a "Hoysala" Temple Complex

**Question.** Match the following: (A) Chennakeshava, Belur (B) Hoysaleswara, Halebidu (C) Keshava, Somanathapura — with (i) 1268 CE, Soma Dandanayaka (ii) 1117 CE, Vishnuvardhana (iii) 1121 CE, Narasimha I.

**Working.** Step 1 — fix the patron. Belur is Vishnuvardhana's commemorative Vaishnava foundation post-conversion, so A → (ii). Step 2 — Halebidu is the twin-shiva temple (Hoysaleswara and Santinatha) raised by Narasimha I in 1121, so B → (iii). Step 3 — Somanathapura is the only one of the three built after the Chalukya-Hoysala handover (1268), so C → (i). The match is A-ii, B-iii, C-i. Step 4 — sanity check: only Somanathapura is a *tri-kuta* (three-shrine) on a stellate platform; Belur and Halebidu are ekakuta complexes.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Options often swap *Chennakeshava* and *Hoysaleswara* patrons. Remember: **Chenna = Vishnuvardhana = Vaishnava**; **Hoysaleswara = Narasimha = Shaiva**. Shaiva–Vaishnava swap is the single most common wrong answer in KAS Prelims on this topic.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Mastery tier — the edges, the exceptions, the questions KAS Mains examiners use to break the script-learnt candidate.

#### Edge Cases the Standard Syllabus Glides Past

The **Kalachuri dynasty of Kalyani** (also called Chalukyas of Kalyani's successor branch, 1156–1183 CE under **Bijjala I**) is a *political* dynasty that briefly patronised the Lingayat/Veerashaiva movement after Basavanna. It has nothing to do with **Kalachakra**, which is a tantric Buddhist ritual cycle. KAS Prelims routinely tests this homonym trap as a 2-mark MCQ; reading the question as "Kalachuri → Buddhist" loses the mark.

Vijayanagara's **administrative manuals** — the *Dharma-shastra* commentaries of **Sayana** (Vedartha Prakasha), the *Rayavachakamu* (a Telugu chronicle, not a state document), and the land-grant *shasana* inscriptions — together make Vijayanagara the best-documented pre-colonial South Indian state. The *Amara-Nayaka* tenure system functioned as military-feudal land grants (*manya*, *araniya* classifications); examiners ask whether they were *hereditary* — the answer is conditional: nominally conditional on performance, but de facto hereditary by the 16th century.

#### Worked Micro-Example: Pinning Down the Battle of 1565

A Mains question asks: "Trace the political consequences of the Battle of Talikota (1565) for the Vijayanagara state." The trap is the date and the dynastic attribution. The battle was fought on 25 January 1565 at **Rakshasa-Tangadi** (modern Talikota), commanded by regent **Rama Raya** of the **Araveedu dynasty** against a coalition of Ahmednagar, Bijapur, Golconda, and Bidar Sultanates. Krishna Deva Raya died in 1529 — he neither commanded nor witnessed the defeat. Hampi was sacked in the months after, but the **Araveedu dynasty** continued at **Penukonda**, then **Chandragiri**, then **Vellore** until **Shriranga III** in 1646, when the last emperor was deposed by Mir Jumla II of Golconda and the Bijapur Sultanate.

#### Advanced Traps and Exceptions

1. **Bahmani ≠ Vijayanagara collapse.** The Bahmani Sultanate collapsed in 1527; Vijayanagara persisted until 1646 — do not equate the two end-dates.
2. **The Hoysala "Vesara" label is contested.** Some art historians label Hoysala temples as a *southern Dravida variant*, not true Vesara. KAS answers that quote "Vesara" verbatim pass; answers that defend "Dravida" need at least one footnoted reference.
3. **The Madhva–Vijayanagara patronage link.** Madhvacharya (1238–1317) predates Vijayanagara's founding, but the *Madhva Vaishnavism* school became state-supported only under the Tuluva dynasty — under **Vadiraja** and **Jayatirtha** in the 15th–16th centuries.
4. **Srirangapatna vs. Srirangam confusion.** The Tuluva-era temple patronage extended to **Srirangapatna** (Karnataka) and the *Vijayanagara* nominal capital remained at Hampi until 1565 — do not conflate the island fortress with the empire's political capital.
5. **Krishna Deva Raya's wars**. He fought **Gajapati** of Kalinga (conquered up to the Krishna delta), the **Bahmani Sultanate** for the Raichur doab, and signed a **1510 treaty with the Portuguese** ( Albuquerque) protecting Calicut–Goa spice routes. The treaty is the *only* formal European pact Vijayanagara signed.
6. **Pre-Hoysala Kadamba–Ganga capitals** at **Talakad** and **Banavasi** are often asked as a separate prelims MCQ on "earliest Kannada-speaking political centres" — distinct from medieval Hoysala/Vijayanagara.

> 📌 **Cross-topic Anchor:** The Vijayanagara military-feudal land tenure (Amara-Nayaka) is the same structural ancestor of the later **Nayak / Polygar** system of Madurai and Thanjavur — a question that links "medieval Karnataka" to "Madurai Nayaks" is a KAS favourite in Mains GS-1.
