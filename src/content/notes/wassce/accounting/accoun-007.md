# Company Accounts

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your WASSCE Accounting paper.

Company Accounts covers how **limited liability companies** in Ghana prepare financial statements under the **Companies Act, 2019 (Act 992)** and **IFRS / IFRS for SMEs**. A company has its own legal personality, separate from its shareholders, so the accounting framework differs from sole proprietorships and partnerships.

- **Authorised capital** = number of shares authorised × par (nominal) value in GHS.
- **Issued capital** = number of shares issued × par value; the excess received over par is **share premium**, credited to a non-distributable reserve.
- **Retained earnings (closing)** = Opening retained earnings + Profit after tax − Dividends declared − Transfers to reserves.

Two highest-yield WASSCE question types ask candidates to (i) prepare a **Statement of Profit or Loss** and **Statement of Financial Position** from an adjusted trial balance, and (ii) compute issues of shares, preference dividends, and ordinary share dividends. Always remember that a proposed dividend is an **appropriation of profit**, not a liability until declared.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months of revision.

#### Nature of a Company and Share Capital
A company incorporated under Act 992 has **limited liability**, perpetual succession, and a separate legal personality. Its capital is raised by issuing **shares**, which are either **ordinary** (equity) or **preference** (often cumulative, fixed dividend). Three capital measures must be distinguished carefully:

| Capital measure | Meaning | Formula |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Authorised | Maximum shares the company may issue | Authorised shares × Par value |
| Issued | Shares actually offered to the public | Issued shares × Par value |
| Called-up | Portion of par value shareholders have been asked to pay | Called-up shares × Par value |

#### Issue of Shares and Share Premium
Shares may be issued **at par**, **at a premium**, or (rarely, under Act 992 conditions) **at a discount**. Cash received for a GHS 1 share issued at GHS 1.50 yields GHS 1 to share capital and GHS 0.50 to **share premium account** — a capital reserve that is **not distributable** as dividend.

#### Appropriation of Profit
The bottom of the Statement of Profit or Loss shows how profit after tax is allocated between **transfers to reserves** (general reserve, dividend equalisation), **proposed dividends** (interim and final), and **retained earnings** carried forward. The closing retained earnings balance becomes an equity item in the Statement of Financial Position.

#### Format Requirements under IFRS
- **Statement of Profit or Loss** lists revenue, cost of sales, gross profit, distribution and administrative expenses, finance costs, profit before tax, and tax expense.
- **Statement of Financial Position** is presented in order of liquidity (or permanence) with non-current assets, current assets, equity, non-current liabilities, and current liabilities.
- **Statement of Changes in Equity** reconciles opening and closing balances of share capital, share premium, and retained earnings.
- **Statement of Cash Flows** uses the indirect method to classify cash flows into operating, investing, and financing activities.

#### Common Pitfalls
- Treating share premium as distributable profit.
- Showing proposed dividends as a current liability.
- Adding depreciation to asset cost instead of deducting accumulated depreciation.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Depreciation and Asset Disposal
Depreciation allocates an asset's cost (less residual value) over its useful life. Two methods dominate WASSCE questions:

| Method | Formula | Effect over time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Straight-line | (Cost − Residual) ÷ Useful life | Equal annual charge |
| Reducing balance | Net book value × Rate | Larger charge in early years |

Accumulated depreciation is shown as a deduction from cost in the Statement of Financial Position, never added to asset cost.

#### Provision for Doubtful Debts and Bad Debts
Bad debts written off in the new year are debited to the income statement, not netted against the old provision. The provision account is updated by adding the specific and general provisions for the year and deducting irrecoverable debts previously provided for. A bad debt recovered is credited to the income statement.

#### Preference Share Arithmetic
Preference dividend = Number of preference shares × Par value × Rate. **Cumulative** preference shares accumulate unpaid dividends year by year; arrears must be paid before ordinary shareholders receive anything. **Participating** preference shares also share in extra profits beyond the fixed rate.

#### Worked Micro-Example
A company has 200,000 ordinary shares of GHS 1 each fully paid and 50,000 8% cumulative preference shares of GHS 1 each, with one year's arrears. Profit after tax is GHS 90,000. The board proposes a GHS 0.20 per ordinary share dividend and to transfer GHS 20,000 to general reserve.

- Preference dividend in arrears = 50,000 × GHS 1 × 8% = GHS 4,000.
- Current year preference dividend = GHS 4,000 (total GHS 8,000).
- Ordinary dividend = 200,000 × GHS 0.20 = GHS 40,000.
- Retained earnings c/f = 90,000 − 8,000 − 20,000 − 40,000 = GHS 22,000.

#### Exam Strategy
WASSCE Ghana sets roughly one structured 30-mark question on Company Accounts (about 3% of the syllabus). Marks are awarded for correct headings, vertical layout of the Statement of Profit or Loss, balanced Statement of Financial Position with totals, and proper disclosure of share capital, reserves and proposed dividends. Allocate about 25 minutes and show every workings line for share and dividend arithmetic.

#### Practice Prompts
1. Prepare a Statement of Profit or Loss and Statement of Financial Position from a given adjusted trial balance, including a proposed dividend and transfer to general reserve.
2. From a share issue scenario, compute share capital, share premium, and the bank amount, then update equity in the Statement of Changes in Equity.
