# Weather and Climate

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Weather** is the short-term state of the atmosphere at a specific time and place, while **climate** is the long-term average of weather conditions, typically over **30 years** as defined by the World Meteorological Organization (WMO). The six elements of weather are temperature, atmospheric pressure, wind, humidity, precipitation (rainfall), and cloud cover.

The must-use formula for humidity is **Relative Humidity**, RH = (actual vapour pressure ÷ saturation vapour pressure) × 100%. The daily **mean temperature** is T_mean = (T_max + T_min) ÷ 2.

- NCEE tests the **weather vs. climate** distinction almost every year.
- Nigeria's climate runs from **tropical rainforest** (south) to **Sahel** (north).
- The **ITCZ** (Inter-Tropical Convergence Zone) migrates with the overhead sun, controlling the wet and dry seasons.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Elements of Weather

Weather is measured using six parameters: temperature (°C), atmospheric pressure (hPa), wind speed and direction (m/s, compass points), humidity (%), precipitation (mm), and cloud cover (oktas). Each parameter is read from a specific instrument such as a thermometer, barometer, anemometer, wind vane, hygrometer, and rain gauge.

#### Weather vs. Climate

| Aspect | Weather | Climate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Time scale | Minutes to days | 30 years (WMO standard) |
| Variability | High, changes hourly | Stable, slowly varying |
| Example | "It rained 25 mm in Lagos today" | "Lagos averages 1500 mm rainfall per year" |
| Study field | Meteorology | Climatology |

#### Factors Affecting Climate of Nigeria

| Factor | Effect on Nigeria |
| --- | --- |
| Latitude | Closer to the Equator means higher temperatures in the south |
| Altitude | Jos Plateau (≈1200 m) is cooler than lowland coastal areas |
| Distance from sea | Coastal south is wetter; inland north is drier |
| Ocean currents | Warm Guinea Current brings rainfall to the south |
| Prevailing winds | SW Monsoon brings rain (May–Oct); NE Harmattan brings dry dust (Nov–March) |

#### Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Writing that climate is the average of one year instead of **30 years**.
- Calling Harmattan a "cold wind" only; it is primarily **dry and dusty**.
- Saying the Sun is directly overhead at the Equator all year; it migrates between the Tropics of Cancer and Capricorn.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### The Water Cycle and Atmospheric Processes

The water cycle continuously moves water through evaporation (from oceans, lakes, rivers), transpiration (from plants), condensation (forming clouds), precipitation (rain, snow), runoff, and infiltration. Evaporation absorbs latent heat from the surface, cooling it, while condensation releases that heat into the atmosphere. **Relative humidity** tells us how close the air is to saturation: RH = 100% means fog or rain is likely; RH below 40% during Harmattan explains the dry, cracked lips and dusty skin felt in northern Nigeria between November and March.

#### The ITCZ and Nigeria's Seasons

The **Inter-Tropical Convergence Zone** is the belt of low pressure where the NE and SE trade winds meet near the Equator. It migrates seasonally following the overhead sun:

1. **January–March**: ITCZ sits over the Gulf of Guinea; the dry, dusty **Harmattan** dominates the north.
2. **April–October**: ITCZ shifts north to about 20°N; the moist **Southwest Monsoon** brings rainfall to most of Nigeria.
3. **November–December**: ITCZ retreats south; the dry season returns.

#### Climate Zones of Nigeria (South to North)

| Zone | Rainfall (mm/year) | Vegetation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tropical rainforest (south) | 1500–4000 | Evergreen trees, mangroves |
| Tropical savanna (Guinea) | 1000–1500 | Tall grasses, scattered trees |
| Sudan savanna | 500–1000 | Shorter grasses, scattered trees |
| Sahel (far north) | Less than 500 | Semi-desert grasses, shrubs |

#### Greenhouse Effect and Climate Change

Major greenhouse gases — water vapour (H₂O), carbon dioxide (CO₂), methane (CH₄), nitrous oxide (N₂O), and CFCs — trap outgoing infrared radiation, warming the lower atmosphere. Burning fossil fuels and deforestation increase CO₂, driving **climate change**, which is a long-term statistical shift in weather patterns, not a single weather event.

#### Micro-Worked Example

A station records T_max = 32 °C and T_min = 24 °C. Using T_mean = (T_max + T_min) ÷ 2, the daily mean is (32 + 24) ÷ 2 = **28 °C**. If actual vapour pressure is 18 hPa and saturation vapour pressure is 30 hPa, then RH = (18 ÷ 30) × 100 = **60%**.

#### Practice Prompts

1. Explain why the Harmattan season is dry and dusty, not primarily cold.
2. Describe how the ITCZ migration produces the two seasons experienced in Abuja.
