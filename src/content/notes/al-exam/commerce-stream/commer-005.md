# Accounting Principles and Financial Statements

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Accounting Principles and Financial Statements** governs how business transactions are recorded, summarised and reported under the **accrual basis** and the **double-entry** system. The framework rests on **Going Concern**, **Historical Cost**, **Prudence**, **Consistency**, and **Materiality**, aligned with **IFRS** in Sri Lanka. The **accounting equation** is `Assets = Liabilities + Owner's Equity`. Final accounts consist of the **Trading Account**, **Profit and Loss Account**, **Balance Sheet**, plus the **Cash Flow Statement** and **Statement of Changes in Equity**. Master the formulas for gross profit, net profit, and depreciation — these recur every paper.

#### Must-Know Formulas
| Formula | Components |
|---|---|
| Gross Profit | Net Sales − Cost of Goods Sold |
| Net Profit | Gross Profit − Operating Expenses − Finance Costs − Tax |
| Straight-Line Depreciation | (Cost − Residual Value) ÷ Useful Life |
| Reducing-Balance Depreciation | Opening Book Value × Depreciation Rate |

- **Paper I (MCQ):** identify principle applied, classify capital vs revenue items.
- **Paper II (Structured):** prepare final accounts from a trial balance with 5–8 adjustments.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Foundational Principles
Financial statements in Sri Lanka follow **IFRS** as adopted by the Institute of Chartered Accountants of Sri Lanka (CA Sri Lanka). The **accrual basis** records income when earned and expenses when incurred, not when cash moves. The **going concern** assumption presumes the business will operate indefinitely, justifying the use of **historical cost** rather than liquidation values. **Prudence** requires recognising losses immediately but only recognising gains when realised. **Consistency** demands the same methods year-on-year; any change must be disclosed with its effect on profit.

#### The Double-Entry System
Every transaction affects at least two accounts with equal debit and credit totals. The **accounting equation** — `Assets = Liabilities + Equity` — must hold after every posting. The **Trial Balance** lists all ledger balances to confirm total debits equal total credits before preparing final accounts.

#### Preparing Final Accounts
The **Trading Account** matches net sales against cost of goods sold (COGS), where `COGS = Opening Inventory + Net Purchases − Closing Inventory`. The **Profit and Loss Account** deducts operating expenses, finance costs and tax from gross profit to arrive at **net profit**. The **Balance Sheet** then presents assets, liabilities and equity at year-end, sequenced by liquidity.

#### Common Adjustments
| Adjustment | Effect on Final Accounts |
|---|---|
| Closing inventory | Credit Trading A/c, list as current asset |
| Depreciation | Debit P&L A/c, deduct from asset in B/S |
| Accrued expenses | Add to expense; show as current liability |
| Prepaid expenses | Deduct from expense; show as current asset |
| Bad debts / provision | Debit P&L; deduct from receivables |

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Capital vs Revenue Distinction
**Capital expenditure** (e.g., machinery purchase) is capitalised on the Balance Sheet and depreciated over its useful life. **Revenue expenditure** (e.g., repairs, wages) is charged fully against the current year's Profit and Loss Account. Misclassification inflates or deflates profit and distorts asset valuations — a frequent Paper II trap.

#### Cash Flow Statement
Under **IAS 7**, the Cash Flow Statement classifies movements into **operating**, **investing** and **financing** activities. Indirect method starts with net profit and adjusts for non-cash items (depreciation) and working capital changes. A Sri Lankan trading firm typically shows positive operating cash flow, negative investing outflows (asset purchases), and financing inflows from bank loans.

#### Worked Example
A firm reports: Opening inventory Rs. 200,000; Net purchases Rs. 1,800,000; Closing inventory Rs. 300,000; Net sales Rs. 2,500,000; Operating expenses Rs. 350,000; Tax @ 20%. `COGS = 200,000 + 1,800,000 − 300,000 = Rs. 1,700,000`. `Gross Profit = 2,500,000 − 1,700,000 = Rs. 800,000`. `Net Profit before tax = 800,000 − 350,000 = Rs. 450,000`. `Tax = 90,000`. `Net Profit = Rs. 360,000`.

#### Common Mistakes
- Forgetting **closing inventory** adjustment, which overstates COGS and understates assets.
- Crediting **bad debts recovered** to P&L without reversing the earlier provision.
- Charging **drawings** to P&L — they reduce capital, not profit.
- Using the **wrong depreciation method** mid-asset-life without restatement.

1. From a trial balance plus adjustments, prepare a Trading and P&L Account for the year ended 31 March 2026.
2. Explain how IAS 1 requires financial statements to be presented, and apply the prudence concept to a doubtful debtor scenario.
