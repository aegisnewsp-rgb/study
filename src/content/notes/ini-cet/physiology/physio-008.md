# Renal Physiology: Tubular Transport and Urine Formation

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your INI CET examination.

The kidneys process approximately 180 liters of glomerular ultrafiltrate daily, selectively reabsorbing more than 99% of filtered water and sodium while eliminating metabolic waste products such as urea, creatinine, and fixed acids. Urine formation involves three sequential processes: glomerular ultrafiltration, tubular reabsorption (luminal fluid to peritubular capillaries), and tubular secretion (peritubular capillaries to tubular lumen).

Tubular transport is driven primarily by the basolateral sodium-potassium ATPase ($Na^+/K^+$-ATPase), which maintains an intracellular sodium concentration of approximately 10 to 15 mEq/L and a negative interior resting membrane potential (-70 mV). This electrochemical gradient drives secondary active transport of glucose, amino acids, bicarbonate, and chloride across the apical brush border.

#### Summary of Segmental Nephron Transport

| Nephron Segment | Primary Apical Transporters | Solute Reabsorption Profile | Primary Diuretic Class & Site |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Proximal Convoluted Tubule (PCT)** | NHE3 ($Na^+/H^+$ exchanger), SGLT2 ($Na^+$-glucose), NaPi-IIa, amino acid cotransporters | 65% to 70% of filtered $Na^+$, $H_2O$, $K^+$, $Cl^-$; 85% of $HCO_3^-$; 100% of glucose and amino acids | Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors (Acetazolamide), SGLT2 inhibitors (Empagliflozin) |
| **Thin Descending Limb of Henle (tDLH)** | Aquaporin-1 (AQP1 water channels); impermeable to NaCl | Obligatory water reabsorption down medullary osmotic gradient (fluid concentrates to 1200 mOsm/kg) | Osmotic diuretics (Mannitol) |
| **Thick Ascending Limb of Henle (TAL)** | NKCC2 ($Na^+/K^+/2Cl^-$ cotransporter), ROMK apical $K^+$ channel | 25% of filtered $Na^+$, $K^+$, $Cl^-$; paracellular $Ca^{2+}$ and $Mg^{2+}$; impermeable to water (diluting segment) | Loop diuretics (Furosemide, Bumetanide, Torsemide) |
| **Early Distal Convoluted Tubule (DCT)** | NCC ($Na^+/Cl^-$ cotransporter), apical TRPV5 calcium channels | 5% to 7% of filtered $Na^+$ and $Cl^-$; PTH-stimulated calcium reabsorption | Thiazide diuretics (Hydrochlorothiazide, Chlorthalidone) |
| **Cortical Collecting Duct (CCD)** | ENaC (epithelial sodium channels), ROMK, apical $H^+$-ATPase (Type A intercalated cells) | 2% to 3% of filtered $Na^+$ reabsorbed; $K^+$ and $H^+$ secreted under aldosterone regulation | Potassium-sparing diuretics (Amiloride, Spironolactone, Eplerenone) |
| **Medullary Collecting Duct (MCD)** | Aquaporin-2 (AQP2 water channels, regulated by ADH), UT-A1 urea transporters | Final regulation of urine osmolality (50 to 1200 mOsm/kg) and water conservation | V2 receptor antagonists (Tolvaptan, Conivaptan) |

⚡ **High-Yield INI CET Principle:** The Thick Ascending Limb is completely impermeable to water. Because it actively reabsorbs NaCl without water, the luminal fluid leaving the TAL and entering the early distal tubule is always hypotonic (approximately 100 mOsm/kg), earning it the title of the cortical diluting segment.

#### 1. Proximal Convoluted Tubule Transport Mechanics

1. **Sodium and Glucose Cotransport:**
   - In the early S1 segment of the proximal tubule, the high-capacity, low-affinity sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) couples the entry of one $Na^+$ with one glucose molecule across the apical membrane. SGLT2 reabsorbs approximately 90% of filtered glucose.
   - The remaining 10% is reabsorbed in the downstream S3 segment by the high-affinity, low-capacity SGLT1 transporter (two $Na^+$ per one glucose).
   - Glucose leaves the basolateral membrane by facilitated diffusion via GLUT2 (S1) and GLUT1 (S3).
   - The transport maximum ($T_{\max}$) for glucose averages 375 mg/min in adult males. The renal threshold for glucose (the plasma concentration at which glucosuria first appears) is approximately 180 to 200 mg/dL. The discrepancy between theoretical $T_{\max}$ and actual renal threshold is called the splay, caused by nephron heterogeneity and non-saturating kinetics at low concentrations.
2. **Bicarbonate Reabsorption and Acid Secretion:**
   - Filtered bicarbonate ($HCO_3^-$) cannot directly cross the apical membrane. Apical NHE3 extrudes $H^+$ in exchange for $Na^+$.
   - In the tubular lumen, secreted $H^+$ combines with filtered $HCO_3^-$ to form $H_2CO_3$. Membrane-bound Carbonic Anhydrase type IV (CA IV) on the brush border dehydrates $H_2CO_3$ into $CO_2$ and $H_2O$.
   - $CO_2$ rapidly diffuses across the apical membrane into the tubular cell, where cytosolic Carbonic Anhydrase type II (CA II) rehydrates it back to $H_2CO_3$, which dissociates into $H^+$ and $HCO_3^-$.
   - $HCO_3^-$ exits across the basolateral membrane via the electrogenic sodium-bicarbonate cotransporter NBCe1 (SLC4A4) at a 1:3 ratio, while $H^+$ is recycled via NHE3.

#### 2. Countercurrent Multiplication and the Medullary Osmotic Gradient

The creation and maintenance of a hypertonic medullary interstitium (ranging from 300 mOsm/kg at the corticomedullary junction up to 1200 mOsm/kg at the papilla) allows humans to concentrate urine up to four times plasma osmolality:

1. **The Countercurrent Multiplier (Loop of Henle):**
   - The hairpin architecture with countercurrent flow multiplies the single effect of active solute transport.
   - NKCC2 in the thick ascending limb actively pumps $Na^+$, $K^+$, and $2Cl^-$ into the medullary interstitium while remaining impermeable to water. This establishes a horizontal gradient of up to 200 mOsm/kg at any given horizontal cross-section.
   - As new isotonic fluid enters the descending limb, water leaves via AQP1 into the hypertonic interstitium, progressively concentrating descending luminal fluid up to 1200 mOsm/kg at the hairpin bend.
   - The concentrated fluid entering the thick ascending limb provides a continuous supply of ions for active pumping, multiplying the vertical longitudinal gradient from 300 to 1200 mOsm/kg.
2. **The Countercurrent Exchanger (Vasa Recta):**
   - The hairpin looping of the peritubular capillary vasa recta prevents washout of medullary solutes.
   - As blood descends into the inner medulla, solutes diffuse in and water diffuses out. As blood ascends back toward the cortex, solutes diffuse out and water diffuses in. Sluggish medullary blood flow (less than 10% of total renal blood flow) preserves the hypertonic gradient.
3. **Urea Recycling:**
   - Urea accounts for approximately 50% of the inner medullary hypertonicity. In the presence of ADH, urea transporters UT-A1 and UT-A3 in the inner medullary collecting duct are activated, facilitating urea efflux into the interstitium. Urea diffuses into the thin ascending limb via UT-A2, recycling back to the collecting duct.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Advanced quantitative clearance formulas, acid-base physiology, and clinical cases.

#### 1. Acid-Base Handling: Intercalated Cells of the Collecting Duct

In the late distal tubule and collecting duct, specialized intercalated cells regulate final acid-base balance:

1. **Type A ($lpha$) Intercalated Cells (Acid Secretion):**
   - Active during systemic metabolic acidosis.
   - Apical $H^+$-ATPase (vacuolar proton pump) and $H^+/K^+$-ATPase extrude $H^+$ into the lumen against steep electrochemical gradients (urinary pH can fall to a minimum of 4.5).
   - Basolateral anion exchanger 1 (AE1 / Band 3) exports synthesized $HCO_3^-$ into the systemic circulation in exchange for $Cl^-$.
   - Failure of the apical $H^+$-ATPase or basolateral AE1 causes Classical Distal Renal Tubular Acidosis (Type 1 RTA), characterized by inability to acidify urine below pH 5.5, hypokalemia, hypercalciuria, and nephrocalcinosis.
2. **Type B ($eta$) Intercalated Cells (Bicarbonate Secretion):**
   - Active during systemic metabolic alkalosis.
   - Polarity is reversed: apical pendrin ($SLC26A4$, an electroneutral $Cl^-/HCO_3^-$ exchanger) secretes $HCO_3^-$ into the tubular lumen while basolateral $H^+$-ATPase extrudes $H^+$ back into the blood.

#### 2. Quantitative Diagnostic Calculations in Nephrology

##### Calculation 1: Fractional Excretion of Sodium ($FE_{Na}$)

The fractional excretion of sodium distinguishes volume-responsive prerenal acute kidney injury from intrinsic acute tubular necrosis (ATN):

$$FE_{Na} = \frac{\text{Sodium Cleared}}{\text{Creatinine Cleared}} \times 100\% = \frac{U_{Na} \times P_{Cr}}{P_{Na} \times U_{Cr}} \times 100\%$$

**Clinical Case Application:**  
A 65-year-old male with postoperative oliguria (urine output < 20 mL/h) undergoes laboratory evaluation:
- Serum Sodium ($P_{Na}$): $140\text{ mEq/L}$
- Serum Creatinine ($P_{Cr}$): $3.0\text{ mg/dL}$
- Urine Sodium ($U_{Na}$): $14\text{ mEq/L}$
- Urine Creatinine ($U_{Cr}$): $60\text{ mg/dL}$

Calculate $FE_{Na}$:

$$FE_{Na} = \frac{14 \times 3.0}{140 \times 60} \times 100\% = \frac{42}{8,400} \times 100\% = 0.5\%$$

**Diagnostic Interpretation:**  
An $FE_{Na} < 1\%$ (along with concentrated urine and low urinary sodium $< 20\text{ mEq/L}$) confirms prerenal azotemia with intact tubular reabsorptive machinery attempting to conserve volume. Administration of intravenous crystalloids will restore renal perfusion. Conversely, an $FE_{Na} > 2\%$ with urinary sodium $> 40\text{ mEq/L}$ indicates acute tubular necrosis.

##### Calculation 2: Free Water Clearance ($C_{H_2O}$)

Free water clearance quantifies the volume of solute-free water excreted by the kidneys per minute:

$$C_{H_2O} = V - C_{osm} = V \left(1 - \frac{U_{osm}}{P_{osm}}\right)$$

where $V$ is urine flow rate (mL/min), $U_{osm}$ is urine osmolality, and $P_{osm}$ is plasma osmolality.

**Clinical Case Application:**  
A patient with central diabetes insipidus produces 12 liters of pale urine in 24 hours ($V = 8.33\text{ mL/min}$).
- Urine osmolality ($U_{osm}$): $75\text{ mOsm/kg}$
- Plasma osmolality ($P_{osm}$): $300\text{ mOsm/kg}$

Calculate Free Water Clearance ($C_{H_2O}$):

$$C_{H_2O} = 8.33 \times \left(1 - \frac{75}{300}\right) = 8.33 \times (1 - 0.25) = 8.33 \times 0.75 = +6.25\text{ mL/min}$$

**Diagnostic Interpretation:**  
A strongly positive free water clearance ($+6.25\text{ mL/min}$, or $+9.0\text{ L/day}$) indicates that the kidney is excreting large volumes of pure water without solutes due to the complete absence of ADH-mediated AQP2 insertion in the collecting ducts. Administration of desmopressin (dDAVP) reverses this abnormality.

#### 3. Common Exam Traps in INI CET

1. **Potassium Handling in Alkalosis vs. Acidosis:** In acute metabolic alkalosis, intracellular $K^+$ shifts into intercalated cells in exchange for $H^+$, stimulating basolateral $Na^+/K^+$-ATPase and increasing intracellular $K^+$ concentration in principal cells. This steepens the electrochemical gradient for apical $K^+$ secretion via ROMK, causing marked kaliuresis and hypokalemia. In chronic metabolic acidosis, proximal tubular sodium and water reabsorption is inhibited, delivering excess fluid and sodium to the collecting duct, paradoxically promoting potassium excretion despite systemic acidosis.
2. **Calcium Handling by Diuretics:** Loop diuretics inhibit NKCC2 in the TAL, collapsing the $+8\text{ mV}$ lumen-positive transepithelial potential. This abolishes the driving force for paracellular calcium and magnesium reabsorption, causing hypercalciuria and nephrocalcinosis (used therapeutically in severe hypercalcemia). Thiazide diuretics inhibit NCC in the distal tubule, lowering intracellular $Na^+$ and stimulating basolateral $Na^+/Ca^{2+}$ exchanger NCX1, which drives apical calcium entry through TRPV5, producing hypocalciuria and hypercalcemia.
