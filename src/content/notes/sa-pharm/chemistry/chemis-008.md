# Aldehydes, Ketones, and Carbonyl Group Chemistry

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your SAPC Chemistry paper.

The **carbonyl group (C=O)** is the defining feature of aldehydes and ketones. In an **aldehyde (R–CHO)**, the carbonyl carbon carries at least one hydrogen; in a **ketone (R–CO–R′)**, it carries two carbon groups.

#### Must-know reactions:

| Reaction | Reagent | Product |
|---|---|---|
| Reduction | H₂ / LiAlH₄ or NaBH₄ | Primary alcohol (from aldehyde) or secondary (from ketone) |
| HCN addition | HCN / KCN | **Cyanohydrin** R₂C(OH)CN |
| Tollens' | [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ | Carboxylate + silver mirror (aldehydes only) |
| Iodoform | I₂ / OH⁻ | Yellow **CHI₃** precipitate (CH₃CO–R or CH₃CHO only) |

> Mnemonic: **Brady's 2,4-DNP test is class-blind — it proves a carbonyl exists but never tells aldehyde from ketone.**

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months before the SAPC exam.

#### Structure and Polarity

The carbonyl carbon is **sp² hybridised** with trigonal-planar geometry (≈120° bond angles). Oxygen pulls electron density, leaving C as **Cδ⁺** and O as **Oδ⁻**. This polarisation makes the carbon an electrophilic target, which is why nucleophilic addition dominates carbonyl chemistry.

#### Relative Reactivity

Aldehydes are **more reactive than ketones** toward nucleophilic addition for two compounding reasons: alkyl groups donate electrons (stabilising the Cδ⁺) and they create steric crowding at the carbonyl carbon.

| Property | Aldehyde | Ketone |
|---|---|---|
| Carbonyl carbon substituents | 1 H + 1 R | 2 R groups |
| Oxidation by Tollens' | Yes (silver mirror) | No |
| Positive iodoform test | Only ethanal (CH₃CHO) | Only methyl ketones (CH₃CO–R) |
| Boiling point | Lower | Higher (more electrons, larger surface) |

#### Nucleophilic Addition Mechanism

A nucleophile (Nu⁻) attacks the **Cδ⁺** of C=O, electrons shift onto oxygen, then a proton from solvent or water quenches the alkoxide to a neutral alcohol. The classic case is **cyanohydrin formation:**

R₂C=O + HCN → R₂C(OH)CN

The tetrahedral alkoxide intermediate is the reason the carbon changes hybridisation from sp² to sp³ during the addition step.

#### Identifying Unknown Carbonyls in the SAPC Paper

- **2,4-DNP (Brady's test):** orange precipitate with **any** carbonyl — confirms presence but not class.
- **Tollens' (silver mirror):** aldehydes give Ag(s); ketones give nothing.
- **Fehling's:** aliphatic aldehydes give brick-red Cu₂O; aromatic aldehydes and ketones do not.
- **Iodoform:** yellow CHI₃ precipitate signals a CH₃CO– group attached to H or to a non-methyl R.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Acid–Base Chemistry of the α-Carbon

**Alpha-hydrogens** (on the carbon next to C=O) are unusually acidic (pKa ≈ 20) because the resulting enolate is stabilised by resonance onto the carbonyl oxygen. Removal of an α-H by a base initiates **aldol condensation**: the enolate adds to a second carbonyl, yielding a **β-hydroxy carbonyl**, which dehydrates under heating to give an **α,β-unsaturated carbonyl**.

> Exam trap: aldol condensation is **addition then E1cb elimination**, not substitution. Many candidates misclassify it.

#### Protecting Groups: Acetals and Ketals

Under acid catalysis, an aldehyde first forms a **hemiacetal** (one alcohol added), then a second alcohol yields an **acetal**. The analogous two-step process for ketones gives **hemiketal → ketal**. Acetals are stable to base and to reducing agents, so they let chemists mask a carbonyl while reactions run elsewhere in the molecule.

#### Hydration Equilibrium

In water, carbonyls partially convert to **gem-diols (hydrates)**:

K_hyd = [gem-diol] / [carbonyl]

Formaldehyde sits near K_hyd ≈ 10³ (highly hydrated), acetone near 10⁻³ (almost none). Highly electron-withdrawing groups (e.g. chloral, trichloroacetaldehyde) push K_hyd above 10⁴ — a useful reasoning point when comparing substrate reactivity.

#### Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Writing "RCOH" without showing the C=O double bond skews oxidation-state bookkeeping.
- Reporting a negative iodoform test as "not a ketone" — only **methyl ketones** and ethanal are positive.
- Treating 2,4-DNP as aldehyde-specific; it reacts with both classes.
- Assuming carbonyl carbon is sp³ — it is sp² until the nucleophile lands.

#### Practice Prompts

1. Propanal reacts with Tollens' reagent, but propanone does not. Write the balanced half-reaction and explain the difference in two sentences using inductive effects.
2. Predict the organic product when cyclohexanone is treated with HCN, then state the hybridisation change at the carbonyl carbon during the reaction.
