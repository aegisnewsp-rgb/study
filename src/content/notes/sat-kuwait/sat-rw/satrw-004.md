# Craft and Structure — Text Structure and Purpose — SAT R&W Notes

Text Structure and Purpose is part of the SAT R&W Craft and Structure content domain. It tests the candidate's ability to analyse how a text is structured, the author's purpose, point of view, and the function of paragraphs and sentences within the overall argument.

> Verify the live SAT specifications on https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/ before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Text structure:** how a text is organised (cause/effect, compare/contrast, problem/solution, chronological, etc.).
- **Author's purpose:** why the author wrote the text (to inform, persuade, entertain, describe).
- **Point of view:** the perspective from which the text is written (first person, third person, etc.).
- **Function of paragraphs/sentences:** the role each part plays in the overall argument.
- **Skills tested:** analytical reasoning, structure identification, purpose identification.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing the topic with the purpose.
- Misidentifying the text structure.

---

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Identifying text structure

To identify the text structure:

1. Read the first sentence of each paragraph.
2. Identify how the paragraphs relate to each other.
3. Choose the structure that best describes the relationship.

Common structures:

- **Cause/effect:** the passage explains why something happens or what results from it.
- **Compare/contrast:** the passage compares two or more things.
- **Problem/solution:** the passage describes a problem and proposes a solution.
- **Chronological:** the passage describes events in order.
- **Argumentative:** the passage argues for or against a position.

#### Identifying author's purpose

To identify the author's purpose:

1. Identify the main claim of the passage.
2. Determine whether the author is trying to inform, persuade, entertain, or describe.
3. Choose the purpose that best fits.

#### Point of view

Point of view describes the perspective:

- **First person:** the author writes about themselves ("I," "we").
- **Third person:** the author writes about others ("he," "she," "they").
- **Second person:** the author addresses the reader ("you").

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where Text Structure and Purpose appears across the SAT R&W section

Text Structure and Purpose items appear across the SAT Reading and Writing section. They test analytical reasoning at a higher level than recall.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Topic equals purpose." No — topic is what the passage is about; purpose is why it was written.
- "First-person is always personal." No — first-person can be journalistic, scientific, or academic.

#### Exam technique

- For structure questions, identify how the paragraphs relate.
- For purpose questions, identify the main claim and the author's intent.
- For point-of-view questions, identify the pronouns and perspective.
