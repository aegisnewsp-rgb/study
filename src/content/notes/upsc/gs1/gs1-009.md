# Science Tech

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Fast revision for the half-hour before you walk into the UPSC Prelims hall.

UPSC rarely frames Science & Technology as a standalone GS1 topic — it is a **transversal strand** that bleeds into History (Nehru-era institution building), Geography (space-based monsoon, seismology, soil mapping) and Polity (Article 51A(h), Fundamental Duty to develop scientific temper). Prelims, however, test ~8–12 factual questions a year on it, so you cannot skip the institutional architecture.

#### Must-know anchors:

- **Article 51A(h)** — Fundamental Duty, not a Fundamental Right, to develop scientific temper, humanism and the spirit of inquiry.
- **DST vs DSIR** — both sit under the Ministry of Science & Technology, but DST funds extramural research while DSIR runs through CSIR labs and the Technology Development Board.
- **STIP 2020** replaces the 2013 and 2003 policies and targets doubling R&D spend to 2% of GDP (current ~0.65–0.7%).

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook (Memory Hook):** Remember the four nodal pillars as **"D-C-I-D"** — **D**ST, **C**SIR, **I**SRO, **D**RDO. Anything else (DSIR, TDB, ANRF, BIRAC) is a sub-body that reports into one of these four.

| Body | Parent Ministry | One-line job |
|---|---|---|
| DST | Ministry of Science & Technology | Funds universities, runs AIM |
| CSIR / DSIR | Ministry of Science & Technology | Industrial R&D, lab network |
| ISRO | Department of Space | Satellites, launch vehicles |
| DRDO | Ministry of Defence | Missiles, defence platforms |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### India's S&T governance map

Three ministries share the S&T mandate. The **Ministry of Science & Technology** houses DST, DSIR and the Department of Biotechnology. **Department of Space** runs ISRO and the commercial arm **NewSpace India Limited (NSIL, 2019)** — note that NSIL replaced the older Antrix Corporation for commercial activities. The **Ministry of Defence** owns DRDO's 50+ labs.

Apex advisory bodies sit on top: the **Principal Scientific Adviser** heads the Prime Minister's Science, Technology and Innovation Advisory Council (PM-STIAC), while the **Scientific Advisory Committee to the PM** is older and reports directly to the PMO. The 2023 **Anusandhan National Research Foundation (ANRF) Act** created a funding vehicle for academic research, replacing parts of the SERB scheme.

#### Policy timeline worth memorising

1. **S&T Policy 2003** — first standalone policy after liberalisation.
2. **STIP 2013** — added innovation and private-sector participation.
3. **STIP 2020** — current policy; emphasises open science, gender inclusion, ethics, and the 2%-of-GDP R&D target.
4. **Technology Vision 2035** — longer-horizon roadmap by TIFAC.

#### Commonly confused bodies — comparison matrix

| Confused pair | Correct distinction |
|---|---|
| DST vs DSIR | DST = extramural funding, AIM, autonomous institutes like TIFAC. DSIR = industrial R&D coordination, runs CSIR labs and the Technology Development Board (TDB). |
| Antrix vs NSIL | Antrix (1992) handled ISRO's commercial arm; NSIL (2019) took over the commercial mandate — both are separate from ISRO's R&D work. |
| Chandrayaan-3 vs Gaganyaan | Chandrayaan-3 (Aug 2023) = uncrewed Moon landing near 69.37°S. Gaganyaan = crewed Low-Earth Orbit mission, no lunar profile. |
| BrahMos vs Nirbhay | BrahMos = Indo-Russian supersonic cruise missile (DRDO + NPO Mashinostroyenia). Nirbhay = indigenous subsonic cruise missile. |
| Atal Innovation Mission components | ATL (school labs), AIC (startup incubators), ACIC (rural innovation), ARISE (industry challenges). Students recall only ATL. |

#### Worked example — Prelims-style MCQ

**Statement:** "India is a signatory to the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty, and all its nuclear facilities are under IAEA safeguards."

This statement is wrong on **both** counts. India has **never signed** the NPT — it remains the only recognised nuclear-weapon state outside the treaty. IAEA safeguards apply **voluntarily** to a subset of India's civilian reactors (under the 2008 India-IAEA safeguards agreement and the 2010 India-specific exemption from full-scope safeguards), while military facilities like the Bhavini fast-breeder complex fall outside IAEA purview.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Prelims options often swap "signatory" with "non-signatory" and "civilian" with "military" facilities. Mark the statement as wrong on the NPT clause alone — you do not need to remember the year of the safeguards agreement to eliminate the option.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

#### Edge cases and boundary conditions

**R&D-spend arithmetic.** The 2% target has been policy since STIP 2013 and was reiterated in STIP 2020. Current expenditure sits around 0.65–0.7% of GDP, with roughly two-thirds borne by government and one-third by private industry — a ratio the policy wants to invert. State-level R&D data is captured by **NSTMIS (National Science and Technology Management Information System)** under DST.

**Quantum Mission scope.** The **National Quantum Mission (2023)** covers five verticals: quantum computing, quantum communication, quantum sensing/metrology, quantum materials, and post-quantum cryptography. Answers that restrict it to "quantum computers" miss four-fifths of the scheme. The mission targets 50–1,000 qubit platforms by 2030.

**Right to Science — a structural nuance.** Article 51A(h) places the duty on citizens. The Supreme Court has read DPSP Articles 39, 41 and 48A together with Article 21 in environmental and health cases to imply **state obligations** to disseminate scientific knowledge — but no enforceable Fundamental Right titled "Right to Science" exists in the Constitution. Confusing duty with right is a recurring answer-killer.

#### Frequently asked traps

1. Mixing up **Chandrayaan-2** (2019, partial failure, orbiter still operational) with **Chandrayaan-3** (2023, full success, Vikram lander + Pragyan rover). Vikram was the lander, Pragyan the rover — not the other way round.
2. Treating **NavIC (IRNSS)** as global like GPS — it is regional, covering India and 1,500 km beyond.
3. Calling **BrahMos-II** operational — it is a hypersonic derivative still in development.
4. Citing the **Bharat 6G Vision** as deployed — it is a roadmap document, not a live network.
5. Stating that the **Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023** creates a dedicated "AI regulator" — it does not; AI-specific regulation remains under MeitY advisories.
6. Assuming **Shakti chip** and **PARAM series** are consumer products — they are research compute platforms under C-DAC for supercomputing workloads.

#### Adjacent-topic linkages

- **Geography (GS1):** ISRO's **Bhuvan** portal, **MOSDAC** for ocean state, and **NavIC**-enabled agri-advisories are answer-ready illustrations of space tech for geography.
- **Modern History (GS1):** Nehru's role in founding CSIR, Atomic Energy Commission (1948), and ISRO's predecessor INCOSPAR (1962) tie directly to S&T institutions.
- **Polity (GS1):** RTI Act 2005 intersects with public-funded research accountability — a high-yield essay angle.

#### Advanced practice prompts

1. Draft a 250-word answer on how the Anusandhan National Research Foundation (ANRF) Act 2023 changes the funding architecture for academic research compared with the pre-existing SERB model. (Hint: industry-academia co-funding, 30% mandatory private share.)
2. Critically evaluate whether Article 51A(h) can be judicially enforced as a duty to provide scientific knowledge, distinguishing it from enforceable Fundamental Rights.
