# Sources of Kenyan Law

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Sources of Kenyan Law are the recognised origins from which rules binding in Kenya draw their legal force. Article 2 of the Constitution of Kenya 2010 sets the hierarchy: the Constitution sits at the top, followed by Acts of Parliament, then delegated legislation, common law, African customary law, Islamic law (for Muslim personal-status matters), and judicial precedent. Ratified international treaties also form part of Kenyan law under Article 2(6).

- **Supreme source:** Constitution of Kenya 2010; any inconsistent law is void (Article 2(1)–(2)).
- **Binding precedent:** Supreme Court → Court of Appeal → High Court → Magistrates' Courts.
- **Limited scope:** Customary and Islamic law apply only where the Constitution and statutes permit.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### The Constitutional Hierarchy
Article 2(1) declares the Constitution the supreme law of the Republic. Article 2(2) invalidates any law, act, or omission inconsistent with it. Article 2(3) obligates every court, tribunal, and State organ to apply the Constitution as the paramount reference. This supremacy clause repealed the entire independence-era constitutional order on 27 August 2010.

#### Primary and Secondary Legislation
Acts of Parliament are laws passed by the National Assembly and Senate and assented to by the President under Article 96. Delegated legislation, including statutory instruments, ministerial regulations, county by-laws, and rules made by professional bodies, derives its validity from a parent Act. A regulation that exceeds the enabling powers of its parent Act is struck down as *ultra vires*.

#### Common Law, Customary Law, and Sharia
Article 2(4) admits the common law of England as adapted to Kenyan circumstances, but only to the extent it is compatible with the Constitution and written statute. Customary law applies subject to the Constitution, statutes, and the Bill of Rights, and must not be repugnant to justice or morality. Islamic law governs Muslim personal-status matters such as marriage, divorce, and inheritance under the Kadhis' Courts (Article 170).

#### Judicial Precedent (Stare Decisis)
The binding portion of a superior court decision is the **ratio decidendi**; incidental remarks are **obiter dicta**. The Supreme Court binds all lower courts, the Court of Appeal binds the High Court and subordinate courts, and the High Court binds magistrates' courts and tribunals.

| Source | Authority | Constraint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Constitution 2010 | Supreme | None higher |
| Acts of Parliament | Primary | Must comply with Constitution |
| Delegated legislation | Secondary | Must stay within parent Act |
| Common law | Subsidiary | Compatible with Constitution |
| Customary / Islamic law | Subsidiary | Subject to statute and Bill of Rights |

- Recognised sources ranked under Article 2: Constitution, statutes, delegated legislation, common law, customary law, Islamic law, and judicial precedent.
- Received English statutes of general application in force on 12 August 1897 continue to apply subject to local conditions.
- Equity is administered alongside common law under the Judicature Act and the Constitution.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Court Interactions
Where the High Court sits as a Constitutional Court under Article 165, its rulings on constitutional interpretation bind all subordinate courts but do not bind the Supreme Court. Decisions of the Supreme Court on matters of constitutional interpretation are final under Article 163(7), and its decisions on any matter are binding on all other courts. Privy Council and House of Lords decisions decided before 12 December 1963 remain persuasive but not binding.

#### International Law and Treaties
Article 2(5) lists the general rules of international law as part of Kenyan law, while Article 2(6) provides that any treaty or convention ratified by Kenya forms part of domestic law automatically. Treaties not ratified require enabling legislation, and unincorporated treaties cannot be enforced directly in domestic courts.

#### Common Mistakes in Examination
- Ranking customary law above statutes, which reverses Article 2(4).
- Treating Islamic law as applying to all Kenyans rather than to Muslim personal-status matters only.
- Quoting the **obiter dicta** of a case as if it were the ratio decidendi.
- Assuming unratified treaties are enforceable.

#### Worked Illustrative Question
If a county by-law conflicts with an Act of Parliament on the same subject, the Act prevails. If both conflict with the Constitution, both fall because Article 2(2) voids inconsistent law regardless of source.

| Tier of court | Binds whom | Bound by whom |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Supreme Court | All other courts | None |
| Court of Appeal | High Court and subordinate courts | Supreme Court |
| High Court | Magistrates' Courts and tribunals | Court of Appeal, Supreme Court |
| Magistrate's Court | None generally | All superior courts |

1. State three conditions under which customary law will be held inapplicable.
2. Explain why a statutory instrument can be struck down even though Parliament passed the parent Act validly.
