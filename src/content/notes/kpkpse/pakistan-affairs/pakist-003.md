# Pakistan's Key Institutions, Reforms, and the Road Ahead

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Pakistan's institutional architecture rests on the 1973 Constitution, which distributes power among Parliament, the Supreme Court (SCP), provincial assemblies, and a network of federal regulators. Reform debates centre on whether these bodies have been strengthened or politicised over the last two decades. The KPK PMS paper tests recall of constitutional articles, amendment dates, and revenue-sharing percentages.

| Institution | Constitutional Basis | Core Mandate |
|---|---|---|
| Parliament (Majlis-e-Shoora) | Articles 50–89 | Federal legislation, budget approval |
| Supreme Court | Article 175 | Apex judiciary, constitutional interpretation |
| Election Commission (ECP) | Article 218 | Conduct of elections, delimitations |
| National Accountability Bureau (NAB) | NAB Ordinance 1999 (amended 2022) | Anti-corruption, asset recovery |
| State Bank of Pakistan (SBP) | SBP Act 1956 | Monetary policy, banking regulation |
| Federal Board of Revenue (FBR) | Income Tax Ordinance 2001 | Tax collection, customs |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"PCES-F-S-N"** — Parliament, Courts (SC + IHC), ECP, SBP, FBR, NAB. Pair each with its founding article for instant recall.

The 18th Amendment (2010) abolished the Concurrent List; the 26th Amendment (2024) created the Federal Constitutional Court. The 8th NFC Award (2023) fixed shares as Punjab 51.74%, Sindh 24.55%, KP 14.62%, Balochistan 9.09%.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Constitutional Pillars and Recent Re-shaping

The 1973 Constitution defines three organs — legislature, executive, judiciary — plus four pillars: ECP, SBP, FPSC, and Wafaqi Mohtasib. Article 175-A, inserted by the 18th Amendment in 2010, replaced the old judges-appointment process with a Judicial Commission of Pakistan (JCP) plus a Parliamentary Committee. The 26th Amendment (2024) reshaped the JCP again and added a Federal Constitutional Court to handle constitutional matters faster, leaving the Supreme Court intact. Article 62 (qualifications) and Article 63 (disqualifications) operate together; Section 63-A of the Elections Act 2017 empowers the ECP to decide 63-based disqualifications, distinct from 62 scrutiny.

| Easily Confused | Article / Provision | Distinct Feature |
|---|---|---|
| Art. 62 vs 63 | 1973 Constitution | 62 = qualifications (good character, education); 63 = disqualifications (loan default, foreign nationality) |
| 17th vs 18th Amendment | 2003 vs 2010 | 17th reversed Zia-era changes (President's powers); 18th devolved subjects, abolished CFL |
| NAB 1999 vs 2022 amendment | NAB Ordinance | Body continues; tenure, reference authority, and prosecutorial limits were restructured |
| 7th vs 8th NFC Award | 2010 vs 2023 | 7th launched 18th-Amendment finance; 8th set Rs 9,475 bn divisible pool with revised provincial shares |

#### Fiscal, Electoral, and Administrative Reforms

The FBR's Track-and-Trace system on sugar/cement, POS integration with retailers, and digital invoicing are aimed at broadening the tax base beyond the traditional 1–2% filer population. The Tajir Dost Scheme (2022) brought retailers into a tiered presumptive-tax net. On the electoral side, the Elections Act 2022 consolidated five laws, introduced electronic voting and biometric verification (IVOAS), and tightened party-funding disclosures. The Civil Servants (Amendment) Act 2023 permitted lateral hiring and fixed performance contracts under PAS/PMS frameworks.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** The 8th National Finance Commission Award (2023) distributes a divisible pool of Rs 9,475 billion. How much does Khyber Pakhtunkhwa receive, and which constitutional article governs NFC Awards?

#### Solution:
1. Identify the KP share under the 8th NFC Award: **14.62%**.
2. Compute the amount: 0.1462 × 9,475 bn = **Rs 1,385.245 bn**.
3. Cite the governing provision: **Article 160** of the 1973 Constitution mandates an NFC every five years; the award also flows through the Council of Common Interests (Article 153).
4. Total provincial share check: 51.74 + 24.55 + 14.62 + 9.09 = 100.00% (validates shares).

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students often write "Punjab 50%" or treat NFC shares as equal divisions. The 8th NFC corrected historical imbalances; quoting rounded or pre-2010 figures costs marks. Also, NFC is **Article 160**, not Article 167 (which covers CCI).

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Cross-Institutional Tensions

Three fault-lines dominate contemporary governance debates. First, judicial reach: the 26th Amendment's Federal Constitutional Court raises questions about precedent hierarchy and whether the original 175-A JCP structure has been effectively replaced. Second, federal fiscal stress: even after the 8th NFC's larger divisible pool, provinces face vertical fiscal imbalance because defence and debt servicing remain federally absorbed, limiting true devolution. Third, regulatory capture: bodies like NEPRA (power), OGRA (oil/gas), and SBP operate with statutory independence, yet their chairmen are appointed by the federal government — a structural tension examiners frequently probe.

| Reform Vector | Driver | Critical Milestone | Risk / Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Judicial | 18th Amend. (2010), 26th Amend. (2024) | JCP + Federal Const. Court | Pendency > 2 lakh cases in SC |
| Fiscal | IMF SBA (2023, ~$3 bn) | FBR digital invoicing, Tajir Dost | Tax base still ~1.5% of adult population |
| Energy | CPEC Phase-II | Net-metering, AEDB renewable IPPs | Circular debt rebounds above Rs 2.6 trn |
| Social | Ehsas / BISP | Rs 25,000 cash transfer, Sehat Sahulat | Targeting errors, fiscal sustainability |
| Federalism | 8th NFC (2023) | Divisible pool Rs 9,475 bn | Vertical imbalance persists |

#### Numerical Anchors for Numerical MCQs

- **NFC divisible pool (8th, 2023):** Rs 9,475 bn; KP share = 14.62% ≈ Rs 1,385 bn.
- **Ehsas emergency cash (COVID, 2020):** Rs 12,000 per family tranche; unconditional.
- **IMF SBA (2023):** USD ~3.0 bn over 9 months, signed 30 June 2023.
- **Sehat Sahulat Card (KP, expanded 2022):** coverage up to Rs 1 million per family per year.

> 📌 **Cross-link:** ECP reform (Elections Act 2022) intersects with the 18th Amendment's Article 140-A on local-government devolution; expect a two-part question pairing the two.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. Trace how Article 175-A evolved from 2010 to 2024 and identify the composition change in the JCP after the 26th Amendment.
2. Evaluate whether the 8th NFC Award genuinely resolves provincial fiscal grievances under Article 160, given the persistent vertical imbalance.
