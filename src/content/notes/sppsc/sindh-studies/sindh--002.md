# History of Sindh (Ancient & Medieval)

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Sindh's history rests on the **Indus (Sindhu)** river, whose flood-fed plains produced the Amri, Kot Diji, and **Mohenjo-daro/Harappan** sites (c. 2600–1900 BCE). Buddhist centres at Sudheranjo-daro flourished under successive Hindu dynasties until the **Rai (Chach Brahmin) line** — ending with **Raja Dahir** in 712 CE.

- **712 CE (93 AH):** Muhammad bin Qasim defeats Dahir at Rawar; first Islamic political authority in South Asia.
- **1051–1351 CE:** **Sumra dynasty** founded by Dodo bin Khafaf Soomro; first Sindhi Muslim ruling house.
- **1351–1521 CE:** **Samma dynasty** under Jam Nindo, Jam Unar; capital at Thatta.
- **Scripts:** Lunda (oldest Sindhi), Sarsi/Saindhu (later), Arabic for Islamic administration.
- **Capitals in sequence:** Aror → Mansura → Bhambhore (Debal) → Thatta → Uch Sharif.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"A-K-M-H-S-S-A"** — Amri → Kot Diji → Mohenjo-daro → Hindu (Rai/Chach) → Habbari/Arab → Sumra → Samma → Arghun (early modern). The first five are within medieval scope; Arghun belongs to the next era.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Ancient Foundations and Buddhist-Hindu Transition
Archaeology divides Sindh's pre-Islamic horizon into three cultures: **Amri** (c. 3600 BCE, early chalcolithic pottery), **Kot Diji** (c. 2500 BCE, fortification and bronze), and the urban **Harappan/Mohenjo-daro** phase (UNESCO World Heritage since 1980). After Harappan decline, Sindh appears in the **Rigveda** as part of the Sapta Sindhu territory, followed by Buddhist monasticism — the Sudheranjo-daro stupa and Brahmanabad (Manora) are dated to the early centuries CE. The **Chach Brahmin (Rai) dynasty** (c. 632–712 CE) ruled from Aror with a feudatory network extending to Multan; the last ruler, Raja Dahir Sen, governed from the same capital.

#### Arab Conquest and Islamic Administration (712–c. 1026 CE)
Muhammad bin Qasim landed at **Debal (Bhambhore)** in 712 CE and routed Dahir's forces at **Rawar**. He established **Mansura** as the Arab capital and imposed a structured fiscal system — *diwan* (central accounts), *sahib-i-diwana* (auditor), *amil* (revenue collector), plus *zakat*, *jizya*, and *kharaj*. Four Arab governors held Sindh before local commanders seized power; the **Habbari dynasty** (c. 841–c. 1026) and brief **Saffarid** (Ya'qub bin Laith, c. 874) and **Samanid/Ghaznavid** overlordship followed. Mahmud of Ghazni's 1026 CE raid targeted the **Somnath-Sindh** corridor.

#### Sindhi Muslim Dynasties: Sumra and Samma
The **Sumras** (c. 1051–1351) under Dodo bin Khafaf Soomro ruled from Mansura and later Thatta; their administration revived Hindu revenue officers under Islamic fiscal terminology. The **Sammas**, founded by **Jam Nindo** and consolidated by **Jam Unar** (r. 1461–1509), shifted the capital firmly to Thatta and patronised Sufi networks. Trade moved through Debal toward Baghdad and Hormuz, financed by Pinyari and Nara canal agriculture.

| Concept | Chach/Rai line | Sumra Dynasty | Samma Dynasty |
|---|---|---|---|
| Approx. dates | c. 632–712 CE | c. 1051–1351 CE | 1351–1521 CE |
| Religion of rulers | Hindu (Brahman) | Muslim (converted) | Muslim |
| Capital | Aror | Mansura → Thatta | Thatta |
| Founder/Key ruler | Chach; last ruler Raja Dahir | Dodo bin Khafaf Soomro | Jam Nindo; Jam Unar |
| Administration | Feudal feudatories | Arab-style diwan + Hindu amin | Centralised Jam-iat system |
| Economy | Agriculture + Indus trade | Canal revival + Debal-Baghdad route | Canal + maritime via Lahari Bander |

> 📌 **Formula Check:** The Arab fiscal formula = **kharaj (land tax) + jizya (per-capita on non-Muslims) + zakat (2.5% on Muslim wealth)** — each had a separate collector under the *diwan* hierarchy; SPSC questions frequently ask which tax applied to whom.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem
**Question:** Who founded the first indigenous Muslim ruling dynasty of Sindh, and in approximately which year?
(a) Jam Nindo, 1351 CE
(b) Dodo bin Khafaf Soomro, c. 1051 CE
(c) Muhammad bin Qasim, 712 CE
(d) Ya'qub bin Laith, c. 874 CE

#### Solution:
1. Filter by *indigenous* — eliminate (c) Qasim (Arab invader) and (d) Saffarid (Khurasani).
2. Filter by *first* Muslim ruling dynasty — (a) is Samma, the second dynasty; (b) is Sumra, the first.
3. Cross-check the founder: **Dodo bin Khafaf Soomro** established Sumra rule c. 1051 CE.
4. **Answer: (b).**

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Many students mark (c) because the Arab conquest was "the first Islamic rule." SPSC distinguishes between *foreign* Islamic rule (Qasim, 712 CE) and *first indigenous Muslim dynasty* (Sumra, 1051 CE). Read the qualifier "indigenous/Sindhi" carefully.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases, Boundary Periods, and Common Traps

1. **712 vs 711 CE:** Hijri 93 AH = 712 CE in most accepted Sindhi historiography (Chachnama tradition). Some sources print 711 CE; SPSC marks 712 CE.
2. **Arghun misplacement:** The Arghun (c. 1521–1554) and Tarkhan/Mughal eras are *early modern*, not medieval — do not include them when the question caps at "1521 CE" or "medieval."
3. **Debal ≠ modern Karachi:** Bhambhore archaeological site lies near modern Karachi but is distinct; the medieval port of Debal functioned as the primary Arabian Sea outlet.
4. **Sindhi linguistic line:** Sindhi is **Indo-Aryan** (Sanskrit-rooted, with early Prakrit inscriptions); Perso-Arabic vocabulary layered on after 712 CE. The Lunda script predates the Arabic script adoption; Sachal/Sarmast poets used **Perso-Arabic** script later.
5. **Sufi role:** Islamisation accelerated via **Shah Bilawal Qadiri**, Sayyid Usman Marwandi (Shahbaz Qalandar at Sehwan), and the Multan-centred **Bahauddin Zakariya Suhrawardi** silsila — not by sword alone.
6. **Alexander's passage (326 BCE):** The Macedonian fleet sailed down the Indus through Sindh; **Patala** is identified with the southern Indus delta. Useful for SPSC's conceptual/short-note section.
7. **Two capitals of Sammas:** Early Sammas split between northern (Sewa-Sun) and southern (Thatta) branches — Jam Unar reunified.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts
1. Trace the **capital shifts** from Aror (Chach) → Mansura (Arab) → Thatta (Sumra-Samma). What geographic and strategic reasons explain each move? (Answer anchors: river-course change, Arab strategic inland base, Indus delta maritime trade.)
2. Compare the **Arab fiscal-administrative framework** (Qasim's diwan) with the **Samma Jam-iat**. Identify one element of *continuity* and one element of *indigenous reform*. (Continuity: land survey/revenue percentage. Reform: Sammas substituted *muqaddam* local headmen for Arab *amil*.)

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"Qasim = foreign rule; Sumra = first Sindhi Muslim dynasty; Samma = cultural peak at Thatta."** If the question mentions "first Muslim ruler of Sindh" without qualifiers, examiners accept Qasim (712 CE). If it says "first indigenous/local Muslim dynasty," the answer is the **Sumras (c. 1051 CE)**.
