# What the UPCAT Science subtest actually measures

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your UPCAT Science subtest.

The UPCAT **Science subtest** draws items from four strands — **Earth Science, Biology, Chemistry, and Physics** — calibrated to senior-high-school readiness, not college-major depth. Each subtest contributes a weighted percentage to your **University Priority Weights (UPG)**; Science carries roughly **3%** of that composite in recent validation tables from the Office of the Vice President for Academic Affairs (OVPAA). Items lean toward **applied conceptual reasoning** rather than memorized equations, and many prompts are anchored in **Philippine contexts** such as typhoon tracks, volcanic hazards, endemic species, or DOH health advisories.

**Must-know anchors:**
- **Strands:** Earth Science, Biology, Chemistry, Physics.
- **Weight:** ~3% of UPG.
- **Difficulty:** HS/SHS-level quantitative reasoning; no calculus.
- **Style:** Reading passages with data tables, diagrams, and short scenarios.

| Strand | Sample UPCAT-style content |
|---|---|
| Earth Science | Plate tectonics, Philippine fault lines, typhoon formation |
| Biology | Cell organelles, Mendelian traits, photosynthesis |
| Chemistry | Balancing equations, pH scale, periodic trends |
| Physics | Newton's laws, circuits, wave basics |

**High-yield pointers:** Skip advanced derivations; practice interpreting **graphs and lab data**; memorize only the **core formulas** (F = ma, V = IR, pH = −log[H⁺], PV = nRT at intro level).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months before the UPCAT.

#### What the UPCAT Science subtest actually measures

UPCAT Science is a **multiple-choice subtest** designed by the UPO (University of the Philippines Office of Admissions) to gauge **general scientific literacy** at the end of senior high school. It does not test advanced specialization; instead, it rewards students who can read a short scientific passage, interpret a diagram, and select the option that best applies a basic principle. The subtest is one of several feeding into the **UPG composite**, alongside language proficiency, mathematics, reading comprehension, and the optional Filipino subtest.

#### The four strands and what each demands

Each strand carries its own typical item count, though UPCAT does not publish exact distributions. Earth Science items frequently involve **Philippine geological hazards** — taal volcano activity, the West Valley Fault, monsoon rainfall, and typhoon-category classification using the Saffir-Simpson scale. Biology items test **cell structure, photosynthesis and respiration stoichiometry, genetics crosses (monohybrid and dihybrid), and ecology concepts** like food webs and biodiversity indices.

Chemistry items stay at the **general-chemistry level**: balancing simple equations, identifying reaction types, applying the periodic table's group/period trends, and computing introductory pH or molarity. Physics items focus on **Newtonian mechanics, work-energy, basic circuits (series and parallel), optics, and wave properties** — all quantitative reasoning at the level of plug-and-chug with defined variables.

#### Worked relationship — interpreting a UPCAT-style table

> **Tip:** When a UPCAT item shows a data table, the answer almost always lies in identifying the **trend**, not the exact value.

Suppose an item presents the table below and asks: *Which variable increases most steeply between trials 1 and 4?*

| Trial | Temperature (°C) | Reaction Rate (mol/s) | pH |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 25 | 0.4 | 7.0 |
| 2 | 35 | 0.9 | 7.0 |
| 3 | 45 | 1.7 | 7.0 |
| 4 | 55 | 2.8 | 7.0 |

The correct approach is to **compute the change** for each variable: temperature rose 30 °C, reaction rate rose 2.4 mol/s, pH stayed constant. The reaction rate has the steepest proportional climb, which a well-prepared student links to the Arrhenius relationship — **higher temperature increases the fraction of molecules exceeding activation energy**, accelerating the rate without changing hydrogen-ion concentration.

#### Common UPCAT traps

- **Distractor by unit swap:** options that use the correct number but the wrong unit (e.g., mL instead of L).
- **Inverse questions:** ask which is *NOT* true, which *decreases*, or which is the *exception*.
- **Passage-dependent answers:** the correct option contradicts general knowledge but matches the specific data in the stimulus.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge cases and how UPCAT tests them

UPCAT writers favor items where the **distractor feels intuitively true** but fails under closer inspection. A classic Biology trap asks students to identify the **limiting reactant** in photosynthesis — many pick "sunlight" because it is the obvious energy input, yet the correct answer is often **CO₂ concentration** when the item specifies shaded greenhouse conditions. In Chemistry, items on **acid-base strength** frequently test the difference between **strong acid concentration** (number of H⁺ ions released) and **strong acid reactivity** (readiness to donate protons), which are not the same property.

Physics items sometimes present a **free-body diagram rotated 45°** so students mis-apply the normal-force formula. The disciplined approach is to **redefine the coordinate system** along the incline, decomposing gravity into components parallel and perpendicular to the surface — *F_parallel = mg sin θ*, *F_normal = mg cos θ* — rather than using horizontal/vertical axes.

#### Connections to adjacent topics

UPCAT Science overlaps meaningfully with **Reading Comprehension**, since many items embed a 3–5 sentence stimulus before the question. Strong readers who extract the **operational definition** of unfamiliar terms (e.g., "biomass" or "albedo") gain an edge on items where the strand knowledge is borderline. Science also pairs with **Mathematics**: rate problems, percentage change, and proportional reasoning appear regularly. Treat the Science subtest as a **quantitative-literacy check**, not a pure-content recall exercise.

#### Common mistakes at the mastery level

- **Over-indexing on Chemistry calculation questions**, which appear less frequently than conceptual Biology and Earth Science items in recent forms.
- **Ignoring local ecology.** Questions on Philippine biodiversity (e.g., *tarsier* habitat, *palm civet* diet, coral triangle reefs) recur because UPCAT wants students who understand their own environment.
- **Skipping unit conversions.** Items routinely mix °C and K, cm and m, or g and kg; missing the conversion drops the mark.

#### Two practice prompts

1. **Earth Science prompt:** A UPCAT item describes a municipality on the eastern coast of Luzon experiencing storm surges during a Category 3 typhoon. Which combination of factors most increases surge height? Apply your knowledge of typhoon structure and coastal geomorphy, then check against the Saffir-Simpson wind-speed ranges.
2. **Chemistry prompt:** A 0.10 M acetic acid solution has pH ≈ 2.87, while a 0.10 M HCl solution has pH ≈ 1.00. Explain in two sentences why the molarities are equal but the pH values differ by nearly two units, referencing the **Ka of acetic acid (≈ 1.8 × 10⁻⁵)** and the **complete dissociation of HCl** in water.
