# II. Genetics and Evolution — HKDSE Biology Notes

This topic covers Topic II of the Compulsory Part of HKDSE Biology. It addresses basic genetics (Mendel's laws, monohybrid and dihybrid crosses, sex-linked inheritance); molecular genetics (DNA structure and replication, transcription, translation, gene expression and regulation, mutations, genetic engineering); and biodiversity and evolution (evidence for evolution, mechanisms, speciation, classification).

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Basic genetics**: Mendel's laws (segregation, independent assortment); monohybrid crosses (one trait, 3:1 ratio in F2); dihybrid crosses (two traits, 9:3:3:1 ratio); codominance, incomplete dominance, multiple alleles, sex-linked inheritance (X-linked recessive, e.g. haemophilia, colour blindness).
- **Molecular genetics**: DNA double helix (Watson-Crick, 1953), semiconservative replication (Meselson-Stahl, 1958), transcription (DNA → mRNA in the nucleus), translation (mRNA → polypeptide at the ribosome), the genetic code (triplet codons, 64 codons for 20 amino acids), gene regulation (operons, transcription factors), mutations (point, frameshift, chromosomal), genetic engineering (recombinant DNA, PCR, gel electrophoresis, gene therapy, GM organisms).
- **Biodiversity and evolution**: evidence for evolution (fossil record, comparative anatomy, comparative embryology, molecular biology); mechanisms (natural selection, mutation, gene flow, genetic drift); speciation (allopatric, sympatric); classification (taxonomic hierarchy: kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus, species).

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing genotype with phenotype — genotype is the genetic makeup, phenotype is the observable trait.
- Treating "dominant" as "more common in the population" — dominance is a relationship between alleles, not a population statistic.
- Forgetting that meiosis is the basis of Mendel's law of segregation — the two alleles of a gene separate during meiosis I.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Basic genetics

**Mendel's experiments**: Mendel's pea-plant experiments (1856-1865) established the particulate theory of inheritance. His first law (segregation) states that the two alleles of a gene separate during gamete formation. His second law (independent assortment) states that genes on different chromosomes assort independently.

**Monohybrid crosses**:

- Parental (P) cross: homozygous dominant × homozygous recessive → F1 all heterozygous (one phenotype).
- F1 × F1: heterozygous × heterozygous → F2 ratio 3:1 phenotype, 1:2:1 genotype.

**Dihybrid crosses**: genes on different chromosomes → F2 ratio 9:3:3:1 (phenotype) when both parents are heterozygous for both genes.

**Non-Mendelian patterns**:

- **Codominance**: both alleles expressed in the heterozygote (e.g. AB blood type).
- **Incomplete dominance**: heterozygote has an intermediate phenotype (e.g. pink snapdragons from red × white).
- **Multiple alleles**: more than two alleles in the population (e.g. ABO blood type has three alleles I^A, I^B, i).
- **Sex-linked inheritance**: X-linked genes (e.g. haemophilia, red-green colour blindness) appear more often in males because they have only one X chromosome.
- **Linked genes**: genes on the same chromosome tend to be inherited together; recombination occurs by crossing-over during meiosis I.

#### Molecular genetics

**DNA structure**: double helix, antiparallel strands, sugar-phosphate backbone on the outside, nitrogenous bases on the inside (A-T with two hydrogen bonds, G-C with three hydrogen bonds).

**DNA replication**: semiconservative, requires DNA helicase (unwinds), DNA polymerase (synthesises new strand), DNA ligase (joins Okazaki fragments on the lagging strand). Occurs during the S phase of the cell cycle.

**Transcription**: in the nucleus, RNA polymerase synthesises a single-stranded mRNA copy of a gene. The mRNA is processed (5' cap, poly-A tail, intron splicing) before export to the cytoplasm.

**Translation**: at the ribosome, mRNA is read in triplet codons; tRNA brings the corresponding amino acid; peptide bonds form between adjacent amino acids. The polypeptide folds into its functional 3D structure.

**Gene regulation**: in prokaryotes, the lac operon (Jacob-Monod) demonstrates inducible gene expression; in eukaryotes, transcription factors, enhancers, silencers, and chromatin modification regulate gene expression.

**Mutations**: point mutations (silent, missense, nonsense), frameshift mutations (insertion, deletion), chromosomal mutations (deletion, duplication, inversion, translocation). Mutagens include radiation, chemicals and viruses.

**Genetic engineering**: recombinant DNA technology (restriction enzymes, ligase, plasmids), PCR (polymerase chain reaction), gel electrophoresis, DNA sequencing (Sanger sequencing, next-generation sequencing), gene therapy, GM organisms, CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing.

#### Biodiversity and evolution

**Evidence for evolution**:

- **Fossil record**: shows changes in organisms over geological time.
- **Comparative anatomy**: homologous structures (same underlying structure, different function — e.g. forelimbs of humans, whales, bats) and analogous structures (same function, different structure — e.g. wings of butterflies and birds).
- **Comparative embryology**: embryos of related species show similar developmental stages.
- **Molecular biology**: DNA and protein sequence similarity reflects evolutionary relatedness.

**Mechanisms of evolution**:

- **Natural selection**: differential survival and reproduction based on heritable traits.
- **Mutation**: ultimate source of genetic variation.
- **Gene flow**: movement of alleles between populations.
- **Genetic drift**: random change in allele frequencies, especially in small populations.

**Speciation**:

- **Allopatric**: geographic isolation leads → reproductive isolation → speciation.
- **Sympatric**: speciation without geographic isolation (e.g. polyploidy in plants, sexual selection in cichlid fish).

**Classification**: the taxonomic hierarchy (kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus, species) groups organisms by evolutionary relatedness. Binomial nomenclature (genus + species, e.g. Homo sapiens).

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-topic links

- **I. Cells and Molecules of Life**: DNA replication, transcription and translation occur in the cellular machinery.
- **III. Organisms and Environment**: population genetics connects to population dynamics.
- **VII. Microorganisms and Humans** (Elective): microbial genetics and biotechnology build on molecular genetics.

#### Exam technique

- **For genetic cross questions**: set up the Punnett square carefully, label each axis with the parental gametes, and read off the offspring ratio. Many errors come from misreading the cross.
- **For molecular biology questions**: state the location (nucleus vs. cytoplasm) and the direction (DNA → mRNA → protein).
- **For evolution questions**: cite specific evidence (e.g. fossil species, molecular homology, biogeography) and a mechanism.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Evolution is just random mutation." Natural selection is non-random; the mutation rate is random, but the selection that determines which mutations persist is non-random.
- "Humans evolved from monkeys." Humans and modern monkeys share a common ancestor; humans did not evolve from any extant species.
- "GM organisms are inherently unsafe." Risk assessment of GM organisms is case-by-case; some applications (e.g. insulin production) are widely accepted, others (e.g. herbicide-tolerant crops) remain contested.
