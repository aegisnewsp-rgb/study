# Cardiovascular System

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

The **cardiovascular system** is a closed circulatory loop: a four-chambered muscular pump (heart) connected to arteries, capillaries, and veins that deliver oxygen, nutrients, and hormones while clearing CO₂ and metabolites. For NEET PG Anatomy, focus on chambers, valves, the conducting system, coronary arteries, pericardial sinuses, and fetal shunts.

- **Chambers:** RA (receives SVC, IVC, coronary sinus), LA (four pulmonary veins), RV, LV — separated by interatrial and interventricular septa.
- **Valves:** AV valves (tricuspid right, mitral left) anchored by **chordae tendineae** to papillary muscles; semilunar valves (aortic, pulmonary) have no chordae.
- **Conducting system order:** SA node → AV node → Bundle of His → right/left bundle branches → **Purkinje fibres**.
- **Cardiac Output (CO)** = SV × HR; SV = EDV − ESV.

| Must-know number | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Normal adult CO at rest | ~5 L/min |
| Ejection fraction (EF) | 55–70 % |
| SA node intrinsic rate | 70–75 /min |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Heart wall and pericardium
The wall has three layers: **endocardium** (inner endothelium + subendothelial connective tissue), **myocardium** (cardiac muscle, thickest in the LV), and **epicardium** (= visceral layer of serous pericardium). The fibrous pericardium is the tough outer sac; the serous pericardium has parietal and visceral layers separated by pericardial fluid. Two sinuses matter surgically: the **transverse pericardial sinus** (between aorta/pulmonary trunk anteriorly and SVC/atria posteriorly — used to clamp great vessels) and the **oblique sinus** (posterior to the left atrium).

#### Valves and auscultation
Remember that auscultation sites differ from anatomical positions — examiners exploit this.

| Valve | Anatomical location | Auscultation site |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Aortic | Left, 3rd ICS sternal edge | Right 2nd ICS |
| Pulmonary | Left, 2nd ICS sternal edge | Left 2nd ICS |
| Tricuspid | Right sternal border, 4th–5th ICS | Lower left sternal border, 5th ICS |
| Mitral | Apex, 5th ICS midclavicular | Apex (5th ICS midclavicular) |

#### Coronary circulation
The **left coronary artery** (LCA) branches into the LAD (anterior 2/3 of interventricular septum, anterior LV) and circumflex (left atrium, posterolateral LV). The **right coronary artery (RCA)** supplies the right atrium, right ventricle, posterior 1/3 of septum, and — in ~70–80 % of people — the SA and AV nodes ("right dominance").

#### Fetal shunts
Three structures bypass the non-functional fetal lung: **foramen ovale** (interatrial, right→left), **ductus arteriosus** (pulmonary trunk → aortic arch), and **ductus venosus** (umbilical vein → IVC). All functionally close shortly after birth; failure to close produces patent ductus arteriosus, etc.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Conducting system in detail
The **SA node** (in the upper right atrium near the SVC opening) is the primary pacemaker at 70–75/min. Impulses travel through atrial myocardium to the **AV node** (in the interatrial septum near the coronary sinus opening), where a 0.1 s delay allows ventricular filling. The **Bundle of His** descends through the membranous interventricular septum and splits into right and left bundle branches, terminating as **Purkinje fibres** beneath the endocardium — these are modified cardiac muscle cells, not nerves. Sympathetic fibres from T1–T5 accelerate the heart; vagal (CN X) fibres slow it. Cardiac referred pain (e.g., MI) tracks the T1–T5 dermatomes, classically radiating down the left arm.

#### Applied and clinical correlates
- **Coarctation of the aorta:** narrowing near the ligamentum arteriosum (remnant of ductus arteriosus); produces radio-femoral delay.
- **Pericarditis vs tamponade:** inflammation of parietal/visceral layers vs fluid compressing chambers — both blur the double-shadow on imaging.
- **Coronary dominance:** defined by which artery gives the posterior interventricular (PDA); right dominance (~70–80 %) means RCA → PDA; left dominance means circumflex → PDA.
- **Ejection fraction formula:** EF = SV/EDV × 100; values <40 % indicate systolic dysfunction.

#### Common traps in MCQs
- Confusing **epicardium** with **pericardium** (epicardium is the visceral serous layer = outermost heart layer).
- Forgetting chordae tendineae exist only on AV valves.
- Calling Purkinje fibres "nerves" — they are modified cardiomyocytes.

1. Trace a drop of blood from the SVC through the heart to the aortic arch, naming each valve crossed.
2. A newborn has a continuous machinery murmur — name the fetal shunt involved and its normal adult remnant.
