# Nutrition in Plants and Animals

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> High-yield summary of modes of nutrition, digestive enzymes, vitamins, and deficiency disorders for TNPSC Group 1 candidates.

**Nutritional Biology — High-Yield Essentials**

1. **Modes of Nutrition:**
   - **Autotrophic:** Organisms synthesize organic compounds from inorganic CO2 and H2O using sunlight (photoautotrophs: green plants, cyanobacteria) or chemical oxidation (chemoautotrophs: *Nitrosomonas*, *Nitrobacter*, *Thiobacillus*).
   - **Heterotrophic:** Holozoic (ingestion of solid food: amoeba, human), Saprophytic (absorption of decayed organic matter: fungi, bread mould *Rhizopus*), and Parasitic (deriving nutrients from living host: *Cuscuta*, *Plasmodium*, tapeworm *Taenia solium*).
2. **Digestive Enzyme Cascades:**
   - **Salivary Amylase (Ptyalin):** Optimal pH ~6.8; hydrolyzes starch into maltose.
   - **Gastric Juice:** Pepsin (hydrolyzes proteins into proteoses and peptones at pH 1.5–2.0) activated by HCl secreted by oxyntic/parietal cells.
   - **Pancreatic Juice:** Trypsin, chymotrypsin, carboxypeptidase, pancreatic lipase (steapsin), and amylopsin (acting at alkaline pH 7.8–8.2).
   - **Bile:** Secreted by liver, stored in gallbladder; contains no digestive enzymes but contains bile salts (sodium glycocholate and sodium taurocholate) essential for fat emulsification and micelle formation.
3. **Vitamin Spectrum & Deficiency Manifestations:**
   - **Fat-Soluble (A, D, E, K):**
     - *Vitamin A (Retinol):* Night blindness (Nyctalopia), Xerophthalmia.
     - *Vitamin D (Calciferol):* Rickets in children, Osteomalacia in adults.
     - *Vitamin E (Tocopherol):* Antisterility factor, erythrocyte fragility.
     - *Vitamin K (Phylloquinone):* Defective blood coagulation, prolonged prothrombin time.
   - **Water-Soluble (B-Complex, C):**
     - *B1 (Thiamine):* Beriberi (wet and dry).
     - *B2 (Riboflavin):* Cheilosis, glossitis.
     - *B3 (Niacin / Nicotinic Acid):* Pellagra (4Ds: dermatitis, diarrhoea, dementia, death).
     - *B12 (Cyanocobalamin):* Pernicious anaemia (requires Castle intrinsic factor).
     - *Vitamin C (Ascorbic Acid):* Scurvy, impaired collagen synthesis, bleeding gums.

⚡ **TNPSC Group 1 Exam Trap:** Remember that bile juice contains **zero** digestive enzymes. Its role is strictly biochemical emulsification of dietary lipids to increase surface area for pancreatic lipase action.

## 1. Human Alimentary Canal and Enzymatic Degradation

Digestion involves mechanical mastication and enzymatic hydrolysis converting non-absorbable macromolecules into absorbable micromolecules:

| Region | Secretion / Enzyme | Substrate | End Products | Regulatory Factor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Oral Cavity** | Ptyalin (Salivary Amylase) | Starch (~30%) | Maltose, Isomaltose | Stimulated by parasympathetic salivation |
| **Stomach** | Pepsin (from Pepsinogen via HCl) | Polypeptides | Proteoses, Peptones | Gastrin hormone stimulates parietal HCl release |
| **Stomach** | Rennin (in infants) | Casein (milk protein) | Paracasein | Precipitates milk in infant abomasum/stomach |
| **Duodenum** | Trypsin (activated by enterokinase) | Basic proteins | Dipeptides, Tripeptides | Cholecystokinin (CCK) and secretin stimulate release |
| **Duodenum** | Pancreatic Lipase (Steapsin) | Emulsified Triglycerides | Monoglycerides, Fatty Acids | Requires bile salts for substrate presentation |
| **Ileum** | Peptidases (Erepsin) | Dipeptides | Free Amino Acids | Absorbed via active transport into portal blood |
| **Ileum** | Disaccharidases (Maltase, Sucrase, Lactase) | Maltose, Sucrose, Lactose | Glucose, Fructose, Galactose | Absorbed into capillary network of intestinal villi |

## 2. Plant Nutrition: Macronutrients, Micronutrients, and Mineral Deficiencies

Plants absorb essential mineral elements from soil solution via root hair epidermal cells. Arnon and Stout (1939) established the criteria for essentiality of 17 chemical elements:

### Classification of Essential Elements
- **Macronutrients (Concentration > 10 mmol/kg dry matter):** Carbon (C), Hydrogen (H), Oxygen (O), Nitrogen (N), Phosphorus (P), Potassium (K), Calcium (Ca), Magnesium (Mg), Sulphur (S).
- **Micronutrients (Concentration < 10 mmol/kg dry matter):** Iron (Fe), Manganese (Mn), Zinc (Zn), Copper (Cu), Boron (B), Molybdenum (Mo), Chlorine (Cl), Nickel (Ni).

### Pathological Symptoms of Mineral Deficiency in Plants
- **Chlorosis (Loss of chlorophyll resulting in yellowing):** Deficiency of N, K, Mg, S, Fe, Mn, Zn, Mo.
- **Necrosis (Localized tissue death, particularly leaf margins):** Deficiency of Ca, Mg, Cu, K.
- **Inhibition of Cell Division:** Deficiency of N, K, S, Mo.
- **Khaira Disease of Rice:** Caused specifically by **Zinc (Zn)** deficiency; widely tested in Tamil Nadu agricultural exams.
- **Whiptail of Cauliflower:** Caused by **Molybdenum (Mo)** deficiency in acidic soils.
- **Heart Rot of Beetroot:** Caused by **Boron (B)** deficiency.

## 3. Human Metabolic Pathologies and Clinical Correlations

#### Worked Diagnostic Scenario: Protein-Energy Malnutrition (PEM)
TNPSC civil service examinations regularly evaluate the clinical distinction between Kwashiorkor and Marasmus:
- **Kwashiorkor:** Caused by severe protein deficiency despite adequate caloric intake. Symptoms include extensive peripheral edema, pot belly, thin limbs, fatty enlarged liver, and peeling dermatitis. It typically strikes children displaced from breastfeeding by a younger sibling.
- **Marasmus:** Caused by severe simultaneous deficiency of both protein and total calories. Symptoms include extreme muscle wasting, absence of subcutaneous fat, dry wrinkled skin, and an aged appearance. Critically, **edema is absent** in pure marasmic patients.

## 4. TNPSC Group 1 Previous Year Questions (PYQs)

**Question 1 (TNPSC Group 1 Prelims):** Pellagra is caused due to the severe deficiency of which vitamin?  
*(A) Thiamine (B) Pyridoxine (C) Niacin (D) Folic acid*  
**Answer:** (C) Niacin (Vitamin B3) (Explanation: Niacin deficiency impairs NAD/NADP biosynthesis, causing Pellagra characterized by the classical triad of dermatitis, diarrhea, and dementia).

**Question 2 (TNPSC Group 1 Prelims):** Which of the following elements is an essential constituent of the nitrogenase enzyme involved in biological nitrogen fixation?  
*(A) Zinc (B) Molybdenum (C) Boron (D) Copper*  
**Answer:** (B) Molybdenum (Explanation: Nitrogenase is a molybdenum-iron (Mo-Fe) protein complex that catalyzes the reduction of atmospheric N2 to ammonia in root nodule bacteroids).

**Question 3 (TNPSC Group 1 Prelims):** Which digestive hormone stimulates the secretion of pancreatic bicarbonate juice to neutralize gastric chyme?  
*(A) Gastrin (B) Secretin (C) Cholecystokinin (D) Enterocrinin*  
**Answer:** (B) Secretin (Explanation: Secretin was the first hormone ever discovered, identified by Bayliss and Starling in 1902. It stimulates the ductal cells of the pancreas to secrete an alkaline, bicarbonate-rich fluid).
