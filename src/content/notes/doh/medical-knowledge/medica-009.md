# Infectious Diseases

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Infectious (communicable) diseases result from pathogenic microorganisms — bacteria, viruses, fungi, protozoa, or helminths — that invade a susceptible host, replicate, and spread via a defined chain. The **chain of infection** has six links: infectious agent → reservoir → portal of exit → mode of transmission → portal of entry → susceptible host. Breaking any single link stops the spread.

- **Agent:** bacteria, viruses, fungi, protozoa, helminths, prions.
- **Reservoir:** human, animal (zoonosis), environmental (soil, water).
- **Portal of exit:** respiratory secretions, blood, faeces, skin.
- **Transmission:** contact, droplet, airborne, vehicle, vector.
- **Portal of entry:** matching mucosa, broken skin, parenteral.
- **Susceptible host:** unvaccinated, immunocompromised, extremes of age.

| Quantitative term | Formula | Unit / dimension |
|---|---|---|
| Attack rate | (new cases / population at risk) × 100 | percent |
| Case fatality rate (CFR) | (deaths / confirmed cases) × 100 | percent |
| Incidence rate | new cases / population at risk per unit time | per 100,000 per year |
| R0 (basic reproduction number) | secondary cases from one index case in a fully susceptible population | dimensionless |
| Re (effective reproduction) | Re = R0 × x (x = susceptible fraction) | dimensionless |

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** "**AIRES-CD**" for the chain of infection — **A**gent, **I**nfectious reservoir, **R**oute of exit, **E**ntry, **S**usceptible host, **C**onveyance of transmission, **D**isease. Break **any** link and you break the chain.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Epidemiology Metrics You Must Compute

Attack rate, CFR, incidence, and prevalence describe a disease from different angles. Attack rate is used in **closed outbreak** settings where everyone is observed over a short window — it has no time denominator. Incidence tracks **new cases over time** in an open or ongoing population. Prevalence depends on duration: the longer a case survives, the more it inflates prevalence, which is why **Prevalence = Incidence × average duration of disease** (a public-health identity worth memorising for MCQs).

R0 predicts outbreak potential only at the start, when everyone is susceptible. Once immunity or vaccination reduces the susceptible fraction **x**, the meaningful number becomes **Re = R0 × x**. An outbreak ends when **Re < 1**, not when R0 < 1.

| Metric | When to use | Numerator | Denominator | Common MCQ trap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Attack rate | Closed outbreak (school, cruise ship) | New cases in window | Population at risk | Adding a time denominator — it is time-free |
| Incidence rate | Open population, ongoing risk | New cases | Person-time at risk | Dividing by total population instead of at-risk |
| Prevalence (point) | Burden of disease on a given day | Existing + new cases | Total population | Calling it "incidence" |
| CFR | Lethality during an outbreak | Deaths | Confirmed cases | Confusing CFR with mortality rate (per total population) |

#### Levels of Prevention

Prevention has four layers tested repeatedly in DOH MCQs:

1. **Primordial** — entire-population measures before risk emerges (clean water policy, smoke-free laws).
2. **Primary** — prevent disease onset (vaccination, hand hygiene, condoms, vector control).
3. **Secondary** — early detection and prompt treatment (screening, contact tracing, Pap smears).
4. **Tertiary** — limit complications and disability (rehabilitation, DOTS for TB, insulin for diabetics).

#### UAE Notifiable Disease Reporting

Under DoH (Abu Dhabi Department of Health) public-health law, the clinician — not the lab — must notify **TB, viral hepatitis B/C, HIV, measles, meningococcal disease, MERS-CoV, and foodborne outbreaks** within **24 hours** of suspicion. Failure to notify is a licensing offence. Differentiate this from sentinel surveillance, where selected sites voluntarily report to track trends.

#### Standard vs Transmission-Based Precautions

**Standard precautions** apply to **every** patient: hand hygiene (WHO 5 Moments), PPE if blood/body fluid exposure is anticipated, safe sharps, sterile instruments. **Transmission-based precautions** are layered on top:

- **Contact** — MRSA, C. difficile, scabies (gown + gloves, private room).
- **Droplet** — influenza, pertussis, meningococcus (surgical mask within 1–2 m; no negative-pressure room required).
- **Airborne** — measles, varicella, disseminated zoster, pulmonary TB (**N95 respirator + negative-pressure room**).

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** During a 14-day outbreak of acute gastroenteritis aboard a cruise ship carrying 2,000 passengers, 240 develop symptoms. Twelve passengers are hospitalised, and 2 die. Calculate the attack rate and the case fatality rate, and identify the most appropriate precaution level for a patient with confirmed *Clostridioides difficile* diarrhoea.

#### Solution:

- **Attack rate** = (240 / 2,000) × 100 = **12%**. Denominator is the population at risk (all passengers), not person-time — this is a closed outbreak.
- **CFR** = (2 / 240) × 100 = **0.83%**. Denominator is confirmed cases, not the 2,000 passengers. Confusing these is the standard MCQ trap.
- **Precaution level** = **Contact precautions** (gown + gloves, private room, soap-and-water handwashing because alcohol gel does not kill *C. difficile* spores).

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students divide deaths by the total cruise-ship population and call it the "mortality rate." The CFR denominator is **confirmed cases**, not the whole population. Mortality rate uses the total population denominator. Also, do **not** place a *C. difficile* patient on airborne isolation — droplet/airborne are wrong layers.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Infectious vs Communicable — Why the Distinction Matters

Every communicable disease is infectious, but not every infectious disease is communicable. **Tetanus** is the classic testable example: *Clostridium tetani* causes severe clinical disease, but it is **not transmitted person-to-person** because the reservoir is soil and the portal of entry is a contaminated wound — there is no interhuman chain. Similarly, **Legionnaires' disease** is infectious but not person-to-person. MCQs exploit this distinction by offering "communicable" as the wrong umbrella term.

#### Colonisation vs Infection — A Frequent MCQ Pivot

**Colonisation** means a pathogen is present and replicating on or in the host **without tissue invasion or clinical disease**. **Infection** implies tissue invasion plus a host response (fever, leucocytosis, symptoms). Only symptomatic, confirmed infections enter incidence counts; colonisation contributes to prevalence but not to "new case" statistics. MRSA colonised nurses and asymptomatic typhoid carriers illustrate this — they are reservoirs, not cases.

#### AMR and the One Health Approach

Antimicrobial resistance is the dominant theme in DOH infection-control questions. The UAE National Antimicrobial Stewardship Programme (DoH/SEHA) enforces:

- Empirical antibiotic choice guided by local antibiograms, not by global textbook defaults.
- Culture before antibiotics whenever feasible, except in sepsis or meningitis.
- Documenting indication, duration, and review date on every prescription.
- One Health surveillance linking human, animal (livestock), and environmental isolates — particularly for ESBL *E. coli*, CRE, and *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*.

#### Advanced Traps and Exceptions

| Edge case | What students assume | Correct interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| R0 reported as 12 for measles | Outbreak is uncontrollable forever | R0 is population-specific; Re drops with rising MMR coverage |
| Negative pressure room for influenza | Airborne precaution is mandatory | Influenza is droplet — surgical mask within 1–2 m is sufficient |
| Reporting TB to DoH within 7 days | Following the "standard" reporting window | Notifiable diseases must be reported within **24 hours** of suspicion |
| Carrier of typhoid working in food handling | Carrier means actively ill | Asymptomatic carriers are reservoirs and must be excluded from high-risk work |
| Hand hygiene with alcohol gel for *C. difficile* | Alcohol-based handrub is universal | Alcohol does not kill spores — use **soap and water** |
| Incubation period = time from symptom onset | Always the correct definition | Incubation = time from **pathogen entry** to **first clinical signs**, not from symptom onset |

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. **Quantitative vignette:** A vaccine programme achieves 90% coverage in a population where R0 = 5. Compute Re and state whether the outbreak continues to grow, remains stable, or declines. *(Re = 5 × 0.10 = 0.5 < 1 → outbreak declines — herd immunity threshold reached.)*
2. **Public-health policy scenario:** A nurse is exposed to active pulmonary TB and has a negative baseline IGRA. Outline the post-exposure workflow required by DoH/HAAD — repeat IGRA at 8–10 weeks, symptom diary, chest imaging if symptomatic, and work restrictions until conversion status is known.

#### Common Mistakes to Eliminate in the Exam Hall

- Do not call attack rate "incidence" — they share a numerator but differ in denominator (at-risk population vs person-time).
- Do not list "airborne precaution" for influenza, pertussis, or meningococcus — they are **droplet**.
- Do not forget that **colonisation ≠ infection** when asked about "cases" for surveillance.
- Do not omit the **24-hour DoH reporting rule** for notifiable diseases; longer windows belong to other jurisdictions.
- Do not confuse R0 (fixed biological property) with Re (real-time, dependent on susceptibility).
