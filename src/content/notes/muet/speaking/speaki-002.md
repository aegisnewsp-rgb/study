# Task 1: The Individual Presentation

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your MUET Paper 3.

Task 1 of MUET Paper 3 (Speaking) is a **2-minute individual presentation** built from a two-part prompt. You receive **2 minutes of preparation time**, then speak for **2 minutes** alone, addressing **both points in the order given**. The examiner scores intelligibility, fluency, grammatical accuracy, lexical appropriacy, and the coherence of your argument.

- **Time budget:** 2 minutes prep + 2 minutes delivery (no extension).
- **Prompt structure:** Two related points — address point 1 first, then point 2.
- **Scoring focus:** Task fulfilment, coherence, elaboration (reasons + examples), grammatical range, lexical resource, fluency, intelligibility.
- **Band range:** Band 1 (very limited) to Band 5 (highly competent) per the official MUET band descriptors issued by Majlis Peperiksaan Malaysia (MPM).

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **PEEL + S** — **P**oint → **E**xplain → **E**xample → **L**ink, finished with **S**ignposting (Firstly, In addition, For example, To conclude). For each of the two prompt points, peel one layer of reasoning.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months of prep.

#### Task Format and Prompt Structure

The examiner hands you a single prompt card containing **two related discussion points** separated by a connector such as "and discuss the challenges" or "and suggest solutions." Task fulfilment requires that you **address both points in the printed sequence**. Reversing the order, merging them into one, or addressing only one point caps your content score regardless of how fluent the delivery sounds.

| Prompt element | Candidate response | Score impact |
|---|---|---|
| Point 1 (e.g. benefits) | Covered first with 30–40 s of development | Satisfies task fulfilment |
| Point 2 (e.g. challenges) | Covered second with 30–40 s of development | Satisfies task fulfilment |
| Connector (e.g. "and suggest…") | A concrete suggestion tied to Point 2 | Boosts elaboration score |
| Order swap | Point 2 before Point 1 | Loses task fulfilment marks |

#### The 2-Minute Preparation Window

Spend the prep time producing a **skeleton**, not a script. A reliable layout allocates roughly 20 s for a brief introduction, 40 s for Point 1, 40 s for Point 2, and 20 s for a conclusion. Jot only **5–8 cue words** per section on the cue card; full sentences invite verbatim reading, which examiners penalise under fluency and intelligibility.

#### Elaboration Technique: The 5 Ws and 1 H

Each prompt point must be developed, not merely stated. Apply **Who, What, When, Where, Why, How** to transform a bare claim into a developed argument. "Social media harms students" becomes a developed point once you specify **who** (teenagers aged 13–18), **what** (sleep disruption), **why** (blue-light exposure before bed), and **how** (school-wide phone-free policies in Finland).

#### Concept Comparison Matrix

| Feature | Strong Task 1 response | Weak Task 1 response |
|---|---|---|
| Prompt coverage | Both points, correct order | One point or reversed order |
| Elaboration | Reasons + concrete example + personal touch | Vague generalisation only |
| Signposting | "Firstly…", "In addition…", "For example…" | No transition markers |
| Delivery | A natural pace with brief glances at cue card | Read verbatim from cue card |
| Time use | Fills close to 2 minutes (≈1:45–2:00) | Stops at 60–90 seconds |
| Accuracy | Consistent tense, subject-verb agreement | Mixed tenses, dropped articles |

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** *You have 2 minutes to prepare and 2 minutes to deliver an individual presentation on the following prompt:*

> *"Many young people today prefer to shop online rather than visit physical stores. Discuss the reasons for this trend, and suggest ways in which retailers can attract customers back to physical stores."*

#### Solution:

1. **Plan during prep (≈20 s intro, 40 s Point 1, 40 s Point 2, 20 s conclusion).** Write cue words: *convenience, variety, price comparison, time-saving* for Point 1; *in-store experience, loyalty rewards, events, try-before-you-buy* for Point 2.
2. **Introduction (≈20 s):** State the trend with a restatement of the prompt: *"Today, more young Malaysians are choosing online shopping over brick-and-mortar stores, and it is worth examining why, and how physical retailers can respond."*
3. **Point 1 — Reasons (≈40 s):** *"Firstly, online platforms offer convenience. Shoppers can browse at midnight without travelling to a mall. Secondly, variety is wider; a single website lists thousands of products, whereas a physical store stocks a limited range. For example, a friend in Kuching can order a Korean skincare product without flying to Seoul."*
4. **Point 2 — Suggestions (≈40 s):** *"In addition, retailers can attract customers back by creating experiences that screens cannot replicate. For instance, bookshops now host author meet-and-greets, turning a routine purchase into a memorable event. Loyalty programmes, in-store cafés, and try-before-you-buy counters also make visits worthwhile."*
5. **Conclusion (≈20 s):** *"To conclude, while online shopping is driven by convenience and variety, physical retailers can compete by offering sensory experiences and community engagement."*
6. **Monitor:** Maintain tense consistency (present simple for trends), pause briefly between signposts, and glance at the cue card only for keywords.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** A frequent blunder is discussing **only the reasons** and forgetting the *suggest ways* clause. Because the prompt contains two points joined by "and suggest," covering just Point 1 — even beautifully — leaves the task half-fulfilled and caps the content band.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Band-by-Band Differentiation of Task 1

MPM's official band descriptors separate candidates on five scales: intelligibility, fluency, grammatical range and accuracy, lexical resource, and task fulfilment/coherence. Strong Task 1 work typically lands at **Band 4 (Competent)** or **Band 5 (Highly Competent)**, where sustained monologue runs close to the full 2 minutes with natural rhythm, varied sentence structures, and topic-specific vocabulary. Band 3 work fills the time but relies on shorter utterances and repetitive connectors. Band 2 work stops early, leans on memorised chunks, and shows frequent errors that strain intelligibility.

#### Edge Cases and Boundary Conditions

- **Two-point prompts with a comparison clause** ("compare A and B") require explicit comparison language (*whereas, in contrast, similarly*) rather than two separate descriptions.
- **Prompts with an evaluative clause** ("do you agree?") require a stated position, not a neutral overview.
- **Prompts with hypothetical framing** ("imagine…") accept conditional structures (*If students were given…*); candidates who ignore the hypothetical lose elaboration marks.
- **Cue-card shorthand temptation:** writing full sentences pushes candidates into reading; examiners recognise a head-down, monotone delivery and mark it down for fluency and intelligibility even when grammar is accurate.

#### Advanced Traps and Exceptions

1. **Over-elaborating Point 1 and starving Point 2** — common when Point 1 is familiar; aim for a 45 s / 45 s split to keep both points evenly developed.
2. **Cycling through examples without reasons** — three examples of online shopping without explaining *why* they matter collapses into listing, which scores low on coherence.
3. **Memorised "model answers" mismatched to the prompt** — examiners detect recycled scripts; they do not fit the printed points and cost both content and coherence marks.
4. **Filler-heavy delivery** — frequent *um*, *uh*, *like* from lack of rehearsal signals low fluency; practising with a 2-minute timer at home reduces filler dramatically.
5. **Mono-register intonation** — flat pitch on every sentence lowers intelligibility even when pronunciation is accurate; vary pitch on key words.
6. **Ignoring the conclusion** — a 5-second "That's all" forfeits coherence marks; a brief restatement of both points closes the argument.

#### Connections to Adjacent Topics

Task 1 sets the register for **Task 2: The Discussion** (group interaction). Signposting habits built here — initiating, agreeing, disagreeing politely — transfer directly. Vocabulary drilled on Task 1 (e.g. education, technology, environment themes drawn from MUET past-year prompts) recurs in the discussion. Confidence built during the 2-minute solo segment also reduces turn-taking anxiety in Task 2.

#### Practice Prompts

1. *Time yourself speaking for 2 minutes on: "Many university students in Malaysia now work part-time jobs. Discuss the reasons, and suggest how employers can better support these students."* Record, then check whether both points are addressed in order and whether signposting is audible.
2. *Prepare in 2 minutes, then deliver: "More tourists are choosing eco-friendly destinations. Discuss the benefits of this trend for local communities, and suggest ways the tourism industry can promote sustainable travel."* Focus on tense consistency and concrete examples.

> 💡 **High-Yield Revision Hook:** Two points, two minutes, in order — **2-2-2** is the MUET mantra. Drill with a phone timer; aim for a 20 / 40 / 40 / 20 split, and rehearse signposting until it sounds automatic.
