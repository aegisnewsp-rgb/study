# GST Framework & Supply

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**GST** is a comprehensive, destination-based, multi-stage indirect tax levied concurrently by Centre and States on the taxable event called **"Supply"** of goods or services under the **CGST Act, 2017, IGST Act, 2017, and SGST/UTGST Acts**.

- **Dual structure:** **CGST + SGST/UTGST** on **intra-state** supply; **IGST** on **inter-state** supply. For a slab rate of 18%, intra-state split = 9% CGST + 9% SGST; IGST = 18%.
- **Three-pronged test for Supply (Section 7):** (i) goods or services, (ii) for **consideration**, (iii) in the course or furtherance of **business**. **Schedule I** items are treated as supply **without** consideration (e.g., stock transfers between distinct persons).
- **Composite Supply** → tax at the **principal supply** rate. **Mixed Supply** → tax at the **highest rate** among components.
- **GST payable** = Output tax − **Input Tax Credit (ITC)** under Section 16, subject to Section 17(5) blocked credits.
- **Reverse Charge (Section 9(3) / 9(4))**: recipient pays tax instead of supplier for notified categories (e.g., GTA, advocate fees, renting from unregistered person).
- **Time of supply** is the **earlier of** invoice date (or last date to issue) and payment/advance date — Section 12 for goods, Section 13 for services.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Constitutional and Statutory Basis

Article **246A** empowers Centre and States to levy GST concurrently, while Article **279A** establishes the **GST Council** (Chair: Union Finance Minister) for recommendations on rates, exemptions, and procedural changes. **Four statutes** operate in parallel: CGST, IGST, and each State's SGST Act.

#### The Taxable Event: "Supply"

Post-GST, the trigger for levy is **"Supply"** defined in **Section 7(1)(a)** of the CGST Act. The three-pronged test must be satisfied unless **Schedule I** (supplies without consideration, e.g., permanent transfer/lease of business assets, stock transfers between distinct persons) or **Schedule II** (deemed supplies, e.g., treatment of works contract as a service) applies. **Schedule III** lists activities that are **neither** supply of goods **nor** services — e.g., funeral services and sale of land/building.

#### Dual GST and Tax Split

| Nature of Supply | Tax Levied | Split Example (18% slab) |
|---|---|---|
| Intra-state (within same state) | CGST + SGST | 9% + 9% |
| Inter-state (across states) | IGST | 18% |
| Union Territory (without legislature) | CGST + UTGST | 9% + 9% |

#### Composite vs Mixed Supply

A **Composite Supply** (Section 2(30)) has naturally bundled goods/services where one supply is principal — valued and taxed at the principal supply's rate (e.g., a meal thali). A **Mixed Supply** (Section 2(74)) has independent supplies bundled at the customer's choice — taxed at the **highest rate** among components (e.g., a gift hamper chosen item-by-item).

#### Transaction Value (Section 15)

**Transaction Value = Price actually paid/payable** (in money or money's worth) for the supply, **excluding GST**, provided: (a) it is the **sole consideration**, (b) supplier and recipient are **not related**, and (c) price is the sole consideration. Incidental expenses, subsidies linked to price, and interest/late fees are **included**; pre-supply discounts and pure time-value discounts are **excluded**.

#### Time of Supply

The liability crystallises at the **earlier of**: (i) date of invoice / last date to issue, or (ii) date of advance / payment. **Section 12** governs goods; **Section 13** governs services. **Reverse charge** payments are timed separately under Section 13(3).

#### Input Tax Credit (ITC)

ITC is the set-off of GST paid on inward supplies against output liability. **Section 16(2)** conditions: possession of **tax invoice/debit note**, **receipt of goods/services**, tax actually **paid to government** (matched under Section 43A), and **filing of return**. **Section 17(5)** blocks ITC on motor vehicles, food & beverages, club membership, personal consumption, and CSR — unless mandated by law.

#### Registration and Place of Supply

Threshold: **₹40 lakh** for goods, **₹20 lakh** for special-category states; services typically ₹20 lakh. **Inter-state suppliers** and **casual taxable persons** have no threshold. **Place of supply of goods** = location of goods at delivery (Section 10, IGST Act); **services** generally = location of recipient (Section 12, IGST Act), with specific overrides for restaurant services, immovable property, and telecom.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases in Deemed Supply

**Schedule I treats the following as supply *without* consideration** and is heavily tested: permanent transfer/lease of business assets, supply between **distinct persons** (different GSTINs under the same PAN), and supply of goods by principal to agent. Students frequently miss the **stock transfer** treatment — although no invoice value flows, the movement is recorded as a supply in Form GSTR-1 of the dispatching unit.

#### Composite vs Mixed — Typical Trap

A hotel offering a **₹5,000 corporate package** (room + breakfast + airport pickup + conference hall) is a **Composite Supply** because the room is the principal supply and others are ancillary. A **Diwali gift box of chocolates and a USB drive** (chosen independently by the buyer) is a **Mixed Supply** and taxed at the rate applicable to the highest-rated component. Misclassification shifts the rate and is a favourite MCQ trap.

#### Reverse Charge Nuances

Under **Section 9(3)**, specific notified categories (legal services by advocate, GTA, renting from unregistered person, services from outside India by OIDAR) shift liability to the recipient. Under **Section 9(4)**, all intra-state supplies from an **unregistered** to a **registered** person attract reverse charge, subject to notifications. The recipient can claim ITC of tax paid under reverse charge — but only after payment and matching on the portal.

#### Pure Agent Exclusion

Section 15 treats disbursements by a **pure agent** (incurred as agent of principal, separately shown in invoice, supported by vouchers, not retained as profit) as **excluded from value**. Common pitfalls: reimbursements for postage, customs duty, or travel booked on behalf of clients.

#### Worked Example

> **Q (inter-state, 18% slab):** XYZ Ltd. in Maharashtra sells goods to ABC Pvt. Ltd. in Karnataka for ₹1,00,000 plus a separately-invoiced packing charge of ₹5,000. The goods travel directly from Mumbai to Bangalore.

**Answer:** Supply is **inter-state** → **IGST** applies. **Taxable value** (Section 15) = ₹1,00,000 + ₹5,000 (incidental to supply) = **₹1,05,000**. IGST @ 18% = **₹18,900**. No CGST/SGST split. If ABC had been in Maharashtra instead, IGST would be **zero**; the supply would attract **9% CGST + 9% SGST = ₹18,900** total.

#### Practice Prompts

1. **Time of supply:** A service provider raises an invoice on 10 February 2026 for services received on 5 February; the customer pays in full on 25 January 2026. Identify the time of supply under Section 13 and explain whether the advance triggers liability before invoice.
2. **Composite or Mixed?** A restaurant offers a fixed-price menu (soup, main course, dessert) at ₹600. Apply Section 2(30) vs 2(74), determine the correct rate, and justify with the dominant-purpose test.
