# International Organisations & Affairs

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**International organisations** are treaty-based institutions created by states to cooperate on cross-border problems such as security, trade, finance, and humanitarian relief. **International affairs** is the study of how states, intergovernmental bodies, and non-state actors interact through diplomacy, law, sanctions, and multilateral negotiation.

SNAP tests this topic as factual recall plus light interpretation, drawing roughly 2–3 questions from a 3% General Knowledge slice.

- **UN** (founded 1945, HQ New York) — peace, development, humanitarian work via the General Assembly, Security Council, ECOSOC, Secretariat, and ICJ.
- **Bretton Woods twins** — IMF (monetary stability, HQ Washington D.C.) and World Bank (development finance, HQ Washington D.C.); **WTO** (trade rules, HQ Geneva) sits separately.
- **Regional bodies to recognise** — NATO (Brussels), EU (Brussels), ASEAN (Jakarta), African Union (Addis Ababa), BRICS, G20.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Mandates of Major Global Bodies

| Organisation | Mandate | Headquarters | Key Feature |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| United Nations | Peace, development, human rights, international law | New York, USA | 193 member states; Security Council is the only binding-decision body |
| IMF | Monetary cooperation, exchange-rate stability, balance-of-payments support | Washington D.C., USA | Quota-based voting; SDR as supplementary reserve asset |
| World Bank | Long-term development finance and policy advice | Washington D.C., USA | IBRD + IDA lending to low- and middle-income countries |
| WTO | Trade rules, negotiation forum, dispute settlement | Geneva, Switzerland | Successor to GATT (1995); consensus-driven rounds |
| ICJ | Settles state disputes, advisory opinions | The Hague, Netherlands | Principal judicial organ of the UN |
| NATO | Collective defence under Article 5 | Brussels, Belgium | 32 members as of 2024; political-military alliance |

#### How Decision-Making Differs

- **Security Council** binding decisions need 9 affirmative votes and no veto from the **P5** (China, France, Russia, UK, USA); Article 41 allows non-military measures, Article 51 covers self-defence.
- **General Assembly** passes recommendations only, but the "Uniting for Peace" procedure can convene an Emergency Special Session when the Council is blocked.
- **WTO** rulings under the Dispute Settlement Mechanism can authorise retaliatory tariffs once authorised by the Dispute Settlement Body.
- **IMF** lending is tied to conditionality (Structural Adjustment / Extended Fund Facility programmes).

#### Common SNAP-Style Traps

- Treating IMF and World Bank as the same institution — IMF handles short-term liquidity, the World Bank funds long-term projects.
- Assuming G20 is a permanent secretariat body — it is a forum of 19 countries plus the EU, chaired rotationally.
- Forgetting that NATO's Article 5 has only been invoked once, after 9/11.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Typology of International Organisations

1. **Universal political** — UN and its agencies (WHO, UNESCO, UNICEF, ILO).
2. **Universal economic** — IMF, World Bank Group, WTO.
3. **Regional security/political** — NATO, OSCE, CSTO, African Union, Arab League.
4. **Regional economic integration** — EU (supranational), ASEAN (intergovernmental), MERCOSUR, GCC.
5. **Club groupings** — G7, G20, BRICS, BRICS+, SCO, G77, OECD.

The **supranational vs. intergovernmental** distinction matters: the EU can pass law that binds member states directly, whereas ASEAN operates by consensus and non-interference.

#### Concepts That Recur in Questions

- **Sovereignty** — the legal basis for state equality in the UN system under Article 2(1).
- **Soft power** — influence through culture, values, and institutions rather than coercion.
- **Sanctions** — UN Security Council sanctions are binding; unilateral sanctions by single states are not.
- **Peacekeeping vs. peace enforcement** — blue-helmet operations require host-state consent; enforcement under Chapter VII is rarely authorised.
- **Humanitarian intervention** — contested when it bypasses the Security Council, as in the 1999 Kosovo campaign.

#### Worked SNAP-Style Prompts

1. *Match the headquarters: Brussels, Geneva, The Hague, Washington D.C., New York — to NATO, WTO, ICJ, IMF, UN.*
2. *Explain in two lines why the IMF, World Bank, and WTO are distinct despite all dealing with cross-border economic issues.*

#### High-Yield Current-Affairs Hooks

- BRICS expansion (Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, UAE, Saudi Arabia invited 2023–24) signals a push for non-Western financial architecture.
- WTO Appellate Body dispute paralysis since 2019; the Multi-Party Interim Appeal Arbitration Arrangement (MPIA) is the workaround.
- UN Security Council reform debates: G4 nations (Brazil, Germany, India, Japan) bid for permanent seats.
