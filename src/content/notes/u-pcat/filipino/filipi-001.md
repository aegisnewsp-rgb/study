# Wastong Gamit ng mga Salita (Correct Word Usage)

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Wastong Gamit ng mga Salita** tests your ability to pick the precise Filipino word for a given sentence context. UPCAT items in most keys ask you to choose the right word among near-synonyms, spot the wrongly spelled form, or pick the formal equivalent of a colloquial phrase. The exam carries roughly 3% of the total UPCAT score and falls under the Filipino subtest's vocabulary and language-awareness cluster.

- **Magkamukha (homophones)** like *kuhà* (to fetch) and *kuha* (a noun, possession) change meaning with stress.
- **Magkasingkahulugan (synonyms)** such as *maganda*, *marikit*, and *maalindog* cannot always be swapped because each carries a different **konotasyon** (connotation).
- **Ng vs. Nang** is the most-tested pair: *ng* marks possession or the object marker, while *nang* is an adverb of time or degree.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core Categories of Word-Choice Errors

UPCAT questions cluster around four recurring decision points: choosing between **magkamukha**, picking among **magkasingkahulugan** based on connotation, correcting spelling of borrowed words, and matching register (pormal, di-pormal, lalawiganin). Mastering each category means recognising why two words cannot be substituted even when a thesaurus lists them as equivalents.

#### Distinguishing Near-Synonyms by Connotation

| Salita | Karaniwang Konteksto | Konotasyon |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *maganda* | Pangkalahatang papuri sa anyo | Neutral, everyday |
| *marikit* | Pagsasalaysay, panitikan | Poetic, refined |
| *maalindog* | Tula, retorika | Highly formal, literary |
| *nakakaakit* | Kasalukuyang paglalarawan | Suggestive, attractive |

A sentence about a town festival can sound flat if you replace *marikit* with *maganda*, and a poem loses tone with *nakakaakit*.

#### High-Frequency Spelling and Function-Word Traps

- **Siyempre**, not *syempre* — Filipino follows Spanish-derived spelling without the *y*.
- **Dahil sa** introduces a noun phrase (*dahil sa ulan*); **dahil** introduces a clause (*dahil umulan*).
- **Ng** marks possession or is a preposition; **nang** is an adverb of manner, time, or degree.
- **Po at Opo** signal respect in direct address but must be dropped in formal written correspondence.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Register Matching Across Situations

The deepest UPCAT items test **rehistro ng wika**: knowing that *sino* fits casual speech while *kanino* suits a formal letter, or that *kuya* and *manong* belong to address terms rather than neutral nouns. Each setting — liham, talumpati, aklat-aralin, text message — has its own acceptable vocabulary layer, and the correct answer is the one matching the implied audience.

> Tip: When two options seem interchangeable, ask "Sino ang mambabasa? Pormal ba o kasintigan?" The answer in most keys reveals which register wins.

#### Common Mistakes Students Lose Points On

| Mali | Tama | Bakit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Kuhà ko ang libro.* | *Kuha ko ang libro.* | Stress on the verb shifts it from noun to verb. |
| *Dahil sa mahal niya ako.* | *Dahil mahal niya ako.* | After *dahil*, expect a full clause, not a noun. |
| *Nang bata pa ako,...* | *Noong bata pa ako,...* | *Nang* never marks past-time "when"; *noong* does. |
| *Syempre, ganoon nga.* | *Siyempre, ganoon nga.* | Filipino spelling retains *iy*, not *y*. |

#### Practice Prompts

1. Identify whether *marikit* or *maganda* fits better in a news report describing a new public park, and justify using **denotasyon** vs. **konotasyon**.
2. Rewrite the colloquial sentence *"Kuya, kuha mo nga 'yung gamit ko, please?"* into a formal request suitable for an official letter, preserving politeness without *po/opo*.
