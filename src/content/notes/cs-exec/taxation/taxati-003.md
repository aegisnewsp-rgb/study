# GST Input Tax Credit & Compliance

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Input Tax Credit (ITC)** under Section 16 of the CGST Act, 2017 lets a registered taxable person set off the GST paid on inward supplies against his outward tax liability. Chapter V (Sections 16–21) plus Rules 36, 39, 42, 43 of the CGST Rules, 2017 govern the mechanics; Sections 35–39 cover the **compliance** obligations (registration, returns GSTR-1/3B/9/9C, e-way bills, accounts).

- **Section 16(4) deadline:** ITC must be claimed by **30 November of the next FY or date of filing GSTR-9**, whichever is earlier.
- **Rule 36(4) cap:** Provisional ITC cannot exceed the amount auto-populated in **GSTR-2B**.
- **Rule 42 reversal formula:** Reversal = Eligible ITC × (Exempt turnover ÷ Total turnover).
- **Section 50 interest:** Wrong ITC attracts **18% p.a.** interest compounded monthly.
- **GSTR-9C threshold:** Mandatory only when aggregate turnover **exceeds ₹5 crore** (Notification 33/2021).

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** **"I-F-E-R"** to recall the four conditions of Section 16(2) — **I**nvoice/debit note in hand, **F**actual receipt of goods/services, tax paid by supplier in **E**xchequer's cash ledger, supplier **R**eturn filed.

| Term | Section / Rule | One-line meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ITC | Section 16 | Credit of tax on inputs against output tax |
| Blocked Credit | Section 17(5) | Specific credits expressly disallowed |
| ISD | Section 20 | Distributes common credit to branches |
| Annual Return | Section 44 | GSTR-9; GSTR-9C if turnover > ₹5 cr |
| E-Way Bill | Rule 138 | Mandatory above ₹50,000 consignment value |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Statutory Conditions for Claiming ITC

Section 16(2) imposes a conjunctive four-part test. Failure of any one limb dis-entitles the recipient, even if tax has been paid. The conditions are: (i) possession of a **tax invoice, debit note, or ISD invoice**; (ii) **receipt of goods or services** (except Schedule III supplies); (iii) tax actually remitted to the Government by the supplier **in cash or through the Input Service Distributor**; and (iv) the supplier has **filed his return** and discharged the tax. Rule 36(4) operationalises limb (iv) by mandating that ITC claimed in GSTR-3B cannot exceed the figure auto-generated in GSTR-2B, a feature introduced from 1 January 2021.

#### Blocked vs Eligible Credit Matrix

| Item under Section 17(5) | ITC allowed? | Exam nuance |
|---|---|---|
| Motor vehicles, except specified | **No** | Car used by driving school, GTA, or for further supply of vehicles is permitted |
| Food & beverages, outdoor catering, beauty treatment, health services, cosmetic surgery | **No** | Applies even if TDS u/s 194R deducted |
| Club membership, health & life insurance | **No** | Insur­ance of goods in transit is allowed |
| Rent-a-cab / health insurance where TDS not deducted | **No** | Only the TDS-non-deduction clause triggers block |
| Goods lost, stolen, destroyed, written off | **No** | Insurance recovery does not re-allow credit |
| Tax paid under Section 74 (fraud) | **No** | Personal liability of supplier, not recipient |
| Gifts and free samples | **No** | Disposal without consideration under Schedule I treated as supply |

#### Pro-Rata Reversal under Rules 42 and 43

Where a taxable person effects both **taxable and exempt supplies**, Rule 42 mandates monthly reversal for inputs and input services using the formula **Reversal = Eligible ITC × (Exempt turnover ÷ Total turnover)**. Rule 43 applies a similar logic to **capital goods** spread over five years on a pro-rata basis (excluding the residual 5%). The reversed amount must be added to output liability in GSTR-3B, and an annual reconciliation is required in GSTR-9 to true-up actuals.

#### Key Procedural Numbers

1. **Registration threshold:** ₹40 lakh for goods; ₹20 lakh for services (₹10 lakh in special-category states).
2. **ITC time-limit:** Earlier of 30 November of next FY or filing of GSTR-9 — Section 16(4).
3. **E-Way Bill:** Mandatory above ₹50,000; validity = 1 day per 100 km plus additional time for hilly states.
4. **Penalty under Section 74:** Tax wrongfully availed + interest @ 18% p.a. compounded monthly.
5. **Composition levy:** Section 10(4) prohibits ITC pass-through and reverses opening stock credit when migrating to normal scheme.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** M/s Prism Pvt. Ltd. (Kerala, ₹80 lakh taxable turnover) purchased raw materials of ₹22,00,000 plus GST @ 18% in October 2024. The supplier filed GSTR-1 on 11 November 2024 but paid tax on 15 January 2025. Prism's GSTR-3B for October 2024 was filed on 18 December 2024, claiming ₹3,96,000 ITC. Prism also has exempt turnover of ₹15 lakh against total turnover of ₹1.10 crore for FY 2024-25, with eligible common ITC of ₹2,20,000. Compute: (i) eligibility of the ₹3,96,000 ITC, and (ii) monthly reversal under Rule 42.

#### Solution:
(i) **Section 16(2) test** — Invoice: held. Receipt: goods received in October. Tax paid by supplier: Yes, on 15 January 2025 (cash ledger credited). Supplier return filed: Yes, 11 November 2024. All four conditions satisfied. ITC of **₹3,96,000 is eligible**. However, if Prism had claimed it in **December 2024 GSTR-3B** (rather than October) and the **annual return for FY 2024-25 is filed before 30 November 2025**, the claim survives; otherwise it is hit by the **30 November 2025 cut-off** under Section 16(4).

(ii) **Rule 42 reversal** = ₹2,20,000 × (₹15,00,000 ÷ ₹1,10,00,000) = ₹2,20,000 × 0.1364 = **₹30,000** (rounded). This amount is added to October 2025 output liability and may be reclaimed in any subsequent month within the same FY if exempt turnover ratio falls.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students frequently allow ITC when the **supplier has filed the return but not paid the tax** in cash before the recipient's filing date. Under Section 16(2)(c), the condition is that tax must have been **actually paid to the Government**, not merely reflected in the supplier's return — remember to check the GSTR-2B cash ledger column, not just the invoice entry.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Advanced Traps and Edge Cases

1. **Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM) under Section 9(3)/(4):** ITC on RCM supplies arises only in the return of the month in which the tax is paid through the cash ledger under RCM; mismatch between GSTR-3B Table 3.1(d) and 4 (ITC) is a frequent audit query.
2. **ISD mechanics:** Credit is distributed through Form GSTR-6 (ISD return) by 13th of the next month, in the ratio of turnover of recipients (Rule 39). The recipient reflects it in GSTR-3B Table 4 only after the ISD invoice appears in his GSTR-2B.
3. **Capital goods under Section 18(1)(a):** A person taking voluntary registration is entitled to credit on capital goods held on stock on the day preceding registration, subject to a **five-year staggered reversal** if used partly for exempt supplies.
4. **Job-work under Section 19 + Rule 45:** Principal can take ITC on inputs sent to job-worker provided goods are received back within **one year (three years for capital goods)**; failure triggers reversal with interest.
5. **GSTR-9C self-certification threshold:** Notification 33/2021 (24 November 2021) revised the earlier ₹2 crore limit to **₹5 crore**, but this is a *relaxation*, not a removal — every taxpayer above ₹5 crore must self-certify reconciliation between books and declared values.
6. **Section 74 penalty regime:** Applies only where fraud, wilful misstatement, or suppression is established; Section 73 governs non-fraud cases with a **general 30-month adjudication window** from the due date of return.
7. **Composition taxpayer migration:** On switching to the normal scheme, ITC on opening stock (goods) is admissible under Section 18(1)(c) but credit on capital goods is restricted to the residual life; Section 10(4) extinguishes any unutilised credit on the date of cancellation.

#### Connections to Adjacent Topics

- **Customs Duty and GST:** Customs duty paid on imports is included in the value for GST under Section 15, and integrated GST paid at customs constitutes eligible ITC.
- **Income Tax Act, 1961:** Section 194R TDS on gifts/perquisia overlaps with GST Section 17(5)(b) blocked credit; both must be tracked separately.
- **Anti-profiteering under Section 171:** Any reduction in GST rate must be passed on to consumers — ITC availed but not passed on invites proceedings under the Anti-Profiteering Authority (though the Authority itself has been sunset from 1 December 2022, contractual liability continues).

#### Practice Prompts

1. ABC Ltd. (Maharashtra) availed ₹14 lakh ITC in March 2024 but did not include a related outward supply of ₹40 lakh (taxable) in GSTR-1 for the same month due to a software error, which was later corrected in September 2024. Examine whether the ITC remains valid and the implications under Section 73 versus Section 74.
2. Determine the Rule 43 annual reversal for capital goods of ₹50,00,000 acquired in FY 2023-24, with exempt turnover of ₹40 lakh and total turnover of ₹3.6 crore in FY 2024-25, given that the opening WDV at the start of FY 2024-25 was ₹40,00,000 (after one year of reversal).
