# VIII. Energy and Use of Energy (Elective) — HKDSE Physics Notes

This topic covers Elective Topic VIII of the HKDSE Physics Elective Part. Candidates sit any two of the four electives on Paper 2. The topic addresses electricity at home (domestic wiring, three-pin plugs, energy-saving appliances); energy efficiency in building and transportation (insulation, heat transfer, combustion efficiency); and renewable and non-renewable energy sources (solar, wind, tidal, geothermal, nuclear, fossil fuels); and energy policy and the environment.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Domestic wiring**: ring main circuit (live, neutral, earth); fuses and circuit breakers for safety; consumer unit (main switch, RCD, MCBs).
- **Three-pin plug**: live (brown), neutral (blue), earth (green/yellow). The earth pin is longer and thicker; it connects first and disconnects last.
- **Energy consumption**: P = IV. Energy used = P × t. Units: kWh (kilowatt-hour).
- **Energy efficiency**: ratio of useful output to total input. Energy-efficient appliances (LED lighting, inverter air conditioners, heat-pump water heaters) reduce energy consumption.
- **Insulation**: reduces heat transfer by conduction and convection. Materials with low thermal conductivity (fibreglass, polyurethane foam). Double-glazed windows trap a layer of air.
- **Heat transfer in buildings**: U-value measures the rate of heat transfer through a building element; lower U-value means better insulation.
- **Combustion efficiency**: ratio of heat released to total chemical energy in the fuel. Improved by preheating air, using oxygen-enriched air, recovering waste heat.
- **Renewable energy sources**: solar (photovoltaic and solar thermal), wind (turbines), tidal (barrages and stream generators), wave, hydroelectric, geothermal, biomass.
- **Non-renewable energy sources**: coal, oil, natural gas, nuclear (uranium).
- **Energy storage**: pumped hydro, batteries (lithium-ion, redox flow), compressed air, thermal storage, hydrogen.
- **Environmental impact**: greenhouse gas emissions from fossil fuels (climate change); nuclear waste; habitat impact of large hydro and wind farms; visual and noise impact.

#### Examiner traps

- Treating "energy efficiency" as the same as "energy saving" — efficiency is the ratio of useful output to total input; saving is the absolute reduction in energy used.
- Confusing renewable with low-carbon — some renewables are not low-carbon (e.g. biomass with carbon capture), and some low-carbon sources are not renewable (e.g. nuclear).

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Domestic electricity

**Three-pin plug (UK/HK standard)**: live, neutral, earth. The fuse (typically 13A for a ring-main plug) protects the appliance cable from overcurrent.

**Ring main**: a ring circuit with parallel branches. Each branch has its own fuse; the total current is shared across the ring. This allows thinner cable to be used while still delivering high total power.

**Safety devices**:

- **Fuse**: a thin wire that melts when the current exceeds the rated value.
- **Circuit breaker (MCB)**: an electromagnetic or thermal-magnetic switch that trips when the current exceeds the rated value. Can be reset.
- **RCD (residual current device)**: detects imbalance between live and neutral currents (indicating a leakage to earth) and trips in ~30 ms. Used for additional safety against electric shock.

**Power and energy**: P = IV (instantaneous power). Energy E = ∫P dt. For constant power, E = P × t. Units: kWh (kilowatt-hour) for electricity bills.

#### Energy efficiency in buildings

**Insulation**:

- **Wall insulation**: cavity wall insulation (foam or mineral wool), external insulation, internal insulation.
- **Roof insulation**: glass wool, cellulose, polyurethane foam.
- **Floor insulation**: rigid foam boards.
- **Window insulation**: double glazing, low-E glass, insulated frames.
- **Draught-proofing**: seals around doors and windows.

**U-value (thermal transmittance)**: the rate of heat transfer through a building element (W/m²K). Lower is better. Typical values: cavity wall (1.5), insulated wall (0.3), double-glazed window (1.2), well-insulated wall (0.15).

**Heat gain and loss**: in summer, heat gain through windows, walls and roof. In winter, heat loss through windows, walls, roof and floors. Energy-efficient design balances orientation, insulation, thermal mass and shading.

#### Transportation energy efficiency

**Vehicle efficiency**: fuel consumption (L/100 km or mpg); CO₂ emissions (g/km). Improvements: hybrid and electric drivetrains, regenerative braking, low rolling resistance tyres, aerodynamic design, lightweight materials.

**Combustion efficiency**: ratio of heat released to total chemical energy in the fuel. Most internal combustion engines have thermal efficiencies of 20-40%. Modern combined-cycle gas turbines reach 60%.

#### Energy sources

**Renewable**:

- **Solar photovoltaic (PV)**: converts sunlight directly into electricity using the photovoltaic effect. Peak output ~1 kW/m² at noon in Hong Kong. Efficiency of commercial modules ~ 20%.
- **Solar thermal**: collects solar energy as heat. Used for water heating (solar hot water panels are common in Hong Kong).
- **Wind**: turbines convert kinetic energy of wind into electricity. Capacity factor ~ 25-40% onshore, ~ 50% offshore.
- **Hydroelectric**: converts the gravitational potential energy of water into electricity. Large dams (e.g. Three Gorges) and small run-of-river schemes.
- **Tidal**: uses the rise and fall of tides. Tidal barrage (Sihwa Lake in South Korea is the largest).
- **Geothermal**: uses heat from the Earth's interior. Most productive in tectonically active regions (Iceland, New Zealand, the Philippines).
- **Biomass**: organic material burned for energy, or converted to biofuels (ethanol, biodiesel).

**Non-renewable**:

- **Coal**: abundant, cheap, high CO₂ emissions.
- **Oil**: liquid, easy to transport, moderate CO₂ emissions.
- **Natural gas**: cleaner than coal or oil (less CO₂ per per unit air pollution, but methane leakage is a concern.
- **Nuclear**: fission of U-235 or Pu-239. Very low CO₂ during operation, but produces long-lived radioactive waste.

#### Energy storage

- **Pumped hydro**: water pumped from a lower reservoir to an upper reservoir when excess electricity is available; released through turbines when electricity is needed. Largest form of grid storage.
- **Batteries**: lithium-ion (most common for portable and EV applications); redox flow (large-scale stationary storage); sodium-sulfur.
- **Compressed air**: compressed in caverns when excess electricity is available; expanded through turbines when needed.
- **Thermal storage**: stored as sensible heat (water, molten salts) or latent heat (phase-change materials).
- **Hydrogen**: produced by electrolysis when excess electricity is available; stored as compressed gas or liquid; used in fuel cells or burned in turbines.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-topic links

- **IV. Electricity and Magnetism**: power transmission, transformers.
- **I. Heat and Gases**: thermodynamics of engines.
- **V. Radioactivity and Nuclear Energy**: nuclear power generation.

#### Exam technique

- **For domestic electricity questions**: identify live/neutral/earth; calculate power, energy and cost.
- **For insulation questions**: use U-value; compare insulation options.
- **For energy source questions**: cite the pros and cons; discuss environmental impact.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Solar panels work on cloudy days." They do, but at reduced output (~ 10-30% of peak).
- "Nuclear power produces no CO₂." Nuclear power produces minimal CO₂ during operation, but lifecycle emissions (construction, fuel processing, waste management) are non-zero.
- "Renewable energy is always cheaper." The levelised cost of electricity (LCOE) varies by source, location and storage requirements.
