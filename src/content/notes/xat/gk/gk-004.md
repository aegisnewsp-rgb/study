# Geography of India and the World

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Geography of India and the World tests factual recall of physiography, climate, soils, rivers, and major world physical features for XAT's General Knowledge section. Expect 1–3 questions, usually MCQs linking a place to a feature (river, soil, mountain, current).

- **India has 6 physiographic divisions**: Northern Mountains, Northern Plains, Peninsular Plateau, Indian Desert, Coastal Plains, Islands.
- **Monsoon timing**: Southwest (June–Sept, Arabian Sea branch + Bay of Bengal branch); Northeast/Retreating (Oct–Dec); Western Disturbances bring winter rain to NW India.
- **Highest peaks**: Mt. Everest (8,848.86 m, highest by elevation); Mauna Kea (tallest from oceanic base).
- **Indian Standard Time** = UTC+5:30, based on the 82.5°E meridian near Mirzapur, Allahabad.
- **Tropic of Cancer (≈23.5°N)** passes through 8 Indian states from Gujarat to Mizoram.

> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Indian Physiography and Rivers

The Peninsular Plateau is the oldest landmass of India, flanked by the Western Ghats (steep, continuous, higher rainfall side) and the lower, broken Eastern Ghats. The Indo-Gangetic Plain owes its fertility to alluvial deposits of the Indus, Ganga, and Brahmaputra systems.

| Division | Key feature | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Northern Mountains | Young fold range, tectonically active | Himalayas, Karakoram |
| Northern Plains | Alluvial, dense population | Punjab, Uttar Pradesh |
| Peninsular Plateau | Old crystalline rocks, mineral-rich | Chota Nagpur Plateau |
| Indian Desert | Arid, sand dunes | Thar (Rajasthan) |
| Coastal Plains | Narrow east, wider west | Konkan, Coromandel |
| Islands | Coral atolls in south, volcanic in north | Lakshadweep, Andaman |

#### Climate and Soils

The monsoon is governed by differential heating of land and sea plus the ITCZ shift. ICAR classifies Indian soils into Alluvial, Black (Regur), Red, Laterite, Desert, Forest/Mountain, and later added Arid and Saline. Cropping seasons follow rainfall: **Kharif** (June–October, monsoon-fed, e.g., rice, maize) and **Rabi** (October–March, winter-fed, e.g., wheat, mustard).

- Tea grows in laterite/acidic soils of hill slopes (Assam, Darjeeling), not alluvial plains.
- Black cotton soil retains moisture and suits cotton in the Deccan.
- Alluvial soil dominates the Indo-Gangetic belt and supports intensive cereals.

#### World Physical Geography and Plate Tectonics

Earth's interior is layered Crust → Mantle (asthenosphere is the ductile part driving plate motion) → Outer Core (liquid iron-nickel) → Inner Core (solid). Three discontinuities matter: **Moho** (crust-mantle), **Gutenberg** (mantle-outer core), **Lehmann** (outer-inner core). The world has 7 major plates; India's collision with the Eurasian Plate began ~50 million years ago and continues, raising the Himalayas by ~5 mm/year.

#### Common Mistakes and Exam Strategy

- Do not equate the Western Ghats with the Aravalli (Aravalli is older, NW India).
- Southwest Monsoon arrives at Kerala around June 1, not uniformly across India.
- IST is based on 82.5°E, not 23.5°N (Tropic of Cancer).
- XAT GK has no sectional cutoff, but scoring 2–3 quick geography MCQs lifts composite percentile; aim to finish each in under 45 seconds.

#### Practice prompts:
1. Match the river to its delta: Godavari, Mahanadi, Kaveri, Krishna — which forms the largest delta?
2. Which soil is best for cotton: alluvial, laterite, black regur, or desert? Justify using parent rock and moisture retention.
