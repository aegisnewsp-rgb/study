# Indian Economy and GDP

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Indian Economy and GDP** is the General Awareness module that tests structural facts about how India's national output is measured and who publishes the numbers. In IBPS Clerk Mains, 2–4 factual questions appear each year, mostly memory-based, drawn from base year, institutional history, and the GDP–GNP–NNP–NDP ladder.

- **Gross Domestic Product (GDP)** — total monetary value of all final goods and services produced *within* India's geographic boundary in a financial year (₹ crore).
- **Gross National Product (GNP)** — GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA); NFIA is negative for India, so GNP < GDP.
- **Net Domestic Product (NDP)** — GDP − Depreciation (capital consumption allowance).
- **Net National Product (NNP)** — GNP − Depreciation; when computed at factor cost it is called **National Income**.
- **Base year** for the official series = **2011-12** (revised from 2004-05 in January 2015, aligned with SNA 2008).
- **Current vs Constant prices** — Current = nominal (includes inflation); Constant = real (base-year prices); only Constant prices are used for comparing growth across years.

> 💡 **High-Yield Memory Hook:** "**G → N → D → I**" — Gross → Net → Domestic → Income. To convert GDP into National Income: **GDP + NFIA → GNP → − Depreciation → NNP → − Net Indirect Taxes → NNP at FC (National Income)**.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Three Methods of Measuring GDP

MoSPI (Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation) releases India's GDP using two of the three classical approaches; the third is theoretical for India.

1. **Production / Value Added method** — sums Gross Value Added (GVA) at basic prices across all sectors.
2. **Income method** — sums factor incomes (wages, rent, interest, profit).
3. **Expenditure method** — uses **GDP = C + I + G + (X − M)**, where C, I, G, X, M are all in ₹ crore.

#### Comparing GDP, GNP, NDP, NNP at a Glance

| Aggregate | Formula | What it captures | Key adjustment |
|---|---|---|---|
| GDP at Market Price | C + I + G + (X − M) + NIT | Output within India at market prices | Includes net indirect taxes |
| GVA at Basic Price | Σ (Value Added by sector) | Sectoral output before product taxes | Subtract Net Product Taxes to get GDP |
| GNP at Market Price | GDP + NFIA | Output by Indian residents anywhere | NFIA negative for India |
| NDP at Market Price | GDP − Depreciation | Output net of capital wear | "Net" = after depreciation |
| NNP at Market Price | GNP − Depreciation | National output net of wear | Used for per-capita calculations |
| NNP at Factor Cost (National Income) | NNP − Net Indirect Taxes | True income earned by factors of production | This is "National Income" in textbooks |

#### Key Formulas with Units

- **GDP Growth Rate (%)** = [(GDP_t − GDP_{t−1}) / GDP_{t−1}] × 100 — dimensionless ratio, computed on constant-price GDP.
- **Real GDP** = (Nominal GDP / GDP Deflator) × 100, where the Deflator is an index with base = 100.
- **Per Capita Income (₹/person)** = National Income / Mid-year Population.
- **Fiscal Deficit (₹)** = Total Expenditure − Total Receipts (excluding borrowings).
- **Primary Deficit (₹)** = Fiscal Deficit − Interest Payments.

#### 🎯 Exam-Level Worked Problem

**Question:** India's GDP at Market Price is ₹295 lakh crore. Net Indirect Taxes amount to ₹20 lakh crore, Depreciation is ₹25 lakh crore, and Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA) is −₹5 lakh crore. What is the National Income (NNP at Factor Cost)?

(a) ₹245 lakh crore (b) ₹250 lakh crore (c) ₹255 lakh crore (d) ₹260 lakh crore

#### Solution:

1. Start with GDP at Market Price = ₹295 lakh crore.
2. GNP at MP = GDP + NFIA = 295 + (−5) = **₹290 lakh crore**.
3. NNP at MP = GNP − Depreciation = 290 − 25 = **₹265 lakh crore**.
4. NNP at FC (National Income) = NNP at MP − Net Indirect Taxes = 265 − 20 = **₹245 lakh crore**.

#### Answer: (a) ₹245 lakh crore.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** Students forget the *order of adjustments*. The correct chain is GDP → GNP → NNP (MP) → NNP (FC). A common error is subtracting Depreciation *before* NFIA, or subtracting Net Indirect Taxes twice. NFIA for India is negative, so GNP is *less than* GDP — many candidates flip the sign.

#### Institutions That Publish the Numbers

- **MoSPI** — National Statistical Office (NSO) under it releases GDP, GVA, CPI (combined).
- **RBI** — Established **1 April 1935** under the RBI Act, 1934; **nationalised on 1 January 1949**. Publishes WPI, monetary statistics, and FX reserves.
- **NITI Aayog** — replaced the **Planning Commission on 1 January 2015**; chaired by the Prime Minister.
- **MPC (Monetary Policy Committee)** — 6-member body (3 RBI + 3 external) constituted under the RBI (Amendment) Act, 2016; sets the **repo rate**.

#### Edge Cases and Frequently Confused Pairs

| Confused Pair | Why students mix them up | Correct distinction |
|---|---|---|
| GDP Deflator vs CPI | Both measure inflation | Deflator covers *all* goods/services in GDP; CPI covers only the *consumer basket* |
| Real GDP vs Nominal GDP | Often used interchangeably | Real uses base-year prices (constant); Nominal uses current-year prices |
| Fiscal Deficit vs Revenue Deficit | Both measure government borrowings | Revenue Deficit = Revenue Expenditure − Revenue Receipts only |
| Per Capita Income vs GDP per capita | Sounds identical | Income uses *National Income*; GDP per capita uses *GDP* |
| RBI established vs nationalised | Two different dates | 1935 (set up), 1949 (nationalised) |
| Planning Commission vs NITI Aayog | Same role, different body | Planning Commission dissolved 1 Jan 2015; NITI Aayog is the successor |
| WPI vs CPI | Both are inflation indices | WPI tracks wholesale; CPI tracks consumer (base 2012 = 100) |
| GNP vs GDP | "+ NFIA" difference | NFIA negative for India ⇒ GNP < GDP |

#### Sectoral Composition and Recent Reforms

India's GVA is split into **Primary** (agriculture, forestry, fishing), **Secondary** (manufacturing, construction, electricity), and **Tertiary** (services, trade, finance, IT). Services dominate at roughly 50%+ of GVA. Three reforms that appear repeatedly in IBPS Clerk questions:

- **GST** — implemented **1 July 2017**, subsuming central excise, service tax, VAT, and other cesses.
- **Demonetisation** — ₹500 and ₹1000 banknotes invalidated on **8 November 2016**.
- **MPC** — first rate decision on **4 October 2016**, replacing the previous RBI Governor-centric framework.

#### India's Global Standing

India ranks **5th** in nominal GDP (after USA, China, Japan, Germany) and **3rd** in PPP terms. Its share of world GDP is roughly **3.5% nominal** and **~7% PPP**, while IMF and World Bank estimates of India's contribution to *global growth* hover around **15–17%** in recent years — the second-largest after China.

#### Advanced Practice Prompts

1. If CPI rises from 158 to 165 over 12 months, calculate the inflation rate and state whether WPI or CPI better reflects a salaried employee's cost of living — justify in two lines.
2. A question claims "India's GNP exceeds its GDP because of large remittance inflows." Identify the factual error, write the correct relationship, and state the sign of NFIA for India.

> 📌 **Formula Check:** All monetary aggregates are in **₹ crore** at the national level. Per capita figures are **₹ per person**. Growth rates and inflation are **dimensionless %**. The GDP Deflator is a **dimensionless index with base = 100**.
