# Medical Ethics and Patient Safety

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid revision summary of the four Beauchamp and Childress ethical pillars, informed consent, confidentiality, and patient safety for SMLE / SGPAT.

**Bioethics & Patient Safety Foundations — Core Essentials**

1. **The Four Classical Principles of Biomedical Ethics:**
   - **Autonomy:** Respecting the decision-making capacity of competent patients. Autonomous patients have the absolute legal and ethical right to accept or refuse any medical or surgical treatment, even if refusal leads to imminent death.
   - **Beneficence:** Acting in the best interest of the patient to promote health, prevent harm, and alleviate suffering.
   - **Non-Maleficence:** *Primum non nocere* (first, do no harm). Avoiding the infliction of unnecessary injury, negligence, or disproportionate risks.
   - **Justice:** Fair distribution of scarce healthcare resources, equal treatment without discrimination, and procedural fairness.
2. **Informed Consent Standards:**
   - A valid consent mandates three fundamental elements: **Capacity (Competence)**, **Adequate Information (Voluntariness)**, and **Absence of Coercion**.
   - The physician must disclose: nature of procedure, anticipated benefits, material risks (>1% frequency or catastrophic rare risks), alternatives, and prognosis without intervention.
3. **Breach of Confidentiality Exceptions (Mandatory Reporting):**
   - Direct imminent threat of bodily harm to identifiable third parties (Tarasoff duty to warn).
   - Statutorily mandated reporting: notifiable infectious diseases (e.g., MERS-CoV, tuberculosis, cholera to the Saudi Ministry of Health).
   - Suspected child abuse, elder abuse, or domestic violence.
   - Court order / subpoena from a judicial authority.
4. **Patient Safety & Error Classification:**
   - **Near Miss:** An event or situation that could have resulted in accident, injury, or illness, but did not either by chance or timely intervention.
   - **Adverse Event:** An injury caused by medical management rather than by the underlying disease of the patient.
   - **Sentinel Event:** An unanticipated occurrence involving death or serious physical/psychological injury (e.g., wrong-site surgery, retained surgical foreign body, hemolytic transfusion reaction). Requires immediate **Root Cause Analysis (RCA)**.

⚡ **SMLE / SGPAT Exam Trap:** In Saudi licensure exams, remember that a competent adult patient's consent is **individually sovereign**. A husband's consent is **not required** for an elective, medically indicated procedure or Caesarean section on a competent adult woman under Saudi Ministry of Health regulations.

## 1. Ethical Principles and Clinical Dilemmas

Clinical practice regularly pits competing ethical duties against one another, requiring systematic deontological and consequentialist reasoning:

| Ethical Principle | Formal Medical Definition | Clinical Scenario | Resolution Protocol |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Respect for Autonomy** | Patient self-determination and informed refusal | Jehovah's Witness patient refusing lifesaving blood transfusion | If adult has decision-making capacity, respect refusal; administer cell salvage or synthetic volume expanders |
| **Paternalism (Soft vs Hard)** | Overriding patient wishes for their perceived benefit | Physician concealing terminal cancer diagnosis at family's request | Ethically impermissible in modern practice; the patient owns their diagnosis unless they explicitly delegate communication |
| **Futility (Medical / Physiological)** | Intervention incapable of producing intended physiological effect | Family demanding CPR on a patient with brain death or irreversible multi-organ failure | Physician is not ethically or legally obligated to offer physiologically futile therapies; engage hospital ethics committee |
| **Surrogate Decision Making** | Substituted judgment when patient lacks capacity | Unconscious trauma patient without advance directive | Hierarchy: Legal healthcare proxy → Spouse → Adult children → Parents → Siblings |

## 2. Patient Safety Culture and Swiss Cheese Model

James Reason's **Swiss Cheese Model of System Accidents** conceptualizes medical errors not as individual moral failings, but as the alignment of latent organizational defects and active failures:
- **Latent Conditions:** Understaffing, inadequate training, poor equipment maintenance, unstandardized handoffs, fatigue, flawed electronic health record (EHR) interfaces.
- **Active Failures:** Direct slips, lapses, mistakes, or rule violations committed by frontline healthcare providers (e.g., administering the wrong dose of insulin).
- **System Defense Layers:** WHO Surgical Safety Checklist, dual-nurse verification of high-alert medications (heparin, insulin, potassium chloride), barcode medication scanning, and closed-loop communication (SBAR: Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation).

## 3. Medicolegal Frameworks and Brain Death in Saudi Arabia

The Saudi Commission for Health Specialties (SCFHS) and the Council of Senior Scholars establish strict guidelines governing end-of-life care:
- **Do Not Resuscitate (DNR) Policies:** In Saudi hospitals, a DNR order for a terminally ill patient with no hope of recovery can be authorized when signed by three competent consultant physicians, in consultation with the patient or family.
- **Brain Death Criteria (Saudi Protocol):**
  1. Complete irreversible cessation of all brain and brainstem functions.
  2. Prerequisites: Known etiology of irreversible brain damage, exclusion of hypothermia (core temp > 36°C), exclusion of central nervous system depressants or neuromuscular blockers, and normal metabolic/endocrine state.
  3. Physical Examination: Absence of all brainstem reflexes (pupillary light reflex, corneal reflex, oculocephalic / doll's eyes, oculovestibular / cold caloric, gag and cough reflexes).
  4. Positive Apnea Test: Absence of spontaneous respiratory efforts with pCO2 rising to >= 60 mmHg (or >= 20 mmHg above baseline).
  5. Confirmation by two independent specialist consultants (neurologist / neurosurgeon / intensivist) not involved in organ transplantation.

## 4. SMLE / SGPAT Examination Questions

**Question 1 (SMLE Ethics):** A 45-year-old competent female is scheduled for laparoscopic cholecystectomy. Her husband arrives at the surgical reception and demands that the surgery be cancelled because he has not given his permission. The patient insists on proceeding. What is the most appropriate action by the surgical team?  
*(A) Cancel the surgery until marital agreement is reached (B) Proceed with surgery based entirely on the patient's informed consent (C) Refer the case to the hospital legal affairs committee (D) Require the husband to sign a waiver*  
**Answer:** (B) Proceed with surgery based entirely on the patient's informed consent (Explanation: Under Saudi healthcare law and international bioethics, every competent adult individual possesses autonomous legal capacity to consent to their own medical care).

**Question 2 (SMLE Patient Safety):** What is the first and most critical step in performing a Root Cause Analysis (RCA) following a sentinel event in a hospital?  
*(A) Determining disciplinary action for the involved nurse (B) Assembling a multidisciplinary team and defining the exact sequence of events (C) Issuing a press release to media (D) Changing the hospital pharmaceutical supplier*  
**Answer:** (B) Assembling a multidisciplinary team and defining the exact sequence of events (Explanation: RCA is a non-punitive, systematic methodology aimed at identifying underlying systems defects rather than assigning individual blame).

**Question 3 (SMLE Ethics):** Under which of the following circumstances is a physician ethically and legally permitted to disclose confidential patient medical information without the patient's consent?  
*(A) When the patient's employer requests sick leave verification details (B) When a patient with active open pulmonary tuberculosis refuses treatment and continues attending crowded public gatherings (C) When the patient's adult sibling inquires about family medical history (D) When a pharmaceutical sales representative requests prescription audit data*  
**Answer:** (B) When a patient with active open pulmonary tuberculosis refuses treatment and continues attending crowded public gatherings (Explanation: The public health exception mandates reporting communicable infectious diseases to state health authorities to prevent imminent harm to the public).
