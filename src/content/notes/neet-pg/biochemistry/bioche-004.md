# Lipid Metabolism

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

- **Lipid metabolism** is the orchestrated synthesis (lipogenesis) and catabolism (β-oxidation, lipolysis, ketogenesis) of fatty acids, triglycerides, phospholipids, cholesterol, and ketone bodies, distributed across the **cytosol, mitochondria, smooth ER, and peroxisomes**.
- The **committed step of fatty acid synthesis** is catalysed by **acetyl-CoA carboxylase** (biotin-dependent): Acetyl-CoA + CO₂ + ATP → **Malonyl-CoA** + ADP + Pi. Malonyl-CoA simultaneously **inhibits CPT-I**, blocking oxidation while synthesis proceeds.
- **β-oxidation of palmitate (C16:0)** yields a net of **106 ATP**: 8 ATP spent on activation + 7 × (1 FADH₂ + 1 NADH) + 8 acetyl-CoA through the TCA cycle.
- **HMG-CoA reductase** is the rate-limiting, **statin-inhibited** enzyme of cholesterol biosynthesis; **SREBP-2** upregulates it when cholesterol falls.
- **NEET-PG focus**: enzyme regulation (ACC, HMG-CoA reductase, CPT-I), carnitine shuttle defects, ketone-body stoichiometry, and inborn errors like Refsum, MCAD, and Smith–Lemli–Opitz.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Fatty Acid Synthesis (Lipogenesis)
Cytosolic, uses **acetyl-CoA carboxylase (ACC)** and the **fatty acid synthase (FAS) homodimer** with acyl carrier protein (ACP) and a phosphopantetheine arm. ACC produces **malonyl-CoA**, the 2-carbon donor, while FAS condenses successive malonyl units, releasing CO₂. The full **C16:0 palmitate** synthesis consumes **8 acetyl-CoA, 7 ATP, 14 NADPH, 14 H⁺**. NADPH is supplied roughly 60% by the **HMP shunt (G6PD, 6PGD)** and 40% by **malic enzyme**. Insulin activates ACC via dephosphorylation; **glucagon and epinephrine** inhibit it via AMPK-mediated phosphorylation.

#### β-Oxidation of Saturated Fatty Acids
Mitochondrial, four-step spiral per 2-carbon cycle: **acyl-CoA dehydrogenase (FADH₂) → enoyl-CoA hydratase → 3-hydroxyacyl-CoA dehydrogenase (NADH) → thiolase (releases acetyl-CoA)**. Before entry, cytosolic activation by **acyl-CoA synthetase** costs 2 ATP equivalents, then the **carnitine shuttle** (CPT-I, carnitine-acylcarnitine translocase, CPT-II) transports acyl groups. **CPT-I is the rate-limiting step**, potently inhibited by malonyl-CoA.

#### Special Substrates
- **Unsaturated fatty acids** need **enoyl-CoA isomerase** (cis-Δ³ → trans-Δ²) and for polyunsaturates, **2,4-dienoyl-CoA reductase**.
- **Odd-chain fatty acids** terminate in **propionyl-CoA** → methylmalonyl-CoA via **biotin-dependent propionyl-CoA carboxylase**, then to **succinyl-CoA** by **B₁₂-dependent methylmalonyl-CoA mutase**.
- **Very long chain fatty acids (VLCFAs, ≥C22)** undergo **peroxisomal β-oxidation** (FADH₂ transferred to O₂ → H₂O₂), generating medium-chain acyl-CoA for mitochondria.

#### Ketogenesis and Cholesterol
Mitochondrial HMG-CoA synthase diverts acetyl-CoA to **acetoacetate and β-hydroxybutyrate** during fasting. **Cholesterol synthesis** (cytosol + smooth ER) condenses 18 acetyl-CoA via the **mevalonate pathway**; HMG-CoA reductase is feedback-inhibited by cholesterol and regulated transcriptionally by **SREBP-2** post-ER cholesterol sensing.

| Pathway | Site | Key Rate-Limiting Enzyme | Clinical Inhibitor |
|---|---|---|---|
| FA synthesis | Cytosol | Acetyl-CoA carboxylase | — |
| β-oxidation | Mito matrix | CPT-I | Malonyl-CoA |
| Ketogenesis | Mito matrix | HMG-CoA synthase | — |
| Cholesterol | Smooth ER | HMG-CoA reductase | Statins |

#### NEET-PG Question Patterns
Direct MCQs on ATP yield, MCAD enzyme defect (medium-chain acyl-CoA dehydrogenase), Reye syndrome (mitochondrial β-oxidation failure), carnitine deficiency, and statin mechanism recur every year.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Regulatory Crosstalk
Insulin and glucose load drive **SREBP-1c** activation → transcription of ACC and FAS. Conversely, fasting glucocorticoids and PPARα transcriptionally upregulate **CPT-I, ACOX1, and HMG-CoA synthase**, switching hepatocytes from storage to oxidation and ketogenesis. **ChREBP** senses xylulose-5-phosphate from the HMP shunt to co-activate lipogenic genes.

#### Edge Cases and Inborn Errors
- **MCAD deficiency**: most common FAO disorder, hypoketotic hypoglycaemia, dicarboxylic aciduria, neonatal death with fasting; management is avoidance of fasting and IV glucose.
- **CPT-I deficiency**: low carnitine, hypoketosis, no dicarboxylic acids.
- **Refsum disease**: peroxisomal α-oxidation defect, phytanic acid accumulation.
- **Zellweger syndrome**: absent peroxisomes, VLCFA accumulation, severe neurological disease.
- **Smith–Lemli–Opitz**: Δ7-dehydrocholesterol reductase defect; cholesterol low, 7-DHC elevated.
- **Propionic and methylmalonic acidaemia**: propionyl-CoA accumulation; B₁₂-responsive forms exist.

#### Common Traps
- The "106 ATP" figure for palmitate assumes the malate–aspartate shuttle (yielding 2.5 ATP/NADH); older texts using the glycerol-3-phosphate shuttle give 105.
- **Ketone bodies are made in liver mitochondria but oxidised in extrahepatic mitochondria**—HMG-CoA synthase is absent from extrahepatic tissues, so peripheral tissues do not waste acetyl-CoA on ketogenesis.
- **HMG-CoA reductase** is inhibited by statins as **competitive with respect to HMG-CoA** (Km effect), not non-competitive.
- **Acetyl-CoA cannot cross the mitochondrial membrane**; it exits as citrate, then ATP-citrate lyase regenerates acetyl-CoA + oxaloacetate in the cytosol for lipogenesis.

#### Worked Example
Calculate net ATP from β-oxidation of **arachidonic acid (C20:4, Δ5,8,11,14)**. Activation = 2 ATP. Nine cycles of β-oxidation: 9 × (1 FADH₂ + 1 NADH) = 9 × 4 = 36 ATP, but unsaturated bonds skip one FADH₂ step per double bond and one NADH step per second double bond, reducing yield. Combined with 10 acetyl-CoA through TCA, total ≈ 122–124 ATP (subtract 2 for activation).

1. A fasting child collapses with hypoketotic hypoglycaemia and elevated C8 acylcarnitine. Which enzyme is defective and what is the acute management?
2. Explain why malonyl-CoA simultaneously commits the hepatocyte to fatty acid synthesis and blocks fatty acid oxidation.
