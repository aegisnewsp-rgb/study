# D. Competition and Market Structure — HKDSE Economics Notes

This topic covers Topic D of the Compulsory Part of HKDSE Economics. It addresses imperfect competition (monopoly, monopolistic competition, oligopoly); barriers to entry; price discrimination; product differentiation; collusive behaviour and cartels; and game theory basics (dominant strategy, Nash equilibrium).

> Verify the live HKDSE Economics Assessment Framework on https://www.hkeaa.edu.hk/ before planning revision.

---

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Monopoly**: a single seller; significant barriers to entry; price setter; produces where MR = MC.
- **Barriers to entry**: economies of scale; brand loyalty and product differentiation; control of essential inputs; patents and copyrights; strategic behaviour (predatory pricing, raising rivals' costs).
- **Monopolistic competition**: many sellers; differentiated products; some pricing power; free entry and exit (long-run zero economic profit).
- **Oligopoly**: a few large sellers; high concentration ratio; strategic interaction (game theory).
- **Price discrimination**: charging different prices to different customers for the same good. Conditions: market power; ability to identify different groups; ability to prevent resale.
- **Types of price discrimination**:
  - **First-degree** (perfect): each consumer pays their maximum willingness to pay.
  - **Second-degree**: discounts for bulk purchase (e.g. quantity discounts).
  - **Third-degree**: different prices for different groups (e.g. student discounts).
- **Product differentiation**: real differences (quality, features), perceived differences (branding, advertising), or both.
- **Collusion**: explicit or tacit agreement between firms to coordinate pricing or output. Cartels (e.g. OPEC) formally collude.
- **Cartel instability**: each member has an incentive to cheat on the agreement. Game theory explains this.
- **Game theory**: models strategic interaction. A **dominant strategy** is the best response regardless of the opponent's action. A **Nash equilibrium** is a set of strategies where no player can improve by changing unilaterally.
- **Prisoner's dilemma**: a classic game where both players would be better off cooperating but each has an incentive to defect. Self illustrates to the difficulty of collusion.

#### Examiner traps

- Confusing monopoly with monopolistic competition — monopoly = one seller; monopolistic competition = many sellers with differentiated products.
- Treating "high price" as a sign of monopoly — high prices alone are not diagnostic; look at barriers to entry.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Monopoly

**Sources of monopoly**:

- **Economies of scale**: a single firm can produce the entire market at lower average cost than multiple smaller firms (natural monopoly).
- **Control of essential inputs**: control of a key resource.
- **Patents and copyrights**: legal protection of inventions and creative works.
- **Network effects**: the value of a product increases with the number of users (e.g. social networks, operating systems).
- **Strategic behaviour**: predatory pricing, exclusive contracts, raising rivals' costs.

**Profit maximisation**: produce where MR = MC. Charge the price on the demand curve corresponding to that quantity.

**Welfare loss**: monopoly produces less than the competitive equilibrium and charges a higher price. The deadweight loss is the triangle between the demand curve, the MR = MC quantity, and the competitive quantity.

**Price discrimination**: conditions (must be able to identify groups with different elasticities and prevent resale). Examples: airline tickets (different classes, advance vs walk-up), student discounts, peak vs off-peak pricing.

#### Monopolistic competition

Many sellers with differentiated products. Each firm has a downward-sloping demand curve (unlike perfect competition) but faces competition from similar products. Short-run: positive or negative economic profit. Long-run: zero economic profit (free entry erodes profit).

**Product differentiation**: real differences (quality, design, features), perceived differences (branding, advertising), location differences, services (after-sales, delivery).

#### Oligopoly

A few large firms. High concentration ratio. Mutual interdependence: each firm's profit depends on the actions of its rivals.

**Models**:

- **Kinked demand curve**: rival firms match price decreases but not price increases. Creates a discontinuity in MR and explains price stickiness.
- **Cournot**: firms choose quantities simultaneously.
- **Bertrand**: firms choose prices simultaneously. With identical products and constant MC, the equilibrium price equals MC (competitive outcome).
- **Stackelberg**: one firm is the leader, others are followers.

**Collusion**: explicit cartels (OPEC) or tacit collusion (price leadership). Cartels are often unstable due to the incentive to cheat.

#### Game theory

**Dominant strategy**: a strategy that is the best response regardless of the opponent's action.

**Nash equilibrium**: a set of strategies where no player can improve by unilaterally changing their strategy.

**Prisoner's dilemma**:

|  | B cooperates | B defects |
|--|--|--|
| **A cooperates** | (3, 3) | (0, 5) |
| **A defects** | (5, 0) | (1, 1) |

The Nash equilibrium is (defect, defect) — both players defect even though (cooperate, cooperate) would give each a higher payoff. This explains why cartels are unstable.

**Repeated games**: in a repeated game (where players meet again), cooperation can emerge. Trigger strategies (e.g. tit-for-tat) sustain cooperation.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Cross-topic links

- **C. Market and Price**: welfare analysis of perfect competition.
- **E. Efficiency, Equity and the Role of Government**: market failure and regulation.
- **J. International Trade and Finance**: international cartels and trade policy.

#### Exam technique

- **For monopoly questions**: state the barrier to entry; compute profit-maximising output; draw the welfare diagram.
- **For oligopoly questions**: identify the model (Cournot, Bertrand, kinked demand); cite the relevant equilibrium.
- **For game theory questions**: construct the payoff matrix; identify dominant strategies and Nash equilibrium.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Monopolies always make a profit." Monopolies can make losses if demand is low or costs are high.
- "Cartels always work." Cartels are inherently unstable due to the incentive to cheat; cooperation requires repeated interaction.
- "Price discrimination is always unfair." Price discrimination can increase total surplus (some consumers who would not buy at a uniform price are served).
