# Whole Numbers and Operations — PSLE Mathematics Notes

Whole Numbers and Operations is the first content strand in the 2021 MOE Primary Mathematics syllabus and underpins every other topic in PSLE Mathematics. A Primary 6 student cannot succeed on the applied questions of Paper 2 without arithmetic fluency here: place value, the four operations with whole numbers, estimation and approximation, and order of operations are all assessed on every PSLE Mathematics paper. The official MOE syllabus strand heading is "Number and Algebra" at Primary 5–6, of which Whole Numbers and Operations is the first sub-strand.

> Verify the live PSLE Mathematics paper format and any in-year syllabus changes on https://www.seab.gov.sg/psle/ before planning revision around the patterns below.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### The seven facts every Primary 6 student needs

1. **Place value** up to 10 million: know what each digit means in a number like 4 257 069 (4 × 1 000 000, 2 × 100 000, etc.).
2. **The four operations** with whole numbers: addition, subtraction, multiplication and division — including long multiplication and long division by a two-digit number.
3. **Order of operations** (BODMAS / PEMDAS): brackets first, then orders (powers and roots), then multiplication and division (left to right), then addition and subtraction (left to right).
4. **Estimation**: round to a sensible number of significant figures or decimal places before calculating; sanity-check the answer.
5. **Approximation**: write the result to a given number of decimal places or significant figures.
6. **Factors and multiples**: factor trees, common factors, common multiples, LCM, HCF.
7. **Word problems with whole numbers**: identify the operation from the question, lay it out line by line, and check the answer against the context.

#### Key notations

- 10 000 = 10⁴
- 1 000 000 = 10⁶
- 10 000 000 = 10⁷
- A factor of n is a whole number that divides n exactly.
- A multiple of n is the product of n and any whole number.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Place value

In a whole number, each digit has a value that depends on its position. For 4 257 069:

| 4 | 2 | 5 | 7 | 0 | 6 | 9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| millions | hundred thousands | ten thousands | thousands | hundreds | tens | ones |
| 4 × 1 000 000 | 2 × 100 000 | 5 × 10 000 | 7 × 1 000 | 0 × 100 | 6 × 10 | 9 × 1 |

Reading a number in words from left to right: "four million, two hundred and fifty-seven thousand and sixty-nine". A common error is to confuse the place value of adjacent digits — for example, reading 6 540 320 as "six million five hundred and four thousand and three hundred and twenty".

#### The four operations

**Addition and subtraction** with whole numbers rely on column alignment: align the ones, tens, hundreds, etc. and add or subtract column by column, carrying or borrowing as needed. For large numbers, use the standard algorithm and check the answer by reversing the operation (add to check a subtraction; subtract to check an addition).

**Multiplication** uses long multiplication: multiply the multi-digit number by each digit of the other number, starting with the ones, then shift the partial product one place left for the tens, another for the hundreds, etc. For example, 357 × 24:

- 357 × 4 = 1 428
- 357 × 20 = 7 140
- Total: 1 428 + 7 140 = 8 568

**Division** uses long division: divide the dividend by the divisor digit by digit. For a divisor with two or more digits, estimate the first quotient digit, multiply back, subtract, and bring down the next digit. The remainder at the end is the final remainder if the question is in whole-number context.

#### Order of operations

A Primary 6 paper will often test whether a student evaluates a long expression in the right order. The convention is BODMAS (or PEMDAS):

1. **B**rackets
2. **O**rders (powers, roots)
3. **D**ivision and **M**ultiplication (left to right)
4. **A**ddition and **S**ubtraction (left to right)

Example: 8 + 6 × 3 − (12 ÷ 4)² = 8 + 6 × 3 − 3² = 8 + 18 − 9 = 17. The brackets evaluate first, then the order (3² = 9), then the multiplication, then the additions and subtractions.

#### Estimation and approximation

**Estimation** rounds each input to one significant figure (or to the nearest convenient value) before calculating. Example: estimate 412 × 39 ≈ 400 × 40 = 16 000. The estimate is not the answer — it is a sanity check.

**Approximation** rounds the result to a given number of decimal places or significant figures. Example: 247 ÷ 31 = 7.9677… ≈ 8.0 (to 2 significant figures) or 7.97 (to 2 decimal places). A common error is to round intermediate steps; keep at least one more digit than the required accuracy until the final step.

#### Factors and multiples

A **factor** of a whole number n is a whole number that divides n exactly. The factor pairs of 36 are (1, 36), (2, 18), (3, 12), (4, 9), (6, 6).

A **multiple** of n is a whole number that is the product of n and a whole number. The first five multiples of 7 are 7, 14, 21, 28, 35.

The **highest common factor (HCF)** of two numbers is the largest factor they share. The **lowest common multiple (LCM)** of two numbers is the smallest positive integer that is a multiple of both.

#### Word problems

Whole-number word problems appear in every PSLE Mathematics paper, often in the form of "X had some items; she gave Y items away and received Z more; how many does she have now?" Use a bar model (heuristic) for the "before-after" form: draw a bar for the original quantity, mark the change, then solve for the unknown. Always re-read the question after solving to check the answer makes sense in context.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Where Whole Numbers and Operations appears across the paper

- **PSLE Mathematics Paper 1**: short-answer and structured questions directly testing the four operations, place value and BODMAS. Heaviest direct load here.
- **PSLE Mathematics Paper 2**: whole-number word problems embedded inside ratio, rate and percentage questions, and as part of the heuristics set.
- **Across both papers**: arithmetic accuracy under time pressure is the single biggest differentiator at the AL1–AL4 boundary.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Carry the 1 to the next column" without specifying which place value. The 1 carried from ones to tens is worth 10, not 1.
- "6 × 4 means 6 groups of 4" vs "6 added together 4 times". The two readings give the same answer (24) for this case, but for division they diverge — 24 ÷ 6 means "how many groups of 6 in 24?", which is 4.
- "Long division ends when the remainder is 0". For PSLE, long division of a whole number dividend by a whole number divisor ends when no further digits can be brought down; the final remainder is the answer if the context is whole numbers.
- "BODMAS means brackets first, then do the rest in order". Division and multiplication have equal priority (left to right), as do addition and subtraction.
- "Estimation is just rounding the answer". Estimation rounds the inputs before calculating; approximation rounds the output after calculating.

#### Exam technique

- Show every column operation step. A single working line for long multiplication or long division is worth at least one method mark even if the final answer is wrong.
- For BODMAS questions, write out each evaluation step on a new line. The marker can then award partial credit even if the final answer slips.
- For word problems, write the bar model before the arithmetic. The model is the method mark; the arithmetic is the answer mark.
- For factor-tree questions, draw the factor tree from the largest prime factor first (2, 3, 5, 7, 11, …) to avoid missing factors.
