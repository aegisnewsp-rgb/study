# Active and Passive Voice

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Active voice places the grammatical **subject** as the doer of the verb's action (e.g., *The boy kicked the ball*). Passive voice places the subject as the receiver, built with a form of the auxiliary **be** plus the **past participle** (e.g., *The ball was kicked by the boy*). Only **transitive verbs** (those taking a direct object) can be passivised; intransitive verbs such as *arrive*, *sleep*, or *happen* have no passive form.

- The transformation rule: **Object + be + past participle (+ by + agent)**.
- The auxiliary **be** must match the original tense: *writes → is written*, *was writing → was being written*, *has written → has been written*.
- Modals survive passivisation: *can write → can be written*, *must complete → must be completed*.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### How the transformation works
The active-to-passive shift involves three mechanical moves: the **object** of the active sentence becomes the **subject** of the passive, the main verb becomes a **past participle** preceded by a form of *be*, and the original subject moves into a *by + agent* phrase. When the agent is unknown, obvious, or irrelevant, the *by*-phrase is dropped, producing an **agentless passive** (*The window was broken* rather than *The window was broken by someone*).

#### Tense correspondence in NECO questions
The auxiliary *be* in the passive carries the tense of the active verb. Candidates must memorise the pairing because examiners test every tense at least once across a paper set.

| Active tense | Active verb | Passive form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Simple Present | writes / teach | is written / is taught |
| Simple Past | wrote / taught | was written / was taught |
| Present Continuous | is writing | is being written |
| Past Continuous | was writing | was being written |
| Present Perfect | has written | has been written |
| Future | will write | will be written |

#### Reporting verbs and passivisation
Reporting verbs with a *that*-clause admit two passive equivalents: **impersonal** (*It was said that he was ill*) and **personal** (*He was said to be ill*). NECO Paper 2 essay and comprehension tasks in many papers require the personal form for tighter sentences.

#### Mechanics students forget
- Pronouns change case: object pronouns *me, him, her, us, them* become subject pronouns *I, he, she, we, they* in the passive (*Him gave the book* → *He was given the book*).
- Past participles of irregular verbs (*taught, broken, stolen, written*) must be spelled correctly.
- The base form after *be* is the past participle, not the base verb (*The book was wrote* is wrong; *was written* is correct).

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge cases that trip up candidates
Three constructions are passivised differently from the standard *SVO* pattern.

1. **Reporting-clause passivisation**: *People believe that she won the race* → *It is believed that she won the race* or *She is believed to have won the race*.
2. **Causative *have/get***: *She had her hair cut* (passive meaning, active form) — NECO sometimes frames this as a "rewrite in passive" trap, but the sentence is already passive in meaning.
3. **Stative participles as adjectives**: *The window is broken* can be a true passive event or a descriptive adjective; only context distinguishes them.

#### Common mistakes examiners exploit

| Mistake | Wrong output | Correct output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Passivising an intransitive verb | *The baby was slept by the mother* | Grammatically impossible — keep active |
| Wrong tense of *be* | *The book wrote by him* | *The book was written by him* |
| Wrong irregular participle | *The match was boken* | *The match was broken* |
| Unchanged pronoun case | *Him was given the book* | *He was given the book* |
| Stating the obvious agent | *The match was won by Nigeria* (when context already names Nigeria) | Drop the *by*-phrase |

#### Exam strategy for NECO
The voice-change question in standard papers appears in **Paper 1 (Objective/Aptitude)** as a sentence-transformation item worth 2–4 marks and can resurface in **Paper 2 (Essay & Comprehension)** when instructors ask for a paragraph rewrite. Spend at most 60 seconds per item: identify the **object**, swap, change the verb to past participle, prepend the matching *be*, then adjust the pronoun. If the agent is conversational, omit *by + agent* to sound natural.

#### Practice prompts
1. Rewrite in passive voice: *The committee has approved the new curriculum.*
2. Rewrite in active voice: *The stolen car was recovered by the police within two hours.*
