# Polymers and Biomolecules

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

- **Polymer** = giant molecule built by linking many **monomers** through covalent bonds. **Degree of polymerisation (n)** = M(polymer) ÷ M(monomer).
- **Addition polymers** (e.g., polythene, PVC, Teflon) form from C=C monomers with **no by-product**. **Condensation polymers** (e.g., nylon, proteins, starch) eliminate a **small molecule (H₂O or HCl)**.
- **Biomolecules** are four classes: **carbohydrates, proteins, lipids, nucleic acids**. Glucose and fructose both = C₆H₁₂O₆ (structural isomers). Starch and cellulose share repeat unit (C₆H₁₀O₅)ₙ but differ in α vs β glycosidic linkage.
- **Peptide bond** = –CO–NH–. **Glycosidic bond** = C–O–C between sugars. **Ester bond** = linkage in triglycerides.
- JAMB tip: 3% weight means 1–2 questions. Memorise PVC ↔ vinyl chloride, nylon-6,6 ↔ hexamethylenediamine + adipic acid.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Polymer Classifications
Polymers are grouped two ways. By **source**: natural (starch, cellulose, proteins, natural rubber) vs synthetic (polythene, nylon, PVC). By **formation mechanism**: **addition** (chain-growth, monomers must carry C=C, no small molecule released) vs **condensation** (step-growth, each linkage eliminates H₂O or HCl).

| Feature | Addition | Condensation |
|---|---|---|
| Monomer requirement | Unsaturated (C=C) | Has –OH/–COOH/–NH₂ groups |
| By-product | None | H₂O, HCl, or NH₃ |
| Examples | Polythene, PVC, Teflon | Nylon-6,6, Terylene, proteins |

#### Natural Rubber and Vulcanisation
Natural rubber is **cis-1,4-polyisoprene**, (C₅H₈)ₙ. Heating with **sulphur (vulcanisation)** introduces disulphide cross-links that stiffen the chains, raise elasticity, and prevent stickiness. Gutta-percha is the **trans** isomer and is harder and less elastic.

#### Biomolecules Overview

| Class | Monomer | Linkage | Key example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Carbohydrate | Monosaccharide | Glycosidic (C–O–C) | Sucrose = glucose + fructose |
| Protein | α-amino acid | Peptide (–CO–NH–) | Insulin, haemoglobin |
| Lipid | Fatty acid + glycerol | Ester | Triglycerides |
| Nucleic acid | Nucleotide | Phosphodiester | DNA, RNA |

**Sucrose hydrolysis:** C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁ + H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ (glucose) + C₆H₁₂O₆ (fructose). Both products share the same molecular formula but differ in functional-group position.

#### Protein Structure
Proteins fold into four levels: **primary** (amino acid sequence), **secondary** (α-helix or β-pleated sheet stabilised by H-bonds), **tertiary** (3-D folding via R-group interactions), **quaternary** (multiple polypeptide subunits). **Enzymes** are globular protein catalysts that are highly specific and denature outside optimum temperature and pH.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Worked Example: Degree of Polymerisation
A PVC sample has M = 62,500 g/mol. Vinyl chloride (CH₂=CHCl) has M = 62.5 g/mol.

#### n = 62,500 ÷ 62.5 = 1,000 monomer units.

This dimensionless ratio tells you how many vinyl chloride molecules condensed into one chain.

#### Worked Example: Hydrolysis of a Condensation Polymer
A polypeptide of **n amino acids** releases **(n − 1) water molecules**, because each peptide bond formed originally cost one H₂O. For a tetrapeptide (n = 4), you get 3 H₂O released and 3 peptide bonds.

#### Exam Traps and Edge Cases
- **Starch vs cellulose:** both (C₆H₁₀O₅)ₙ — identical empirical formula. Starch has **α-1,4-glycosidic** links (digestible); cellulose has **β-1,4** links (humans lack cellulase).
- **Nitrogen test:** proteins and nucleic acids contain N; carbohydrates and most lipids do **not**. Biuret test (purple) detects peptide bonds; Fehling's test detects reducing sugars.
- **DNA vs RNA:** both polynucleotides, but DNA uses **thymine**, RNA uses **uracil**, and RNA's sugar is ribose (with 2′-OH) while DNA's is deoxyribose.
- **Saponification:** triglyceride + NaOH → glycerol + sodium salt of fatty acid (soap). This is base hydrolysis of an ester.

#### Practice Prompts
1. **JAMB-style MCQ:** "The by-product of the polymerisation of amino acids is…" Answer: water (H₂O).
2. **JAMB-style MCQ:** "The difference between starch and cellulose is…" Answer: the type of glycosidic linkage (α vs β).
