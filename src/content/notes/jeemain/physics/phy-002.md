# Motion in 1D

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Motion in a straight line is described by a single coordinate **x(t)**. Its time derivatives give the kinematic variables:

- **Instantaneous velocity:** v = dx/dt (signed; sign = direction along axis)
- **Instantaneous acceleration:** a = dv/dt = d²x/dt²
- **Displacement** (vector, signed) ≠ **distance** (scalar, path length, always ≥ 0)

For **uniformly accelerated motion** (a = constant), the three equations of motion are the workhorse:

1. v = u + at
2. s = ut + ½at²
3. v² = u² + 2as

JEE Main uses **g = 10 m/s²** unless the problem states otherwise. In free fall with upward taken positive, a = −g. The displacement in the *n*-th second is sₙ = u + (a/2)(2n − 1). Average velocity for constant a is (u + v)/2, **not** the arithmetic mean of speeds over intervals.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Definitions and sign convention
Choose an origin and a positive direction on the line. Position x(t) is signed; **displacement** Δx = x_f − x_i is also signed, while **distance** is the total path length and is non-negative. A negative displacement simply means the final position lies on the negative side of the origin relative to the initial position. Velocity and acceleration inherit signs from these definitions: v > 0 means motion in the positive direction, a > 0 means the velocity is becoming more positive (speeding up if v > 0, slowing down if v < 0).

#### Equations for constant acceleration
When a is constant in magnitude **and** sign, integrating a = dv/dt gives v = u + at; integrating once more gives s = ut + ½at². Eliminating t yields v² = u² + 2as. These are valid only for constant a. The average velocity for constant acceleration is (u + v)/2, and using s = v_avg · t reproduces the second equation.

#### Graphical interpretation
On a **v–t graph**, the slope at any instant is the instantaneous acceleration, and the signed area under the curve equals displacement. On an **x–t graph**, the slope gives instantaneous velocity. Concavity (upward/downward) of the x–t curve reflects the sign of a.

#### Direction reversal
Whenever v changes sign, the particle turns around. The displacement over the interval can be zero (round trip) while the distance covered is non-zero. To find when v = 0, set v(t) = 0 and solve for t — this is the standard trick for maximum-height problems in free fall.

#### Relative motion in 1D
Define velocity of A relative to B as v_AB = v_A − v_B (with consistent signs). Transforming to a frame moving with constant velocity removes uniform translational motion of both bodies, which is why train, river, and separation problems collapse to a single moving particle.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Variable acceleration via calculus
When a = a(t) is not constant, integrate: v(t) = u + ∫₀ᵗ a(t′) dt′, and x(t) = x₀ + ∫₀ᵗ v(t′) dt′. The graphical equivalents are: area under the a–t curve gives change in velocity; area under the v–t curve gives displacement. This is the bridge to work–energy methods, since a · v integrated over time gives the velocity-dependent energy terms.

#### Worked micro-example
A ball is thrown upward from a 20 m cliff with u = 15 m/s. Take upward as positive, g = 10 m/s².

- Time to reach the highest point: 0 = 15 − 10t → t = 1.5 s. Max height above launch: s = 15(1.5) − ½(10)(1.5)² = 11.25 m.
- Total time to hit the ground (y = −20): −20 = 15t − 5t² → t² − 3t − 4 = 0 → t = 4 s.
- Velocity at impact: v = 15 − 10(4) = −25 m/s (downward, as expected).
- Total distance travelled = 11.25 m (up) + (11.25 + 20) m (down) = 32.25 m, while net displacement = −20 m.

The 1.5 s and 4 s come from the same kinematic equations; the difference is the sign convention on s and the y-coordinate.

#### Common traps in JEE Main
- Applying v² = u² + 2as across an interval where the particle reverses direction and a changes sign — split the motion at the turning point.
- Equating **average speed** (total distance / total time) to the magnitude of average velocity; they coincide only for motion in one direction.
- Treating g as positive in one equation and negative in another within the same problem.
- In relative motion, adding speeds like 5 + 3 = 8 without converting to a common frame; always use v_AB = v_A − v_B with consistent signs.
- Reading displacement off a v–t graph as the unsigned area when the curve crosses the t-axis — use **signed** area.

#### Connections
Motion in 1D underlies projectile motion (decomposed into independent 1D motions), SHM (a = −ω²x), and the kinematics of circular motion along a diameter. Mastery here directly transfers to the *kinematics* unit in JEE Main (≈ 3% weightage) and to every mechanics problem that follows.

#### Practice prompts
1. A car brakes uniformly from 72 km/h to rest in 5 s while a scooter passes it at a constant 54 km/h. Find the time during which the car and scooter are separated by less than 10 m after braking begins.
2. From a balloon rising at 5 m/s, a stone is dropped and hits the ground in 4 s. Using g = 10 m/s², find the balloon's height at the moment of release.
