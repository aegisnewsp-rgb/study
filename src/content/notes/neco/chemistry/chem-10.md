# Chemical Kinetics and Rate Laws

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Chemical kinetics** measures how fast reactants become products. The **rate of reaction** is the change in concentration per unit time, in mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹. A **rate law** (rate equation) relates that rate to reactant concentrations:

> Rate = k[A]ᵐ[B]ⁿ

The exponents **m** and **n** are the **orders of reaction**, found only by experiment, never from the balanced equation. The **overall order** is m + n.

- **Zero order:** rate independent of [A]; [A] vs t gives a straight line.
- **First order:** rate ∝ [A]; t½ = 0.693/k, independent of starting concentration.
- **Second order:** rate ∝ [A]²; t½ = 1/(k[A]₀), dependent on [A]₀.
- **Arrhenius:** k = A·e^(−Ea/RT); ln k vs 1/T gives slope = −Ea/R.
- A **catalyst** lowers Ea without changing ΔH or the equilibrium position.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Rate, Order, and the Rate Law

The **instantaneous rate** equals the gradient of the tangent on a concentration–time curve at a chosen time. The **initial rate** is the tangent at t = 0, used to compare trials and deduce orders. For a reaction A + B → products, the experimentally determined form is Rate = k[A]ᵐ[B]ⁿ, where **k** is the **rate constant**. Units of k depend on overall order so that the rate always comes out in mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹.

#### Integrated Rate Laws and Half-Life

Each order has a characteristic plot that stays linear:

| Order | Integrated form | Linear plot | Half-life t½ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | [A] = [A]₀ − kt | [A] vs t | [A]₀/(2k) |
| 1 | ln[A] = ln[A]₀ − kt | ln[A] vs t | 0.693/k |
| 2 | 1/[A] = 1/[A]₀ + kt | 1/[A] vs t | 1/(k[A]₀) |

A useful shortcut: if doubling [A] doubles the rate, order = 1; if it quadruples the rate, order = 2; if the rate is unchanged, order = 0.

#### Factors That Change the Rate

- **Concentration** raises collision frequency for orders > 0.
- **Temperature** shifts the Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution so more molecules exceed Ea; roughly, a 10 °C rise doubles k.
- **Surface area** of solids increases contact sites.
- **Light** drives photochemical reactions.
- **Catalysts** offer a lower-Ea pathway and are unchanged at the end.

#### Mechanisms and the Rate-Determining Step

A **mechanism** is a sequence of elementary steps whose **molecularities** (uni, bi, termolecular) sum to the stoichiometry. The **slowest step is the rate-determining step**; its molecularity fixes the observed rate law. **Intermediates** formed in fast prior steps cancel during derivation.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Collision Theory vs Transition State Theory

**Collision theory** requires collisions with energy ≥ Ea **and** correct orientation. Rate = Z·ρ·f, where Z is collision frequency, ρ is the steric/orientation factor, and f is the fraction with Ea = e^(−Ea/RT). **Transition state theory** adds the idea of an **activated complex** at the energy maximum; equilibrium between reactants and the activated complex gives the Eyring rate expression, explaining why a catalyst shortens the path to products.

#### Arrhenius in Practice

The linear form, ln k = ln A − (Ea/R)(1/T), is plotted with 1/T on the x-axis (in K⁻¹). Slope = −Ea/R, so Ea = −slope × 8.314 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. Two-temperature form:

> ln(k₂/k₁) = (Ea/R)(1/T₁ − 1/T₂)

#### Edge Cases and Common Traps

1. **Order can be zero, fractional, or negative**; only elementary steps have whole-number molecularity.
2. **Pseudo-order** arises when one reactant is in vast excess (e.g. hydrolysis in water), making its effective concentration constant.
3. **Catalysts do not alter ΔH, K, or yield**, only how fast equilibrium is reached.
4. Plot choice matters: only the correct [A] vs t, ln[A] vs t, or 1/[A] vs t graph is linear for a given order.

#### Worked Micro-Example

For a first-order reaction, [A]₀ = 0.100 mol dm⁻³ and k = 2.6 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹.
- t½ = 0.693 / (2.6 × 10⁻³) = **267 s** (≈ 4.4 min).
- Time for [A] to fall to 0.020 mol dm⁻³: t = ln(0.100/0.020)/k = ln 5 / 2.6 × 10⁻³ ≈ **617 s**.

1. A reaction is 50% complete in 25 min and 75% complete in 50 min. Identify the order and calculate k.
2. From Arrhenius data, ln k = 14.0 at 300 K and ln k = 17.5 at 320 K. Determine Ea in kJ mol⁻¹.
