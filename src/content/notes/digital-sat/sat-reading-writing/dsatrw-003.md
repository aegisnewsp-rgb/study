# Craft and Structure: Cross-Text Connections — Digital SAT Reading and Writing Notes

Cross-Text Connections items present two short passages (Passage 1 and Passage 2) addressing a shared topic, debate, or historical phenomenon. In the official Digital SAT examination, this topic carries a weight rating of 4 out of 5 and appears consistently across testing administrations. Candidates preparing for high percentiles must master both theoretical fundamentals and rapid practical problem-solving strategies.

> Verify live testing patterns and syllabus specifications on official examination portals before scheduling your test sitting.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Weight & Frequency**: Rated 4/5 in priority; this topic appears reliably in every sitting.
- **Timing Goal**: Aim for disciplined pacing per item to leave sufficient buffer for complex multi-step problems at the end of the section.
- **Core Principle**: Compare how Passage 2 author would react to a specific claim or methodological choice in Passage 1.
- **Common pitfall**: Identify the point of contention or agreement between the two authors. Never assume they disagree on everything; often Passage 2 accepts Passage 1's facts while disputing the interpretation.
- **Systematic Strategy**: Always eliminate obviously incorrect distractors before attempting detailed calculations or granular textual analysis.
- **Answer Verification**: Cross-check final values or textual line references against the explicit wording of the prompt to avoid answering the wrong question.

#### Examiner traps

- Misreading key constraint words in the prompt (such as *except*, *not*, *least*, *integer*, *constant*).
- Falling for attractive partial answers that represent an intermediate calculation step rather than the final required quantity.
- Basing textual answers on outside background knowledge rather than direct evidence supplied within the passage.
- Spending excessive time struggling on a single difficult problem instead of flagging it, making an educated guess, and maintaining forward momentum.

### 🔴 Deep Dive — Mastery & Edge Cases (3mo–2yr)

#### Advanced variations and high-difficulty edge cases

Cross-text items ask about relationships between authors rather than about either one alone:

- **Connection Type**: Classify each relationship as agreement, contrast, extension, example, or shared cause, quoting the words that trigger your choice in both texts.
- **Anchored Comparison**: State one connection, cite a detail from each source, and explain what the comparison reveals beyond the two isolated points in the response.
- **Synthesis Boundary**: Keep the writer's separate positions distinct, then build a comparison that answers the prompt without flattening disagreements between the texts themselves.

#### Systematic drill schedule

Cross-text items reward deciding the type of connection before comparing content:

- **Relationship naming**: Name the relationship first: reinforces, qualifies, contradicts, or applies a method. The type determines what a valid comparison looks like.
- **Quoted anchoring**: Quote the specific line from each passage before judging them. Comparing from memory is where cross-text items are lost.
- **All-or-nothing test**: Reject any option asserting agreement or disagreement on everything. Two authors who agree on a method can still differ sharply in conclusion.
