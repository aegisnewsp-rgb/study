# Biotechnology

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Biotechnology applies biological systems and living organisms — chiefly enzymes, plasmids, and recombinant DNA — to make products such as insulin, Bt cotton, and the hepatitis B vaccine. The NEET UG Zoology paper pulls 3–4 questions from this chapter, mostly on rDNA steps, restriction enzymes, PCR, vectors, and biopiracy.

- **Core process:** restriction digestion → ligation into vector → transformation → selection → expression → downstream processing.
- **Two enzymes to memorise:** EcoRI cuts **GAATTC**, HindIII cuts **AAGCTT**; both leave sticky 5′ overhangs.
- **PCR triplet:** denaturation **94–95 °C**, annealing **50–60 °C**, extension **72 °C** with Taq polymerase.
- **Somatic vs germ-line gene therapy:** only the treated individual is cured in somatic; germ-line changes are heritable and ethically restricted in humans.
- **Biopiracy cases:** Basmati (Ricetec, USA), turmeric (USA patent revoked), and neem — all cited under India's Biological Diversity Act, 2002.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Recombinant DNA Technology Steps

The six-step pipeline begins with **isolation of the desired DNA** and ends with **downstream processing** of the expressed product. The cutting tool is a **restriction endonuclease** that recognises a palindromic 4–8 bp sequence; the joining tool is **DNA ligase** (ATP-dependent in E. coli, NAD⁺-dependent in T4 phage). The vehicle is a **cloning vector** — most in many papers plasmid **pBR322**, which carries two antibiotic-resistance genes (**amp^R**, **tet^R**), an **ori** (origin of replication), and multiple restriction sites.

#### Key Vectors and Markers

| Vector / Marker | Key feature | NEET-relevant detail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| pBR322 | 4.36 kb plasmid | amp^R has PstI site; tet^R has BamHI/HindIII/SalI sites |
| Ti plasmid | Agrobacterium tumefaciens | T-DNA segment integrates into plant nuclear genome |
| amp^R | Selectable marker | Insertional inactivation via PstI gives ampicillin-sensitive recombinants |
| lacZ (blue-white) | Screening marker | Recombinants appear white, non-recombinants blue on X-gal |

#### PCR Amplification

Each cycle **doubles** the target DNA copies. After *n* cycles starting from C₀, copies = C₀ × (1+e)ⁿ, where *e* is per-cycle efficiency (≈1 under ideal conditions). Required reagents: **template DNA, two primers (forward and reverse), dNTPs, Taq polymerase, and Mg²⁺ as cofactor**. The melting temperature follows **Tₘ = 81.5 + 16.6·log₁₀[Na⁺] + 0.41·(%GC) − 600/length (bp)**, and annealing is in standard papers set 3–5 °C below Tₘ.

- Gel electrophoresis uses **agarose gel** in TAE/TBE buffer; DNA migrates toward the **anode (+)** because it is negatively charged.
- **ELISA** detects antigen or antibody using enzyme-linked antibodies (in most keys **alkaline phosphatase** or **HRP**) producing a colour change.
- **Bt cotton** expresses **cry genes** (e.g., *cryIAc*) from *Bacillus thuringiensis*; the protoxin is activated by **alkaline pH** in the bollworm midgut.
- Insulin from *E. coli* uses Eli Lilly's process — chains A and B are produced separately and joined by disulphide bonds.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Gene Therapy, Bioethics, and Biopiracy

**Somatic gene therapy** introduces a functional gene into body cells (e.g., lymphocytes for ADA-SCID) and affects only the patient. **Germ-line therapy** alters gametes or zygotes; changes pass to offspring and are banned for humans under India's ICMR guidelines (2017) and most international frameworks. **Gene therapy is currently permitted only for somatic cells** in clinical trials.

#### Transgenic Applications and Distinctions

| Application | Host organism | Gene / Product | Function |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bt cotton | *Gossypium* | *cryIAc* / Cry toxin | Kills *Helicoverpa* bollworm larvae |
| Flavr Savr tomato | *Solanum lycopersicum | Antisense polygalacturonase | Delays fruit softening |
| Hepatitis B vaccine | *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* | Surface antigen (HBsAg) | Recombinant subunit vaccine |
| Human insulin | *Escherichia coli* | Insulin chains A & B | First rDNA therapeutic, 1982 |

#### Common Mistakes and Exam Traps

- Mixing **extension (72 °C)** with **denaturation (94–95 °C)** — these temperatures are not interchangeable.
- Stating that Bt toxin kills the adult moth; it actually kills the **larva** after gut activation.
- Believing **T-DNA = Ti plasmid**; T-DNA is only the segment transferred and integrated, not the whole 200 kb plasmid.
- Confusing **ELISA** with **Western blot** — ELISA works on soluble antigens/antibodies; Western blot resolves proteins by molecular weight on a membrane.
- Treating **biopiracy** as general biodiversity loss — it specifically means unauthorised commercialisation of bioresources or traditional knowledge without benefit-sharing.

#### Practice Prompts

1. A vector has *PstI* cutting *amp^R* and *BamHI* cutting *tet^R*. A gene is ligated at *PstI*; predict the phenotype on ampicillin and tetracycline plates and explain insertional inactivation.
2. List, in order, the temperatures used in one PCR cycle and name the enzyme active at each non-initial step; justify why Mg²⁺ is essential.
