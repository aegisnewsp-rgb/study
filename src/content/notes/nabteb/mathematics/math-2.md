# Fractions, Decimals and Percentages

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Fractions, decimals, and percentages represent three interchangeable mathematical representations of proportional parts of a whole for the NABTEB National Technical Certificate (NTC) and National Business Certificate (NBC) Mathematics syllabus. Fluency in translating seamlessly between these three forms is essential for solving arithmetic, commercial finance, trade discounts, and mensuration problems.

| Numerical Representation | Standard Mathematical Form | Conversion Procedure to Other Forms | High-Frequency NABTEB Trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Common / Vulgar Fraction** | $\frac{a}{b}$ ($b \neq 0$) | Decimal: Divide numerator by denominator; Percent: Multiply fraction by $100\%$ | Forgetting to simplify to lowest terms, or adding denominators directly |
| **Terminating Decimal** | $0.d_1 d_2 \dots d_k$ | Fraction: Write over $10^k$ and reduce; Percent: Shift decimal point two places right | Misplacing decimal points when multiplying by decimals $< 1$ |
| **Recurring / Repeating Decimal** | $0.\bar{a}$ or $0.a\bar{b}$ | Set $x = 0.\bar{a}$, multiply by $10^k$, and subtract original equation | Treating $0.\dot{3}$ as $3/10$ instead of $1/3$ |
| **Percentage** | $p\% = \frac{p}{100}$ | Fraction: Write over $100$ and cancel; Decimal: Shift decimal point two places left | Computing percentage increase on final value instead of original cost |

#### 2. Operations with Fractions and Order of Precedence

Calculations with fractions adhere strictly to the BODMAS / PEMDAS convention:
1. **Brackets**: Evaluate expressions inside parentheses first.
2. **Of**: Multiplicative evaluation connecting quantities (e.g., $\frac{2}{3} \text{ of } ₦1,200 = \frac{2}{3} \times 1,200 = ₦800$).
3. **Division**: Invert the divisor fraction and multiply: $\frac{a}{b} \div \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a}{b} \times \frac{d}{c}$.
4. **Multiplication**: Multiply numerators together and denominators together: $\frac{a}{b} \times \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a c}{b d}$.
5. **Addition and Subtraction**: Find the Lowest Common Multiple (LCM) of denominators before adding or subtracting numerators.

| Operation Form | Algebraic Rule | Numerical Example | Common Error to Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fraction Addition** | $\frac{a}{b} + \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a d + b c}{b d}$ | $\frac{3}{4} + \frac{2}{5} = \frac{15 + 8}{20} = \frac{23}{20} = 1\frac{3}{20}$ | Adding across: $\frac{3+2}{4+5} = \frac{5}{9}$ (Incorrect) |
| **Fraction Division** | $\frac{a}{b} \div \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a d}{b c}$ | $\frac{5}{8} \div \frac{3}{4} = \frac{5}{8} \times \frac{4}{3} = \frac{20}{24} = \frac{5}{6}$ | Inverting dividend instead of divisor |
| **Mixed Number Conversion** | $A\frac{b}{c} = \frac{A c + b}{c}$ | $3\frac{2}{7} = \frac{3 \times 7 + 2}{7} = \frac{23}{7}$ | Adding whole number to numerator without multiplying denominator |

#### Recurring Decimal Algebraic Proofs

A recurring decimal is converted into a vulgar fraction by creating two equations that isolate the repeating block.

**Proof: Converting $0.1\dot{6}$ to a Vulgar Fraction:**
1. Let $x = 0.1666\dots$
2. Multiply by $10$ to place non-repeating digits before the decimal:
   $$10x = 1.6666\dots \quad \text{--- (Equation 1)}$$
3. Multiply by $100$ to include one full repeating cycle:
   $$100x = 16.6666\dots \quad \text{--- (Equation 2)}$$
4. Subtract Equation 1 from Equation 2:
   $$100x - 10x = 16.6666\dots - 1.6666\dots$$
   $$90x = 15$$
5. Solve for $x$ and reduce to lowest terms:
   $$x = \frac{15}{90} = \frac{1}{6}$$

#### Commercial Arithmetic: Profit, Loss, and Trade Discount Schedules

In business and technical commerce, percentage problems require identifying the correct base quantity:
- $\%\text{ Profit} = \frac{\text{Selling Price} - \text{Cost Price}}{\text{Cost Price}} \times 100\%$
- $\%\text{ Loss} = \frac{\text{Cost Price} - \text{Selling Price}}{\text{Cost Price}} \times 100\%$
- Successive trade discounts ($d_1\%$ and $d_2\%$) do not add directly:
  $$\text{Net Multiplier} = (1 - d_1)(1 - d_2)$$

| Transaction Type | Base Quantity for Percentage | Formula | Exam Application |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Markup on Cost** | Cost Price ($CP$) | $\text{Selling Price} = CP \times (1 + \text{Markup}\%)$ | Pricing fabricated metal components |
| **Margin on Selling Price** | Selling Price ($SP$) | $CP = SP \times (1 - \text{Margin}\%)$ | Retail hardware pricing |
| **Trade Discount** | Marked / List Price | $\text{Invoice Price} = \text{List Price} \times (1 - \text{Discount}\%)$ | Wholesale electrical parts supply |
| **Value Added Tax (VAT)** | Net Taxable Amount ($7.5\%$ in Nigeria) | $\text{Total Invoice} = \text{Net Amount} \times 1.075$ | Final commercial contract billing |

#### Worked NABTEB Examination Solutions

**Problem 1: Complex Multi-Operation Fraction Evaluation**  
*Problem:* Evaluate the following expression without using a mathematical table or calculator:
$$\frac{3\frac{1}{4} - 1\frac{2}{3}}{2\frac{1}{2} \times 1\frac{1}{5}} \div \frac{19}{36}$$

*Step-by-Step Solution:*
1. Convert all mixed numbers into improper fractions:
   $$3\frac{1}{4} = \frac{13}{4}, \quad 1\frac{2}{3} = \frac{5}{3}, \quad 2\frac{1}{2} = \frac{5}{2}, \quad 1\frac{1}{5} = \frac{6}{5}$$
2. Simplify the numerator:
   $$\frac{13}{4} - \frac{5}{3} = \frac{39 - 20}{12} = \frac{19}{12}$$
3. Simplify the denominator:
   $$\frac{5}{2} \times \frac{6}{5} = \frac{30}{10} = 3$$
4. Divide numerator by denominator:
   $$\frac{19/12}{3} = \frac{19}{12} \times \frac{1}{3} = \frac{19}{36}$$
5. Perform final division:
   $$\frac{19}{36} \div \frac{19}{36} = \frac{19}{36} \times \frac{36}{19} = 1$$
*Final Answer:* The evaluated expression equals **$1$**.

**Problem 2: Successive Discounts and Net Realization**  
*Problem:* A building contractor buys plumbing fixtures listed at $₦480,000$. The supplier offers successive trade discounts of $15\%$ and $5\%$, plus an additional cash discount of $2\%$ for immediate settlement within $10$ days. Calculate the final cash amount paid by the contractor.

*Step-by-Step Solution:*
1. Calculate amount after first trade discount ($15\%$):
   $$\text{Discount 1} = 0.15 \times ₦480,000 = ₦72,000$$
   $$\text{Balance 1} = ₦480,000 - ₦72,000 = ₦408,000$$
2. Calculate amount after second trade discount ($5\%$):
   $$\text{Discount 2} = 0.05 \times ₦408,000 = ₦20,400$$
   $$\text{Invoice Price} = ₦408,000 - ₦20,400 = ₦387,600$$
3. Calculate cash discount ($2\%$ on invoice price):
   $$\text{Cash Discount} = 0.02 \times ₦387,600 = ₦7,752$$
   $$\text{Final Cash Paid} = ₦387,600 - ₦7,752 = ₦379,848$$
*Final Answer:* The final cash amount paid by the contractor is **$₦379,848$**.

#### Common Traps and Exam Pitfalls

- **Adding Successive Discounts**: Successive discounts of $15\%$ and $5\%$ do not equal a single $20\%$ discount ($15\% + 5\%$ gives $₦384,000$, whereas true successive discounting yields $₦387,600$).
- **Percentage Change Base**: In a problem where a price decreases from $₦2,500$ to $₦2,000$, the percentage decrease is $\frac{500}{2500} \times 100\% = 20\%$, not $\frac{500}{2000} \times 100\% = 25\%$.
- **Repeating Decimal Notations**: Distinguish between $0.35$ (terminating, $\frac{35}{100} = \frac{7}{20}$), $0.\dot{3}\dot{5}$ (repeating $35$, $\frac{35}{99}$), and $0.3\dot{5}$ (repeating only $5$, $\frac{35 - 3}{90} = \frac{32}{90} = \frac{16}{45}$).
