# Moving Charges

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

**Moving Charges** is the CUET UG Physics unit linking electric current to magnetism. Hans Christian Oersted showed in 1820 that a current-carrying wire deflects a nearby compass needle, proving that **moving charges produce a magnetic field**.

The two formulas you must memorise are:

- **Lorentz force:** F = qvB sinθ (force on a single moving charge, units: N)
- **Force on a conductor:** F = BIL sinθ (macroscopic form, length L in m)

For a charge moving perpendicular to a uniform B, the path is circular with radius **r = mv / qB** and cyclotron frequency **f = qB / 2πm**.

High-yield exam pointers:

- Use **Fleming's left-hand rule** for force direction on a current.
- Use the **right-hand thumb rule** for the direction of B around a wire.
- In a velocity selector (crossed E and B), only charges with **v = E/B** pass undeflected.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Current and Oersted's discovery

Electric current is the rate of flow of charge, **I = q/t** in amperes. Conventional current runs opposite to electron drift. Oersted placed a straight wire above a compass needle; when current flowed, the needle rotated perpendicular to the wire, demonstrating that a magnetic field circles the conductor.

#### Biot–Savart law

The magnetic field at a distance r from a small current element I d**l** is:

$$dB = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \cdot \frac{I\, dl \sin\theta}{r^2}$$

where μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ T·m/A, and θ is the angle between the current element and the line joining it to the field point. This is the magnetic counterpart of Coulomb's law.

#### Ampere's circuital law

For any closed loop, **∮ B · dl = μ₀ I_enclosed**. Applying it to a long solenoid gives the uniform interior field **B = μ₀ n I**, where n is turns per unit length.

#### Force and torque on conductors

| Quantity | Formula | Key variable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Force on straight wire | F = BIL sinθ | L in metres |
| Torque on current loop | τ = NBIA sinθ | A = loop area |
| Magnetic moment | m = NIA n̂ | direction: right-hand rule |

Exam pattern notes:

- Numerical questions on the **field on the axis of a circular loop** are common.
- One CUET question per paper typically tests Lorentz force or cyclotron radius.
- Assertion–reason items often probe direction rules.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Lorentz force and circular motion

The general Lorentz force on a charge is **F = q(E + v × B)**. In a pure magnetic field, F is always perpendicular to v, so the kinetic energy stays constant and the charge spirals or circles. Only the velocity component perpendicular to B drives circular motion; the parallel component produces uniform drift. Hence the correct radius is **r = m v⊥ / qB**, not r = mv / qB unless v ⊥ B.

#### Cyclotron and velocity selector

A **cyclotron** uses a perpendicular B to bend a charged particle into a circular path while an alternating electric field between two "dees" accelerates it each half-revolution. The frequency of the AC source must equal the cyclotron frequency f = qB / 2πm, which is independent of particle speed — a key conceptual point.

A **velocity selector** has crossed, perpendicular E and B fields. Setting qE = qvB gives **v = E/B**; only charges at this speed travel straight through and enter the next region of pure B, where they are sorted by mass in a mass spectrometer.

#### Common mistakes students make

- Forgetting that magnetic force does **zero work**, so speed (not velocity) is unchanged.
- Swapping Fleming's left-hand rule with the right-hand thumb rule.
- Writing F = qvB without sinθ when v is not perpendicular to B.
- Believing a solenoid's exterior field is strong; inside an ideal long solenoid it is uniform and strong, outside it is nearly zero.

#### Worked micro-example

A proton (m = 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ kg, q = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C) enters B = 0.5 T perpendicular to the field with v = 2 × 10⁶ m/s. The radius is r = mv / qB = (1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ × 2 × 10⁶) / (1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 0.5) = 4.18 × 10⁻² m ≈ 4.2 cm. The period T = 2πm / qB ≈ 1.31 × 10⁻⁷ s, independent of v.

#### Practice prompts

1. A circular loop of radius R carries current I. Find B on the axis at distance x from the centre, and show B → μ₀I / 2R at the centre (x = 0).
2. Two long parallel wires 5 cm apart carry 2 A each in the same direction. Compute the force per unit length and state whether it is attractive or repulsive.
