# Data Interpretation from Simple Tables

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Data Interpretation from Simple Tables means reading numbers organised in rows and columns, then answering questions using addition, subtraction, percentages, ratios, or averages. The table's **heading** tells you what each row and column measures; every entry sits at the intersection of one row and one column.

#### Must-know operations:

- **Total** = sum of all relevant entries in the chosen row or column.
- **Difference** = larger value − smaller value.
- **Percentage** = (part ÷ whole) × 100%.
- **Average** = sum of values ÷ number of values.
- **Ratio** = a : b, then simplify by dividing both terms by their highest common factor.

| Operation | When to use | Quick check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Addition | "How much altogether?" | Same units in every entry |
| Percentage | "What % of the whole?" | Part must be inside the whole |
| Average | "How much per item?" | Sum divided by count |
| Ratio | "How many times more?" | Compare two like quantities |

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Reading a Table Correctly
Start with the **title** and the **column headings**, then the **row labels**. Each cell gives a single value for one category measured at one time or condition. Units (naira, kilograms, pupils, bags) must match before you add or compare.

#### The Six Question Types
NCEE tables usually ask one of these six things:

1. **Total** — sum a column or row.
2. **Difference** — subtract to compare two entries.
3. **Percentage** — part ÷ whole × 100%.
4. **Ratio** — express two values as a : b, then simplify.
5. **Average** — total ÷ number of entries.
6. **Conclusion** — pick the statement that the numbers actually support.

#### Worked Pattern (Sales Table)
Imagine a table of rice bags sold: Monday 20, Tuesday 35, Wednesday 15, Thursday 30, Friday 25.

- Total sold = 20 + 35 + 15 + 30 + 25 = **125 bags**.
- Wednesday was **125 − 15 = 110 bags** fewer than the total — so Wednesday alone is 15/125 = **12%** of weekly sales.
- Highest day (Tuesday, 35) ÷ Lowest day (Wednesday, 15) gives ratio **35 : 15 = 7 : 3**.

| Question style | Operation | Trap to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "How many more…?" | Subtraction | Confusing which value is larger |
| "What percentage…?" | Part ÷ whole × 100 | Using wrong denominator |
| "What is the average…?" | Sum ÷ count | Forgetting to count every entry |

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Edge Cases and Traps
- **Mixed units:** A table may list values in thousands (₦000). Read the heading before dividing.
- **Cumulative vs daily figures:** "Total to date" includes earlier periods; do not re-add them.
- **"How many times more" ≠ percentage:** "Three times more" means a ratio of 3 : 1, not 300%.
- **Range vs maximum:** Range = maximum − minimum, not just the maximum value alone.
- **Conclusion questions:** Only one option matches every entry; eliminate choices that contradict a single cell.

#### Practice Prompts
1. A table shows six pupils scoring 4, 6, 8, 5, 7, 6 marks. Find the **mean**, the **range**, and the **ratio** of the highest score to the lowest. Answer: mean = 6, range = 4, ratio = 8 : 4 = 2 : 1.
2. Out of 250 voters, 150 chose option A. What **percentage** chose option B, and what is the **ratio** of A to B? Answer: B = 100 voters = 40%; ratio A : B = 150 : 100 = 3 : 2.

#### Common Mistakes
- Adding entries from rows that measure different things.
- Dividing by the wrong whole in a percentage.
- Reporting a difference without naming the two categories.
- Treating "average" and "total" as the same answer.

| Skill | Quick method | Typical NCEE marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Total | Column addition | 1 mark |
| Percentage | Part ÷ whole × 100 | 2 marks |
| Average | Sum ÷ count | 2 marks |
| Ratio | Simplify by HCF | 2 marks |
