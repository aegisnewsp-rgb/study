# Drainage System of Rajasthan

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> One fact to fix in your head first: **the Aravalli decides everything.** It runs SW–NE across Rajasthan and splits the state's rivers into three groups.

Rajasthan carries three drainage families. East of the Aravalli, rivers (Chambal, Banas, Banganga) run into the Bay of Bengal via the Yamuna–Ganga network. West of the Aravalli, the Mahi and Sabarmati reach the Arabian Sea (Gulf of Khambhat). Between and south-west of the Aravalli lies an **endorheic (inland) belt** where rivers die in salt lakes or the Rann of Kutch — Luni is the headliner here.

- **Longest river in Rajasthan:** Luni, ~495 km, Ajmer to Rann of Kutch.
- **Largest inland salt lake:** Sambhar (Nagaur/Ajmer).
- **Most-asked trap:** the direction of Luni (SW, not south) and the source of Chambal (Janapav, Madhya Pradesh — not Rajasthan).

> 💡 **Memory Hook — "ABS"** for the three drainage types: **A**rabian Sea (west), **B**ay of Bengal (east), **S**alt-lake / Inland (endorheic). Picture the Aravalli as the letter **V** lying on its side — west arm = A, east arm = B, the trough inside = S.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Mid-depth pass: how each drainage family forms, which rivers belong where, and how examiners test the topic in RAS Prelims and Mains.

#### Three Drainage Families of Rajasthan

| Drainage Family | Direction | Aravalli Position | Key Rivers | Terminal Point |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bay of Bengal (perennial) | East | East of Aravalli | Chambal, Banas, Banganga, Gambhir, Parvati, Kalisindh, Mej | Yamuna → Ganga → Bay of Bengal |
| Arabian Sea (perennial) | West | West of Aravalli | Mahi, Sabarmati (headwaters), Som, Jakham | Gulf of Khambhat → Arabian Sea |
| Inland / Endorheic (seasonal) | SW or terminate inland | South-west of Aravalli | Luni, Jawai, Bandi, Sukri, Khari, Guhia, Ghaggar-Hakra | Rann of Kutch / salt lakes / sand |

#### Luni — the Salient Endorheic River

Luni rises at **Naga hills near Ajmer** (Pushkar valley region) and runs **south-west** through Pali, Jodhpur, Barmer and Jalore. Total length inside Rajasthan is about **495 km**, making it the longest river entirely or substantially within the state. Water becomes saline past Balotra, so it is poor for direct irrigation but is impounded in dams (Jawai, Sardar Samand, Bisalpur-linked systems) for storage.

**Major tributaries (right bank):** Jawai, Bandi, Sukri, Khari, Guhia.

#### Chambal and its Eastern Network

Chambal originates at **Janapav Kuti (Mhow, Madhya Pradesh)** — not in Rajasthan — and enters the state near Chittorgarh, carving the famous **Chambal ravines** before joining the Yamuna in Uttar Pradesh. Rajasthan's major tributaries that meet it are **Banas, Parvati, Kalisindh, Mej and Chakan**. The Banas itself rises near **Khamnor hills, Kumbhalgarh (Rajsamand)** and is fed by Berach, Mashi, Morel and Kothari.

#### Worked Resolution of a Typical RAS Question

**Statement-style item:** *"Luni river is a tributary of the Chambal."* — Is this true?

1. Trace Luni's course: Ajmer → Pali → Jodhpur → Barmer → Jalore → **Rann of Kutch**.
2. Trace Chambal's course: Janapav (MP) → Kota border → joins Yamuna (UP).
3. Compare terminal points: Luni dies in the Rann (endorheic); Chambal leaves the state northward.
4. Conclusion: the two rivers **never merge**. Luni is independent.

> ⚠️ **Examiner Trap:** the high-frequency wrong option in RAS Prelims pairs Luni with Chambal as "tributary." They share the Aravalli watershed region but belong to **different drainage families** (inland vs Bay of Bengal). Pick this correctly and you dodge one of the most repeated distractors.

#### Comparison Matrix — Luni vs Chambal

| Feature | Luni | Chambal |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | Naga hills, Ajmer (Rajasthan) | Janapav hills, Mhow (Madhya Pradesh) |
| Length in Rajasthan | ~495 km (longest in state) | ~135 km of major course inside Rajasthan |
| Direction | South-west | North-east (then north) |
| Drainage type | Inland / endorheic | Bay of Bengal (perennial) |
| Terminal point | Rann of Kutch | Yamuna river in UP |

#### Salt Lakes and Closed Basins

Rajasthan has about **15 closed basins** occupying roughly **61% of the state's area**. The four commercially important salt lakes are **Sambhar, Didwana, Pachpadra and Lunkaransar**. Salinity builds up because evaporation far exceeds inflow in the arid climate; Sambhar alone supplies a large share of India's inland salt production.

> 📌 **Quick recall:** Sambhar is **largest by area**, Didwana and Pachpadra sit in Nagaur–Barmer belt, Lunkaransar lies in Bikaner. None of them feeds the sea — all are terminal evaporative basins.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Edge cases, ancient-river debates, sub-basin structure, and the traps RAS paper-setters exploit when they want to differentiate rank holders.

#### Sub-basin Structure and Catchment Numbers

The state is officially organised into **9 major river basins and 19 minor / sub-basins**, covering a total catchment of roughly **3.42 lakh sq. km**. The largest sub-basin is the Chambal complex (Chambal + Banas + tributaries), followed by the Luni inland basin. Only about **1% of the state's geographical area** carries perennial surface flow — that single statistic explains why every dam, anicut and lift project in Rajasthan is politically charged.

#### Ghaggar-Hakra — the "Lost" Vedic River

Ghaggar enters Rajasthan near **Ganganagar** and flows through **Hanumangarh** before vanishing in the Thar sands of Haryana. Most geomorphologists identify it with the **Vedic Saraswati**. Do not call it a tributary of the Indus — it never reaches the Indus system; it is a defunct seasonal channel of the inland family. RSPS papers love asking "Ghaggar is a tributary of _____" with "Indus" as the planted wrong option.

#### Advanced Traps and Edge Cases

1. **Banganga vs Banas** — both rise in Rajasthan but at different places (Banganga in Jaipur district → Yamuna system; Banas in Khamnor hills → Chambal) and join different parent rivers.
2. **Mahi's classification** — although it originates in the Vindhyas (MP), its lower course through Banswara and Pratapgarh is in Rajasthan and it is **west-flowing, draining into the Gulf of Khambhat** (Arabian Sea system). It is **not** inland drainage.
3. **Chambal's ravines** — formed by gully erosion in the alluvial upland between Kota and Etawah; they hide the **National Chambal Sanctuary** (gharial, mugger, Gangetic dolphin).
4. **Didwana vs Sambhar** — Didwana is shallower and seasonal; Sambhar is the only one with year-round brine and commercial salt works since the **Sambhar Salts Limited (Sambhar Salts Pvt. Ltd., a subsidiary of Hindustan Salts Ltd.)** operations.
5. **Aravalli's hydrological role** — beyond being a divide, the range's elevation (~900–1100 m average) forces orographic rainfall on its eastern flank, sustaining the headwaters of Banas, Banganga and Chambal's tributaries.
6. **Endorheic percentage trick** — a frequent Mains question asks "what percentage of Rajasthan has inland drainage?" The safe answer to reproduce is "about **three-fifths (~61%) of the state area is covered by closed basins**," without inventing precise hydrological fractions.

#### Two Advanced Practice Prompts

1. **Map-sketch question:** Draw the Aravalli as a diagonal line and label three rivers on each side with their drainage family and terminal point. Add the four salt lakes with district names.
2. **200-word Mains answer:** "Explain why western Rajasthan is dominated by inland drainage and discuss its economic consequences." Cover: arid climate, evaporation > inflow, salinity, scope for salt extraction, water harvesting through tanks and anicuts.

> 📌 **Last-mile tip for the exam hall:** before writing any drainage answer, mentally place the Aravalli. If the river is east of it, default to **Bay of Bengal**; if west of it but reaches the sea, it is **Arabian Sea**; if it dies in the desert or a salt lake, it is **inland**. Three out of every four RAS drainage questions resolve under this one mental test.
