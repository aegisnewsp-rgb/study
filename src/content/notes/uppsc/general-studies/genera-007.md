# Sectoral Structure & National Income

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your exam.

Sectoral structure maps an economy's output and labour across **primary** (agriculture, forestry, fishing, mining), **secondary** (manufacturing, construction, utilities) and **tertiary** (trade, transport, banking, public services) activities. **National income** is the net income accruing to normal residents of a country from domestic and foreign production during an accounting year.

The aggregation chain from output to disposable income follows a fixed order: **GVA → GDP → GNI → NNI → PI → DI**.

- **GVA (mp)** = Output − Intermediate consumption
- **GDP (mp)** = Σ GVA at basic prices + Product taxes − Product subsidies
- **GNI (mp)** = GDP (mp) + Net primary income from abroad (NFIA)
- **NNI (mp)** = GNI (mp) − Depreciation (CFC)

UPPSC RO/ARO Prelims typically asks one MCQ on the GDP–NNP–National Income chain or on sectoral share trends; expect a statement–conclusion type item where the trap is conflating factor cost with market price.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Three-sector classification

The **primary sector** covers agriculture, livestock, forestry, logging, fishing, mining and quarrying. The **secondary sector** converts raw materials through manufacturing, construction, electricity, gas and water supply. The **tertiary sector** delivers services — trade, transport, banking, insurance, public administration, defence, education and health. Sector shares move with the structural transformation hypothesis: as per-capita income rises, agriculture's share of output and employment falls while services expand.

#### Valuation: market price, basic price and factor cost

Three valuation bases recur in UPPSC questions. **Market price** includes product taxes net of product subsidies. **Basic price** excludes product taxes but includes product subsidies, matching the producer's receipts. **Factor cost** equals basic price minus other production taxes plus other production subsidies, leaving only payments to factors — compensation of employees, operating surplus and mixed income.

#### National income aggregates

National income in Indian accounts is **NNP at factor cost**, identical to **NNI at factor cost**. The bridge from GDP (mp) to NNP (fc) is:

GDP (mp) → (− Depreciation + NFIA) → NNI (mp) → (− Net indirect taxes) → NNI (fc).

| Aggregate | Definition | Key adjustment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GDP (mp) | Value produced within domestic territory | Includes product taxes, excludes product subsidies |
| NDP (fc) | GDP − depreciation, at factor cost | Net of consumption of fixed capital |
| GNI (mp) | GDP + net primary income from abroad | Residents' gross income |
| NNP (fc) | National income | Depreciation and net indirect taxes deducted |

#### Personal and disposable income

**Personal income (PI)** = National income − Undistributed corporate profits − Corporate tax − Social-security contributions + Transfer payments. **Disposable income (DI)** = PI − Personal direct taxes. Neither PI nor DI is a measure of production; both are post-distribution measures used in household consumption analysis.

#### Common traps

- Treating GDP as "income of the nation" without deducting depreciation.
- Using gross output (sales × price) instead of value added, which double-counts intermediate inputs.
- Comparing current-price sector shares across years and reading the change as a real-volume shift.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Measurement approaches

National income is measured by three approaches that must reconcile. The **production approach** sums gross value added at basic prices across resident producing units and adds product taxes less product subsidies to obtain GDP (mp). The **income approach** aggregates factor incomes — compensation of employees, operating surplus, mixed income — and adjusts to market-price valuation. The **expenditure approach** sums final consumption, gross capital formation and net exports. Any divergence among the three in published data is a statistical discrepancy reported by MoSPI.

#### Current vs. constant prices

Series at **current prices** mix volume and price changes. Series at **constant prices** (base year 2011-12 in the latest NAS) hold prices fixed, isolating real growth. Per-capita comparisons across time should use constant-price NNP (fc); cross-country comparisons should ideally use **PPP exchange rates** from the World Bank/ICP rather than market exchange rates, which distort service-sector weights.

#### Worked micro-example

Suppose an economy produces only biscuits. Wheat farmer's output = ₹200, flour miller's output = ₹300, baker's output (final biscuits) = ₹500. Intermediate consumption of the miller = ₹200 (wheat); intermediate consumption of the baker = ₹300 (flour).

| Stage | Output (₹) | Intermediate consumption (₹) | GVA (₹) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wheat farmer | 200 | 0 | 200 |
| Flour miller | 300 | 200 | 100 |
| Baker | 500 | 300 | 200 |
| **Sum (GDP)** | — | — | **500** |

Summing outputs gives ₹1,000, which double-counts wheat and flour — the textbook error. Summing GVA correctly yields ₹500, equal to the final expenditure on biscuits, satisfying the production–expenditure identity.

#### Practice prompts

1. If GDP (mp) = ₹120, depreciation = ₹10, product taxes = ₹8, product subsidies = ₹2, and net primary income from abroad = −₹3, compute NNP (fc) and identify each step.
2. India's services share at current prices has risen faster than at constant prices since 2011-12. List two reasons (price effect + composition effect) and one implication for structural-change interpretation.
