# Consumer Behaviour

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

**Consumer Behaviour** studies how a household splits a fixed income across goods and services to maximise satisfaction (utility). CA Foundation Economics tests two parallel frameworks:

- **Cardinal Utility (Marshall)** — utility measurable in *utils*. Equilibrium follows the **Law of Equi-Marginal Utility**: `MUₓ / Pₓ = MUᵧ / Pᵧ = MUₘ` (marginal utility of money). Demand slopes downward because of the **Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility** (DMU).
- **Ordinal Utility (Hicks–Allen)** — utility is ranked, not measured. Equilibrium is at tangency of **Indifference Curve** and **Budget Line**, where **MRSₓᵧ = Pₓ / Pᵧ**.

The **Price Effect** decomposes into **Substitution Effect + Income Effect**; for a **Giffen good** (an extreme inferior good) the income effect is so large it outweighs substitution so demand slopes upward. Revision anchors: TU = ΣMU, PₓX + PᵧY = M, MRS = ΔY/ΔX.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### Cardinal Approach — Marshall
Utility is quantified. **Total Utility (TU)** is the sum of satisfactions from all units; **Marginal Utility (MU)** is the addition to TU from one extra unit: `MUₙ = TUₙ − TUₙ₋₁`. The **Law of DMU** says that as consumption of a good rises (holding others constant), MU falls — the consumer's willingness to pay drops, generating the inverse demand relationship `P = MUₘ⁻¹`.

**Equilibrium condition:** `MUₓ / Pₓ = MUᵧ / Pᵧ = MUₘ`, derived by maximising utility subject to the budget. If MUₓ/Pₓ > MUᵧ/Pᵧ, the consumer gains by spending one more rupee on X and one less on Y. **Consumer's Surplus** is the triangular area between the demand curve and the price line.

#### Ordinal Approach — Hicks & Allen
Utility is **ranked**, not measured, resting on four axioms: completeness, transitivity, non-satiation (more is better), and **convexity** (MRS diminishes as X increases — indifference curves bow toward the origin). An **Indifference Set** is the bundle graph; an **Indifference Map** stacks these curves.

The **Budget Line** `Pₓ·X + Pᵧ·Y = M` shows affordable bundles; its slope is `−Pₓ/Pᵧ`. The attainable region is the **Budget Set**.

**Equilibrium (ordinal):** tangency point where `MRSₓᵧ = Pₓ / Pᵧ`. At this bundle, the consumer's willingness to exchange Y for X exactly equals the market rate, and any shift reduces attainable satisfaction.

#### Price, Income and Substitution Effects
When price of X falls, the **Price Effect** = gain in real income. **Substitution Effect** (Hicks) reflects the move along the original indifference curve to a tangency with the new price ratio. **Income Effect** shifts to a higher indifference curve. For a **normal good** both effects raise demand; for an **inferior good** the income effect works against substitution but is smaller, so demand still slopes downward; for a **Giffen good** the negative income effect dominates and demand slopes upward.

| Concept | Cardinal Result | Ordinal Result |
|---|---|---|
| Equilibrium | MUₓ/Pₓ = MUᵧ/Pᵧ | MRSₓᵧ = Pₓ/Pᵧ |
| Slope of demand | Derived from DMU | Derived from Price Effect |
| Surplus | Marshallian area | Equivalent variation |

CA Foundation MCQs commonly test the equilibrium equation, properties of indifference curves, and direction of demand for normal/inferior/Giffen goods.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)

#### Worked Numerical — Equi-Marginal Choice
Suppose M = ₹80, Pₓ = ₹10, Pᵧ = ₹5. At a candidate bundle (X=5, Y=6):
- MUₓ = 20 utils, MUᵧ = 8 utils.
- MUₓ/Pₓ = 20/10 = **2**; MUᵧ/Pᵧ = 8/5 = **1.6**.
- Since 2 > 1.6, reallocate ₹1 from Y to X: buy (6, 4), leaving ₹0 to spend.
- New ratio: MU at (6,4) → assume MUₓ=16, MUᵧ=10 → 16/10 = **1.6**, 10/5 = **2.0**. Cross — optimum reached when both equalise at MUₘ = 1.6 utils per rupee.

#### Edge Cases & Assumptions
- **Giffen paradox**: violates monotonicity only in aggregate behaviour; it requires a strongly inferior staple whose expenditure share is large.
- **Quasi-linear preferences** flatten income effects: demand depends purely on substitution (MRS = Pₓ/Pᵧ irrespective of income).
- **Diminishing MRS** (convexity) is an empirical regularity, not a logical necessity; counter-examples include perfect substitutes (L-shaped indifference curves, MRS constant) and perfect complements (right-angled curves, MRS undefined).
- **Marshall vs. Hicks Consumer's Surplus**: Marshall's measure uses MU of money constant; Hicks uses compensating/equivalent variation — they coincide under constant MUₘ.

#### Common Mistakes
1. Confusing TU with MU slopes — TU rises at a decreasing rate; MU falls but stays positive until TU peaks.
2. Treating budget line shift as a rotation only when ONE price changes; if income changes, the line shifts parallel; if prices move proportionally, it slides.
3. Forgetting that on a typical MCQ the *MU of money* is constant only under the Marshallian assumption.
4. Assuming Giffen good = Veblen good; Veblen goods are conspicuous-demand goods unrelated to the income effect.

#### Practice Prompts
1. Given `MUₓ = 40 − 2X`, `MUᵧ = 30 − 3Y`, Pₓ = ₹4, Pᵧ = ₹6, M = ₹80, derive the consumer's optimal bundle and verify the equi-marginal condition.
2. With `Pₓ = ₹10`, price falls to ₹6. Decompose the change in quantity demanded into substitution and income effects using a sketch; classify X as normal, inferior, or Giffen based on direction.
