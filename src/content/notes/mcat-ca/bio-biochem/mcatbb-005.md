# Metabolism: Glycolysis, Krebs and Oxidative Phosphorylation — MCAT Bio/Biochem

Glycolysis, the citric acid (Krebs) cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation form the core of AAMC Foundational Concept 3 of the Bio/Biochem section. The MCAT tests the enzymes, intermediates, energy yield, and regulation of these central metabolic pathways. These topics underpin every metabolic passage on the exam and connect to biochemistry, physiology, and pharmacology.

### Lite — Quick Review (1h-1d)

#### Core concepts

- **Glycolysis:** glucose → 2 pyruvate. Net yield: 2 ATP, 2 NADH. Occurs in the cytosol. Ten steps; key regulatory enzyme: phosphofructokinase-1 (PFK-1).
- **Pyruvate dehydrogenase:** pyruvate → acetyl-CoA. Yields 1 NADH per pyruvate (2 NADH per glucose). Occurs in the mitochondrial matrix.
- **Citric acid cycle:** acetyl-CoA → 2 CO₂. Yields 3 NADH, 1 FADH₂, 1 GTP (per acetyl-CoA). Occurs in the mitochondrial matrix. Key regulatory enzyme: isocitrate dehydrogenase.
- **Oxidative phosphorylation:** NADH and FADH₂ donate electrons to the electron transport chain (ETC); the ETC pumps protons; ATP synthase uses the proton gradient to make ATP. Yields ~2.5 ATP per NADH, ~1.5 ATP per FADH₂.
- **ATP yield per glucose:** ~30-32 ATP (theoretical maximum). Actual yield is lower due to proton leak and the cost of transporting NADH from the cytosol into the mitochondrion.

#### One-pass drill

1. Identify the pathway: glycolysis, pyruvate dehydrogenase, citric acid cycle, or oxidative phosphorylation.
2. Recall the inputs, outputs, and regulatory enzymes.
3. Apply to the specific scenario.

#### Examiner traps

- Forgetting that glycolysis happens in the cytosol, not in the mitochondrion.
- Treating NADH and FADH₂ as equivalent. NADH yields more ATP than FADH₂.
- Confusing ATP yield per glucose. The number depends on the shuttle used (malate-aspartate yields more ATP than glycerol-3-phosphate).

### Standard — Regular Study (2d-2mo)

#### Glycolysis in detail

The MCAT tests the 10 steps of glycolysis at varying depths:

- **Investment phase (steps 1-5):** 2 ATP consumed. Glucose is phosphorylated, isomerised, and cleaved into two triose phosphates.
- **Payoff phase (steps 6-10):** 4 ATP and 2 NADH produced. The two triose phosphates are converted to pyruvate.
- **Net yield:** 2 ATP + 2 NADH per glucose.
- **Regulation:** PFK-1 is the key regulatory enzyme; activated by AMP, fructose-2,6-bisphosphate; inhibited by ATP and citrate.

#### Pyruvate dehydrogenase complex

Pyruvate is transported into the mitochondrial matrix and decarboxylated by the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex:

- **Inputs:** pyruvate, NAD⁺, CoA.
- **Outputs:** acetyl-CoA, NADH, CO₂.
- **Regulation:** inhibited by high NADH and acetyl-CoA (product inhibition); activated by high NAD⁺ and CoA.

#### Citric acid (Krebs) cycle

Acetyl-CoA enters the cycle by combining with oxaloacetate to form citrate:

- **Inputs:** acetyl-CoA, 3 NAD⁺, 1 FAD, 1 GDP + Pᵢ.
- **Outputs:** 2 CO₂, 3 NADH, 1 FADH₂, 1 GTP.
- **Regulation:** isocitrate dehydrogenase (inhibited by ATP and NADH; activated by ADP and Ca²⁺).

#### Oxidative phosphorylation

**Electron transport chain (ETC):** NADH and FADH₂ donate electrons to the ETC. Electrons pass through Complexes I, II, III, and IV. The energy released pumps protons from the matrix to the intermembrane space.

**ATP synthase:** uses the proton gradient to phosphorylate ADP to ATP. Called chemiosmosis.

**Yield:** ~2.5 ATP per NADH, ~1.5 ATP per FADH₂.

**Final electron acceptor:** O₂. Without O₂, the ETC stops, NADH accumulates, and the cycle halts.

#### Practice routine

- Take 5 glycolysis items. Identify the inputs, outputs, and regulatory enzymes.
- Take 5 Krebs cycle items. Identify the intermediates, NADH/FADH₂ yield, and regulation.
- Take 5 oxidative phosphorylation items. Identify the complexes, the proton gradient, and ATP yield.
- Take 5 integrated metabolism items. Trace glucose from glycolysis to the ETC.

### Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo-6mo)

#### How this topic connects to the other Bio/Biochem topics

- **Lipid and amino acid metabolism** feed into the Krebs cycle (acetyl-CoA from fatty acids; carbon skeletons from amino acids).
- **Photosynthesis** (in plants) reverses the process: CO₂ + H₂O → glucose + O₂.
- **Pharmacology** often targets metabolic enzymes: metformin activates AMPK; statins inhibit HMG-CoA reductase.

#### Common errors and the correction

- **"Glycolysis produces the most ATP."** Glycolysis yields only 2 ATP per glucose. Oxidative phosphorylation yields the most (~26-28 ATP per glucose).
- **"FADH₂ and NADH yield the same ATP."** NADH yields ~2.5 ATP; FADH₂ yields ~1.5 ATP.
- **"O₂ is needed for glycolysis."** Glycolysis does not require O₂. Anaerobic glycolysis converts pyruvate to lactate (in muscle) or ethanol (in yeast).

#### Exam technique

- For pathway items, identify the location (cytosol vs mitochondrion) and the inputs/outputs.
- For regulation items, identify the key regulatory enzyme and its activators/inhibitors.
- For yield items, use the standard ~2.5 ATP per NADH and ~1.5 ATP per FADH₂.
- For integrated items, trace the carbon from glucose to CO₂ and the electrons from NADH to O₂.
