# English reading and writing at Level 2 — NCEA UE Literacy Notes

NZQA publishes a list of Level 2 and Level 3 achievement standards from a range of subjects that contribute to the University Entrance literacy requirement. For English, the key Level 2 standards include AS 91101 (Analyse specified aspect(s) of studied written text(s)) and AS 91102 (Analyse specified aspect(s) of studied visual or oral text(s)), plus writing-focused standards such as AS 91105 (Produce creative writing) and AS 91106 (Produce formal writing). Students taking Level 2 English typically earn several UE literacy credits through these standards.

> Verify the live UE literacy standards spreadsheet on https://www2.nzqa.govt.nz/ncea/understanding-secondary-quals/university-entrance/literacy-for-ue/ before planning revision around the figures below.

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)

#### Core facts in one pass

- **Reading and writing credits** available at Level 2 through English achievement standards.
- Common standards: AS 91101 (Analyse written texts), AS 91102 (Analyse visual/oral texts), AS 91105 (Creative writing), AS 91106 (Formal writing).
- All count toward the UE literacy requirement (per the UE literacy spreadsheet published by NZQA).
- Most students in Level 2 English earn 8-12 literacy credits across the year, easily exceeding the 10-credit UE requirement.

#### Examiner traps

- Assuming "reading" and "writing" credits are interchangeable — UE requires at least 5 of each, so a student with 10 reading credits and 0 writing credits does not meet UE.
- Not checking the UE literacy spreadsheet — standards can shift between reading and writing contributions across years.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)

#### The English Level 2 standards contributing to UE literacy

The exact standards contributing to UE literacy are listed on the NZQA UE literacy spreadsheet. The Level 2 English standards typically include:

- **AS 91101** Analyse specified aspect(s) of studied written text(s) — credits contribute to UE reading and/or writing.
- **AS 91102** Analyse specified aspect(s) of studied visual or oral text(s) — credits contribute to UE reading and/or writing.
- **AS 91105** Produce creative writing — credits contribute to UE writing.
- **AS 91106** Produce formal writing — credits contribute to UE writing.

Some standards can have their credits split between reading and writing contributions (e.g., 4 credits might give 2 reading + 2 writing). The UE literacy spreadsheet specifies how each standard contributes.

#### How the credits map to UE

A typical Level 2 English programme has a student attempt 4-6 standards. Many of these will contribute to UE literacy. With careful planning, a student can earn 8-12 literacy credits at Level 2 alone — more than enough for UE, with surplus carried forward.

#### Reading vs writing contributions

The UE requirement is 5 reading credits and 5 writing credits. The reading contribution usually comes from analysis standards (AS 91101, AS 91102), and the writing contribution usually comes from production standards (AS 91105, AS 91106). A student doing both types will satisfy both halves of the UE requirement.

#### Preparation strategy

For each English standard:

1. **Read the standard carefully** — know what is being assessed.
2. **Look at exemplars** — NZQA publishes student work samples at each grade level.
3. **Practice the genre** — analysis essays, creative writing, formal writing.
4. **Plan before writing** — outline before drafting.
5. **Revise and proofread** — check for language features and accuracy.

#### Combining with the dedicated co-requisites

A student who has achieved US32403 (reading) and US32405 (writing) at Level 1 has already met the UE literacy requirement. Level 2 English credits are then surplus (and may help with NCEA Level 2 certificate credits, but not specifically with UE literacy once UE is met).

A student who has NOT achieved the dedicated co-requisites can use Level 2 English credits toward UE literacy instead, provided the credits meet the reading/writing split.

### 🔴 Deep — Long-Term Mastery (1mo–6mo)

#### Excellence in English

Merit in Level 2 English shows understanding; Excellence shows perceptive understanding with developed personal response.

For AS 91101 (Analyse written text):

- Merit: Identifies features, explains effects, supports with examples.
- Excellence: Identifies features, explains effects, supports with examples, AND develops a personal response that integrates with the analysis.

The Excellence answer weaves analysis and response together; the Merit answer presents them as separate sections.

#### Transferable skills

The English Level 2 standards test skills that are directly applicable to university study:

- Critical analysis of texts (essential for arts and humanities).
- Argumentation and evidence (essential for social sciences).
- Clear written communication (essential for all university work).

Achieving UE literacy through Level 2 English gives the student not just the credits but the skills for university-level work.

#### Common misconceptions (and the correction)

- "Level 1 English credits count toward UE." No — UE literacy credits must be at Level 2 or above.
- "Any English standard counts." Check the UE literacy spreadsheet — not all English standards contribute.
- "Reading and writing credits are the same." No — UE requires at least 5 of each.
- "Once you meet UE, more credits don't matter." They still count toward the NCEA Level 2 certificate and the Level 3 certificate.

#### Specification reference

The English Level 2 achievement standards are internally and externally assessed by the school. The UE literacy contribution for each standard is published on the NZQA UE literacy spreadsheet. Re-check the live spreadsheet on nzqa.govt.nz before final revision.
