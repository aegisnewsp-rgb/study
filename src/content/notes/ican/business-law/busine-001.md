# Key Facts for ICAN (Nigeria)

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your ICAN Business Law paper.

ICAN Business Law tests Nigerian statutory provisions, chiefly the **Companies and Allied Matters Act (CAMA) 2020**, alongside contract, tort, agency, partnership, and negotiable-instrument principles. The paper assumes you know landmark cases by name and ratio, not merely by outcome.

- A valid **contract** requires offer, acceptance, consideration, intention, capacity, and legality. Vitiating factors are misrepresentation, duress, undue influence, and mistake.
- A **condition** in a sale of goods contract goes to the root of the agreement; breach lets the buyer repudiate. A **warranty** entitles only to damages.
- Under *Salomon v A Salomon & Co Ltd [1897] AC 22*, an incorporated company has separate legal personality from its members.
- *Donoghue v Stevenson [1932] AC 562* established the neighbour principle: you owe a duty of care to persons closely and directly affected by your act.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months.

#### Core contract framework

Every Nigerian law of contract question reduces to whether a binding agreement exists and whether some vitiating factor defeats it. **Offer** is a clear willingness to be bound on specific terms; **acceptance** must mirror the offer (mirror-image rule). **Consideration** must move from the promisee but need not be adequate. **Intention to create legal relations** is presumed in commercial agreements. Capacity requires the parties to be of age (21 under Nigerian contract law for non-necessaries) and of sound mind.

#### Sale of Goods: conditions versus warranties

The Sale of Goods Act (applicable in Nigeria via received English law and the Act of 1893 as adapted) distinguishes stipulations that go to the substance of the contract from those that are collateral.

| Stipulation | Effect of breach | Buyer's remedy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Condition | Substantial failure of the contract | Repudiate and claim damages, or affirm and claim damages |
| Warranty | Minor breach only | Damages only; no right to reject |
| Innominate term | Depends on consequence | Repudiate only if breach substantially deprives benefit |

#### Company law fundamentals under CAMA 2020

CAMA 2020 abolished the constructive notice doctrine and rendered **ultra vires** largely inapplicable to a company's external dealings (s. 21). Promoters owe fiduciary duties before incorporation; directors owe fiduciary duties and a statutory duty of care, skill, and diligence after incorporation. The **Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC)** is the registry for incorporation, charges, and annual returns.

- Promoters owe a fiduciary duty to disclose any secret profit.
- Directors must disclose interests in proposed transactions (s. 303 CAMA 2020).
- A **holder in due course** takes a negotiable instrument free of equities only if value, good faith, and absence of notice coexist.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students on a longer study timeline.

#### Directors' duties: the two-track analysis

ICAN examiners separate the **fiduciary duty** (loyalty, no-conflict, no-profit, acting in good faith) from the **duty of care, skill, and diligence** (objective standard of a reasonably diligent director). A director who honestly believes a decision serves the company may still breach the no-conflict rule if a personal interest exists. CAMA 2020 Part C codifies both tracks and adds disclosure of related-party transactions and insider information. Penalty for insider dealing under the Investments and Securities Act is criminal, with director disqualification consequences.

#### Common mistakes examiners exploit

- Confusing **dissolution of partnership** (governed by the Partnership Act / LPA provisions) with **winding up of a company** (governed by CAMA Part F).
- Treating ordinary partnerships as separate legal persons; they are not.
- Citing *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* without stating the offer was made to the world at large.
- Assuming apparent authority equals actual authority; apparent authority arises from the principal's representation to a third party.
- Skipping the **two-stage Caparo test** (foreseeability + proximity) before applying *Donoghue v Stevenson*.

#### Practice prompts

1. A promoter signs a contract on behalf of a company not yet incorporated. Who is liable, and how does CAMA 2020 treat this after incorporation?
2. A buyer rejects goods for a breach that is neither a condition nor clearly a warranty. Apply the **innominate term** test from *Hong Kong Fir Shipping v Kawasaki Kisen Kaisha*.
