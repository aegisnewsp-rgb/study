# International Organizations & Relations

### 🟢 Lite — Quick Review (1h–1d)
> Rapid summary for last-minute revision before your SPSC General Knowledge paper.

International Organizations & Relations covers intergovernmental bodies (UN, IMF, World Bank, WTO, WHO, UNESCO, ILO, IAEA, OIC, SAARC, ECO, SCO) and the diplomatic treaties that produce them. For SPSC GK (about 3% weight), this sub-topic is pure fact-recall and usually yields 2–3 marks.

- **UN principal organs (6):** General Assembly, Security Council, ECOSOC, ICJ, Secretariat, Trusteeship Council (suspended).
- **UNSC composition:** 5 permanent (China, France, Russia, UK, US) with **veto power**, plus 10 non-permanent members elected for 2-year terms.
- **Pakistan's memberships:** founding UN member (1945), IMF, World Bank, OIC, SAARC (1985), ECO, and SCO (full member since 2017).
- **Headquarters quick-list:** UN → New York; WHO → Geneva; IMF & World Bank → Washington D.C.; OIC → Jeddah; SAARC → Kathmandu; ICJ → The Hague.

### 🟡 Standard — Regular Study (2d–2mo)
> Standard content for students with a few days to months of prep time.

#### UN System and Principal Organs

The United Nations operates through six principal organs listed in the UN Charter. The **General Assembly (UNGA)** is a deliberative body where every member has one vote; its resolutions are recommendatory, not binding. The **Security Council (UNSC)** holds enforcement power under Chapter VII, and only its decisions are legally binding under Article 25. The **International Court of Justice (ICJ)** settles legal disputes between states at The Hague, distinct from the International Criminal Court.

#### Specialized Agencies and Bretton Woods Twins

Article 57 of the UN Charter allows legally autonomous specialized agencies to enter relationship agreements with the UN. These include WHO (health), UNESCO (education/culture), ILO (labour), FAO (food), and IAEA (atomic energy). The **IMF** stabilizes the exchange-rate system and lends to countries facing balance-of-payments crises. The **World Bank Group** (IBRD, IDA, IFC, MIGA, ICSID) finances long-term development.

| Organization | Primary Mandate | Pakistan's Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IMF | Exchange-rate stability, BoP lending | Founding member |
| World Bank (IBRD) | Development lending | Member |
| WHO | Global health norms | Member |
| OIC | Muslim-world cooperation | Founding member (1969) |
| SAARC | Regional cooperation in South Asia | Founding member (1985) |

#### Regional Bodies Pakistan Joins

SAARC has **8 members** since Afghanistan's 2007 admission (previously 7). The ECO (Tehran) links Iran, Pakistan, Turkey, and Central Asian states. Pakistan became a full SCO member in 2017, giving it access to the Russia-China security dialogue.

- The Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations (1961) governs ambassadors, immunity, and protocol.
- The Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties (1969) sets rules for treaty formation and interpretation.
- **Soft power** (Nye) versus **hard power** is a recurring conceptual MCQ distinction.

### 🔴 Extended — Deep Study (3mo+)
> Comprehensive coverage for students targeting high scores on the SPSC paper.

#### Edge Cases and Examiner Traps

Several recurring traps catch unprepared candidates. The OIC was renamed from the Organisation of the Islamic Conference to the Organisation of **Islamic Cooperation** in 2011; older textbooks still print the old name. The ICJ (judicial organ of the UN) and the ICC (independent treaty-based court) both sit at The Hague but are legally separate bodies. NATO and ASEAN memberships are commonly misattributed to Pakistan; Pakistan is in neither.

#### Treaty Frameworks

The 1961 Vienna Convention codified diplomatic immunity, persona non grata rules, and the inviolability of mission premises. The 1969 Vienna Convention governs treaty validity, reservation rules, and the rebus sic stantibus doctrine for changing circumstances.

| Treaty / Concept | Year | Key Provision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations | 1961 | Diplomatic immunity |
| Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties | 1969 | Treaty validity & interpretation |
| UN Charter Art. 2(7) | 1945 | Non-intervention principle |
| UN Charter Art. 25 | 1945 | Binding force of SC decisions |

#### Exam Strategy for SPSC

The General Knowledge section in SPSC carries roughly 3% weight, but International Organizations is one of its highest-yield clusters because the questions are pure fact-recall MCQs. Memorize 15–20 headquarters, founding years, and current heads of the UN, WHO, IMF, World Bank, WTO, OIC, and SAARC. Know that UNGA votes are one-state-one-vote but non-binding, while UNSC decisions under Chapter VII are binding. Practice prompts:

1. Which organ of the UN has its seat at The Hague, and how does it differ from the ICC?
2. State the difference between IMF and World Bank mandates, and name Pakistan's status in each.
